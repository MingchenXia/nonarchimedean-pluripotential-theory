# General big classes: finite-energy polar barriers from section tails

This unreviewed supporting proof uses the actual section energy of own144 and genuine energy/closure of own145/150. It constructs a genuine original relative-E1 pole barrier for every genuine theta-psh pole set, using only actual ample section systems and the true volumes of L-beta Omega. It proves polar-nullness for the intrinsic finite-relative-dual targets, assuming their necessary reference-pole convention. It does not prove that every output M_theta(u) has finite relative dual energy, full weighted continuity, comparison or the all-target inverse. Original P16.5 remains open.

## 1. Original objects and an actual ample section net

Keep actual big rational L, theta=c1(L), V=vol(L)>0 and the original smooth characteristic-zero trivially valued domain of own143. Choose one actual ample rational Omega with Omega-theta ample, as in own145. Let a be any genuine theta-psh function, normalized by a(v_triv)=0. Its original decreasing GF net on L_i, c1(L_i)->theta, can on a tail be placed in the same actual Omega with identical functions, by tensoring the zero-FS generating systems of the actual ample Omega-L_i. Denote that decreasing fixed-Omega GF net by g_i. Its generic values c_i decrease to zero. Take a tail with 0<=c_i<=1; the generic maximum property gives g_i<=1 everywhere.

Thus each g_i is a weighted maximum of actual sections of a divisible power M_i Omega. A section attaining its largest generic weight c_i has q_i=log|s_i|/M_i and q_i+c_i<=g_i. In particular

    q_i<=g_i and q_i<=(g_i-1)+1.                         (1.1)

No finite-energy property is assigned to this individual GF stage. The original arbitrary net and actual tensor identifications are retained.

Fix 0<gamma<1/2 and let p=gamma/(1-gamma)<1. For K>0 define the continuous original obstacle

    psi_i,K=max(-(1-g_i)^gamma,-K).

At g_i=-infinity the value is -K. Continuity follows by composing the continuous nonnegative finite maximum of weighted section norms, including its zero locus, with this capped logarithmic function. These obstacles are <=0 and decrease with both i and increasing K. Their pointwise limit is

    psi=-(1-a)^gamma,

with value -infinity on {a=-infinity}. They need not be PL; own144 supplies actual section-energy limits for every original continuous obstacle.

## 2. Many section weights have a uniform lower bound

Take a sufficiently small positive rational beta with L-beta Omega big. In degrees m for which beta m/M_i is an integer and all bundles are actual integral powers, multiply the section s_i^(beta m/M_i) by every section of m(L-beta Omega). This gives an injective linear map into H^0(X,mL), since X is integral. Its image U_m,beta has dimension h^0(X,m(L-beta Omega)).

For a nonzero product s, its canonical trivial log norm satisfies q_s=log|s|/m<=beta q_i, because the remaining regular section has log norm <=0. Put z=1-g_i>=0 at a point where g_i is finite. Equation (1.1) gives

    psi_i,K-q_s>=-z^gamma+beta z-beta
                 >=-C_gamma beta^(-p)-beta,              (2.1)

where C_gamma=(1-gamma) gamma^(gamma/(1-gamma)), the exact maximum of z^gamma-beta z over z>=0. At a pole of g_i the chosen section and every product vanish on the semivaluation support, so q_s=-infinity and the finite capped obstacle yields filtration bracket +infinity. The bound still holds without subtracting two infinities.

Consequently the actual high-weight flag for psi_i,K at level -m T(beta), where

    T(beta)=C_gamma beta^(-p)+beta,

contains U_m,beta. All weights are <=0 by evaluation at the generic point. True actual section asymptotics therefore bound the asymptotic low-weight mass beyond T(beta) by

    V-vol(L-beta Omega).                                 (2.2)

The bound is independent of i and K; divisibility by a particular M_i changes only the permissible subsequence of degrees in this fixed stage, not its true energy limit.

## 3. An integrable dyadic tail, without exchanging uncontrolled limits

The ordinary volume is C1 on the big cone. Thus there are fixed beta_0>0 and C_0<infinity with L-beta Omega big and

    0<=V-vol(L-beta Omega)<=C_0 beta

for rational 0<beta<=beta_0. Choose T_0 sufficiently large. For T_j=2^j T_0 select rational beta_j comparable to T_j^(-1/p), small enough for the volume bound and large enough that T(beta_j)<=T_j. This is possible since the beta term is bounded and the first term tends to infinity as beta decreases; increase T_0 once if necessary. More explicitly one can take beta_j between r_j and 2r_j, r_j=(2C_gamma/T_j)^(1/p), with 2r_0<=min(beta_0,1) and T_0>=2.

In a fixed obstacle psi_i,K, choose only the finitely many dyadic intervals meeting [0,K]. Pass to degrees divisible by the finitely many beta_j denominators and by M_i. On this subsequence all the actual high-weight dimension bounds of Section2 hold simultaneously. For its intrinsic weights w/m in [-K,0], the normalized negative weight sum is the integral over [0,K] of the normalized low-weight rank. The floors introduce only a vanishing r_m/m error. Bound [0,T_0] by the total rank, and on [T_j,T_(j+1)] use its rank at the left endpoint. Taking the actual section limits yields

    -E_theta^obs(psi_i,K)
        <=T_0 V+sum_{j>=0} T_j C_0 beta_j
        <=C_*<infinity.                                 (3.1)

The sum is finite because its terms are bounded by a fixed multiple of 2^(j(1-1/p)) and p<1. This argument uses a finite common degree for each fixed K and then a convergent numerical upper bound independent of K. It does not exchange a full infinite collection of section-asymptotic limits. Nor does it impose countability on the original i net. The constant depends only on X,L,Omega,gamma, not on a's defining stages or K.

## 4. A genuine finite-energy barrier on the entire original carrier

Let b_i,K=P_theta(psi_i,K). They are genuine continuous-obstacle envelopes and form a decreasing product net. Their raw energies lie in [-C_*,0] by (3.1). Since E_theta(w)<=V w(v_triv) for any genuine w, their generic values are bounded below by -C_*/V and above by zero. Own150 Section5 gives a pointwise genuine theta-psh limit b on every original analytic point. Own145 energy continuity gives

    E_theta(b)>=-C_*>-infinity.

Thus b belongs to the entire genuine relative E1 and b<=psi=-(1-a)^gamma. In particular

    {a=-infinity} subset {b=-infinity}.                   (4.1)

Adding a constant to normalize b does not change this pole inclusion. Its generic value is finite. This construction retains the original a, actual L and carrier; Omega is used only for actual section products, and L-beta Omega remains a big actual bundle in the section-count calculation. No ample inverse equation or replacement finite-energy space occurs.

## 5. A necessary condition on the intrinsic dual targets

Let nu be a positive original Radon measure of raw mass V with nu({V_theta=-infinity})=0. For normalized genuine E1 u, define its relative nonnegative gap H_nu(u)=integral(V_theta-u) dnu on the complement of the reference poles, and use the intrinsic dual supremum

    D_nu=sup_u [E_theta(u)+H_nu(u)].

If D_nu<infinity, every such gap is integrable: H_nu(u)<=D_nu-E_theta(u)<infinity. Normalize the barrier b from Section4. At any point in {a=-infinity} outside the reference poles, V_theta is finite and b=-infinity, so the relative gap is +infinity. Equation (4.1) and the gap integrability therefore imply

    nu({a=-infinity})=0 for every genuine theta-psh a.    (5.1)

The already imposed reference-pole convention handles the excluded subset. All these sets are Borel, since genuine psh functions are usc. No countable union of Zariski closed sets is used to discard arbitrary semivaluation supports.

This proves a necessary intrinsic polar-nullness condition, not sufficiency of polar-nullness for finite dual energy. It also does not yet prove D_(M_theta(u)) finite, so (5.1) is not silently applied to every operator output. Full relative mixed/weighted estimates, comparison and general existence/uniqueness remain open.
