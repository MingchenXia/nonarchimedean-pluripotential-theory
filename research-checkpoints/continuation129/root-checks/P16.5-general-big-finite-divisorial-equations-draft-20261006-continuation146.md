# General big bundles: finite divisorial equations on genuine model envelopes

This unreviewed continuation uses own143--145, with their explicit external algebraic and general-envelope premises retained. It proves finite divisorial target existence using the actual operator on continuous-obstacle envelopes, without granting any nonample equation. It includes targets with zero weights on the other central components. It does not extend that operator to the entire genuine relative E1 space or prove uniqueness. Original P16.5 remains open.

## 1. Domain, conventions and finite-coordinate growth

Let X be smooth integral projective of dimension n over an algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero field k, and L an actual big rational bundle, theta=c1(L), V=vol(L)>0. Keep genuine PSH(theta), the canonical reference V_theta=P_theta(0), the raw energy E_theta of own145, and the obstacle operator M_theta(P_theta(f)) of own144. No nefness, finite generation, change of carrier, or ample perturbation of the equation is assumed.

Fix a smooth equivariant snc model rho:Y -> X times P1, isomorphic away from zero, with central fiber F=sum b_j E_j. Its central points x_j are the restrictions of b_j^(-1)ord_Ej. For a real vertical divisor D=sum b_j d_j E_j, let f_D be the original continuous model function. Then f_D(x_j)=d_j. Real coefficients are continuous real linear combinations of rational model functions; own143--144 apply by their fixed-model continuity and the real-class volume differential. The actual generic bundle remains L.

These central points are distinct: their Gauss extensions are the distinct normalized prime-divisor valuations on Y. Thus varying one coordinate really does test the mass of that one atom.

Choose one actual ample Omega with Omega-theta ample. By the direct zero-FS tensor construction of own145, the defining net for any genuine u in PSH(theta) can, on a tail, be viewed as GF FS functions on the same actual Omega. The elementary section bound at any divisorial x_j gives

    u(v_triv)-T_Omega(x_j) <= u(x_j) <= u(v_triv).           (1.1)

The growth T_Omega(x_j) is finite. The upper bound is the generic maximum property, since all actual section norms are at most one. The lower bound follows at each FS stage from m^(-1)v(s)<=T_Omega(v), followed by the original decreasing net. At the trivial valuation the growth is zero. This argument is independent of any finite-energy equation or net-flattening statement.

## 2. All-point obstacle conditions are finite vertex conditions

For every genuine u,

    u<=f_D on all of X^an iff u(x_j)<=d_j for every j.      (2.1)

Here is a direct proof of the nontrivial implication, retaining semivaluation supports. Let x be any original analytic point, w its Gauss extension, and p its center on Y. Work near p. The components E_j through p have local equations z_j, and

    t = unit times product_j z_j^(b_j).

Since w(t)=1, each w(z_j) is finite, lambda_j=b_j w(z_j)>=0, and sum_j lambda_j=1. The model function is

    f_D(x)=sum_j lambda_j d_j.

Let s be any nonzero global section of an actual rational bundle A in a divisible degree m. Trivialize A near the image of p on X. The pulled back section is regular near p. If a_j=ord_Ej(s), its local equation is divisible by product z_j^(a_j); its remaining local factor is regular. Evaluating this factor at the centered semivaluation gives a nonnegative value, possibly +infinity. Therefore

    m^(-1)log|s|(x)
       <= sum_j lambda_j m^(-1)log|s|(x_j).

For a finite weighted maximum defining a GF FS function phi, the identity sum lambda_j=1 and max(sum)<=sum(max) give

    phi(x)<=sum_j lambda_j phi(x_j).                       (2.2)

Apply (2.2) to every stage of the actual defining net for u. There are only finitely many vertices through p, and each limiting vertex value is finite by (1.1). Decreasing limits commute with this finite weighted sum. Hence u(x)<=sum lambda_j u(x_j)<=f_D(x). If u(x)=-infinity the inequality still has its literal meaning. No subtraction at a pole and no restriction to valuations with full support has occurred. The reverse implication of (2.1) is evaluation at the vertices.

The underlying Gauss/vertical-model conventions have also been checked in the local primary arXiv:1801.08229v4, literal LF724--755 and the complete snc model representation and inverse-limit proof LF6767--6857. The convex inequality just proved uses actual sections directly and does not import its ample Theorem A.4 as a general-big theorem.

## 3. Removing zero-target coordinates exactly

Let S be a nonempty subset of the central indices, with desired positive weights p_i>0 for i in S and sum_i p_i=V. Indices outside S have desired weight zero. Put

    T=max_{i in S} T_Omega(x_i).

For a=(a_i) in R^S set M(a)=max_i a_i+T+1, and form D(a) with d_i=a_i on S and d_j=M(a) outside S. By (1.1), any genuine u satisfying u(x_i)<=a_i for i in S satisfies

    u(v_triv)<=min_i [a_i+T_Omega(x_i)]<=max_i a_i+T,
    u(x_j)<=max_i a_i+T<M(a) for j outside S.              (3.1)

Consequently (2.1) identifies the same family of genuine functions in the following two definitions:

    {u:u<=f_D(a)} = {u:u(x_i)<=a_i for i in S}.

Thus w_a=P_theta(f_D(a)) is the genuine supremum for precisely the prescribed positive-coordinate constraints. Its actual obstacle measure mu_a has zero mass at every extra vertex. Indeed own143 gives a finite atomic measure supported on all the central vertices, and own144 contact orthogonality applies. Equation (3.1) makes every extra vertex strictly outside the contact set. At these vertices all values of w_a are finite by (1.1), so this conclusion uses no convention about subtracting infinities.

## 4. A coercive finite-dimensional maximum

Define

    G(a)=E_theta(w_a)-sum_{i in S} p_i a_i.

The function is finite and continuous: a -> f_D(a) is continuous in the original uniform norm and own144 has V-Lipschitz continuous obstacle energy. Shifting every a_i by c shifts f_D(a), w_a and E_theta(w_a) by c, c and cV respectively. Since sum p_i=V, G(a+c)=G(a). Normalize max_i a_i=0.

Every genuine w satisfies w<=V_theta+w(v_triv), by the definition of the normalized minimal reference. Energy monotonicity and its constant-shift identity therefore give E_theta(w)<=V w(v_triv). Apply this to w_a and (3.1):

    E_theta(w_a)<=V min_i a_i+VT.

Let p_min=min_i p_i>0 and osc(a)=max_i a_i-min_i a_i. Then

    G(a)<=VT-sum_i p_i(a_i-min a)
         <=VT-p_min osc(a).                              (4.1)

The last step uses an index attaining max a. It is valid also for |S|=1, when the normalized domain is one point. In general, every upper level set of G on {max a=0} is bounded and closed in the finite-dimensional Euclidean space. It is compact. A maximizing sequence has a convergent subsequence in one such level set, so G attains its global maximum at a*. This is an actual maximum of the obstacle functional, not just an abstract Legendre dual value.

## 5. Derivatives give the actual equation, including zero weights

The use of M(a)=max a+T+1 is not a differentiability assumption about max. Fix a base a and a small neighborhood of it, and choose a single real R larger than max b_i+T for every b in that neighborhood. Set the extra divisor coordinates to this fixed R instead. By (3.1) the corresponding envelope for every b is still exactly w_b; consequently its obstacle energy is the same function E_theta(w_b). It is now a linear obstacle family in the coordinates b_i, and own144 two-sided derivatives give

    partial/partial a_i E_theta(w_a)=mu_a({x_i}).           (5.1)

At the global maximum a* of the constant-invariant function G on R^S, each partial derivative vanishes. Equations (5.1) give mu_a*({x_i})=p_i for every i in S. Its other central masses vanish by Section3, and there are no other atoms by own143. Therefore the actual target-independent obstacle operator satisfies

    M_theta(w_a*) = sum_{i in S} p_i delta_x_i.             (5.2)

Furthermore w_a* has minimal relative singularities: the bounded continuous model obstacle gives V_theta-||f_D||<=w_a*<=V_theta+||f_D||. It belongs to the entire genuine relative E1 defined in own145, since its energy is the finite obstacle energy. The measure in (5.2) is the operator already defined before this target was selected. It is not assigned by inverting the target.

## 6. The entire-cone variational maximum for this target

Write nu=sum p_i delta_x_i. All genuine values of u and V_theta at these finitely many atoms are finite. Thus the relative objective is literally

    F_nu(u)=E_theta(u)-sum_i p_i [u(x_i)-V_theta(x_i)].

It is unchanged by constant shifts. For normalized u, (1.1) and V_theta<=0 bound V_theta(x_i)-u(x_i)<=T_Omega(x_i); E_theta(u)<=0. Hence the intrinsic relative dual supremum is finite, bounded above by sum_i p_i T_Omega(x_i), and is at least zero by testing V_theta.

Given any genuine u, put a_i=u(x_i). The finite-constraint construction gives u<=w_a, so energy monotonicity yields

    F_nu(u)<=G(a)+sum_i p_i V_theta(x_i)
             <=G(a*)+sum_i p_i V_theta(x_i).

Since mu_a* has positive weight p_i at each target atom, contact orthogonality gives w_a*(x_i)=a_i*. The final upper bound is therefore exactly F_nu(w_a*). Thus the same genuine solution attains the relative variational supremum over the entire original genuine cone, including every original relative-E1 function. This does not extend the operator beyond its already established obstacle-envelope domain.

## 7. Realizing arbitrary finite rational divisorial supports

The preceding proof is already complete for any subset of the vertices of one snc model. With the standard characteristic-zero equivariant extraction/resolution background, it covers every finite support in the original X^div (which includes v_triv and has rational positive divisorial scales).

Use the Gauss extension w with w(t)=1 of a rational divisorial valuation. It is divisorial and invariant under G_m. Repeatedly blow up its center, which stays invariant and lies over zero. Zariski's divisorial-center theorem makes this center a prime divisor after finitely many steps. Normalization and subsequent equivariant snc resolution retain that divisor. All modifications are supported over zero; the punctured product is preserved. For finitely many valuations take a common model. The product central component represents v_triv.

This is the construction in the complete primary Theorem4.6 and Lemma4.7 proofs of Boucksom--Hisamoto--Jonsson, [Uniform K-stability, Duistermaat--Heckman measures and singularities of pairs](https://numdam.org/item/10.5802/aif.3096.pdf), rendered lines2473--2525, printed pp.784--785. Zariski's termination theorem and equivariant resolution remain imported algebraic premises. No horizontal replacement of the original generic X is used. Real irrational divisorial scales are not included in the original rational X^div convention.

## 8. What has and has not been established

Conditional on the unreviewed own143--145 dependency chain and the stated algebraic/general-envelope inputs, this proves finite divisorial target existence and actual entire-cone relative variational attainment for every actual big rational L in every dimension, with raw volume mass and a genuine minimal-singularity solution on the whole original carrier. The proof covers zero weights on extra central components and uses no nonample equation grant.

It does not prove that two such genuine solutions differ by a constant, give full comparison or domination, construct the entire-E1 Monge--Ampere measure, or handle arbitrary intrinsic finite-energy targets. Weak compactness of the normalized energy sublevels alone does not justify passing the equation through limits of these atomic targets. Those are still open obligations for original P16.5. No original problem closure or reviewed-paper count follows from this unreviewed note.
