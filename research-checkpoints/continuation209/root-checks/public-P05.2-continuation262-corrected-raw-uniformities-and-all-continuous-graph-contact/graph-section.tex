\section{A further sector: every continuous obstacle on a finite graph}\label{graph-sector}
The new progress beyond the continuity repair is in dimension one. Here
we allow the \emph{actual} $\gamma\in\PA_\R(X)\cap\PC_q(X)$ to have
arbitrary real, including irrational, affine coefficients, on a finite
\emph{rational} carrying graph. The graph is globally embedded, finite,
pure, and has actual positive real edge weights $w_e$ satisfying lattice
balancing. Its vertices and edge lengths in primitive coordinates are
rational. The conclusion is for \emph{all} $u\in C_b(X)$, not just finite
PA obstacles. Abstract compact chart spaces are outside this theorem.

For each vertex $v$ let $u_{e/v}$ be the primitive outgoing direction and
\[
 K_v=\{\lambda\geq0:\sum_{e\ni v}\lambda_eu_{e/v}=0\}.
\]
The actual weight vector $w_v$ is strictly positive and lies in $K_v$.
We may insert a rational bivalent vertex on a line without vertices;
its cone is one ray, and this does not change the operator or contact.

\begin{lemma}[Continuous graph slopes and supports]\label{graph-slopes}
A continuous printed-PC function $f$ is ordinarily convex on every edge.
Its outgoing one-sided derivatives $d_{v,e}$ are finite at every vertex,
and satisfy $d_v\cdot\lambda\geq0$ for every $\lambda\in K_v$.
Conversely, continuous edgewise convex functions with these finite
vertex derivatives and inequalities satisfy every PCC. They have
ambient affine local supports at every point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Contained-edge Jensen inequalities pass from rational to real endpoints
by continuity; thus they give ordinary convexity. For rational $t>0$
small enough on every germ, the endpoints $v+t u_{e/v}$ are rational.
The weighted relation $w_v$ gives
$\sum_e w_e(f(v+tu_{e/v})-f(v))/t\geq0$.
Each quotient is nondecreasing as $t$ increases, and is bounded above
near zero by its finite quotient at one fixed small radius. Its limit
therefore lies in $[-\infty,\infty)$; none can be $-\infty$, because
all $w_e>0$ and all other terms have fixed finite upper bounds.
This proves finite derivatives. Applying the same endpoint construction
to any $\lambda\in K_v$ and passing $t\downarrow0$ gives the inequality.
The coefficients can be real; the actual endpoints remain rational.

Conversely the finite duality proof in Lemma~\ref{supports}, applied
to the matrix of primitive directions, gives an ambient $\ell_v$ with
$\ell_v(u_{e/v})\leq d_{v,e}$. Convexity on each germ gives
$f(v+tu_{e/v})\geq f(v)+t d_{v,e}$, hence this is a local support
at $v$. At an edge interior point the left and right convex derivatives
are finite; any slope between them is a supporting affine function
on its line and can be extended ambiently. Thus there are supports at
every point, and Lemma~\ref{localglobal} proves all real-endpoint PCC
inequalities. No PA or operator premise was used in this lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-continuous real graph regularization]\label{graph-density}
For the actual real PA reference $\gamma$ positive at infinity, every
$f\in C(X)\cap\PC_q(X)$ with bounded difference from $\gamma$ is a
uniform decreasing limit of relative $\PAPC_\R$ functions on finite
rational carrying structures. In particular every unrestricted envelope
$P_\gamma(u)$, $u\in C_b(X)$, lies in this real regularizable domain.
The statement asserts no literal integral-slope density.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Refine the original graph so $\gamma$ is affine on its edges. Use the
primitive coordinate on each edge. On an unbounded end, for $t\geq T_e$
write $\gamma(t)=g_et+c_e$, $g_e>0$, with a fixed rational $T_e$.
Convexity of $f$ and bounded relative difference force its limiting
slope to be $g_e$. Indeed secant slopes at infinity tend to $g_e$,
and monotonicity of convex derivatives identifies their limit with
that secant limit. Therefore $r_e(t)=f(t)-g_et$ is convex,
nonincreasing, and bounded. It has a finite limit $l_e$.
For any $\eta>0$ choose rational $S_e\geq T_e$ so
$r_e(S_e)-l_e<\eta$. Replace the tail of $f$ by
$f(S_e)+g_e(t-S_e)$, which majorizes $f$ and differs from it by at
most $\eta$. Its slope $g_e$ is at least every preceding chord slope.

On every bounded edge, and every initial segment $[0,S_e]$ of an end,
choose a sufficiently fine finite mesh of rational coordinates including
all vertices, and interpolate the \emph{actual real values} of $f$.
Convex chords majorize $f$, are uniformly close by uniform continuity
on these finitely many compact intervals, and have nondecreasing slopes.
At an original vertex the new outgoing first chord slope is at least
$d_{v,e}$, on both ends of every bounded edge. Since all relation
coefficients are nonnegative, every vertex relation cost remains
nonnegative. At newly inserted bivalent vertices, monotone edge slopes
give exactly the same condition. Lemma~\ref{graph-slopes} proves PC.
Shared vertex values are unchanged, so the resulting function is
continuous. Its affine slopes and constants are real, and all mesh
positions are rational; hence it is actual $\PA_\R$ on a finite
rational structure. Each tail has the \emph{actual} $g_e$ slope,
so bounded difference from $\gamma$ is preserved globally.
The errors can be made arbitrarily small on the whole graph, including
all ends. This construction never takes a finite PA maximum with the
irrational reference, and has introduced no irrational carrying wall.

Extract errors $\leq2^{-n}$ and add $4\cdot2^{-n}$ as in
Theorem~\ref{raw-extension}. The resulting relative real PA PC sequence
is strictly decreasing and converges uniformly. Finally R230 applies
to this finite positive balanced embedded graph and the actual
continuous PC reference: for any $u\in C_b$ its unrestricted envelope
is continuous, PC, and satisfies $|P_\gamma(u)-\gamma|\leq\|u\|_\infty$.
The just-proved approximation applies to that actual endpoint. This is
an operator-domain antecedent, not merely CONT PC membership.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Genuine graph raw completion for all real coefficients]\label{graph-operator}
On the domain in Theorem~\ref{graph-density}, the sequence-independent
uniform completion of the primitive one-dimensional raw operator is
\[
 \Delta f=\sum_v\left(\sum_{e\ni v}w_ed_{v,e}\right)\delta_v
          +\sum_e w_e\,d(f'_+)\big|_{\operatorname{relint}e}.
\]
The second terms are pushforwards in the primitive edge coordinate;
there is no Euclidean arclength or additional factorial. The output is
positive, local, finite, and has mass $M=\sum_{\text{ends }e}w_eg_e
=\deg(\gamma)$. Every uniform PA approximating family converges against
all $C_b$ tests, and agrees with literal raw recursion when restricted
to its actual literal completion domain.

For each fixed $C<\infty$, all these completed real outputs with
$\|f-\gamma\|_\infty\leq C$ satisfy the common tail bound
\[
 \Delta f(X\setminus K_R)\leq\frac{2C}{R}\sum_{\text{ends }e}w_e,
 \quad R>0,
\]
where $K_R$ contains all bounded edges and the end portions
$0\leq t\leq T_e+R$. In addition, choose fixed rational $c$ with
$H=\gamma+c\geq0$ and let $J=\int H\,d\Delta\gamma$. Every completed
function in that same fixed-$C$ family has
$\int H\,d\Delta f\leq2CM+J$. The map is continuous for weak $C_b$, globally
uniformly continuous for vague $C_c$, and uniformly continuous for
weak $C_b$ and $d_{\rm BL}$ on every such \emph{fixed-$C$} family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a convex edge function, $f'_+$ is nondecreasing and right continuous.
Right continuity follows directly from the convex secant inequalities:
the right limit of $f'_+$ at $t$ is bounded above by every secant from
$t$ to $t+h$, and taking $h\downarrow0$ gives $f'_+(t)$ itself.
On a compact interval construct positive test functionals from sums
$\sum_i g(\xi_i)(f'_+(t_{i+1})-f'_+(t_i))$.
Two such sums on a common refinement differ by at most the oscillation
of $g$ at their mesh scale times the finite derivative variation.
They therefore converge for continuous tests, are positive and linear,
and the positive-functional lemma constructs their Radon measure.
Continuous level cutoffs and right continuity identify its interval
values, including any endpoint jump. Its Stieltjes measure is determined by
$d(f'_+)((a,b])=f'_+(b)-f'_+(a)$. This determines a positive measure:
on finite intervals, step approximations of the monotone derivative
construct its distribution measure by the same open-set or monotone
interval construction as in Lemma~\ref{radon-lemma}. Countable additivity
follows by continuity of the monotone distribution from the right and
finite interval subdivision; equivalently positive integration functionals
on compact continuous tests and that lemma give the identical interval
values. The finite endpoint derivatives in Lemma~\ref{graph-slopes}
and the limiting end slope $g_e$ give finite edge mass.
Vertex coefficients are nonnegative by the actual relation $w_v$.
On a bounded edge of primitive length $L$, its interior mass is
$w_e(f'_-(L)-f'_+(0))$; its two endpoint contributions to the vertex
sum are $w_ef'_+(0)$ and $-w_ef'_-(L)$. They cancel exactly.
On an unbounded end the interior mass is $w_e(g_e-d_{v,e})$;
adding its vertex contribution leaves $w_eg_e$. Summing the finite
edge list proves the mass identity. Applied to PA functions this is
exactly the actual primitive-normal recursion, including zero artificial
vertices, and proves $M=\deg(\gamma)$.

For a compact rational ambient PA test $\psi|_X$, edgewise Stieltjes
integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{graph-ibp}
 \int\psi\,d\Delta f=\int f\,d\Delta\psi.
\end{equation}
Here $\Delta\psi$ is the signed finite primitive raw divisor of the
compact test against $[X]$. To justify the identity, on each interval
where $\psi$ is affine, integrate its constant derivative against the
increments of $f$, and integrate $\psi$ against the increments of the
monotone $f'_+$. Telescoping interval partitions gives
$\int\psi\,d f'_+=[\psi f']-\int\psi'f'\,dt$.
The latter ordinary integral equals the corresponding affine-test slope
times the endpoint increment of $f$: convex functions with finite
endpoint slopes are Lipschitz on the interval and their increments are
the integrals of their monotone derivatives, as follows by upper and
lower secant Riemann sums. The derivative jumps of $\psi$ supply
$\int f\,d\psi'$. At vertices the boundary terms cancel against the
explicit weighted vertex derivatives on both sides of
\eqref{graph-ibp}. Unbounded edges have no outer boundary term because
the test vanishes there. Constants and continuity cancel the remaining
tangent values. This proves the identity without assuming PA for $f$.

It follows that
$|\langle\psi,\Delta f-\Delta h\rangle|
\leq\|\Delta\psi\|_{\rm TV}\|f-h\|_\infty$.
Uniform density of compact rational PA tests, with controlled supports,
and mass $M$ give convergence for every $C_c$ test and sequence
independence. Locality follows directly from the displayed derivative
formula, or from localized tests in \eqref{graph-ibp}.
For $\|f-\gamma\|_\infty\leq C$, convexity implies
\[
 f'_+(T_e+R)\geq\frac{f(T_e+R)-f(T_e)}R
                         \geq g_e-2C/R.
\]
The end measure beyond that point is
$w_e(g_e-f'_+(T_e+R))\leq2Cw_e/R$.
This proves the asserted common bound for \emph{every} completed function
in the family. A uniformly convergent approximation to a fixed relative
function has a common finite $C$, so compact cutoff truncation upgrades
its vague convergence to every $C_b$ test. At one input use a radius-one
uniform neighborhood with $C_f=1+\|f-\gamma\|_\infty$ to get ordinary
weak-$C_b$ continuity. The compact-test estimate gives global vague
uniform continuity. For each fixed $C$, the common end-tail bound and
finite compact-test approximation give weak-$C_b$ uniform continuity;
the finite mesh and value-quantization net of BL restrictions on $K_R$
from Theorem~\ref{raw-extension} gives BL uniform continuity with
that same fixed $C$. If $M=0$, positivity gives the zero output and
all claims are immediate.
For completeness the moment bound holds for all these outputs even with
irrational coefficients of the actual reference. The continuous convex
$q_j=\max(\gamma-j,-c)$ is boundedly relative to $\gamma$.
It is not asserted to have rational carrying walls; Theorem~\ref{graph-density}
places it in the actual real completion by rational chords. Its measures
and those chord approximants have already been proved to converge for
all $C_b$ tests. For finite relative PA $a$, apply the finite bounded
edge IBP identity to actual PA approximants of $q_j$ and pass to the
limit, using the bounded differences as tests. This gives
\[
 \int(\gamma-q_j)\Delta a
 =\int(\gamma-a)\Delta q_j+\int(a-q_j)\Delta\gamma.
\]
The first term and the part $a-\gamma$ of the second have absolute
value at most $CM$ each; the remaining term is
$\int\min(H,j)\Delta\gamma\leq J$.
Approximate any completed $f$ with relative bounds $C+o(1)$ and use
all-$C_b$ convergence on the bounded test $\min(H,j)$ to obtain
$\int\min(H,j)\Delta f\leq2CM+J$. Monotone convergence proves
the stated common moment bound for every completed function in the
fixed-$C$ family. This application has an actual operator antecedent
for $q_j$ before any measure is used.
There is no arbitrary-relative-bound weak
uniform guarantee; the line counterexamples still apply.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-continuous graph contact classification]\label{graph-contact}
For the finite globally embedded positive balanced rational graph and
actual positive-growth $\gamma\in\PA_\R\cap\PC_q$ of this section,
the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every $u\in C_b(X)$ has a real-regularizable actual envelope and
$\int(P_\gamma(u)-\gamma-u)\Delta P_\gamma(u)=0$.
\item $K_v=\R_{\geq0}w_v$ at every vertex.
\end{enumerate}
If the second condition fails, an actual bounded real PA obstacle already
has a finite relative real PA PC envelope with a strictly negative
contact integral, even for irrational coefficients of the actual reference.
This is a one-dimensional sector theorem, not the full original P05.2.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All actual envelopes have their real-domain antecedent and genuine
operator by Theorems~\ref{graph-density}--\ref{graph-operator} and R230.
Let $f=P_\gamma(u)$ and suppose the local cones have one ray.
At a noncontact vertex with positive atom, its derivative vector $d_v$
has $d_v\cdot w_v>0$, hence strictly positive cost on every nonzero
relation. The corrected strict-floor separation argument applies to
this finite derivative vector: it gives $\ell$ and $c>0$ with
$d_{v,e}t-\ell(tu_{e/v})\geq c\|tu_{e/v}\|$ on every germ.
Edge convexity gives the same lower bound for
$f(v+tu)-f(v)-\ell(tu)$. On a small star with positive continuous
obstacle gap, raise $f$ to
$\max(f,f(v)+b+\ell(x-v))$ with $b$ less than the gap and the strict
collar margin; outside leave it unchanged. Supports of $f$ exist by
Lemma~\ref{graph-slopes}, and supports of active entries persist at
ties. The collar makes the localized function continuous and PC by
Lemma~\ref{localglobal}. It preserves actual growth and raises $f(v)$,
contradicting the unrestricted envelope. Thus every noncontact vertex
has zero atom. No finite PA germ for $f$ was assumed here.

At an interior noncontact point choose a sufficiently small closed
edge interval $[a,b]$ in the positive-gap set, with oscillation of $f$
less than the gap there. Replacing $f$ on that interval by its chord
raises it by less than that gap. The incoming derivative at $a$ is
at most the secant slope and the outgoing derivative at $b$ is at
least it, so the glued edge remains convex. All original vertex
derivatives are untouched, hence supports and PCC inequalities remain
valid. If $f$ were not affine there, this would be a competitor raising
an interior point. Consequently $f$ is locally affine throughout the
open noncontact portion of every edge, and its curvature measure
vanishes there. Together with the vertex conclusion, the positive
measure is supported on the closed contact set. Its total mass is
finite and the gap is bounded continuous, giving the exact zero integral.

For necessity suppose $\dim K_v>1$. Since $w_v$ is strictly positive,
$K_v$ has dimension $\dim\ker U_v$. Choose an extreme ray $\kappa$
of this rational cone. It omits some edge coordinate $e_0$:
otherwise an independent kernel vector would permit small two-sided
perturbations of its strictly positive coefficients, contradicting
extremality. The ray can be chosen rational by the finite circuit proof.
Set $s_{e_0}=1$ and all other $s_e=0$. This homogeneous rational
edgewise affine germ $h$ is nonnegative, has the zero local support,
and is PC; $s\cdot\kappa=0$ while its actual raw mass is
$s\cdot w_v=w_{e_0}>0$. Real positive weights are retained exactly.

We realize this germ with the \emph{actual irrational-coefficient}
reference, using graph interpolation rather than irrational PA maximum
closure. Coercivity gives $\gamma\geq a\rho-b_0$. Choose rational
$\varepsilon>0$ smaller than $a$, and rational $C>\gamma(v)$.
The continuous PC function
$\Psi=\max(\gamma,C+\varepsilon\rho(x-v))$ equals its second entry
near $v$ and equals $\gamma$ outside a compact set. It is \emph{not}
asserted to belong to rational-geometry PA. Choose a rational star
radius $r$, rational $\delta>0$ with $\delta|h|\leq\varepsilon\rho/2$,
and rational $0<t<\varepsilon r/4$.
Inside the star set $G=\max(\Psi,C+t+\delta h)$ and outside set $G=\Psi$.
The same strict collar and local-support argument as in the rational
jet theorem gives a continuous PC global $G$, boundedly relative to
the actual $\gamma$, with germ $C+t+\delta h$.

Now apply the \emph{proved graph} chord construction to $G$, choosing
its first rational sample on every germ at $v$ inside that unchanged
linear germ. The first chord is exactly the germ; subsequent meshes,
other vertex slopes, and tail slopes have the properties proved in
Theorem~\ref{graph-density}. This produces an actual relative
$F\in\PAPC_\R$ on finite rational geometry with the same germ at $v$,
flat relation $\kappa$, and raw mass $\delta w_{e_0}>0$.
It need not have rational coefficients elsewhere; these are allowed
real coefficients on rational carrying cells. Both $F$ and the actual
$\gamma$ are now on finite rational structures, so $F-\gamma$ is
bounded real PA. The exact rational roof theorem applies in that real
category, producing $u=F-\gamma+(R-\rho(x-v))_+$ with actual
unrestricted envelope $F$ and contact $-R\delta w_{e_0}<0$.
All endpoint and operator antecedents are verified. The intermediate
continuous maximum received no operator from its continuity; it was
replaced by an actual PA function before the roof calculation.

Subdivision adds only bivalent stars with a single relation ray, so the
condition is intrinsic to this carrying graph. The proof uses global
embedded coercivity and does not transfer the necessity construction
to abstract compact charts. It also proves no higher-dimensional
approximation or non-PA contact criterion.
\end{proof}
