# 3. Complete finite algebraic decomposition on a surface

From now through Section 7, $\dim X=2$. A bundle is pseudoeffective if its numerical class lies in the closure of the cone of effective rational divisors. **Lemma 3.1 (maximal nef subdivisor).** An effective rational divisor $D$ has a decomposition
\[
 D=P_D+N_D,
 \quad P_D\text{ effective nef},\quad N_D\geq0,\quad
 P_D\cdot C=0\text{ on }\operatorname{Supp}N_D,
                                                               \tag{3.1}
\]
whose negative support has negative definite intersection matrix. Both parts are rational.

**Proof.** Write $D=\sum_{i=1}^r d_iC_i$ with distinct prime curves. The compact rational polytope
\[
 K=\left\{(p_i):0\leq p_i\leq d_i,\quad
                  \sum_i p_i(C_i\cdot C_j)\geq0\ (1\leq j\leq r)
      \right\}                                                \tag{3.2}
\]
is nonempty. Its points are exactly the effective nef subdivisors: intersections with every curve outside this finite support are nonnegative. Maximize $\sum p_i$ and choose a rational maximizing vertex.

Coordinatewise maxima preserve feasibility. For the inequality tested against $C_j$, choose the original feasible vector realizing the larger $j$th coordinate. The diagonal contribution stays fixed and all other coordinates increase with nonnegative off-diagonal coefficients. Consequently the maximizing vector is the unique greatest feasible vector: a feasible vector exceeding it in any coordinate would make their coordinatewise maximum have larger sum. Set $P_D=\sum p_iC_i$, $N_D=D-P_D$.

If $a_j=d_j-p_j>0$ and $P_D\cdot C_j>0$, a small increase of $p_j$ preserves the $j$th inequality and the cuboid; all other tested intersections only increase. This contradicts maximality. Thus the claimed orthogonality holds.

Let $Q$ be the intersection matrix on the support of $N_D$. If its largest eigenvalue were nonnegative, choose a unit vector maximizing its Rayleigh quotient. Replacing it by its coordinatewise absolute value cannot decrease that quotient, since the off-diagonal entries are nonnegative. A nonnegative maximizing vector $q\ne0$ is an eigenvector, so $Qq\geq0$. The effective real divisor $F=\sum q_iC_i$ is nef: its tested intersections on its support are nonnegative, and those with any other curve are nonnegative. Adding a sufficiently small positive multiple of $F$ to $P_D$ stays inside (3.2), since every corresponding coefficient has positive room $a_i$. It improves the maximum, a contradiction. Therefore $Q$ is negative definite. $\square$

**Lemma 3.2 (inverse sign and numerical minimality).** For a negative definite symmetric $Q$ with nonnegative off-diagonal entries, $Q^{-1}$ has nonpositive entries. If a class $\ell$ has a decomposition $\ell=p+[N]$ as in (3.1), with $p$ nef and $Q$ negative definite, then in every numerical decomposition
\[
 \ell=q_0+[M],\qquad q_0\text{ nef},\quad M\geq0,
                                                               \tag{3.3}
\]
one has the actual divisor inequality $M\geq N$.

**Proof.** Put $A=-Q$. It is positive definite with nonpositive off-diagonal entries. If $Ax=b\geq0$ and $x=x_+-x_-$ has disjoint nonnegative parts with $x_-\ne0$, then
\[
 0\leq b\cdot x_-=x_+^TAx_- -x_-^TAx_-<0.
\]
The first term is nonpositive and the second is strictly positive. Hence $x\geq0$. Apply this to the coordinate vectors $b$ to obtain $A^{-1}\geq0$ and $Q^{-1}\leq0$.

Write $N=\sum a_iC_i$, and split $M=\sum m_iC_i+M'$ with $M'$ having no component in the negative support. Orthogonality and nefness of $q_0$ give
\[
 Q(a-m)_i-M'\cdot C_i\geq0.
\]
Every $M'\cdot C_i\geq0$. Therefore $Q(a-m)\geq0$, and the inverse sign implies $a-m\leq0$. Thus $M\geq N$. $\square$

Any two decompositions with these properties have the same negative divisor, by applying minimality in both directions. In particular the divisor $N$ depends only on the numerical class, not on the chosen effective representative, section, or ample perturbation. The positive part of an **actual** bundle $L$ is the actual rational bundle $P=L-\mathcal O(N)$, abbreviated to $L-N$; it is not an unspecified numerical representative.

**Lemma 3.3 (exact section division).** For a rational bundle $L=P+N$ with these properties, multiplication by the canonical section is an actual isomorphism
\[
 H^0(X,mP)\xrightarrow{\;\cdot s_{mN}\;}H^0(X,mL)
                   \quad(mP,mN,mL\text{ integral}).              \tag{3.4}
\]
More generally every section of integral $mL$ vanishes along $\lceil mN\rceil$.

**Proof.** The effective zero divisor $T$ of a nonzero section of $mL$ is numerically $mL$. Write $T=\sum t_iC_i+T'$ outside the negative support. Intersecting gives
\[
 Q(t-ma)_i=-T'\cdot C_i\leq0.
\]
Multiplying by $Q^{-1}\leq0$ yields $t\geq ma$. Since $t_i$ are integers, $t_i\geq\lceil ma_i\rceil$. Dividing a section by the indicated canonical section is globally a section of the actual residual line bundle: its divisor is effective, and the division agrees in every local frame. Conversely multiplication is injective and every residual section gives an $mL$ section. This proves (3.4), including the zero-dimensional section spaces. $\square$

# 4. Pseudoeffective rational surfaces: finite support and the perturbation limit

\[
 L=P+N,

\]
with negative definite support when $N\ne0$. If $N_\epsilon$ and $P_\epsilon$ are the parts of $L+\epsilon H$, for positive rational $\epsilon$, then
\[
 0\leq N_\epsilon\leq N,\quad
 N_\epsilon\uparrow N\text{ coefficientwise as }\epsilon\downarrow0,
 \qquad c_1(P_\epsilon)\to c_1(P).                              \tag{4.2}
\]
All negative supports in this family lie in one finite set of prime curves. No effectivity or section of $L$ is required.

**Proof.** For every $\epsilon>0$, $L+\epsilon H$ is an actual big rational bundle. Indeed choose effective rational divisors $D_j$ with classes tending to $c_1(L)$. On a tail $L+\epsilon H-D_j$ is actual ample. A divisible section of $D_j$ multiplies the sections of this ample difference into those of the actual $L+\epsilon H$. Ample Serre vanishing and Riemann--Roch give positive quadratic section growth. Thus choose an effective representative of $L+\epsilon H$ and apply Lemmas 3.1--3.2.

If $0<\epsilon'<\epsilon$, then
\[
 L+\epsilon H=(P_{\epsilon'}+(\epsilon-\epsilon')H)+N_{\epsilon'}
\]
is a nef-plus-effective decomposition. Minimality gives $N_\epsilon\leq N_{\epsilon'}$. Their supports are nested as $\epsilon$ decreases.

Let $\rho=\dim N^1(X)_{\mathbb R}$. Each negative support has at most $\rho$ curves: a numerical dependence between these curves would give a kernel vector of their negative definite intersection matrix. The nested union also has at most $\rho$ curves. Otherwise choose $\rho+1$ distinct curves in it and one rational $\epsilon$ smaller than the finitely many parameters exhibiting them; its negative support would contain all of them. Hence the union is finite. Choose $\epsilon_0$ for which it is the entire support, and retain its negative definite matrix $Q$.

For $0<\epsilon\leq\min(1,\epsilon_0)$,
\[
 0\leq N_\epsilon\cdot H
       \leq (L+\epsilon H)\cdot H\leq L\cdot H+H^2.
\]
Every supported prime curve has positive intersection with $H$. Therefore each nondecreasing coefficient of $N_\epsilon$ is bounded. Let its limit define $N$. Its supported coefficients are positive because each is already positive at $\epsilon_0$. The closed nef cone gives $P=L-N$ nef. On the entire finite support, $P_\epsilon\cdot C_i=0$ for $\epsilon\leq\epsilon_0$, so $P\cdot C_i=0$. In vector notation
\[
 Q a=(L\cdot C_i)_i.
\]
$Q$ is invertible with rational entries and the right side is rational. Thus $a$ is rational, and $P$ is an actual rational bundle. Its negative matrix is $Q$. Lemma 3.2 applies and proves uniqueness and numerical minimality. It also gives $N_\epsilon\leq N$ by applying it to $L+\epsilon H=(P+\epsilon H)+N$. These are exactly (4.2). The empty-support case gives $N=0$ without a matrix. $\square$

Theorem 5.1, applied to each $L+\epsilon H$, provides $u=\sigma_{N_\epsilon}+v_\epsilon$ with genuine normalized nef $v_\epsilon$. Lemma 2.5 and (4.2) give a genuine normalized nef limit $v$ on a subnet, whose divisorial coordinates are $u(z)-\sigma_N(z)$. The constructed $\sigma_N+v$ is genuine in the $L$ cone. The function $u$ is already genuine in, for example, the $L+H$ cone; both embed into one actual ample cone. Determination makes their equality all-pointwise. Formula (5.3) witnesses membership with one net.

Now fix $\epsilon>0$. For all sufficiently large $i$, $c_1(L+\epsilon H)-\theta_i$ is ample. Lemma 2.2 puts the same $u_i$, on that tail, into the entire genuine $L+\epsilon H$ cone. Its decreasing closure from Theorem 5.1 puts the same all-point limit there. This holds for each rational $\epsilon$, so (6.1) applies. # 7. Algebraic volume and raw mass: what is and is not constructed

**Theorem 7.1.** For every actual pseudoeffective rational surface bundle $L=P+N$ of Theorem 4.1,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_X(L)=\operatorname{vol}_X(P)=P^2.             \tag{7.1}
\]
In the big case $P^2>0$. Thus any full-mass nonpluripolar equation in this domain must use the raw mass $P^2$, which need not equal $L^2$.

**Proof.** First prove an upper bound that also handles bounded twists and residue degrees. For a fixed actual integral twist $B$ and sufficiently divisible $m$ for $P$, choose $tH-B$ with a nonzero section. Then
\[
 h^0(mP+B)\leq h^0(mP+tH).
\]
For rational $\delta>0$, multiply further by a nonzero section of $\lceil m\delta\rceil H$ for all large $m$. The resulting bundle is
\[
 mP+(t+\lceil m\delta\rceil)H.
\]
After separating the finitely many residue classes modulo a denominator for $P$ and $\delta$, it is a growing integral multiple of the fixed ample rational $P+\delta H$ plus one of finitely many fixed actual twists. Serre vanishing and Riemann--Roch give
\[
 \limsup_m \frac{2h^0(mP+B)}{m^2}\leq(P+\delta H)^2.
                                                               \tag{7.2}
\]
The same argument for any permissible arithmetic progression is valid. Let $\delta\downarrow0$ to get upper bound $P^2$. Also $P^2\geq0$, since $P+\delta H$ is ample.

If $P^2>0$, then $P\cdot H>0$. Otherwise choose small rational $a>0$ with $H-aP$ ample; nefness would give $0\leq P\cdot(H-aP)=-aP^2$, a contradiction. For large divisible $m$, $K_X-mP$ has negative intersection with $H$, so it has no nonzero section. Serre duality and Riemann--Roch imply
\[
 h^0(mP)\geq\chi(mP)=\tfrac12m^2P^2+O(m).
\]
Together with (7.2) this gives volume $P^2$. If $P^2=0$, the upper bound and nonnegative section counts give volume zero.

Lemma 3.3 gives section-space equality for all degrees divisible by both $L$ and $N$. To verify the upper bound for every degree clearing $L$, and not merely one subsequence, every section divides by $\lceil mN\rceil$. Choose $q$ clearing $L,N,P$ and write the permissible $m=aq+r$. Then
\[
 mL-\lceil mN\rceil=aqP+B_r,
 \qquad B_r=rL-\lceil rN\rceil,
\]
with only finitely many actual integral residual twists $B_r$. Apply (7.2) with these twists and with growing degree $aq$; $aq/m\to1$. This bounds the entire section-volume limsup of $L$ by $P^2$. The divisible section-space equality and the lower bound for $P$ give the reverse inequality when $P^2>0$; when $P^2=0$ nonnegativity suffices. This proves (7.1) without an unproved arithmetic-subsequence interchange. $\square$

For example, on the Hirzebruch surface with section $S^2=-2$, fiber $F^2=0$, and $S\cdot F=1$, the actual divisor $L=2S+3F$ has
\[
 N=\tfrac12S,\quad P=\tfrac32S+3F,\quad L^2=4,
                     \quad\operatorname{vol}(L)=P^2=\tfrac92.
\]
For $H=S+3F$ and $0<\epsilon\leq1$, $N_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)S/2$. This is an explicit section-division and perturbation diagnostic, not a finite proof of the general theorem.

The canonical trivial nef metric on $P$ has the ordinary product-model raw top product $P^2\delta_{x_0}$. Removing the fixed canonical $N$ factor from the reference metric leaves this actual moving model metric. This verifies the reference raw moving mass. Theorem 7.1 alone does **not** construct a measure $\operatorname{MA}_L(u)$ for every unbounded relative finite-energy $u$. Assigning the desired mass to an inverse-selected measure would not supply that operator.

# 8. Two explicit zero-volume equation ranges

## 8.1. Entire numerical-zero class in every positive dimension

**Theorem 8.1.** For an actual numerically trivial rational $B$ on the original $X$ of any dimension $n\geq1$,
\[
 \operatorname{PSH}(c_1(B))=\mathbb R.
                                                               \tag{8.1}
\]
With canonical reference zero, the actual entire finite-energy space is $\mathbb R$, raw energy is zero, and the genuine raw model operator is zero. Its only positive target is the zero Radon measure. All solutions are unique modulo constants.

**Proof.** Lemma 2.3 puts constants in the cone, even if $B$ is nontorsion and has no sections. Let $u$ be normalized in the cone. Integer tensor powers of its original defining net show directly that $mu$ is genuine in the zero numerical class for each positive integer $m$. Lemma 2.2 embeds these unchanged normalized functions into one actual ample $H$ cone. At a fixed divisorial $z$, the continuous finite evaluation on its compact normalized cone has a finite lower bound $-C_z$. Thus
\[
 -C_z\leq m u(z)\leq0 \qquad(m\geq1).
\]
It follows that $u(z)=0$. Ample determination for the already genuine $u$ and zero in the same ample cone gives $u=0$ at every point. This proves (8.1).

Every section of a divisible numerically trivial bundle is either zero or nowhere vanishing. Indeed a nonzero effective zero divisor would have strictly positive intersection with $H^{n-1}$ but numerical class zero. A nonzero section therefore trivializes that power, and proper integrality gives dimension at most one for its section space. Its volume is zero.

On the product model, the actual canonical metric has raw top product $(B^n)\delta_{x_0}=0$. Constants do not change it. A positive Radon measure of raw mass zero is the zero measure. The intrinsic dual energy is $\sup_c(E(c)-\int c\,d0)=0$. Comparison is the equality of two zero integrals. Every constant solves the genuine zero equation, and any two solutions differ by a constant. The normalized cone is a compact single point. For every continuous obstacle $f$, its envelope is the constant $\min f$ and its outer energy is zero; both energy derivatives are zero. No probability normalization or division by volume occurs. $\square$

## 8.2. Surface classes with numerically zero positive part

**Theorem 8.2 (flat positive-part relative equation).** For the surface domain of Theorem 5.1, assume additionally $c_1(P)=0$ in the canonical decomposition $L=P+N$. Then
\[
 \operatorname{PSH}(c_1(L))=\{\sigma_N+c:c\in\mathbb R\}.
                                                               \tag{8.2}
\]
With intrinsic reference $V_L=\sigma_N$, its entire relative finite-energy space is this set, its genuine raw nonpluripolar top product is zero, and its relative energy is zero. The sole positive target is zero; the equation is unique modulo constants. This includes nonzero pseudoeffective classes, for example the exceptional divisor on a blown-up surface.

**Proof.** Equation (8.2) is Theorem 5.1 and Theorem 8.1. It remains to specify the relative product and energy rather than silently using a signed fixed-divisor self-intersection.

Let $U=X\setminus\operatorname{Supp}N$. In the actual local frame obtained by the nonvanishing canonical $N$ section on $U$, the metric with scalar potential $\sigma_N+c$ is precisely the canonical metric of the actual $P$, shifted by $c$. The $N$ factor has been removed as an actual section, not by scalar subtraction at a pole. The product-model curvature product of this numerically trivial metric is zero on $U^{\rm an}$. Define its nonpluripolar extension to the original $X^{\rm an}$ by giving zero mass to the reference pole set $(\operatorname{Supp}N)^{\rm an}$. The result is the zero measure. This is the extension of the actual local intersection product, independent of any target and of any choice of lift. On the nef positive part, it is also the weak limit of actual ample constant products on $P+\epsilon H$, whose masses are $\epsilon^2H^2\to0$.

The relative energy difference of two translated metrics is the usual integral of their constant difference against the sum of the local positive curvature products, extended with zero polar mass. Every term is zero. Normalize $E_L(\sigma_N)=0$; thus $E_L(\sigma_N+c)=0$. This constructs the actual relative energy on the **entire** cone (8.2). The relative floors in (5.8) stay in this same cone and give no further endpoints. Its entire finite-energy space is therefore (8.2).

At the reference poles, integrals against the only target zero are interpreted on $U^{\rm an}$, with no $\infty-\infty$ convention. Intrinsic target mass is $\operatorname{vol}(L)=P^2=0$ and relative dual energy is zero. Comparison integrals vanish. Every translated reference is an actual solution and any two differ by a constant. Normalization gives one actual point, so attainment is literal. For a continuous perturbation $f$, the largest cone member below $\sigma_N+c+tf$ is $\sigma_N+c+\inf_{U^{\rm an}}tf$ (with equality at reference poles automatic). # 10. A quantitative higher-dimensional ideal calculation

The following is a genuine algebraic check of the suggested Skoda mechanism. It is not a theorem about regularizers of arbitrary non-Archimedean psh potentials.

**Lemma 10.1 (smooth-center multiplier ideal, with full proof).** Let $Y$ be smooth in characteristic zero, let $Z\subset Y$ be nonempty and smooth of codimension $c\geq1$, and let $I$ be its ideal. For real $t\geq0$,
\[
 \mathcal J(I^t)=I^{r(t)},\qquad
              r(t)=\max(0,\lfloor t\rfloor-c+1).                \tag{10.1}
\]

**Proof.** For $c\geq2$, blow up $Z$, with $\pi:\widetilde Y\to Y$ and exceptional divisor $E$. In smooth center coordinates $x_1,\ldots,x_c$, a chart has coordinates $x_1,x_2/x_1,\ldots,x_c/x_1$ and the unchanged complementary coordinates. Its Jacobian has factor $x_1^{c-1}$. Hence $K_{\widetilde Y/Y}=(c-1)E$, and $I\mathcal O_{\widetilde Y}=\mathcal O(-E)$. This is a log resolution with one smooth divisor. The defining multiplier formula gives
\[
 \mathcal J(I^t)=\pi_*\mathcal O((c-1-\lfloor t\rfloor)E).
\]
If the coefficient is nonnegative, its pushforward is $\mathcal O_Y$: a rational function with possible poles only above codimension at least two is regular on the smooth normal base, since it has no pole at a height-one point. If it is $-r<0$, the pushforward is $I^r$. To see the latter equality, a regular function has exceptional order at least $r$ exactly when its order in the regular-sequence ideal $(x_1,\ldots,x_c)$ is at least $r$. The associated graded algebra of this ideal is the polynomial algebra on $I/I^2$, so a nonzero leading homogeneous term gives exactly that exceptional order. This is an equality of ideal sheaves and is compatible with the smooth coordinate changes. For $c=1$, the ideal is already invertible and the identity resolution gives $\mathcal J(I^t)=I^{\lfloor t\rfloor}$. These are (10.1). $\square$

For integer $m\geq1$ and $\epsilon\geq0$, (10.1) implies
\[
 \mathcal J(I^{m(1+\epsilon)})\subseteq I^m
                    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                         m\epsilon\geq c-1.                    \tag{10.2}
\]
The factor $1+\epsilon$ really can absorb the codimension loss, while $\epsilon$ tends to zero as $m\to\infty$. This is an exact containment, not a pole-set statement.

**Proposition 10.2 (actual weighted sections and class cost in a specified model).** On $Y=\mathbb P^n$, let $1\leq c\leq n$, let $I=(Z_0,\ldots,Z_{c-1})$ be the ideal sheaf of the corresponding linear subspace, and fix arbitrary real weights $\lambda_i$ with $\max_i\lambda_i=0$. Define the original-carrier FS function on $\mathcal O(1)$ by
\[
 f=\max_{0\leq i<c}(\log|Z_i|+\lambda_i).
\]
For integer $m\geq1$ and $m\epsilon\geq c-1$, set
\[
 r=\lfloor m(1+\epsilon)\rfloor-c+1,\qquad
 q=\lceil m(1+\epsilon)\rceil.
\]
There is an actual degree-$q$ section system, with local base ideal exactly $I^r$, whose potential is
\[
 \psi_{m,\epsilon}=\frac rm f
   \in\operatorname{FS}^{\rm gf}\bigl(\mathcal O(q/m)\bigr),
 \qquad \psi_{m,\epsilon}\leq f\text{ at every point}.           \tag{10.3}
\]
The class cost is $q/m-1\leq\epsilon+1/m$. All weights and normalized divisorial slopes are retained explicitly.

**Proof.** The sheaf $I^r(r)$ is generated by all degree-$r$ monomials in $Z_0,\ldots,Z_{c-1}$. Give each its sum of the $\lambda_i$ weights, with multiplicity. Tensor with the weight-zero generating system of $\mathcal O(q-r)$ consisting of all degree-$q-r$ monomials in all coordinates. The latter has zero potential by Lemma 2.1. This is a global generation statement proved by the displayed sections, without any vanishing theorem. Their normalized maximum is exactly $(r/m)f$, and their local ideal is $I^r$ since the residual system generates. Since $r/m\geq1$ and $f\leq0$, their all-point comparison with $f$ holds. At its poles both sides are $-\infty$. The ceiling gives the class bound. Along every scaled divisorial valuation the recession slope of the actual system is exactly $r/m$ times the corresponding minimum generator order. The real weights are handled by the actual sums in the monomials, not by rounding them into an unweighted ideal. $\square$

For $\epsilon_m=(c-1)/m$, the construction has $r=m$, $q=m+c-1$, and $\psi_{m,\epsilon_m}=f$ with classes tending to $c_1(\mathcal O(1))$. This establishes the geometry, generation, class estimate, and shared-pole order for this **fixed linear-ideal model**. The unweighted multiplier identity is (10.1); we do not identify it with a yet undefined weighted multiplier ideal for an arbitrary function $u$.

For comparison, on $\mathbb P^1$ put $\tau=\log|s|$ and $f_j=\max(\tau,-j)$. At $a\operatorname{ord}_{s=0}$, the attempted correction $f_j+\delta\tau+C$, $0<\delta<1$, has value $-j-\delta a+C$ for $a\geq j$. It is at most $\tau=-a$ only if $(1-\delta)a\leq j-C$, which fails at large $a$. Proposition 10.2 preserves at least the full required order; a small coefficient of the pole does not.
