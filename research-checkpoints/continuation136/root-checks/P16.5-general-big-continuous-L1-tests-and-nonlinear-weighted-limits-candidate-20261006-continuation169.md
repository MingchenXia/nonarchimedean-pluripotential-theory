# General big classes: continuous L1 approximation and nonlinear weighted limits

This unreviewed candidate strengthens own166's scalar identification to continuous approximation in L1 along each weakly converging bounded-cost family of MODEL outputs. It gives genuine relative weighted weak convergence and bounded Lipschitz functions of several relative potentials. This is the prerequisite for a strict-set comparison passage; weak convergence is never applied directly to an indicator. Original P16.5 remains open.

## 1. Retained data and exact approximation assertion

Keep the arbitrary-cardinality trivially valued algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, smooth integral projective original X, actual big rational L and genuine original E1(theta),E_theta,M_theta,V_theta,V=vol(L)>0 of own143--168. Let

    mu_j=M_theta(w_j) ->nu

weakly on the whole original C0, where the w_j are normalized MODEL envelopes with B(w_j)<=B_0 and nu is an intrinsic finite-dual target. For each fixed genuine E1 test v and epsilon>0, we propose an ORIGINAL continuous h with

    limsup_j integral |(v-V_theta)-h| dmu_j <epsilon,
              integral |(v-V_theta)-h| dnu <epsilon.  (1.1)

Relative differences are defined off the common null polar sets; their values there do not affect these integrals. All integrals are finite by own161/162/158. The assertion concerns the specified converging family, not a uniform approximation over all bounded-cost outputs without a prescribed weak limit.

## 2. Model tests: absolute errors in the two capped envelopes

First let v=P_theta f be a normalized model test, with f<=0, f(v_triv)=0, M=-min f. Use own166's complete section functions phi_m,f,phi_m,0, and for K>M+1 set

    F_m,K=max(phi_m,f,-K),       G_m,K=max(phi_m,0,-K),
    z_m,K=P_theta F_m,K,       y_m,K=P_theta G_m,K,
    d_m,K=z_m,K-y_m,K,        h_m,K=F_m,K-G_m,K.

With K fixed before m tends factorially to infinity, z_m,K<=v and y_m,K<=V_theta, their energies tend to E_theta(v) and0, and their generic constants tend to0. The actual obstacle difference h_m,K is original C0. Own164/166 prove I(z_m,K,v)->0 and I(y_m,K,V_theta)->0, with bounded normalized costs.

The needed strengthening of the scalar argument is elementary. Because each of the TWO component errors is nonnegative,

    |(v-V_theta)-d_m,K|
        <=(v-z_m,K)+(V_theta-y_m,K).                 (2.1)

The model four-point estimate bounds the integral of v-z_m,K uniformly over all the MODEL output bases of cost B_0. Its generic difference is retained as V times that constant; the remaining measure difference subtracts M_theta(V_theta), and the estimate is O(I(z_m,K,v)^q), q=2^(-n), on the bounded-cost tail. Thus this nonnegative integral tends to zero uniformly over those bases. The same proof applies to V_theta-y_m,K. Equivalently, apply the precise ordered estimates underlying own166 Section2 separately before subtracting the two tests. Hence the L1 error in (2.1) tends to zero UNIFORMLY over the mu_j family, for fixed K.

For the FIXED intrinsic nu, strong relative integration own160 applies separately to z_m,K->v and y_m,K->V_theta, including their vanishing generic constants. Their ordered gaps have integrals tending to zero, so (2.1) tends to zero in L1(nu) too.

## 3. The continuous test and the fixed closed collar

Fix a nonempty complete degree ell with proper base-ideal pole set Z_ell. For m divisible by ell, own166 proves

    d_m,K=h_m,K outside C_K,
    |d_m,K-h_m,K|<=2(M+1) 1_CK,
    C_K={phi_ell,0<=-K+M+1}.                        (3.1)

The set C_K is closed in the original compact carrier, decreases countably to Z_ell, and nu(Z_ell)=0 by full polar nullness own158. Thus nu(C_K)->0. Original closed-set Portmanteau gives limsup_j mu_j(C_K)<=nu(C_K) for each FIXED K.

Given epsilon, choose K first so that the two collar errors are small. Then keep K fixed and choose m divisible by ell so that the two absolute component errors in Section2 are small. The resulting fixed continuous h=h_m,K satisfies (1.1), by (2.1)--(3.1). The measure index tends last. No merely weak limit of a moving unbounded function has been integrated, and no energy-finite status is assigned to the uncapped original section functions.

Real fixed-model coefficients follow by uniform obstacle approximation. The envelope sup-norm bound gives the corresponding absolute integral errors bounded by V times that approximation tolerance.

## 4. Full genuine E1 tests

For a normalized arbitrary genuine v, take its cofinal normalized model envelopes v_i decreasing to v. Own166(5.1), with order retained, gives

    sup_(model w,B(w)<=B0) integral(v_i-v)dM_theta(w)
        <=C_(n,B0,B(v))[E_theta(v_i)-E_theta(v)]^q.   (4.1)

The gap is nonnegative, so this is an L1 bound, not just a signed scalar error. Against nu, own160 gives integral(v_i-v)dnu->0. Choose one fixed model v_i making both errors small, and apply Sections2--3 to that model. The triangle inequality proves (1.1) for every full v. Adding constants gives the unnormalized statement as well.

## 5. Relative weighted measures and Lipschitz functions

Let g be any original continuous test. Use (1.1), multiply the two errors by ||g||, and pass the FIXED continuous product gh through mu_j->nu. Then let the tolerance tend to zero. This proves actual signed relative weighted weak convergence

    (v-V_theta) mu_j -> (v-V_theta) nu               (5.1)

against all original C0. Absolute integrability of V_theta is neither asserted nor needed.

More generally fix finitely many full E1 tests a_1,...,a_p and a bounded Lipschitz function Phi on R^p, using the sum norm for its Lipschitz constant. Apply (1.1) to each a_l-V_theta, with a sufficiently small common tolerance, to obtain continuous h_l. The Lipschitz inequality bounds the integral difference between Phi(a_l-V_theta) and Phi(h_l) by the sum of the L1 errors. The product g Phi(h_1,...,h_p) is original C0. Therefore

    Phi(a_1-V_theta,...,a_p-V_theta) mu_j
       ->Phi(a_1-V_theta,...,a_p-V_theta) nu          (5.2)

weakly on every original continuous g. In particular every bounded Lipschitz function of a-b has this convergence, because a-b=(a-V_theta)-(b-V_theta). This bounded nonlinear statement does not presume a new psh property of Phi.

If a_l,i are normalized cofinal MODEL majorants decreasing to a_l, their nonnegative relative gaps have integrals tending to zero UNIFORMLY over every bounded-cost model output family, by (4.1). The Lipschitz bound therefore permits these moving tests in (5.2). A common product directed set retains the original arbitrary nets. This is the precise moving-test case needed for comparison; a general Borel function of moving endpoints is not claimed.

## 6. Scope

The argument only strengthens the explicitly identified scalar estimates of own166 by retaining their ordered absolute errors. It uses the original closed polar collars, continuous tests and intrinsic null sets already constructed there, and the exact model numerical bounds of own164. It proves no comparison inequality by itself and constructs no mixed nonnef product.
