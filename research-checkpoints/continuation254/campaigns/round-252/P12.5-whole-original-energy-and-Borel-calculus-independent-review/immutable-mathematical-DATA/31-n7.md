# n7. Primary energy invariance, full conductor descent, and dimensions one and zero
Supports: G331, B341, n1, n4, n5, n6.

For another proper primary $\mathfrak b$, put $d(v)=v_{\mathfrak a}(\mathfrak b)$ and $T(v)=v/d(v)$. On compact $X_{\mathfrak a}$, $0<d_{\min}\le d\le d_{\max}<\infty$, and $u_{\mathfrak b}(Tv)=u_{\mathfrak a}(v)/d(v)$. G331 transports actual $C,D$ representations. B341 gives for every bounded tuple
$$\mu_{\mathfrak b}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak b})=T_*[d\,\mu_{\mathfrak a}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak a})].\tag{29}$$
Use the actual bounded-test pairing of n4, including discontinuous inputs, to cancel $d$ against the last transformed potential. This proves invariance of the full bounded $n$-form, not just ideal moments.

For an unbounded $u$, pulling its $\mathfrak b$-truncation back with its homogeneous factor gives $w_k=\max(u_{\mathfrak a},-kd)$. It is bounded $D$ by finite maxima with the primary profile $-k v_{\mathfrak a}(\mathfrak b)$, and
$$\max(u_{\mathfrak a},-k d_{\max})\le w_k\le\max(u_{\mathfrak a},-k d_{\min}).\tag{30}$$
The two constant cutoff levels are cofinal. Bounded form invariance and scalar order therefore prove $F_{\infty,\mathfrak b}(u_{\mathfrak b}^{[n]})=F_{\infty,\mathfrak a}(u_{\mathfrak a}^{[n]})$, including infinite values. Thus $E^1$ is primary-gauge independent. For an $E^1$ tuple, $(w_{i,k})$ are individually bounded decreasing approximants to the original tuple. Apply (21),(22) and test the weak limits in (29) with the fixed continuous function $d$ times a transported continuous test. We obtain
$$F_{\infty,\mathfrak b}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak b})=F_{\infty,\mathfrak a}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak a}),\quad E_{\mathfrak b}(u_{\mathfrak b})=E_{\mathfrak a}(u_{\mathfrak a}),\quad\mu_{E,\mathfrak b}=T_*[d\,\mu_{E,\mathfrak a}].\tag{31}$$
There is exactly **one** measure factor, not $d^n$, $d^{n-1}$ or an inverse factor. For $\mathfrak b=\mathfrak a^r$, it is $r$, while potentials divide by $r$. G331's homogeneous cocycle makes (31) compose exactly for three primary gauges. A finite reference $w$ is transported along with $u$, so (27) and its cocycle are invariant as well.

Let $S$ normalize $R$ finitely, with all local normal centres $S_q$ over $\mathfrak m$. Write $f_q=[\kappa(q):\kappa(\mathfrak m)]$ and $\rho_q$ for the full restriction map of their sections with gauge $\mathfrak aS_q$. These are total residue degrees, including inseparable ones. B341's bounded top-degree identity, paired with a bounded $D$ test using n4, gives the full bounded $n$-form identity. For an extended $D$ tuple, constant truncations pull back exactly to constant truncations on every centre. There are finitely many centres and all forms are nonnegative; finite sums commute with increasing scalar suprema. Therefore, with extended values allowed,
$$F_{\infty,R}(\boldsymbol u)=\sum_{\dim S_q=n}f_q F_{\infty,S_q}(\rho_q^*\boldsymbol u),\qquad E_R(u)=\sum_{\dim S_q=n}f_q E_{S_q}(\rho_q^*u).\tag{32}$$
This is top-degree normalization, not a general finite-map trace. Algebraically its weight is justified by finite Artinian factorization: each length-one $S_q$ residue factor has $R$-length $f_q$, while normalization is generically the identity on every reduced component field. Smaller-dimensional factors have no degree-$n$ coefficient. In particular no inseparability is dropped and no vertical generic degree is fabricated. Every smaller-dimensional centre is still part of the function and conductor domain, with zero contribution in this convention.

For $E^1$ inputs, (32) proves that all top-dimensional pullbacks are $E^1$ and that this condition is equivalent to downstairs finite energy. Pass their bounded measures to the weak limits, a finite sum with fixed continuous restriction maps, to obtain
$$\mu_{E,R}(\boldsymbol u)=\sum_{\dim S_q=n}f_q\rho_{q,*}\mu_{E,S_q}(\rho_q^*\boldsymbol u).\tag{33}$$
G331 already proves full $D$ positivity descent: an upstairs tuple of $D$ functions descends exactly when constant on **every** restriction fibre, including conductor-infinite and nonminimal supports, all-zero, mixed-zero and partial-bottom cases. Combining this with (32) gives the exact energy descent statement: fibre-constant upstairs $D$ data descend to $E^1_R$ if and only if their top-dimensional entries have finite diagonal energy. No own lower-dimensional finite-energy condition is imposed; all lower-dimensional data still satisfy full fibre compatibility. At finite conductor value fibres are singletons; at infinite value equality on the conductor quotient is precisely the identification. Formula (33) adds masses on identified fibres. It asserts nothing about automatically tracing incompatible arbitrary upstairs tuples.

For every excellent reduced curve, B341's independent Cartier reference measure is
$$\mu_{\mathfrak a}=\sum_q f_q a_q\delta_{\operatorname{ord}_q/a_q},\qquad a_q=\operatorname{ord}_q(\mathfrak a)>0.$$
G331's actual $D$ is all extended nonpositive vectors on the finite branch set. Consequently $E^1$ is exactly the finite vectors, and
$$E(u)=\sum_q f_q a_q u(q),\qquad dE_u(v)=\sum_q f_q a_q v(q).\tag{34}$$
Every coefficient is strictly positive, so a single curve-branch pole has infinite energy. This is actual zero-arity Cartier energy on all primary curve models, with nonnormal and inseparable residue cases included, not an ideal-cone analogy. For example $R=k+t\ell[[t]]\subset\ell[[t]]$, $[\ell:k]=d$, has generic normalization degree one, residue degree $d$, and maximal-ideal reference mass $d$. Its primary ideal $t^r\ell[[t]]$ has multiplicity $dr$, so replacing the residue factor by a generic-degree-only factor is wrong, including for inseparable extensions. Gauge weights and profiles cancel exactly in (34). On a smooth DVR the maximal-ideal mass is one; a primary gauge $(t^r)$ has mass $r$ on its rescaled singleton. In dimension zero the reduction is a field, its centred section is empty, $E=0$ and the unique measure is zero. Nilpotents require the explicit reduction convention in n1: $k[[t]]$ and $k[[t]][\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ have the same reduced section and $(t)$ gauge but unreduced multiplicities one and two; no boundary-only reconstruction can distinguish those lengths.
