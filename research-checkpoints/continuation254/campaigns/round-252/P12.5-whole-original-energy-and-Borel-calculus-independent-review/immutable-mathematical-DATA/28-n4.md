# n4. Bounded full form, directed integration, and the mixed inequalities
Supports: G331, B341, n1, n2, n3.

For rational primary-or-unit profiles $u_i=-v(I_i)/q_i$ define
$$F(u_1,\ldots,u_n)=e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n)/(q_1\cdots q_n).\tag{12}$$
B341's kernel makes this independent of all presentations, symmetric, additive on the negative cone and nonnegative, with order reversed relative to the functions in **all $n$ slots**. For $u_i\in[-M,0]$, its bound is $M^n m_{\mathfrak a}$. Negative-constant shifts give the error bound $[(M+\varepsilon)^n-M^n]m_{\mathfrak a}$ for uniformly close rational tuples. Thus uniform approximation defines the same symmetric form on $C$, with all these identities for real scalars.

For bounded $D$ tuples choose actual decreasing $C$ representations in all $n$ entries. Their scalar forms increase, bounded as above. Their supremum is representation independent: for any fixed continuous majorant $h_i\ge u_i$, $\{g_{i,\alpha}<h_i+\varepsilon\}$ is a directed open cover; compactness selects a common eventual index in each of the finitely many slots. Then $g_{i,\alpha}-\varepsilon\le h_i$, so order and the shift error compare $F(\boldsymbol h)$ to the representation supremum. Reverse the two representations and let $\varepsilon\to0$. This also proves order for arbitrary bounded $D$ entries and
$$F(\boldsymbol u)=\sup\{F(\boldsymbol h):h_i\in C,\ h_i\ge u_i\}.\tag{13}$$
Addition and homogeneity use actual product-index $C$ representations of sums and scalar multiples; their finite scalar limits commute with finite sums. If bounded $D$ nets decrease to a specified bounded $D$ tuple with a common bound, their USC strict-test open covers compared to each fixed $C$ majorant prove convergence of the forms using the same error. No new membership of an infimum is asserted. For continuous $v\in C$, ideal approximation and B341's bounded weak continuity prove
$$F(v,u_1,\ldots,u_p)=\int(-v)\,d\mu(u_1,\ldots,u_p).\tag{14}$$

The following measure lemma justifies an actual discontinuous test. Let $f_\alpha$ be a directed increasing family of nonnegative continuous functions on compact Hausdorff $X$ and $f=\sup f_\alpha$, possibly infinite. For finite positive Radon $\sigma$,
$$\int f\,d\sigma=\sup_\alpha\int f_\alpha\,d\sigma.\tag{15}$$
Here is a proof including the regularity step. For any nonnegative continuous $h\le f$, the open sets $\{f_\alpha>h-\varepsilon\}$ cover $X$; directed compactness gives one index and thus $\int f_\alpha\ge\int h-\varepsilon\sigma(X)$. Also $\int f$ is the supremum of these continuous-minorant integrals: first truncate $f$ at a finite level and partition its range into finitely many steps. Each open superlevel set admits compact subsets whose measure approximates it from below. Urysohn functions equal to one on these compacts and supported inside the superlevel sets, multiplied by the step increments and summed, give a continuous nonnegative minorant with integral approximating the step integral. For increments of size $\eta$ and levels $j\eta$, the sum $\eta\sum_j h_j$ is at most $f$ pointwise, because nonzero $h_j$ requires $f>j\eta$; the approximation error is at most $\eta\sigma(X)$ plus the finitely many chosen compact errors. Let errors and step size vanish and then the truncation level grow. Ordinary sequence integration applies to the finite-level scalar approximations. This proves the assertion and (15). It is not monotone convergence for arbitrary measurable nets, which would be false.

If bounded $v\in D$ has an actual decreasing $C$ representation $g_\alpha$, apply (15) to $-g_\alpha\uparrow-v$ against the fixed measure in (14). For each fixed $g_\alpha$, (14) gives its integral. Commuting its supremum with the independent potential-representation suprema proves (14) for this discontinuous bounded $D$ test. Directly testing weak convergence by $v$ would not justify it.

We now prove conditional Cauchy for arbitrary fixed mixed entries. The fresh published input is the complete immediate proof of SH17.7.1, valid for every two-dimensional Noetherian local ring and finite two-dimensional module. Its argument is as follows. Pass to $R(z)$ if necessary, preserving dimension and all finite lengths by composition series. Choose a two-generated reduction $(a,b)$ of the primary ideal $I$. For positive integers $r,s,N$, the quotient length estimate
$$\ell(M/I^{rN}J^{sN}M)\le2\ell(M/J^{sN}M)+\ell(M/(a^{rN},b^{rN})M)$$
follows by replacing $I^{rN}$ by $(a^{rN},b^{rN})$ and mapping two copies of $M/J^{sN}M$ onto the remaining subquotient. Divide by $N^2/2$. Lech's parameter asymptotic formula and reduction invariance give
$$e(I^rJ^s;M)\le2e(I;M)r^2+2e(J;M)s^2.$$
The mixed polynomial expands its left side as $e(I)r^2+2e(I,J)rs+e(J)s^2$. Optimizing rational $r/s$ yields $e(I,J)^2\le e(I)e(J)$, since mixed multiplicity is nonnegative (and positive for primary slots). The complete SH17.7.2--3 statements/proofs were checked as context; the arbitrary fixed-slot result below is stronger than simply citing their two-ideal diagonal sequence.

Here is generalized $n$-entry Holder, with the finite-simplex argument included. For finitely many proper ideal types consider counts $\alpha_i\ge0$, $\sum\alpha_i=n$, and subtract $\sum\alpha_i\log F(u_i^{[n]})/n$ from $\log F(u^{\boldsymbol\alpha})$. Call this $f(\alpha)$; primary positivity makes each logarithm finite and $f=0$ at the vertices. By (16), for positive $\alpha_i,\alpha_j$,
$$2f(\alpha)\le f(\alpha+e_i-e_j)+f(\alpha-e_i+e_j).$$
If its finite global maximum were positive, choose a maximizer with the smallest support. Both displayed neighbours must be maximizers. Repeatedly transfer one unit from $j$ to $i$ until $j$ vanishes; every intermediate point remains a maximizer by the same inequality. This contradicts minimal support. Consequently $f\le0$. Perturbing zero entries by $-\varepsilon$, then taking uniform and bounded decreasing limits, proves
$$F(u_1,\ldots,u_n)\le\prod_i A(u_i),\qquad A(u)=F(u^{[n]})^{1/n}.\tag{17}$$
Expanding $F((u+w)^{[n]})$ and applying (17) termwise gives $A(u+w)\le A(u)+A(w)$. For a signed cone difference $z=U-V$, (16) implies
$$F(z,z,K)=F(U,U,K)+F(V,V,K)-2F(U,V,K)\ge0.$$
Each direction in a cone segment is such a difference. Thus $E_{\mathrm{bd}}(u)=-F(u^{[n]})/n$ is concave: its second derivative is $-(n-1)F(z,z,u_t^{[n-2]})\le0$; for $n=1$ it is linear. No strictness or separation follows in nonpure rings.
