\section{n1. Domain, imports, and the theorem's
conventions}\label{n1.-domain-imports-and-the-theorems-conventions}

Supports: G331, B341.

This is a mathematical draft, not an acceptance or a formal certificate.
Only the ENTIRE qualified G331 and B341 are imported results. Their
unchanged full bodies, mandatory annotations, ledgers, exact scopes and
the SH17.4.7 colon qualification accompany this paper verbatim. The
supplied ROOT342 and ROOT344 are unreviewed data: the new arguments
below are not conditional on accepting either draft. No private active
campaign was accessed.

Let \((R,\mathfrak m)\) be excellent local. \textbf{Throughout replace
\(R\) by \(R_{\mathrm{red}}\), explicitly}, and use the image of each
gauge there. This is a theory of its reduced semivaluation boundary, not
a reconstruction of unreduced generic lengths. Put \(n=\dim R\). When
\(n>0\), fix a proper \(\mathfrak m\)-primary ideal \(\mathfrak a\) and
the compact Hausdorff section
\[X_{\mathfrak a}=\{v:v(\mathfrak a)=1,\ 0<v(\mathfrak m)<\infty\}.\]
All prime supports strictly below \(\mathfrak m\) are included.
Functions are homogeneous of degree one on the centred rays. G331
supplies actual evaluation of exceptional Cartier divisors using the
valued field of the support quotient, and supplies the independent
geometric cone: actual negative effective finite real Cartier
presentations, curve-nef on actual primary blowups. It proves its
continuous uniform class \(C\) equals the ideal uniform class, and its
actual decreasing-net completion \(D\) equals the geometric completion.
We use its addition, nonnegative scaling (with \(0u=0\)), finite maxima,
upper semicontinuity, primary-gauge cocycle, rational primary-or-unit
profile density, full normalization-fibre descent, and density of the
rational difference lattice of ideal gauges in
\(C(X_{\mathfrak a},\mathbb R)\). No arbitrary infimum or
increasing-limit closure is imported.

B341 supplies \textbf{only} the bounded algebraic moment measures with
\(p=n-1\) potential slots, full all-field mixed kernel,
symmetry/product/power/integral-closure laws, scalar ideal-moment order,
real uniform continuity, bounded specified-limit decreasing-net
continuity, one-factor gauge and top-degree residue-weighted
normalization. It supplies actual curve zero-arity Cartier geometry and
the bounded BFJ comparison in its precise published range. It supplies
no higher-dimensional geometric comparison, locality, energy, or
arbitrary finite-map theorem; n2--n3 provide the new geometric work.

Use raw degree-\(n\) length coefficients, without dividing by
\(e_R(\mathfrak a)\). In a nonpure ring all smaller-dimensional
components and all their supports remain in \(X\) and \(D\), but have
zero contribution to this top-degree convention. Their own lower-arity
energies would define additional conditions; they are not silently
added. Put \(m_{\mathfrak a}=e_R(\mathfrak a)>0\) and
\(\delta_{\mathfrak a}=m_{\mathfrak a}^{1/n}\). The additive energy
anchor is the zero homogeneous potential. The constant \(-1\) on this
section is the homogeneous profile \(-v(\mathfrak a)\); its reference
mixed measure has raw mass \(m_{\mathfrak a}\). A different gauge
changes that homogeneous reference profile; it does not change the
anchor zero.

The theorem constructed below is: a canonical energy
\(E:D\to[-\infty,0]\), its cone \(E^1=\{E> -\infty\}\), finite positive
Radon mixed measures on \((E^1)^{n-1}\), actual Cartier comparison on
geometric cores, ordinary open locality, mixed integrability and
polynomial variation, and continuity for every decreasing net with a
specified \(D\) and \(E^1\) limit. Energy is invariant under primary
changes, measures have exactly one gauge factor, and
normalization/conductor descent uses every fibre and actual residue
degrees. The smooth specialization is precisely the
\textbf{finite-self-intersection extension of BFJ Definition 4.3},
proved in n8, rather than an assertion about a differently defined
analytic or projective energy space. For \(n=1\) this is actual
reduced-curve linear Cartier energy. For \(n=0\) set \(E=0\) on the
unique empty function and use the zero measure; no negative arity or
division by \(n\) is made.

\section{n2. Independent supported Cartier
intersection}\label{n2.-independent-supported-cartier-intersection}

Supports: G331, B341, n1.

Write \(Y=\operatorname{Bl}_I R\) for an actual primary blowup. It is
reduced, projective, is the identity away from \(\mathfrak m\), and
every component dominates its reduced component of \(R\). Its effective
exceptional Cartier divisor \(E_I\) has
\(\mathcal O_Y(-E_I)=\mathcal O_Y(1)\) ample. These facts follow also
from whole Stacks31.33: its charts are \(R[I/f]\subset R_f\), and \(f\)
is regular on its chart. Common product-ideal blowups dominate any
finite collection. No arbitrary proper-model cofinality is needed.

For an effective exceptional integral Cartier divisor \(E\), a power of
\(\mathfrak m\) kills \(\mathcal O_E\): coherence and a finite affine
cover give a common power from closed-fibre support. Its coherent
cohomology has finite \(R\)-length by Stacks30.16. Define
\[\chi_R(E,\mathcal F)=\sum_j(-1)^j\ell_R H^j(E,\mathcal F).\] For line
bundles \(L_i\) on \(Y\), the function
\(\chi_R(E,\mathcal O_E\otimes\bigotimes_i L_i^{t_i})\) is a polynomial
of total degree at most \(p=n-1\). To prove this in mixed
characteristic, filter \(\mathcal O_E\) by the finitely many powers of
\(\mathfrak m\). Each successive coherent quotient lives on the proper
residue-field scheme defined by \(\mathfrak m\mathcal O_E\). Tensoring
by a line bundle is exact; Euler additivity and Stacks33.45.1 give a
finite sum of field-scheme polynomials. Supports have dimension at most
\(p\): on each integral component an exceptional regular equation cuts a
divisor, and the excellent catenary dimension formula identifies its
fibre dimension. No coefficient field is chosen and no nilpotent
thickness is discarded.

For integral exceptional \(F_1,\ldots,F_p\), define
\(Q_Y(F_1,\ldots,F_p,E)\) as the coefficient of \(t_1\cdots t_p\) in
this polynomial with \(L_i=\mathcal O(F_i)\). For \(p=0\) it is the
finite zero-dimensional length. If \(Z\) runs over integral reduced
dimension-\(p\) components of \(E\), Stacks33.45.2, applied to each
filtration quotient, gives
\[Q_Y(F_1,\ldots,F_p,E)=\sum_Z\ell_{\mathcal O_{Y,\eta_Z}}(\mathcal O_{E,\eta_Z})(\mathcal O(F_1)|_Z\cdots\mathcal O(F_p)|_Z\cdot Z).\tag{1}\]
Indeed localized composition lengths of the filtration add to the
indicated length; the residue fields of the thickened and reduced local
schemes are the same. Lower-dimensional supports give smaller polynomial
degree. A fixed extra line-bundle twist cannot change the leading
coefficient, since it shifts a polynomial's arguments by constants.

Polynomial substitution proves additivity in the first slots and their
symmetry. The exact sequence
\[0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{E_1}(-E_2)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{E_1+E_2}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{E_2}\longrightarrow0\]
and fixed-twist invariance prove additivity in the effective last slot.
Every exceptional integral \(F\) is a difference of effective ones, by
G331's actual Cartier order unit \(E_I\). This extends the last slot
independently of decomposition, and then all slots extend to finite real
presentations.

Full symmetry requires an actual supported calculation. For effective
\(E,F\), their two-term locally free Cartier complexes
\([\mathcal O(-E)\to\mathcal O]\) and \([\mathcal O(-F)\to\mathcal O]\)
have isomorphic tensor total complexes after signed interchange. Their
cohomology is supported on \(E\cap F\), hence over an Artinian base and
of finite cohomological length. Computing its Euler characteristic in
the two orders, with any invertible \(L\), proves
\[\chi_R(E,L|_E)-\chi_R(E,L(-F)|_E)=\chi_R(F,L|_F)-\chi_R(F,L(-E)|_F).\tag{2}\]
This uses the locally free resolution of each Cartier divisor and Euler
additivity; it does not require one divisor to be regular on the other.
Take
\(L=\mathcal O(F)^t\mathcal O(E)^s\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n-2}\mathcal O(G_j)^{r_j}\).
Set \(t=s=0\) after taking the difference, and extract
\(r_1\cdots r_{n-2}\). The degree bound \(n-1\) leaves only
\(t r_1\cdots r_{n-2}\) on the left, respectively
\(s r_1\cdots r_{n-2}\) on the right; higher powers cannot contribute.
This exchanges \(F\) and \(E\). Effective differences and real linearity
prove full \(n\)-slot symmetry. For \(n=1\) no exchange is necessary.

Here is the precise projection argument. For \(\operatorname{Spec}R\)
use the dimension function \(\delta(\mathfrak q)=\dim R/\mathfrak q\);
catenarity in a local ring makes its drop across an immediate
specialization exactly one, also for nonpure \(R\). Extend by residue
transcendence degree to finite-type models. Excellence supplies
universal catenarity, so Stacks Situation42.7.1 and Lemma42.26.3 apply.
For a proper map between primary models, or their finite normalization,
corresponding top integral components map birationally, with
function-field degree one. At a generic dimension-\(p\) divisor point
the local ring is reduced one-dimensional. Its injection into the finite
sum of its minimal-component domain quotients has finite-length
cokernel. For multiplication by a regular local Cartier equation, the
cokernel's multiplication kernel and quotient have equal length.
Consequently the Cartier divisor cycle is the sum of its component
divisor cycles, with the top component generic weights one.
Stacks42.26.3 therefore yields the \textbf{actual cycle} identity
\[f_*[f^*E]_p=[E]_p.\tag{3}\] Smaller-dimensional components cut only
smaller-dimensional cycles. Stacks33.45.7 now applies to the proper
residue-field schemes in (1); components with smaller-dimensional image
have zero intersection against \(p\) pulled-back bundles. Thus
\[Q_{Y'}(f^*F_1,\ldots,f^*F_n)=Q_Y(F_1,\ldots,F_n).\tag{4}\] This is
supported degree, not a degree on global \(\mathrm{CH}_0(Y)\). For a DVR
the principal divisor \((t)\) has supported degree one although its
global Chow class is zero. Formula (3) preserves exactly that degree.
Residue extensions, inseparable as well as separable, remain in the
field-scheme numerical degrees.

For a primary \(I\), apply whole Stacks30.14.3 to the standard Rees
algebra \(A=\bigoplus I^q\) and \(M=A\). It gives, as actual submodules
of global rational sections, \(H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(-qE_I))=I^q\) for
large \(q\). Stacks30.16.1(4) gives higher-cohomology vanishing. The
Cartier exact sequence consequently gives
\[\chi_R(E_I,\mathcal O_{E_I}(-(q-1)E_I))=\ell_R(I^{q-1}/I^q).\] Their
degree-\(p\) leading coefficients are \(Q_Y((-E_I)^{[p]},E_I)/p!\) and
\(e_R(I)/p!\). Hence \(-Q_Y((-E_I)^{[n]})=e_R(I)\). On a common product
blowup put \(D_{I_j}=-E_{I_j}\). For positive integers \(t_j\) the
product ideal has divisor \(\sum t_jD_{I_j}\), so diagonal comparison
and (4) give an identity of degree-\(n\) polynomials. B341's length
polynomial shows that the coefficient of \(t_1\cdots t_n\) in its
multiplicity is \(n!e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n)\). The Cartier coefficient is
\(-n!Q_Y(D_{I_1},\ldots,D_{I_n})\). Therefore
\[-Q_Y(D_{I_1},\ldots,D_{I_n})=e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n).\tag{5}\] Units give
the zero divisor and zero coefficient. No comparison of coefficients of
an inequality was used; no CM or infinite-field hypothesis entered this
geometric argument.

\section{n3. Positive geometric core measure, exact bridge, and bounded
locality}\label{n3.-positive-geometric-core-measure-exact-bridge-and-bounded-locality}

Supports: G331, B341, n1, n2.

Normalize a primary model \(Y\) finitely to \(N\), retaining every top
normal component. For its exceptional integral prime divisors \(Z\) of
dimension \(p\), set \(b_Z=\operatorname{ord}_Z(\mathfrak a)>0\) and
\(v_Z=\operatorname{ord}_Z/b_Z\) on \(R\), with that component's prime
support. For actual negative effective real curve-nef core divisors
\(D_i\), \textbf{define geometrically first}
\[\mu_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_p)=\sum_Z b_Z(\mathcal O(D_1)|_Z\cdots\mathcal O(D_p)|_Z\cdot Z)\delta_{v_Z}.\tag{6}\]
For \(p=0\) the parenthesis is the residue-field degree of the point.
There are finitely many exceptional primes. The weights are nonnegative:
the restricted bundles are curve-nef on the projective integral
residue-field scheme \(Z\). On the \textbf{original actual primary model
\(Y\)}, G331's correctly qualified real-nef-plus-ample and finite-span
rationalization produce negative rational ample \(L_{i,j}\to D_i\) in
Cartier coefficient space. Their finite normalization pullbacks are
ample by Stacks29.38.7(2), since a finite map is affine and thus
quasi-affine; restrictions to the closed \(Z\) remain ample. Clear
denominators and apply Stacks33.45.9. Polynomial continuity on this
finite span yields nonnegative weights at the limit. Thus no new Keeler
theorem on an ungranted model or nonnormal Weil substitute is assumed,
and no ideal-moment positivity defines the weights.

An explicit refinement lemma is needed before testing on larger models.
A proper birational map of normal top components carries the
dimension-\(p\) exceptional primes either to smaller-dimensional strata,
whose pulled-back \(p\)-bundle intersection is zero by33.45.7, or to an
exceptional prime \(Z\). In the latter case localize the base at
\(\eta_Z\), a DVR. Apply (3) to a uniformizer on a neighbourhood: the
sum of positive integer orders times residue degrees over \(Z\) is one.
Thus there is exactly one dominating prime, its order and residue degree
are one, and its DVR valuation equals that of \(Z\). To see the last
assertion directly, both DVRs have the same fraction field and the
second dominates the first. Writing every field element as a uniformizer
power times a unit in the first forces identical orders. The numerical
projection formula preserves its intersection weight and \(b_Z\).
Normalizations of common primary refinements map to \(N\) by the
normalization universal property, componentwise. Therefore (6) is
unchanged as a measure on the full boundary under every primary
refinement. This fills the refinement step before any algebraic
identification.

For an exceptional Cartier test \(F\) determined on the same model,
\(H_F(v_Z)=\operatorname{ord}_Z(F)/b_Z\). The normal divisor cycle and
(1),(4) give
\[\int H_F\,d\mu_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_p)=Q_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_p,F).\tag{7}\] For
tests determined only on a common larger primary model, the refinement
lemma reduces to this formula there.

We now prove the bridge for real cores, rather than infer intersection
convergence from function convergence. On a fixed common \(Y\), let
\(T=H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\). G331 supplies a fixed \(h\) with
\(\mathfrak m^h(T/R)=0\), and the \textbf{primary}
\(\mathfrak c_0=\mathfrak m^{h+1}\) satisfies
\(\mathfrak c_0T\subset\mathfrak m\) as elements of \(R\). For a
negative rational ample exceptional \(L\), sufficiently divisible large
\(q\) makes \(\mathcal O_Y(qL)\) globally generated. Put
\(J_q=\mathfrak c_0H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(qL))\subset R\). This is a proper
ideal, unit off the closed point by localization of sections, hence
primary. Fix \textbf{one} refinement
\(Y'=\operatorname{Bl}_{I\mathfrak c_0}R\), and let \(D_0\) be the
pulled-back negative divisor of \(\mathfrak c_0\). Global generation is
a surjection onto an actual invertible subideal of \(\mathcal O_Y\).
Right exact pullback, then multiplication by the invertible image of
\(\mathfrak c_0\), gives the actual image equality
\[J_q\mathcal O_{Y'}=\mathcal O_{Y'}(qf^*L+D_0).\tag{8}\] The inclusion
into \(\mathcal O_{Y'}\) remains injective because its Cartier local
equation is nonzero on every reduced generic component. Thus (8) is
stronger than equality of valuations. The blowup universal property
gives \(Y'\to\operatorname{Bl}_{J_q}R\). For simultaneous \(L_i,q_i\),
(4),(5),(8) yield
\[\frac{e_R(J_{1,q_1},\ldots,J_{n,q_n})}{\prod q_i}=-Q_{Y'}(f^*L_1+D_0/q_1,\ldots,f^*L_n+D_0/q_n).\tag{9}\]
All intersections are on this one model. Evaluations satisfy
\(-v(J_q)/q=H_L-v(\mathfrak c_0)/q\), uniformly since \(\mathfrak c_0\)
is primary.

For every actual negative effective real nef \(D_i\) on \(Y\), G331's
finite-span perturbation and upward rational rounding give negative
rational ample \(L_{i,j}\to D_i\) in coefficient space. Their
evaluations converge uniformly. Use the same \(\mathfrak c_0,Y'\) for
all \(i,j\), choosing divisible \(q_{i,j}\to\infty\) with global
generation. Equation (9) and polynomial intersection continuity converge
to \(-Q_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_n)\); the uniformly approximating profiles
converge to \(H_{D_i}\). B341's uniform moment continuity and continuous
test pairing give the same limit on the algebraic side. Therefore the
algebraic bounded form on core tuples is \(-Q\), and its measure
satisfies (7).

Every exceptional integral Cartier test \(F\) is a difference of two
negative ample exceptional divisors: choose \(N\) so \(F-NE_I\) is
negative ample, and subtract \(-NE_I\). Extend by real linearity and use
common primary refinements. In particular every primary ideal test is
covered. Dense ideal-difference tests and finite mass identify (6) with
B341's measure on every core tuple. This proves actual geometric
presentation and primary-model independence, not just algebraic
presentation independence.

Here is strict maximum locality on rational cores. Synchronize
denominators and write \(u=-v(A)/q\), \(w=-v(B)/q\). On a common
normalized primary model make \(A,B,A+B\) and the remaining ideals
invertible. Divide their image ideals by \(S=(A+B)\mathcal O_N\) to
obtain actual invertible subideals \(L=A\mathcal O_N/S\),
\(M=B\mathcal O_N/S\), with \(L+M=\mathcal O_N\). At an atom \(Z\) with
\(u(v_Z)>w(v_Z)\), \(\operatorname{ord}_Z(M)>0\). The map
\(M|_Z\to\mathcal O_Z\) vanishes generically and therefore everywhere,
since \(Z\) is integral and this is a line-bundle morphism. Restricting
\(L+M=\mathcal O_N\) gives \(L|_Z\simeq\mathcal O_Z\). Hence \(D_A|_Z\)
and \(D_{A+B}|_Z\) have the same bundle class and intersection weight
with every fixed other input. On this common atomic support
\[\mu(\max(u,w),u_2,\ldots,u_p)|_{\{u>w\}}=\mu(u,u_2,\ldots,u_p)|_{\{u>w\}}.\tag{10}\]
Unit/zero cases are included in the subideal argument. There is no
regularity or SNC assumption on closed points of \(N\).

Uniform rational approximation proves (10) for continuous \(u,w\) and
continuous other slots: a continuous test supported on compact
\(K\subset\{u>w\}\) has a uniform positive gap there; apply (10) to
approximants and pass weakly to the limit. Actual bounded decreasing
representations then extend the other slots to bounded \(D\), keeping
the open set fixed. Compact regularity and Urysohn functions supported
inside an open set identify Radon restrictions.

We need uniform weak continuity on bounded \(D\) as well. B341's scalar
order and additive negative-constant expansion give, if entries are in
\([-M,0]\) and coordinatewise distance at most \(\varepsilon\),
\[|B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))-B_{\boldsymbol w}(v(J))|\le[(M+\varepsilon)^p-M^p]e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J).\tag{11}\]
Both comparisons use \(u_i-\varepsilon\le w_i\); expanding the shifts
bounds every term with \(r\) constant slots by
\(\varepsilon^r M^{p-r}e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J)\). Dense tests and the
common mass bound extend this to weak convergence. This asserts
continuity inside the established domain, not increasing-limit
membership.

Finally suppose bounded \(u,w\in D\) agree on open \(U\). Take actual
decreasing \(C\) representations \(u_\alpha,w_\beta\), with common bound
\([-M,0]\); their terms already dominate the targets, or may be clamped
there. For compact \(K\subset U\), fixed \(\alpha\), and
\(\varepsilon>0\), the open sets \(\{w_\beta-\varepsilon<u_\alpha\}\)
cover \(K\). Finite subcover and directedness give one \(\beta_0\) whose
open set contains \(K\) and works for every later \(\beta\). Continuous
strict locality gives equality of the test integrals for
\(\max(u_\alpha,w_\beta-\varepsilon)\) and \(u_\alpha\). First take the
bounded decreasing \(\beta\) limit, whose specified domain limit is
\(\max(u_\alpha,w-\varepsilon)\), then the \(\alpha\) limit to
\(\max(u,w-\varepsilon)\). These are two justified limits, not a
flattening assertion. Reverse the roles. The two maxima are within
\(\varepsilon\) uniformly of the already known \(D\) function
\(\max(u,w)\). Equation (11) gives the common limit, proving equality of
the measures on \(U\). Replacing slots successively proves full bounded
mixed ordinary open locality. The \(p=0\) case is equality of the fixed
reference measure.

\section{n4. Bounded full form, directed integration, and the mixed
inequalities}\label{n4.-bounded-full-form-directed-integration-and-the-mixed-inequalities}

Supports: G331, B341, n1, n2, n3.

For rational primary-or-unit profiles \(u_i=-v(I_i)/q_i\) define
\[F(u_1,\ldots,u_n)=e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n)/(q_1\cdots q_n).\tag{12}\]
B341's kernel makes this independent of all presentations, symmetric,
additive on the negative cone and nonnegative, with order reversed
relative to the functions in \textbf{all \(n\) slots}. For
\(u_i\in[-M,0]\), its bound is \(M^n m_{\mathfrak a}\).
Negative-constant shifts give the error bound
\([(M+\varepsilon)^n-M^n]m_{\mathfrak a}\) for uniformly close rational
tuples. Thus uniform approximation defines the same symmetric form on
\(C\), with all these identities for real scalars.

For bounded \(D\) tuples choose actual decreasing \(C\) representations
in all \(n\) entries. Their scalar forms increase, bounded as above.
Their supremum is representation independent: for any fixed continuous
majorant \(h_i\ge u_i\), \(\{g_{i,\alpha}<h_i+\varepsilon\}\) is a
directed open cover; compactness selects a common eventual index in each
of the finitely many slots. Then \(g_{i,\alpha}-\varepsilon\le h_i\), so
order and the shift error compare \(F(\boldsymbol h)\) to the
representation supremum. Reverse the two representations and let
\(\varepsilon\to0\). This also proves order for arbitrary bounded \(D\)
entries and
\[F(\boldsymbol u)=\sup\{F(\boldsymbol h):h_i\in C,\ h_i\ge u_i\}.\tag{13}\]
Addition and homogeneity use actual product-index \(C\) representations
of sums and scalar multiples; their finite scalar limits commute with
finite sums. If bounded \(D\) nets decrease to a specified bounded \(D\)
tuple with a common bound, their USC strict-test open covers compared to
each fixed \(C\) majorant prove convergence of the forms using the same
error. No new membership of an infimum is asserted. For continuous
\(v\in C\), ideal approximation and B341's bounded weak continuity prove
\[F(v,u_1,\ldots,u_p)=\int(-v)\,d\mu(u_1,\ldots,u_p).\tag{14}\]

The following measure lemma justifies an actual discontinuous test. Let
\(f_\alpha\) be a directed increasing family of nonnegative continuous
functions on compact Hausdorff \(X\) and \(f=\sup f_\alpha\), possibly
infinite. For finite positive Radon \(\sigma\),
\[\int f\,d\sigma=\sup_\alpha\int f_\alpha\,d\sigma.\tag{15}\] Here is a
proof including the regularity step. For any nonnegative continuous
\(h\le f\), the open sets \(\{f_\alpha>h-\varepsilon\}\) cover \(X\);
directed compactness gives one index and thus
\(\int f_\alpha\ge\int h-\varepsilon\sigma(X)\). Also \(\int f\) is the
supremum of these continuous-minorant integrals: first truncate \(f\) at
a finite level and partition its range into finitely many steps. Each
open superlevel set admits compact subsets whose measure approximates it
from below. Urysohn functions equal to one on these compacts and
supported inside the superlevel sets, multiplied by the step increments
and summed, give a continuous nonnegative minorant with integral
approximating the step integral. For increments of size \(\eta\) and
levels \(j\eta\), the sum \(\eta\sum_j h_j\) is at most \(f\) pointwise,
because nonzero \(h_j\) requires \(f>j\eta\); the approximation error is
at most \(\eta\sigma(X)\) plus the finitely many chosen compact errors.
Let errors and step size vanish and then the truncation level grow.
Ordinary sequence integration applies to the finite-level scalar
approximations. This proves the assertion and (15). It is not monotone
convergence for arbitrary measurable nets, which would be false.

If bounded \(v\in D\) has an actual decreasing \(C\) representation
\(g_\alpha\), apply (15) to \(-g_\alpha\uparrow-v\) against the fixed
measure in (14). For each fixed \(g_\alpha\), (14) gives its integral.
Commuting its supremum with the independent potential-representation
suprema proves (14) for this discontinuous bounded \(D\) test. Directly
testing weak convergence by \(v\) would not justify it.

We now prove conditional Cauchy for arbitrary fixed mixed entries. The
fresh published input is the complete immediate proof of SH17.7.1, valid
for every two-dimensional Noetherian local ring and finite
two-dimensional module. Its argument is as follows. Pass to \(R(z)\) if
necessary, preserving dimension and all finite lengths by composition
series. Choose a two-generated reduction \((a,b)\) of the primary ideal
\(I\). For positive integers \(r,s,N\), the quotient length estimate
\[\ell(M/I^{rN}J^{sN}M)\le2\ell(M/J^{sN}M)+\ell(M/(a^{rN},b^{rN})M)\]
follows by replacing \(I^{rN}\) by \((a^{rN},b^{rN})\) and mapping two
copies of \(M/J^{sN}M\) onto the remaining subquotient. Divide by
\(N^2/2\). Lech's parameter asymptotic formula and reduction invariance
give \[e(I^rJ^s;M)\le2e(I;M)r^2+2e(J;M)s^2.\] The mixed polynomial
expands its left side as \(e(I)r^2+2e(I,J)rs+e(J)s^2\). Optimizing
rational \(r/s\) yields \(e(I,J)^2\le e(I)e(J)\), since mixed
multiplicity is nonnegative (and positive for primary slots). The
complete SH17.7.2--3 statements/proofs were checked as context; the
arbitrary fixed-slot result below is stronger than simply citing their
two-ideal diagonal sequence.

For \(n>2\) and proper \(K_3,\ldots,K_n\), simultaneously choose one
superficial element in \(K_3\) for the three families
\((K_3,\ldots,K_n,I,I)\), \((K_3,\ldots,K_n,I,J)\),
\((K_3,\ldots,K_n,J,J)\). SH17.2.2's proof removes finitely many proper
subspaces in the same \(K_3/\mathfrak mK_3\), with minimal-prime
avoidance included. Their intersection has a point over the infinite
residue field. SH17.4.6 applies to each family on this same quotient, of
dimension exactly \(n-1\): minimal-prime avoidance gives the upper
bound, and a lifted parameter system and the height theorem give the
lower bound. Repeat in the fixed slots. This produces the \textbf{same
two-dimensional quotient} for all three numbers; it can be nonreduced,
non-CM and have embedded or smaller-dimensional components. Nothing is
deleted, and no regular sequence is claimed. Apply the just-checked
two-dimensional inequality there. In a finite residue field the exact
extension \(R(z)\) changes no length polynomial; it is only an algebraic
device and no boundary equivalence is assumed. We obtain
\[F(x,y,K_3,\ldots,K_n)^2\le F(x,x,K_3,\ldots,K_n)F(y,y,K_3,\ldots,K_n).\tag{16}\]
The needed superficial/reduction foundations are SH17.2.1--4 and17.4.6,
checked in full; SH17.4.7's literal unquotiented colon is not used. Its
correction is the finite annihilator on the quotient by previous
parameters. Ordinary reduction existence, power-reduction invariance and
Lech's formula are disclosed earlier published dependencies;
complete11.2.9--10 were additionally read, while every historical
reduction proof is not claimed freshly reconstructed. Unit slots follow
by adding \(-\varepsilon v(\mathfrak a)\) and uniform continuity; real
\(C\) and bounded \(D\) entries follow by their proved form limits. For
\(n=1\) the form is linear.

Here is generalized \(n\)-entry Holder, with the finite-simplex argument
included. For finitely many proper ideal types consider counts
\(\alpha_i\ge0\), \(\sum\alpha_i=n\), and subtract
\(\sum\alpha_i\log F(u_i^{[n]})/n\) from
\(\log F(u^{\boldsymbol\alpha})\). Call this \(f(\alpha)\); primary
positivity makes each logarithm finite and \(f=0\) at the vertices. By
(16), for positive \(\alpha_i,\alpha_j\),
\[2f(\alpha)\le f(\alpha+e_i-e_j)+f(\alpha-e_i+e_j).\] If its finite
global maximum were positive, choose a maximizer with the smallest
support. Both displayed neighbours must be maximizers. Repeatedly
transfer one unit from \(j\) to \(i\) until \(j\) vanishes; every
intermediate point remains a maximizer by the same inequality. This
contradicts minimal support. Consequently \(f\le0\). Perturbing zero
entries by \(-\varepsilon\), then taking uniform and bounded decreasing
limits, proves
\[F(u_1,\ldots,u_n)\le\prod_i A(u_i),\qquad A(u)=F(u^{[n]})^{1/n}.\tag{17}\]
Expanding \(F((u+w)^{[n]})\) and applying (17) termwise gives
\(A(u+w)\le A(u)+A(w)\). For a signed cone difference \(z=U-V\), (16)
implies \[F(z,z,K)=F(U,U,K)+F(V,V,K)-2F(U,V,K)\ge0.\] Each direction in
a cone segment is such a difference. Thus
\(E_{\mathrm{bd}}(u)=-F(u^{[n]})/n\) is concave: its second derivative
is \(-(n-1)F(z,z,u_t^{[n-2]})\le0\); for \(n=1\) it is linear. No
strictness or separation follows in nonpure rings.

\section{n5. Finite energy, noncircular approximation, and every
decreasing
net}\label{n5.-finite-energy-noncircular-approximation-and-every-decreasing-net}

Supports: G331, B341, n1, n3, n4.

For \(u\in D\) put \(u^k=\max(u,-k)\in D^{\mathrm{bd}}\), and define
initially extended forms
\[F_\infty(u_1,\ldots,u_n)=\sup_{k_1,\ldots,k_n}F(u_1^{k_1},\ldots,u_n^{k_n}),\quad E(u)=-F_\infty(u^{[n]})/n,\quad E^1=\{u\in D:E(u)>-\infty\}.\tag{18}\]
Use positive integers for \(k_i\); allowing real levels is cofinal.
Scalar monotonicity makes these suprema well defined. The exact formula
on the entire \(D\) domain, also for infinite values, is
\[F_\infty(\boldsymbol u)=\sup\{F(\boldsymbol h):h_i\in C,\ h_i\ge u_i\}.\tag{19}\]
For one direction, a fixed finite continuous majorant \(h_i\) dominates
\(u_i^{k_i}\) once \(k_i\ge\|h_i\|\), so order gives
\(F(\boldsymbol h)\le F_\infty(\boldsymbol u)\). For the other direction
(13) for each truncation uses only \(C\) majorants that also majorize
\(u_i\). This proves both bounds without a finite lower bound on \(u_i\)
and without new completion membership.

Write \(A_i=F_\infty(u_i^{[n]})^{1/n}\). Inequality (17) on truncations
proves \(F_\infty(\boldsymbol u)\le\prod A_i\) for \(u_i\in E^1\). Every
\(D\) majorant of an \(E^1\) function is in \(E^1\) by truncation order,
with no larger size. Globally bottom has infinite energy since
\(F((-k)^{[n]})=k^n m_{\mathfrak a}\). Poles confined to components
invisible to top degree may have finite energy; they remain points of
the domain.

Before unbounded additivity we prove a bounded approximation lemma.
Suppose \(f_{i,\lambda}\in D^{\mathrm{bd}}\) decrease to specified
\(u_i\in E^1\), each term individually bounded, with \textbf{no common
finite lower bound required}. For a fixed \(C\) majorant tuple
\(h_i\ge u_i\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), USC gives open sets
\(\{f_{i,\lambda}<h_i+\varepsilon\}\) covering \(X\). Directed
compactness supplies one eventual index for all slots with
\(f_{i,\lambda}-\varepsilon\le h_i\). Each term has diagonal size at
most \(A_i\). Bounded additivity and (17) bound its shift error by
\[0\le F(f_{1,\lambda}-\varepsilon,\ldots,f_{n,\lambda}-\varepsilon)-F(\boldsymbol f_\lambda)\le\prod_i(A_i+\varepsilon\delta_{\mathfrak a})-\prod_i A_i.\tag{20}\]
The right side sums the nonempty subsets of shifted slots using Holder;
it does not depend on the individual sup norms. Order compares the
shifted tuple to \(\boldsymbol h\). Let \(\varepsilon\to0\) and then
take the supremum (19). The increasing scalar limit is
\[F(\boldsymbol f_\lambda)\uparrow F_\infty(\boldsymbol u).\tag{21}\]
The upper bound follows also by extending each bounded term to its own
truncation formula. Separate indices are handled by the actual finite
product directed set. This proof uses no unbounded additivity.

To prove cone closure first, take \(u,w\in E^1\) and
\(s_k=u^k+w^k\downarrow u+w\in D\). The last membership is G331's actual
product-representation addition rule. Bounded Minkowski gives
\(A(s_k)\le A(u)+A(w)\). For any \(h\in C\) majorizing \(u+w\), the open
sets \(\{s_k<h+\varepsilon\}\) cover \(X\), so one \(k\) has
\(s_k-\varepsilon\le h\). Bounded Holder and Minkowski give
\(F(h^{[n]})\le(A(u)+A(w)+\varepsilon\delta_{\mathfrak a})^n\). Formula
(19) and \(\varepsilon\to0\) prove \(u+w\in E^1\) and Minkowski there.
Positive scaling follows from cofinal rescaled cutoff levels, and scalar
zero is treated separately as the zero function. Now apply (21) to sums
of truncations and to the other truncated entries. Bounded additivity
passes to the finite limits. Thus \(F_\infty\) is a finite symmetric
additive cone \(n\)-form on \(E^1\), nonnegative-real homogeneous, with
order, (16),(17) and Minkowski. Concavity passes by bounded
approximations, or by its finite polynomial on each segment.

For \(u_1,\ldots,u_p\in E^1\), their bounded truncation measures have
primary moments
\[\int v(J)\,d\mu(u_1^{k_1},\ldots,u_p^{k_p})=F(u_1^{k_1},\ldots,u_p^{k_p},-v(J)),\]
which increase to finite \(F_\infty(u_1,\ldots,u_p,-v(J))\). Their
masses are at most \(\delta_{\mathfrak a}\prod_{i=1}^p A_i\).
Difference-lattice test integrals therefore converge and remain positive
on nonnegative tests. Their uniform mass bound extends the limit to a
unique positive continuous functional on \(C(X)\), hence a finite
positive Radon measure, denoted \(\mu_E(\boldsymbol u)\). Explicitly
approximate any continuous test uniformly by a rational
difference-lattice test and bound both integral errors by its sup-norm
error times the common mass bound. This proves actual weak convergence
of the net, without a compactness subsequence or metrizability
assertion. Its exact moment rule is
\[\int v(J)\,d\mu_E(\boldsymbol u)=F_\infty(\boldsymbol u,-v(J)).\tag{22}\]
Lemma (21) proves the same convergence for \textbf{any individually
bounded decreasing \(D\) approximants} to the specified finite-energy
targets. Thus the construction is canonical, agrees with B341 on bounded
inputs, and inherits additivity/homogeneity by moments and density. It
is geometrically compared on cores by n3.

For an arbitrary decreasing net \(u_{i,\lambda}\in E^1\) with specified
\(u_i\in E^1\) limit in \(D\), all sizes are at most \(A_i\). USC
compact covers compare them to every fixed \(C\) majorant tuple as
above. Now the already proved unbounded multilinearity and Holder give
(20) for \(F_\infty\). Taking \(\varepsilon\to0\) and supremum (19)
proves
\[F_\infty(\boldsymbol u_\lambda)\uparrow F_\infty(\boldsymbol u).\tag{23}\]
Fixed primary tests give every measure moment; the same common mass
bound proves weak convergence
\(\mu_E(\boldsymbol u_\lambda)\to\mu_E(\boldsymbol u)\). Diagonals give
\(E(u_\lambda)\downarrow E(u)\). This is a theorem for full directed
nets, not just sequences. It does not assert membership of arbitrary
infima. One useful extra follows without circularity: if a decreasing
\(E^1\) net has a \textbf{specified \(D\) limit} and uniformly bounded
sizes, compact-cover comparison to each majorant and (19) prove that
limit belongs to \(E^1\); then (23) applies. Specified \(D\) membership
remains essential.

\section{n6. Unbounded pairing, variations, capacity, and ordinary
locality}\label{n6.-unbounded-pairing-variations-capacity-and-ordinary-locality}

Supports: G331, B341, n1, n3, n4, n5.

For \(v\in E^1\), take an actual decreasing \(C\) representation
\(g_\alpha\downarrow v\). Each \(g_\alpha\) is bounded and majorizes
\(v\), so its size is at most \(A(v)\). Equation (15) against the
\textbf{fixed} finite-energy measure gives
\[\int(-v)\,d\mu_E(\boldsymbol u)=\sup_\alpha\int(-g_\alpha)\,d\mu_E(\boldsymbol u).\]
For fixed \(g_\alpha\), continuous-test weak convergence in n5
identifies its integral with
\(\sup_{\boldsymbol k}F(g_\alpha,\boldsymbol u^{\boldsymbol k})\). These
nonnegative scalar suprema commute. Lemma (21) applies to the product
net \((g_\alpha,\boldsymbol u^{\boldsymbol k})\) of individually bounded
functions with finite-energy targets. Hence
\[\int(-v)\,d\mu_E(u_1,\ldots,u_p)=F_\infty(v,u_1,\ldots,u_p)\le A(v)\prod_iA(u_i)<\infty.\tag{24}\]
This supplies actual integrability for discontinuous or unbounded tests.
It does not use weak convergence against such a test. Every such mixed
measure gives zero mass to the pole set of \textbf{every} \(v\in E^1\),
since otherwise (24) would be infinite. In particular
\[E(u)=\frac1n\int u\,d\mu_E(u^{[p]}).\tag{25}\]

There is also the fully extended test identity for every \(v\in D\),
with the other entries in \(E^1\): its bounded truncations \(v^k\) are
in \(E^1\), so (24) applies to each. Ordinary nonnegative sequence
integration gives \(\int(-v)=\sup_k\int(-v^k)\). By the defining cutoff
formula (18), the suprema over \(k\) and the other entries' cutoffs
commute and are cofinal in the complete product cutoff set. Hence
\(\int(-v)\,d\mu_E(\boldsymbol u)=F_\infty(v,\boldsymbol u)\) in
\([0,\infty]\). Finiteness and no pole mass are asserted only when the
test is \(E^1\) (or its displayed mixed value happens to be finite).

For \(u,v\in E^1\), finite polynomial multilinearity gives, for
\(t\ge0\),
\[\frac{d}{dt}E(u+tv)=-F_\infty(v,(u+tv)^{[p]})=\int v\,d\mu_E((u+tv)^{[p]}).\tag{26}\]
At zero this is a one-sided derivative. On an admissible segment with
direction \(v-u\), cone differences give the same polynomial derivative,
with absolute integrability by (24) applied to each term. Its second
derivative is nonpositive by (16), so \(E\) is concave on the whole
finite-energy cone. No variation outside the class is asserted.

For a fixed finite-energy reference \(w\), define \(E_w(u)=E(u)-E(w)\).
Telescoping the \(n\)-form gives the full finite reference-cocycle
formula
\[E_w(u)=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{p}\int(u-w)\,d\mu_E(u^{[j]},w^{[p-j]}).\tag{27}\]
Each term is absolutely integrable by (24) separately for \(u,w\).
Consequently \(E_w(u)+E_z(w)=E_z(u)\). In particular \(E_0=E\),
\(E(0)=0\), \(E(-1)=-m_{\mathfrak a}/n\), and
\(\mu_E((-1)^{[p]})(X)=m_{\mathfrak a}\); zero entries annihilate
positive-arity measures and forms as dictated by multilinearity. The
\(n=1\) operator has no potential entries, so that annihilation
statement has no zero-arity analogue.

For Borel \(A\) define the elementary capacity
\[\operatorname{Cap}_{\mathfrak a}(A)=\sup\{\mu(\phi^{[p]})(A):\phi\in D^{\mathrm{bd}},\ -1\le\phi\le0\}.\]
It is monotone and countably subadditive by the same properties for each
measure and \(\sup_\phi\sum_j\le\sum_j\sup_\phi\). Its total value is at
most \(m_{\mathfrak a}\). For \(u\in E^1\), \(t>0\), (24) and
\(A(\phi)\le\delta_{\mathfrak a}\) give
\[\operatorname{Cap}_{\mathfrak a}(\{u<-t\})\le A(u)\delta_{\mathfrak a}^{p}/t.\tag{28}\]
Thus every \(E^1\) pole set has this capacity zero. This estimate alone
is not a Choquet theorem or a quasicontinuity theorem. No capacity
regularity, equation uniqueness, or measure order is inferred from
scalar moment order.

If two finite-energy tuples agree entrywise on an open \(U\), their
corresponding constant truncations agree there. N3 gives equality of
their bounded mixed restrictions. A continuous test supported on compact
\(K\subset U\) passes to the finite-energy weak limit of n5. Urysohn
functions and Radon regularity determine restrictions, proving ordinary
open locality for \(\mu_E\). We do not assert a nonpluripolar cutoff
identity on \(\{u>-k\}\): for upper semicontinuous \(u\) that strict
Borel set need not be open, and neither the proved locality nor the
capacity estimate establishes such an identity. The constructed calculus
uses bounded decreasing extension; it makes no claim to an unproved
Borel/plurifine extension convention.

\section{n7. Primary energy invariance, full conductor descent, and
dimensions one and
zero}\label{n7.-primary-energy-invariance-full-conductor-descent-and-dimensions-one-and-zero}

Supports: G331, B341, n1, n4, n5, n6.

For another proper primary \(\mathfrak b\), put
\(d(v)=v_{\mathfrak a}(\mathfrak b)\) and \(T(v)=v/d(v)\). On compact
\(X_{\mathfrak a}\), \(0<d_{\min}\le d\le d_{\max}<\infty\), and
\(u_{\mathfrak b}(Tv)=u_{\mathfrak a}(v)/d(v)\). G331 transports actual
\(C,D\) representations. B341 gives for every bounded tuple
\[\mu_{\mathfrak b}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak b})=T_*[d\,\mu_{\mathfrak a}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak a})].\tag{29}\]
Use the actual bounded-test pairing of n4, including discontinuous
inputs, to cancel \(d\) against the last transformed potential. This
proves invariance of the full bounded \(n\)-form, not just ideal
moments.

For an unbounded \(u\), pulling its \(\mathfrak b\)-truncation back with
its homogeneous factor gives \(w_k=\max(u_{\mathfrak a},-kd)\). It is
bounded \(D\) by finite maxima with the primary profile
\(-k v_{\mathfrak a}(\mathfrak b)\), and
\[\max(u_{\mathfrak a},-k d_{\max})\le w_k\le\max(u_{\mathfrak a},-k d_{\min}).\tag{30}\]
The two constant cutoff levels are cofinal. Bounded form invariance and
scalar order therefore prove
\(F_{\infty,\mathfrak b}(u_{\mathfrak b}^{[n]})=F_{\infty,\mathfrak a}(u_{\mathfrak a}^{[n]})\),
including infinite values. Thus \(E^1\) is primary-gauge independent.
For an \(E^1\) tuple, \((w_{i,k})\) are individually bounded decreasing
approximants to the original tuple. Apply (21),(22) and test the weak
limits in (29) with the fixed continuous function \(d\) times a
transported continuous test. We obtain
\[F_{\infty,\mathfrak b}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak b})=F_{\infty,\mathfrak a}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak a}),\quad E_{\mathfrak b}(u_{\mathfrak b})=E_{\mathfrak a}(u_{\mathfrak a}),\quad\mu_{E,\mathfrak b}=T_*[d\,\mu_{E,\mathfrak a}].\tag{31}\]
There is exactly \textbf{one} measure factor, not \(d^n\), \(d^{n-1}\)
or an inverse factor. For \(\mathfrak b=\mathfrak a^r\), it is \(r\),
while potentials divide by \(r\). G331's homogeneous cocycle makes (31)
compose exactly for three primary gauges. A finite reference \(w\) is
transported along with \(u\), so (27) and its cocycle are invariant as
well.

Let \(S\) normalize \(R\) finitely, with all local normal centres
\(S_q\) over \(\mathfrak m\). Write
\(f_q=[\kappa(q):\kappa(\mathfrak m)]\) and \(\rho_q\) for the full
restriction map of their sections with gauge \(\mathfrak aS_q\). These
are total residue degrees, including inseparable ones. B341's bounded
top-degree identity, paired with a bounded \(D\) test using n4, gives
the full bounded \(n\)-form identity. For an extended \(D\) tuple,
constant truncations pull back exactly to constant truncations on every
centre. There are finitely many centres and all forms are nonnegative;
finite sums commute with increasing scalar suprema. Therefore, with
extended values allowed,
\[F_{\infty,R}(\boldsymbol u)=\sum_{\dim S_q=n}f_q F_{\infty,S_q}(\rho_q^*\boldsymbol u),\qquad E_R(u)=\sum_{\dim S_q=n}f_q E_{S_q}(\rho_q^*u).\tag{32}\]
This is top-degree normalization, not a general finite-map trace.
Algebraically its weight is justified by finite Artinian factorization:
each length-one \(S_q\) residue factor has \(R\)-length \(f_q\), while
normalization is generically the identity on every reduced component
field. Smaller-dimensional factors have no degree-\(n\) coefficient. In
particular no inseparability is dropped and no vertical generic degree
is fabricated. Every smaller-dimensional centre is still part of the
function and conductor domain, with zero contribution in this
convention.

For \(E^1\) inputs, (32) proves that all top-dimensional pullbacks are
\(E^1\) and that this condition is equivalent to downstairs finite
energy. Pass their bounded measures to the weak limits, a finite sum
with fixed continuous restriction maps, to obtain
\[\mu_{E,R}(\boldsymbol u)=\sum_{\dim S_q=n}f_q\rho_{q,*}\mu_{E,S_q}(\rho_q^*\boldsymbol u).\tag{33}\]
G331 already proves full \(D\) positivity descent: an upstairs tuple of
\(D\) functions descends exactly when constant on \textbf{every}
restriction fibre, including conductor-infinite and nonminimal supports,
all-zero, mixed-zero and partial-bottom cases. Combining this with (32)
gives the exact energy descent statement: fibre-constant upstairs \(D\)
data descend to \(E^1_R\) if and only if their top-dimensional entries
have finite diagonal energy. No own lower-dimensional finite-energy
condition is imposed; all lower-dimensional data still satisfy full
fibre compatibility. At finite conductor value fibres are singletons; at
infinite value equality on the conductor quotient is precisely the
identification. Formula (33) adds masses on identified fibres. It
asserts nothing about automatically tracing incompatible arbitrary
upstairs tuples.

For every excellent reduced curve, B341's independent Cartier reference
measure is
\[\mu_{\mathfrak a}=\sum_q f_q a_q\delta_{\operatorname{ord}_q/a_q},\qquad a_q=\operatorname{ord}_q(\mathfrak a)>0.\]
G331's actual \(D\) is all extended nonpositive vectors on the finite
branch set. Consequently \(E^1\) is exactly the finite vectors, and
\[E(u)=\sum_q f_q a_q u(q),\qquad dE_u(v)=\sum_q f_q a_q v(q).\tag{34}\]
Every coefficient is strictly positive, so a single curve-branch pole
has infinite energy. This is actual zero-arity Cartier energy on all
primary curve models, with nonnormal and inseparable residue cases
included, not an ideal-cone analogy. For example
\(R=k+t\ell[[t]]\subset\ell[[t]]\), \([\ell:k]=d\), has generic
normalization degree one, residue degree \(d\), and maximal-ideal
reference mass \(d\). Its primary ideal \(t^r\ell[[t]]\) has
multiplicity \(dr\), so replacing the residue factor by a
generic-degree-only factor is wrong, including for inseparable
extensions. Gauge weights and profiles cancel exactly in (34). On a
smooth DVR the maximal-ideal mass is one; a primary gauge \((t^r)\) has
mass \(r\) on its rescaled singleton. In dimension zero the reduction is
a field, its centred section is empty, \(E=0\) and the unique measure is
zero. Nilpotents require the explicit reduction convention in n1:
\(k[[t]]\) and \(k[[t]][\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\) have the same reduced
section and \((t)\) gauge but unreduced multiplicities one and two; no
boundary-only reconstruction can distinguish those lengths.

\section{n8. Exact smooth specialization via the primary local
intersection
definition}\label{n8.-exact-smooth-specialization-via-the-primary-local-intersection-definition}

Supports: G331, B341, n1, n4, n5, n6, n7.

The primary source is Boucksom--Favre--Jonsson, \emph{Valuations and
plurisubharmonic singularities}, published RIMS44 (2008), particularly
its complete Setting, Proposition1.14, the relevant2.4--2.8 units, and
\textbf{Definition4.3} within the entire Section4. The source has been
read at its exact hypotheses: a smooth \textbf{closed} point of an
affine variety over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field,
dimension \(n\ge2\), with maximal-ideal section. No analytic arbitrary
germ, positive characteristic, singular resolution, or broader
multiplier theorem is asserted. G331 already supplies full cone
comparison on all semivaluations with bottom adjoined; B341 supplies
bounded operator comparison. These grants are not unbounded energy
citations.

Here is an explicit local smooth extension convention, selected by a
published \textbf{local} primary definition rather than a global
projective energy theorem. For a BFJ formal psh function \(g\), let
\(Z(g)\) be its nef Weil divisor. Define its local intersection energy
and finite-energy class by
\[E_{\mathrm{BFJ,int}}(g)=\frac1n\langle Z(g)^{[n]}\rangle,\qquad E^1_{\mathrm{BFJ,int}}=\{g:\langle Z(g)^{[n]}\rangle> -\infty\},\tag{35}\]
where the bracket is \textbf{exactly BFJ Definition4.3}: the infimum of
the intersections of nef Cartier majorants of its entries. The zero
anchor, sign, raw multiplicity and degree-\(n\) normalization are
explicit; this is a local degree-\(n\) energy, not a degree-\(n+1\)
projective Aubin--Mabuchi formula or a probability normalization.
Equation (35) specifies the canonical finite-self-intersection
convention; we do not claim the source uses this \(E^1\) notation or
proves an independently named energy theorem.

We prove the full comparison, rather than assume that convention agrees
with truncation. Let \(u_i\in D\) be nonbottom BFJ functions with
associated \(Z_i\). For a nef Cartier \(W_i\), its function \(w_i\) is
continuous and belongs to \(C\), by BFJ1.14 and G331's exact class
comparison. Moreover \(W_i\ge Z_i\) coefficientwise if and only if
\(w_i\ge u_i\) on the entire section. One direction is immediate at
divisorial points. Conversely coefficient order gives function order
there; on each closed simplex both formal functions are continuous by
the precise G331/BFJ comparison, and rational divisorial points are
dense, so order holds at every quasi-monomial. Taking their canonical
infima over all retractions, as BFJ2.8 and G331 prescribe, preserves
order at every semivaluation. B341's bounded operator comparison and the
actual bounded test pairing of n4 identify
\(F(\boldsymbol w)=-\langle\boldsymbol W\rangle\).

Definition4.3's negative intersection is therefore the supremum over
these Cartier majorants, which is at most the full \(C\)-majorant
supremum (19). To prove the other bound, fix any \(C\) majorants
\(h_i\ge u_i\). If one is zero, its form value is zero and contributes
no positive supremum. Otherwise G331's normal zero dichotomy, or BFJ2.4
in this smooth range, gives \(h_i\le-c_i<0\) uniformly. For
\(0<\eta<1\), approximate \((1-\eta)h_i\) uniformly by a rational
primary profile \(r_{i,\eta}\) with error less than \(\eta c_i/2\) and
tending to zero as \(\eta\to0\). Then
\[r_{i,\eta}\ge h_i+\eta c_i/2\ge u_i.\] Each is a nef Cartier majorant,
and \(r_{i,\eta}\to h_i\) uniformly. N4's uniform form convergence
implies \(F(\boldsymbol h)\) is at most the Cartier-majorant supremum.
Thus
\[F_\infty(u_1,\ldots,u_n)=-\langle Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\rangle\quad\text{in }[0,\infty].\tag{36}\]
This argument does not invoke an unbounded continuity or energy grant
from BFJ. It compares directly with its primary local definition. Zero
target entries give zero on both sides; bottom has infinite self energy
and is excluded from (35). G331 says every other \(D\) function is a
full BFJ function, so (36) proves equality of the \textbf{entire}
finite-energy spaces under convention (35), not merely smooth core or
bounded agreement.

For finite-energy tuples, Holder guarantees that the mixed moment with

\section{n2. An unconditional primary mixed-multiplicity
kernel}\label{n2.-an-unconditional-primary-mixed-multiplicity-kernel}

Use the domain and full-boundary conventions of n1. This note proves the
algebraic kernel needed for moment measures, with arbitrary
characteristic, residue field, non-Cohen--Macaulay rings, and
non-equidimensional reduced components allowed. It proves no geometric
Monge--Ampere operator.

Here are the precise published applications. In the CURRENT July 2024
author version of Swanson--Huneke, Theorem 17.4.2 says that for a
Noetherian local ring, finite module \(M\), and primary ideals
\(I_1,\ldots,I_s\), the function
\(\ell_R(M/I_1^{t_1}\cdots I_s^{t_s}M)\) is a rational polynomial of
total degree \(\dim M\) for all sufficiently large independent integer
\(t_i\). Definition 17.4.3 takes its degree-\(\dim R\) coefficients,
using factorial denominators, as mixed multiplicities; they are zero if
\(\dim M<\dim R\). These statements and their full immediate proof were
read. We use this degree convention even when components have different
dimensions.

The complete 17.2.2 proof gives a nonempty Zariski open set of choices
for a superficial element in \(I_1/\mathfrak m I_1\), with finitely many
prescribed primes avoidable when \(I_1\) is not contained in them.
Complete 17.2.4 and 17.4.6 show that a superficial element \(x\in I_1\)
avoiding the minimal primes reduces a mixed multiplicity having at least
one \(I_1\) entry to the corresponding mixed multiplicity over \(R/xR\)
with that entry removed, when \(\dim R>1\). No regular sequence or
Cohen--Macaulay hypothesis is required. The finite annihilator
correction in dimension one is part of the source, but we do not need
that formula to compare the last ideal.

Finite residue fields cause no restriction. Pass to
\(R(Z)=R[Z]_{\mathfrak m R[Z]}\) solely for these algebraic proofs.
Lemma 8.4.2 gives faithful flatness, equal dimension, preservation of
primary ideals and lengths. Explicitly, a composition series of any
finite-length \(R\)-module tensors to a series with factors
\(\kappa(\mathfrak m)(Z)\), so its length is unchanged. Thus every
multigraded length polynomial and its coefficients are unchanged. No
identification of boundaries across this transcendental extension is
used.

Write \(e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n)\) for the coefficient with one entry of each
ideal. Product and power linearity follow directly from the polynomial:
replace the variables for \(I\) and \(J\) by the same variable to
represent \((IJ)^t\). The coefficient with exponent one in that variable
and one in the other \(n-1\) variables receives exactly the terms with
exponent one in \(I\) or in \(J\). Hence
\[e_R(IJ,I_2,\ldots,I_n)=e_R(I,I_2,\ldots,I_n)+e_R(J,I_2,\ldots,I_n),\]
and \(e_R(I^r,I_2,\ldots,I_n)=r e_R(I,I_2,\ldots,I_n)\). Relabelling
gives symmetry. If an ideal is the unit ideal, its length polynomial is
independent of that variable, so the corresponding mixed multiplicity is
zero. This convention is compatible with the identities.

We give the single-slot inclusion proof, rather than trying to compare
individual coefficients of unequal multivariate polynomials. Suppose
\(J\subset K\) are primary and the other \(n-1\) ideals are fixed. Over
an infinite residue field, choose in the second slot an element
superficial for BOTH tuples \((I_2,I_3,\ldots,I_n,J)\) and
\((I_2,I_3,\ldots,I_n,K)\), and avoiding all minimal primes. The two
allowed sets are nonempty opens in the same finite-dimensional vector
space \(I_2/\mathfrak m I_2\), obtained by removing finitely many proper
subspaces in the 17.2.2 proof. Their intersection still has a rational
point over an infinite field. The avoidance conditions can be included
in that finite union. Each quotient has dimension one less: avoiding the
minimal primes gives the upper bound, and lifting a system of parameters
from the quotient and using the height theorem gives the lower bound.
The quotient images of \(J,K\) remain primary and included. Apply 17.4.6
to both tuples. Repeat in the remaining fixed slots, choosing
simultaneous superficial elements afresh in each quotient. In dimension

\[\ell(R/J^N)\ge\ell(R/K^N),\qquad e(J)\ge e(K)\] by dividing by \(N\)

\[J\subset K\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e_R(J,I_2,\ldots,I_n)\ge e_R(K,I_2,\ldots,I_n).\]
The same reduction to a one-dimensional primary multiplicity proves
positivity for primary slots. The positivity of the last one-variable

\section{n3. Bounded real and actual decreasing-net moment

Use n1 and the proved algebraic kernel n2. This note constructs finite

\[B_{\boldsymbol u}(1)=B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(\mathfrak a))=\frac{e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_p,\mathfrak a)}{\prod k_i}.\]
