# n5. Finite energy, noncircular approximation, and every decreasing net
Supports: G331, B341, n1, n3, n4.

For $u\in D$ put $u^k=\max(u,-k)\in D^{\mathrm{bd}}$, and define initially extended forms
$$F_\infty(u_1,\ldots,u_n)=\sup_{k_1,\ldots,k_n}F(u_1^{k_1},\ldots,u_n^{k_n}),\quad E(u)=-F_\infty(u^{[n]})/n,\quad E^1=\{u\in D:E(u)>-\infty\}.\tag{18}$$
Use positive integers for $k_i$; allowing real levels is cofinal. Scalar monotonicity makes these suprema well defined. The exact formula on the entire $D$ domain, also for infinite values, is
$$F_\infty(\boldsymbol u)=\sup\{F(\boldsymbol h):h_i\in C,\ h_i\ge u_i\}.\tag{19}$$
For one direction, a fixed finite continuous majorant $h_i$ dominates $u_i^{k_i}$ once $k_i\ge\|h_i\|$, so order gives $F(\boldsymbol h)\le F_\infty(\boldsymbol u)$. For the other direction (13) for each truncation uses only $C$ majorants that also majorize $u_i$. This proves both bounds without a finite lower bound on $u_i$ and without new completion membership.

Write $A_i=F_\infty(u_i^{[n]})^{1/n}$. Inequality (17) on truncations proves $F_\infty(\boldsymbol u)\le\prod A_i$ for $u_i\in E^1$. Every $D$ majorant of an $E^1$ function is in $E^1$ by truncation order, with no larger size. Globally bottom has infinite energy since $F((-k)^{[n]})=k^n m_{\mathfrak a}$. Poles confined to components invisible to top degree may have finite energy; they remain points of the domain.

Before unbounded additivity we prove a bounded approximation lemma. Suppose $f_{i,\lambda}\in D^{\mathrm{bd}}$ decrease to specified $u_i\in E^1$, each term individually bounded, with **no common finite lower bound required**. For a fixed $C$ majorant tuple $h_i\ge u_i$ and $\varepsilon>0$, USC gives open sets $\{f_{i,\lambda}<h_i+\varepsilon\}$ covering $X$. Directed compactness supplies one eventual index for all slots with $f_{i,\lambda}-\varepsilon\le h_i$. Each term has diagonal size at most $A_i$. Bounded additivity and (17) bound its shift error by
$$0\le F(f_{1,\lambda}-\varepsilon,\ldots,f_{n,\lambda}-\varepsilon)-F(\boldsymbol f_\lambda)\le\prod_i(A_i+\varepsilon\delta_{\mathfrak a})-\prod_i A_i.\tag{20}$$
The right side sums the nonempty subsets of shifted slots using Holder; it does not depend on the individual sup norms. Order compares the shifted tuple to $\boldsymbol h$. Let $\varepsilon\to0$ and then take the supremum (19). The increasing scalar limit is
$$F(\boldsymbol f_\lambda)\uparrow F_\infty(\boldsymbol u).\tag{21}$$
The upper bound follows also by extending each bounded term to its own truncation formula. Separate indices are handled by the actual finite product directed set. This proof uses no unbounded additivity.

To prove cone closure first, take $u,w\in E^1$ and $s_k=u^k+w^k\downarrow u+w\in D$. The last membership is G331's actual product-representation addition rule. Bounded Minkowski gives $A(s_k)\le A(u)+A(w)$. For any $h\in C$ majorizing $u+w$, the open sets $\{s_k<h+\varepsilon\}$ cover $X$, so one $k$ has $s_k-\varepsilon\le h$. Bounded Holder and Minkowski give $F(h^{[n]})\le(A(u)+A(w)+\varepsilon\delta_{\mathfrak a})^n$. Formula (19) and $\varepsilon\to0$ prove $u+w\in E^1$ and Minkowski there. Positive scaling follows from cofinal rescaled cutoff levels, and scalar zero is treated separately as the zero function. Now apply (21) to sums of truncations and to the other truncated entries. Bounded additivity passes to the finite limits. Thus $F_\infty$ is a finite symmetric additive cone $n$-form on $E^1$, nonnegative-real homogeneous, with order, (16),(17) and Minkowski. Concavity passes by bounded approximations, or by its finite polynomial on each segment.

For $u_1,\ldots,u_p\in E^1$, their bounded truncation measures have primary moments
$$\int v(J)\,d\mu(u_1^{k_1},\ldots,u_p^{k_p})=F(u_1^{k_1},\ldots,u_p^{k_p},-v(J)),$$
which increase to finite $F_\infty(u_1,\ldots,u_p,-v(J))$. Their masses are at most $\delta_{\mathfrak a}\prod_{i=1}^p A_i$. Difference-lattice test integrals therefore converge and remain positive on nonnegative tests. Their uniform mass bound extends the limit to a unique positive continuous functional on $C(X)$, hence a finite positive Radon measure, denoted $\mu_E(\boldsymbol u)$. Explicitly approximate any continuous test uniformly by a rational difference-lattice test and bound both integral errors by its sup-norm error times the common mass bound. This proves actual weak convergence of the net, without a compactness subsequence or metrizability assertion. Its exact moment rule is
$$\int v(J)\,d\mu_E(\boldsymbol u)=F_\infty(\boldsymbol u,-v(J)).\tag{22}$$
Lemma (21) proves the same convergence for **any individually bounded decreasing $D$ approximants** to the specified finite-energy targets. Thus the construction is canonical, agrees with B341 on bounded inputs, and inherits additivity/homogeneity by moments and density. It is geometrically compared on cores by n3.

For an arbitrary decreasing net $u_{i,\lambda}\in E^1$ with specified $u_i\in E^1$ limit in $D$, all sizes are at most $A_i$. USC compact covers compare them to every fixed $C$ majorant tuple as above. Now the already proved unbounded multilinearity and Holder give (20) for $F_\infty$. Taking $\varepsilon\to0$ and supremum (19) proves
$$F_\infty(\boldsymbol u_\lambda)\uparrow F_\infty(\boldsymbol u).\tag{23}$$
Fixed primary tests give every measure moment; the same common mass bound proves weak convergence $\mu_E(\boldsymbol u_\lambda)\to\mu_E(\boldsymbol u)$. Diagonals give $E(u_\lambda)\downarrow E(u)$. This is a theorem for full directed nets, not just sequences. It does not assert membership of arbitrary infima. One useful extra follows without circularity: if a decreasing $E^1$ net has a **specified $D$ limit** and uniformly bounded sizes, compact-cover comparison to each majorant and (19) prove that limit belongs to $E^1$; then (23) applies. Specified $D$ membership remains essential.
