## n4. Independently supported Cartier intersection

Let Y=Bl_I Spec R be an actual primary model. G331 gives its reduced dominating components, regular effective E_I, ample O(-E_I), directed refinements and actual exceptional Cartier order unit. For an effective exceptional integral Cartier E, a power of m kills O_E. Indeed its coherent support is in the closed fibre; on a finite affine cover the radical containment becomes a common power containment. E is proper over the resulting Artinian quotient of R. Its coherent cohomology is finite over R by 0B5S and killed by that power; higher cohomology is bounded in degree on a finite affine cover. Define chi_R(E,F)=sum_j (-1)^j l_R H^j(E,F).

The dimension bound needed here is dim E<=p, also for nonpure R. On a component dominating R/P, the catenary finite-type dimension function delta(x)=dim(R/image(x))+trdeg of residue fields has generic value dim R/P. A point over m has delta equal to its residue transcendence degree, and a proper closed exceptional subset has delta-dimension at most dim R/P-1. Thus each closed-fibre component has ordinary k-dimension at most p. This also shows why smaller dominating components cannot supply a dimension-p exceptional component.

Do not apply a field Euler lemma directly to E in mixed characteristic. Filter O_E by m^j O_E. Every graded quotient G_j is coherent on the proper k-scheme E_0 and has support dimension <=p. Tensoring by any line bundle is exact. Euler additivity, equality of R-length and k-dimension for the quotient cohomologies, and complete Stacks 0BEL 33.45.1 show that

    P_E(t)=chi_R(E,O_E tensor prod O(F_i)^t_i)

is a polynomial of total degree <=p, for all integer twists. Define Q_Y(F_1,...,F_p,E) as its coefficient t_1...t_p. When p=0 this is l_R H^0(E,O_E); a proper zero-dimensional scheme over an Artinian base is finite and has no higher cohomology. The definition includes every nilpotent layer of E.

Apply 33.45.2 to each G_j. At a dimension-p generic point eta_Z, the local lengths add to l_(O_Y,eta_Z)(O_E,eta_Z). The m-quotient local ring has the same residue field; the finite modules killed by m have the same composition length over both local rings. Lower-dimensional supports have degree <p. It follows that

    Q_Y(F_1,...,F_p,E)
       =sum_(dim Z=p) l_(O_Y,eta_Z)(O_E,eta_Z)
                 (O(F_1)|Z ... O(F_p)|Z . Z).            (C)

Here Z is integral reduced and proper over k; the parenthesis is the coefficient-defined numerical intersection of 33.45.3. A fixed extra twist only translates polynomial variables and changes no degree-p coefficient.

Variable relabeling and substitution prove symmetry and additivity of the first p slots. The actual Cartier sequence

    0 -> O_E1(-E_2) -> O_(E_1+E_2) -> O_E2 -> 0

and fixed-twist invariance prove additivity of the effective last slot. G331's order unit expresses any integral exceptional F as (F+NE_I)-NE_I with both effective. Equality of two such differences is equality of effective sums, so extension is independent of decomposition. Then extend over the real Cartier tensor group by multilinearity.

Full symmetry requires a supported calculation even for improper intersections. For effective E,F take their ACTUAL two-term locally free resolutions K_E=[O(-E)->O], K_F=[O(-F)->O]. Their tensor products, with a line bundle L, are canonically the same total Koszul complex up to interchange signs. To compute its Euler characteristic, resolve first E and then F, or conversely: the resulting complexes [O_E tensor L(-F)->O_E tensor L] and [O_F tensor L(-E)->O_F tensor L] have finite-length cohomology in every term. The free terms on Y individually need NOT have finite length; no alternating length of those free terms is taken. Their hypercohomology Euler identities give

    chi_R(E,L)-chi_R(E,L(-F))
      =chi_R(F,L)-chi_R(F,L(-E)).                         (K)

The complexes remain valid if either induced cut is improper or a zerodivisor. Let L=O(F)^t O(E)^s prod_(j=1)^(n-2) O(G_j)^r_j, set t=s=0 in (K), and read r_1...r_(n-2). The left finite difference is the coefficient t r_1...r_(n-2): a higher power of t would exceed the total-degree bound p. The right is the corresponding s coefficient. Therefore Q(F,G_1,...,G_(n-2),E)=Q(E,G_1,...,G_(n-2),F). Effective differences and real linearity give all transpositions. There is no exchange to prove at n=1.

This local degree is not a degree homomorphism on global CH_0(Y). For the DVR Y=Spec k[[t]], Q((t))=1 while [(t)] is the principal, hence zero, Chow class. Our definition works on the proper supported thickening and retains the one.

For proper primary refinements and finite normalizations use Spec R with delta(P)=dim R/P. Local catenarity gives the unit drops across immediate specializations; excellence supplies universal catenarity for Stacks 02QL. Extend delta to finite type models by residue transcendence degrees. Each top component has delta-dimension n. The pullback of an exceptional Cartier equation is regular: on every reduced dominating component it is nonzero at the generic point; regularity follows by injection into the product of component domains. Smaller components have exceptional fibre dimension <=dim R/P-1<p and cannot meet a dimension-p generic point as components through it. At that point the model local ring B is reduced one-dimensional. The injection B -> direct sum_(minimal P) B/P has a finite-length cokernel T, since it is an isomorphism at the minimal primes. Multiplication by a regular equation g and the kernel/cokernel sequence, with l(T[g])=l(T/gT), give

    l(B/gB)=sum_P l((B/P)/g(B/P)).

Thus the top divisor cycle equals the sum of divisor cycles on the integral top components, whose generic weights are one. On each such component Stacks 0AYA 42.26.3 applies to the proper dominant map, equal delta-dimension, and the pulled-back Cartier meromorphic section. The function-field degree is one. BEFORE any Chow quotient this proves

    f_*[f^*E]_p=[E]_p.                                   (P)

Apply 0BEL 33.45.7 to the proper k-schemes consisting of each closed-fibre prime and its image. This formula includes residue degrees and gives zero if the image dimension is <p. Formula (C) and (P) yield

    Q_Y'(f^*F_1,...,f^*F_n)=Q_Y(F_1,...,F_n).             (PI)

Extend from effective last slots by differences. No use of global Chow rational equivalence is made to preserve local degree.

For the raw ideal diagonal set D_I=-E_I. Its Rees algebra is finite standard graded over Noetherian R and O(D_I)=O(1). Complete Stacks 0AG7 30.14.3 with the module equal to this algebra gives H^0(Y,O(qD_I))=I^q for all large q. Complete 0B5S 30.16.1(4) gives simultaneous higher vanishing for q,q-1. The actual Cartier sequence

    0 -> O(qD_I) -> O((q-1)D_I)
       -> O_EI((q-1)D_I) -> 0

gives l_R(I^(q-1)/I^q)=chi_R(E_I,O_EI((q-1)D_I)). Its degree-p coefficient on the left is e(I)/p!, and on the right is Q(D_I^[p],E_I)/p!, by substituting the sum of p separately labeled variables. Thus Q(D_I^[p],E_I)=e(I), or -Q(D_I^[n])=e(I), including p=0.

For I_1,...,I_n use a common product blowup. The product prod I_j^t_j has negative divisor sum t_j D_Ij. Formula (PI) permits its diagonal identity on that model. If H(t) is the degree-n multigraded length form, the product's Samuel multiplicity is n!H(t), by replacing all exponents with qt_j. Hence -Q((sum t_j D_Ij)^[n])=n!H(t) for every positive integral t. Equality on this grid is polynomial identity. Its t_1...t_n coefficients give

    -Q(D_I1,...,D_In)=e(I_1,...,I_n).                     (I)

Units give zero slots. No normality, Cohen--Macaulayness, infinite residue field, or parameter-colength formula was used in this geometry.
