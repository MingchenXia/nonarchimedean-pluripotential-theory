## n3. Positive moment measures and all bounded nets

For rational inputs u_i=-v(I_i)/q_i, i=1,...,p, define B_u(v(J))=e(I_1,...,I_p,J)/prod q_i. At p=0 the denominator is one and this is e(J). Extend to rational ideal differences. Every rational linear combination of gauges is representable as (v(J)-v(K))/d, by synchronized denominators and products, with unit-or-primary J,K. If two such representations agree, then v(J^d' (K')^d)=v(K^d' (J')^d) everywhere. Proper products have equal integral closures by n2; equality with a unit forces both products to be units. Their mixed moment laws prove equality of the proposed values. Nonnegativity of a difference gives inclusion in the closure of the opposite ideal and thus functional positivity by mixed monotonicity. This proves test relation independence on every support, as well as input independence.

Since 1=v(a), positivity on r+f and r-f for rational r>||f|| gives |B(f)|<=||f|| B(1). G331's separating rational lattice is uniformly dense in the REAL C(X); no irrational ideal expression is needed. Its continuous extension is real-linear by rational scalar limits and positive: approximate h>=0 by f_j in the lattice and use f_j+r_j>=0, with rational r_j>||h-f_j|| tending to zero. Compact-Hausdorff Riesz gives the unique finite positive Radon measure mu_u with mass B(1)=e(I_1,...,I_p,a)/prod q_i. This uses neither metrizability nor measure order.

Write E_J=e(a^[p],J). If every input is in [-M,0], comparison with -M=-M v(a) gives

    0<=B_u(v(J))<=M^p E_J,
    mu_u(X)<=M^p e(a).

Real M is obtained by decreasing rational upper bounds. For two tuples in this range, ||u_i-w_i||<=epsilon gives u_i-epsilon<=w_i and conversely. The legitimate negative shift is an input in the same cone. Multilinearity expands its moment into the unshifted term and one term for every nonempty subset of shifted slots. A subset of size r contributes <=epsilon^r M^(p-r) E_J. Therefore

    |B_u(v(J))-B_w(v(J))|
      <=Delta_p(M,epsilon) E_J,
    Delta_p(M,epsilon)=(M+epsilon)^p-M^p.                 (U)

Use rational upper errors when coefficients are initially rational; Delta_0=0 and M^0=1, including M=0.

Approximate any C tuple uniformly by rational profiles. One common bound makes all ideal moments Cauchy by (U). Their limits define a positive bounded lattice functional and hence a measure. Different approximations give the same limits. Symmetry, addition and real nonnegative homogeneity pass from rational tuples. If u<=w, approximation errors eta give u_app-2eta<=w_app; (U) removes the shift and proves moment order. Thus all these laws and (U) hold on C. For any continuous test, first approximate by the dense lattice and bound the two residual integrals by its sup error times the common mass bound. Consequently moment convergence implies weak convergence, also for uniformly convergent nets.

For bounded u_i in D take ANY actual decreasing C representations g_(i,alpha_i) down to u_i. Since g>=u>=-M and g<=0, they already lie in [-M,0]. On the finite product directed set the ideal moments increase and have a finite supremum. Differences of their limits define the positive bounded lattice functional: each defining approximant is positive on nonnegative lattice tests and has the common norm bound. Riesz and the preceding density estimate give the measure and weak convergence.

Compare another representation f_beta. For fixed beta and epsilon>0 the continuous strict open sets {g_(i,alpha)<f_(i,beta)+epsilon} form directed covers of compact X. Select one alpha for each slot valid everywhere. Moment order and (U) give

    B_fbeta(v(J))<=B_(g_alpha-epsilon)(v(J))
      <=sup_alpha B_galpha(v(J))+Delta_p(M,epsilon)E_J.

Send epsilon to zero, then take sup_beta, and interchange representations. This proves full representation independence. Applying the same cover argument to any C majorant h>=u, and observing that the representing terms themselves are majorants, gives the exact formula

    B_u(v(J))=sup { B_h(v(J)): h_i in C, h_i>=u_i }.      (M)

All these majorants are in [-M,0]. The majorant set for a larger tuple is smaller, proving ideal-moment order on bounded D. This is not an order of measures: nonnegative ideal tests do not exhaust all nonnegative continuous tests for comparisons between measures.

Addition uses actual finite product representations: g_alpha+f_beta decreases to u+w, with known D membership from G331, and at each index C additivity holds. Bounded scalar limits prove additivity, symmetry and nonnegative real scaling on D. Zero scaling is the actual zero function. Consequently the negative shift expansion and (U) hold on the entire established bounded D domain. Uniform convergence inside this domain, with its eventual common bound, gives weak convergence by the lattice and mass argument. No increasing-limit membership is asserted.

Finally let u_(i,lambda) decrease in bounded D with common [-M,0] bound to a SPECIFIED u_i in D. Moment order bounds sup_lambda B_u_lambda by B_u. Fix a C majorant tuple h and epsilon>0. The sets {u_(i,lambda)<h_i+epsilon} are open because D functions are upper semicontinuous; they are increasing directed covers since their pointwise limits are <=h_i. Compactness selects one common lambda. Thus

    B_h(v(J))<=B_(u_lambda-epsilon)(v(J))
      <=B_u_lambda(v(J))+Delta_p(M,epsilon)E_J.

Taking sup_lambda, sending epsilon to zero, and taking the majorant supremum (M) proves exact target moment convergence. Common mass and lattice density give weak convergence for every continuous test. Limit membership is essential to this statement; neither arbitrary-infimum closure nor nested-net flattening was used. The entire construction is uniquely forced by the rational moments and these two continuities.
