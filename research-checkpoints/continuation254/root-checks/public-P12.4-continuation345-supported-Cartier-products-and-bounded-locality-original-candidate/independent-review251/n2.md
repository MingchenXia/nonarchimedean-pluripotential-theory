## n2. Algebraic reconstruction

For a finite R-module M and proper primary labels I_1,...,I_r, use SH 17.4.2 at this Noetherian local ring: l(M/(prod I_j^s_j)M) is eventually a polynomial of degree dim M. Define its degree-n form H_M, with coefficients e(I_1^[d_1],...,I_r^[d_r];M)/(prod d_j!), |d|=n. This is the ambient-ring-degree convention of 17.4.3: H_M=0 if dim M<n. In n separately labeled slots e(I_1,...,I_n) is the coefficient of s_1...s_n. These definitions also apply on the possibly nonreduced auxiliary rings below, always using their current dimension. A unit variable is absent; any mixed coefficient using it is zero.

Unused variables are not removed by evaluating an eventual formula at exponent zero. Fix an unused B exponent at a large c. With another primary label K choose K^N subset B^c. For the remaining product P_s, P_s K^N subset B^c P_s subset P_s. Consequently the middle length is squeezed between two eventual polynomials whose degree-n forms are the same, since an exponent shift is constant. On each positive rational ray, after clearing its denominator, the difference divided by the ray parameter^n tends to zero. Thus the middle degree-n form is the form of the remaining labels. In the full polynomial the terms that retain degree n in the other variables have B exponent zero. Equality on all positive rational rays is polynomial identity, proving unused-label deletion. If every label is unused the positive-degree coefficient vanishes.

Permutation of variables proves symmetry. Replacing the variable for I by l times itself proves scaling by l for one mixed entry I^l. To split IJ introduce separate I,J variables and substitute both by one variable s. In the coefficient s s_2...s_n, the I,J degrees have sum one, so only (1,0) and (0,1) occur. Deleting their unused labels proves e(IJ,K_2,...,K_n)=e(I,K_2,...,K_n)+e(J,K_2,...,K_n). Iteration handles every product and power, including unit factors. Repeated equal labels are handled by replacing their separate variables by their sum in the polynomial for the merged label, which gives exactly the factorial convention.

Integral-closure invariance is equality, not coefficientwise order. If I subset J and J is integral over I, the finitely many generators of Jt satisfy homogeneous integral equations over R[It]. Hence R[Jt] is a finite graded module over R[It]. Homogeneous generators in degrees <=c imply J^(m+c) subset I^m for every m>=1. For m>c and any remaining product P_s,

    I^m P_s subset J^m P_s subset I^(m-c) P_s.

The outer length polynomials have the same highest form. The rational-ray squeeze proves equality of the middle highest form, hence equality of all mixed coefficients. Equal closures reduce to this twice through I+J; every generator of I+J is integral over either ideal. No implication from a nonnegative polynomial to coefficient order is used: (s-t)^2 already disproves such an implication.

For monotonicity compare J subset I with the SAME other labels K_2,...,K_n. When necessary set T=R[z]_(mR[z]). This local extension is faithfully flat, with residue k(z). A finite-length composition series tensors exactly to a composition series with residue factors k(z), preserving length, including all the length functions in use. Its dimension is n: a system of n parameters extends to an mT-primary ideal, giving dim T<=n by the height theorem, and flat going-down lifts an n-chain below mT, giving dim T>=n. Thus the polynomial coefficients do not change. There is no assertion about boundary extension to T.

Over the infinite residue field, SH 17.2.2 gives a complement of finitely many proper linear subspaces in K_n/mK_n for each compared family, with the selected label relabeled first. Intersect both complements and avoid the current minimal primes. This avoidance remains possible in nonpure quotients: if K_n subset P+mK_n, Nakayama on K_n/(K_n intersect P) would give K_n subset P, impossible for a primary ideal and a minimal P below the maximal ideal in positive dimension. Choose ONE x for BOTH families and quotient by it. If the current dimension is d>0, then dim R/(x)=d-1. Indeed any quotient chain can be extended strictly below its first prime to a minimal prime avoiding x, giving the upper bound; a parameter system of the quotient together with x gives the lower bound by height. No equidimensionality or CM assumption enters this step.

Here is the colon calculation explicitly. Write P_s=I_1^s_1...I_r^s_r with x in I_1. A submodule direct sum of intersections inside the finite module over R[I_1t_1,...,I_rt_r] is finitely generated. Taking maximum generator multidegrees yields multi-ideal Artin--Rees, SH 17.1.6. Applying it to xR intersect P_s gives

    (P_s:x) subset (0:x)+P_(s-e)

for one fixed multivector e and large s. Because all radicals are m, some I_1^h is contained in I_1^c prod_(j>1) I_j^e_j. Hence for large s the remainder P_(s-e) lies in I_1^c prod_(j>1) I_j^s_j, the truncated superficial range. Intersecting with the colon there and using superficiality gives

    (P_s:x)=(0:x)+P_(s-e_1).

For large fixed other exponents, (0:x) intersect P_(s-e_1) is zero: an element in the annihilator and the truncated range lies, by superficiality at every arbitrarily large first exponent, in I_1^N R for all N; Krull intersection makes it zero. This is the radical and separation content of SH 17.2.4, not a regular-element claim. Thus (0:x) injects into a finite primary quotient and has finite length. Multiplication by x in the four-term exact sequence yields

    l(R/P_s)-l(R/P_(s-e_1))
       =l(R/(x,P_s))-l(0:x).

For d>1 the annihilator is a constant term, so comparing degree d-1 gives exactly SH 17.4.6. Choose the selected K_n exponent one in the mixed coefficient; its removal gives the mixed coefficient in R/(x), with the unused K_n variable deleted by the preceding argument. Repeat in K_(n-1),...,K_2, using simultaneous choices in each common quotient. Primary images stay proper primary. Embedded primes and nilpotents are allowed. The final one-dimensional ring R' gives e_R'(JR') and e_R'(IR'); inclusion of their powers and the one-variable length leading coefficients give e_R'(JR')>=e_R'(IR'). This proves every-slot mixed monotonicity by symmetry, and positivity for all proper entries by the positive one-dimensional Samuel leading coefficient. For n=1 the last argument is direct; units yield zero. Combined with closure invariance this also gives monotonicity for closure inclusions.

For a short exact sequence M_1 -> M_2 -> M_3, use multi-ideal Artin--Rees on P_s M_2 intersect M_1. It lies between P_s M_1 and P_(s-c) M_1. Their length highest forms agree by the same ray squeeze. The exact sequence of the actual induced quotients, rather than a false injection modulo P_s, now gives H_M2=H_M1+H_M3. This reconstructs SH 17.4.4 and gives zero contribution for modules of dimension below n.

G331's primary valuative criterion applies on ALL X: v(J)>=v(K) iff J subset bar(K). A positive-dimensional local reduced R has no zero-dimensional minimal component, so the extra zero-image condition in its componentwise criterion is vacuous here. A unit comparison is direct. Equal rational profiles -v(I)/q=-v(I')/q' give equal closures of I^q' and (I')^q. The proved power laws give identical mixed inputs. Profile inequalities give the correct reversed order of positive ideal moments. This criterion is used on R, not on an unexamined quotient boundary.

SH 17.4.7's literal colon length is NOT used. At the final step it must mean

    l(0 :_(M/(x_1,...,x_(d-1))M) x_d)
      =l(((x_1,...,x_(d-1))M :_M x_d)/(x_1,...,x_(d-1))M).

The unquotiented printed module can have infinite length: in k[[x,y]], (x):y=(x). Our source pixels on PDF 370, printed 356, show the omitted quotient in the literal printing. Repeated 17.4.6 above supplies the finite valid correction.
