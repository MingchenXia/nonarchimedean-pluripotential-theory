# n3. Positive geometric core measure, exact bridge, and bounded locality
Supports: G331, B341, n1, n2.

Normalize a primary model $Y$ finitely to $N$, retaining every top normal component. For its exceptional integral prime divisors $Z$ of dimension $p$, set $b_Z=\operatorname{ord}_Z(\mathfrak a)>0$ and $v_Z=\operatorname{ord}_Z/b_Z$ on $R$, with that component's prime support. For actual negative effective real curve-nef core divisors $D_i$, **define geometrically first**
$$\mu_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_p)=\sum_Z b_Z(\mathcal O(D_1)|_Z\cdots\mathcal O(D_p)|_Z\cdot Z)\delta_{v_Z}.\tag{6}$$
For $p=0$ the parenthesis is the residue-field degree of the point. There are finitely many exceptional primes. The weights are nonnegative: the restricted bundles are curve-nef on the projective integral residue-field scheme $Z$. On the **original actual primary model $Y$**, G331's correctly qualified real-nef-plus-ample and finite-span rationalization produce negative rational ample $L_{i,j}\to D_i$ in Cartier coefficient space. Their finite normalization pullbacks are ample by Stacks29.38.7(2), since a finite map is affine and thus quasi-affine; restrictions to the closed $Z$ remain ample. Clear denominators and apply Stacks33.45.9. Polynomial continuity on this finite span yields nonnegative weights at the limit. Thus no new Keeler theorem on an ungranted model or nonnormal Weil substitute is assumed, and no ideal-moment positivity defines the weights.

An explicit refinement lemma is needed before testing on larger models. A proper birational map of normal top components carries the dimension-$p$ exceptional primes either to smaller-dimensional strata, whose pulled-back $p$-bundle intersection is zero by33.45.7, or to an exceptional prime $Z$. In the latter case localize the base at $\eta_Z$, a DVR. Apply (3) to a uniformizer on a neighbourhood: the sum of positive integer orders times residue degrees over $Z$ is one. Thus there is exactly one dominating prime, its order and residue degree are one, and its DVR valuation equals that of $Z$. To see the last assertion directly, both DVRs have the same fraction field and the second dominates the first. Writing every field element as a uniformizer power times a unit in the first forces identical orders. The numerical projection formula preserves its intersection weight and $b_Z$. Normalizations of common primary refinements map to $N$ by the normalization universal property, componentwise. Therefore (6) is unchanged as a measure on the full boundary under every primary refinement. This fills the refinement step before any algebraic identification.

For an exceptional Cartier test $F$ determined on the same model, $H_F(v_Z)=\operatorname{ord}_Z(F)/b_Z$. The normal divisor cycle and (1),(4) give
$$\int H_F\,d\mu_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_p)=Q_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_p,F).\tag{7}$$
For tests determined only on a common larger primary model, the refinement lemma reduces to this formula there.

We now prove the bridge for real cores, rather than infer intersection convergence from function convergence. On a fixed common $Y$, let $T=H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$. G331 supplies a fixed $h$ with $\mathfrak m^h(T/R)=0$, and the **primary** $\mathfrak c_0=\mathfrak m^{h+1}$ satisfies $\mathfrak c_0T\subset\mathfrak m$ as elements of $R$. For a negative rational ample exceptional $L$, sufficiently divisible large $q$ makes $\mathcal O_Y(qL)$ globally generated. Put $J_q=\mathfrak c_0H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(qL))\subset R$. This is a proper ideal, unit off the closed point by localization of sections, hence primary. Fix **one** refinement $Y'=\operatorname{Bl}_{I\mathfrak c_0}R$, and let $D_0$ be the pulled-back negative divisor of $\mathfrak c_0$. Global generation is a surjection onto an actual invertible subideal of $\mathcal O_Y$. Right exact pullback, then multiplication by the invertible image of $\mathfrak c_0$, gives the actual image equality
$$J_q\mathcal O_{Y'}=\mathcal O_{Y'}(qf^*L+D_0).\tag{8}$$
The inclusion into $\mathcal O_{Y'}$ remains injective because its Cartier local equation is nonzero on every reduced generic component. Thus (8) is stronger than equality of valuations. The blowup universal property gives $Y'\to\operatorname{Bl}_{J_q}R$. For simultaneous $L_i,q_i$, (4),(5),(8) yield
$$\frac{e_R(J_{1,q_1},\ldots,J_{n,q_n})}{\prod q_i}=-Q_{Y'}(f^*L_1+D_0/q_1,\ldots,f^*L_n+D_0/q_n).\tag{9}$$
All intersections are on this one model. Evaluations satisfy $-v(J_q)/q=H_L-v(\mathfrak c_0)/q$, uniformly since $\mathfrak c_0$ is primary.

For every actual negative effective real nef $D_i$ on $Y$, G331's finite-span perturbation and upward rational rounding give negative rational ample $L_{i,j}\to D_i$ in coefficient space. Their evaluations converge uniformly. Use the same $\mathfrak c_0,Y'$ for all $i,j$, choosing divisible $q_{i,j}\to\infty$ with global generation. Equation (9) and polynomial intersection continuity converge to $-Q_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_n)$; the uniformly approximating profiles converge to $H_{D_i}$. B341's uniform moment continuity and continuous test pairing give the same limit on the algebraic side. Therefore the algebraic bounded form on core tuples is $-Q$, and its measure satisfies (7).

Every exceptional integral Cartier test $F$ is a difference of two negative ample exceptional divisors: choose $N$ so $F-NE_I$ is negative ample, and subtract $-NE_I$. Extend by real linearity and use common primary refinements. In particular every primary ideal test is covered. Dense ideal-difference tests and finite mass identify (6) with B341's measure on every core tuple. This proves actual geometric presentation and primary-model independence, not just algebraic presentation independence.

Here is strict maximum locality on rational cores. Synchronize denominators and write $u=-v(A)/q$, $w=-v(B)/q$. On a common normalized primary model make $A,B,A+B$ and the remaining ideals invertible. Divide their image ideals by $S=(A+B)\mathcal O_N$ to obtain actual invertible subideals $L=A\mathcal O_N/S$, $M=B\mathcal O_N/S$, with $L+M=\mathcal O_N$. At an atom $Z$ with $u(v_Z)>w(v_Z)$, $\operatorname{ord}_Z(M)>0$. The map $M|_Z\to\mathcal O_Z$ vanishes generically and therefore everywhere, since $Z$ is integral and this is a line-bundle morphism. Restricting $L+M=\mathcal O_N$ gives $L|_Z\simeq\mathcal O_Z$. Hence $D_A|_Z$ and $D_{A+B}|_Z$ have the same bundle class and intersection weight with every fixed other input. On this common atomic support
$$\mu(\max(u,w),u_2,\ldots,u_p)|_{\{u>w\}}=\mu(u,u_2,\ldots,u_p)|_{\{u>w\}}.\tag{10}$$
Unit/zero cases are included in the subideal argument. There is no regularity or SNC assumption on closed points of $N$.

Uniform rational approximation proves (10) for continuous $u,w$ and continuous other slots: a continuous test supported on compact $K\subset\{u>w\}$ has a uniform positive gap there; apply (10) to approximants and pass weakly to the limit. Actual bounded decreasing representations then extend the other slots to bounded $D$, keeping the open set fixed. Compact regularity and Urysohn functions supported inside an open set identify Radon restrictions.

We need uniform weak continuity on bounded $D$ as well. B341's scalar order and additive negative-constant expansion give, if entries are in $[-M,0]$ and coordinatewise distance at most $\varepsilon$,
$$|B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))-B_{\boldsymbol w}(v(J))|\le[(M+\varepsilon)^p-M^p]e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J).\tag{11}$$
Both comparisons use $u_i-\varepsilon\le w_i$; expanding the shifts bounds every term with $r$ constant slots by $\varepsilon^r M^{p-r}e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J)$. Dense tests and the common mass bound extend this to weak convergence. This asserts continuity inside the established domain, not increasing-limit membership.

Finally suppose bounded $u,w\in D$ agree on open $U$. Take actual decreasing $C$ representations $u_\alpha,w_\beta$, with common bound $[-M,0]$; their terms already dominate the targets, or may be clamped there. For compact $K\subset U$, fixed $\alpha$, and $\varepsilon>0$, the open sets $\{w_\beta-\varepsilon<u_\alpha\}$ cover $K$. Finite subcover and directedness give one $\beta_0$ whose open set contains $K$ and works for every later $\beta$. Continuous strict locality gives equality of the test integrals for $\max(u_\alpha,w_\beta-\varepsilon)$ and $u_\alpha$. First take the bounded decreasing $\beta$ limit, whose specified domain limit is $\max(u_\alpha,w-\varepsilon)$, then the $\alpha$ limit to $\max(u,w-\varepsilon)$. These are two justified limits, not a flattening assertion. Reverse the roles. The two maxima are within $\varepsilon$ uniformly of the already known $D$ function $\max(u,w)$. Equation (11) gives the common limit, proving equality of the measures on $U$. Replacing slots successively proves full bounded mixed ordinary open locality. The $p=0$ case is equality of the fixed reference measure.
