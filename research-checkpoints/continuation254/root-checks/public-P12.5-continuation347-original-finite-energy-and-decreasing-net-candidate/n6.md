# n6. Unbounded pairing, variations, capacity, and ordinary locality
Supports: G331, B341, n1, n3, n4, n5.

For $v\in E^1$, take an actual decreasing $C$ representation $g_\alpha\downarrow v$. Each $g_\alpha$ is bounded and majorizes $v$, so its size is at most $A(v)$. Equation (15) against the **fixed** finite-energy measure gives
$$\int(-v)\,d\mu_E(\boldsymbol u)=\sup_\alpha\int(-g_\alpha)\,d\mu_E(\boldsymbol u).$$
For fixed $g_\alpha$, continuous-test weak convergence in n5 identifies its integral with $\sup_{\boldsymbol k}F(g_\alpha,\boldsymbol u^{\boldsymbol k})$. These nonnegative scalar suprema commute. Lemma (21) applies to the product net $(g_\alpha,\boldsymbol u^{\boldsymbol k})$ of individually bounded functions with finite-energy targets. Hence
$$\int(-v)\,d\mu_E(u_1,\ldots,u_p)=F_\infty(v,u_1,\ldots,u_p)\le A(v)\prod_iA(u_i)<\infty.\tag{24}$$
This supplies actual integrability for discontinuous or unbounded tests. It does not use weak convergence against such a test. Every such mixed measure gives zero mass to the pole set of **every** $v\in E^1$, since otherwise (24) would be infinite. In particular
$$E(u)=\frac1n\int u\,d\mu_E(u^{[p]}).\tag{25}$$

There is also the fully extended test identity for every $v\in D$, with the other entries in $E^1$: its bounded truncations $v^k$ are in $E^1$, so (24) applies to each. Ordinary nonnegative sequence integration gives $\int(-v)=\sup_k\int(-v^k)$. By the defining cutoff formula (18), the suprema over $k$ and the other entries' cutoffs commute and are cofinal in the complete product cutoff set. Hence $\int(-v)\,d\mu_E(\boldsymbol u)=F_\infty(v,\boldsymbol u)$ in $[0,\infty]$. Finiteness and no pole mass are asserted only when the test is $E^1$ (or its displayed mixed value happens to be finite).

For $u,v\in E^1$, finite polynomial multilinearity gives, for $t\ge0$,
$$\frac{d}{dt}E(u+tv)=-F_\infty(v,(u+tv)^{[p]})=\int v\,d\mu_E((u+tv)^{[p]}).\tag{26}$$
At zero this is a one-sided derivative. On an admissible segment with direction $v-u$, cone differences give the same polynomial derivative, with absolute integrability by (24) applied to each term. Its second derivative is nonpositive by (16), so $E$ is concave on the whole finite-energy cone. No variation outside the class is asserted.

For a fixed finite-energy reference $w$, define $E_w(u)=E(u)-E(w)$. Telescoping the $n$-form gives the full finite reference-cocycle formula
$$E_w(u)=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{p}\int(u-w)\,d\mu_E(u^{[j]},w^{[p-j]}).\tag{27}$$
Each term is absolutely integrable by (24) separately for $u,w$. Consequently $E_w(u)+E_z(w)=E_z(u)$. In particular $E_0=E$, $E(0)=0$, $E(-1)=-m_{\mathfrak a}/n$, and $\mu_E((-1)^{[p]})(X)=m_{\mathfrak a}$; zero entries annihilate positive-arity measures and forms as dictated by multilinearity. The $n=1$ operator has no potential entries, so that annihilation statement has no zero-arity analogue.

For Borel $A$ define the elementary capacity
$$\operatorname{Cap}_{\mathfrak a}(A)=\sup\{\mu(\phi^{[p]})(A):\phi\in D^{\mathrm{bd}},\ -1\le\phi\le0\}.$$
It is monotone and countably subadditive by the same properties for each measure and $\sup_\phi\sum_j\le\sum_j\sup_\phi$. Its total value is at most $m_{\mathfrak a}$. For $u\in E^1$, $t>0$, (24) and $A(\phi)\le\delta_{\mathfrak a}$ give
$$\operatorname{Cap}_{\mathfrak a}(\{u<-t\})\le A(u)\delta_{\mathfrak a}^{p}/t.\tag{28}$$
Thus every $E^1$ pole set has this capacity zero. This estimate alone is not a Choquet theorem or a quasicontinuity theorem. No capacity regularity, equation uniqueness, or measure order is inferred from scalar moment order.

If two finite-energy tuples agree entrywise on an open $U$, their corresponding constant truncations agree there. N3 gives equality of their bounded mixed restrictions. A continuous test supported on compact $K\subset U$ passes to the finite-energy weak limit of n5. Urysohn functions and Radon regularity determine restrictions, proving ordinary open locality for $\mu_E$. We do not assert a nonpluripolar cutoff identity on $\{u>-k\}$: for upper semicontinuous $u$ that strict Borel set need not be open, and neither the proved locality nor the capacity estimate establishes such an identity. The constructed calculus uses bounded decreasing extension; it makes no claim to an unproved Borel/plurifine extension convention.
