# n2. Independent supported Cartier intersection
Supports: G331, B341, n1.

Write $Y=\operatorname{Bl}_I R$ for an actual primary blowup. It is reduced, projective, is the identity away from $\mathfrak m$, and every component dominates its reduced component of $R$. Its effective exceptional Cartier divisor $E_I$ has $\mathcal O_Y(-E_I)=\mathcal O_Y(1)$ ample. These facts follow also from whole Stacks31.33: its charts are $R[I/f]\subset R_f$, and $f$ is regular on its chart. Common product-ideal blowups dominate any finite collection. No arbitrary proper-model cofinality is needed.

For an effective exceptional integral Cartier divisor $E$, a power of $\mathfrak m$ kills $\mathcal O_E$: coherence and a finite affine cover give a common power from closed-fibre support. Its coherent cohomology has finite $R$-length by Stacks30.16. Define
$$\chi_R(E,\mathcal F)=\sum_j(-1)^j\ell_R H^j(E,\mathcal F).$$
For line bundles $L_i$ on $Y$, the function $\chi_R(E,\mathcal O_E\otimes\bigotimes_i L_i^{t_i})$ is a polynomial of total degree at most $p=n-1$. To prove this in mixed characteristic, filter $\mathcal O_E$ by the finitely many powers of $\mathfrak m$. Each successive coherent quotient lives on the proper residue-field scheme defined by $\mathfrak m\mathcal O_E$. Tensoring by a line bundle is exact; Euler additivity and Stacks33.45.1 give a finite sum of field-scheme polynomials. Supports have dimension at most $p$: on each integral component an exceptional regular equation cuts a divisor, and the excellent catenary dimension formula identifies its fibre dimension. No coefficient field is chosen and no nilpotent thickness is discarded.

For integral exceptional $F_1,\ldots,F_p$, define $Q_Y(F_1,\ldots,F_p,E)$ as the coefficient of $t_1\cdots t_p$ in this polynomial with $L_i=\mathcal O(F_i)$. For $p=0$ it is the finite zero-dimensional length. If $Z$ runs over integral reduced dimension-$p$ components of $E$, Stacks33.45.2, applied to each filtration quotient, gives
$$Q_Y(F_1,\ldots,F_p,E)=\sum_Z\ell_{\mathcal O_{Y,\eta_Z}}(\mathcal O_{E,\eta_Z})(\mathcal O(F_1)|_Z\cdots\mathcal O(F_p)|_Z\cdot Z).\tag{1}$$
Indeed localized composition lengths of the filtration add to the indicated length; the residue fields of the thickened and reduced local schemes are the same. Lower-dimensional supports give smaller polynomial degree. A fixed extra line-bundle twist cannot change the leading coefficient, since it shifts a polynomial's arguments by constants.

Polynomial substitution proves additivity in the first slots and their symmetry. The exact sequence
$$0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{E_1}(-E_2)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{E_1+E_2}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{E_2}\longrightarrow0$$
and fixed-twist invariance prove additivity in the effective last slot. Every exceptional integral $F$ is a difference of effective ones, by G331's actual Cartier order unit $E_I$. This extends the last slot independently of decomposition, and then all slots extend to finite real presentations.

Full symmetry requires an actual supported calculation. For effective $E,F$, their two-term locally free Cartier complexes $[\mathcal O(-E)\to\mathcal O]$ and $[\mathcal O(-F)\to\mathcal O]$ have isomorphic tensor total complexes after signed interchange. Their cohomology is supported on $E\cap F$, hence over an Artinian base and of finite cohomological length. Computing its Euler characteristic in the two orders, with any invertible $L$, proves
$$\chi_R(E,L|_E)-\chi_R(E,L(-F)|_E)=\chi_R(F,L|_F)-\chi_R(F,L(-E)|_F).\tag{2}$$
This uses the locally free resolution of each Cartier divisor and Euler additivity; it does not require one divisor to be regular on the other. Take $L=\mathcal O(F)^t\mathcal O(E)^s\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n-2}\mathcal O(G_j)^{r_j}$. Set $t=s=0$ after taking the difference, and extract $r_1\cdots r_{n-2}$. The degree bound $n-1$ leaves only $t r_1\cdots r_{n-2}$ on the left, respectively $s r_1\cdots r_{n-2}$ on the right; higher powers cannot contribute. This exchanges $F$ and $E$. Effective differences and real linearity prove full $n$-slot symmetry. For $n=1$ no exchange is necessary.

Here is the precise projection argument. For $\operatorname{Spec}R$ use the dimension function $\delta(\mathfrak q)=\dim R/\mathfrak q$; catenarity in a local ring makes its drop across an immediate specialization exactly one, also for nonpure $R$. Extend by residue transcendence degree to finite-type models. Excellence supplies universal catenarity, so Stacks Situation42.7.1 and Lemma42.26.3 apply. For a proper map between primary models, or their finite normalization, corresponding top integral components map birationally, with function-field degree one. At a generic dimension-$p$ divisor point the local ring is reduced one-dimensional. Its injection into the finite sum of its minimal-component domain quotients has finite-length cokernel. For multiplication by a regular local Cartier equation, the cokernel's multiplication kernel and quotient have equal length. Consequently the Cartier divisor cycle is the sum of its component divisor cycles, with the top component generic weights one. Stacks42.26.3 therefore yields the **actual cycle** identity
$$f_*[f^*E]_p=[E]_p.\tag{3}$$
Smaller-dimensional components cut only smaller-dimensional cycles. Stacks33.45.7 now applies to the proper residue-field schemes in (1); components with smaller-dimensional image have zero intersection against $p$ pulled-back bundles. Thus
$$Q_{Y'}(f^*F_1,\ldots,f^*F_n)=Q_Y(F_1,\ldots,F_n).\tag{4}$$
This is supported degree, not a degree on global $\mathrm{CH}_0(Y)$. For a DVR the principal divisor $(t)$ has supported degree one although its global Chow class is zero. Formula (3) preserves exactly that degree. Residue extensions, inseparable as well as separable, remain in the field-scheme numerical degrees.

For a primary $I$, apply whole Stacks30.14.3 to the standard Rees algebra $A=\bigoplus I^q$ and $M=A$. It gives, as actual submodules of global rational sections, $H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(-qE_I))=I^q$ for large $q$. Stacks30.16.1(4) gives higher-cohomology vanishing. The Cartier exact sequence consequently gives
$$\chi_R(E_I,\mathcal O_{E_I}(-(q-1)E_I))=\ell_R(I^{q-1}/I^q).$$
Their degree-$p$ leading coefficients are $Q_Y((-E_I)^{[p]},E_I)/p!$ and $e_R(I)/p!$. Hence $-Q_Y((-E_I)^{[n]})=e_R(I)$. On a common product blowup put $D_{I_j}=-E_{I_j}$. For positive integers $t_j$ the product ideal has divisor $\sum t_jD_{I_j}$, so diagonal comparison and (4) give an identity of degree-$n$ polynomials. B341's length polynomial shows that the coefficient of $t_1\cdots t_n$ in its multiplicity is $n!e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n)$. The Cartier coefficient is $-n!Q_Y(D_{I_1},\ldots,D_{I_n})$. Therefore
$$-Q_Y(D_{I_1},\ldots,D_{I_n})=e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n).\tag{5}$$
Units give the zero divisor and zero coefficient. No comparison of coefficients of an inequality was used; no CM or infinite-field hypothesis entered this geometric argument.
