This note uses only n1's unconditional quotient geometry, not F_L. It constructs a bounded locally generated ordinary CLD theory and an actual off-canonical-skeleton atom. It does not identify its class with classical curve-test psh, semipositive model PL, weak-smooth psh or a full intrinsic psh cone.

On a deck sheet put x_j=-log|z_j| and q=(x_1²+x_2²)/2. Since q(x+n)-q(x)=n·x+|n|²/2 has d′d″=0, θ=d′d″q descends. Define C_θ to be the continuous real functions u on X such that q+u is locally, on ordinary opens, a locally uniform limit of ordinary CLD smooth functions with middle-positive Hessian. This specified generated class is not defined circularly through the operator constructed below. Its functions are bounded because X is compact.

The ambient cone repair is essential. A symmetric (p,p) coefficient form is a symmetric matrix on Λ^p V, with the usual sign convention. Middle positivity is positive semidefiniteness. Strong positivity is generated by ww^t with w decomposable. For dim V=4, p=2, every w=a∧b satisfies w12w34-w13w24+w14w23=0. The resulting linear functional on symmetric coefficient matrices vanishes on the span of strong squares, but takes value one on (e12+e34)(e12+e34)^t. Thus strong squares do not span all ambient symmetric forms. Analytic dimension two does not establish the contrary assertion for arbitrary-dimensional moment presentations.

For middle positivity a Euclidean positive form λ has λ^p represented by p! times a positive definite identity matrix. On a fixed compact tropical support, every smooth symmetric coefficient matrix B satisfies -Cλ^p≤B≤Cλ^p for some finite C. A smooth nonnegative cutoff equal to one on that support and compactly supported in the SAME ordinary open gives a decomposition into two compactly supported middle-positive forms. A finite moment-chart cover and partition of unity globalize this. The positive supports may be larger than the original support, but stay compact in that open. This reconstructs the applied CLD 1.2.6–13 and 6.5.1–3 arguments; their complete statements and immediate proofs were freshly read.

Let T be a middle-positive current, including symmetry as in CLD 7.2.1. For a positive test form η, φ↦〈T,φη〉 is a positive scalar functional. A cutoff plateau bounds it by the supremum norm on compact sets. Density and Riesz extend it to a Radon measure. For β=η_+-η_-, subtract these measures to obtain μ_(T,β). Equality on smooth scalars proves independence of decomposition and support in supp β. Antisymmetric tests pair to zero. Continuous multiplication bT is therefore defined, with
|〈bT,β〉|≤sup_(supp η_+∪supp η_-) |b| ·〈T,η_++η_-〉.

If positive T_j→T weakly and b_j→b locally uniformly, then b_jT_j→bT. For a fixed β choose smooth g uniformly ε-close to b on the compact union of positive supports and split b_jT_j-bT=(b_j-g)T_j+g(T_j-T)+(g-b)T. The bound controls the first and last terms because 〈T_j,η_++η_-〉 stays bounded. The middle term tends to zero; let ε↓0. This proves the needed multiplication continuity. CLD 7.2.8 and 7.2.11–12 provide the scalar/coefficient measure foundations. The printed strong domination in 7.2.9 is not used.

For closed symmetric T and smooth g, Leibniz with complementary test degrees gives d′d″(gT)=d′d″g∧T. For continuous g the left side is defined by order-zero multiplication. Arbitrary continuous multiplication is not asserted to preserve closedness. Smooth local-potential changes with d′d″g=0 leave recursive products unchanged.

On a sheet write f=q+u, g=q+v and define α_u=θ+d′d″[u] and B(u,v)=d′d″(f d′d″[g]). Local smooth-positive approximants give d′d″g_j→d′d″g, and the preceding lemma gives f_j d′d″g_j→f d′d″g. Differentiating proves convergence of their smooth mixed products. These products are symmetric and positive, so the limit is symmetric, positive and independent of both sequences. The smooth-null rule glues it on ordinary overlaps. These are the bounded recursive arguments of CLD 8.5, 8.7 and 8.8; complete applied statements and immediate proofs were read. CLD 15.7.1–2 equips every topologically separated pure n-dimensional analytic space, with its analytic boundary, with its canonical calibrated tropical level n. Applied to our boundaryless surface, the positive top current is an actual analytic Radon measure.

The identity torus moment chart has saturated lattice Z² and analytic degree one. Consequently θ²=2i_*dx, where dx is probability Lebesgue measure on R²/Z². The RAW reference mass is 2; the factor is 2!, not a probability convention. Canonical integration retains its full generic multiplicities. Only smooth identity and translated identity charts, where degree really is one, are used in the explicit calculations. The deeper CLD/Berkovich integration foundations are cited, not claimed to have been rebuilt completely.

Globally B(u,v)=θ∧α_v+d′d″(uα_v) and θ∧α_v=θ²+d′d″(vθ). Pairing differentiated currents with constant 1 on compact boundaryless X gives B(u,v)(X)=2. Put MA_θ(u)=B(u,u). The same argument proves weak continuity under uniform convergence within C_θ. Dini gives decreasing continuity when the decreasing limit is still continuous. No unbounded decreasing-limit assertion follows.

Convex nondecreasing smooth regularizations of max preserve positive Hessians, so max(u,v)∈C_θ. Ordinary locality and equal mass, for w=max(u,v-ε), give
MA_θ(v){u<v-ε}≤2-MA_θ(u){u>v-ε}=MA_θ(u){u≤v-ε}.
Letting ε↓0 gives bounded comparison MA_θ(v){u<v}≤MA_θ(u){u<v}. This is not full-E¹ comparison or uniqueness.

Now construct the branch atom. Let G(y)=y²/2-y/2+1/12 on [0,1], periodically extended, and u_0=-G(x_1)-G(x_2). Its endpoint slope drop gives G″=dy-δ_0. Positive periodic convolutions of -G have 1+h_ε″≥0 and converge uniformly. Their identity-chart diagonal Hessian squares are 2i_*((dy+h_ε″)⊗(dy+h_ε″)), tending to 2δ_(i(0)). The proved operator continuity yields u_0∈C_θ and MA_θ(u_0)=2δ_(i(0)). Its range is [-1/6,1/12].

On V={|x_j|<1/4}, let N≥1, r_N=|t|^N=e^-N, b_j=log max(1,|z_j|), ℓ_(j,N)=log max(r_N,|z_j-1|), d_(j,N)=ℓ_(j,N)-b_j, and D_N=max(d_(1,N),d_(2,N)). Each d_j≤0 and equals zero whenever |z_j|≠1. Hence D_N is nonzero only in the compact fiber F=τ^-1(0)⊂V. Extend it by zero outside V. The extension is continuous, since F lies compactly inside V and the function is zero near every point with at least one |z_j|≠1. Its negative locus is the ordinary open W={|z_j-1|<1 for both j}. Put U_N=u_0+D_N. On V,
q+U_N=max(ℓ_(1,N)+b_2,b_1+ℓ_(2,N))-(1/2)log|z_1z_2|-1/6.
The final log is smooth pluriharmonic. A truncated log is locally constant near its zero, and elsewhere is a convex maximum of invertible-function logarithms. Ordinary moment charts and smooth convex approximations prove U_N∈C_θ, including points z_j-1=0.

Inside W set w_j=z_j-1. The weight is H_N=log max(r_N,|w_1|,|w_2|)-1/6. Let ξ_N be the Gauss point of the closed w-polydisc with both radii r_N. It is an actual point of W. There |z_j-1|=r_N<1, whereas |z_j-1|=1 at i(0), so ξ_N is off the original Gauss section.

Near ξ_N the w_j are invertible and give a translated identity-torus chart with saturated Z² lattice and degree one. Center logarithmic coordinates at r_N. The regularization ε log(1+exp(y_1/ε)+exp(y_2/ε)) of max(0,y_1,y_2) has gradient a diffeomorphism onto the interior of the simplex with vertices 0,e_1,e_2. Change of variables gives Hessian-determinant integral 1/2. Scaling gives concentration at y=0; the CLD exterior square multiplies by 2!, giving mass one. Its smooth measures lie on the translated Gauss section. Continuity of that section gives convergence against analytic continuous tests, and bounded operator continuity identifies the limit. Away from the vertex, the weight is locally affine or, after a pluriharmonic subtraction, depends on one coordinate, so its Hessian square vanishes. At w_j=0 the inactive truncated log can be omitted locally, again giving rank at most one. Therefore
MA_θ(U_N)|W=δ_(ξ_N), and MA_θ(U_N)(X)=2.

The residual mass one outside W is not identified. This proves an analytic atom, not merely its tropical pushforward.
