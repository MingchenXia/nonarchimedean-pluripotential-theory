\begin{abstract}
For every positive raw-mass-two Radon measure with finite full bounded
dual energy, every normalized bounded maximizing net has convergent
real energies, diagonal Monge--Ampere moments and actual weak
Monge--Ampere measures. The resulting measure is independent of the
maximizing net. Its full dual is exactly the limiting tangent dual,
and the original net maximizes that dual as well. Thus the construction
is an idempotent map of the finite-full-dual measure class, decreasing
dual energy and fixing every actual generated Monge--Ampere measure.
Moments of every fixed generated finite-energy weight also converge.
These results combine the separately qualified universal coercivity
and nonordered estimates; no domination of the prescribed measure is
assumed. Actual analytic representation of the limiting tuple, equality
of the constructed and prescribed measures, and prescribed-measure
existence remain unresolved. Original P16.7 is not declared solved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Actual object and qualified foundations}
Throughout use the actual compact Hausdorff analytic surface
\[
 K=\mathbb C((s)),\quad |s|=e^{-1/8},\quad t=s^8,
 \qquad X=(\mathbb G_m^2)^{\mathrm{an}}_K/
                  \langle(t,2),(3,t)\rangle.
\]
On injective torus charts let $r_i=-\log|z_i|$,
$\rho=|r|^2/2$ and $\theta=\DD\rho$. Affine logarithmic changes
of reference have zero $\DD$. The raw mass is
$\int_X\theta^2=2$. The original continuous class $\C$ includes
\emph{every stage} of the minimal continuous CLD psh sheaf:
$a\in\C$ means $\rho+a$ is in that sheaf locally.
Put $\theta_a=\theta+\DD a$, $\MA(a)=\theta_a^2$ and
\[
 E(a)=\frac13\int_X a\,[\theta^2+
                   \theta\wedge\theta_a+\theta_a^2].
\]
All measures below are actual Radon measures on $X$, not just
tropical images. In particular prescribed support is unrestricted.

The current qualified bounded calculus gives positive gradient
pairings $Q_T$ for positive closed $(1,1)$ currents, linear in $T$,
with Cauchy--Schwarz and the actual Green identity
\begin{equation}\label{green}
 Q_T(f,g)=-\int_X f\,\DD(gT)
         =-\int_X g\,\DD(fT).
\end{equation}
Continuous dpsh functions used here are in its strong-gradient domain;
the displayed differentiated currents are signed Radon when used in
this pairing. Constants, convex combinations and finite maxima
preserve $\C$. All bounded mixed products have raw mass two.
First variation, concavity, monotonicity and the tangent bound are
\begin{align}
 \frac{d}{dq}E((1-q)a+qb)&=\int_X(b-a)\MA((1-q)a+qb),\label{variation}\\
 E(b)&\le E(a)+\int_X(b-a)\MA(a),\label{tangent}\\
 E(0)&=0,\qquad E(a+c)=E(a)+2c.\label{constant}
\end{align}
The span of $\C$ is uniformly dense in $C(X,\R)$. Its qualification
is the actual separation proof in current409: small periodic smooth
weights separate tropical classes, and actual capped Laurent-polynomial
psh functions separate seminorms in one fibre. Constants and the
finite-maximum lattice formula then give lattice approximation.
No countable dense set is presumed.

For $C\ge0$ write
\[
 \C_C=\{a\in\C:\sup_Xa=0,\ E(a)\ge-C\}.
\]
The bounded mixed-moment estimates imply, whenever $p,q\in\C$, $p,q\le0$
have energies at least $-L$,
\begin{equation}\label{gradient}
 Q_{\theta_q}(p,p)\le6L,\qquad
 E^*(\MA(q))=E(q)-\int_Xq\MA(q)\le2L.
\end{equation}
Here
\[
 E^*(\mu)=\sup_{a\in\C}\left(E(a)-\int_Xa\,d\mu\right)
\]
is the \emph{full} original bounded dual. Finite dual for a positive
measure forces mass two by \eqref{constant}. For a measure of that
mass normalization by constants leaves the objective unchanged.

The new results use the complete bounded estimates of current411
and412, recalled precisely next. Their actual Green, Gauss,
finite-grid/point-atom and generated-calculus foundations remain
qualified analytic applications. This paper supplies the new
arguments below in full; it does not claim a new independent
recursive audit of all those foundations or of nonalgebraization.
The immediate CLD/GR applications and their original reading limits
remain attached to current407/409/411/412. A compiler, source identity

\subsection{The two bounded inputs and the ordered generated input}
For $a,b\in\C_C$ define
\[
 I(a,b)=\int_X(a-b)(\MA(b)-\MA(a)).
\]
Green and the cubic energy identity give
\begin{equation}\label{midpoint}
 I(a,b)=Q_{\theta_a}(a-b,a-b)+Q_{\theta_b}(a-b,a-b)\ge0,
 \quad E((a+b)/2)-\frac{E(a)+E(b)}2=\frac{I(a,b)}8.
\end{equation}
Current412 supplies a finite nonnegative nondecreasing modulus
$H_C$, with $H_C(q)\to0$ as $q\downarrow0$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{endpoint}
 \int_X|a-b|\MA(a),\ \int_X|a-b|\MA(b)\le H_C(I(a,b)),
 \quad 0\le E(\max(a,b))-E(a),\ E(\max(a,b))-E(b)
               \le H_C(I(a,b)).
\end{equation}
The modulus comes from fixed finite calibrated Gauss grids and
smooth absolute-value approximations with continuous derivative
multipliers. For every fixed $w\in\C$ there is also a modulus
$J_{C,w}(q)\to0$ at zero for
\begin{equation}\label{fixed}
 \int_X|a-b|\MA(w)\le J_{C,w}(I(a,b)).
\end{equation}
For each actual point $x$ of total graded dimension two, its bounded
actual atom supplies $P_{C,x}(q)\to0$ with
\begin{equation}\label{point}
 |a(x)-b(x)|\le P_{C,x}(I(a,b)).
\end{equation}
These are separate fixed-$w$ and fixed-$x$ bounds; there is no uniform
bound asserted over all points or all tests.

Current411 gives \emph{universal} coercivity. If $B=E^*(\mu)<\infty$
and $a\in\C$ is normalized, then
\begin{equation}\label{coercivity}
 F_\mu(a):=E(a)-\int_Xa\,d\mu
 \le -c+1+B+\sqrt{6Bc},\qquad c=-E(a).
\end{equation}
This uses the actual Gauss bound $\int(-a)\theta^2\le1$ and
quadratic small-scale energy, not domination by bounded MA tests.
In particular $F_\mu(a)\ge-1$ implies
\begin{equation}\label{Cmu}
 -E(a)\le C_\mu:=\frac14
       \left(\sqrt{6B}+\sqrt{10B+8}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
For the scaling proof later, write $A=\int(-a)\theta^2$,
$q_0=Q_\theta(a,a)$, $q_1=Q_{\theta_a}(a,a)$.
All are nonnegative, and
\begin{equation}\label{cubic}
 -E(za)=zA+z^2(1-z/3)q_0+z^3q_1/3,
 \qquad -E(a)=A+2q_0/3+q_1/3.
\end{equation}

The actual generated class $\G$ consists of pointwise
$c_j\downarrow u$, $c_j\in\C$, $\inf_jE(c_j)>-\infty$.
Its actual energies, measures and moving moments are canonical in
current409. A finite-full-dual measure integrates every such $u$;
decreasing presentations converge in its $L^1$, with generated
pole sets null. If $p_j\downarrow u$, $p_j\le0$ and
$E(u)\ge-L$, the ordered estimate, extended by sequential
monotone convergence, is
\begin{equation}\label{ordered}
 \int_X(p_j-u)\MA(w)\le F_L(E(p_j)-E(u)),\qquad
 F_L(d)=3d+\sqrt{12L(9d/2+\sqrt{126Ld})},
\end{equation}
for every nonpositive bounded $w$ with $E(w)\ge-L$.
Here $F_L(d)\to0$ at zero and the constant is uniform over these
$w$. These qualified generated facts are used only for already
generated fixed weights, not to recognize a new nonordered limit.

\section{Uniform bounds and nonordered Cauchy control for every measure}
\begin{theorem}\label{universal}
Let $\mu$ be any positive mass-two Radon measure with
$B=E^*(\mu)<\infty$, and let $(a_\alpha)$ be any normalized net
in $\C$ with $F_\mu(a_\alpha)\to B$. Put
$\varepsilon_\alpha=B-F_\mu(a_\alpha)\ge0$.
On a tail all $a_\alpha\in\C_{C_\mu}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{deficit}
 I(a_\alpha,a_\beta)\le
             4(\varepsilon_\alpha+\varepsilon_\beta).
\end{equation}
There are real limits
\[
 E(a_\alpha)\to e,\qquad
 D_\alpha:=\int_X a_\alpha\MA(a_\alpha)\to D,
 \qquad \int_Xa_\alpha\,d\mu\to\ell=e-B.
\]
The net is Cauchy in $L^1(\MA(w))$ for every fixed bounded $w$,
and at every fixed total-dimension-two point. Its four moving
moving endpoint moments converge to $D$ when both indices tend
along this tail.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $F_\mu(0)=0$, $B\ge0$; a maximizing tail has objective
at least $-1$. Apply \eqref{Cmu}. For two terms on the tail the
midpoint is a full bounded competitor. Its measure term is affine,
so \eqref{midpoint} and its objective bound by $B$ give
$I/8\le(\varepsilon_\alpha+\varepsilon_\beta)/2$.
This proves \eqref{deficit} without any attainment assumption.

Put $C=C_\mu$ and $m=\max(a_\alpha,a_\beta)$.
Both $E(m)-E(a_\alpha)$ and $E(m)-E(a_\beta)$ lie in
$[0,H_C(I)]$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{energycauchy}
 |E(a_\alpha)-E(a_\beta)|\le H_C(I).
\end{equation}
This proves convergence of real energies without $L^1(\mu)$
convergence. Objective convergence then gives the signed integral
limit $\ell=e-B$.

For the diagonal moments set $f=a_\alpha-a_\beta$ and
$T=\theta_{a_\alpha}+\theta_{a_\beta}$.
The actual product transgression is
\[
 \MA(a_\alpha)-\MA(a_\beta)=\DD(fT),
 \qquad Q_T(f,f)=I.
\]
Decompose the diagonal difference as
\[
 D_\alpha-D_\beta=
 \int_X f\MA(a_\alpha)+
 \int_Xa_\beta(\MA(a_\alpha)-\MA(a_\beta)).
\]
The first integral has absolute value at most $H_C(I)$.
For the second, Green, Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{gradient}
give $|Q_T(a_\beta,f)|\le\sqrt{12CI}$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{diagonalcauchy}
 |D_\alpha-D_\beta|\le H_C(I)+\sqrt{12CI}.
\end{equation}
Thus $D_\alpha$ is a real Cauchy net and converges to $D$.
Moreover
\[
 \left|\int_Xa_\alpha\MA(a_\beta)-D_\beta\right|
 \le\int_X|a_\alpha-a_\beta|\MA(a_\beta)\le H_C(I).
\]
Swapping indices gives both cross moments; both diagonal moments
already converge. All four joint-tail limits are $D$.
Finally \eqref{fixed} and \eqref{point} give the asserted Cauchy
properties and actual measurable $L^1$ limits by Banach completeness.
They do not assert that those measurable limits form a psh weight.
\end{proof}

\section{An actual canonical weak measure and its exact full dual}
\begin{theorem}\label{measure}
Under Theorem~\ref{universal}, the actual measures
$\MA(a_\alpha)$ converge weakly against every continuous function
to a positive mass-two Radon measure $\nu$. Its full dual is
\begin{equation}\label{dualnu}
 E^*(\nu)=e-D\le2C_\mu,
 \qquad E^*(\MA(a_\alpha))\longrightarrow E^*(\nu).
\end{equation}
Furthermore
\begin{equation}\label{calibration}
 \int_Xa_\alpha\,d\nu\to D,
 \qquad F_\nu(a_\alpha)\to E^*(\nu).
\end{equation}
All these limits are independent of the normalized maximizing net.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $w\in\C$, subtract its supremum and set
$L=\max(C_\mu,-E(w-\sup w),0)$. Constants disappear because
the endpoint masses agree. Transgression, Green and
\eqref{gradient} yield
\begin{equation}\label{weakbound}
 \left|\int_Xw(\MA(a_\alpha)-\MA(a_\beta))\right|
       \le\sqrt{12L\,I(a_\alpha,a_\beta)}.
\end{equation}
Thus every $\C$-test integral, and every finite linear combination,
is Cauchy. Uniform density of the span and common positive mass two
extend this to each $h\in C(X,\R)$: replacing $h$ uniformly by
a span element incurs total error at most $4\|h-h_0\|_\infty$
in a difference of integrals. The limits give a positive bounded
linear functional on $C(X,\R)$, taking $1$ to two. Compact
Hausdorff Riesz representation gives an actual positive Radon $\nu$.
This proves whole-net weak convergence without extracting a
subsequence or imposing metrizable/countable test hypotheses.

For each \emph{fixed} bounded competitor $b$, \eqref{tangent}
implies
\[
 E(b)-\int_Xb\MA(a_\alpha)\le E(a_\alpha)-D_\alpha.
\]
Pass to the established weak and real limits and take the full
supremum in $b$. It follows that $E^*(\nu)\le e-D$.
The right side is finite and at most $2C_\mu$ by
\eqref{gradient}; integrability of generated weights therefore
precedes their later use.

Fix an index $\alpha$ on the common energy tail and let $\beta$
tend to infinity in \eqref{weakbound} with $w=a_\alpha$.
Here $L=C_\mu$. Weak convergence and \eqref{deficit} give the
quantitative calibration
\begin{equation}\label{quantcal}
 \left|\int_Xa_\alpha\,d\nu-D_\alpha\right|
              \le\sqrt{48C_\mu\varepsilon_\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Together with diagonal convergence this proves
$\int a_\alpha\,d\nu\to D$. Consequently
$F_\nu(a_\alpha)\to e-D$. Each such objective is bounded above
by $E^*(\nu)$, proving the reverse inequality and
\eqref{dualnu}--\eqref{calibration}. Bounded tangent gives
$E^*(\MA(a_\alpha))=E(a_\alpha)-D_\alpha$, so its limit is
the same exact dual value.

For another normalized maximizing family $b_\gamma$ of the
\emph{same} $\mu$, both tails have the same bound $C_\mu$.
The midpoint proof applies to a term of each family, with
$I(a_\alpha,b_\gamma)\le4(\varepsilon_\alpha+
\varepsilon'_\gamma)$. Equations \eqref{energycauchy},
\eqref{diagonalcauchy} and \eqref{weakbound} show equality of
both real limits and all continuous measure-test limits. They also
identify each fixed-test $L^1$ limit and each full-point value limit.
This proves independence without a common cofinal indexing sequence.
\end{proof}

\section{Dual decrease, idempotence and actual generated fixed points}
Let $\mathfrak M$ be all positive mass-two Radon measures with
finite full bounded dual. Maximizing sequences exist from the real
supremum defining the dual. The preceding independence defines
\[
 \mathcal V:\mathfrak M\longrightarrow\mathfrak M,
 \qquad \mathcal V(\mu)=\nu.
\]
\begin{theorem}\label{retract}
For every $\mu\in\mathfrak M$,
\begin{equation}\label{retraction}
 E^*(\mathcal V(\mu))\le E^*(\mu),\qquad
 \mathcal V(\mathcal V(\mu))=\mathcal V(\mu).
\end{equation}
Every actual $\MA(u)$, $u\in\G$, is a fixed point. In particular
all bounded Monge--Ampere measures are fixed points. These conclusions
do not identify $\mathcal V$ with the identity on $\mathfrak M$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Keep a maximizing tail $a_\alpha$, put $C=C_\mu$, and let
$\ell_\alpha=\int_Xa_\alpha\,d\mu$. Formula \eqref{cubic}
gives $q_0\le3C/2$, $q_1\le3C$ and, for $0\le z\le1$,
\[
 \left|\frac{d^2}{dz^2}E(za_\alpha)\right|
      =2[(1-z)q_0+zq_1]\le6C.
\]
First variation at $z=1$ is $D_\alpha$. Taylor's formula with
its integral remainder therefore gives, for $0<h\le1$,
\[
 E((1-h)a_\alpha)\ge E(a_\alpha)-hD_\alpha-3Ch^2.
\]
Scaling preserves $\C$, so this is another full bounded competitor
for $\mu$. Subtracting its measure term gives
\[
 \varepsilon_\alpha\ge
     h(\ell_\alpha-D_\alpha)-3Ch^2.
\]
Pass to limits for fixed $h$ and then let $h\downarrow0$.
It follows that $\ell\le D$. Since
$\ell=e-E^*(\mu)$ and $E^*(\nu)=e-D$, this is exactly
the dual decrease in \eqref{retraction}.

By \eqref{calibration} the same normalized net maximizes the
full dual of $\nu$, and its MA measures already converge to $\nu$.
Theorem~\ref{measure} applied to $\nu$, with independence of
the maximizing family, therefore proves $\mathcal V(\nu)=\nu$.

Finally take an \emph{already generated} $u\in\G$ and an
actual continuous decreasing presentation $c_j\downarrow u$.
The qualified generated calculus gives $\mu=\MA(u)\in\mathfrak M$,
\[
 E^*(\mu)=E(u)-\int_Xu\,d\mu,
 \quad E(c_j)\to E(u),\quad
 \int_Xc_j\,d\mu\to\int_Xu\,d\mu,\quad
 \MA(c_j)\rightharpoonup\mu.
\]
Normalize $b_j=c_j-\sup_Xc_j$. Its objective is unchanged,
so $b_j$ is a normalized maximizing sequence for $\mu$, and its
MA measure is unchanged too. Definition and independence of
$\mathcal V$ give $\mathcal V(\mu)=\mu$.
The $b_j$ need not decrease; no decreasing presentation of a new
nonordered limit is assumed in this argument.
\end{proof}

\section{Convergence of all fixed generated moments}
\begin{theorem}\label{generatedmoment}
For every fixed $u\in\G$ and any maximizing net in
Theorem~\ref{universal},
\begin{equation}\label{gmoment}
 \int_Xu\MA(a_\alpha)\longrightarrow
                      \int_Xu\,d\mathcal V(\mu).
\end{equation}
Every displayed integral is finite. The bound used to pass from
continuous presentations of $u$ is uniform over the maximizing tail.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $c_j\downarrow u$ be an actual presentation and
$M=\sup_Xc_1$, a finite real number. Then $p_j=c_j-M\le0$
decreases to $v=u-M$. Choose
$L\ge\max(C_\mu,-E(v),0)$. The translation rule and energy
monotonicity ensure $E(p_j)\ge E(v)\ge-L$.
The bounded tests $a_\alpha\le0$ also have energies at least
$-L$. Apply \eqref{ordered} to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{uniformpresentation}
 0\le\int_X(c_j-u)\MA(a_\alpha)
       \le F_L(E(c_j)-E(u))\longrightarrow0
\end{equation}
uniformly in $\alpha$ on the tail. The generated dual inequality
against $\MA(a_\alpha)$, using its uniformly bounded finite
full dual, proves that the $u$ integrals are finite; this also
justifies the sequential monotone passage in \eqref{ordered}.
The limit measure $\nu=\mathcal V(\mu)$ has finite full dual
by Theorem~\ref{measure}, so $c_j\to u$ in $L^1(\nu)$.
For fixed $j$, weak convergence gives
$\int c_j\MA(a_\alpha)\to\int c_j\,d\nu$.
First choose $j$ so that both its uniform error in
\eqref{uniformpresentation} and its $L^1(\nu)$ error are small;
then take $\alpha$ large for this fixed continuous $c_j$.
The triangle inequality proves \eqref{gmoment}.
This uses sequential convergence for the fixed presentation only;
it does not invoke measurable monotone convergence for arbitrary nets.
\end{proof}

\section{Precise remaining original gaps}
Universal coercivity and nonordered estimates now give canonical
real-energy, diagonal-moment, full-point-value, fixed-test-$L^1$ and
weak-MA limits for every finite-full-dual prescribed measure.
The canonical measure map is idempotent and fixes actual generated
MA measures. None of these conclusions constructs an actual USC
psh representative for the limiting full-point tuple, or proves
that such a representative is in the original $\G$.

In particular $\nu=\mathcal V(\mu)$ has not been shown equal to
the prescribed $\mu$ for every eligible measure. Equality of
$\mathcal V(\mu)$ and $\mu$ is a necessary condition for a generated
solution by Theorem~\ref{retract}; sufficiency is not asserted.
The signed convergence of $\int a_\alpha\,d\mu$ does not prove
$L^1(\mu)$ convergence of the weights. Nor does convergence of all
fixed generated moments justify an unproved moving unbounded moment
at a new nonordered analytic limit.

Adequate actual representation or a proved replacement, recognition
in the original finite-energy domain, suitable envelope/orthogonality
or variational perturbations, equality with every prescribed measure,
and the full existence-and-uniqueness theory at the original scope
remain open. GR decreasing-net closure applies only after actual
psh/USC representatives are supplied; GR limit statements do not
manufacture the missing upper regularization. Counts remain
