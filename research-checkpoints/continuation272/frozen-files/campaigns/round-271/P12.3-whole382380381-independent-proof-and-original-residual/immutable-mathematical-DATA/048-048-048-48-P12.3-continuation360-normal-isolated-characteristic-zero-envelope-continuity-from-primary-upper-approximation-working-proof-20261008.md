# Continuous geometric envelopes on normal isolated algebraic singularities

ROOT working paper, continuation 360, 2026-10-08. **UNREVIEWED.** Held for a future independent native reconstruction; it is absent from the frozen inputs of the active campaigns 256 and 257. This paper does not close the excellent-germ original P12.3 or increment any accepted-paper count.

## Abstract

Let R be the local ring at a closed point of a normal algebraic variety over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Suppose the point is regular or is an isolated singularity. On the entire normalized semivaluation boundary, including points with nonzero support, the greatest geometric plurisubharmonic minorant of every finite continuous obstacle is continuous. The geometric cone is the actual primary-model Cartier cone of the entire source-qualified G331, defined by curve degrees before comparison with ideals. The argument uses the primary-ideal upper approximation of a Cartier nef envelope in Boucksom–de Fernex–Favre, The volume of an isolated singularity, Theorem 4.11, and its variational characterization in Corollary 2.13. Coefficientwise approximation becomes uniform by testing the obstacle on the finite set of exceptional prime divisors of its determining primary model. Every decreasing-net competitor is controlled at those finitely many divisors through one of its actual continuous majorants. Continuous-function inequalities then extend by divisorial density to all supports. Arbitrary bounded-above extended upper-semicontinuous obstacles have greatest geometric decreasing-net minorants, and envelopes commute with decreasing nets of such obstacles.

## 1. Definitions, grants and exact scope

Fix a proper m-primary ideal a of R. Write V_a(R) for the compact Hausdorff section v(a)=1 of centered semivaluations R -> [0,infinity]. A semivaluation is evaluated on its support quotient; no fraction-field extension of a semivaluation with nonzero support is used. Let C_R be the uniform completion of the geometric core consisting of evaluations of negative effective real Cartier divisors on actual primary blowup models, having nonnegative degree on every proper integral curve contracted to Spec R. Let D_R consist of pointwise limits of ONE downward-directed decreasing net in C_R, including the all-bottom function. These functions are nonpositive. Actual geometric positivity precedes the ideal comparison.

The only previously accepted mathematics used is ENTIRE G331, with its mandatory source, model and support qualifications. In particular it supplies actual primary-model evaluation, the equality of the continuous/decreasing geometric and ideal completions, finite MAX and addition/nonnegative scalar stability, negative constants, compactness, primary-gauge transport, divisorial density on a normal domain at ALL supports, and separation by primary profiles. It supplies no envelope regularity here. No energy theorem, product theorem, surface proof 355 or unreviewed envelope paper is a premise.

For a finite continuous f on V_a(R), define

    P_R(f)(v) = sup { h(v) : h in D_R and h <= f everywhere }.

Negative constants give nonempty competitors. The theorem to prove is that this raw supremum is in C_R and is a greatest D_R minorant. The obstacle can have either sign; cone functions remain nonpositive.

For the algebraic argument assume dim R >= 2 and the marked point is an isolated singularity. At a regular closed point the unit correction described in Section 2 gives the same upper approximation, using ordinary smooth asymptotic multiplier ideals and the same nonzero-W argument. In dimension one, normality makes R a DVR and its boundary a single point; the envelope is min(f,0). Dimension zero has empty boundary. This paper does not claim the singular multiplier-ideal estimates in arbitrary or mixed characteristic, at a nonclosed algebraic point, on an arbitrary nonalgebraic excellent ring, or for a nonisolated singularity.

## 3. Geometric continuous functions give source-nef coefficients

If h belongs to the actual geometric core, it is determined by a negative effective real Cartier divisor on a projective actual primary model. Every curve contracted by that model lies over 0, since the model is an isomorphism elsewhere. Its geometric nonnegative-degree condition is exactly relative Cartier nefness in the source definition. Thus its coefficient b-divisor is X-nef in the source sense, by an actual geometric comparison.

For h in C_R, choose a uniform sequence of such core functions. For every divisorial valuation ord_E centered at 0, normalized by ord_E(a), coefficient convergence follows from uniform convergence. On divisors not centered at 0 all core coefficients are zero. These limits define a Weil b-divisor H: each fixed model has finitely many prime components over 0, and the remaining coefficients are zero. All approximating traces on X are zero, since dim X>=2 and the point has codimension at least two. Lemma 1.12(a) and the closed source nef cone show that H is source-nef.

This comparison is only needed for C_R functions and for their shifts by the actual constant -epsilon. No converse theorem equating all source-nef Weil b-divisors with D_R is assumed. In particular we do not deduce function inequalities for a discontinuous competitor merely from its values on a dense set.

## 4. Dense rational Cartier obstacles on an actual primary model

For an m-primary ideal I put psi_I(v)=-v(I), since v(a)=1, and put psi_R=0. Consider finite rational signed combinations of these profiles, including rational constants by psi_a=-1. They are continuous, may have either sign, and are evaluations of rational Cartier b-divisors over zero. Multiplying by one positive integer clears all coefficients.

They form a rational vector lattice. After clearing denominators, a signed profile is a difference of two ideal profiles, using products and unit ideals. For example

    min(v(A)-v(B), v(C)-v(D))
       = v(AD+CB)-v(BD),

with the same gauge denominator understood. Every ideal involved is unit or m-primary. Negation gives MAX. Primary profiles separate distinct boundary points by G331; rational constants are present. The uniform closure is therefore a real vector lattice containing all constants and separating points, so the lattice Stone–Weierstrass theorem gives uniform density in the finite continuous functions. Irrational coefficients can equivalently be approximated rationally because each fixed primary profile is bounded on the section.

For one such obstacle f, use a common ACTUAL primary normalized blowup pi:Y->X that principalizes all of its finite ideals AND a. For instance their product ideal gives a blowup dominating each individual ideal blowup: the pulled-back product is invertible, hence each factor is locally invertible on the integral model, and normalization preserves this property. This is within the G331 admissible primary system; no regularity or SNC hypothesis on Y is required.

Let C be the rational Cartier b-divisor evaluating to f and determined on Y. Let E_1,...,E_r be the prime divisors of Y over 0. Write

    C_Y = sum_i d_i E_i,
    Z(a)_Y = -sum_i b_i E_i,       b_i=ord_Ei(a)>0.

These are all possible nonzero coefficients of C_Y. The positivity of every b_i follows from the center being 0; principalization ensures the entire closed fiber is the support of the pulled-back boundary ideal. Taking M>0 with f>=-M, the actual constant -M gives the source-nef lower competitor M Z(a)<=C, by divisorial evaluation. The source envelope W=Env_X(C) is consequently well defined, lies over zero, and satisfies

    M Z(a) <= W <= C,       W <= 0.

The last inequality follows from source negativity and W_X=0. It does not assert that an arbitrary pushed-forward trace is nef.

If W=0, then C>=0 on all divisors centered at 0. Divisorial density and continuity of f imply f>=0 on the entire boundary. The actual zero function is then its greatest D_R minorant, and P_R(f)=0. Suppose henceforth W!=0. Clear the rational coefficients by C_0=N C integral. Source homogeneity gives Env_X(C_0)=N W. Applying the qualified nonzero Theorem 4.11 to C_0 and dividing its approximants by N gives

    B_k = q_k Z(b_k) >= W,       B_k -> W coefficientwise,

with q_k>0 rational and b_k genuinely m-primary. Every B_k is an actual globally generated primary-model Cartier divisor, hence its boundary function u_k=-q_k v(b_k) belongs to the geometric core, before completing the cone.

## 5. Finite trace errors yield uniform estimates on the full boundary

Let beta_ki=ord_Ei B_k. Since beta_ki -> ord_Ei W <= d_i, the finite maximum

    eta_k = max_i max(beta_ki-d_i,0)/b_i

tends to zero. Set epsilon_k=eta_k+1/k. The shifted Cartier b-divisor B_k+epsilon_k Z(a) is source-nef. Its trace on Y is <= C_Y, including the zero coefficients on every other prime divisor. Corollary 2.13 and Remark 2.17 therefore give the GLOBAL coefficient inequalities

    B_k + epsilon_k Z(a) <= W <= B_k.                    (5.1)

This is the essential step. It uses the variational envelope of the trace obstacle; it does not pull a possibly non-nef trace back as if it were nef.

On every normalized divisorial valuation centered at 0, (5.1) says

    u_k - epsilon_k <= w <= u_k,

where w is the coefficient function of W on those valuations. Consequently

    |u_k-u_l| <= epsilon_k+epsilon_l

on the divisorial boundary. Both functions in this last inequality are continuous on the ENTIRE boundary. The G331 density theorem extends this inequality to every semivaluation, including nonzero and nonminimal supports and infinite coordinates. Thus u_k is uniformly Cauchy and converges to U in C_R. Its divisorial coefficients are exactly W. Since W<=C, (5.1) also gives u_k<=f+epsilon_k on the divisorial boundary. Continuity extends that inequality to all points and then U<=f everywhere.

All approximating u_k are actual negative primary profiles; no function value for W at a nonzero-support semivaluation was assumed in defining U. Uniform convergence constructs that value, and it is independent of the chosen upper approximants. The finite trace bound is not a finite sampling substitute for divisorial density.

## 6. Control of EVERY decreasing-net competitor at all supports

Let h in D_R satisfy h<=f everywhere. Choose its ONE actual decreasing net h_alpha in C_R, with h=inf_alpha h_alpha. This is the defining representation, not an increasing supremum or a pointwise infimum of unrelated cone functions.

Fix epsilon>0. At the finitely many normalized divisorial points v_i=ord_Ei/b_i, we have h(v_i)<=f(v_i)=d_i/b_i. For each i, decreasing convergence gives an index with h_alpha(v_i)<=f(v_i)+epsilon; directedness gives ONE index alpha satisfying all these inequalities. This also works when h(v_i)=-infinity.

Let H_alpha be the source-nef coefficient b-divisor of the CONTINUOUS function h_alpha, as established in Section 3. Then

    (H_alpha+epsilon Z(a))_Y <= C_Y.

Source Corollary 2.13 gives H_alpha+epsilon Z(a)<=W on all divisors. Therefore h_alpha-epsilon<=U on the divisorial boundary. Both sides are continuous, so the inequality holds on all supports. Since h<=h_alpha,

    h <= U+epsilon everywhere.

Letting epsilon decrease to zero proves h<=U. As U is itself a C_R minorant of f, it is the greatest D_R minorant and P_R(f)=U in C_R. This proof covers unbounded, partial-bottom and all-bottom competitors without asserting that a dense-set inequality automatically controls an upper-semicontinuous function.

## 7. Every finite continuous obstacle

For arbitrary finite continuous f,g the raw envelopes are nonexpansive whenever they are finite: if ||f-g||<=delta, then any minorant h of f has h-delta in D_R and h-delta<=g. Reversing the argument gives

    ||P_R(f)-P_R(g)|| <= ||f-g||.

For the already solved rational profiles these raw envelopes are in C_R. Choose rational profiles f_j uniformly converging to arbitrary f. Their envelopes U_j=P_R(f_j) are uniformly Cauchy, so U=lim U_j is in C_R. The estimate U_j<=f_j gives U<=f. Any D_R minorant h of f satisfies h-||f-f_j||<=f_j, hence h<=U_j+||f-f_j||. Passing to the limit gives h<=U. Thus P_R(f)=U is the greatest geometric D_R minorant and is continuous on the whole boundary.

This extension uses the FULL obstacle lattice, not only obstacles determined by one fixed model. It proves continuity of the actual raw envelope and therefore of its USC regularization.

## 8. Extended USC obstacles and decreasing-net stability

Let phi be bounded above and upper semicontinuous, with values in [-infinity,infinity). Replace it by phi'=min(phi,0), which has the same nonpositive geometric competitors. On a compact Hausdorff space the finite continuous majorants of phi' taking values <=0 are downward directed by ordinary MIN, and their pointwise infimum is phi'. To see the assertion at a point v, choose c with phi'(v)<c<0, a neighborhood on which phi'<c, and a continuous cutoff chi with chi(v)=1 supported in that neighborhood. Then c chi is a finite continuous majorant, with value c at v. This also supplies arbitrarily negative values at a pole. At points phi'=0, the majorant 0 suffices.

For each such continuous majorant f use the already proved C_R envelope P_R(f). Order preservation makes these envelopes ONE decreasing C_R net. Define

    H = inf_{f continuous, phi'<=f<=0} P_R(f).

Then H belongs to D_R and H<=phi'. Every D_R minorant h of phi' is <=P_R(f) for every f, hence <=H. Thus H is the greatest minorant, is upper semicontinuous as an infimum of continuous functions, and equals both the raw and USC-regularized envelope of phi. No general closure under arbitrary infima is used.

If phi_beta decreases as a net of bounded-above extended USC obstacles to phi, monotonicity gives E_R(phi_beta)>=E_R(phi). For any finite continuous majorant f of min(phi,0) and epsilon>0, the open sets

    { min(phi_beta,0) < f+epsilon }

increase and cover the compact boundary. Directedness and a finite subcover give an index after which min(phi_beta,0)<=f+epsilon everywhere. Its greatest D_R minorant is then <=P_R(f+epsilon)<=P_R(f)+epsilon, by the continuous nonexpansivity just proved. Taking the pointwise net infimum, then epsilon down to zero, then the infimum over f, proves

    E_R(phi_beta) decreases to E_R(phi)

at every support point. The limit is in D_R because it is the already constructed E_R(phi); no separate unproved decreasing-net closure theorem is required.
