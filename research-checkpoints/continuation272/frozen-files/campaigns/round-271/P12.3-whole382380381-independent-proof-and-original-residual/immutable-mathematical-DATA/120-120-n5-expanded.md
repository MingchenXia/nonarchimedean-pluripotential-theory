# n5. Actual finite covers and the full conductor envelope rule

Use qualified G331 and n1–n4. Let A be excellent normal local and B a finite reduced algebra containing A. Assume EVERY minimal source component dominates A. This hypothesis excludes a fabricated generic degree on vertical components. On the semilocal source boundary let rho be restriction, retaining all support primes. G331 gives full-fibre positive C/D descent and full-support finite normal-target trace; its generic inseparable multiplicities and quotient-field evaluation qualifications persist.

The fibre maximum M(h)(v)=max_(rho(w)=v) h(w) preserves C and D. To see positivity, work first on a source domain component and normalize it, then take a finite normal closure M of its function-field extension over Frac(A), and the integral closure E of A in M. Its embeddings are automorphisms G and its generic inseparable multiplicity is q. The qualified G331 coefficient trace constructs, from any support-quotient lift w, the measure q sum_(g in G) delta_(g w), and proves its support is the FULL fibre. Therefore each fibre is exactly this finite orbit. This is also the usual integral Galois/radicial transitivity argument, but here it follows from the actual granted full-support trace, including inseparable multiplicity. The construction first kills the support; no valuation of Frac(A) is falsely attached to a nonzero support. The finite maximum of the automorphic pullbacks of h is in C, is fibre constant, and descends positively to A by G331. Across finitely many dominating components take their finite maximum. For an actual decreasing C-net the finite fibre maximum commutes with its pointwise infimum: for any finite upper test of the maximum limit, all finitely many lifts eventually lie below it, and directedness gives one common index. This proves D preservation, including bottom, rather than using an increasing completion rule.

For a base finite continuous obstacle f, fibre maxima of source competitors are base competitors, and base competitors pull back. Therefore p_B(rho^*f)=rho^*p_A(f). If the ACTUAL source has CE, the left side is continuous, and the compact surjective quotient makes p_A(f) continuous. Thus CE transfers through a good finite cover, with every component dominating. No theorem asserts that such a cover exists for every normal singularity. Gauge factors are constant along a fibre and give the exact same transport.

An actual example is A=k[U,V,W]/(UW-V^2) at the vertex, B=k[s,t] at its origin, U=s^2,V=st,W=t^2. The base is normal in every characteristic by the hypersurface S2 and codimension-one regularity test. The extension is finite integral, generically degree two (including inseparable degree in characteristic two), but its closed fibre has basis 1,s,t and length three, so it is not flat of rank two. Adding any number of polynomial variables preserves finiteness, normality and the actual cover. In characteristic zero, with algebraically closed k and closed origin, B is in the exact smooth CE range of n4; hence A, including the nonisolated examples with extra variables, has CE. In characteristic two only the unextended surface follows from n2 unless an independently proved source CE theorem is supplied. Failed Jacobian bounds in n3 therefore do not constitute a normal CE counterexample.

Now let R be any excellent reduced local ring, S its finite normalization with all local normal centres, c its conductor, and rho its compact boundary quotient. Fibres are singleton at finite v(c), and precisely the conductor-quotient fibres at infinity, by G331. The finite-conductor locus is OPEN and dense upstairs: each normal component sees a nonzero conductor element, and its divisorial points have finite value and are dense. The infinity stratum is CLOSED, not removed.

Fix finite continuous f>=-B, clipped by zero. For any source continuous competitor g<=rho^*f, use the PRIMARY masks

b_N(v)=-(B/N)v(c+a^N).

They belong to C, equal -B whenever v(c)>=N (including infinity), and tend to zero wherever v(c) is finite. The source function max(g+rho^*b_N,-B) is in C, below rho^*f, and exactly -B on the entire infinite-conductor stratum. It is constant on every full fibre and descends positively. Taking suprema and the mask limit proves rho^*p_R(f)=p_S(rho^*f) at every finite-conductor point, and <= holds everywhere. Continuous source competitors suffice by n1.

Suppose EVERY actual normal centre of S has CE, and put U=P_S(rho^*f), continuous. Since rho^*p_R<=U with equality on a dense finite-conductor locus, its upstairs upper regularization is U. For any bounded F on a compact quotient, F*(v)=max_(rho(w)=v) (rho^*F)*(w). The <= direction follows by covering the compact fibre with finitely many neighbourhoods controlling upstairs upper limits, and using closedness of rho to find a base neighbourhood whose inverse image lies in their union. The reverse direction follows by taking images of neighbourhoods, or directly testing every base neighbourhood at each lift. Applying this yields the exact selected formula

p_R(f)*(v)=max_(rho(w)=v) U(w).

Its positivity is equivalent to U being constant on EVERY full fibre. Indeed if U is fibre constant, G331 C descent gives an actual continuous geometric competitor downstairs, forcing raw p_R to equal its descent. Conversely if p_R* belongs to D, n1 gives continuous raw p_R; its pullback agrees with U on the dense finite-conductor locus and therefore everywhere. This also proves equivalence with continuity of the displayed fibre maximum and with existence of a maximal D minorant. The positive domain of continuous obstacles is closed under uniform limits, by nonexpansivity and closedness of C.

Without source CE there is still an exact criterion: p_R* is positive iff the raw source envelope is continuous and full-fibre constant.
