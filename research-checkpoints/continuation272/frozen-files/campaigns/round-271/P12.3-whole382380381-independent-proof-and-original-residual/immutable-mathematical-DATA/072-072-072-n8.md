# Strictly negative continuous obstacle on a nonnormal surface has no maximal D minorant.

Take R=k[x,y,z]_{(x,y,z)}/(xy), over ANY field, with maximal-ideal gauge. Its normalization is A=k[x,z]_{(x,z)} and B=k[y,z]_{(y,z)}. The conductor is (x) on A and (y) on B; both quotient boundaries are the singleton order of k[z]. Thus exactly q_A (support (x)) and q_B (support (y)) are identified. All other fibres are singleton. Define f=-1-1/v(y), with reciprocal infinity interpreted as zero. This is a finite continuous obstacle in [-2,-1]. On A it is -1; on B its value at ord_m is -2.

For every primary profile on regular B, v(I)>=ord_m(I) on the full section; finite real combinations, uniform limits and the specified decreasing nets preserve h(v)<=h(ord_m). Thus every B competitor under f is <=-2 everywhere. Constant -2 attains it, so its source envelope is -2; the A source envelope is -1.

On A let J_n=(x^n,xz^{n-1},z^{2n}). Write a=v(x), b=v(z), min(a,b)=1. Its profile j_n=-v(J_n)/n is -1 if a<=b; if finite a>b then b=1 and j_n=-min(1+(a-1)/n,2); at q_A it is -2. The tuple (j_n,-2) is continuous positive upstairs and agrees at the ENTIRE identified fibre, so G331 descends it positively. It is below f: on A j_n<=-1, on B -2<=f. These competitors tend increasingly to -1 on A away from q_A and -2 at q and throughout B. Source bounds and the B order inequality give the matching upper bound. Hence this is EXACTLY p_R(f).

Monomial A valuations v_L(x)=L,v_L(z)=1 converge pointwise to q_A. Their raw values are -1, whereas p_R(q)=-2; raw p_R is not USC and is not in D. By n1 no maximal D minorant exists. Formula n7 gives p_R*(q)=-1. On B its values at ord_m and q_B are -2 and -1, violating the necessary h(q_B)<=h(ord_m) inequality, so the regularization is not geometric. This is an actual all-characteristic negative-obstacle counterexample for the nonnormal domain, not a failure of an estimate and not a normal counterexample.

More generally, when two distinct normal centres share a fibre and have CE, choose a finite-conductor divisorial point on the first centre, whose singleton image lies outside the compact image of the second centre. A downstairs continuous cutoff obstacle can be -1 there and zero on the second image, taking values in [-1,0]. For every first-centre minorant g, G331 gives g(nu)>=C_nu g(v) for every v; hence g(nu)<=-1 forces g(v)<=-1/C_nu throughout that centre. The second-centre envelope is zero. Thus the two source envelopes disagree at the shared fibre. This supplies an obstruction in that particular setting. It does not classify internal fibres within one normal centre; n7's full-fibre criterion is the precise general statement. Full proof and finite diagnostic inputs: n8.md and diagnostic-inputs.json.
