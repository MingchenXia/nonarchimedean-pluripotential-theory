## 1. Divisorial density without singular SNC or resolution

Let A be an excellent local domain of positive dimension, normalized by a proper primary a. We propose a direct proof that divisorial valuations centered at its closed point are dense in V_a(A), including semivaluations with nonzero prime support.

Fix v in that section and finitely many open coordinate conditions. Choose a generator a0 of a with v(a0)=1. All nonzero a-generators may be divided by a0. For a coordinate f with finite positive value choose rational strict bounds p/q<v(f)<r/s lying inside the requested interval, with p>=0, q,s>0. If its value is infinity keep just a rational lower bound p/q. Unit values zero and the zero element's infinity are fixed for every centered semivaluation, so their coordinate tests impose no further restriction. Include in a finite type birational subalgebra B of Frac(A) the following ratios:

    a_j/a0,
    f^q/a0^p       for each lower test,
    a0^r/f^s       for each finite upper test.

The denominators have finite v-value; hence v extends to a semivaluation of B with values nonnegative on B. Its center P is the prime of strictly positive values. Every strict-test ratio and a0 belongs to P. Consequently B_P is an excellent local domain of positive dimension. Normalize the blowup of its maximal ideal and take a Rees DVR centered at P B_P. This DVR exists by the usual primary-ideal Rees valuation construction; excellence makes the relevant normalization finite. Its valuation nu has every strict-test ratio positive and every a_j/a0 nonnegative. Thus nu(a)=nu(a0)>0 and after rescaling by this value it satisfies all the chosen coordinate tests.

It is divisorial OVER A, not merely some higher-rank dominating valuation. The dimension formula for the finite type birational algebra B gives dim(B_P)+trdeg_(A/m) kappa(P)=dim(A); the Rees divisor's residue transcendence degree over kappa(P) is dim(B_P)-1. Hence its residue transcendence degree over A/m is dim(A)-1, and its rank-one discrete valuation is divisorial. The chart and the blowup can be extended to a birational proper model by clearing the finitely many rational denominators and taking a graph closure, or this residue-degree characterization can be used directly. Since a is primary and nu(a)>0, its center on A is m. This establishes the claimed density in the actual pointwise topology. No smooth fan, quasimonomial retraction, or resolution in positive characteristic is assumed. The dimension-formula and Rees-divisor applications are background gates to check independently.

## 2. A normal branch cannot have an isolated zero value

Let A now be excellent NORMAL local. Its completion is normal, hence a domain, by the normal-formal-fiber theorem (Stacks tag0C23); thus it is analytically irreducible. Apply the general characteristic-free Izumi--Rees theorem at these precise hypotheses: for a divisorial nu centered at m, there is a finite C_nu with

    nu(f) <= C_nu ord_m(f)       for nonzero nonunits f in A.

For units both values are zero. This is the ordinary Izumi--Rees/order consequence for analytically irreducible Noetherian local rings, not BFJ's smooth quasimonomial bound. It follows from comparability with the finitely many m-Rees valuations and the analytically-unramified asymptotic-order bound, absorbing its additive constant on nonunits. Exact sources and hypotheses are discussed below.

For any v0 in V_a(A), including nonminimal support, v0(f)>=ord_m(f) v0(m). Choose r with m^r subset a, so v0(m)>=1/r. Normalize nu also by a, and absorb its normalizer into C_nu. For each proper primary I, choose f attaining its finite v0-value. Then

    nu(I) <= nu(f) <= C_nu r v0(I).

It follows for every negative ideal profile g, then by uniform closure for every g in C_a(A), that

    g(nu) >= C_nu r g(v0).

If g(v0)=0, nonpositivity forces g(nu)=0 at every normalized divisorial valuation. Density from Section1 and continuity make g identically zero. Thus a nonzero continuous C-function is strictly negative at EVERY point, not just at the divisorial locus.

The same dichotomy holds in D_a(A): if u=inf_i g_i and u(v0)=0, each nonpositive g_i(v0) must be zero, so every g_i is identically zero and u=0. Every nonzero D-function is therefore everywhere strictly negative, with bottom allowed. Upper semicontinuity on compact V_a then bounds it above by a negative constant. This uses neither continuity of u nor a finite lower bound.

## 3. The exact active-branch indicator descends

It is fiber constant: an active and an inactive point cannot share a normalization fiber, as their u-values would be respectively negative and zero. ## 4. Continuous insertion on the active set

For every delta>0 the function u-delta is in D_S, remains fiber constant, and is everywhere strictly negative. Constants -delta are in C_S, and addition is justified by the product of the two representing directed nets. ROOT319 therefore gives

    u_bar-delta in D_R.

This fact by itself is not enough to let delta decrease to zero; that would be an increasing limit, and no such closure is assumed. We instead build genuine decreasing continuous majorants, using sigma.

Let F be any finite continuous function on the compact clopen T with F>u_bar there. The extended lower semicontinuous gap has a positive minimum e. Choose 0<eta<1/2 so small that eta(||F||+e)<e/4. Set

    delta=eta c/[2(1-eta)].

On T, with extended negative values allowed, the scaled target obeys

    (1-eta)(u_bar-delta)
       = u_bar+eta(-u_bar-c/2) > u_bar,
    (1-eta)(u_bar-delta) < F-e/2.

The first strict inequality is for finite u_bar; at bottom points any finite majorant is larger. Choose a decreasing C_R-family g_i -> u_bar-delta. The open sets where (1-eta)g_i<F-e/4 cover T, so compactness and directedness select one i satisfying that strict upper bound throughout T. Choose N sufficiently large that -N<min_T F. Then

    q=max((1-eta)g_i, N sigma) in C_R.

On T, both terms are below F, while the first is strictly above u_bar at finite points and q is finite at bottom points. Off T, the second term is zero and the first nonpositive, hence q=0=u_bar EXACTLY. Thus we have constructed a continuous geometric-uniform-cone majorant with

    q=0 off T,        u_bar<q<F on T.                       (1)

No positive constant is added to a nef cone, and no min of two psh functions is presumed psh. All allowed operations are positive scaling, addition of already negative constants for the strict-descent step, and the finite maximum in C_R.

Let Q be all continuous C_R-functions zero off T and strictly greater than u_bar on T. It contains zero. Given q1,q2, the continuous test F=min(q1,q2) on T is strictly above u_bar; (1) supplies another q below both. Thus Q is downward directed. For any v in T and t>u_bar(v), upper semicontinuity and compact Hausdorff normality supply a finite continuous test F>u_bar on T with F(v)<t, by the Urysohn construction used in ROOT319. Equation(1) then supplies q(v)<t. Off T all Q-members are exactly zero. Hence

    inf_(q in Q) q = u_bar

and the directed family Q is an actual decreasing C_R-net. This proves the proposed full normalization descent, including all zero patterns and -infinity values. It does NOT rely on a general assertion that decreasing limits of already completed functions can be flattened.
