For an excellent reduced Noetherian local A and a proper primary boundary ideal a, let C_a(A) be the uniform closed cone of zero and the finite negative rational primary ideal profiles -v(I)/k. Equivalently one may take finite nonnegative REAL combinations and their uniform closure, by synchronizing rational denominators and then approximating real coefficients. On a finite semilocal algebra use the finite product over its closed centers and allow componentwise unit ideals. All C-functions are finite, continuous, nonpositive. Let D_a(A) be the set of pointwise decreasing NET limits of C_a(A), with values in [-infinity,0]. This definition refers to continuous uniform-cone functions, not solely proper-primary profiles; its exact zero patterns must not be conflated with the proper-primary decreasing class. D-functions are upper semicontinuous. Nilpotent reduction preserves these classes.

**Proposed theorem.** For a finite injective map A -> B, a fiber-constant u in D_(aB)(B) descends to u_bar in D_a(A) PROVIDED u_bar(v)<0 at every point of the primary section. Values -infinity are allowed and u need not be bounded below or continuous. The whole zero function descends separately. There is no claim here for a function having both zero and negative values. Every semivaluation and every conductor fiber, including nonminimal supports and conductor value infinity, is retained.

Strict negativity gives a UNIFORM upper bound u_bar<=-c for some c>0: upper semicontinuity and compactness make its finite supremum a strictly negative maximum, unless it is identically -infinity. The bottom is directly the limit of -n v(a)=-n. Thus in the proof below we may choose a finite c>0 even if the function has some infinite negative values.

## 1. Normal targets and the induction

For a normal local domain A, keep each minimal component of B that dominates A, normalize it, and apply the generic-degree norm-coefficient trace to its continuous C-functions. The trace preserves C, is positive, and is a finite sum over each restriction fiber with total mass the field degree. Applied to a decreasing family converging to the fiber-constant u, its normalized sum converges to u_bar, also when that value is -infinity. Finite sums commute with the decreasing extended-real limit here because all summands are nonpositive and all finite weights are positive. The normalized trace functions lie in C_a(A), so u_bar is in D_a(A). Vertical components need no invented generic degree: fiber constancy on the FULL original B boundary ensures every retained trace has the actual same target value. At least one retained component dominates. Normalization and finite trace existence include prime supports.

Let H_d be this strict-negative descent assertion for all finite injections into excellent reduced local targets of dimension at most d. Let N_d be its normalization instance. Dimension zero has empty primary boundary. Prove N_d from H_(d-1), and then H_d by base-changing an arbitrary finite injection to the normalization, reducing, applying the normal-target result on each local normal branch, and descending by N_d. The tensor product spectrum surjects onto the normal base, so its reduction remains an injective finite extension of that reduced base. Its target function is the pullback of u_bar and remains strictly negative. Finite products of local decreasing nets form one decreasing net. This reduces everything to the conductor step below.

## 2. A continuous upper-bound insertion lemma

Let R be reduced excellent local, S its finite normalization, c its conductor, C=R/c and E=S/c. We have a finite injection C -> E and the conductor fiber product R=S times_E C. A regular element belongs to c; unless S=R, dim C<dim R. Nilpotent reductions of C and E keep the injection and the boundary classes.

Suppose u in D_(aS)(S) is fiber-constant and strictly negative, with descended upper semicontinuous function u_bar<=-c0<0. Its restriction u_E lies in D_(aE)(E): restrict every continuous C-approximant, then the decreasing net. By H_(d-1), u_E descends to u_C in D_(aC)(C). On the conductor-infinite stratum this is exactly u_bar, and retains all quotient fibers. If V_(aC)(C) is empty no quotient estimate is needed; all base points have finite conductor value.

**Insertion claim.** For EVERY finite continuous F on V_a(R) with F(v)>u_bar(v) at every point, there is a continuous negative real-scaled primary ideal profile q in C_a(R) such that

    u_bar < q < F                                              (1)

everywhere. At -infinity points the first inequality means a finite q. No continuity or global finite lower bound on u_bar is assumed.

The extended lower semicontinuous function F-u_bar has a positive minimum e>0 on the compact section. For the all-bottom case take any e after replacing F with a sufficiently low continuous bound as needed. On the normalization choose a continuous C-approximant g of u with

    u <= g <= -c0/2,        g < F pulled_back - e/2.            (2)

The open sets defined by these strict upper tests cover the section along the directed decreasing family: each point's limit is u, and the desired thresholds lie strictly above u. Compactness and directedness select one index satisfying BOTH tests globally. This is NOT Dini uniform convergence to u.

Put M_g=||g||. Choose 0<b<1/2 with b M_g<e/8. Then (1-b)g is still below F-e/4 and is at least u+b c0/2. Uniform C-approximation supplies a single rational componentwise primary ideal profile j=-w(J)/k arbitrarily close to (1-b)g. Choose the error below min(e/8,b c0/4). After harmless tighter constants, we obtain

    u + s <= j < F pulled_back - e/8,                         (3)

with s>0, and j<0 uniformly. Thus J is proper on each positive-dimensional normalization center, contains a boundary power, and J^N intersect R is proper primary. The denominator can subsequently be multiplied by common powers without changing j. Fix M=||j||, which is finite.

Next choose an arbitrary small delta>0. If the quotient section is nonempty, we can choose a rational primary profile kappa=-v(K)/k on C with

    u_C <= kappa,            kappa pulled_back < j_E+delta.   (4)

Here j_E is the restriction of j on E, not an assumed function on C. To prove (4), start with a decreasing continuous C_C-family g_i -> u_C. Choose 0<b1<1/2 so b1 M<s/4. Since u_E<=j_E-s, we have

    (1-b1)u_E <= (1-b1)(j_E-s) < j_E-s/4.

Compactness of the E section selects i with (1-b1)g_i pulled_back below j_E-s/8; simultaneously select g_i<=-c0/2 on the C section. The latter inequality and g_i>=u_C imply (1-b1)g_i>=u_C+b1 c0/2. Uniform approximation by a rational K-profile with error smaller than b1 c0/4 and s/16 gives (4). This uses a continuous upper TEST j_E against an upper semicontinuous limit, not a uniform convergence assumption. Synchronize the two rational denominators k by ideal powers; make 1/k<delta.

Write J_E,K_E for the image/extension ideals. Equation (4) implies

    w(K_E)>w(J_E)-k delta

on the whole quotient primary section. Choose an integer L with k delta<L<=k delta+1, so L/k<2delta. For Q=K_E(aE)^L all pertinent valuations satisfy w(Q)>=w(J_E). The ideal J_E contains a boundary power. Hence the rank-one valuative ideal-integral-dependence criterion, with minimal-support reduction, gives Q subset integral_closure(J_E). On a zero-dimensional component the boundary ideal is nilpotent, and the same integrality assertion follows by reduction; unit components are automatic. There is no missing finite-conductor correction here.

Integral dependence of the finitely generated ideal Q gives a finite graded Rees extension E[(J_E+Q)t] over E[J_E t]. Homogeneous finite generators yield an integer h>=0 such that

    Q^(N+h) subset J_E^N          for every N>=1.             (5)

Equivalently use the elementary finite ideal-reduction theorem and its power recurrence. This asserts finiteness only for this integral extension, not normalization of a nonreduced full Rees algebra.

Set I_N=J^N intersect R. The conductor fiber product gives the exact equality

    image(I_N in C)=J_E^N intersect C.                         (6)

Thus K^(N+h)(aC)^(L(N+h)) is contained in that image. At a point with v(c)=infinity, (5)/(6) imply

    limsup_N v(I_N)/(kN) <= v(K)/k+L/k,
    liminf_N [-v(I_N)/(kN)] >= kappa(v)-2delta
                                  >= u_bar(v)-2delta.        (7)

At finite conductor value there is a unique normalization lift w and

    w(J)/k <= v(I_N)/(kN) <= w(J)/k+v(c)/(kN),                (8)

because c J^N subset I_N. The limit of the negative profiles is j(w)>=u_bar(v)+s. The term v(c)/N is used ONLY in (8). Infinite-conductor points use (7). If the quotient section is empty, (8) covers all points.

For dyadic N=2^n put f_n=-v(I_(2^n))/(k2^n). These functions are finite continuous, increase (I_N^2 subset I_(2N)), lie in C_R, and satisfy f_n<=j on every normalization lift. Their limits are at least u_bar-2delta on every point by (7)/(8). The sets

    U_n={v: f_n(v)>u_bar(v)-3delta}

are OPEN because u_bar is upper semicontinuous, and they increase and cover the compact section. Select one n covering the whole section. This crucial selection remains valid even when u_bar=-infinity somewhere or is discontinuous. Therefore

    f_n>u_bar-3delta,             f_n<=j.                     (9)

Choose delta sufficiently small that eta=3delta/c0<1/2 and eta M<e/16. Put q=(1-eta)f_n. For finite u_bar<=-c0,

    q > (1-eta)(u_bar-3delta)
      >= u_bar+eta c0-3delta(1-eta)
       = u_bar+3delta eta > u_bar.

At -infinity points q is finite, hence also strictly larger. From f_n<=j and j>=-M,

    q <= (1-eta)j <= j+eta M < F.

The scaled profile remains in C_R (real scale by rational approximation if necessary). This proves (1). Strict negativity is exactly what permits the last scaling while staying nonpositive. No mixed-zero claim is hidden in this step.

## 3. Producing the decreasing net and finishing the induction

Let Q be all continuous C_R-functions strictly greater than u_bar. It is nonempty since zero is one. Given q1,q2 in Q, the continuous F=min(q1,q2) is strictly greater than u_bar. The insertion lemma supplies q in Q below both. Thus Q is downward directed. For any v and real t>u_bar(v), compact Hausdorff normality and upper semicontinuity give a finite continuous F strictly greater than u_bar with F(v)<t. Explicitly, take a global upper bound -c0/2 and a sufficiently negative level below t but above u_bar(v), choose an open neighborhood where u_bar lies below that level, and use a Urysohn cutoff inside it; keep the global upper bound elsewhere. If t is larger than the global bound no cutoff is necessary. Applying insertion gives q(v)<t. Consequently inf_(q in Q) q=u_bar pointwise. Ordered by the reverse pointwise relation, Q provides an actual decreasing net of C_R-functions converging to u_bar. This proves N_d.

The normal-target/base-change step in Section1 now proves H_d from N_d. All excellence, local quotient, nilpotent-reduction, dimension-drop and support conditions are preserved, so induction proves the proposed strict-negative theorem in all finite dimensions. Pullback preservation is extension of ideals and commutation with uniform/decreasing limits. Surjectivity of boundary restriction makes it injective and isometric on continuous classes. For arbitrary primary gauges the radial normalization maps are continuous positive rescalings bounded above/below on compact sections, so strict negativity and the finite/decreasing cone definitions respect the established gauge cocycle; this is not an identification of different normalizers without that cocycle.
