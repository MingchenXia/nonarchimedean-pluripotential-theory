# Completion invariance at the primary boundary and the precise smooth interface

ROOT working mathematics, unreviewed. No original closure or accepted-paper count is asserted. This document is not supplied to active frozen campaigns232/233. It supplements the independent numerical-nef comparison proposed in ROOT312, rather than treating that unreviewed comparison as a caller grant.

Let (R,m) be a Noetherian local ring and a a proper m-primary ideal. On the centered real semivaluation section V_a(R), v(a)=1. Fix r with m^r contained in a. Then v(m)>=1/r. Values are nonnegative, infinity is permitted, and v(m) is finite. Nilpotents have infinite value. Write Rhat for the m-adic completion.

**Exact completion lemma.** Restriction V_(a Rhat)(Rhat) -> V_a(R) is a homeomorphism. Every primary ideal of Rhat is the extension of its contraction to R. Corresponding primary ideal functions -t v(I), for t>0, agree exactly on these sections. Consequently their uniform closed cones and their pointwise decreasing completions are preserved. The lemma itself needs neither excellence, normality, characteristic zero nor a model resolution.

Here is the full proof. For F in Rhat choose representatives F_N in R of its classes modulo m^N. If M>=N, v(F_M-F_N)>=N v(m). If v(F_N)<N v(m), the ultrametric equality for two different values gives v(F_M)=v(F_N) for every M>=N. If this finite stabilization never happens, then v(F_N)>=N v(m) for every N and the values tend to infinity. Thus the limit vhat(F)=lim_N v(F_N) exists in [0,infinity], is independent of representatives, and restricts to v on R. The same error estimate proves, for every N,

    min(vhat(F), N v(m)) = min(v(F_N), N v(m)).                 (1)

The scalar values of sums and products are obtained by taking representatives of the sums and products. Addition obeys the ultrametric inequality by passing to the limits. For products, use F_N G_N as representatives modulo m^N; multiplicativity of v and the existence of the two limits give vhat(FG)=vhat(F)+vhat(G), including infinity. The conventions vhat(0)=infinity and vhat(1)=0 hold. A unit of Rhat has a unit representative modulo m and hence value zero. Finite generation of a, together with restriction on its generators and nonnegative coefficient values, gives vhat(a Rhat)=v(a)=1. The extension is therefore a centered semivaluation with the required normalization.

For uniqueness, let w be any extension. The inclusion F-F_N in m^N Rhat gives w(F-F_N)>=N w(m Rhat)=N v(m). Therefore w satisfies (1). Since N v(m) tends to infinity, these identities determine w(F) uniquely.

Restriction is continuous for the pointwise topology. To show continuity of extension, fix F and use (1). The maps v -> v(m) and v -> v(F_N) are continuous, the first as the finite minimum on a generating set of m. Let d(s)=s/(1+s), with d(infinity)=1, a coordinate on the compact extended interval. The difference between d(vhat(F)) and d(min(v(F_N),N v(m))) is at most 1/(1+N/r), uniformly on the section. The second expression is continuous, so its uniform limit is continuous. This proves continuity of every extended coordinate and therefore the claimed homeomorphism. It retains prime supports: no infinite-coordinate stratum has been removed. Compactness may alternatively prove the inverse continuity once the bijection is established, but is not needed in this argument.

For a primary ideal J of Rhat, choose N with m^N Rhat contained in J. The natural isomorphism R/m^N -> Rhat/m^N Rhat lets us choose representatives j_1,...,j_l in R of generators of J modulo m^N Rhat. They belong to J intersect R. Their extensions, together with m^N Rhat, generate J. If I=J intersect R, it follows that I Rhat=J; I is primary because it contains m^N and is proper. Conversely I Rhat intersect R=I for a primary I, by the same quotient argument with a sufficiently high boundary power. Equality of their ideal values follows from finite generators and nonnegative coefficients. This establishes the ideal-function and closure assertions. Convergence in the uniform norm and pointwise monotone convergence are unchanged by a homeomorphism.

**What this resolves for a smooth specialization.** In the author-hosted BFJ paper, primary approximating ideals in Proposition3.13 occur in the completed smooth local ring, although earlier Cartier model ideals may lie in the uncompleted ring. The completion lemma identifies these approximants exactly with primary ideals downstairs. It is therefore insufficient to reject or broaden the smooth comparison merely because a source uses Rhat. The lemma supplies this interface on all semivaluations, including nonzero support.

The proposed ROOT312 geometric comparison says that the uniform closed cone of nonpositive exceptional relatively numerical-nef Cartier values on actual primary blowups equals the uniform closed cone of primary ideal functions. That comparison, if accepted after its own algebraic-geometric validation, would make the geometric continuous cone completion-invariant as a consequence of an explicit comparison theorem. This document does not assert a direct equivalence between all model categories of R and Rhat, or an equivalence of every weak b-divisor completion.

For a smooth complex germ in BFJ's hypotheses, the precise distinction is as follows. Definitions2.5/2.8 define formal psh functions by strong continuity on every closed dual-complex face, a nef Weil divisor, and canonical extension by the infimum of all retractions. Proposition3.13's construction gives a decreasing sequence on ALL of V, with the necessary zero/unit qualification below; a globally continuous limit on compact V has uniform convergence by Dini. The uniform primary cone is thus the continuous BFJ subcone, once the converse weak-nef/closed-face identification is proved. The additional nontrivial decreasing completion gives the full formal class only after that identification, not by calling every bounded usc function continuous.

The zero qualification is essential and was independently identified by completed native232. Under the proper-primary convention, every positive multiple of -v(I) is strictly negative at every normalized valuation. A decreasing sequence of these functions cannot converge to zero. The source's formula a_k=L2(2^k g)+m^(4^k) gives the UNIT ideal when g=0, so the literal all-primary wording has a zero-case defect. Keep zero as its own constant model, or permit unit ideals explicitly. For nonzero formal g, Proposition3.14 gives g(v_m)<0; the multiplier bound at v_m forces L2(2^k g) to be proper for all sufficiently large k. Those a_k contain m^(4^k), hence are then proper primary. Discard the finitely many initial unit terms. This repair preserves the source's nonzero decreasing approximation and all-semivaluation extension; no unqualified primary-only approximation of zero is used here.

The converse argument proposed in ROOT312 can be made explicit. For a decreasing net g_i of continuous BFJ formal psh functions with nonbottom limit g, the values at the order valuation v_m cannot tend to minus infinity: g_i(v)<=g_i(v_m) on every point, so this would force g to be identically minus infinity. Proposition3.14 bounds every quasimonomial value below by C_v g_i(v_m); the divisorial coefficients therefore have finite limits. The limit Weil divisor is nef by the closed-cone definition. Proposition3.11 supplies its continuous convex function on every closed face. On the relative interior of the minimal face containing a given point, the finite convex limit g is continuous and agrees with that function on the dense rational divisorial points. They agree there; applying the argument separately to all lower-dimensional faces includes every boundary point. Finally,

    g(v)=inf_i inf_pi g_i(r_pi(v))
        =inf_pi inf_i g_i(r_pi(v))
        =inf_pi g(r_pi(v)).                                    (2)

The two infima commute as infima over a product of index sets, with extended-real values allowed. Equation(2) supplies exactly the canonical all-semivaluation extension; it is not merely a divisorial equality. Conversely the zero-qualified Proposition3.13 and the completion lemma give decreasing continuous primary approximants to every NONZERO formal psh function, and the zero model represents zero separately. The identically-minus-infinity function can be retained as a separate bottom element. A smooth one-dimensional local ring has a single branch ray and must be verified directly; BFJ's higher-dimensional model discussion does not silently supply that case.
