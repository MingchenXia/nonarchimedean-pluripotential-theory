# Inductive bounded conductor descent by approximate ideal matching

ROOT independent working proof, UNREVIEWED. No original closure, caller grant or accepted-paper count follows. This note is not sent to active frozen native234 and was developed independently of its private outputs. It concerns uniform ideal cones. Its transfer to numerical-nef model cones requires the separately unreviewed numerical comparison of ROOT312/native232; it does not identify an unbounded completion or solve original P12.2 by itself.

For an excellent Noetherian local ring A and a proper primary ideal a, let C_a(A) be the uniform closure, on the compact centered real semivaluation section V_a(A), of zero and the functions -v(I)/k, where I is proper primary and k is a positive integer. On a finite semilocal A-algebra use the same boundary ideal aB and its finite disjoint union of local sections; the ideal family allows ideals containing a boundary power and the unit ideal, with rational positive scales. This is equivalently the product of the local uniform cones: contract local ideals primary at one center and unit at all other centers, intersect and synchronize denominators. All functions are finite, continuous and nonpositive. Nilpotent reduction preserves these definitions. This note uses the topology on ALL semivaluations, including nonminimal supports.

**Proposed theorem.** For every finite injective map A -> B of excellent reduced rings with A local and B nonzero finite, restriction f:V_(aB)(B)->V_a(A) is a compact quotient, and

    f* C_a(A) = {u in C_(aB)(B): u is constant on every f-fiber}.    (1)

Pullback is an isometry. In particular normalization has exact bounded descent, even when its conductor has positive-dimensional support and infinite value. The criterion uses every actual conductor fiber, not only generic/divisorial restrictions. For a noninjective map the statement is not here asserted outside its exact image or for the target's unreduced quotient conventions.

Two declared ordinary algebraic inputs are used with precise domains: the valuative criterion for integral dependence of ideals in an excellent Noetherian ring, and finite normal-target norm-coefficient traces. The first may be checked on the finitely many Rees valuations after reduction; if an ideal contains a primary boundary power, all nontrivial Rees centers are among those boundary centers. The second is reconstructed below. Neither input assumes resolution, characteristic zero, flatness, purity or a defectless extension. These background applications still need independent source validation before this working proof is accepted.

**Normal-target finite descent.** First suppose A is a normal local domain. Decompose the reduced B into minimal components, retaining those whose minimal prime contracts to zero. At least one component is retained by lying over the generic point. Normalize each retained component, obtaining finite normal domains D_j over A, possibly semilocal. Let n_j be its fraction-field degree. Source components with positive-codimension image are not assigned a generic degree; they need not be absent. They are harmless for this direction because the stipulated source function is constant on EVERY restriction fiber.

For a finite domain extension A subset D with fraction fields K subset L, degree n, and an ideal J containing a boundary power, choose finite A-module generators b_l of J. The coefficient ideal of

    N_(L(T)/K(T))(sum_l T_l b_l)

belongs to A: coefficients are integral over A and lie in K, hence normality puts them in A. Substitution of A-linear combinations of generators in both directions proves generator independence. In a finite extension containing all K-embedding images, factor the norm polynomial as the product of the embedded linear forms, each with generic inseparable multiplicity q. The minimum value of polynomial coefficients is multiplicative: quotient a semivaluation by its support, divide by a least-valued nonzero coefficient, and reduce in the residue field; products of nonzero reduced polynomials are nonzero. Infinite coefficient values cause no exception. Thus at every base semivaluation,

    v(N(J)) = q sum_sigma w_sigma(J),                         (2)

where each w_sigma is a normalized extension of v. The finite positive measure q sum_sigma delta_(w_sigma) has mass n. It is independent of choices: the bounded ideal gauges containing boundary powers separate points, contain constants, and their real linear span is a vector lattice by product/sum/difference identities. The elementary lattice Stone--Weierstrass argument on the compact section gives density in all continuous functions, so equal values on these gauges and equal mass determine the measures. Redundant generators from a^N show that N(J) contains a boundary power; it therefore has a finite continuous gauge. Formula(2) and the common mass bound extend the trace to a positive continuous-function operator of norm n, preserving the closed sign-reversed ideal cone by uniform approximation. All multiplicities are generic inseparable lengths, without a valuation-defect simplification.

If u in C_(aB)(B) is fiber-constant, its pullback to each D_j remains in that ideal cone. The sum of these traces divided by sum_j n_j belongs to C_a(A). At a base v every traced extension has source value exactly the descended value u_bar(v), so the normalized sum is u_bar. This proves(1) for normal A, including possible vertical source components and all prime supports. The compact quotient guarantees continuous descent. Injection/surjectivity of restriction and valuation extensions are the standard finite integral boundary facts, with the full support retained.

**Induction structure.** Let F_d denote(1) for every excellent local target of dimension at most d and every finite injective reduced source. Let N_d denote it just for normalization of such a target. In dimension zero the centered sections are empty on both finite sides, so F_0 is vacuous. We will prove N_d from F_(d-1), and then F_d from N_d and the normal-target result. Nilpotent reductions may be applied to intermediate conductor quotients: an injective map remains injective after taking reductions, since nilpotence in an injected subring is detected in the larger ring. Corresponding ideal gauges and uniform cones agree exactly with their reductions.

To prove N_d let R be reduced excellent local and S its finite normalization. If S=R the assertion is immediate. Otherwise put c=(R:S), C=R/c and D=S/c. The conductor is an ideal of both rings and contains a regular element by denominator clearing, and

    R = S times_D C.                                         (3)

The map C -> D is finite injective. Excellence/catenarity and the regular conductor imply dim(C)<=d-1. Its primary boundary has the inherited ideal aC. A continuous source u in the product of normalization uniform cones descends continuously to u_bar on V_a(R) if it is fiber-constant. On the closed stratum v(c)=infinity, its restriction is a fiber-constant function u_D in C_(aD)(D). This follows by quotient restriction of primary ideal functions and uniform limits. By F_(d-1) there is u_C in C_(aC)(C) with pullback u_D. It equals the restriction of u_bar. If C is zero-dimensional that normalized stratum is empty; the original conductor is primary and the previously elementary isolated correction already proves the result. We therefore discuss only the positive-dimensional case.

**Approximate trace matching, not an infinite conductor correction.** Fix delta>0. Choose a rational ideal profile -w(J)/k approximating u on the whole normalization section within delta, and a rational profile -v(K)/k approximating u_C within delta. Synchronize denominators by taking powers. Increase k by another common power so 1/k<delta. Unit or zero component approximants are allowed; if necessary multiplying J by aS at arbitrarily large synchronized k ensures a proper contraction with an additional error <delta, absorbed by choosing the preliminary errors smaller. Alternatively u identically zero has the zero downstairs model directly. J and K contain boundary powers. Let J_D and K_D be their images/extensions to D. At every normalized semivaluation of D,

    |w(K_D)-w(J_D)| < 2k delta.

Take a positive integer L with 2k delta < L <= 2k delta+1. Then L/k<3delta and, setting Q=K_D (aD)^L,

    w(Q)>=w(J_D) for every normalized boundary semivaluation.  (4)

The valuative integral-dependence criterion gives Q subset integral_closure(J_D). All pertinent Rees valuations are among these semivaluations because J_D contains a boundary power. On zero-dimensional components, aD is nilpotent and Q is integral over every ideal; on components where J_D is the unit ideal the inclusion is automatic. Nilpotents add no obstruction: an integral relation modulo the nilradical can be lifted and raised to a fixed nilpotence power to give a monic integral relation with coefficients in the required ideal powers.

This particular finite ideal integrality gives an ACTUAL power inclusion. The Rees algebra D[(J_D+Q)t] is finite over D[J_D t]. Choose homogeneous module generators of degree at most s. In degree n>=s, its generators give (J_D+Q)^n subset J_D^(n-s), hence

    Q^(N+s) subset J_D^N for every N>=1.                     (5)

No assertion that the full integral closure of a Rees algebra in a nonreduced ring is finite is needed: integrality of the finitely generated ideal Q is sufficient.

Define downstairs I_N=J^N intersect R. Its contraction contains a power of a and is proper, so it is primary. Identity(3) gives the EXACT trace identity

    image(I_N in C)= J_D^N intersect C.                       (6)

Indeed a residue in the intersection has a representative in J^N whose residue comes from C; that representative belongs to R by(3). No intersection/base-change commutation beyond this conductor fiber product is assumed. By(5), the image in D of the C-ideal K^(N+s)(aC)^(L(N+s)) lies in J_D^N. Thus that ideal lies in the right side of(6).

For a normalized v with v(c)=infinity, pass to its semivaluation on C. Equations(5)/(6) give

    v(I_N)/(kN) <= (1+s/N)(v(K)/k+L/k).                       (7)

All ideal values in(7) are finite; its asymptotic right side is at most -u_bar(v)+4delta. At a point with v(c)<infinity, there is a unique normalization lift w. The conductor inclusion c J^N subset I_N gives instead

    w(J)/k <= v(I_N)/(kN) <= w(J)/k+v(c)/(kN).                (8)

Only at these finite-conductor points is the division by N used. At infinite-conductor points it is replaced by the trace argument(7). On EVERY point and every normalization lift, I_N subset J^N also gives

    v(I_N)/(kN) >= w(J)/k >= -u_bar(v)-delta.                 (9)

**Compact monotone selection.** Put N=2^n and h_n(v)=v(I_(2^n))/(k2^n). These are finite continuous functions and decrease, because I_N^2 subset I_(2N). They are globally bounded: J contains (aS)^M, so I_N contains a^(MN). Equations(7)--(9) show that their pointwise limit obeys

    -u_bar-delta <= inf_n h_n <= -u_bar+4delta.                (10)

The open sets U_n={v:h_n(v)+u_bar(v)<5delta} increase and cover the compact primary section. A finite subcover therefore gives one index n with the inequality everywhere. Combined with(9), this yields

    || -h_n-u_bar ||_infinity <=5delta.

The function -h_n is a downstairs rational primary ideal profile. Sending delta to zero proves u_bar in C_a(R), establishing N_d. This argument needs neither metrizability nor a claim that a discontinuous fiber extremum is a uniform limit. It uses continuity of the actual fiber-constant target and compactness for one monotone selection.

**Finite-map induction step.** For general finite injective R -> B, pull back along the normalization S of R and take the reduction B'=(B tensor_R S)_red. The base-changed spectrum still surjects onto Spec S, so S -> B' is injective: any kernel lies in every prime of reduced S. Pullback of u to B' preserves the uniform ideal cone. At each local normal branch of S, that function is constant on its restriction fibers, since two B' points with the same S restriction also have the same R restriction and therefore the same B source value. Normal-target descent puts its descended function in the corresponding cone on S. These descended functions are exactly u_bar pulled back to S, hence constant on the normalization fibers. N_d descends them into C_a(R). This proves F_d. Together with the dimension-zero base and the preceding conductor argument, the induction gives(1) in every finite dimension.

Pullback preservation is immediate from extending ideals; it is isometric because the restriction map is surjective. Thus the result proves both inclusions in(1). In the reduction convention it also handles nonreduced conductor algebras, without assigning multiplicity data to semivaluations.

**Comparison with the exact existing cones.** Let F_R be the finite nonnegative real sums of negative ideal gauges downstairs, and F_R^nu the continuous functions whose normalization pullbacks lie in the analogous finite cones on all normal components and are constant on the known conductor fibers. The theorem gives

    F_R subset F_R^nu subset C_a(R),
    closure_uniform(F_R)=closure_uniform(F_R^nu)=C_a(R).       (11)

The first inclusion is extension of ideals; the second uses(1) because each finite upstairs ideal-cone function belongs to its uniform cone. The equality of closures follows by sandwiching. This does not claim equality before closure or realization by a single downstairs ideal. If the proposed numerical-Cartier/ideal comparison is accepted, the same bounded uniform cone is independently described by numerical nefness and has this exact conductor/finite-image rule. That would remove the general BOUNDED normalization interface gap of native232.

**What is NOT proved.** The theorem does not say that v(J^N intersect R)/N always converges to the maximum of J-values on a restriction fiber. That assertion is false already for the diagonal finite map R -> R times R: take R=k[[x,y]], J=((x,y^2),(x^2,y)). At v(x)=v(y)=1, the maximum of the two ideal values is one, while the asymptotic intersection constraints a+2b>=2N and2a+b>=2N force order at least4N/3, with even diagonal exponents attaining that asymptotic bound. The proof instead uses a globally fiber-compatible target and approximate conductor matching. Nor does(1) identify a nontrivial decreasing completion: the limiting completed function may be unbounded or discontinuous, and compact open-set selection against a continuous target cannot simply be reused. Smooth full BFJ recovery remains a separate proposed ROOT314/source interface. General unbounded conductor descent, products, energy, geometric traces and the original exact scope decision remain unproved at this stage.
