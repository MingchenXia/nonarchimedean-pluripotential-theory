# Proved scope and remaining gates

Theorem A is proved for every positive-dimensional excellent reduced Noetherian local ring, with arbitrary characteristic, residue field and component dimensions. For a nonreduced excellent ring the theorem is applied to its reduction; this matches the semivaluation boundary and loses nilpotent multiplicity deliberately. The zero-dimensional reduction has empty boundary. The main proof actually requires only the stated Noetherian projective background; excellence supplies boundary nonemptiness and finite normalization in the auxiliary statements.

The geometric core uses primary-ideal blowups and negative divisors in the cone generated by effective exceptional **integral Cartier** divisors with nonnegative real coefficients. This is an explicit convention on nonnormal models, not a claim about all possible nonnormal real Weil-effective divisors. Evaluation is proved for all exceptional integral Cartier divisors and their arbitrary real linear combinations. Numerical nefness is actual curve-degree nefness, with pullback preservation and reflection proved. The primary-blowup system is directed; no cofinality among every projective modification is asserted.

The exact comparison is between two uniform closures in finite continuous functions on a compact primary boundary. The dossier's finite real ideal-gauge cone is also explicitly compared after reversing its positive-gauge sign: it lies in the numerical model core and has the same uniform closure. This does not identify either unclosed core with that closure, assert monotone ideal approximation, or identify decreasing limits with formal psh functions. A uniform approximating sequence is constructed by choosing rational perturbations and high powers with errors tending to zero, even when the boundary is not metrizable. This is not a metrizability assertion.

Theorem B proves an isometric product description for any finite birational algebra `T` with `T/R` supported at the closed point. In the normalization instance, local branch rings are normal and excellent. It proves function descent through finite ideals, not exact descent of line bundles. For arbitrary normalization there is a proved quotient of branch semivaluation boundaries and a proved inclusion of downstairs bounded cones in fiber-constant upstairs cones. The raw normalization-ideal cone in the finite-transfer fragment is a separate subclass of that product; comparison between it and the downstairs bounded cone remains unproved in general. In the isolated case the numerical cone contains the raw normalization cone and equals its uniform closure. Surjectivity onto the fiber-constant cone remains unproved when the conductor has positive-dimensional support.

The curve corollary is a geometric theorem: normalization is a blowup of an invertible conductor ideal, contracted curves are absent, and every finite nonpositive branch profile is a model value. The finite-link decreasing completion is all extended nonpositive profiles. Comparison with the task's **specified** radial class is exact as a comparison of those specified sets; a new independent CLD/classical classification is not claimed because GR25/Chapter 15 original source units are not bound.

The smooth interface proves, within the explicit BFJ simple-model framework, that the bounded cone has weak-nef divisorial coefficients, finite continuous convex restrictions to closed faces, and the exact all-semivaluation retraction extension. It does not prove the reverse globally continuous comparison or recover all bounded discontinuous or unbounded BFJ members. Original Hironaka/principalization bytes are unavailable in this frozen packet; that smooth-framework dependency is marked conditional rather than assigned a source-PASS. The main algebraic theorems are independent of it.
