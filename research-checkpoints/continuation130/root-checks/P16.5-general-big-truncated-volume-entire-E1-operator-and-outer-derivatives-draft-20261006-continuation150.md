## 1. Exact domain and the truncation identity

Keep smooth integral projective X of dimension n over any algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero k, actual big rational L, V=vol(L)>0 and d=n+1. Fix a smooth equivariant snc model Y over X times P1, with F=sum b_i E_i, vertical rational D and original model obstacle f_D. Put

Let w_m,j be the actual lattice weights of own143 and r_m=h^0(X,mL). For every nonnegative rational c the exact direct-image splitting gives

    h^0(Y,m alpha_D(c))=sum_j max(w_m,j+mc+1,0).

Thus

    g_D(c)=lim_m (n!/m^(n+1))sum_j (w_m,j+mc)_+.           (1.1)

The discarded +1 has error bounded by n!r_m/m^(n+1), tending to zero. The required section-volume limit is the stated imported algebraic premise when alpha_D(c) is big. If it is not big, its volume is zero, so the nonnegative normalized section count has limsup zero and hence limit zero. The integer c-degree conventions are absorbed in the common divisible subsequence. Continuity extends (1.1)'s consequences to real c. On a fixed real vertical-divisor space the same identities extend by monotone rational coefficient sandwiches and continuity.

Suppose f_D<=0 and E(D)>=-B for a fixed B>=0. Effectivity of model functions means D<=0. All lattice weights are <=0. The actual full-weight limit is E(D). Consequently g_D is convex and V-Lipschitz as a function of c>=0, g_D(0)=0, and

Whenever c>B/V, alpha_D(c) is big and vol(alpha_D(c))>=d(cV-B)>0. No lower coefficient bound for D is assumed. The zero value at c=0 also follows from alpha_D(0)<=rho^*L, whose volume in dimension n+1 is zero.

## 3. Uniform big-class margins from volume and an upper class

Fix a rational model direction h=f_H carried on one fixed smooth model Z, and a fixed c>B/V. On any common refinement carrying D with f_D<=0 and E(D)>=-B, put

    kappa=d(cV-B)>0.

Then alpha<=gamma in pseudoeffective order, gamma is a fixed big Cartier class on Z, and vol(alpha)>=kappa. Let T be a fixed ample Cartier class on Z, pulled to each refinement, and write Q_gamma=<gamma^n>. Put M=d Q_gamma.T>=0.

There is a uniform r_0>0 such that alpha-r_0 T is big for every such D; for instance take r_0=kappa/[2(M+1)]. To check this, follow the segment alpha-rT until its first exit from the big cone on the particular refinement. Inside that cone, positive-product monotonicity and the nef pairing with T give

    0<=d< (alpha-rT)^n>.T<=M.

The volume differential integrated along the segment gives vol(alpha-rT)>=kappa-Mr. If the segment left the big cone before r_0, continuity of volume would make its endpoint volume zero, contradicting this strictly positive lower bound. Thus it remains big at r_0. This argument is uniform across all higher models because gamma and T are pulled back from the one fixed Z and positive products have their birational Cartier/Weil pairings.

In particular alpha>=r_0 T. Choose a>0 with aT>=+/-H, possible since T is ample on the fixed Z. Then (a/r_0)alpha>=+/-H. For epsilon=(a/r_0)|t|<1,

    (1-epsilon)alpha<=alpha+tH<=(1+epsilon)alpha.

Positive-product monotonicity and homogeneity now give uniform bounds for <(alpha+tH)^n> between the corresponding multiples of Q_alpha. Decompose H=T_1-T_2 with fixed nef Cartier T_1,T_2 on Z. Pairing these bounds with T_1,T_2 and using Q_alpha<=Q_gamma yields

    |[<(alpha+tH)^n>-Q_alpha].H|<=K_c |t|,                (3.1)

for uniformly small t, with K_c independent of the model and of D. No nefness of the trace of Q_alpha is assumed. The fixed-c volume therefore has the uniform Taylor estimate

    |g_(D+tH)(c)-g_D(c)-t integral h dmu_D,c|
          <=K_c t^2/2.                                  (3.2)

Unlike own144, the hypothesis is an energy bound with an upper obstacle bound. The obstacles may have arbitrarily negative coefficients and arbitrarily large absolute norm.

## 7. Ordered energy gaps and agreement with the obstacle operator

The derivative error in (6.1) has an additional useful consequence.     |integral h d(mu_f-mu_g)|
       <=Delta/|t|+K_c |t|+2 epsilon_c,                  (7.1)

where epsilon_c tends to zero and the constants depend only on the common upper and energy bounds and the fixed direction. A common constant shift makes both obstacles nonpositive. Consequently an ordered pair whose energy gap tends to zero has measures whose original continuous integrals tend to the same value: first let the energy gap tend to zero for fixed c,t, then t tend to zero, then c tend to infinity.
