# Curve trace: nontrivial scalar extension and independence of slicing choices

## 1. A nontrivially valued extension also handles trivial H(s)

The curve boundary argument need not split into trivial and nontrivial
F cases. If F is trivially valued, extend to F((t)) with |t|=exp(-1),
then take its completed algebraic closure Omega. If F is nontrivially
valued, take its completed algebraic closure directly. In the intended
applications F=H(s) extends a trivially valued characteristic-zero field,
so its residue field has characteristic zero; Omega retains that property.
Omega is complete, algebraically closed and nontrivially valued, exactly
the standing scope of Wanner1801.04713v3 LF168--174.

The actual common-neighborhood rational tropical PL approximation of a
locally psh function pulls back under this field extension. Continuous
convex PL weights have positive CLD curvature by local convex smoothing;
Wanner4.7 gives the subharmonic disk comparison. Thuillier3.1.8(ii) passes
through the decreasing sequence. Thus the boundary trace proof of
continuation66 applies over Omega in either case. Trace base change is
the finite Artinian fiber-dimension identity proved in the determinant
note of continuation67. It does not require already regularizable w.

For clarity, the tropical convex convention here includes the corrected
support inequality f(x')>=l(x')+C when f(x)=l(x)+C. The source's literal
Definition3.1(3), omitting C from that inequality, does not establish
convexity and is not used to infer current positivity. The correction
and its effects must be stated in the integrated proof, as in continuation65.

After splitting a finite closed-point fiber over Omega, choose small local
disk coordinates z_i at its smooth source points and t at the target.
The finite curve map has t=c_i z_i^(e_i) times a unit. For a sufficiently
small disk the unit is analytically 1-close to a nonzero constant. Since
|e_i|=1, the convergent binomial e_i-th root changes coordinates to give
t=c_i y_i^(e_i). A target Gauss point of radius r in |Omega^*| has exactly
one source Gauss point on each such disk, of radius
(r/|c_i|)^(1/e_i) and fiber degree e_i. The residue extension at that
Gauss point has degree e_i (the reduction equation Y^(e_i)=T is
irreducible over the rational-function residue field), so this is the
dimension in the actual analytic fiber algebra, not an unweighted count.
Properness excludes other preimages for sufficiently small target disks.

On each closed disk the subharmonic maximum principle bounds the value
at its center by its value at the single boundary Gauss point. Upper
semicontinuity gives the reverse limiting inequality. This proves radial
convergence to the center value even when it is -infinity; identically
-infinity restricted components are handled separately. Taking the finite
weighted sum and using local upper bounds proves the trace equality at
the target center. Ordinary punctured-neighborhood convergence is neither
needed nor generally true. The entire proof is to be independently checked.

## 2. A fixed curve may fail to lift to a given birational model

Wu6.12 takes a slicing line first and a proper birational model witnessing
weak pshness later. Its assertion that the normalized slicing curve
automatically maps to the proper transform must be checked: a curve can
be contained in the image of the model's exceptional locus. Merely being
generically in the finite map's etale locus does not prevent this.

The following test-function argument can make the slicing multiplicities
independent of the chosen dense open and line before using a specially
adapted line for a particular weakly psh function.

Keep the original algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero
ground field k, an integral affine Y and finite surjective pi:Y->S, with S
affine open in A^n. Choose any dense principal open W in S such that
pi^-1(W)->W is finite etale. At s take F=H(s), its F-rational lift s',
and a line through s' generically contained in W_F. Reduce the scheme
preimage and normalize to obtain the smooth finite-flat curve q:D->C.
Each component of the scheme preimage has dimension one by the height
bound and finiteness; the normalization therefore covers all points over
s'. The etale generic fiber is reduced of degree d=deg(pi), so q has
degree d. It supplies a positive integer weight m_(y,C) at each original
analytic fiber point y, by summing the actual q-fiber dimensions over
the normalization points above y. The total is d.

Fix a globally affine FS test h on Y. On U=pi^-1(W), choose an actual
nonnegative affine FS exhaustion v_U including its localized algebra
generators. Then max(h,v_U/m-m) decreases to h|U and is actual affine FS
on U: the extra branches include the inverse defining function and the
original generators, with a rational floor. Thus h|U is already globally
FS-regularizable. The direct finite-flat determinant lemma proves that
N_pi(h)|W is globally FS-regularizable on W.

Because pi is finite, every source coordinate satisfies a monic equation
with base coefficients. The non-Archimedean root bound gives
h<=A g_S+B on all of Y for a fixed base affine FS exhaustion g_S and
some rational A>0. Thus its trace over W satisfies the same upper-growth
bound, multiplied by d. The legitimate already-regularizable removable
extension theorem on the **smooth original-ground-field** S gives a unique
regularizable v_h on S extending that trace. This is the repaired smooth
extension, not arbitrary-H(s) regularization.

The pullback of h to every normalized slicing curve is locally psh directly,
without choosing a birational witness. Apply the radial boundary trace
argument above to v_h and h on that curve, and obtain

    v_h(s)=sum_(y over s) m_(y,C) h(y).

Different W choices give the same v_h by uniqueness of smooth removable
extension, since their traces agree on their common dense open. Hence every
allowed slicing curve gives the same weighted sum for every global affine
FS test h.

These tests determine a measure on the finite fiber. Differences of affine
FS functions form a rational vector lattice: sums and maxima are closed,
and max(h_1-g_1,h_2-g_2)=max(h_1+g_2,h_2+g_1)-g_1-g_2.
Constants belong to this lattice as differences. Distinct analytic points
have distinct regular-function seminorms. A truncated log norm, with very
low added coordinate-generator branches, separates any prescribed pair
on the finite fiber. Thus the lattice separates these points. The elementary
finite-set lattice approximation argument, or real closure followed by
Stone--Weierstrass, makes its restrictions dense in all real functions on
that finite set. Equal FS test integrals therefore force equal individual
weights. This proves the proposed independence of m_(y,C) from W and C.

Now let u be weakly psh, witnessed by a proper birational mu:Ytilde->Y.
Shrink W further so mu is an isomorphism over pi^-1(W), as well as pi
etale there; finiteness sends the proper exceptional-image subset to a
proper closed subset of S. Choose a line through s' generically in this
smaller W_F. The generic inverse to mu lifts its normalized preimage curve;
properness and the valuative criterion extend the lift across each closed
point of the smooth curve. Pulling back u o mu gives the required actual
local psh function w on D. The radial boundary argument proves the trace
formula for u at s. The test-function independence just proved makes its
weights the fixed multiplicities of pi, independent of this u-specific
choice. This removes the hidden curve-lifting dependency from the proposed
repair and targets exactly Wu6.12 Step3.
