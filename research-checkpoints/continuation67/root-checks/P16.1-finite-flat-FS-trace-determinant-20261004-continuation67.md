# A direct determinant proof of the finite-flat affine FS trace

Unreviewed root proof proposal, 2026-10-04. This supplies a possible
independent replacement for Wu6.11's projective norm-metric/model-gluing
proof. No original problem is closed and no active worker receives it.

Let A be an irreducible affine coordinate algebra over a complete
non-Archimedean field K of characteristic zero, and B a reduced affine
algebra finite locally free of rank d over A. For s in (Spec A)^an the
fiber algebra is

    B_s=B tensor_A H(s)=prod_(y over s) B_(s,y),
    m_y=dim_(H(s)) B_(s,y), sum_y m_y=d.

These are the actual multiplicities of Wu Equation6.1, including fiber
nilpotents and finite residue extensions. The trace of a function is
N(u)(s)=sum_y m_y u(y).

Suppose u is actual affine FS on Spec B:

    u=max(lambda, c_i log|f_i|+C_i),

where f_i generate B over K, c_i positive rational and C_i,lambda rational.
Clear all c_i with a positive integer D. Put b_0=1,
b_i=f_i^(D c_i), w_0=D lambda and w_i=D C_i. Thus

    D u=log max_(i>=0)(exp(w_i)|b_i|).

The finite locally free algebra has a basis-independent norm polynomial

    P(T_0,...,T_r)=det_B(mult_(sum_i T_i b_i))
                 =sum_(|beta|=d) a_beta T^beta, a_beta in A.

The determinant is the scalar endomorphism of the invertible A-module
det_A B, so its coefficients are globally in A even when B is not free.
It is homogeneous of degree d and has a_(d,0,...,0)=1.

At s extend the complete residue field F=H(s) to its completed algebraic
closure. The finite algebra B_s splits into local Artinian factors with
residue field Fhat. If the geometric factor at x has length l_x, then
the determinant of multiplication by sum_i T_i b_i is

    prod_x (sum_i T_i b_i(x))^(l_x).

This follows by a composition series for the local Artinian algebra:
each successive quotient is its residue field and multiplication induces
the same scalar on every quotient. The total lengths over the points
restricting to y sum to m_y, including the residue-field degree.

The weighted Gauss norm with radii exp(w_i) is multiplicative for every
positive real choice of radii. For a linear polynomial its value is
max_i exp(w_i)|b_i(x)|. Multiplicativity can be checked by the associated
graded algebra, or by the usual Gauss lemma after adjoining radius-valued
variables; no rationality of radii in the field's value group is required.
Scalar extension preserves coefficient norms. Hence the exact identity is

    D N(u)(s)=log max_beta(exp(sum_i beta_i w_i)|a_beta(s)|).

Equivalently

    N(u)=max_beta(D^(-1)log|a_beta|+D^(-1)sum_i beta_i w_i).

This is a finite regular-function maximum with positive rational logarithm
coefficients, rational intercepts, and the rational constant floor d lambda.
No reduced-fiber assumption, model-PL identification or projective envelope
is involved. Zero coefficients are discarded.

It remains to verify the exact affine FS **algebra-generator** condition;
the norm coefficients alone need not generate A. Choose generators g_j of
A over K. Their images in B are polynomials in the given f_i. Polynomial
degree bounds and the positive c_i give rational eps_j>0 and rational
M_j sufficiently large such that on all of (Spec B)^an,

    u(y)>=eps_j log|g_j(pi(y))|-M_j.

For example bound |g_j| by a coefficient maximum times
max(1,|f_1|,...,|f_r|)^(e_j), and use
max_i log|f_i|<=max(0,(u-min_i C_i)/min_i c_i) with the floor u>=lambda.
Constants are absorbed into M_j; a constant g_j causes no difficulty.
This gives a bound globally, not just on a compact set. Summing with
positive multiplicities yields

    N(u)(s)>=d eps_j log|g_j(s)|-d M_j.

Add these dominated generator branches to the displayed finite maximum.
Its value is unchanged, while its list now explicitly contains the original
generators g_j with positive rational coefficients and rational constants.
It is therefore **actual affine FS on Spec A**.

For u_k decreasing in affine FS to a generically finite u, the fixed finite
fiber sum gives N(u_k) decreasing to N(u), also if one term tends to
-infinity. Upper growth descends through the finite map by polynomial
bounds and the constant rank. Thus the norm of an already globally
FS-regularizable upper-growth function is globally FS-regularizable.
Generic finiteness of the trace must be checked in each application; it
does not follow merely from a formal finite sum if every fiber meets a
polar locus. In the intended irreducible source application the retained
generic/divisorial finiteness and degree supply the required check.

If u is continuous, on the compact inverse image of each compact base
set the continuous FS approximants converge uniformly by Dini. Their
traces differ by at most d times the source uniform error. Thus N(u) is
continuous, with no use of flat analytic openness needed in this step.

Scalar extension preserves the norm polynomial and its coefficient
formula, proving trace base change for FS inputs and their decreasing
limits. More generally the dimension identity for each finite Artinian
fiber algebra proves base change of the trace of any fiber-constant
pulled-back function directly: aggregate local dimensions over all points
restricting to one original y. This is the algebraic base-change input in
continuation66's proposed curve boundary replacement and has no FS
regularizability antecedent.

This determinant calculation should be independently reconstructed with
the genuine analytic finite-fiber algebra convention. It targets the
finite-flat part of Wu6.12. Extending across its nonflat/branch locus still
needs the smooth-ground-field removable theorem and the curve boundary
argument, with a valid proper-transform lift and consistent multiplicities.
Those separate steps are not conclusions of the determinant identity.
