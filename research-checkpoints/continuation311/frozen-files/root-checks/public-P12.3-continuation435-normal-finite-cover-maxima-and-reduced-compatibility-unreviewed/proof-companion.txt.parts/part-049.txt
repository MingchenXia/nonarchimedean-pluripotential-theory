\section{n2. An unconditional primary mixed-multiplicity
kernel}\label{n2.-an-unconditional-primary-mixed-multiplicity-kernel}

Use the domain and full-boundary conventions of n1. This note proves the
algebraic kernel needed for moment measures, with arbitrary
characteristic, residue field, non-Cohen--Macaulay rings, and
non-equidimensional reduced components allowed. It proves no geometric
Monge--Ampere operator.

Here are the precise published applications. In the CURRENT July 2024
author version of Swanson--Huneke, Theorem 17.4.2 says that for a
Noetherian local ring, finite module \(M\), and primary ideals
\(I_1,\ldots,I_s\), the function
\(\ell_R(M/I_1^{t_1}\cdots I_s^{t_s}M)\) is a rational polynomial of
total degree \(\dim M\) for all sufficiently large independent integer
\(t_i\). Definition 17.4.3 takes its degree-\(\dim R\) coefficients,
using factorial denominators, as mixed multiplicities; they are zero if
\(\dim M<\dim R\). These statements and their full immediate proof were
read. We use this degree convention even when components have different
dimensions.

The complete 17.2.2 proof gives a nonempty Zariski open set of choices
for a superficial element in \(I_1/\mathfrak m I_1\), with finitely many
prescribed primes avoidable when \(I_1\) is not contained in them.
Complete 17.2.4 and 17.4.6 show that a superficial element \(x\in I_1\)
avoiding the minimal primes reduces a mixed multiplicity having at least
one \(I_1\) entry to the corresponding mixed multiplicity over \(R/xR\)
with that entry removed, when \(\dim R>1\). No regular sequence or
Cohen--Macaulay hypothesis is required. The finite annihilator
correction in dimension one is part of the source, but we do not need
that formula to compare the last ideal.

Finite residue fields cause no restriction. Pass to
\(R(Z)=R[Z]_{\mathfrak m R[Z]}\) solely for these algebraic proofs.
Lemma 8.4.2 gives faithful flatness, equal dimension, preservation of
primary ideals and lengths. Explicitly, a composition series of any
finite-length \(R\)-module tensors to a series with factors
\(\kappa(\mathfrak m)(Z)\), so its length is unchanged. Thus every
multigraded length polynomial and its coefficients are unchanged. No
identification of boundaries across this transcendental extension is
used.

Write \(e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n)\) for the coefficient with one entry of each
ideal. Product and power linearity follow directly from the polynomial:
replace the variables for \(I\) and \(J\) by the same variable to
represent \((IJ)^t\). The coefficient with exponent one in that variable
and one in the other \(n-1\) variables receives exactly the terms with
exponent one in \(I\) or in \(J\). Hence
\[e_R(IJ,I_2,\ldots,I_n)=e_R(I,I_2,\ldots,I_n)+e_R(J,I_2,\ldots,I_n),\]
and \(e_R(I^r,I_2,\ldots,I_n)=r e_R(I,I_2,\ldots,I_n)\). Relabelling
gives symmetry. If an ideal is the unit ideal, its length polynomial is
independent of that variable, so the corresponding mixed multiplicity is
zero. This convention is compatible with the identities.

We give the single-slot inclusion proof, rather than trying to compare
individual coefficients of unequal multivariate polynomials. Suppose
\(J\subset K\) are primary and the other \(n-1\) ideals are fixed. Over
an infinite residue field, choose in the second slot an element
superficial for BOTH tuples \((I_2,I_3,\ldots,I_n,J)\) and
\((I_2,I_3,\ldots,I_n,K)\), and avoiding all minimal primes. The two
allowed sets are nonempty opens in the same finite-dimensional vector
space \(I_2/\mathfrak m I_2\), obtained by removing finitely many proper
subspaces in the 17.2.2 proof. Their intersection still has a rational
point over an infinite field. The avoidance conditions can be included
in that finite union. Each quotient has dimension one less: avoiding the
minimal primes gives the upper bound, and lifting a system of parameters
from the quotient and using the height theorem gives the lower bound.
The quotient images of \(J,K\) remain primary and included. Apply 17.4.6
to both tuples. Repeat in the remaining fixed slots, choosing
simultaneous superficial elements afresh in each quotient. In dimension
one, \(J^N\subset K^N\) gives
\[\ell(R/J^N)\ge\ell(R/K^N),\qquad e(J)\ge e(K)\] by dividing by \(N\)
and using the one-variable Hilbert--Samuel polynomial. Thus, in every
dimension,
\[J\subset K\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e_R(J,I_2,\ldots,I_n)\ge e_R(K,I_2,\ldots,I_n).\]
The same reduction to a one-dimensional primary multiplicity proves
positivity for primary slots. The positivity of the last one-variable
leading coefficient is Theorem 11.1.3 and Definition 11.1.5, whose
immediate proof/context were read. Unit slots give zero. These arguments
also work for the nonreduced quotients created by superficial elements.

Integral-closure invariance has a separate proof. If
\(K\subset\overline J\), put \(L=J+K\). Each degree-one generator \(xt\)
of \(R[Lt]\) is integral over \(R[Jt]\) by its homogeneous
ideal-integrality equation. Thus \(R[Lt]\) is a finite graded
\(R[Jt]\)-module. A finite homogeneous generating list, with degrees at
most \(h\), gives \(L^{t+h}\subset J^t\) for all \(t\ge1\): its degree
\(t+h\) coefficients lie in \(J^{t+h-d}\subset J^t\). Also
\(J^t\subset L^t\). After multiplying by any fixed products
\(A=I_2^{t_2}\cdots I_s^{t_s}\), this gives
\[\ell(R/L^tA)\le\ell(R/J^tA)\le\ell(R/L^{t+h}A).\] On every ray of
positive integral exponents, divide by the common large parameter to the
power \(n\). The two outside polynomials have the same degree-\(n\)
homogeneous part; the fixed shift \(h\) contributes only smaller
degrees. Thus the degree-\(n\) parts for \(J\) and \(L\) agree on every
positive rational ray and therefore as polynomials. All mixed
coefficients agree. If \(J,K\) have equal integral closure, apply this
twice to \(J+K\). This proves invariance without transferring
coefficient inequalities from a length inequality. The finite graded
argument is proved here and does not require finite normalization of a
Rees algebra.

Finally, the order used in these moments is the order of functions on
ALL \(X_{\mathfrak a}\). For primary or unit \(J,K\),
\[v(K)\ge v(J)\text{ on }X_{\mathfrak a}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\subset\overline J.\]
For proper primary \(J\), apply the complete Swanson--Huneke 6.8.4
centred rank-one DVR criterion to each domain \(R/P\) for
\(P\in\operatorname{Min}R\). Each quotient has positive dimension: in a
positive-dimensional reduced local ring the maximal ideal cannot be
minimal. Normalize these DVRs by the nonzero primary image of
\(\mathfrak a\); they are actual points of \(X_{\mathfrak a}\) with
support \(P\). Then 6.8.3 (or 1.1.5 componentwise integral dependence)
assembles the conclusion. Conversely a monic ideal-integrality equation
and the unique-smallest-term argument in the quotient by any
semivaluation support give the inequality at that support; primary
values are finite. For \(J=R\) the conclusion is automatic. For \(K=R\)
and proper \(J\) the inequality is impossible on the nonempty section.
These cover unit cases. Nonminimal supports are not removed merely
because the criterion can be tested on a subset.

For rational profiles \(u_i=-v(I_i)/k_i\) and a primary ideal test
\(v(J)\), define
\[B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))=\frac{e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_p,J)}{k_1\cdots k_p}.\]
For \(p=0\) this means \(e_R(J)\). A zero input may be represented by
the unit ideal and gives zero if \(p\ge1\). Equal rational profiles
have, after integer powers, equal primary ideal values, hence equal
integral closures; invariance and power linearity make the definition
independent of presentation. Any rational linear relation among ideal
tests becomes \(v(A)=v(B)\) after synchronizing denominators and
multiplying ideals, so the same argument makes the test functional
well-defined. If a rational test function \(v(A)-v(B)\) is nonnegative,
the criterion gives \(A\subset\overline B\). Since \(A+B\) has the same
integral closure as \(B\), inclusion monotonicity and invariance give
\(e(\boldsymbol I,A)\ge e(\boldsymbol I,B)\). Thus the functional is
positive on the whole rational test lattice.

Input order also follows: \(u\le w\) implies the moments for \(u\) are
at least those for \(w\) in that slot. For \(u=-v(I)/k,w=-v(J)/l\), use
\(I^l\subset\overline{J^k}\) and divide by \(kl\). All slots are
symmetric and additive on addition of negative rational profiles. These
are the algebraic kernel and order laws, not Cartier intersection
comparison or locality.

\section{n3. Bounded real and actual decreasing-net moment
measures}\label{n3.-bounded-real-and-actual-decreasing-net-moment-measures}

Use n1 and the proved algebraic kernel n2. This note constructs finite
positive Radon MOMENT measures \(\nu_{\mathfrak a}(u_1,\ldots,u_p)\) on
the entire boundary for every tuple in \((D^{\rm bd}_{\mathfrak a})^p\).
The name deliberately does not assert the geometric
intersection/locality properties still missing in n7. The proof covers
arbitrary real continuous and bounded completed functions, not just
rational profiles.

For a rational tuple from n2 its positive functional on the rational
test lattice has mass
\[B_{\boldsymbol u}(1)=B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(\mathfrak a))=\frac{e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_p,\mathfrak a)}{\prod k_i}.\]
If \(q>\|f\|_\infty\) is rational, \(q\pm f\) are nonnegative lattice
tests. Positivity gives \(|B(f)|\le qB(1)\), hence
\(|B(f)|\le\|f\|_\infty B(1)\). Uniform density from n1 extends this
rational functional uniquely to a bounded real-linear functional on all
real continuous functions: real linearity follows by rational
approximation of scalars and norm continuity. Positivity persists by
approximating a nonnegative continuous function and adding a vanishing
positive rational constant to the approximation. The compact-Hausdorff
Riesz correspondence identifies it with a finite positive regular Borel
(Radon) measure. Equivalently, in the convention explicitly used in BFJ
Section 4.2, a Radon measure is its positive continuous test functional.
The Riesz correspondence is ordinary functional background at exactly
compact Hausdorff hypotheses, not an extra geometric positivity theorem.

There is a uniform bound on gauge moments. If \(-M\le u_i\le0\) for
every rational entry, order comparison with the constant \(-M\) (first
take rational constants just above \(M\)) gives
\[0\le B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))\le M^p e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J),\qquad \nu_{\mathfrak a}(\boldsymbol u)(X_{\mathfrak a})\le M^p e_R(\mathfrak a).\]
Here brackets mean repeated ENTRIES, not ideal exponentiation;
\(e_R(\mathfrak a)=e_R(\mathfrak a^{[n]})\). For \(p=0\) the factor is
one. No order of measures, as opposed to order on ideal-gauge moments,
is asserted.

The decisive error estimate uses legitimate negative constants in \(C\).
For rational tuples \(\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol w\) in \([-M,0]\) with
coordinatewise sup-norm difference at most \(\varepsilon\), use
\(u_i-\varepsilon\le w_i\) and the reverse inequalities, with a rational
slightly larger error if necessary. Expanding the additive kernel after
adding \(-\varepsilon=-\varepsilon v(\mathfrak a)\) in each slot gives
\[\left|B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))-B_{\boldsymbol w}(v(J))\right|\le [(M+\varepsilon)^p-M^p]e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J).\tag{1}\]
Indeed each nonempty subset of shifted slots contributes a nonnegative
term bounded by \(\varepsilon^{|S|}M^{p-|S|}e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J)\);
summing them is the binomial expression. For \(p=0\) the expression is
zero.

Now take arbitrary REAL \(C\) inputs and approximate each uniformly by
rational profiles using n1. The approximants may lie in \([-M-1,0]\),
which supplies a common bound. Estimate (1) makes every ideal-test
moment Cauchy and independent of approximation. Every rational lattice
test is a difference of such gauges, so its functional values converge.
Positivity and the mass bound pass to the limit, hence give a measure by
the preceding extension argument. Finite uniform density then proves
weak convergence on every continuous test. The same estimate with
arbitrarily slightly larger bounds/errors passes to the real \(C\)
inputs. Symmetry, nonnegative real homogeneity, additivity within the
negative cone, and order on ideal-gauge moments pass to the limit. For
additivity use rational approximations of each summand and their sum,
and for real homogeneity approximate the scalar rationally. These are
properties of the proved moment functional; the proof does not cite BFJ
4.4 outside its smooth range.

For each \(u_i\in D^{\rm bd}_{\mathfrak a}\) choose an ACTUAL decreasing
\(C\)-net \(g_{i,\alpha_i}\downarrow u_i\). By n1 all its terms have the
bound \([-M,0]\) if the tuple has that bound. The finite Cartesian
product of the directed index sets represents the mixed tuple. For every
ideal test \(v(J)\), its moments increase under refinement and stay
bounded by the preceding estimate. Define \(B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))\) as
their finite supremum. This is an increasing limit of bounded SCALARS,
not an increasing limit in the function class. Values for difference
tests converge by subtraction. Positive test functionals and their
common mass bound pass to the limit on the rational lattice and extend
to a finite positive measure as before.

We prove representation independence without a flattening assumption.
Let \(g_{i,\alpha_i}\) and \(f_{i,\beta_i}\) represent the same bounded
tuple. Fix a tuple of indices \(\boldsymbol\beta\) and
\(\varepsilon>0\). Since \(u_i\le f_{i,\beta_i}\), the directed open
sets \[\{g_{i,\alpha_i}<f_{i,\beta_i}+\varepsilon\}\] cover the compact
space. A finite subcover and directedness select one \(\alpha_i\) for
which the inequality holds everywhere. Do this for each of the finitely
many slots. Then \(g_{i,\alpha_i}-\varepsilon\le f_{i,\beta_i}\). Moment
order and the real \(C\) negative-shift expansion give
\[B_{\boldsymbol f_{\beta}}(v(J))\le B_{\boldsymbol g_{\alpha}-\varepsilon}(v(J))\le \sup_{\boldsymbol\alpha}B_{\boldsymbol g_{\alpha}}(v(J))+[(M+\varepsilon)^p-M^p]e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J).\]
Take the supremum over \(\boldsymbol\beta\), send \(\varepsilon\) to
zero, and reverse the roles. Equality of every gauge moment, density,
and the mass bound give equality of the measures. The same argument, now
with limit tuples satisfying \(u_i\le w_i\) and fixed continuous terms
majorizing \(w_i\), proves order on ideal-gauge moments throughout the
bounded \(D\) domain.

Addition of two actual decreasing \(C\)-nets, indexed by their finite
product, gives an actual decreasing \(C\)-net to their sum. Thus the sum
belongs to \(D\) by its definition, with a finite bound. Taking scalar
moment limits in the \(C\) additivity identity proves additivity on
bounded \(D\) entries. Scaling is handled the same way; scalar zero
produces zero. Symmetry is immediate. In particular the
negative-constant shift expansion and (1) are now valid on bounded \(D\)
inputs. No closure under arbitrary \(D\) infima or arbitrary nested-net
flattening was invoked.

The useful exact formula, for every ideal \(J\), is
\[B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))=\sup\{B_{\boldsymbol g}(v(J)):g_i\in C,\ g_i\ge u_i\text{ for all }i\}.\tag{2}\]
Every such \(g_i\) lies in the common bound \([-M,0]\). The order just
proved gives the upper bound; the terms of any representing nets give
the reverse bound. This is a formula for scalar moments, not a new
definition of \(D\) by all majorants.

Here is the precise descending-net theorem. Let \(\lambda\) range over
one directed set, let \(u_{i,\lambda}\in D^{\rm bd}_{\mathfrak a}\)
decrease pointwise to a SPECIFIED \(u_i\in D^{\rm bd}_{\mathfrak a}\),
and suppose all entries and limits have a common finite bound
\([-M,0]\). Then
\[\nu_{\mathfrak a}(u_{1,\lambda},\ldots,u_{p,\lambda})\ \longrightarrow\ \nu_{\mathfrak a}(u_1,\ldots,u_p)\]
weakly, for the net. The membership of the limit is an actual
hypothesis; no new infimum closure is being claimed. To prove it, order
gives a bound above by the target gauge moment. Fix a continuous
majorant tuple \(g_i\ge u_i\). Every \(D\) function is upper
semicontinuous, so \(\{u_{i,\lambda}<g_i+\varepsilon\}\) is open. The
decreasing net gives a directed cover; compactness selects one common
\(\lambda\) for all slots with \(u_{i,\lambda}-\varepsilon\le g_i\).
Thus
\[B_{\boldsymbol u_\lambda}(v(J))\ge B_{\boldsymbol g}(v(J))-[(M+\varepsilon)^p-M^p]e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J).\]
First send \(\varepsilon\) to zero, then take the supremum in (2). The
gauge moments converge to the target moments. They determine convergence
for the rational difference lattice; density and the uniform mass bound
extend it to every continuous test. This proves the actual weak
descending-net theorem for this moment construction. It is not
convergence in total variation, and it is not locality.

\section{n5. Actual geometric curve reference measure and dimension-zero
convention}\label{n5.-actual-geometric-curve-reference-measure-and-dimension-zero-convention}

Use qualified G331 and n1--n4. Here there IS an actual independent
Cartier intersection comparison and locality theorem, in the exact range

and residue field. Arity is zero, so this is a reference measure and not

Let \(S\) be the finite normalization, with local DVR centres \(S_q\),
residue degrees \(f_q=[\kappa(q):\kappa(\mathfrak m)]\), and orders
\(a_q=\operatorname{ord}_q(\mathfrak a)>0\). Every centred semivaluation
of a DVR is a positive multiple of its order: write a nonzero element as
a power of a uniformizer times a unit, whose value is zero. A support
cannot be its maximal ideal, because that would make the boundary ideal
infinite. There is no other nonzero prime support in a DVR. Thus G331's
normalization presentation identifies the entire downstairs boundary
with the finite discrete set \[v_q=\operatorname{ord}_q/a_q.\] A regular
conductor element has finite value on every branch, so ALL fibres in
this curve case are singletons. These are local centres of
normalization, not merely minimal components.

Define the reference measure directly from Cartier order on normalized
curves: \[\mu_{\mathfrak a,R}=\sum_q f_q a_q\,\delta_{v_q}.\tag{5}\]
This definition uses actual residue field degrees; there is no generic
degree assigned to a vertical component. Every coefficient is positive
and finite. The construction is geometric before any ideal-moment
comparison.

We prove comparison with an arbitrary actual primary blowup model
\(Y=\operatorname{Bl}_I R\) and every exceptional REAL Cartier divisor
\(F\) on it. Since \(I S\) is invertible on the semilocal normal curve,
the universal property gives a morphism \(h:\operatorname{Spec}S\to Y\).
It is finite: its graph is closed in \(\operatorname{Spec}S\times_R Y\)

Its image contains the generic point of every component of \(Y\), which
is reduced and generically birational on all components by the primary
blowup construction in G331. The finite image is closed, hence the map
is surjective. It is the normalization of \(Y\): locally it is a finite
normal birational extension with the same total quotient fields. This
special curve argument establishes domination of these actual primary
models. It makes no arbitrary higher-dimensional proper-model cofinality
assertion.

For an effective integral exceptional Cartier divisor \(A\), its
independent one-dimensional intersection degree is the proper zero-cycle
degree
\[\deg_R[A]=\sum_{y\in\operatorname{Supp}A}\ell_{\mathcal O_{Y,y}}(\mathcal O_{Y,y}/(g_y))\,[\kappa(y):\kappa(\mathfrak m)],\]
where \(g_y\) is a regular local equation. The support is a finite
subset of the closed fibre: the finite normalization surjects onto it,
and its fibre above \(\mathfrak m\) is finite. Each relevant local ring
is reduced of dimension one, and \(A\) cuts a finite-length quotient.
Its residue field is finite over the base residue field because it is a
closed point of the proper zero-dimensional fibre. This is the usual
local Cartier zero-cycle, not a degree defined from valuative moments.

Here is a direct normalization proof of that degree. Put
\(T=\mathcal O_{Y,y}\) and let \(N\) be its finite semilocal
normalization. The cokernel \(C=N/T\) has finite length, since
normalization is generically identical on a reduced curve.
Multiplication by the regular element \(g=g_y\) in
\(0\to T\to N\to C\to0\) gives
\[0\to C[g]\to T/gT\to N/gN\to C/gC\to0.\] For the finite-length module
\(C\), its multiplication exact sequence gives
\(\ell(C[g])=\ell(C/gC)\). Therefore \(\ell_T(T/gT)=\ell_T(N/gN)\). The
DVR factors of \(N\) give
\[\ell_T(N/gN)=\sum_{q\mapsto y}[\kappa(q):\kappa(y)]\operatorname{ord}_q(g).\]
Multiplying by \([\kappa(y):\kappa(\mathfrak m)]\) and summing proves
\[\deg_R[A]=\sum_q f_q\operatorname{ord}_q(h^*A).\tag{6}\] This proof
works without normality or Cohen--Macaulay assumptions on \(Y\). It uses
local actual Cartier equations and the finite normalization, not a
replacement by nonnormal Weil coefficients.

the negative sign of a negative divisor. Pairing the independently

\[\int f_F\,d\mu_{\mathfrak a,R}=\deg_R(F).\tag{7}\] The normalized
degree is unchanged by any common primary-model pullback, since its
pullback to \(S\) is unchanged. Formula (6) also shows that the
independent Cartier zero-cycle degree on each such model is that same
degree. This proves actual model independence and exact Cartier
comparison for every real core presentation and every Cartier test in
this curve range.

For a primary ideal \(J\), apply one-variable multiplicity additivity

\section{n6. Precise degeneracies and a locality

separate-dimensional products would have another arity. Nilpotent
generic lengths are not reconstructed from the reduced boundary. In
dimension zero the boundary is empty and the unique energy and measure
are zero. In dimension one the product has zero potential entries,
treated directly in Section 9.

For \(u\in D\) set \(u^k=\max(u,-k)\), where the constant is
\(-k v(\mathfrak a)\) before choosing the section. Sections 2--5
construct a symmetric bounded \(n\)-form \(F\) with
\[F(v,u_1,\ldots,u_p)=\int(-v)\,d\mu(u_1,\ldots,u_p)\quad(v\in D^{\rm bd}),\]
and identify its core restriction with minus an independently defined
supported Cartier intersection. Define
\[F_\infty(u_1,\ldots,u_n)=\sup_{k_1,\ldots,k_n}F(u_1^{k_1},\ldots,u_n^{k_n}),\qquad
E(u)=-\frac1n F_\infty(u^{[n]}),\qquad
\mathcal E^1=\{u\in D:F_\infty(u^{[n]})<\infty\}.\] Brackets indicate
repeated slots, never ideal powers. We prove this class is a cone; it
has canonical finite Radon mixed measures, extended test pairings, full
decreasing-net continuity with specified \(D\) limits, variation and
reference formulas, and exact gauge and conductor rules. We also prove
bounded Borel strict-maximum locality, nonpolar cutoff identities, total
variation convergence for canonical integer truncations, comparison,
capacity absolute continuity, decreasing-net convergence in capacity and
quasicontinuity. These additional conclusions require Sections 10--13,
not just weak approximation.

The polar class is specified precisely: poles of \(z\in D\) with finite
reference moment \(\int(-z)\,d\mu((-1)^{[p]})\). Section 13 proves a
branchwise Izumi reduction identifying that condition with exclusion of
an entire bottom restriction on every top-dimensional normal centre. All
finite-energy functions qualify. A globally nonbottom function which is
bottom on one top-dimensional branch need not qualify. No whole bottom
branch is called negligible. No general Choquet compact-above assertion,
arbitrary-net total variation convergence, equation uniqueness, or
complete P12.6 is claimed.

\subsection{2. Supported Cartier intersection without a coefficient
field}\label{supported-cartier-intersection-without-a-coefficient-field}

Let \(Y\to\operatorname{Spec}R\) be an actual primary blowup, and let
\(E\) be an effective exceptional \textbf{Cartier} divisor. All
components of \(Y\) dominate positive-dimensional reduced components of
\(R\) (G331); its primary ideal is locally generated by a regular
element. Some power of \(\mathfrak m\) kills \(\mathcal O_E\): on
finitely many affine charts the closed-fibre support gives this
containment, and a common exponent works. Thus \(E\) is proper over
\(R/\mathfrak m^N\). Every coherent cohomology group has finite
\(R\)-length. Define \(\chi_R\) as the alternating sum of those lengths.

For invertible bundles \(L_1,\ldots,L_s\) on \(E\), the full proper
Euler-polynomial statement Stacks 0BEL gives that
\[\chi_R(E,\bigotimes_i L_i^{t_i})\] is polynomial for all integer
\(t_i\), of degree at most \(\dim E\). Here is the precise reduction to
its field version: filter \(\mathcal O_E\) by the finite powers of
\(\mathfrak m\). Each quotient is a coherent sheaf over the
residue-field scheme \(E_k\). Tensoring with invertible bundles
preserves the filtration; cohomological Euler additivity applies. An
\(R\)-length of a \(k\)-vector space equals its \(k\)-dimension. No
coefficient-field embedding in \(R/\mathfrak m^N\) is used. For every
top-dimensional integral component \(Z\) the multiplicity in the
resulting leading coefficient is
\[\sum_j\ell_{\mathcal O_{E_k,\eta_Z}}((\mathfrak m^j\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m^{j+1}\mathcal O_E)_{\eta_Z})
=\ell_{\mathcal O_{E,\eta_Z}}(\mathcal O_{E,\eta_Z}).\] This is the full
generic Artinian thickness, not the reduced fibre multiplicity. The
equality is composition-series additivity in the generic Artinian local
ring; the filtration quotients have its residue field as simple factors.
The top coefficient is therefore the usual intersection on this cycle
with these lengths, by the full 0BEL cycle/intersection theorem.

For integral exceptional Cartier divisors \(D_1,\ldots,D_p\) define
\(Q_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_p,E)\) to be the coefficient of \(t_1\cdots t_p\) in
\[\chi_R(E,\mathcal O_Y(\sum_i t_iD_i)|_E).\] Equivalently it is the
\(p\)-fold finite difference; lower-degree terms vanish. It is additive
in the first slots by polynomial substitution. Additivity in the last
slot follows from
\[0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{E_1}(-E_2)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{E_1+E_2}
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{E_2}\longrightarrow0.\] Tensoring by the
variable invertible bundle and taking Euler characteristics shows the
twist \(-E_2\) changes only lower coefficients, so
\(Q(\boldsymbol D,E_1+E_2)=Q(\boldsymbol D,E_1)+Q(\boldsymbol D,E_2)\).

Symmetry with the last slot must also hold when two cuts have common
components. Use the supported Koszul complexes
\(K_E=[\mathcal O(-E)\to\mathcal O]\),
\(K_H=[\mathcal O(-H)\to\mathcal O]\). Their derived tensor is supported
on the closed fibre and is symmetric under exchanging its factors, even
if the cuts are improper. Tensor with \(L\); its Euler characteristic is
computable from either two-term presentation, giving
\[\chi_R(E,L|_E)-\chi_R(E,L(-H)|_E)
=\chi_R(H,L|_H)-\chi_R(H,L(-E)|_H).\] For clarity this does not subtract
undefined infinite Euler characteristics on \(Y\): compute the second
Koszul differential on \(L|_E\), whose two terms have proper Artinian
support, or in the reverse order. Both compute the same bounded
supported complex. Set \(L=\mathcal O(tH+sE+\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}r_jG_j)\).
Take the coefficient of \(\prod r_j\) in the two differences at
\(t=s=0\). Only terms of total degree \(n-1\) with one \(H\),
respectively one \(E\), survive; powers at least two would exceed that
degree. The signs of the two finite differences are the same. Hence
\(Q(H,\boldsymbol G,E)=Q(E,\boldsymbol G,H)\). For \(n=1\) there is only
one slot and no symmetry step.

Every integral exceptional Cartier divisor is a difference of effective
exceptional Cartier divisors by the actual Cartier order-unit assertion
in G331. Extend \(Q\) by differences and finite real combinations.
Additivity makes this extension independent of the choice of positive
parts; the Koszul symmetry makes it a symmetric real \(n\)-form. This is
an independent local intersection, not a definition using
mixed-multiplicity moments.

\subsubsection{Actual cycle projection and nonpure
dimensions}\label{actual-cycle-projection-and-nonpure-dimensions}

If \(f:Y'\to Y\) is a common primary-model refinement, then for an
effective exceptional \(E\) \[f_*[f^*E]_{n-1}=[E]_{n-1}\] as
\textbf{actual cycles before quotienting by rational equivalence}.
Stacks 0AYA, especially its componentwise divisor-cycle projection
assertion, applies separately to each dominating integral component. All
these component maps are birational, of generic degree one.
Lower-dimensional components have no degree-\(n\) cycle. Dimension
comparisons use the catenary dimension convention of 02QL; excellence
supplies universal catenarity. In particular a height-one point on a
dimension-\(d\) component has closure dimension \(d-1\), so one does not
promote a cut on a smaller component to dimension \(n-1\).

One can check the multiplicities directly. At a relevant codimension-one
point the reduced local ring \(A\) is one-dimensional. It injects into
the sum of its domain component quotients, with finite-length cokernel
\(T\). For a regular equation \(g\), the multiplication snake sequence
shows the discrepancy in the two quotient lengths is
\(\ell(T/gT)-\ell(T[g])=0\). These two lengths coincide by the
multiplication sequence on the finite-length module \(T\). On each
component the divisor-cycle projection gives the order times its residue
degree. Summing recovers exactly \(\ell_A(A/gA)\). Thus embedded
thickness of the cut and the genuine residue degrees are preserved;
there is no nonnormal Weil-coefficient replacement for actual Cartier
effectivity.

Apply the numerical projection formula on each proper integral component
of the supported cycle. It proves
\[Q_{Y'}(f^*D_1,\ldots,f^*D_n)=Q_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_n).\] Passing first to a
global Chow class would be invalid here: a principal parameter on a DVR
has supported degree one, whereas its class can be zero in the global
affine Chow group. The supported cycle proof avoids this loss.

\subsubsection{Raw Rees sections and the ideal
diagonal}\label{raw-rees-sections-and-the-ideal-diagonal}

Let \(Y=\operatorname{Bl}_I R\), \(E_I\) its actual exceptional Cartier
divisor, \(D_I=-E_I\), with \(\mathcal O(D_I)\) the ample tautological
bundle. Stacks 0AG7, applied to the \textbf{raw} finitely generated Rees
algebra, gives \[H^0(Y,\mathcal O(qD_I))=I^q\quad(q\gg0).\] Stacks 0B5S
gives Serre vanishing for this ample bundle. The exact sequence cutting
\(E_I\) consequently gives
\[\chi_R(E_I,\mathcal O((q-1)D_I)|_{E_I})=\ell_R(I^{q-1}/I^q)\quad(q\gg0).\]
Compare leading coefficients with the Samuel polynomial: the right side
has leading coefficient \(e_R(I)/(n-1)!\). The left has
\(Q_Y(D_I^{[p]},E_I)/(n-1)!\). Therefore \[-Q_Y(D_I^{[n]})=e_R(I).\] For
several primary ideals use their common product blowup. For positive
integers \(t_i\), \(\sum_i t_iD_{I_i}\) is the divisor of the product
ideal \(\prod_i I_i^{t_i}\) on that model, which maps to its blowup by
01OF's universal property. The diagonal identity and the mixed length
polynomial imply polynomial equality in all \(t_i\). The coefficient of
\(t_1\cdots t_n\) is \(n!\) times the corresponding mixed coefficient on
both sides. Hence \[-Q(D_{I_1},\ldots,D_{I_n})=e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n).\]
Unit slots are zero. This proves the ideal intersection comparison
without using a normalized-Rees section formula or a finite-window guess
for the polynomial's stabilization.

\subsection{3. Positive real Cartier atoms, refinement, and the
fixed-primary
bridge}\label{positive-real-cartier-atoms-refinement-and-the-fixed-primary-bridge}

Normalize a primary model \(Y\) componentwise; excellence makes this
finite. Call it \(N\). For each prime \(Z\) of dimension \(p\) in its
closed fibre, the normal generic local ring is a DVR. Let \(\nu_Z\) be
its order, \(b_Z=\nu_Z(\mathfrak a)>0\), and \(v_Z=\nu_Z/b_Z\in X\). For
negative real curve-nef core divisors \(D_i\) set
\[\mu_Y(D_1,\ldots,D_p)=\sum_Z b_Z\,\deg_R(D_1|_Z\cdots D_p|_Z)\,\delta_{v_Z}.\]
The degree is the field intersection on the proper integral
\(R/\mathfrak m\)-scheme \(Z\), including its actual residue-field
degree. Normalization plus the supported cycle projection in Section 2
proves, for every exceptional real Cartier test \(H\),
\[\int H_H\,d\mu_Y=Q_Y(H,D_1,\ldots,D_p).\] Indeed the pullback of \(H\)
has coefficient \(\nu_Z(H)\) on \(Z\), and \(H_H(v_Z)=\nu_Z(H)/b_Z\).
This accounts for the one normalization factor in the atom, and retains
inseparable residue degrees.

These atoms are positive \textbf{before} comparison with ideal measures.
The restrictions \(D_i|_Z\) are real curve-nef. By the entire qualified
G331 real ample-perturbation result, perturb each \(D_i\) on the
original \(Y\) by \(-\varepsilon E_I\) and approximate within its fixed
finite coefficient span by rational ample classes, retaining actual
negativity. Pullback by the finite normalization preserves ampleness
(01VG) and restriction to proper \(Z\) is ample. After clearing
denominators the ample mixed degrees are positive, by full 0BEL. The
degrees are finite polynomial functions of the finitely many
coefficients, so their limits are nonnegative. This argument works on
nonnormal/nonreduced fibres and arbitrary residue fields; no singular
resolution or unstated normal-model positivity theorem is inserted. For
\(p=0\) the degree is the residue degree of a point.

Refinement independence is likewise prior to ideal comparison. Between
normal component models, a prime on the new model whose image has
dimension below \(p\) contributes zero to the intersection of \(p\)
pulled-back bundles, by numerical projection. A prime dominating \(Z\)
has the same fraction field; the proper birational map over the normal
DVR at \(\eta_Z\) is an isomorphism there. Alternatively the
divisor-cycle projection of a uniformizer gives a sum of positive
integral orders times residue degrees equal to one, forcing a single
order-one, residue-degree-one contributor. Its valuation, \(b_Z\), and
intersection weight are unchanged. Thus the atomic measure is unchanged.
Componentwise normalization of a common product refinement provides a
comparison model. Lower components are not deleted; they simply have no
dimension-\(p\) contribution.

Here is the missing uniform-function-to-intersection bridge. Fix
\textbf{one} model \(Y=\operatorname{Bl}_I R\). G331 gives finite
\(T=H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\subset Q(R)\) and an integer \(h\) with
\(\mathfrak m^h(T/R)=0\). Fix once
\[\mathfrak c_0=\mathfrak m^{h+1},\qquad \mathfrak c_0T\subset\mathfrak m\subset R.\]
This is primary, not the possibly nonprimary normalization conductor.
For a negative rational ample exceptional \(L\) and sufficiently
divisible large \(q\), \(\mathcal O(qL)\) is globally generated. Put
\[J_{q,L}=\mathfrak c_0 H^0(Y,\mathcal O(qL)).\] This is an actual ideal
of \(R\), contained in \(\mathfrak m\), and unit off \(\mathfrak m\);
hence proper primary. On the single refinement
\[Y'=\operatorname{Bl}_{I\mathfrak c_0}R,\qquad D_0=-E_{\mathfrak c_0},\]
the exact image of the pulled-back generating map gives
\[J_{q,L}\mathcal O_{Y'}=\mathcal O_{Y'}(qf^*L+D_0).\] Right exactness
suffices: the original evaluation map is surjective, its pulled-back
image is the indicated invertible sheaf, and the primary correction is
invertible on \(Y'\). This equality concerns actual ideal sheaves with
regular generic generators, not just numerical classes. Thus \(Y'\) maps
to \(\operatorname{Bl}_{J_{q,L}}R\) by 01OF.

At every semivaluation support the pulled-back invertible module has a
generating section with a unit coefficient at its valued centre.
Consequently \[-v(J_{q,L})/q=H_L(v)-v(\mathfrak c_0)/q.\] This is
uniform on all of \(X\), since \(\mathfrak c_0\) is primary. More

\[\frac{e_R(J_{q_1,L_1},\ldots,J_{q_n,L_n})}{\prod_iq_i}
=-Q_{Y'}(f^*L_1+D_0/q_1,\ldots,f^*L_n+D_0/q_n).\] The right side
converges by finite-coefficient polynomial continuity on this
\textbf{fixed} refinement. Uniform functional approximation alone would
not prove this identity or convergence.

Approximate real negative curve-nef \(D_i\) on \(Y\) by negative
rational ample \(L_{i,j}\) in their finite span, using G331, then take
divisible \(q_i\) large. The same \(\mathfrak c_0,Y',D_0\) works for

\subsection{4. Strict maximum identity and ordinary open
locality}\label{strict-maximum-identity-and-ordinary-open-locality}

First take rational ideal profiles \(u=-v(A)/q\), \(v=-v(B)/q\), and
core data in all other slots. Use a common primary model where
\(A,B,S=A+B\) are invertible. The ratios \(L=A\mathcal O/S\mathcal O\)
and \(M=B\mathcal O/S\mathcal O\) are legitimate actual subideals, and
\(L+M=\mathcal O\). At a fibre prime \(Z\) where \(u(v_Z)>v(v_Z)\),
\(M|_Z\to\mathcal O_Z\) vanishes generically and hence identically on
integral \(Z\). The surjectivity of \(L+M\) then makes
\(L|_Z\to\mathcal O_Z\) an isomorphism. The restrictions of the \(A\)
and \(S\) divisor bundles therefore coincide on \(Z\), as do their
atomic weights with the other slots fixed. This proves the strict-max
identity at the atom level. It does not divide a noninvertible ideal by
a rational function on a nonnormal model.

Uniform rational approximation extends it to continuous \(C\) inputs.
Explicitly, take a continuous test with compact support
\(K\subset\{u>v\}\); the gap has a positive minimum on \(K\), so uniform
approximation preserves its sign there. Weak convergence then preserves
the tested identity. On a compact Hausdorff space, equality on such
legitimate continuous tests gives equality of the Radon restrictions to
the open strict set. Approximate the other bounded \(D\) slots by their
actual bounded decreasing \(C\) nets and use B341 weak continuity on the
same fixed test. Thus strict-max locality holds when the compared
entries are continuous and the other slots bounded \(D\).

For ordinary open locality let bounded \(u,v\in D\) agree on an open
\(U\), fix the other bounded slots, and fix a continuous test supported
in compact \(K\subset U\). Choose actual decreasing continuous
representations \(u_\alpha\downarrow u\), \(v_\beta\downarrow v\). For
fixed \(\alpha\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), the open sets
\[\{v_\beta-\varepsilon<u_\alpha\}\] cover \(K\), since their limits
equal \(u\) on \(K\). A finite subcover and directedness give one
\(\beta\) and all its successors. Continuous strict-max locality makes
the tested measure of \(u_\alpha\) equal that of
\(\max(u_\alpha,v_\beta-\varepsilon)\). Let \(\beta\) decrease to its
specified bounded \(D\) limit \(\max(u_\alpha,v-\varepsilon)\), then let
\(\alpha\) decrease to \(\max(u,v-\varepsilon)\). These maxima belong to
\(D\) by its finite-max rule. This gives the tested equality with \(u\).
Interchange \(u,v\) and use the second compact cover to obtain the
analogous equality with \(v\). Finally both maxima are within
\(\varepsilon\) of \(\max(u,v)\), globally; bounded uniform continuity
lets \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). The tested measures of \(u,v\)
coincide. Radon regularity gives equality on \(U\); replacing finitely
many slots one at a time proves full bounded open locality. There is no
measurable-net monotone-convergence step and no increasing-\(D\)
completion step here.

\subsection{5. The full bounded symmetric form and discontinuous
tests}\label{the-full-bounded-symmetric-form-and-discontinuous-tests}

On rational profiles define
\[F(-v(I_1)/k_1,\ldots,-v(I_n)/k_n)=e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n)/\prod_i k_i.\]
B341 supplies its presentation independence, symmetry, cone
multilinearity and order. For entries in \([-M,0]\), negative-constant
shifts and expansion imply
\[|F(\boldsymbol u)-F(\boldsymbol w)|\le[(M+\varepsilon)^n-M^n]e_R(\mathfrak a)\]
if every coordinate differs uniformly by at most \(\varepsilon\).
Rational slightly larger bounds remove rationality restrictions. Uniform
approximation defines the same form on \(C\). For bounded \(D\) tuples
take the finite product of their actual decreasing \(C\)
representations. Form values increase and are bounded by
\(M^n e_R(\mathfrak a)\). Define \(F\) as their supremum.

Representation independence follows from a compact \textbf{continuous}
strict cover: for fixed majorants \(h_i\in C\), \(h_i\ge u_i\), the sets
\(\{g_{i,\alpha}<h_i+\varepsilon\}\) cover \(X\). One index works in
each of the finitely many slots. Thus
\(g_{i,\alpha}-\varepsilon\le h_i\); shift expansion bounds the
discrepancy by the displayed vanishing error. Reversing two
representations proves independence. It also proves the exact formula
\[F(\boldsymbol u)=\sup\{F(h_1,\ldots,h_n):h_i\in C,\ h_i\ge u_i\}.\]
Here all majorants inherit the common bound from the bounded target. The
same compact cover for upper-semicontinuous bounded \(D\) nets proves
full decreasing-net continuity when the specified limit belongs to
\(D^{\rm bd}\) and all entries have a common bound. Addition is obtained
by product nets of continuous summands; scaling and symmetry pass
through the scalar limits. This proves the full \(n\)-form, not just an
\((n-1)\)-slot weak operator.

We need a Radon directed-integral lemma, not general measurable-net
monotone convergence. If \(f_\alpha\ge0\) is an increasing net of
continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space and
\(f=\sup f_\alpha\), then for a finite Radon measure \(\sigma\)
\[\int f\,d\sigma=\sup_\alpha\int f_\alpha\,d\sigma.\] Indeed \(f\) is
lower semicontinuous. Inner regularity of its open superlevels, finitely
many level steps and Urysohn cutoffs show that its integral is the
supremum of integrals of continuous nonnegative minorants. For any such
minorant \(h\) and any \(\varepsilon>0\), the open sets
\(\{f_\alpha>h-\varepsilon\}\) cover the compact space; directedness
selects one index. This proves the formula, also for infinite integrals.
A pointwise increasing net of arbitrary measurable functions would not
justify the same argument.

For bounded \(v\in D\) choose an actual continuous representation
\(g_\alpha\downarrow v\). Against fixed bounded \(\mu(\boldsymbol u)\)
the lemma applied to \(-g_\alpha\) computes the integral of \(-v\). For
fixed continuous \(g_\alpha\), B341 weak convergence in the other slots
identifies its integral with the corresponding limit of \(F\). All these
nonnegative scalar suprema commute over the finite product of directed
sets. It follows that
\[\int(-v)\,d\mu(\boldsymbol u)=F(v,\boldsymbol u)\] for every bounded
\textbf{discontinuous} \(D\) test. In particular this pairing is
symmetric in all \(n\) entries. It is the additional test-continuity
statement needed in the Borel and finite-energy arguments below.

\subsection{6. Conditional Cauchy, full Hölder, and
concavity}\label{conditional-cauchy-full-huxf6lder-and-concavity}

The required mixed inequality is
\[F(a,b,c_3,\ldots,c_n)^2\le F(a,a,c_3,\ldots,c_n)F(b,b,c_3,\ldots,c_n).\tag{6.1}\]
For ideals this is proved by reducing all three expressions over the
\textbf{same} sequence of quotients, not by separately choosing
reductions. If the residue field is finite, pass algebraically to
\(R(Z)=R[Z]_{\mathfrak mR[Z]}\). Full SH 8.4.1--2 gives faithful
flatness and equal dimension. To check lengths exactly, tensor a finite
composition series: each residue-field factor becomes \(k(Z)\) and has
length one over the new local ring. Thus all length polynomials and
coefficients agree. No boundary identification under this transcendental
extension is asserted.

Over the infinite residue field, in the slot \(K_3\) choose a
superficial element simultaneously for the three tuples
\((I,I,K_3,\ldots)\), \((I,J,K_3,\ldots)\) and \((J,J,K_3,\ldots)\),
avoiding every minimal prime of the current quotient. Full SH 17.2.1--4
supplies each allowed set as the complement of finitely many proper
subspaces in \(K_3/\mathfrak mK_3\). Their finite intersection still has
a rational point. At each next slot repeat in the same quotient ring for
the three remaining tuples. Avoiding the minimal primes makes its
dimension drop exactly one: the upper bound follows from avoidance, and
the lower bound from lifting a system of parameters and the height
theorem. Nonreduced, embedded and nonpure quotient components are
allowed.

For completeness the annihilator correction is finite. The
eventual-colon assertion in SH 17.2.4 implies \((0:x)\cap AM=0\) for a
sufficiently deep primary product \(A\). Therefore \((0:x)\) injects
into \(M/AM\) and has finite length. In dimension greater than one its
contribution is below the leading degree, so SH 17.4.6 reduces the mixed
coefficient exactly. At the final dimension-one reduction its finite
annihilator term must be kept. The literal SH 17.4.7 printed colon
length must be read as \[\ell\big(0:_{M/(x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1})M}x_d\big)
=\ell\big(((x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1})M:x_d)/(x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1})M\big).\] The
unquotiented printed colon can have infinite length. In a regular
parameter sequence this finite quotient annihilator is zero; outside
that case we do not discard it or assert a regular sequence.

After \(n-2\) simultaneous cuts, full SH 17.7.1--3 applies in dimension
two. Here is the essential inequality at its actual hypotheses. By SH
8.3.7/9 replace \(I\) by a two-generated minimal reduction \((a,b)\)
without changing multiplicities. The length inequality used in 17.7.1,
followed by the dimension-two parameter bound (SH 11.2.9--10), gives,
for positive integers \(r,s\), \[e(I^rJ^s)\le2r^2e(I)+2s^2e(J).\]
Product linearity expands its left side as
\(r^2e(I)+2rs e(I,J)+s^2e(J)\). Hence \[2rs e(I,J)\le r^2e(I)+s^2e(J).\]
Optimize over positive rational \(r/s\) (dense in the positive reals) to
get \(e(I,J)^2\le e(I)e(J)\). The source proof applies to arbitrary
Noetherian local dimension-two rings with infinite residue field; it
does not assume Cohen--Macaulayness. The same quotient sequence in the
preceding paragraph proves (6.1) for arbitrary fixed mixed slots. Power
scaling gives rational profiles, and uniform and actual decreasing
continuity give bounded \(C,D\) inputs. Unit or zero entries follow
directly, or by adding a vanishing negative primary constant before
taking limits.

This conditional inequality implies the \textbf{full} Hölder bound
\[F(u_1,\ldots,u_n)\le\prod_{i=1}^n F(u_i^{[n]})^{1/n}.\tag{6.2}\] Here
is a finite proof, avoiding an unexplained invocation of generalized
Alexandrov--Fenchel. First perturb each input by a positive multiple of
\(r=-1\), so all relevant form values are positive. On the finite
integer simplex \(\{\alpha\in\mathbb N^s:|\alpha|=n\}\) set
\[H(\alpha)=\log F(u_1^{[\alpha_1]},\ldots,u_s^{[\alpha_s]})-\sum_i\frac{\alpha_i}{n}\log F(u_i^{[n]}).\]
It is zero at the vertices. Whenever \(\alpha_i,\alpha_j>0\), (6.1)
gives \(2H(\alpha)\le H(\alpha+e_i-e_j)+H(\alpha-e_i+e_j)\). If its
maximum were positive, choose a maximizing point with the smallest
number of nonzero coordinates. Both neighbours also maximize. Transfer
one unit repeatedly from \(j\) to \(i\); as long as both remain positive
the same maximum argument applies. Eventually \(j=0\), giving a
maximizing point of smaller support, a contradiction. Thus \(H\le0\).
Apply this to the tuple with each entry once, then let the perturbations
vanish using bounded multilinearity. This proves (6.2), including zero
diagonal values.

Set \(A(u)=F(u^{[n]})^{1/n}\) for bounded inputs, and
\(\delta=e_R(\mathfrak a)^{1/n}\). Expand \(F((u+v)^{[n]})\) and use
(6.2) term by term. The binomial theorem gives \(A(u+v)\le A(u)+A(v)\),
and \(A(tu)=tA(u)\) for \(t\ge0\). This is a seminorm on the negative
cone; nonpure null directions are possible.

For bounded \(U,V\) and fixed bounded negative slots \(K\), (6.1) shows
\[F(U-V,U-V,K)\ge(\sqrt{F(U,U,K)}-\sqrt{F(V,V,K)})^2\ge0.\] The form
here extends algebraically to differences; \(F(U,V,K)\) is nonnegative
and at most the geometric mean. For \(u_t=(1-t)U+tV\), differentiating
its finite polynomial energy gives
\[\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\big[-F(u_t^{[n]})/n\big]=-(n-1)F(V-U,V-U,u_t^{[n-2]})\le0.\]
For \(n=1\) the energy is linear and this second derivative assertion is
unnecessary. Concavity on unbounded finite-energy inputs follows later
by decreasing bounded approximations. Positivity of this signed
quadratic form does not imply uniqueness in null directions.

\subsection{7. Truncations, all majorants, and the first unbounded
approximation
lemma}\label{truncations-all-majorants-and-the-first-unbounded-approximation-lemma}

For arbitrary \(D\) inputs the extended form in Section 1 is well
defined as a nonnegative supremum. Its independent truncation levels are
cofinal with a common integer level. Moreover
\[F_\infty(\boldsymbol u)=\sup\{F(h_1,\ldots,h_n):h_i\in C,\ h_i\ge u_i\}.\tag{7.1}\]
If \(h_i\ge u_i\), then for \(k_i\ge\|h_i\|_\infty\),
\(u_i^{k_i}\le h_i\) and bounded form order gives
\(F(\boldsymbol h)\le F(\boldsymbol u^{\boldsymbol k})\). Conversely
each bounded truncation has the bounded all-majorant formula of Section
5; its continuous majorants are also majorants of \(u_i\). Taking the
suprema proves both directions. This argument does not create a new
definition of \(D\) or flatten nested nets.

Let \(u_i\in\mathcal E^1\) and write \(A_i=F_\infty(u_i^{[n]})^{1/n}\).
Suppose \textbf{bounded} \(D\) tuples \(f_{i,\lambda}\) decrease to the
specified \(u_i\), with no common lower bound. Their individual diagonal
sizes are at most \(A_i\) by order, so bounded Hölder bounds every mixed
form by \(\prod A_i\). For fixed continuous majorants \(h_i\ge u_i\),
the open sets \(\{f_{i,\lambda}<h_i+\varepsilon\}\) cover \(X\). Upper
semicontinuity of \(f_{i,\lambda}\) and continuity of \(h_i\) make these
sets open; compactness and directedness select one common index for
every slot. Thus \(f_{i,\lambda}-\varepsilon\le h_i\). Expand the
\textbf{bounded} form:
\[F(\boldsymbol h)\le F(\boldsymbol f_\lambda)+\prod_i(A_i+\varepsilon\delta)-\prod_i A_i.\]
For each nonempty subset of shifted slots, Hölder gives the
corresponding product of \(A_i\) and \(\varepsilon\delta\). All
constants are target diagonal sizes, independent of the growing sup
norms. Send \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), then take (7.1). Together with
order this proves
\[F(\boldsymbol f_\lambda)\uparrow F_\infty(\boldsymbol u).\tag{7.2}\]
This is the \textbf{first} finite-energy approximation theorem. It has
been proved before assuming \(\mathcal E^1\) is additive or that
\(F_\infty\) is multilinear. It also gives extended Hölder directly by
taking truncations. In particular
\(F_\infty(\boldsymbol u)\le\prod A_i<\infty\) for finite-energy
entries.

Now prove the cone property. If \(u,v\in\mathcal E^1\), \(s_k=u^k+v^k\)
is bounded \(D\), decreases to the already specified \(D\) function
\(u+v\) (G331's addition rule), and bounded Minkowski gives
\(A(s_k)\le A(u)+A(v)\). Applying the compact-majorant estimate used
above to every diagonal majorant of \(u+v\), with this fixed bound, and
then (7.1), proves \(F_\infty((u+v)^{[n]})^{1/n}\le A(u)+A(v)\). Thus
the sum is in \(\mathcal E^1\). Positive scaling follows from cofinal
rescaling of truncation levels; zero is separate and has energy zero.

Only \textbf{after} this cone proof use (7.2) for
\(u^k+v^k\downarrow u+v\) and the other truncated finite-energy slots.
Bounded additivity passes to the limits and proves full cone
multilinearity of \(F_\infty\). Its symmetry, order, Hölder and
Minkowski survive. Bounded concavity and pointwise convergence of the
finite energies give concavity of \(E\) on \(\mathcal E^1\). Globally
bottom has infinite energy whenever \(n>0\), since
\(F((-k)^{[n]})=k^ne_R(\mathfrak a)\); bottom on a zero-degree lower
component can instead be a null direction. Both facts are part of the
convention, not a claim that all bottom sets are polar.

\subsection{8. Canonical finite measures, extended tests, and full
nets}\label{canonical-finite-measures-extended-tests-and-full-nets}

For \(\boldsymbol u\in(\mathcal E^1)^p\), the bounded measures
\(\mu(u_1^k,\ldots,u_p^k)\) have mass at most \(\delta\prod_i A_i\), by
(6.2) with the test \(r=-1\). Each primary ideal gauge moment tends by
(7.2) to \[F_\infty(-v(J),u_1,\ldots,u_p).\] Differences of these
continuous gauges are uniformly dense in all continuous functions by
G331. Their limiting functionals are positive, and the common mass bound
extends them uniquely to a positive bounded functional on
\(C(X,\mathbb R)\). Compact-Hausdorff Riesz gives the canonical finite
positive Radon measure \(\mu(\boldsymbol u)\). Density also gives weak
convergence. The same argument with any bounded decreasing
approximations to the finite-energy targets, \textbf{without} a common
bound, proves independence and weak convergence by (7.2).

For \(v\in\mathcal E^1\) choose an actual decreasing \(C\)
representation \(g_\alpha\downarrow v\). All terms lie in
\(\mathcal E^1\) and have size at most \(A(v)\). Apply the directed
Radon lemma of Section 5 against the fixed \(\mu(\boldsymbol u)\). For
fixed continuous \(g_\alpha\), weak convergence of the truncation
measures and (7.2) identify its integral with
\(F_\infty(g_\alpha,\boldsymbol u)\). Commuting nonnegative scalar
suprema and (7.1), or the same compact-majorant argument, gives
\[\int(-v)\,d\mu(\boldsymbol u)=F_\infty(v,\boldsymbol u)\le A(v)\prod_iA_i.\tag{8.1}\]
Hence every finite-energy test is integrable against every finite-energy
mixed measure. For arbitrary \(v\in D\), use the integer sequence
\(v^k\) and ordinary sequential monotone convergence; (8.1) extends to
an equality in \([0,\infty]\). In particular every finite-energy input
pole has zero mixed measure, since its nonnegative integral is finite.
No \(\infty-\infty\) operation is made.

For decreasing finite-energy nets
\(u_{i,\lambda}\downarrow u_i\in\mathcal E^1\), their diagonal sizes are
bounded by those of the specified limits. Repeat the continuous-majorant
compact cover and use the now proved unbounded multilinearity and Hölder
in the negative-shift expansion. This proves
\(F_\infty(\boldsymbol u_\lambda)\uparrow F_\infty(\boldsymbol u)\) and
weak mixed-measure convergence on the dense gauge-difference tests, then
all continuous tests. The net need not be sequential or commonly bounded
in sup norm.

The exact uniform-energy criterion is: if \(u_\lambda\in\mathcal E^1\)
decreases to a \textbf{specified} \(u\in D\), then
\[u\in\mathcal E^1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sup_\lambda F_\infty(u_\lambda^{[n]})<\infty.\]
The forward direction is order. For the reverse direction take any
continuous majorant \(h\ge u\), select \(\lambda\) with
\(u_\lambda-\varepsilon\le h\) by the open compact cover, and use the
proved finite-energy negative-shift expansion with the uniform diagonal
bound. Then (7.1) gives finite diagonal size for \(u\). The net
continuity just proved applies afterwards. Limit membership in \(D\) is
an explicit hypothesis; this criterion does not assert infimum closure
of the geometric class.

\subsection{9. Variation, reference, primary gauge, normalization, and
smooth
convention}\label{variation-reference-primary-gauge-normalization-and-smooth-convention}

Fix a finite-energy reference \(w\). Define the relative energy by the
absolutely meaningful expression
\[E_w(u)=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{p}\int(u-w)\,d\mu(u^{[j]},w^{[p-j]}).\] Each
of the two negative tests is separately integrable by (8.1), so the
difference is defined almost everywhere and integrable; common poles are
null for these measures. Cone multilinearity gives the telescoping
identity \[E_w(u)=E(u)-E(w),\qquad E_z(u)=E_w(u)+E_z(w).\] On a
finite-energy segment \(u_t=(1-t)u+tv\), differentiation of the finite
polynomial gives \[\frac d{dt}E(u_t)=\int(v-u)\,d\mu(u_t^{[p]}).\] This
formula, endpoint versions included, follows by polynomial expansion of
\(F_\infty\), with integrability supplied before subtraction. Concavity
is Section 7. Zero is the energy anchor; \(r=-1\) is a primary reference
with \textbf{nonzero curvature} and mass \(e_R(\mathfrak a)\), not a
locally flat additive constant.

For another proper primary gauge \(\mathfrak b\), put
\(d(v)=v(\mathfrak b)\) on \(X_{\mathfrak a}\) and \(T(v)=v/d(v)\). Both
\(d\) and \(1/d\) are bounded positive continuous. Homogeneous inputs
obey \(u_{\mathfrak b}(T(v))=u_{\mathfrak a}(v)/d(v)\). The bounded B341
identity is
\[\mu_{\mathfrak b}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak b})=T_*[d\,\mu_{\mathfrak a}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak a})].\tag{9.1}\]
Pair a transformed test with this measure: its \(1/d\) cancels this
\textbf{one} factor \(d\), so the bounded \(n\)-form is gauge invariant.
For unbounded inputs the transformed truncation pulls back to
\(\max(u_{\mathfrak a},-kd)/d\). The numerator
\(w_k=\max(u_{\mathfrak a},-kd)\) is bounded \(D\) and decreases to
\(u_{\mathfrak a}\). It is squeezed between constant cuts with levels
\(k\inf d\) and \(k\sup d\), which are cofinal. Consequently its
diagonal supremum equals the constant-truncation diagonal supremum, also
when infinite. This proves gauge invariance of
\(F_\infty,E,\mathcal E^1\). For finite-energy tuples the first bounded
approximation theorem and the continuous weight \(d\) pass (9.1) to the
limit. The gauge cocycle is immediate from multiplying the ray
denominators; there is neither \(d^n\) nor an inverse-power factor.

Let \(S\) be the finite normalization and \(q\) its local normal
centres, with \(f_q=[\kappa(q):\kappa(\mathfrak m)]\), and restriction
\(\rho_q\). B341's bounded identity passes through finite sums and
truncations to
\[F_{\infty,R}(\boldsymbol u)=\sum_{\dim S_q=n}f_q F_{\infty,S_q}(\rho_q^*\boldsymbol u),\qquad
\mu_R(\boldsymbol u)=\sum_{\dim S_q=n}f_q\rho_{q,*}\mu_{S_q}(\rho_q^*\boldsymbol u).\tag{9.2}\]
All terms are nonnegative before forming the energy, so infinite suprema
commute with the finite sum. These are actual residue degrees, including
inseparable ones; normalization has generic degree one on each reduced
component. Finite energy downstairs is equivalent to finite energy on
every top-dimensional normal centre. The \(D\)-class condition still
requires constancy on the \textbf{full} restriction fibres: singleton
fibres at finite conductor value, exact conductor-quotient fibres at
infinite value. Lower-dimensional centres remain constrained by this
class descent and are not discarded. Arbitrary fibre-constant upstairs
data must first descend in \(D\) by G331; (9.2) then supplies their
energy criterion. This does not define a general finite-map energy
pushforward or solve all P12.6.

For a reduced excellent curve, the entire boundary is the finite set
\(v_q=\operatorname{ord}_q/a_q\) where
\(a_q=\operatorname{ord}_q(\mathfrak a)>0\). The geometric
zero-potential reference measure from B341 is
\[\mu=\sum_q f_q a_q\delta_{v_q},\qquad E(u)=\sum_qf_qa_qu(v_q).\] Thus
\(\mathcal E^1\) consists exactly of finite nonpositive branch vectors.
All residue degrees, units, zeros and mixed-zero vectors are retained. A
branch bottom is infinite energy and has positive reference mass. A
smooth DVR with maximal-ideal gauge has reference mass one. Dimension
zero has the empty measure and energy zero, without negative arity. For
nonreduced rings these formulas use the reduction: \(k[[t]]\) and
\(k[[t]][\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\) have identical reduced boundary and
gauge but lengths \(N\) and \(2N\) for their parameter quotients.
Unreduced intersection multiplicity needs additional generic-length
data.

\subsubsection{Direct comparison with exact BFJ Definition
4.3}\label{direct-comparison-with-exact-bfj-definition-4.3}

Here, and only here, the germ is a smooth affine \textbf{closed} point
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, \(n\ge2\).
G331 identifies \(C,D\) with the full BFJ formal psh class on all
semivaluations, with global bottom adjoined. The complete BFJ Setting,
1.14, 2.3--2.8 and Section 4 give its Cartier evaluation,
closed-simplex/all-retraction extension, and canonical nef intersection
\[\langle Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\rangle=\inf\{\langle W_1,\ldots,W_n\rangle:W_i\text{ nef Cartier},\ W_i\ge Z_i\}.\tag{BFJ4.3}\]
The value may be \(-\infty\). BFJ itself does \textbf{not} name an
\(\mathcal E^1\) space here. Define the canonical
finite-self-intersection convention directly as those formal psh \(g\)
for which \(\langle Z_g^{[n]}\rangle> -\infty\), with energy
\(\langle Z_g^{[n]}\rangle/n\).

We prove equality with our class, rather than attaching a smooth name.
The coefficient order \(W\ge Z_g\) is equivalent to \(H_W\ge g\) on all
semivaluations. Divisorial order gives order on each closed simplex by
the actual BFJ continuity and dense divisorial points; all-retraction
infima then preserve it on every support. Conversely divisorial values
are part of the full boundary. BFJ 1.14/2.2 makes a nef Cartier
evaluation finite continuous formal psh, hence in \(C\) by G331. For an
arbitrary BFJ nef Cartier tuple, B341's exact bounded BFJ operator
comparison together with the full BFJ 4.6 test pairing gives
\(F(H_{W_1},\ldots,H_{W_n})=-\langle W_1,\ldots,W_n\rangle\). All these
evaluations are bounded, so this use of B341 remains inside its grant.
It does not assert that every BFJ model is dominated by a primary model.
On primary ideal tuples the coefficient/generic-length convention agrees
by repeated SH 17.4.6 and the corrected zero annihilator in the regular
ring, as in B341's qualification. Thus Cartier majorants give a subset
of the all-\(C\) majorants in (7.1), with exactly the same bounded form
values.

For the reverse equality, take any nonzero \(C\) majorant \(h\ge u\). On
this normal branch G331's zero dichotomy makes \(h\) uniformly negative,
say \(h\le-c<0\). For small \(\eta>0\), approximate \((1-\eta)h\)
uniformly by a rational primary profile with error \(<\eta c/2\). The
profile is at least \(h+\eta c/2\ge u\), is a nef Cartier majorant, and
tends uniformly to \(h\) as \(\eta\downarrow0\). For a tuple do this in
each slot; bounded form continuity proves equality of the suprema. A
zero majorant gives zero form and needs no approximation, and an input
\(u=0\) has only the zero nonpositive majorant in that slot. Global
bottom is excluded by infinite diagonal size. Therefore
\[F_\infty(\boldsymbol u)=-\langle Z_{u_1},\ldots,Z_{u_n}\rangle,\] and
our \(\mathcal E^1,E\) equal \textbf{exactly} the BFJ
finite-self-intersection convention just stated. This proves the
requested smooth specialization without inventing a named BFJ energy
space.

For finite-energy tuples Hölder gives finite reference mass, so BFJ
4.6's unique measure exists and its ideal moments identify it with ours.
BFJ 4.4 gives decreasing-family continuity: coefficientwise decreasing
nets converge in the weak coefficient topology, and upper semicontinuity
together with monotonicity gives the limit. BFJ 4.8 alone is sequential
and is not used as a net theorem. The DVR and empty cases above are
direct; no singular or positive-characteristic resolution assertion is
inferred.

\subsection{10. Bounded test algebra and full Borel strict-max
locality}\label{bounded-test-algebra-and-full-borel-strict-max-locality}

The real span of \(D^{\rm bd}\) is a product algebra. To prove this from
the actual class, fix \(u\in[-M,0]\cap D\) and set \(q_M(t)=t^2+2Mt\).
On \([-M,0]\) it is convex, increasing and nonpositive. Its tangent at
\(s\in[-M,0]\) is \[2(s+M)t-s^2,\] with nonnegative slope and
nonpositive constant. Finite maxima of these tangents evaluated at a
bounded \(g\in C\) belong to \(C\) and converge uniformly to \(q_M(g)\),
so \(q_M(g)\in C\). If \(g_\alpha\downarrow u\) is its actual \(C\)
representation, all terms are in \([-M,0]\) and monotonicity gives
\(q_M(g_\alpha)\downarrow q_M(u)\). Thus \(q_M(u)\in D^{\rm bd}\) by an
actual representation. Hence
\[u^2=q_M(u)-2Mu\in\operatorname{span}D^{\rm bd},\qquad
uv=((u+v)^2-u^2-v^2)/2\in\operatorname{span}D^{\rm bd}.\] Constants
belong to this span; distributivity gives a real product algebra. No
increasing closure or arbitrary continuous functional calculus of \(D\)
is assumed.

This construction also justifies \textbf{moving product tests}. For
example, with actual bounded continuous representations
\(u_\alpha\downarrow u\), \(v_\beta\downarrow v\), the identity
expressing products as sums of \(q_M\) applied to their sums yields
finite linear combinations of bounded \(D\) entries with actual
decreasing \(C\) representations and specified limits. Section 5's full
symmetric \(n\)-form continuity therefore passes their pairings to the
limit when the measure inputs also move. This is stronger than weak
convergence tested against a fixed continuous function; weak convergence
alone would not justify these moving tests.

For bounded \(u,v\) let \(w=\max(u,v)\) and \(\Delta=w-v\ge0\), and fix
the other bounded slots \(\boldsymbol t\). We claim
\[\Delta\,[\mu(u,\boldsymbol t)-\mu(w,\boldsymbol t)]=0.\tag{10.1}\] For
continuous compared inputs this follows from Section 4 on \(\{u>v\}\);
elsewhere \(\Delta=0\). Approximate compared inputs by their actual
bounded continuous nets, and the other slots similarly. For any fixed
ideal-difference continuous test \(h\), the moving \(\Delta_\alpha h\)
is in the product algebra just proved, with the explicit decreasing
representations for its constituent terms. Full form continuity in
\textbf{all} slots passes the weighted identity to the limits. Thus
(10.1) holds tested against every such \(h\). Uniform density and the
finite mass/bounded weight bounds extend it to every continuous \(h\).
Multiplication of a finite Radon measure by a bounded Borel function is
again Radon: approximate that function in measure by finite simple
functions and use the regularity of the original finite measure. Riesz
uniqueness now proves (10.1) as a signed Radon measure identity.

On \(\{\Delta\ge1/m\}\) divide the zero signed weighted measure by its
bounded measurable reciprocal. Taking the countable union over \(m\)
gives
\[\mu(u,\boldsymbol t)|_{\{u>v\}}=\mu(\max(u,v),\boldsymbol t)|_{\{u>v\}}.\tag{10.2}\]
The strict set is Borel because the inputs are Borel; it need not be
open for two upper-semicontinuous functions. This is full Borel
strict-max locality, independently proved. It is not an attempted use of
ordinary open locality on a nonopen set. For \(p=0\) both measures are
the same reference measure and this identity is automatic.

\subsection{11. Finite-energy nonpolar cutoffs and canonical total
variation}\label{finite-energy-nonpolar-cutoffs-and-canonical-total-variation}

Let \(u_1,\ldots,u_p\in\mathcal E^1\) and
\[U_k=\bigcap_i\{u_i>-k\},\qquad \mu_k=\mu(u_1^k,\ldots,u_p^k).\] We
prove the setwise cutoff identity
\[\mathbf1_{U_k}\mu_k=\mathbf1_{U_k}\mu(\boldsymbol u).\tag{11.1}\] Fix
one slot \(u\), put \(w=\max(u,-k)\) and \(\Delta=w+k\). This
nonnegative bounded span test is positive exactly on \(\{u>-k\}\). For
every integer \(\ell\ge k\), bounded strict-max locality gives
\[\Delta[\mu(u^\ell,\boldsymbol t^\ell)-\mu(w,\boldsymbol t^\ell)]=0.\]
Test it with fixed \(h\) in the ideal-difference lattice. The fixed
\(\Delta h\) is a finite linear combination of bounded \(D\) tests by
Section 10, hence finite-energy tests. Section 8's full form continuity
and extended pairing pass every measure slot to its finite-energy limit.
Density then gives the weighted signed measure identity with \(u\) and
the finite-energy other slots. Divide on the slices where
\(\Delta\ge1/m\) as before. This replaces \(u\) by \(u^k\) on
\(\{u>-k\}\). Repeat the finitely many slots and intersect the strict
sets to get (11.1). This proof uses a \textbf{fixed bounded \(D\) test}
during the all-slot limit, not a discontinuous moving indicator in a
weak limit.

The union of \(U_k\) is the complement of the input poles. Each pole has
zero target mixed measure by (8.1). Therefore the measures
\(\mathbf1_{U_k}\mu_k\) are increasing setwise restrictions of the
target and converge setwise to it. This is the nonpolar construction and
proves its agreement with the canonical finite-energy measure.

There is also \textbf{total variation for these canonical truncations}.
In the diagonal case put \(A=A(u)\). The two measures agree on
\(\{u>-k\}\). On its complement,
\[\mu(u^{k[p]})\{u\le-k\}\le F((u^k)^{[n]})/k\le A^n/k,\] because
\(-u^k=k\) there. Likewise
\(\mu(u^{[p]})\{u\le-k\}\le F_\infty(u^{[n]})/k\). Consequently
\[\|\mu(u^{k[p]})-\mu(u^{[p]})\|_{\rm TV}\le2A^n/k.\tag{11.2}\] For
mixed inputs put \(P=\prod_{j=1}^p A_j\). On \(X\setminus U_k\) use the
union bound and (8.1), for both the target and truncated tuple, with
test \(u_i\). Hölder bounds each such test integral by \(A_iP\). Thus
\[\|\mu_k-\mu(\boldsymbol u)\|_{\rm TV}\le\frac2k\sum_{i=1}^p A_iP.\tag{11.3}\]
These estimates remain valid when a size is zero, by the same Hölder
bound; if \(p=0\) the operator is constant and its total variation
difference is zero. No total variation statement for arbitrary
decreasing nets follows from this argument. Weak convergence for those
nets was Section 8, and is distinct.

\subsection{12. Capacity, comparison, convergence in capacity, and
quasicontinuity}\label{capacity-comparison-convergence-in-capacity-and-quasicontinuity}

For a Borel \(B\subset X\) define the mixed-degree reference capacity
\[\operatorname{Cap}(B)=\sup\{\mu(\phi^{[p]})(B):\phi\in D,\ -1\le\phi\le0\}.\]
Every measure in this supremum has mass at most \(m=e_R(\mathfrak a)\),
so the capacity is finite. It is monotone and countably subadditive,
since each measure is. For increasing Borel sets it is continuous from
below: the suprema over the set index and \(\phi\) commute. We do
\textbf{not} deduce compact continuity from above or the full Choquet
axioms from these facts. For \(n=1\) this is simply the fixed finite
branch measure.

For diagonal finite energy, (11.1) and a tail bound give
\[\mu(u^{[p]})(B)\le k^p\operatorname{Cap}(B)+A(u)^n/k.\] Here \(u^k/k\)
is an admissible capacity input. The proof of Section 11 works unchanged
with any fixed positive real cut level \(k\), taking integer
\(\ell\ge k\) for its approximations. Optimizing at
\(k=A(u)/\operatorname{Cap}(B)^{1/n}\) yields
\[\mu(u^{[p]})(B)\le2A(u)^p\operatorname{Cap}(B)^{1/n}\quad(A(u)>0,\ \operatorname{Cap}(B)>0).\tag{12.1}\]
Zero capacity follows by sending \(k\to\infty\); zero size gives a zero
measure if \(p>0\). For \(p=0\) the capacity is the reference measure
and the corresponding assertion is its tautological absolute continuity,
including a zero-size test. For mixed tuples, positivity and
multilinearity give an \textbf{actual measure} expansion
\[p!\,\mu(u_1,\ldots,u_p)\le\mu((u_1+\cdots+u_p)^{[p]}).\] All expansion
terms are positive measures. Apply (12.1) and Minkowski to the sum. Thus
every finite-energy mixed measure is uniformly absolutely continuous
with respect to this capacity. This measure inequality comes from
positive expansion, not from ordering scalar gauge moments.

\subsubsection{The precise polar class}\label{the-precise-polar-class}

Put \(r=-1\) and \(L_z=\int(-z)\,d\mu(r^{[p]})\) for \(z\in D\). If
\(L_z<\infty\), then, by the extended pairing and \textbf{scalar form
order} \(\phi\ge r\),
\[\int(-z)\,d\mu(\phi^{[p]})\le L_z\qquad(-1\le\phi\le0).\] For an
arbitrary \(z\) take its truncations first, then use sequential monotone
convergence. Therefore
\[\operatorname{Cap}\{z<-t\}\le L_z/t,\qquad \operatorname{Cap}\{z=-\infty\}=0.\tag{12.2}\]
Every \(\mathcal E^1\) input has \(L_z\le A(z)\delta^p\) by Hölder. A
broader branch-admissible reduction is proved in Section 13; no
unrestricted global nonbottom-pole claim is made here.

\subsubsection{Comparison, with equality-locus and reference
curvature}\label{comparison-with-equality-locus-and-reference-curvature}

For bounded \(u,v\), set \(w_\varepsilon=\max(u,v-\varepsilon)\), with
\(v-\varepsilon=v+\varepsilon r\). On the Borel set
\(\{u<v-\varepsilon\}\), (10.2) identifies \(\mu(w_\varepsilon^{[p]})\)
with \(\mu((v-\varepsilon)^{[p]})\); on \(\{u>v-\varepsilon\}\) it
identifies it with \(\mu(u^{[p]})\). Scalar mass order, tested by
\(-1\), says \(\mu(w_\varepsilon^{[p]})(X)\le\mu(u^{[p]})(X)\).
Decompose into these two strict sets and the equality locus. Cancel the
identical contribution on the second strict set and discard the
nonnegative left equality-locus contribution, obtaining
\[\mu((v-\varepsilon)^{[p]})\{u<v-\varepsilon\}\le\mu(u^{[p]})\{u\le v-\varepsilon\}.\]
The positive reference-curvature expansion gives
\(\mu(v^{[p]})\le\mu((v+\varepsilon r)^{[p]})\) as measures, because
every added mixed term is positive. The right set is contained in
\(\{u<v\}\). The left strict sets increase to \(\{u<v\}\) as
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Hence
\[\mu(v^{[p]})\{u<v\}\le\mu(u^{[p]})\{u<v\}.\tag{12.3}\] With fixed
other slots the same proof gives the one-slot mixed comparison. The
equality-locus term has been retained until it can legitimately be
discarded; it is not silently assumed zero.

For finite-energy inputs apply the bounded comparison to \(u^k,v^k\).
The changing strict set is exactly \[\{u^k<v^k\}=\{u<v\}\cap\{v>-k\},\]
which increases to the full strict set; input poles are null. Canonical
total variation convergence (11.2/11.3), and then setwise continuity of
the limiting finite measures, justify the passage despite the moving
Borel sets. This proves finite-energy comparison. Ordinary weak
convergence would not suffice.

\subsubsection{Decreasing-net capacity
convergence}\label{decreasing-net-capacity-convergence}

Let \(u_\lambda\downarrow u\in\mathcal E^1\) with specified limit.
Define the nonnegative integrable gap to be zero on their common poles;
its ambiguity is on a capacity-zero set by (12.2). By Section 8
\[I_\lambda=\int(u_\lambda-u)\,d\mu(u^{[p]})
=F_\infty(u,u^{[p]})-F_\infty(u_\lambda,u^{[p]})\downarrow0.\] For any
