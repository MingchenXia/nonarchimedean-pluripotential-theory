\begin{abstract}
We prove a scoped all-obstacle theorem, including its genuine operator
antecedents. On a complete rational affine space, every continuous convex
function boundedly relative to an actual real rational-geometry PA reference
has global decreasing rational-geometry real PA approximants. A finite
primal epigraph construction uses rational sample positions and the exact
recession rays, rather than irrational maximum walls. Every bounded
continuous obstacle consequently has a genuinely completed envelope and
zero raw contact integral. For finite positive sums of rational affine
$d$-planes, all-continuous envelope regularity and contact together hold
exactly when the distinct planes are pairwise disjoint. Intersecting planes
give a rational bad jet for every actual positive-growth real reference.
More generally a positive complete linear subcycle and a transverse
two-sided tangent line obstruct universal contact. We reconstruct legal
PCC-tree continuity and localized measure continuity used in the
non-PA passage. The entire original P05.2 on general singular or glued
spaces remains OPEN; candidate=false.
\end{abstract}

\section{Elementary convex duality on a polyhedral reference}
We identify a complete rational affine $d$-plane with $\R^d$ using a
rational origin and a basis of its saturated lattice. Ordinary convexity
and continuous PCC convexity agree there: a contained segment lies in
a rational polyhedron containing its endpoints (enclose it in a rational
box); continuity passes rational endpoints to real endpoints. Conversely
ordinary convexity implies every PCC inequality. R230 handles the
possibly discontinuous competitors in the envelope.

\begin{lemma}\label{support}
A finite continuous convex function on $\R^d$ has a supporting affine
plane at every point and equals the supremum of those planes.
For $f^*(p)=\sup_x(p\cdot x-f(x))$, it follows that
$f(x)=\sup_p(p\cdot x-f^*(p))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The epigraph is closed and convex. For $(x,f(x)-t)$, $t>0$, take its
nearest epigraph point. Existence follows by minimizing on a sufficiently
large closed ball. Squared-distance minimization gives a separating unit
normal. As $t\downarrow0$, the nearest points converge to $(x,f(x))$;
a subsequence of normals converges to a unit normal supporting the
epigraph there. Its vertical component, directed into the epigraph, is
nonnegative. It cannot be zero: the epigraph contains a point over
every $y\in\R^d$, so a horizontal supporting normal would pair
nonnegatively with every $y-x$ and hence be zero. Divide by the positive
vertical component to obtain a supporting affine plane. That plane
gives equality in the displayed conjugate supremum at $x$; all other
planes lie below $f$. This proves both assertions.
\end{proof}

A convex finite PA reference is a maximum of finitely many ambient
affine forms, $\gamma(x)=\max_i(p_i\cdot x+b_i)$. Indeed its affine
piece on the interior of a top cell is a global support by segment
convexity; finitely many such pieces cover the function, including
their boundary values. Put $\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{p_i\}$.
Its support function is $h(v)=\max_i p_i\cdot v$.
The reference differs boundedly from $h$, because its finitely many
constants are bounded. Its positive growth implies $h(v)>0$ for
$v\ne0$; in particular $\Delta$ is bounded, full dimensional and
contains zero in its interior.

There is a finite list of rational vectors $r_j$ with lifted heights
$h(r_j)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{recession}
 \operatorname{epi}h=\operatorname{cone}\{(r_j,h(r_j)),(0,1)\},
 \qquad \Delta=\{p:p\cdot r_j\leq h(r_j)\ \forall j\}.
\end{equation}
To see this, take generators of the rational recession cones of the
reference carrier, including both signs for lineality. Their cones
cover $\R^d$: along any ray, finiteness of the carrier supplies a
cell containing an unbounded subsequence, whose defining inequalities
put that direction in its recession cone. On each such cone $h$ is
the reference's recession linear part. Nonnegative generator
representations therefore give its whole epigraph after adding the
vertical ray. Convexity of $h$ proves the reverse inclusion. Pairing
with these generators gives the second equality; a point outside
$\Delta$ is separated from this compact convex hull and violates one
of the inequalities. Rational cone generation follows by taking a
bounded section of a pointed cone after removing rational lineality;
its vertices solve finite rational active systems.

\begin{lemma}\label{dualcompact}
If $f$ is continuous convex and $\|f-\gamma\|_\infty\leq C$, then
$f^*$ has domain exactly $\Delta$ and is finite and continuous on that
compact polytope. Moreover
$\gamma^*-C\leq f^*\leq\gamma^*+C$ there.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Conjugating the two bounds proves the inequalities and equality of
domains. For $p=\sum t_i p_i\in\Delta$,
$\gamma(x)\geq p\cdot x+\sum t_i b_i$, so $\gamma^*(p)$ is bounded
above by $\max_i|b_i|$ and below by $-\gamma(0)$. Outside $\Delta$,
separation and $\gamma-h$ bounded make the conjugate infinite along
a separating ray. Thus $f^*$ is finite bounded on $\Delta$. It is
convex and lower semicontinuous as a supremum of affine continuous
functions.

Here is the needed boundary-continuity argument, which is special to
a polytope. At $p\in\Delta$, its tangent cone is finitely generated,
including both signs for tangent lineality. Choose finitely many
anchors $p+\eta v_i\in\Delta$ on these directions. Every sufficiently
nearby $y\in\Delta$ has $y-p=\sum a_i v_i$, $a_i\geq0$, with
$\sum a_i\leq A\|y-p\|$. Eliminate dependent active columns until
they are independent; the finitely many inverse column maps bound
their coefficient norms, proving this estimate. For a small enough
distance the sum is at most $\eta$, giving a convex representation
of $y$ by $p$ and the fixed anchors. Convexity gives
$f^*(y)\leq f^*(p)+A'\|y-p\|$. Lower semicontinuity gives the
opposite limiting inequality. This proves continuity at every boundary
point as well as every interior point. Compactness gives uniform
continuity. No dual polytope rationality is claimed.
\end{proof}

\section{A global primal regularization with rational geometry}
\begin{theorem}\label{affinedensity}
On a complete rational affine $d$-plane with actual positive-growth
$\gamma\in\PA_\R\cap\PC$, every continuous convex $f$ boundedly
relative to $\gamma$ is in $\D$. In fact it admits finite relative
real PA convex majorants with arbitrarily small global error, whose
carriers are rational. Hence it admits a global decreasing uniform
regularization on rational geometry.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\eta>0$. On $\Delta$, $f^*(p)=\sup_{x\in\Q^d}
(p\cdot x-f(x))$, by continuity of $f$ and density of rational
positions. For each $p$ choose one rational $x$ making this value
greater than $f^*(p)-\eta$. Continuity of $f^*$ makes the same strict
inequality persist in a neighborhood of $p$. A finite subcover gives
a nonempty finite rational sample set $A$ such that
\[
 g_A(p)=\max_{x\in A}(p\cdot x-f(x)),\qquad
 f^*(p)-\eta\leq g_A(p)\leq f^*(p)\quad(p\in\Delta).
\]
Use the \emph{exact} recession list \eqref{recession} and form
\[
 E_A=\operatorname{conv}\{(x,f(x)):x\in A\}
       +\operatorname{epi}h.
\]
This set is closed: its first term is compact and its second term
closed. It projects onto all $\R^d$, and lies in $\operatorname{epi}f$.
For the last assertion, Lemma~\ref{support} and the conjugate domain
give $f(x+v)\leq f(x)+h(v)$; convex combinations and addition of
epigraph recession vectors preserve the epigraph. Every vertical
fiber of $E_A$ is therefore a finite upward ray. Let its lower
boundary be $F_A$. The set is a finitely generated polyhedron, so
$F_A$ is a finite continuous convex polyhedral function. Direct
maximization on its generating vertices and rays gives
$F_A^*=g_A$ on $\Delta$ and infinity outside it. Applying
Lemma~\ref{support} to $F_A$ and $f$, the preceding uniform dual
error gives
\[
                  f\leq F_A\leq f+\eta\quad\hbox{on all }\R^d.
\]

The carrier is rational even when all sampled heights and reference
slopes are irrational. Each lower epigraph face consists of the convex
hull of its active sampled vertices plus the cone of its active lifted
recession generators. Its projection is thus a polyhedron generated
by rational positions $x$ and rational directions $r_j$. On a lower
face the vertical projection is injective, since its affine support
is a graph. The projected lower faces and their faces form a finite
rational complex covering $\R^d$; intersections correspond to
intersections of graph faces. A supporting affine plane carries $F_A$
on each such face. Consequently $F_A\in\PA_\R\cap\PC$ on this
finite rational carrier. It has bounded difference from the actual
$\gamma$ by its displayed global error. This construction never
perturbs the reference recession slopes.

Choose errors at most $2^{-n}$ and add $4\cdot2^{-n}$. Consecutive
drops are at least
$2\cdot2^{-n}-(2^{-n}+2^{-n-1})=2^{-n-1}>0$.
These rational constant shifts preserve the real category and actual
relative growth. They give the asserted decreasing sequence. The
construction does not claim integral slopes.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{finiteenvelope}
For $u\in\PA_{\R,b}$ on a complete rational affine plane, the actual
unrestricted envelope is itself finite real PA on rational geometry.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Refine the common rational carrier of $\gamma,u$ by a rational
coordinate arrangement so all cells are pointed. Each cell is a
bounded rational vertex hull plus its rational recession cone.
On it $a=\gamma+u$ is affine, so its epigraph is generated by
lifted rational vertices $(v,a(v))$, lifted rational recession
directions $(r,h(r))$, and the vertical ray. Boundedness of $u$
gives the exact recession height $h(r)$.

Let $E$ be the convex hull of all those finitely many lifted vertices
plus the cone of all those lifted rays and the vertical ray. It is
closed (compact hull plus closed finitely generated cone), contains
$\operatorname{epi}a$, and is the closed convex hull of that epigraph.
Indeed all its vertices lie in the original epigraph and each listed
ray is a recession ray of one of its pieces. Such a ray added to a
point in the convex hull lies in its closure: mix a coefficient
approaching zero at points tending to infinity along that ray in its
original piece. This proves both inclusions.

Since $\gamma-\|u\|_\infty$ is a convex minorant, $E$ lies above
its graph. Its projection is the whole plane, so its lower boundary
$F$ is finite convex, satisfies $\gamma-\|u\|_\infty\leq F\leq a$,
and has the required growth. Every continuous convex minorant of
$a$ has an epigraph containing $E$, so it is at most $F$. R230
identifies the unrestricted envelope with this continuous envelope;
hence it is $F$. The projected lower faces are rational for exactly
the vertex/ray reason in Theorem~\ref{affinedensity}. Thus $F$ is
actual finite rational-geometry real PA. A constant approximating
sequence already checks its operator antecedent.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite contact without an Alexandrov substitution}
\begin{lemma}\label{flatline}
A homogeneous convex finite PA germ on a complete $d$-plane with a
nontrivial flat PCC relation has zero raw top atom.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $h$ be the germ and choose a linear support $\ell$ at zero.
Then $b=h-\ell$ is nonnegative, convex and homogeneous, hence
subadditive. In a flat relation
$\sum\lambda_i d_i=0$, $\sum\lambda_i h(d_i)=0$,
all active $b(d_i)$ are zero. Pick an active nonzero $d_j$.
The relation writes $-d_j$ as a nonnegative combination of the
other zero-cost directions, so $b(-d_j)=0$ as well. Subadditivity
in both directions gives $b(x+t d_j)=b(x)$ for all real $t$.
Thus $h$ is affine along this nonzero line everywhere on the plane.

Take its polyhedral linearity fan with this line as common lineality.
Every face has positive dimension. Recursive divisors on this fan
therefore have no zero-dimensional face after $d$ steps, and the raw
top cycle is zero. Refinement independence retains zero if rational
subdivision creates artificial vertices. The line is rational when
chosen from rational star generators; that is the only instance
needed here. Constants and subtraction of the ambient linear form
do not change the divisor. No subgradient-volume formula is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{finitecontact}
Every bounded real PA obstacle on a complete affine plane has zero
raw contact integral with its finite PA envelope.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Its envelope is finite PA by Lemma~\ref{finiteenvelope}. At a
noncontact carrying vertex, the finite-contact argument in S262
supplies a flat relation. Explicitly, the PC costs are nonnegative;
if no relation has zero cost, their normalized compact section has
positive minimum. Finite separation gives a linear floor $\ell$
with $h-\ell\geq a\|x\|$. Raise the floor slightly within a small
positive-gap ball and take its maximum with the envelope; the strict
outer collar permits gluing to the old envelope. Local supports give
a PC competitor with the same growth and a larger value, a
contradiction. The inserted competitor need not be PA and is not
measured. The PC hypothesis is essential in this argument.

Lemma~\ref{flatline} makes every noncontact vertex atom zero. The
genuine raw measure is finite atomic and all its remaining positive
atoms are contact points. Each term in the contact integral vanishes.
\end{proof}

\section{Legal trees and localized continuity, reconstructed}
These lemmas apply on any of the finite embedded positive balanced
spaces with actual positive-growth reference, not just affine planes.
They do not enlarge $\D$.

\begin{lemma}\label{tree}
Let $u,v\in C_b(X)$ have norms at most $C$ and choose $c$ such that
$H=\gamma+c\geq0$ is proper. For every $R>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{treeestimate}
 |P_\gamma(u)(x)-P_\gamma(v)(x)|
 \leq \sup_{\{H\leq R\}}|u-v|+
            \frac{2C(H(x)+2C)}{R}.
\end{equation}
Thus bounded local-uniform convergence of obstacles gives local-uniform
convergence of their actual unrestricted envelopes, also for nets.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For finite real-valued $F$ define $TF(x)$ as the infimum of $F(x)$
and all legal PCC weighted endpoint values. Each split has its own
rational witnessing structure; the endpoints are rational and the
weights are real. Start with $F_0=\gamma+u$. Its iterates decrease
and stay between $\gamma-C$ and $\gamma+C$. For a fixed finite
PCC, decreasing limits commute with its finite weighted sum. Infima
over the split and the iterate index commute too. Hence their limit
$F_\infty$ satisfies $TF_\infty=F_\infty$, exactly the PCC
inequalities. Every unrestricted PC minorant of $F_0$ is below each
iterate by monotonicity. The limit has the prescribed same-reference
growth and is itself a competitor, so it is the actual envelope.
R230, applied only to this endpoint, gives continuity.

Induction on depth identifies each iterate with the infimum of
terminal costs on finite legal splitting trees, allowing no-op nodes.
To combine near-optimal child trees, distribute a finite error among
the finitely many children. Conversely every such tree gives the
iterated infimum bound. Let $\nu$ be its finite terminal probability
distribution; a global barycenter alone is insufficient for legality.
For an $e$-optimal tree,
\[
 \int H\,d\nu\leq H(x)+2C+e,\qquad
 \nu\{H>R\}\leq (H(x)+2C+e)/R.
\]
The first inequality follows from the upper near-optimal cost and
$-u\leq C$, not from normalization of any raw measure. Evaluate the
same legal tree with obstacle $v$, split its endpoint cost difference
at $H=R$, and let $e\downarrow0$. Reverse $u,v$ to obtain
\eqref{treeestimate}. Properness makes each sublevel compact and
$H$ bounded on any root compact set, proving the local convergence
claim. Intermediate iterates need not be continuous, PC or PA and
receive no measure.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{localma}
On the existing genuine $\D^d$, completed tuples converging locally
uniformly to an already completed tuple, with one common relative
bound $C$, have vaguely and weakly $C_b$ convergent mixed measures.
If $u_n\to u$ boundedly locally uniformly and all actual envelopes
are in $\D$, their raw contact integrals converge.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a compact rational PA test $\psi$ supported in $K$, S272 supplies
actual relative real PA PC auxiliaries with difference
$\epsilon\eta_\psi\psi$. Telescope finite tuples and use bounded
raw IBP on the remaining positive balanced one-cycle $Z$.
$D_\psi Z$ is supported in $K$, since the test is zero on its open
complement. Its total variation is at most
$L_\psi=2M/(\epsilon\eta_\psi)$, from the two positive auxiliary
mixed measures of mass $M$. Therefore
\[
 |\langle\psi,\MA(\mathbf a)-\MA(\mathbf b)\rangle|
 \leq L_\psi\sum_i\sup_K|a_i-b_i|.
\]
Pass through genuine globally uniform approximants for each of two
already completed tuples, using S272's completion. The same local
estimate holds on that domain. Controlled-support density of compact
rational PA tests, proved in S272 by rational norm bumps, and common
mass $M$ give vague convergence. Equation~\eqref{grantedmoment}
provides tightness for \emph{all} tuples of the fixed-bound family,
before weak convergence is invoked. Compact cutoffs at $H_0=T$
then give convergence against each fixed $C_b$ test. No entire-domain
weak or BL modulus is asserted.

For envelopes, Lemma~\ref{tree} gives local convergence and R230
gives the common relative bound from the obstacle norms. The gaps
$g_n=P_\gamma(u_n)-\gamma-u_n$ are bounded by $2C$ and converge
locally uniformly. On a fixed compact cutoff, local gap convergence
and vague measure convergence pass their integrals. Outside its
height sublevel, the two tails are bounded by $4CK_C/T$. Increase
$T$ to pass the full raw contact integrals. Every measure used in
this paragraph has its explicitly assumed genuine domain antecedent.
\end{proof}

\section{All continuous affine contact}

nonnegative continuous test take a fine rational net of its support,
rational sampled heights, and finite bumps
$(b_j-L\rho(x-v_j))_+$ with integer $L$ chosen at its continuity
scale. Their maximum approximates it uniformly, has rational affine

\section{A transverse-line structural obstruction}
\begin{lemma}\label{detmass}
On a saturated complete $d$-plane $Q$ with weight $c$, let
$h=\sum_{j=1}^d|\ell_j|$ for rational linear forms, and let $A$
be their restriction matrix in a lattice basis of $Q$. Then
\[
             (D_h^d c[Q])\{0\}=c\,2^d d!|\det A|.
\]
If $A$ is singular the right side and the raw top cycle are zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For one rational form on a saturated plane, opposite primitive inward
normals give divisor twice its positive primitive evaluation on its
kernel plane. Rational scaling reduces this statement to an integral
form. The Euclidean algorithm on the integer row evaluations changes
a lattice basis until the row is $(g,0,\ldots,0)$, where $g$ is their
positive gcd; this constructs the saturated kernel basis and primitive
complement used here. Repeating the \emph{same} factor gives zero since it vanishes
on that kernel. If the forms are independent, successively intersect
their saturated kernels. A basis of each kernel followed by a primitive
complement is a lattice basis of the preceding lattice. In the resulting
nested bases the restriction matrix is triangular; its positive diagonal
evaluations multiply to $|\det A|$. Thus each ordered product using
all $d$ distinct factors gives $2^d|\det A|\delta_0$. All terms with
repeated indices are zero. Raw commutativity and expansion of the sum
give $d!$ surviving permutations. If the matrix is singular, the germ
has nonzero rational common lineality, and the no-vertex argument of
Lemma~\ref{flatline} gives zero. Constants and weights do not alter
these primitive index calculations. The factorial is derived from the
expansion, not appended to the operator.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{transverse}
Let $v$ be a rational point of a finite embedded positive balanced
$d$-space. Suppose its weighted tangent cycle $C_v$ contains a full
rational linear $d$-plane subcycle: for some $c>0$,
$C_v-c[Q]$ is nonnegative and balanced. Suppose its tangent support
contains a two-sided rational line $L$ with $L\cap Q=\{0\}$.
Then every actual real PA PC positive-growth reference on $X$ has a
bounded real PA obstacle whose actual envelope is genuine finite relative
