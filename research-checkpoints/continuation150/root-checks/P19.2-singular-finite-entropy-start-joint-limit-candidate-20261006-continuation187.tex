\begin{abstract}
We give a candidate extension of the joint approximation and large-time
entropy limit to genuine singular finite-energy initial potentials of
finite entropy. It uses the supplied entire-ray entropy classification
and actual model recovery theorem, and reconstructs the uniform
initial-point estimate from the finite-energy mixed pairing bound.
The same argument permits varying initial points with uniformly bounded
entropy. Prescribed singularity endpoints and the entire residual
P19.2 approximation problem remain open. This new argument has not
passed independent mathematical review.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact signature and mathematical inputs}
Let $(X,\omega)$ be a connected smooth compact K\"ahler manifold of
dimension $n\geq1$. Put $\alpha=[\omega]$, $V=\int_X\omega^n$,
$\mu_0=\omega^n/V$, and use
\[
 dd^c=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial,
 \qquad t=-\log|\tau|^2,\qquad
 \MA(u)=V^{-1}\langle(\omega+dd^cu)^n\rangle .             \tag{1}
\]
The energy $E$ is probability normalized, $E(0)=0$ and
$E(u+c)=E(u)+c$. Entropy $H$ is full relative probability entropy
against $\mu_0$, with value $+\infty$ for a measure not absolutely
continuous with respect to $\mu_0$.
The usual datum lives on $X^\beth$ over trivially valued
$\mathbf C$, including semivaluation supports. Write
$H_\alpha(\phi)=\int A_X\MA_\alpha(\phi)$, where the
non-Archimedean measure is a probability and $A_X(v_{\rm triv})=0$.

The following supplied mathematical results retain their whole proofs
and signatures in the accompanying P19.1 paper [R]. For each
$\phi\in\Eone_\NA(\alpha)$ and $w\in\Eone(\omega)$ there is an
actual maximal weak ray $r(w,\phi)$, ordered in both arguments, with
\[
 E(r(w,\phi)_t)=E(w)+tE_\alpha(\phi).                    \tag{2}
\]
A maximal ray with a finite-entropy slice has entropy slope
$H_\alpha(\phi)$, possibly infinite. A datum with finite
$H_\alpha$ admits actual smooth equivariant relatively K\"ahler
cohomological model configurations $\eta_j$ with
\[
 \eta_j\longrightarrow\phi\text{ strongly},\qquad
 H_\alpha(\eta_j)\longrightarrow H_\alpha(\phi).          \tag{3}
\]
These are mathematical premises, rather than a historical verdict.
They do not themselves assert prescribed $P$ endpoints.

We also use the established finite-energy foundations: ordered
$d_1(a,b)=E(a)-E(b)$ for $a\geq b$; supporting energy inequalities;
and the mixed pairing estimate [MP, Lemma 5.3.2]. For mean-normalized
slots in $\Eone(\omega)$, its bound is
\[
 |\Delta\mathcal P|\leq C D^q R^{1-q},\qquad q=2^{-n},    \tag{4}
\]
when pairwise slot $d_1$ distances are at most $D$, and $R$ bounds
their constant-invariant $J$ values plus the fixed
supremum-minus-mean constant. The forms and dimension are fixed.
Here the source's constant-invariant relative $J$ distance is bounded
by a constant times $d_1$; it is unchanged by subtracting means.
Signed smooth forms are handled by multilinearity on fixed forms.
The entire finite-energy extension and decreasing continuity are part
of this bound. In the source's $i/\pi$ and unsquared-log convention,
use background $2\omega$, potential $u_{2s}$, and datum $2\phi$;
dividing by $t=2s$ restores (1). The constant factor in (4) is harmless.

Finally [BDL] gives lower semicontinuity and geodesic convexity of
\[
 M=H+G,\qquad
 G=T_{-\Ric(\omega)}+\bar S E,\qquad
 T_\xi(u)=\frac1V\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
 \int_Xu\,\xi\wedge\omega_u^k\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}.        \tag{5}
\]
Smooth contracted energies are $d_1$ continuous and have the
calibrated maximal-ray slopes $G_\alpha(\phi)$, strongly continuous
in the usual datum [R, MP]. The coefficient of entropy in (5) is one.

\section{Uniform restoration of the initial potential}
For any $u$ let $m(u)=\int u\,d\mu_0$ and $u^0=u-m(u)$.
Set $J(u)=m(u)-E(u)$, so $J\geq0$ is constant invariant.

\begin{lemma}
Let $\eta_j\to\phi$ strongly. Let $w_j\in\Eone(\omega)$ satisfy
$\sup_Xw_j=0$ and $A_j=-E(w_j)$ bounded. Put
$a_{j,t}=r(0,\eta_j)_t$ and $b_{j,t}=r(w_j,\eta_j)_t$.
For $t\geq1$, uniformly in $j$,
\[
 0\leq a_{j,t}-b_{j,t},\quad d_1(a_{j,t},b_{j,t})=A_j,
 \quad
 |G(a_{j,t})-G(b_{j,t})|
 \leq C\{A_j+A_j^q(t+A_j+1)^{1-q}\}.                   \tag{6}
\]
For each fixed divisor-compatible smooth model test $\Psi_t$,
\[
 \left|\int\Psi_t\MA(a_{j,t})-\int\Psi_t\MA(b_{j,t})\right|
 \leq C_\Psi A_j^q(t+A_j+1)^{1-q}.                      \tag{7}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order and (2) give the first two assertions. From zero,
$\sup a_{j,t}=t\sup\eta_j$ and the compact psh
supremum-minus-mean bound give $J(a_{j,t})\leq Ct$;
the constant is uniform because the data's energies and suprema
converge. Since $m(b_{j,t})\leq m(a_{j,t})$ and their energy difference
is $A_j$, we have
\[
 J(b_{j,t})\leq J(a_{j,t})+A_j\leq Ct+A_j.               \tag{8}
\]
Apply (4) to all slots equal to the two mean-normalized potentials.
For the energy this gives
\[
 |E(a_{j,t}^0)-E(b_{j,t}^0)|
 \leq C A_j^q(t+A_j+1)^{1-q}.
\]
Using $E(a)-E(b)=m(a)-m(b)+E(a^0)-E(b^0)$ therefore yields
\[
 |m(a_{j,t})-m(b_{j,t})|
 \leq A_j+C A_j^q(t+A_j+1)^{1-q}.                       \tag{9}
\]
This restores the means without assuming the singular start is bounded
below or changing the background to a singular current. Applying (4)
to each smooth contracted pairing and then (9) to its constant
restoration proves (6), including the term $\bar S E$.

For (7) express the integral as the pairing
$(0,\Psi_t)(\omega,u_t)^n/V$ and replace slots one at a time.
Split the fixed test into smoothly compatible positive model weights
on one common model, as in [MP, Theorem 5.3.4]. These fixed slots have
$J=O(t+1)$, while (8) bounds the changing slots. Constants in the
$u_t$ slots have zero contribution, since their coefficient is an
integral of $dd^c\Psi_t$ against a closed product on the compact
noncentral fiber. A constant in $\Psi_t$ cancels between probability
measures. Thus no term from (9) is needed in (7). The constant depends
on this fixed test, never on a varying model of $\eta_j$.
\end{proof}

\section{The joint entropy limit}
\begin{theorem}[Candidate partial extension]
Let $H_\alpha(\phi)<\infty$, and take the actual models (3).
For every fixed $w\in\Eone(\omega)$ with $H(w)<\infty$,
\[
 \frac{H(r(w,\eta_j)_t)}t\longrightarrow H_\alpha(\phi)
 \quad\text{jointly as }j,t\longrightarrow\infty.         \tag{10}
\]
More generally (10) holds with $w_j\to w$ strongly in analytic $d_1$
if $\sup_j H(w_j)<\infty$. The rays have their actual prescribed
initial points. This theorem makes no claim about prescribed endpoint
$P$-envelopes or exact poles.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Normalize each $w_j$ by its supremum; entropy and measures are
unchanged. Strong convergence gives convergent suprema and energies,
so the $A_j$ in the lemma are bounded. Smooth contracted continuity
and the entropy bound give $\sup_j|G(w_j)|<\infty$ and an upper bound
on $M(w_j)$.

For each fixed $j$, the supplied entire-ray classification applies to
the actual maximal ray $b_j=r(w_j,\eta_j)$: the slice at zero has
finite entropy. Its entropy slope is $h_j=H_\alpha(\eta_j)$.
The lemma and the calibrated zero-start contracted slope imply that
its contracted slope is $g_j=G_\alpha(\eta_j)$. Hence its Mabuchi slope
is $h_j+g_j$. Geodesic convexity, with finite value at zero, implies
\[
 M(b_{j,t})\leq M(w_j)+t(h_j+g_j).                       \tag{11}
\]
Indeed for a convex lower semicontinuous function with finite initial
value, each secant slope is bounded by its finite recession slope;
a positive infinite value at a finite time would contradict that
finite recession slope. Thus (11) is valid also for these singular
initial points.

For zero starts the uniform mixed estimate and strong convergence
give, along any $j_\ell,t_\ell\to\infty$,
$G(a_{j_\ell,t_\ell})/t_\ell\to G_\alpha(\phi)$.
To verify this estimate, compare each $a_j$ with $r(0,\phi)$ using
the common upper datum $\max(\eta_j,\phi)$; ordered energy bounds
the distance by $tB_j$, where
\[
 B_j=2E_\alpha(\max(\eta_j,\phi))
            -E_\alpha(\eta_j)-E_\alpha(\phi)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The supporting inequality bounds the maximum's energy defect by
$\int|\eta_j-\phi|\MA_\alpha(\phi)\to0$.
Apply (4), and restore means using convergent zero-start supremum
slopes and the fixed supremum-minus-mean bound. Divide by $t$.
The resulting error is $O(B_j^q)+o_j(1)+O(t^{-1})$.
Equation (6) now gives the same joint contracted limit for $b_j$.
Since $h_j\to H_\alpha(\phi)$, $g_j\to G_\alpha(\phi)$ and
$M(w_j)/t_\ell\to0$, (11) proves the entropy upper bound.

For the lower bound fix one smooth dominating SNC test model
$\mathcal Y$ before both limits. Its log-canonical fiber volume
$\mu_{\mathcal Y,t}$ has test
$\Psi_t=\log(\mu_{\mathcal Y,t}/\mu_0)$ and
\[
 \int_X\mu_{\mathcal Y,t}\leq C_\mathcal Y(1+t)^n,
 \quad f_\mathcal Y=A_X\circ r_\mathcal Y.
\]
These are the calibrated volumes of [R]; their local logarithmic
radial domains are weighted simplices of dimension at most $n$.
Gibbs' inequality gives
\[
 H(b_{j,t})\geq\int\Psi_t\MA(b_{j,t})
                    -\log C_\mathcal Y-n\log(1+t).      \tag{12}
\]
By (7), the normalized difference from the zero-start pairing tends
uniformly to zero as $t\to\infty$. The fixed-test mixed comparison
between $a_j$ and $r(0,\phi)$ has error $C_\mathcal YB_j^q$ after
division by $t$. The calibrated slope consequently gives a joint
lower bound $\int f_\mathcal Y\MA_\alpha(\phi)$.
Only after this joint liminf take the directed supremum over SNC
models. The discrepancy integral identity gives
$H_\alpha(\phi)$. This proves (10). The fixed-start statement is the
constant sequence $w_j=w$.
\end{proof}

The same proof permits varying finite-entropy starts when their
energies remain bounded and $M(w_j)/t_j\to0$ along the particular
joint sequence. The stated uniform-entropy hypothesis is a simple
sufficient condition, not an assertion that strong convergence alone
preserves entropy. For an arbitrary singular start with infinite
entropy, a stationary bounded potential with a singular
Monge--Amp\`ere measure already has infinite entropy slope despite
zero ordinary datum; (10) cannot be asserted without a slice condition.

\section{Remaining original requirements}
The actual models in (3) recover the usual datum, its energy and
discrepancy entropy. Equations (6)--(12) extend the joint entropy
assertion to the precise singular-start signature above. They do not
construct an endpoint-preserving sequence or prove convergence of
prescribed relative $P$-envelopes, zero-mass levels or exact polar sets.
The endpoint-loss example of the separate P19.2 partial paper [S]
refutes a general implication from energy and entropy recovery; it
does not refute existence of a better sequence. No arbitrary-ray,
degenerating-family or automorphism-equivariant extension is claimed.
Original P19.2 remains open, with no change in the solved-problem count.
