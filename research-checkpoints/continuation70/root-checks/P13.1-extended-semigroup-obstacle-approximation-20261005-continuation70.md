# Extended semigroup route to the remaining obstacle-volume inequality

This is an unreviewed root mathematical proposal. It does not accept round60's preceding claims, solve the original problem, or supply an operational instruction. Reconstruct the mathematical statements independently. The proposal targets the exact reverse inequality missing from completed P13.1 round60, without assuming singular envelope regularity or increasing-limit energy continuity.

## 1. The algebraic approximation statement

Let V be an integral projective variety of dimension p over an algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero field. Let L be ample. Replace L by a sufficiently high multiple so that it is very ample and its full section ring R is generated in degree one. This reduction has the usual degree-p volume and degree-(p+1) raw-energy scalings; the normalized spectral mean and normalized energy scale linearly. Fix any bounded multiplicative graded norm on R. Write its additive filtration weight as chi(s)=-log||s||, with |chi(s)|<=Bm for nonzero s in R_m. The bound follows relative to the trivial canonical norm, which is one on every nonzero section. For an obstacle g relative to the canonical reference, the bound is B=||g|| and multiplicativity follows directly from the supremum norm.

Choose C an integer with C>B+1. Put F^a R_m={s:chi(s)>=a} for integer a. Define the extended semigroup

    Gamma={(m,nu(s),a):m>=1, -Cm<=a<=chi(s), a integer, s!=0 in R_m} union {0}.

Here nu is a full-rank monomial valuation at a regular k-rational point x, after a local frame of L has been fixed. Choose graded lexicographic order, so the total degree of nu(s) is ord_x(s). Global smoothness is unnecessary. The smooth locus contains such a point in the present field scope.

The valuation has one-dimensional leaves. Gaussian elimination on leading monomials shows, for every finite-dimensional subspace U of R_m,

    dim U = #nu(U minus {0}).

Indeed eliminate the coefficient of a common leading monomial of two independent sections; each pivot removes one dimension. There are only finitely many possible leading indices in R_m by the linear bound below. Thus no infinite elimination or attainment of an infimum is required. The trivial-field filtration has finitely many jumps in each finite-dimensional R_m, since a strictly increasing flag has finite length. Every jump is attained.

There is a uniform D with |nu(s)|<=Dm. To check this without smoothness of V, take p-1 sufficiently ample general hyperplanes through the fixed regular point x and not contained in the zero divisor of the specified nonzero s. Locally their intersection is a smooth curve, and its vanishing order at x is at least ord_x(s). Its total intersection with div(s) is m times a fixed numerical degree. Bertini and proper intersection must be checked on the regular neighborhood of x; global singular points do not change the fixed total intersection number. For p=1 use the divisor degree directly. For p=0 the quotient is a point and the obstacle identity is immediate, so omit it from the semigroup argument.

After the chosen very ample tensor power, pick degree-one sections s_0,s_1,...,s_p whose local leading terms are 1,z_1,...,z_p. Their values are 0,e_1,...,e_p. Gamma contains (1,0,-C), (1,e_i,-C), and (1,0,-C+1), since all their weights exceed -(C-1). Differences of these vectors and one degree-one vector generate the entire lattice Z^(p+2). This checks both the degree period and the lattice index explicitly.

Gamma is additive by multiplicativity, and has a bounded degree-one slice: nu/m lies in [0,D]^p and a/m lies in [-C,B]. Let Delta be the degree-one slice of the closed cone generated by Gamma, a compact convex body in R^(p+1). It has nonempty interior because Gamma generates the whole lattice and its positive-degree cone is full-dimensional.

## 2. Elementary finite semigroup conductor and counting

Let S be a finitely generated positive-degree semigroup in Z^d whose generators span Z^d as a group. Its real cone is a rational polyhedral cone K with bounded degree-one slice Delta_S. There is c in S such that

    c+(K intersect Z^d) is contained in S.

Proof: write any point y in K intersect Z^d as sum t_i b_i with t_i>=0. Subtract sum floor(t_i)b_i. Its residue r lies in the bounded zonotope sum [0,1]b_i and in Z^d, so only finitely many residues are possible. Each such r, because the b_i generate the whole group, is u_r-v_r with u_r,v_r in S. Set c=sum_r v_r. Then c+y=sum floor(t_i)b_i+u_r+(c-v_r) belongs to S. This proves the conductor statement, including boundary points. No normality of a semigroup algebra or external lattice-saturation theorem is assumed.

Counting between the cone slices and their conductor translates gives

    #S_m = vol_(d-1)(Delta_S) m^(d-1)+O(m^(d-2)).

The upper slice consists of lattice points in m Delta_S. The lower slice is c plus the lattice points in (m-deg c) Delta_S. Since the whole lattice, including its degree coordinate, is generated, there is no missing congruence or index factor. Ordinary lattice Riemann sums for a rational polytope give the leading coefficient and error. This proof also shows why a full-lattice hypothesis cannot be omitted.

For the possibly non-finitely generated Gamma above, take finite subsets of Gamma, always adjoining the explicit full-lattice vectors, and the semigroups they generate. Their degree-one polytopes increase in the directed sense, and their union is dense in Delta. Finite convex hulls approximate the volume of a compact convex body from below: choose a finite inner polytope, with vertices approximated by rational convex combinations of Gamma's normalized vectors. Thus

    sup_S vol(Delta_S)=vol(Delta).

For each such S, #Gamma_m>=#S_m. Conversely #Gamma_m is at most the number of lattice points in m Delta. Lattice Riemann sums for a compact convex body, whose boundary has zero volume, give the upper leading coefficient. Hence

    lim_m #Gamma_m/m^(p+1)=vol_(p+1)(Delta).

This argument proves the necessary lattice asymptotic instead of invoking Boucksom's Lemma1.13.

## 3. Recovering the signed spectral mean

Let N_m=dim R_m and let w_m,j be the spectral weights of chi on R_m. The one-dimensional-leaf identity gives the exact formula

    #Gamma_m = sum_(a=-Cm)^floor(Bm) dim F^a R_m
             = sum_j floor(w_m,j)+(Cm+1)N_m.

The floor error is at most N_m. With N_m=a_0 m^p+O(m^(p-1)), a_0=(L^p)/p!, this proves the signed limit

    lim_m sum_j w_m,j/(m N_m)=vol(Delta)/a_0-C.

No separate convergence theorem for every threshold subseries is needed. In particular there is no hidden assumption that each high-weight subseries is birational or has lattice index one. The extra integer weight coordinate records the first moment all at once.

For a finite S as in Section2, choose, for each generator (m,alpha,a), an actual section with value alpha and chi(s)>=a. Give this section assigned weight a. Also include a basis of R_1, with assigned weight -C. Let chi_S be the quotient filtration of the whole R generated by these finitely many weighted sections. Then chi_S<=chi, since every weighted monomial is genuinely admitted to the original filtration. Every nonzero element of R_m has chi_S>=-Cm because R is generated in degree one. Its extended value semigroup contains S, since products of the chosen witnesses have summed valuations and at least their summed assigned weights. Moreover every generated weight is at most B times its degree.

The preceding counting identity and the conductor lower bound show

    liminf_m sum_j w^S_m,j/(m N_m)>=vol(Delta_S)/a_0-C.

The reverse upper bound follows from chi_S<=chi. The finitely generated filtration has an actual spectral mean, by its Rees test configuration, ordinary Hilbert polynomial and model energy computation on the normal projective determination. Therefore its signed mean tends up to the full signed mean as the finite set grows, in the directed supremum sense. This is sufficient: it is not necessary to prove convergence of every degree-m generated approximation in order to prove the obstacle supremum identity.

## 4. The obstacle-energy equality

For a continuous obstacle g on V^an, form the bounded graded supnorm ||s||_(mg) and its chi. Every witness section assigned a<=chi(s) satisfies

    (log|s|+a)/deg(s)<=g

in the canonical reference convention. The finite weighted section set, including the globally generating degree-one basis, defines an actual continuous FS metric psi_S<=g. The quotient filtration chi_S and its metric agree: its supremum over all weighted monomials is the maximum over the assigned generators divided by degree. Addition uses the ultrametric valuation inequality, and products use weighted averages. Real obstacle weights cause no problem because the assigned a are integers; the discarded floor error is o(mN_m).

For the finitely generated filtration, genuine probability-normalized model energy equals its degree-wise signed spectral mean. This must be reconstructed with the actual normal-projective BHJ/BE/CLD hypotheses, intersection multiplicities and normalization, rather than relying on unreviewed round60. Taking finite subsets whose polytopes approach Delta then yields

    sup_(psi continuous psh, psi<=g) E_L(psi)>=vol(L,g,h_0)/(L^p).

The raw relative volume is lim p! m^(-p-1) sum_j w_m,j; dividing by (L^p) gives exactly the spectral mean since N_m~(L^p)m^p/p!. The reverse inequality follows from continuous-psh E=vol and volume monotonicity. This establishes the proposed equality without an envelope theorem, a metrizable analytic space, or global smoothness. Tensor-power reduction returns it to the original ample rational polarization with all degree scalars retained.
