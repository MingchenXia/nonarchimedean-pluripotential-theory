                                           OVER GENERAL NON-ARCHIMEDEAN FIELDS
                                                                           REBOULET Rémi

                                                                                  Abstract
                                                 Given an ample line bundle L over a projective K-variety X, with K a
                                                 non-Archimedean field, we study limits of non-Archimedean metrics on L
                                                 associated to submultiplicative sequences of norms on the graded pieces of
                                                 the section ring R(X, L). We show that in a rather general case, the corre-
                                                 sponding asymptotic Fubini-Study operator yields a one-to-one correspon-
                                                 dence between equivalence classes of bounded graded norms and bounded
                                                 plurisubharmonic metrics that are regularizable from below. This general-
                                                 izes results of Boucksom-Jonsson where this problem has been studied in
                                                 the trivially valued case.

Introduction.
Let X be a projective variety deﬁned over a non-Archimedean ﬁeld K, endowed
with a line bundle L which we will assume to be ample. To those objects,
one can associate their Berkovich analytification, allowing the use of tech-
niques analogue to those of complex analytic geometry. By considering the
non-Archimedean avatar of the space of Kähler potentials: the space H(L) of
Fubini-Study potentials, one can deﬁne the class of plurisubharmonic met-
rics. In this article, we look at the quantization of such plurisubharmonic metrics
by sequences of norms acting on sections of tensor powers of L, in line with the
ideas of [DLR18]. In particular, we generalize results in this direction obtained
in [BJ18a], building on the theory developed in [BE20].

A central object of study in complex pluripotential theory is the space of Kähler
potentials: given a polarized compact connected Kähler manifold (X/C, L), and
the curvature ωL of a reference metric φ0 on L,

                       H = {φ ∈ C ∞ (X), ωL + ddc φ > 0}.

lytiﬁcations of K-varieties, we similarly choose a reference metric φ0 on L, which
gives a function s 7→ |s|φ0 = |s| acting on sections of L (which extends naturally
to sections of powers of L), then consider the set of Fubini-Study potentials
H(L) whose objects are continuous functions (or potentials) ϕ from the Berkovich

The existence of the pointwise is ensured by Fekete’s lemma, thanks to the sub-
multiplicativity condition and the boundedness of ζ• , and the usc regularization
turns out to be plurisubharmonic provided the pair (X, L) satisﬁes some mild
condition, continuity of envelopes, conjectured to always hold as soon as X
is normal, and known to be true e.g. for line bundles on smooth varieties deﬁned
over a discretely or trivially valued ﬁeld K of equal characteristic zero (the im-
portant case of the ﬁeld of Laurent series C((t)) is such an example; a review of
the currently known cases is presented in Example 5.2.3).

This operator maps bounded graded norms to the set of plurisubharmonic func-
tions regularizable from below PSH↑ , i.e. psh functions which are limits of in-
creasing nets of Fubini-Study potentials. It is not injective. However, in [BJ18a,
Theorem C], S. Boucksom and M. Jonsson prove that, if K is trivially valued
and of characteristic 0, this operator descends to an injection from the space of
bounded graded norms modulo the equivalence relation ∼, onto PSH↑ . The main
result of this article is a generalization of this statement.

Theorem (A). Assume (X, L) to admit continuity of envelopes. The asymptotic
Fubini-Study operator FS then defines a bijection:

                           FS : N• (L)/ ∼ → PSH↑ (L).

The main ingredient in the proof of Theorem A is the following Theorem, which
builds on the main result of [BJ18a] and generalizes it:

Theorem (B). With the hypotheses of Theorem A, and given two bounded graded
norms ζ• , ζ•′ , we have that

                         E(FS(ζ• ), FS(ζ•′ )) = vol(ζ• , ζ•′ ).

A similar result has been proved, again in the characteristic 0, trivially valued
case, in [BJ18a].

The term on the left-hand side is the relative Monge-Ampère energy be-
tween two continuous psh functions, which exists thanks to the theory of non-
Archimedean Monge-Ampère operators of A. Chambert-Loir and A. Ducros ([CLD12]),
and has been studied for example in [BE20].

The volume, on the right-hand side, is the ﬁrst moment of the asymptotic spectral
measure σ(ζ• , ζ•′ ).

It is a generalization of the relative volumes of balls studied in [BB10], and Theo-
rem B is closely related to the results of the latter article. In the aforementioned
article, where the base ﬁeld is C, one considers two speciﬁc norms on spaces of

H. Chen and C. Maclean have studied volumes more generally in [CM15], and
indeed the proof of Theorem B uses their techniques. They rely on Okounkov
bodies, which are convex bodies associated to a semigroup, reﬂecting its asymp-
totic properties. Originally introduced in [Oko03], they have ﬁrst been studied
extensively in [KK12] and [LM09], and the reader may ﬁnd a summary of their
most important properties in [Bou12].

    The most important part of the proof of Theorem B, and also of interest
as a stand-alone result, is the following Theorem stating that we can recover
the volume of two bounded graded norms by approximating it with volumes
associated to simpler graded norms:

The reason why this result is useful is that graded norms generated in degree
one are simpler to study: their asymptotic behaviour is governed by that of the
algebra of sections R(X, L), and by the norm ζ1 on H 0 (L).

The proof of Theorem B also relies on the theory of models of varieties over the
valuation ring: to any globally generated model (X , L) of (X, L) as described
in e.g. [BE20] is associated a lattice norm on H 0 (L), that is, a norm for
which there exists an orthonormal basis in the non-Archimedean sense. Using
a speciﬁc construction of such a model L, one obtains a graded norm for which
Theorem B essentially holds, thanks to the results of [BE20]. We then pass from
this speciﬁc case, to the more general case of norms generated in degree one, by
approximation of volumes: namely, the set of lattice norms is dense in the set of
norms on a ﬁxed vector space, for the sup (or d∞ ) distance. One can then create
an approximation by norms "almost" generated in degree one (see Remark 3.3.9),
and use Lipschitz continuity of volumes with respect to this distance. Finally, an
application of Theorem C concludes the proof.

Notation.
K and L always denote ﬁelds, where our ﬁelds are assumed to be commutative.

A variety X over K is a geometrically integral, separated scheme, of ﬁnite type
over K.

Given L a line bundle over a K-variety X, H 0 (X, L) is identiﬁed with the space
of sections Γ(X, L) of L over X. When no confusion can arise, we will often write
H 0 (L). h0 (X, L) is the K-dimension of the K-vector space H 0 (X, L).

Given a K-vector space V , and a positive integer n, V ⊗n refers to the n-th tensor
power of V ; V ⊙n to the n-th symmetric power of V ; and V ∧n to the n-th exterior
power of V .
