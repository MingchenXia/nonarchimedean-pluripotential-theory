## 1. Fixed carrier, action, and normalization

Let $R$ be the coordinate ring of the normal cone, of dimension $n$, with
the good torus action of the problem, $R_0=k$, and homogeneous maximal
ideal $\mathfrak m$. Choose nonzero homogeneous algebra generators
$h_1,\ldots,h_q\in\mathfrak m$ with weights $\beta_i$. Write
$d_\xi(\alpha)=\langle\xi,\alpha\rangle$. Every supported nonzero
weight is a nonnegative integral combination of the $\beta_i$: take a
homogeneous polynomial expression of a nonzero element of $R_\alpha$
and retain a nonzero monomial. In particular the generators control all
degree ratios, not just the weights in a particular FS presentation.

We use the semivaluation convention $\log|f|(v)=-v(f)$. Torification is
$q(v)(\sum f_\alpha)=\min_\alpha v(f_\alpha)$. On its image let
$$
 (v+t\eta)(f)=\min_\alpha\{v(f_\alpha)+t d_\eta(\alpha)\}.
 \tag{1}
$$
These are the analytic action and torification recorded in A,
L218--246; hence they are semivaluations, not merely formal functions.
The action is additive on torified points. It is jointly continuous,
since evaluation on each polynomial is a minimum of finitely many
continuous homogeneous evaluations. This also proves continuity of $q$.

Set $Y=q(X^{\rm an}\setminus\{o\})$ and
$$
 K=X_0^T=\{v\in Y: v(\mathfrak m)=0\}.
$$
This is one fixed carrier for all Reeb fields. On $K$ all regular functions
have nonnegative value: a polynomial in the $h_i$ has nonnegative value
when all $v(h_i)\geq0$. Moreover
$v(\mathfrak m)=\min_i v(h_i)$. The closed unit polydisc in the
coordinates $h_i$ is compact; imposing the equations of $R$ and
$\min_i v(h_i)=0$ gives a closed compact subset. Imposing $q(v)=v$
is a further closed condition. Thus $K$ is compact.

The canonical reference function and the orbit section are
$$
 \rho_\xi(v)=\max_i\frac{-v(h_i)}{d_\xi(\beta_i)},\qquad
 s_\xi(v)=v+\rho_\xi(v)\xi\in K.                 \tag{2}
$$
At least one $h_i$ has finite value for $v\ne o$, so the maximum is
finite. Each generator has nonnegative value at $s_\xi(v)$, and at
least one has value zero. Also
$\rho_\xi(v+t\xi)=\rho_\xi(v)-t$.
Consequently a torified $\xi$-equivariant function $u$ obeys
$$
 u(v)=u(s_\xi(v))+\rho_\xi(v).                  \tag{3}
$$
In particular its restriction to $K$ determines it on $Y$, and then on
$X^{\rm an}$ by torification and the value $-\infty$ at $o$.
Uniform differences on $K$ are uniform differences on all of $Y$.
The reference (2) is independent of the homogeneous algebra generators:
it is the unique equivariant function zero on $K$.

An FS list in A, Definition 2.9 (L277--286), need only generate a primary
ideal. If its homogeneous elements are $f_j$, then $\mathfrak m^r$
is contained in their ideal for some $r$. At $v\in K$, choose $h_i$
with $v(h_i)=0$. If all $v(f_j)>0$, an expression
$h_i^r=\sum a_j f_j$ has every summand of positive value, since
$v(a_j)\geq0$, contradicting $v(h_i^r)=0$. Therefore at least one
$v(f_j)=0$, while all $v(f_j)\geq0$. The zero-weight FS function
defined by this primary list is zero on $K$, and equals (2) everywhere
by (3). This proves reference independence for primary lists as well.

All FS functions factor through $q$ and satisfy (3). The same is true of
their local uniform limits and decreasing pointwise limits. These are
exactly the CPSH and PSH definitions used here (A, L344--345 and
L762--764), rather than a different intrinsic definition of psh.
For a CPSH function, local uniform convergence on $Y$ is equivalent to
uniform convergence on $K$ within a fixed polarization, by (3).

The trivial valuation $v_{\rm triv}$ is in $K$. For FS functions,
$$
 \sup_K u=u(v_{\rm triv})=\max_j\frac{\lambda_j}{d_\xi(\alpha_j)}.
 \tag{4}
$$
This passes to CPSH by uniform approximation. For a decreasing CPSH
net defining $u\in\operatorname{PSH}(\xi)$, the inequality
$u(v)\leq u(v_{\rm triv})$ passes pointwise. If the right side were
$-\infty$, (3) would make $u$ identically $-\infty$, which is excluded.
Thus (4), with a finite right side, holds for every actual PSH function.
The normalized representative is
$$
 N_\xi u=u-u(v_{\rm triv}).                    \tag{5}
$$
It is uniquely characterized by $\sup_K N_\xi u=0$.

## 2. The orbit-root comparison on the completed classes

For two Reeb fields define
$$
 c=c(\xi,\eta)=\min_i\frac{d_\eta(\beta_i)}{d_\xi(\beta_i)},\qquad
 C=C(\xi,\eta)=\max_i\frac{d_\eta(\beta_i)}{d_\xi(\beta_i)}.
 \tag{6}
$$
Both are positive. Taking weighted averages in any monomial proves
$c\leq d_\eta(\alpha)/d_\xi(\alpha)\leq C$ for every supported
nonzero weight $\alpha$. In particular these constants are independent
of every FS list, its weights, and all approximating potentials.

For $u\in\operatorname{PSH}(\xi)$ the following inequalities hold for
$t\geq0$, first termwise for FS functions, then by local uniform and
decreasing limits:
$$
 u(v)-Ct\leq u(v+t\eta)\leq u(v)-ct.           \tag{7}
$$
The reverse-orbit inequalities follow by applying (7) at $v-t\eta$.
If $u(v)$ is finite, (7) makes the function $t\mapsto u(v+t\eta)$
finite, strictly decreasing, Lipschitz, and surjective onto $\mathbb R$.
It therefore has exactly one zero. If $u(v)=-\infty$, (7) in both
directions makes the entire $\eta$ orbit $-\infty$.

Define $\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}u(v)$ to be this unique zero parameter,
and define it to be $-\infty$ in the second case. Extend by $q$ and
put $\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}u(o)=-\infty$. For an FS presentation this is
the exact formula
$$
 u=\max_j\frac{\lambda_j-v(f_j)}{d_\xi(\alpha_j)}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal R_{\xi\eta}u=
 \max_j\frac{\lambda_j-v(f_j)}{d_\eta(\alpha_j)}.       \tag{8}
$$
Indeed all terms in $u(v+t\eta)$ are nonpositive if and only if
$t$ is at least the right side of (8); at equality one term is zero.
Equation (8) follows from the intrinsic zero definition. It does not
define a map by choosing a presentation. This proves presentation
independence, including redundant generators and primary lists.

**Theorem 1.** The map $\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}$ gives order
isomorphisms on $\mathcal H$, CPSH and PSH. On these classes,
$$
 \mathcal R_{\eta\xi}\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}=\mathrm{id},\qquad
 \mathcal R_{\eta\zeta}\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}=\mathcal R_{\xi\zeta}.
 \tag{9}
$$
It preserves decreasing nets and their pointwise limits, including
$-\infty$ behavior. On CPSH it is continuous for local uniform
convergence. On PSH it is a homeomorphism for the topology of pointwise
convergence on *all* analytic points. This last assertion does not claim
continuity for the weaker, quasi-monomial topology used in A, L805,
nor continuity for a strong finite-energy topology.

*Proof.* Formula (8) proves FS membership. If $u\leq w$, their zero
parameters satisfy $\mathcal R u\leq\mathcal R w$, because the orbit
functions decrease. If $u,w$ are CPSH and
$\|u-w\|_K\leq\epsilon$, equation (3) gives this bound on the
entire source orbit. The lower slope bound in (7) consequently gives
$$
 \|\mathcal R u-\mathcal R w\|_K\leq\epsilon/c.       \tag{10}
$$
Uniform FS approximation on $K$, followed by (10), proves that the
intrinsic root lies in CPSH of the target polarization. Its
$\eta$-equivariance follows either from the zero definition or from
the approximants; thus convergence on $K$ implies local uniform
convergence everywhere by the target version of (3).

Now let $u_a\downarrow u$ be a defining CPSH net. The roots decrease.
If $r=\mathcal R u(v)$ is finite, $u(v+(r-\epsilon)\eta)>0$ and
$u(v+(r+\epsilon)\eta)<0$. Eventually the corresponding values of
$u_a$ have those signs, so their roots converge to $r$. If $u(v)=-\infty$,
then $u(v-M\eta)=-\infty$ for every $M>0$. Eventually
$u_a(v-M\eta)<0$, so their roots are less than $-M$. This proves
pointwise convergence also in this case. The limit is not identically
$-\infty$, since $u(v_{\rm triv})$ is finite. Hence it belongs to
the target PSH by its actual definition.

The root has exactly the same zero set and exactly the same $-\infty$
locus as $u$. Roots along a third Reeb field are therefore identical,
proving the cocycle. Taking the third field to be $\xi$, where
$u(v+t\xi)=u(v)-t$, proves the inverse. The inverse is order preserving,
so these are order isomorphisms. The same bracket argument applies to
any pointwise convergent PSH net: at a finite root use two fixed orbit
points, and at $-\infty$ use $v-M\eta$. It proves pointwise continuity
of the map and inverse. It also proves preservation of all existing
decreasing PSH limits. Equation (10) proves local uniform continuity
on CPSH. $\square$

For completeness, target Reeb variation has local uniform continuity
for a fixed CPSH function. On a compact set of $v$ the roots remain in
a bounded interval by (7), and $\eta$ variation in a compact set makes
the associated orbit points range in a compact subset of $Y$. Uniform
continuity of $u$ there and the uniform positive lower slope bound
give continuity of the roots. This uses actual continuity of $u$,
not qualitative rational approximation of energy.

For an FS function, $u(v_{\rm triv})=0$ means all $\lambda_j\leq0$
and at least one $\lambda_j=0$. The general zero definition gives the
same conclusion about the value at $v_{\rm triv}$ without needing an
FS presentation: $\mathcal R u(v_{\rm triv})=0$ if $u(v_{\rm triv})=0$.
Thus (9) restricts to normalized PSH and induces genuine bijections
of constant quotients by the rule
$$
 [u]\longmapsto[\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}(N_\xi u)].       \tag{11}
$$
This is independent of representatives because (5) is. The output is
already normalized, so (9) proves inverse and cocycle also for (11).

Normalization before applying the map is essential. For $R=k[x,y]$,
$\xi=(1,1)$ and $\eta=(1,2)$,
$$
 \mathcal R_{\xi\eta}(\rho_\xi+2)
 =\max\{-v(x)+2,(-v(y)+2)/2\}.
$$
Its difference from $\rho_\eta$ is $2$ at $v_{\rm triv}$ and $1$
at the monomial valuation $(v(x),v(y))=(3,0)\in K$. The raw map
does not descend to constant quotients.

For normalized PSH, the exact sign-sensitive comparison on $K$ is
$$
 \frac{u}{c}\leq\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}u\leq\frac{u}{C}\leq0.
 \tag{12}
$$
For a nonnegative value of an unnormalized function the inequalities
instead read $u/C\leq\mathcal R u\leq u/c$. Both follow directly
from (7), including extended values. Let $Q$ be a compact subset of
the Reeb cone and set
$$
 \delta(\xi,\eta)=\max_i\left|\log
 \frac{d_\eta(\beta_i)}{d_\xi(\beta_i)}\right|.
$$
Then $e^{-\delta}\leq c\leq C\leq e^\delta$, and, wherever $u$ is
finite,
$$
 |\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}u-u|\leq(e^\delta-1)(-u)
 \quad\text{on }K.                                  \tag{13}
$$
There is $L_Q<\infty$, depending only on the chosen generators, the
norm on $N_{\mathbb R}$, and $Q$, such that
$\delta\leq L_Q\|\xi-\eta\|$. Indeed the derivative of
$\log d_\xi(\beta_i)$ has norm at most
$\|\beta_i\|/\min_{\theta\in Q}d_\theta(\beta_i)$ along the segment;
on the convex hull of $Q$ this denominator is still bounded below.

For any *fixed* probability measure $\mu$ on $K$, (12) also gives
$$
 \int(-\mathcal R u)\mathbf1_{\{-\mathcal R u>A\}}\,d\mu
 \leq c^{-1}\int(-u)\mathbf1_{\{-u>cA\}}\,d\mu.       \tag{14}
$$
This is a genuine integrability/tail statement with a fixed measure.
It asserts nothing about moving measures $\operatorname{MA}_\eta(\mathcal R u)$.

There is also a limited genuine-energy consequence for bounded
potentials. If $u\in\operatorname{CPSH}(\xi)$ is normalized and
$-B\leq u\leq0$ on $K$, (3) and (12) compare it and its image with
the constant translates of the respective reference functions. Actual
same-polarization energy monotonicity and translation (A, Proposition
4.2(5),(7)) give
$$
 -B\leq E_\xi(u)\leq0,\qquad
 -B/c\leq E_\eta(\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}u)\leq0.
$$
The coefficient $1/c$ is uniform on compact Reeb slices. This is an
oscillation bound. It does not bound the target energy in terms of
the source energy alone and is insufficient on an energy-bounded
family with unbounded oscillations.

## 3. Signed finite-type norms and primary-list representation

Let $g_1,\ldots,g_p$ be homogeneous algebra generators, allowing
additional redundant homogeneous elements, with arbitrary real assigned
weights $\lambda_i$. For $\alpha\ne0$, define
$$
 F^a R_\alpha=\operatorname{span}\{g^{\mathbf e}:
              \text{weight}(g^{\mathbf e})=\alpha,
              \sum e_i\lambda_i\geq a\},\qquad
 \chi(f)=\sup\{a:f\in F^a R_\alpha\}.                \tag{15}
$$
For general $f$ use the minimum of its nonzero homogeneous components'
weights, and give $1$ weight zero. For fixed $\alpha$ there are only
finitely many eligible monomials: their exponent sum is bounded by
$d_\xi(\alpha)/\min_i d_\xi(\operatorname{wt}g_i)$.
Thus (15) is finite, left continuous, exhaustive and separated on
each weight space, and the supremum is attained. It is a quotient
non-Archimedean norm with
$$
 \chi(f+g)\geq\min\{\chi(f),\chi(g)\},\qquad
 \chi(fg)\geq\chi(f)+\chi(g).                        \tag{16}
$$
These follow by adding and multiplying the spanning expressions.
No assumption that the $g_i$ are algebraically independent is made.
For homogeneous $f$,
$$
 \min_i\frac{\lambda_i}{d_\xi(\operatorname{wt}g_i)}\cdot d_\xi(\alpha)
 \leq\chi(f)\leq
 \max_i\frac{\lambda_i}{d_\xi(\operatorname{wt}g_i)}\cdot d_\xi(\alpha).
 \tag{17}
$$
Every eligible monomial has weight in that interval, which proves both
inequalities, including the upper bound after quotienting by relations.

The actual weight $\chi(g_i)$ may exceed its assigned $\lambda_i$.
Nevertheless replacing all assigned weights by the actual weights
leaves (15) unchanged. One inequality follows from $\chi(g_i)\geq\lambda_i$;
the other follows from (16), which bounds the minimum of the actual
monomial weights in every expression by $\chi(f)$. Taking an expression
attaining the old weight proves attainment also with the actual weights.
This verifies the finite-type condition of B, Definition 3.6
(L493--502), with the saturated norm, rather than assuming that assigned
generator weights are its actual weights.

The exact potential correspondence is
$$
 \sup_{0\ne f\in R_\alpha,\ \alpha\ne0}
 \frac{\chi(f)-v(f)}{d_\xi(\alpha)}
 =\max_i\frac{\lambda_i-v(g_i)}{d_\xi(\operatorname{wt}g_i)}.
 \tag{18}
$$
To prove the upper bound, take an expression of $f$ attaining $\chi(f)$.
The valuation inequality gives
$\chi(f)-v(f)\leq\max_{\mathbf e}(\sum e_i\lambda_i-v(g^{\mathbf e}))$.
After division by the common degree each monomial expression is a
weighted average of generator expressions, at most the right side of
(18). For the reverse bound use $f=g_i$ and $\chi(g_i)\geq\lambda_i$.
The proof permits $v(g_i)=+\infty$: a nonzero finite-valued term in an
expression supplies the bound, while entirely infinite-valued expressions
give the trivial $-\infty$ bound. Thus norm saturation does not alter
the FS potential.

An arbitrary primary FS list has a representation of this kind on any
chosen compact Reeb slice $Q$. Keep its elements $f_j$ and weights
$\lambda_j$ and append the fixed algebra generators $h_i$ with weight
$-M$. On $K$ at least one $f_j$ has value zero, so
$$
 u_\xi(v)\geq\min_j\lambda_j/d_\xi(\alpha_j)\geq-B
 \quad(\xi\in Q)
$$
for a finite $B\geq0$. Choose
$M\geq B\max_{\xi\in Q,i}d_\xi(\beta_i)$. Every appended term
is at most $-B$ on $K$, so the potential remains exactly unchanged
there. Equivariance and (3) give exact equality everywhere, for every
$\xi\in Q$. This is an exact finite-type representation, not an
algebra-generating assumption on the original list. The chosen $M$
can depend on this potential; the constants (6), (12), and (22) below
do not depend on $M$, weights, or potentials.

For rational assigned weights, multiply their filtration indices by a
common denominator. The resulting integer Rees algebra is generated
over $k[t]$ by the finitely many elements $g_i t^{-\ell_i}$, where
$\ell_i$ are the scaled assigned weights. Indeed every filtered monomial
at an index below its assigned weight is its generator product times a
nonnegative power of $t$; spanning expressions prove the assertion
also in the presence of relations. Localization at $t$ recovers
$R[t,t^{-1}]$. Negative weights are allowed. For irrational weights
we assert finite type in the real-norm sense (15), not a Noetherian
integer Rees algebra.

## 4. Valid shifts and uniform signed cutoff estimates

Negating a norm is not a valid treatment of negative weights. For
$R=k[x]$ with assigned weights $\lambda_x=\lambda_{x^2}=-1$,
(15) gives $\chi(x)=-1$ and $\chi(x^2)=-1>-2$.
Then $-\chi(x^2)=1<2=(-\chi(x))+(-\chi(x))$, violating multiplicativity.

The valid operation is a degree shift. For any Reeb field $\beta$ set
$$
 \chi_{A,\beta}(f)=\chi(f)+A d_\beta(\alpha)
 \quad(f\in R_\alpha),                              \tag{19}
$$
and extend by minima over homogeneous components. This is precisely
the construction (15) with assigned weights
$\lambda_i+A d_\beta(\operatorname{wt}g_i)$: every homogeneous
expression acquires the same added weight. It is therefore
multiplicative and finite type. For sufficiently large $A$, (17)
proves nonnegativity on all of $R$, including the quotient norm;
the filtration then has $F^0R=R$ and satisfies every condition of B,
Definition 3.5 (L470--488). It is linearly bounded by (17).

Let $N_\xi(m)=\dim\bigoplus_{d_\xi(\alpha)\leq m}R_\alpha$,
$V_\xi=\lim n!N_\xi(m)/m^n>0$, and let $W_\xi^\chi(m)$ be the
sum of the norm's spectral weights on this direct sum, counting
multiplicity. Spectral weights mean the weights of an adapted basis
of the finite-dimensional filtration, equivalently its jumps with their
dimension multiplicities. They are intrinsic to (15). Define
$D_\xi^\beta(m)=\sum_{d_\xi(\alpha)\leq m}
d_\beta(\alpha)\dim R_\alpha$. The exact finite identity is
$$
 W_\xi^{\chi_{A,\beta}}(m)=W_\xi^\chi(m)+A D_\xi^\beta(m).
 \tag{20}
$$
It holds because the entire filtration of $R_\alpha$ is translated by
one scalar. This is the cutoff identity that must precede subtraction
of limits.

Existence and positivity of $V_\xi$ are the volume result in B,
Definition 2.4 and Theorem 3.3, L204--209 and L454--460, for the
normal good cone over the algebraically closed characteristic-zero
field. Its displayed $\mathbb C$ dimensions are ordinary dimensions
over the standing field $K$ of L181--191. The real-cutoff version follows
by sandwiching between adjacent integer cutoffs. Stieltjes summation
gives the exact identity
$D_\xi^\xi(m)=mN_\xi(m)-\int_0^mN_\xi(t)\,dt$.
Consequently
$$
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{D_\xi^\xi(m)}{mN_\xi(m)}=
 \frac{n}{n+1}.                                    \tag{21}
$$
To justify the integral limit, divide by $m^{n+1}$ and use
$N_\xi(t)=V_\xi t^n/n!+o(t^n)$ beyond an arbitrary fixed cutoff;
the bounded initial integral vanishes after this division.

For each fixed $\xi$, use (19) with $\beta=\xi$ to obtain a nonnegative
filtration. B, Corollary 3.13 (L635--645), then gives convergence of
$W_\xi^{\chi_{A,\xi}}(m)/(mN_\xi(m))$. Its assumptions have just
been checked. Combining the exact identity (20) with the proved
limit (21) proves existence of the signed cutoff mean
$S_\chi(\xi)=\lim W_\xi^\chi(m)/(mN_\xi(m))$ and the exact rule
$S_{\chi_{A,\xi}}(\xi)=S_\chi(\xi)+A n/(n+1)$.
This is not a difference of two unproved limits.

If all assigned weights are nonpositive, all spectral weights are
nonpositive by (17). The degree inclusions
$R_{\xi,\leq m/C}\subset R_{\eta,\leq m}
\subset R_{\xi,\leq m/c}$ give finite inequalities
$W_\xi^\chi(m/c)\leq W_\eta^\chi(m)\leq W_\xi^\chi(m/C)$.
Using the established limits and volume asymptotics yields
$$
 \frac{V_\xi}{V_\eta}c^{-(n+1)}S_\chi(\xi)
 \leq S_\chi(\eta)\leq
 \frac{V_\xi}{V_\eta}C^{-(n+1)}S_\chi(\xi).          \tag{22}
$$
The volume inclusions also give
$V_\xi/C^n\leq V_\eta\leq V_\xi/c^n$.
Thus
$$
 e^{-(2n+1)\delta}(-S_\chi(\xi))
 \leq -S_\chi(\eta)\leq
 e^{(2n+1)\delta}(-S_\chi(\xi)).                    \tag{23}
$$
There is no division by $S$ in this argument: if it is zero, all
three quantities are zero. These constants are independent of the
assigned weights and appended generators. The fields in (22) may
be irrational and nonproportional.

A normalized primary FS function can be represented by such a
nonpositive norm: replace its weights by
$\lambda_j-u(v_{\rm triv})d_\xi(\alpha_j)$ and append sufficiently
negative algebra-generator weights. At least one retained weight is
zero. Using the same resulting weights at another Reeb field gives
exactly its normalized orbit comparison. This connects (22) to norm
representations of the correct normalized potentials. It does *not*
identify $S_\chi$ as a presentation-independent functional of the
potential; nor does it identify $S_\chi$ with genuine cone energy.

Rational weight approximation is also harmless at this algebraic stage.
If $|\lambda_i-\lambda_i'|\leq\epsilon d_\xi(\operatorname{wt}g_i)$,
all monomial weights of fixed degree differ by at most
$\epsilon d_\xi(\alpha)$. Filter inclusions imply that the ordered
spectral weights in $R_\alpha$ differ by at most that scalar.
Therefore the cutoff sums differ by at most
$\epsilon D_\xi^\xi(m)$, and the limiting means differ by at most
$\epsilon n/(n+1)$. Equation (18) also gives uniform potential
approximation with error at most $\epsilon$. These are norm and
potential approximation results; they do not supply missing analytic
energy convergence.

There is a precise cancellation issue. At its own Reeb field, (18)
gives $u_{\chi_{A,\xi},\xi}=u_{\chi,\xi}+A$, and the genuine energy
translation gives $E_\xi(u+A)=E_\xi(u)+A$. If a cone representation
were $E_\xi(u)=a_nS_\chi(\xi)$, its shift coefficient is forced by
(21) to be $a_n=(n+1)/n$. At another field $\eta$, a shift by
$A d_\xi$ changes the numerator by a degree-dependent quantity;
it is not generally a constant shift. One cannot cancel $A$ at both
fields after using one reference degree. Equations (20)--(23) avoid
that invalid operation.

## 5. Genuine normalization checks and an exact projective result

The genuine affine-cone convention in A, Theorem 2.12 and Definition
4.1, L321--328 and L522--529, is
$$
 \operatorname{MA}_\xi(u_1,\ldots,u_{n-1})=
 V_\xi^{-1}dd^c u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dd^c u_{n-1}
                  \wedge dd^c\rho_\xi^+,
$$
$$
 E_\xi(u)=\frac1{nV_\xi}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
 \int (u-\rho_\xi)(dd^c u)^j(dd^c\rho_\xi)^{n-1-j}
                              dd^c\rho_\xi^+.       \tag{24}
$$
Here $dd^c=d'd''$ and $\rho_\xi^+=\max(\rho_\xi,0)$.
The mixed measures have mass one and are carried by $K$ (A,
Theorem 2.12). This convention has $n-1$ potential factors and one
radial factor, with $n$ terms in energy. Substituting
$u=\rho_\xi+a$ in (24), the currents are unchanged and each integral
equals $aV_\xi$, giving $E_\xi(\rho_\xi+a)=a$.
This argument uses the actual probability normalization, not B,
Theorem 5.15.

For $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, $\xi=(1,\ldots,1)$ and the degree norm
$\chi(x^{\mathbf e})=|\mathbf e|$, formula (18) gives
$u=\rho_\xi+1$. For integer $m\geq1$,
$$
 N(m)=\binom{m+n}{n},\qquad
 W(m)=\sum_{|\mathbf e|\leq m}|\mathbf e|
      =n\binom{m+n}{n+1}.
$$
The last identity follows by summing
$d\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}=n\binom{d+n-1}{n}$ and the binomial
summation identity. Thus $W(m)/(mN(m))=n/(n+1)$ *at every cutoff*,
whereas (24) gives $E(u)=1$. In particular for $k[x,y]$ the two
values are $2/3$ and $1$. This is a mathematical counterexample to
the unmodified printed equality $E=S$, not just a numerical diagnostic.

For $n=1$, on $k[x]$ with $d_\xi(x)=a>0$, $V_\xi=1/a$.
On the calibrated one-dimensional torus skeleton,
$dd^c\max(\log|x|/a,0)$ has the slope-jump mass $1/a$ at
$v(x)=0$. Dividing by $V_\xi$ gives one Dirac probability mass.
The product of zero potential factors in (24) is the empty product,
so $E(\rho_\xi+c)=c$, while the degree-shift cutoff coefficient
is $1/2$. This checks the low-dimensional convention directly.
It supplies no nonproportional comparison in dimension one.

For every cone the same scaling calculation in (24) gives
$V_{s\xi}=s^{-n}V_\xi$ and
$E_{s\xi}(u/s)=E_\xi(u)/s$, since $\rho_{s\xi}=\rho_\xi/s$.
There are $n$ current factors, and the energy integrand has one more
scalar factor. These are proportional identities only.

There is an exact signed *projective test-configuration* representation
available without a smoothness assumption. Let $Z$ be normal
projective of dimension $n-1$, let $A$ be ample, and let
$(\mathcal Z,\mathcal A)$ be an ample test configuration. Replace $A$
by a sufficiently divisible multiple so that its induced filtration
on the full section ring $\bigoplus_{d\geq0}H^0(Z,dA)$ is the one
in H, Proposition 2.15. Signed rational weights are permitted: clear
denominators by the corresponding integer scaling. By H, Proposition
5.9 (not a Theorem in this version), the rescaled degree-wise spectral
measures converge to its DH probability measure. Linear upper growth
and the lower bound of H, Remark 5.2, put them in a common compact
interval, so their signed means converge as well. H, Proposition
3.12(iii), identifies that mean with the actual intersection number
$$
 e=\frac{(\overline{\mathcal A}^{n})}{n(A^{n-1})}
   =E^{\rm NA}_{\rm proj}(\phi).                    \tag{25}
$$
The second equality is H, Definition 7.2. Thus a distribution theorem
is not being substituted for an energy identification.

To check its MA convention, take a common normal determination
dominating the model and the product, as in H, Definition 2.18:
the normalization of the graph supplies one. Pullbacks preserve the
intersection numbers; the pulled-back ample class is relatively
semiample, and the corresponding metric is unchanged. H, Theorem
3.14, retains the DH measure under normalization because the generic
projective variety is reduced. Thus no smoothness or projective
normality assumption is inserted here. Use H, Section 6.7: the mixed
model measure is
$$
 V_A^{-1}\sum_E b_E(\mathcal A_1|_E\cdots
                  \mathcal A_{n-1}|_E)\delta_{v_E},
$$
of mass one. If a vertical
divisor has coefficient $r_E$, its potential value at $v_E$ is
$r_E/b_E$. H, Remark 6.12, therefore gives the intersection pairing
as $V_A$ times its integral against this probability measure. Expanding
the difference of $n$-fold intersections using H, Lemma 7.4, proves
the energy formula with $n$ terms and denominator $nV_A$. There is
no further $V_A^{-1}$ after integrating a probability measure. This
derivation fixes the normalization even though the extract of H,
Remark 7.5, prints an additional $1/V$ alongside the statement
$\mu_j=\operatorname{MA}^{\rm NA}(\cdots)$.

Write $N_d=h^0(Z,dA)$ and $W_d$ for the signed total degree-$d$
weight. The preceding mean convergence says
$W_d/(dN_d)\to e$. Hilbert asymptotics give
$N_d=a d^{n-1}+O(d^{n-2})$, with $a=V_A/(n-1)!$; for a point
($n=1$) use $N_d=1$ directly. For all $\epsilon>0$, beyond a
fixed degree $D$ the error in $W_d-e dN_d$ is bounded by
$\epsilon dN_d$. Summing, dividing by
$m\sum_{d\leq m}N_d$, and making the fixed initial sum negligible
proves
$$
 S_{\rm cumulative}=\frac{n}{n+1}e,\qquad
 E^{\rm NA}_{\rm proj}(\phi)=\frac{n+1}{n}S_{\rm cumulative}.
 \tag{26}
$$
This proof handles signed weights without negating a filtration.
Multiplying the line bundle or clearing weights changes both sides
by the explicitly corresponding scaling; $V_A$ is always the volume
of the line bundle actually used. Normality of $Z$ is the hypothesis
here; smoothness is not inferred.

Equations (25)--(26) are a proved application to actual projective
test configurations. They are not yet a proof that every primary
cone FS norm, at every rational Reeb field with weighted grading,
has exactly the same energy as (24) after all Veronese and radial
calibrations. That remaining bridge is not hidden in the notation.

## 6. Source audit and the limits of the analytic argument

**The energy inconsistency.** B, Lemma 3.16(b), L688--755,
states and proves $S=\frac{n}{n+1}\widetilde S$, where
$\widetilde S$ is the degree-wise mean of L679--685. B, Corollary
5.14, L1344--1354, identifies cone and projective energies under
its proposed bridge. The final proof, L1366--1372, then writes
$E=\widetilde S=S$. The degree example above disproves this last
equality under the printed definitions. Formula (26) repairs the
factor for the projective situation in its stated scope. It does
not prove the universal cone representation from the diagnostic.

**Rational quotient and radial calibration.** Normality and projectivity
of a rational quotient can be checked algebraically: degree-zero
localized charts of a normal graded domain are normal, and a sufficiently
divisible Veronese presents $\operatorname{Proj}R$ as projective
with $\mathcal O(d)$ ample. But this does not make the quotient smooth,
nor does it prove the radial energy factor. B, Proposition 5.13,
L1282--1304, uses sections of $mL$, writes a product trivialization
via $s_0$, and expresses the potential as
$\pi^*\widetilde\phi-z_0$ in the moment coordinates of those
sections. In those coordinates its preceding expression has radial
coefficient $-z_0/m$. A nonvanishing section of $mL$ does not
trivialize $L$. A valid replacement must account for the degree-$m$
fiber moment map and the integral lattice/calibration before cancelling
factors. For $\eta=s\eta_0$ it must also connect
$\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Proj}R}(d)/(sd)$, the cone volume,
and the radial current, including ineffective integral actions and
nonstandard weighted gradings. Those operator identifications are not
proved here. F, Theorem 4.13, cannot fill this gap unchanged: F,
L525, has the standing assumption that the projective variety is
smooth. H supplies (25), but still requires the cone-to-model bridge.

**What CLD does supply.** G, Theorem 15.7.1, L22100--22131,
places a topologically separated pure-dimensional analytic space,
with its analytic boundary, in the calibrated tropical framework.
Smoothness is not a hypothesis; trivial valuation is explicitly
included in G, L585--610. For an analytification of a normal affine
variety, separation and pure dimension hold, and the analytic space
without the vertex has no analytic boundary. Local tropical charts
still require their actual calibration. G, Proposition 8.7.2,
L10432--10449 (called a Theorem in the assignment), permits positive
closed currents to vary and gives weighted-current convergence when
all the continuous potentials converge locally uniformly. The zeroth
weight is continuous and locally uniformly convergent too. It does
not handle unbounded decreasing potentials by itself. G, Corollary
18.3.3(b), L26449--26457, gives the finite-map factor only when the
calibrated generic degree is the specified constant. Omitting that
degree in a Veronese moment-map argument is not justified.

**Proposition 2 (an actual bounded analytic limit).** Suppose
$\xi_l\to\xi$ and $u_{i,l}\in\operatorname{CPSH}(\xi_l)$ converge
locally uniformly on $X^{\rm an}\setminus\{o\}$ to
$u_i\in\operatorname{CPSH}(\xi)$, for $1\leq i\leq n-1$.
Then their actual mixed probability measures converge weakly on $K$.
If $u_l\in\operatorname{CPSH}(\xi_l)$ converges locally uniformly to
$u\in\operatorname{CPSH}(\xi)$, then
$$
 E_{\xi_l}(u_l)\longrightarrow E_\xi(u).
$$
In particular this applies to rational approximants of
$\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}u$ for a fixed CPSH function $u$. It is a
sequence/local-uniform statement, not an unbounded decreasing-net
or uniform energy-range estimate.

*Proof.* Use the fixed algebra generators in (2) for every reference.
They give $\rho_{\xi_l}\to\rho_\xi$ locally uniformly, by the
root parameter-continuity argument in Section 2. Equivalently it is
the finite-list continuity of (8). The reference is continuous even
where some generators vanish, since at least one is nonzero away
from $o$. Max with zero gives local uniform convergence of the radial
potentials too. Degree inclusions and their volume consequences after
(22) prove $V_{\xi_l}\to V_\xi>0$.

Each CPSH function belongs to CLD's continuous psh sheaf: FS functions
are locally uniform limits of smooth psh functions (A, L318--319),
and G, Definition 8.1.7 and Proposition 8.1.9, retain membership under
local uniform limits. On a compact neighborhood, the uniform topology
is metrizable, so FS approximations may be chosen sequentially there;
the sheaf property gives the conclusion on the open space. This is
a membership argument, not a converse inferred from positivity of a
distribution alone. The radial reference with a zero floor
is psh-approachable by the same convex-max construction. Work on the
separated, pure-dimensional, boundaryless analytic space obtained by
removing the vertex. G, Theorem 15.7.1, supplies its calibrated tropical
structure and local differential separation. The positive closed
currents $T_l=dd^c\rho_{\xi_l}^+$ converge to
$T=dd^c\rho_\xi^+$ by distributional differentiation of the local
uniform limit. Apply G, Proposition 8.7.2, with these $T_l$ and
exactly $n-1$ continuous psh potentials. This proves convergence of
the actual unnormalized mixed top currents. For $n=1$ use its
$q=0$ case; there are no extra potential factors.

All these mixed measures are supported on the common compact $K$.
For CPSH this follows also by uniform-on-$K$ FS approximation,
which is locally uniform everywhere by (3), and the same CLD argument
with fixed Reeb field, starting with A, Theorem 2.12. The mass test is
legitimate: G, Corollary 6.2.4, gives a compactly supported smooth
function equal to one near $K$. Dividing by the convergent volumes
therefore yields probability measures of mass one on $K$. Smooth
test convergence extends to continuous tests by G, Corollary 6.2.6:
extend a continuous function on $K$ to a compact neighborhood using
Tietze's theorem on that compact Hausdorff neighborhood, multiply
by a continuous cutoff, and approximate uniformly by smooth functions.
The uniform probability mass bounds the approximation error by its
uniform norm. This proves weak convergence on $K$ with its actual
topology, not merely distributional convergence against a smaller
set of tests.

For each of the $n$ summands in (24), apply the weighted form of
G, Proposition 8.7.2, with zeroth weight
$u_l-\rho_{\xi_l}$, which is continuous and locally uniformly
convergent, $j$ potential factors $u_l$, $n-1-j$ reference factors,
and radial $T_l$. Test against the same smooth cutoff equal to one
near $K$. The resulting integrals converge, with no dropped moving
weight. Finally divide their sum by $nV_{\xi_l}$. This proves
convergence of the genuine energy in (24). No projective bridge or
filtration energy formula enters this argument. $\square$

**The envelope chain.** E, Definition 3.15(2), L766--768,
uses lower growth $g\leq u+O(1)$, whereas Definition 6.1(2),
L1891--1893, uses upper growth $u\leq g+O(1)$. These are different:
on $\mathbb A^1$, $u=\log|z|$ is bounded above by
$g=\max(\log|z|,0)$ and is the decreasing limit of
$\max(\log|z|,-j)$, but is not bounded below by an FS function
up to a constant near $z=0$. FS functions in E include a finite
constant floor. The coherent convention for this audit is the upper
growth inequality; it is not silently substituted in quotations.

Changing this inequality does not complete the application. E,
Proposition 4.19, L1560--1576, invokes Lemma 3.25(1) to extend
$u-cAg$. The invoked lemma, L950--957, explicitly assumes
$u\in\operatorname{PSH}^{\log}_{\rm reg}$; membership in this
regularizable class is the conclusion of Proposition 4.19.
The attempted chain is Proposition 4.19 to Proposition 6.2(2),
then the smooth starting case and singular induction in Theorem
6.3, then Theorem 6.5, and finally Theorem 7.1. The complete
relevant proof bodies are retained in the provenance file.

A plausible repair would first establish local truncation/gluing of
$u-cAg$ on a smooth compactification without regularizability, then
apply a valid smooth local-to-global theorem and global FS approximation.
This would need proof that each truncation is in the local psh class
at every boundary point, the relevant global theorem under its true
net/countability hypotheses, and its propagation through the singular
induction. This document does not supply that repair. It does not
conclude that Theorem 7.1 is false, and does not reject it for being
a v1 preprint. It simply does not use its unverified application as
an indispensable premise.

Theorem 7.1's first stage assumes a countable dense subfield and
descends a psh function from a resolution using connected geometric
fibers and weak-to-global psh comparison. Its final stage, L2718--2747,
descends PL data to countable subfields and uses orthogonality,
base-change of MA and domination to remove the cardinality restriction.
For a trivially valued uncountable field, a countable subfield is not
dense in the discrete field topology. That final stage would therefore
have to be used and justified, not avoided by assuming countability.
Any cone application would further have to check its global reference
metric, the ample rational quotient class after Veronese/scaling,
residual-torus invariance and the identification of the resulting
carrier with this document's fixed $K$. These are not consequences
of normality alone.

**Uniform differentiation.** C, Theorem 8.5, L4843--4852,
is an actual projective result for $E^\downarrow$, with a quadratic
remainder for PL directions, uniform in the potential. The agreement
with enveloped genuine energy for all finite-energy potentials retains
an envelope hypothesis. C, Corollary 8.6, extends differentiability,
not a universal quadratic estimate, to continuous directions.
Its proof uses uniform approximation and the energy's Lipschitz
and translation properties. The two signs are handled by replacing
$f$ by $-f$. The extracted proof L4947 and L4954 has mismatched
extra factors/signs in the approximating inequalities: the correct
elementary inequality is $f_m-\epsilon_m\leq f\leq f_m+\epsilon_m$,
which gives the upper derivative bound with $+\epsilon_m$, not
$-\epsilon_m$. These harmless corrections repair that elementary
squeeze; they do not transfer the projective theorem to cones.

An explicit remaining cone target is the following. For a common
dense direction class $D_Q\subset C(K)$, compact Reeb slice $Q$,
and normalized energy range $-E_\xi(u)\leq B$, identify an actual
extended functional $F_\xi$ with the genuine operator's energy after
enveloping, and prove, for each appropriate dense test direction $f$,
$$
 |F_\xi(u+tf)-E_\xi(u)-t\int_K f\,\operatorname{MA}_\xi(u)|
 \leq C_{Q,B,f}t^2                                  \tag{27}
$$
for both signs and a uniform small-$t$ interval, or prove another
explicit uniform modulus sufficient for the variational method.
No such $F_\xi$, envelope agreement, dense class with uniform
positivity domination, or remainder theorem is established here.
If (27) were available only in a dense class, continuous directions
would require the actual uniform-approximation estimate
$2\|f-f_m\|_K+ C_{Q,B,f_m}|t|$ for the derivative quotient.
This implies a uniform differentiability modulus by choosing $m$
before $t$, but gives no quadratic bound for every continuous $f$.

D cannot bypass these steps. Its results, including Theorem 4.6,
assume a common linear space of closed forms with mixed products,
order inclusions $D_\omega\subset D_{\omega'}$, and the associated
integration and positivity framework; its envelope/differentiation
statements also retain orthogonality. The nonlinear root map supplies
none of these mixed-form axioms across different Reeb fields.

## 7. What is still missing from the exact task

First, prove an intrinsic representation of (24) for every primary FS
presentation on every rational weighted quotient, including signed
norms, norm saturation, quotient/model normalizations and calibrated
radial factors. Proposition 2 supplies the bounded local-uniform
analytic passage to irrational fields once that representation is
established rationally. The projective equality (26) and the translation
check fix necessary factors but do not establish this representation.

Second, turn (22) into an estimate for the *normalized genuine energy*
with constants uniform on $Q$. Only after this is proved could a
defining decreasing net $u_a\downarrow u\in\mathcal E^1(\xi)$
be shown to have target energies bounded below, and hence to define
an element of $\mathcal E^1(\eta)$. Theorem 1 by itself only preserves
PSH membership.

Third, establish uniform integrability and tails for all required
moving mixed MA measures, for example terms of the form
$$
 \int_K(-\mathcal R u_a)\mathbf1_{\{-\mathcal R u_a>A\}}
 \operatorname{MA}_\eta((\mathcal R u_a)^{[j]},
                                     \rho_\eta^{[n-1-j]}),
 \tag{28}
$$
and the mixed tests needed when both the potential and polarization
vary. No estimate tending to zero uniformly in the relevant limits
is proved for (28). The fixed-measure bound (14) does not imply one.
Strong-topology continuity and finite-energy mixed-integrability
are consequently unproved.

Fourth, supply the valid envelope/extended-energy construction and
the actual two-sign, Reeb-uniform differentiation estimate (27),
with a proved dense direction class and continuous-direction extension.
This is the missing variational input. Proportional identities,
FS approximation, signed count convergence, and compilation do not
constitute it. The original all-cone, all-Reeb P13.1 therefore remains
unresolved by this draft.

# P13.1: effective grading, finite Veronese degree, and energy factors

## Effective grading must be specified first

Work on an integral normal component of dimension n>=1. A normal affine cone
with R_0=k and a unique vertex has no intersecting distinct normal components;
the component formulation therefore covers the usual integral cone. For a
rational Reeb field choose an integral grading q_0. If all nonzero homogeneous
degrees have gcd g, divide this grading by g before taking a Veronese. Write
the original field as eta=s eta_e, where eta_e has effective integer degrees
of gcd one. eta_e need not be primitive in a larger ineffective ambient torus;
only the resulting grading and its effective G_m action are used below.

Choose d divisible enough that A=R^(d) is generated in its new degree one and
O_ProjR(d) is an ample line bundle L_d. The inclusion A->R is integral: for
every homogeneous f, f^d is in A. It is finite because R is finitely generated.
Moreover A=R^(mu_d). Normality of R implies normality of A: an element of
Frac(A) integral over A is integral over R, hence lies in R and is fixed by
mu_d, so lies in A. The effective grading makes the mu_d action faithful on
Frac(R): an element fixing every homogeneous generator has order dividing
the gcd of their degrees, which is one. The finite invariant-field extension
has degree d in characteristic zero. Thus pi:Spec R->Spec A has generic
degree d, even when the original positive grading was not standard.

The target C=Spec A is now a standard graded normal cone over V=Proj A=Proj R.
On a degree-one localization, A[a^{-1}]=A[a^{-1}]_0[a,a^{-1}], so C minus its
vertex is the actual G_m torsor of L_d^{-1}. This localization justifies the
product calculation; a section of mL_d does not by itself trivialize L_d.
Normality in dimension n>=2 also supplies extension across the codimension-n
vertex, identifying the section ring with the graded normal ring. In dimension
one the effective normal positive ring is k[t], and the assertion reduces to
the elementary point quotient.

## Finite-cover current factor

Put eta_C=eta_e/d on A, so its new degree-one elements have Reeb weight one.
A finite primary FS list on R can be raised to powers of a common degree D
divisible by d. Its FS potential then has the exact form

    phi_e = (1/d) pi^*Phi,

where Phi is an FS potential for eta_C on C. The coefficient of f_i^(D/q_i)
is (D/q_i)lambda_i, and its new degree is D/d; consequently the displayed
identity is exact. A zero-weight primary FS reference satisfies the same
relation rho_e=(1/d)pi^*rho_C. One can use these compatible reference
presentations; their agreement with the intrinsic zero-weight reference on
the invariant carrier follows from equivariance and the primary-list unit
property there. Also rho_e^+=(1/d)pi^*rho_C^+.

For FS potentials, all n functions in the mixed cone product are continuous
CLD-psh on the punctured cone. CLD Proposition16.1.5 applies to the finite
map between the boundaryless pure-dimensional analytifications. Its calibrated
degree is d on the generic locus, by Proposition13.3.7 and the reduced target's
generic multiplicity one. Multilinearity and the finite projection formula give

    pi_*[ddc phi_1 ... ddc phi_(n-1) ddc rho_e^+]
       = d^(1-n) [ddc Phi_1 ... ddc Phi_(n-1) ddc rho_C^+].

There are n factors of 1/d before pushing forward, and one degree-d factor
from pi. Omitting the latter produces an incorrect raw mass. The same formula
applies to each mixed reference term of the energy.

Restriction gives a homeomorphism of the torified invariant links: the value
of any homogeneous f is uniquely recovered from f^d in A, and torification
recovers the value of a sum from its homogeneous components. Compactness of
the links and Hausdorffness turn this continuous bijection into a homeomorphism.
This argument concerns invariant links, not arbitrary non-invariant points of
the finite map.

## Standard-cone radial calibration: the remaining local proof obligation

On an actual L_d frame, write a standard-cone FS potential as log|t|+h and its
reference as log|t|+h_0. Here h and h_0 are local projective metric weights.

the G_m torsor, while max(log|t|+h_0,0) supplies the radial factor. Fiberwise,
ddc max(log|t|+c,0) has mass one at the Gauss point of radius exp(-c). The
desired mixed product identity is therefore the Gauss-section pushforward of
the projective mixed curvature measure, with no extra degree.

A full proof should establish this identity for local test functions using

    V(eta_e) = d^(-(n-1)) (L_d^(n-1)),

## Signed spectral means and residue classes

For rational signed generator weights, the induced finite-type norm restricted
to A has a finitely generated rational filtration. Clearing denominators and
taking the required further section-ring Veronese, BHJ Proposition2.15 gives
an ample test configuration. BHJ Proposition3.12(iii) and Lemma7.3 identify

statements have normal-projective scope and do not require the smooth standing
hypothesis of BJ1805.11160v1 Theorem4.13.

The passage back from A to all degrees of R needs justification. Each residue
module R^(d,j) has generic rank one over A: it is the corresponding character
eigenspace of the cyclic degree-d function-field extension. Positive degrees
of gcd one give homogeneous multipliers into and out of every residue class.
Multiplication is injective and increases filtration weights by at least the
fixed weight of the multiplier. Dimension differences after a fixed degree
shift are O(m^(n-2)); all signed weights are O(m). Thus the two injections
squeeze normalized degree-wise sums to the same limit as residue zero, with
error O(m^(n-1)) in unnormalized sums. In dimension one the modules have
eventual dimension one and the error is O(1). A full paper should write the
ordered-jump comparison explicitly, including the missing-dimensional
contribution bounded by linear boundedness, rather than infer the conclusion
from the word Veronese.

D(M) the cumulative sum of Reeb degrees. The exact step-function identity

    D(M)=M N(M)-integral_0^M N(t)dt

and N(M)~V M^n/n! yield D(M)/(M N(M))->n/(n+1). Hence the cumulative signed
average is n/(n+1) times e. Together with the genuine quotient calculation,
the resulting proposed identity is

    E_eta = (n+1)/n S_signed(chi,eta).

This corrects the cumulative-versus-per-degree mismatch only after the genuine
current and model-energy identifications above, not from a constant-shift
example alone. Real generator weights require their actual quotient-norm

    E_Ld(phi)=(n+1)/n*S_standard.

The remaining algebraic passage is

    S_chi(eta)=S_standard/(s*d).

Here the norm on the full R, rather than only R^(d), must be handled.
The finitely generated Rees algebra Gamma is finite over Gamma^(d):
every homogeneous generator has its d-th power in the latter algebra.
Its fraction-field rank is d. Choose homogeneous elements giving a free
rank-d submodule F inside Gamma over Gamma^(d); multiply denominators

They induce filtered injective maps on finite degree-cutoff spaces with

of the restricted norm. A fixed degree shift changes cutoff dimensions

changes total weights by O(N(m))=O(m^n). All these errors vanish after
division by m*N(m). Formalize the spectral min-max inequalities for the

is an isometry. This should give the stated mean conversion, including
signed weights. Nonstandard degrees and residues modulo d need checking.

    E_eta(u_chi,eta)=(n+1)/n*S_chi(eta).

## 2. Genuine energy comparison and E1 preservation

Assuming the representation gate has actually been proved, the signed
cutoff estimate gives, for normalized FS u and its intrinsic root image,

    exp(-(2*n+1)*delta)*(-E_xi(u))
       <= -E_eta(R_xi,eta u)
       <= exp((2*n+1)*delta)*(-E_xi(u)),

where delta is the fixed-generator degree-ratio logarithmic distance.
These constants are independent of the primary list and its weights.
Uniform FS approximation gives the same estimate on normalized CPSH.

For u in normalized E1, do not silently assume a defining decreasing
CPSH net is normalized. Let u_i decrease to u and a_i=u_i(v_triv)
decrease to zero. N_i=u_i-a_i need not be decreasing, but each is
normalized CPSH and E_xi(N_i)=E_xi(u_i)-a_i. The root-shift slope
bounds give ||R(u_i)-R(N_i)||_K <= |a_i|/c. Monotonicity and the
unit translation rule bound the energy difference by the same amount.
The raw R(u_i) decrease to R(u). The quantitative lower energy bound
therefore proves target E1 membership; decreasing energy continuity
gives the displayed estimate for u. Applying the inverse proves onto
E1 on the normalized constant quotients.

This proves a useful uniform finite-energy comparison. It does not by
itself prove continuity for arbitrary strongly convergent nets, nor
uniform tails over an entire merely energy-bounded family. Those stronger
claims must not be asserted or made artificial prerequisites for the
following differentiation argument if it avoids them.

## 3. A fixed dense direction class with uniform positive domination

Fix one rational Reeb xi0. Take D to be the rational linear span of
differences of its actual FS potentials with rational weights, restricted
to the common torified compact carrier K. Allow arbitrary rational
positive equivariant slope in representations; differences of FS at
one fixed slope alone need not be dense. Verify the lattice/Stone--Weierstrass

must be established for this exact class. Sums and scalar multiples can
be written a-b with a,b having the same positive rational equivariant

max(a-b,c-d)=max(a+d,c+b)-(b+d) proves lattice closure, with slope
allowed to increase. Rational constants and separation then give uniform
density by the real Stone--Weierstrass theorem applied to its closure.
For separation, distinct torified points differ on a T-homogeneous

max((lambda-v(g))/d_xi0(wt(g)),-M). Suitable rational lambda and M

    a_eta=A*rho_eta+A0*phi_xi0(v+rho_eta(v)*eta)

## 4. The continuous-obstacle energy, without an envelope antecedent

For g in C(K) define an actual energy extremum

    F_eta(g)=sup{E_eta(w): w in CPSH(eta), w|K<=g}.

This is finite, monotone, translation equivariant and 1-Lipschitz. The
reference plus min(g) is a competitor; any competitor is bounded above
by the reference plus max(g). If g is the restriction of an actual CPSH
potential w, then F_eta(g)=E_eta(w) by energy monotonicity. In the
rational quotient verify equality with the precise BJ continuous-obstacle
energy. This comparison involves the existing genuine energy, not a
new inverse measure.

That equality must account for the residual torus: cone competitors
correspond initially to residual-torified projective competitors, whereas
BJ's supremum allows every projective competitor. Define the continuous
torification q on the entire quotient and identify K with its image via
the unique torified Gauss lift. The induced obstacle satisfies g=g*q.
For a projective FS metric psi, decomposing its finitely many sections
into residual-torus weight components gives psi*q as another actual FS
metric. The ultrametric inequality gives psi*q>=psi at every analytic
point. If psi<=g, then psi*q<=g as well. Pass through uniform FS
approximation to actual CPSH; monotonicity of the genuine energy then
shows the unrestricted and torified suprema agree. Verify linearization,
the canonical invariant reference, continuity and the identification
with K; do not equate the two suprema without this step.

For a fixed bounded obstacle |g|<=M, truncate competitors from below
by max(w,rho_eta-M). The supremum can thus be taken over potentials
bounded by M on K. Their intrinsic root changes are uniformly O(M*delta).
For a bounded w, put a=w(v_triv), Nw=w-a. The root-shift bound gives

    ||R(w)-R(Nw)-a||_K <= (exp(delta)-1)*|a|.

The normalized energy comparison and -E(Nw)<=2M then give a uniform
O(M*delta) energy change. Transfer competitors with R and subtract the
small uniform constraint error. Applying the reverse map proves
continuity of F_eta(g) in eta, locally uniformly for bounded obstacles.
Do not try to control arbitrary competitors by the unbounded pointwise
root estimate without this legitimate truncation.

For u in CPSH(xi), rational eta_l -> xi with u_l=R_xi,eta_l u have
locally uniform convergence. The continuous-obstacle extrema converge,
as do the actual probability measures by round55's CLD theorem. Passing
the *uniform* rational quadratic estimate to the limit supplies it for
irrational xi and bounded u, still with the same fixed f in D.

## 5. Outer extension and decreasing limits

For an upper-semicontinuous obstacle h on K, bounded above, set

    F_eta_down(h)=inf{F_eta(g): g in C(K), g>=h}.

The proposed canonical extension agrees with the genuine E_eta at every
actual PSH potential, including E1. To prove this, use u_i in CPSH
decreasing to u. For any continuous g>=u and epsilon>0, the open sets
{u_i<g+epsilon} eventually cover compact K; directedness gives
u_i<=g+epsilon everywhere for all sufficiently late i. Hence
E(u)<=F(g)+epsilon. Conversely g=u_i gives F_down(u)<=E(u_i).
Let i tend to its limit and epsilon to zero.

The same compactness argument, with u_i+t*f decreasing to u+t*f,
shows F_down(u+t*f)=lim_i F(u_i+t*f). This proves independence from
the defining net. Its finite value for u in E1 follows from monotonicity
and the translation bounds E(u)-|t|*||f|| <= F_down(u+t*f)
<=E(u)+|t|*||f||.

For fixed eta the existing genuine mixed operator's decreasing-net weak
continuity gives MA_eta(u_i)->MA_eta(u). Passing the bounded quadratic
estimate through this net proves

    |F_eta_down(u+t*f)-E_eta(u)-t*integral_K f MA_eta(u)|
       <= C_(Q,f)*t^2

for every eta in Q and every u in E1(eta), with both signs. The extension
is defined using the actual CPSH energy and agrees with it on E1; its
derivative is the actual cone probability operator. No equality with
E(P(u+t*f)) is asserted if an actual global envelope is unavailable.
Judge explicitly whether this is the differentiation intended by the
unchanged original task, rather than declaring an unproved envelope
equality or changing the target to obtain acceptance.

For any fixed continuous f on K choose f_m in D uniformly. Monotonicity,
the unit translation rule and probability mass bound the difference of
derivative remainders divided by |t| by

    2*||f-f_m||_K + C_(Q,f_m)*|t|.

Choose m before t. This is an explicit uniform differentiability modulus
over Q and all E1 potentials for each continuous direction. It does not
claim a quadratic bound for every continuous f or uniformity over the
entire unit ball of C(K).
