                                                                                  Abstract
                                                 Given an ample line bundle L over a projective K-variety X, with K a
                                                 non-Archimedean field, we study limits of non-Archimedean metrics on L
                                                 associated to submultiplicative sequences of norms on the graded pieces of
                                                 the section ring R(X, L). We show that in a rather general case, the corre-
                                                 sponding asymptotic Fubini-Study operator yields a one-to-one correspon-
                                                 dence between equivalence classes of bounded graded norms and bounded
                                                 plurisubharmonic metrics that are regularizable from below. This general-
                                                 izes results of Boucksom-Jonsson where this problem has been studied in
                                                 the trivially valued case.

Introduction.
Let X be a projective variety deﬁned over a non-Archimedean ﬁeld K, endowed
with a line bundle L which we will assume to be ample. To those objects,
one can associate their Berkovich analytification, allowing the use of tech-
niques analogue to those of complex analytic geometry. By considering the
non-Archimedean avatar of the space of Kähler potentials: the space H(L) of
Fubini-Study potentials, one can deﬁne the class of plurisubharmonic met-
rics. In this article, we look at the quantization of such plurisubharmonic metrics
by sequences of norms acting on sections of tensor powers of L, in line with the
ideas of [DLR18]. In particular, we generalize results in this direction obtained
in [BJ18a], building on the theory developed in [BE20].

A central object of study in complex pluripotential theory is the space of Kähler
potentials: given a polarized compact connected Kähler manifold (X/C, L), and
the curvature ωL of a reference metric φ0 on L,

                       H = {φ ∈ C ∞ (X), ωL + ddc φ > 0}.

In non-Archimedean pluripotential theory, where we look at the Berkovich ana-
lytiﬁcations of K-varieties, we similarly choose a reference metric φ0 on L, which
gives a function s 7→ |s|φ0 = |s| acting on sections of L (which extends naturally
to sections of powers of L), then consider the set of Fubini-Study potentials
H(L) whose objects are continuous functions (or potentials) ϕ from the Berkovich
analytiﬁcation X an to the reals, of the form

                           ϕ = m−1 max{log |si | + ci },

where the (si ) are sections of mL forming a basis of the space H 0 (mL), and the
ci are real constants. By allowing decreasing limits, we obtain a much larger
class, PSH(L), that of φ0 -plurisubharmonic functions (or φ0 -psh functions).

The existence of the pointwise is ensured by Fekete’s lemma, thanks to the sub-
multiplicativity condition and the boundedness of ζ• , and the usc regularization
turns out to be plurisubharmonic provided the pair (X, L) satisﬁes some mild
condition, continuity of envelopes, conjectured to always hold as soon as X
is normal, and known to be true e.g. for line bundles on smooth varieties deﬁned
over a discretely or trivially valued ﬁeld K of equal characteristic zero (the im-
portant case of the ﬁeld of Laurent series C((t)) is such an example; a review of
the currently known cases is presented in Example 5.2.3).

This operator maps bounded graded norms to the set of plurisubharmonic func-
tions regularizable from below PSH↑ , i.e. psh functions which are limits of in-
creasing nets of Fubini-Study potentials. It is not injective. However, in [BJ18a,
Theorem C], S. Boucksom and M. Jonsson prove that, if K is trivially valued
and of characteristic 0, this operator descends to an injection from the space of
bounded graded norms modulo the equivalence relation ∼, onto PSH↑ . The main
result of this article is a generalization of this statement.

Theorem (A). Assume (X, L) to admit continuity of envelopes. The asymptotic
Fubini-Study operator FS then defines a bijection:

                           FS : N• (L)/ ∼ → PSH↑ (L).

The main ingredient in the proof of Theorem A is the following Theorem, which
builds on the main result of [BJ18a] and generalizes it:

Theorem (B). With the hypotheses of Theorem A, and given two bounded graded
norms ζ• , ζ•′ , we have that

                         E(FS(ζ• ), FS(ζ•′ )) = vol(ζ• , ζ•′ ).

A similar result has been proved, again in the characteristic 0, trivially valued
case, in [BJ18a].

The term on the left-hand side is the relative Monge-Ampère energy be-
tween two continuous psh functions, which exists thanks to the theory of non-
Archimedean Monge-Ampère operators of A. Chambert-Loir and A. Ducros ([CLD12]),
and has been studied for example in [BE20].

The volume, on the right-hand side, is the ﬁrst moment of the asymptotic spectral
measure σ(ζ• , ζ•′ ).

It is a generalization of the relative volumes of balls studied in [BB10], and Theo-
rem B is closely related to the results of the latter article. In the aforementioned
article, where the base ﬁeld is C, one considers two speciﬁc norms on spaces of

H. Chen and C. Maclean have studied volumes more generally in [CM15], and

as a stand-alone result, is the following Theorem stating that we can recover

The reason why this result is useful is that graded norms generated in degree
one are simpler to study: their asymptotic behaviour is governed by that of the
algebra of sections R(X, L), and by the norm ζ1 on H 0 (L).

The proof of Theorem B also relies on the theory of models of varieties over the
valuation ring: to any globally generated model (X , L) of (X, L) as described
in e.g. [BE20] is associated a lattice norm on H 0 (L), that is, a norm for
which there exists an orthonormal basis in the non-Archimedean sense. Using
a speciﬁc construction of such a model L, one obtains a graded norm for which
Theorem B essentially holds, thanks to the results of [BE20]. We then pass from
this speciﬁc case, to the more general case of norms generated in degree one, by
approximation of volumes: namely, the set of lattice norms is dense in the set of
norms on a ﬁxed vector space, for the sup (or d∞ ) distance. One can then create
an approximation by norms "almost" generated in degree one (see Remark 3.3.9),
and use Lipschitz continuity of volumes with respect to this distance. Finally, an
application of Theorem C concludes the proof.

Notation.
K and L always denote ﬁelds, where our ﬁelds are assumed to be commutative.

A variety X over K is a geometrically integral, separated scheme, of ﬁnite type
over K.

Given L a line bundle over a K-variety X, H 0 (X, L) is identiﬁed with the space
of sections Γ(X, L) of L over X. When no confusion can arise, we will often write
H 0 (L). h0 (X, L) is the K-dimension of the K-vector space H 0 (X, L).

Given a K-vector space V , and a positive integer n, V ⊗n refers to the n-th tensor
power of V ; V ⊙n to the n-th symmetric power of V ; and V ∧n to the n-th exterior
power of V .

ζ always refers to a norm on a vector space.

Unless stated otherwise, d refers to the dimension of some vector space, or of
some variety X, whichever is natural in context.

1.1     Non-Archimedean fields.
Definition 1.1.1. Let K be a ﬁeld. A real-valued non-Archimedean absolute
value on K is a function
                                | · | : K → R+
satisfying the following properties:
    (i) |0| = 0, |1| = 1;

 (ii) |xy| = |x| · |y|, for x, y ∈ K;

(iii) |x + y| ≤ max{|x|, |y|}, for x, y ∈ K.
We say that K is:
     • trivially valued if |x| = 1 for all x 6= 0;

     • discretely valued if K is not trivially valued, and the set of values of | · |
       is a discrete subgroup of R+ ;

     • densely valued otherwise (we recall that a subgroup of R+ is either dis-
       crete or dense).
   Note that a real-valued non-Archimedean absolute value enriches K with a
topology.
Definition 1.1.2. Let K be a ﬁeld with a real-valued non-Archimedean absolute
value. We say that K is a non-Archimedean field if it is Cauchy complete with
respect to the topology induced by its absolute value.
   The reader may ﬁnd a review of non-Archimedean valued ﬁelds and their
ﬁner completeness properties in [CS18].
   To any non-Archimedean ﬁeld K, one may associate the following objects:
     • its valuation ring K◦ , deﬁned as the set of elements in K with absolute
       value ≤ 1;

     • the maximal ideal of K◦ , K◦◦ , characterized as the set of elements in K◦
       (or K) with absolute value < 1;

     • its residue field K̃ = K◦ /K◦◦ .
Definition 1.1.3. Let K be a non-Archimedean ﬁeld. If

                                char K = char K̃ = p

for some p, we say that K is of equicharacteristic p; otherwise, i.e. char K = 0
and char K̃ = p ≥ 2, we say that K has mixed characteristic.
Remark 1.1.4. By [CS18], a non-trivially valued non-Archimedean ﬁeld may
always be embedded into a ﬁeld which satisﬁes the following properties:

   • Cauchy complete;
   • algebraically closed.
Indeed, let K̂ be the metric completion of the algebraic closure of K. Then K̂ is

1.2   Berkovich analytification.
Fix a non-Archimedean ﬁeld (K, | · |).

In the complex case, brieﬂy, there exists an analytification functor sending a
complex projective variety (X, OX ) to its associated complex analytic space
(X an , OX an ), and the categories of coherent sheaves over X and X an are equiva-
lent. This principle fails a priori in the non-Archimedean case when one applies
the same constructions mutatis mutandis. The category of Berkovich spaces,
which we will not describe here but in its most simple cases, serves as a "cor-
rect" category for a non-Archimedean GAGA principle. The essential result is
the following theorem:
Theorem 1.2.1. Let (X, OX ) be a projective variety over K. The analytifica-
tion functor yields the Berkovich analytification (X an , OX an ), which has the
following topological properties:
   • connectedness;
   • compactness;
   • Hausdorff.
   The reader is invited to consult the book of V. Berkovich [Ber90], or for
example [Poi10], for an exposition of the theory.
Definition 1.2.2. Let R be any ring. A multiplicative seminorm on R is a
function which satisﬁes the properties (i), (ii), and (iii) of Deﬁnition 1.1.1.
Example 1.2.3 (Aﬃne X an ). Assume X = Spec A, where A is an algebra of
ﬁnite type over K.
   • the set of points of X an is the set of multiplicative seminorms | · |A on A,
     such that
                                       |a|A = |a|
      for all a ∈ K, that is, | · |A extends the absolute value on K;
   • the topology on X an is the topology of pointwise convergence.
In general, given any variety over K, one proceeds by gluing local spaces as
constructed above, similarly to the construction of abstract schemes by gluing
aﬃne schemes.
Remark 1.2.4. The general construction also applies to Archimedean ﬁelds,
and in the complex case, the Berkovich analytiﬁcation of X coincides with the
complex analytic space X an .

Throughout this section, unless otherwise speciﬁed, we ﬁx a non-Archimedean

                                     ζ : V → R+ ,

   • ζ(v) = 0 if and only if v = 0V ;

5.2   Envelopes.
Following [BE20, 7.5], we deﬁne two important notions of envelopes for bounded
functions.

Definition 5.2.1. Let φ be a bounded metric on L. The psh envelope of φ is
deﬁned as
                     P (φ) = sup{φ′ ∈ PSH(L), φ′ ≤ φ}.
The regular psh envelope of f is deﬁned as

                  Q(φ) = sup{φ′ ∈ PSH(X) ∩ C 0 (X), φ′ ≤ φ}.

We similarly deﬁne those envelopes for functions, by identifying the space of
continuous metrics on a line bundle as an aﬃne space modelled on the space of
continuous functions as before.

                                            37
Definition 5.2.2 (Continuity of envelopes). We say that the pair (X, L) admits
continuity of envelopes if the following property holds true:
   • if φ is a continuous metric on L, then P (φ) is continuous.
Note that we then have P (φ) = Q(φ).
Example 5.2.3. By [BJ18b], a smooth, projective variety X deﬁned over any
ﬁeld K which satisﬁes all of the following properties:
   • K is of equal characteristic 0;

   • K is either trivially or discretely valued,
admits continuity of envelopes for any line bundle L over X. Furthermore, by
[GJKM19], continuity of envelopes also holds:
   • for any line bundle on a curve, over any ﬁeld;

   • for all line bundles on a variety X over K, where K is a discretely valued
     ﬁeld of positive characteristic p, provided we have resolution of singularities
     over K in any dimension.
Definition 5.2.4. The asymptotic Fubini-Study operator is deﬁned on the
set of graded norms as the upper semi-continuous regularization of the limit of
the usual Fubini-Study operators, that is, given a bounded graded norm ζ• ,

5.3   Plurisubharmonic functions regularizable from below.
In this section, we investigate the image of the asymptotic Fubini-Study operator.
Most of the material here is simply the translation of results from [BJ18a] from
the trivially valued case to our case.

We ﬁrst need to consider an important set of valuations contained in the Berkovich
analytiﬁcation of a variety.
Definition 5.3.1. A valuation ν : K(X)∗ → R is said to be divisorial if there
exists a normal, projective, birational model Y → X of X, a prime divisor E ⊂ Y ,
and a positive real number c, such that

                                   ν = c · ordE .

The set X div of divisorial valuations on X is dense in X an , see e.g. [JM12, 6.3].

                                        38
Definition 5.3.2. We say that a plurisubharmonic function φ is regularizable
from below, and we write
                                 φ ∈ PSH↑
if and only if φ is the pointwise limit on X div of an increasing net of Fubini-Study
potentials, equivalently of an increasing net of continuous, psh functions.

Remark 5.3.3. We then have that φ is the usc regularized supremum of such
a net. Furthermore, by [BJ18a, L4.4] (which extends to our non-trivially valued
case where continuity of envelopes holds), φ ∈ PSH↑ if and only if φ = Q∗ (φ),
where Q∗ (φ) denotes the usc-regularized envelope

                Q∗ (φ) = usc sup{φ′ ∈ PSH(X) ∩ C 0 (X), φ′ ≤ φ}.


We then show that PSH↑ coincides with the image of the asymptotic Fubini-
Study operator.

Theorem 5.3.4. A function belongs to PSH↑ if and only if there exists a bounded
graded norm ζ• such that FS(ζ• ) = φ.

Proof. Assume φ is the image of some bounded graded norm ζ• by the asymptotic
Fubini-Study operator, i.e. φ = usc (limm FSm (ζm )). In particular, φ is psh, by
continuity of envelopes. By the remark above, it is then enough to show that
φ = usc Q(φ), which is clear by construction.

We now assume φ ∈ PSH↑ . Now, by [BE20, T7.27], we have that Q(φ) =
limm FSm (Nm (φ)). Then, by deﬁnition, Q∗ (φ) = FS(N• (φ)). Since φ ∈ PSH↑ ,
we have that φ = Q∗ (φ), thus

                                  φ = FS(N• (φ)),

which proves the Theorem.

5.4   The asymptotic Fubini-Study operator descends to a bijec-
      tion.
In this section, we prove the following theorem, which is a generalization of
[BJ18a, T4.16]:

Theorem 5.4.1 (Theorem A). Let (X, L) admit continuity of envelopes, with L
ample. The asymptotic Fubini-Study operator FS then defines a bijection

                              N• (L)/ ∼ → PSH↑ (L).

The proof follows that of the aforementioned theorem. We start with preparatory
lemmas:

Lemma 5.4.2. Assume that ζ• ≥ ζ•′ pointwise. Then,

                        d1 (ζ• , ζ•′ ) = 0 ⇔ FS(ζ• ) = FS(ζ•′ ).

                                          39
Proof. We ﬁrst notice that, since ζ• ≤ ζ•′ pointwise, the deﬁnition of d1 using
successive minima implies

                                 d1 (ζ• , ζ•′ ) = vol(ζ• , ζ•′ ),

and this volume is equal to 0 by our hypothesis. Using Theorem 5.1.1, we then
have that
                            E(FS(ζ• ), FS(ζ•′ )) = 0.
But [BJ18b, C6.28] implies that FS(ζ•′ ) − FS(ζ• ) is constant, hence equal to zero
since E(FS(ζ• ), FS(ζ•′ )) = 0.

Remark 5.4.3. Note that [BJ18b, C6.28] holds modulo a certain hypothesis
(see [BJ18b, R6.27]), which is true if (X, L) has property (ce).

Lemma 5.4.4. Let ζ• be an element of N• (L). We then have that ζ• ≥ N• (FS(ζ• )),
and furthermore those norms are equivalent.

Proof. The ﬁrst assertion follows from [BE20, L7.24] (and its proof). To show
asymptotic equivalence, by the previous lemma, it is thus enough to show that
FS(N• (FS(ζ• ))) = FS(ζ• ). But, by [BE20, T7.27],

                            FS(N• (FS(ζ• ))) = Q(FS(ζ• )),

which in turn is equal to FS(ζ• ) itself, since it is a limit of an increasing net of
Fubini-Study potentials.

We now prove Theorem 5.4.1.

Proof. Note that

                   d1 (ζ• , ζ•′ ) = vol(ζ• , ζ• ∨ ζ•′ ) + vol(ζ•′ , ζ• ∨ ζ•′ ),

and by Theorem 5.1.1, the right-hand side is in fact equal to

                E(FS(ζ• ), FS(ζ• ∨ ζ•′ )) + E(FS(ζ•′ ), FS(ζ• ∨ ζ•′ )).           (6)

The trick is now to prove the following:

                         FS(ζ• ∨ ζ•′ ) = Q(FS(ζ• ) ∧ FS(ζ•′ )),

where ∧ denotes the min operator. Since, by Lemma 5.4.4,

                                     ζ• ≥ N• (FS(ζ• )),

                                     ζ• ∼ N• (FS(ζ• )),
and the same holds for ζ•′ , then

                         ζ• ∨ ζ•′ ≥ N• (FS(ζ• )) ∧ N• (FS(ζ•′ )),

                         ζ• ∨ ζ•′ ∼ N• (FS(ζ• )) ∧ N• (FS(ζ•′ )),

                                               40
and furthermore, by [BJ18a, (4.4)],

                 N• (FS(ζ• )) ∨ N• (FS(ζ•′ )) = N• (FS(ζ• ) ∧ FS(ζ•′ )).

Lemma 5.4.2 then implies

                      FS(N• (FS(ζ• ) ∧ FS(ζ•′ ))) = FS(ζ• ∨ ζ•′ ),

and the left-hand side is equal to Q(FS(ζ• ) ∧ FS(ζ•′ )), by [BE20, T7.27]. We may
now rewrite (6) as:

 d1 (ζ• , ζ•′ ) = E(FS(ζ• ), Q(FS(ζ• ) ∧ FS(ζ•′ ))) + E(FS(ζ•′ ), Q(FS(ζ• ) ∧ FS(ζ•′ ))).

Now,
                            FS(ζ• ) ≥ Q(FS(ζ• ) ∧ FS(ζ•′ ))
and
                            FS(ζ•′ ) ≥ Q(FS(ζ• ) ∧ FS(ζ•′ )),
so that the two energies above have the same sign. In particular, the distance
