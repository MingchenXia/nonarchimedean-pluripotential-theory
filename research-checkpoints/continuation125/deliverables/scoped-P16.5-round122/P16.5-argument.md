## 2. Bounded genuine potentials fit the actual published product domain

Write B(theta)=PSH(theta) intersect bounded functions. This is a statement about the entire genuine numerical bounded cone, not a newly defined smaller test family. Since theta is nef, zero and every constant are genuine theta-psh by BJ4.3. If u belongs to B(theta), then u belongs to PSH(theta+h), since zero is h-psh and numerical addition is BJ4.7. It is bounded, so its ample energy is finite by monotonicity between its constant bounds. Thus the restriction of u to X^lin belongs to the actual ambient vector space E~1 of Definition7.12. The bounded potential itself remains a function on the entire original X^an; the auxiliary restriction is only the published pairing domain.

Theorems7.14 and7.18 now apply with their stated hypotheses. Define the raw bounded energy and mixed measures by their actual energy-pairing formulas:

$$E_\theta(u)=\frac{(\theta,u)^{n+1}-(\theta,0)^{n+1}}{n+1},\qquad
M_\theta(u_1,\ldots,u_n)=\bigwedge_{i=1}^n(\theta+dd^cu_i).$$

The total mass is theta^n, and M is positive for genuine bounded theta-psh inputs, by Theorem7.18 and Proposition7.19(i). The energy shifts by c theta^n under u -> u+c. At a finite PL/model stage, Proposition7.19(ii) gives the actual sum of component Dirac measures with coefficients

$$\operatorname{ord}_F(\mathcal X_0)\prod_i(\theta_{\mathcal X}+D_i)|_F.$$

These are actual model metric intersection products with the given reference, component multiplicities and bundle class. For continuous nef inputs, CLD compatibility follows by the actual metric decomposition (L,u)=(L+H,u)-(H,0). Each term has an actual ample class and a verified continuous psh metric. BJ Remark7.23 applies to those ample terms; BE v3 Sections7.14 and8.4--8.7 provide the multilinear difference-of-psh extension and regularization. Expanding the actual metric differences identifies the continuous nef products. Remark7.23 alone is not cited as a nonample statement. Individual current factors are not asserted to have been constructed on all bounded inputs merely from the wedge notation.

One can also recover the same bounded theta-products as limits of actual positive ample operators before taking any limit. For epsilon>0 rational, each u_i remains genuinely (theta+epsilon h)-psh. The actual G0 ample operator on L+epsilon H is defined, positive, and has raw mass (theta+epsilon h)^n. Multilinearity of the published mixed measures makes its pairing with each continuous test a finite polynomial in epsilon, with the constant term M_theta(u_1,...,u_n). Thus the positive ample products converge weakly to the displayed genuine theta-product. Uniqueness in Theorem7.18 makes this independent of H. The limit is identified with an already constructed original metric product; it is not the definition of an arbitrary inverse lift.

For a decreasing net u_i in B(theta) with bounded genuine limit u, any tail has common upper and lower bounds. All terms lie in E1(theta+h), and Proposition7.19(iv) yields the original bounded mixed weak continuity. Theorem7.14 gives bounded-energy continuity for these nets. Integrability and the energy-pairing identity for an ambient E~1 test are Proposition7.19(v). This proves no weighted theorem for a test outside that stated ambient domain.

## 3. Genuine envelopes at the nef boundary

For clarity we can prove the nef class-closure step directly, without a general abstract monotone-net diagonal assertion. There is an equality of genuine cones

$$\operatorname{PSH}(\theta)=\bigcap_{\epsilon\in\mathbb Q_{>0}}\operatorname{PSH}(\theta+\epsilon h).$$

The forward inclusion is numerical addition with the genuine constant h-psh metric. For the converse, the actual ample approximation theorem gives finite continuous FS functions f_{epsilon,j} decreasing to a member u of every displayed ample cone. Use triples (epsilon,j,delta), with delta>0, and functions f_{epsilon,j}+delta. Given two triples, choose a smaller positive epsilon_3 and delta_3 smaller than both previous deltas. Their old minimum b is finite continuous, and u+delta_3<b at every point. The increasing open sets {f_{epsilon_3,j}+delta_3<b} cover compact X^an. A finite subcover, followed by one common later net index, provides a third triple dominated by both old functions. Including epsilon and delta as ordered coordinates makes this a decreasing defining FS net. Refining any prescribed finite pointwise threshold, including at a pole, proves its endpoint is u. Its classes approach theta, so the numerical definition gives u in PSH(theta).

Consequently a decreasing family whose classes approach theta and which stays above a finite constant belongs, at its endpoint, to every fixed approaching ample cone and hence to PSH(theta). This proves the particular closure needed below. It does not claim a proof of the source's general abstract Lemma4.6.


For continuous f on the original compact X^an, let P_theta f be the pointwise supremum of genuine theta-psh functions bounded by f. Constant competitors show that it lies between min f and max f. The nef envelope property makes the upper regularization theta-psh; continuity of f makes that regularization <=f, so it is itself a competitor. Hence P_theta f equals that regularization, is genuinely theta-psh, and is bounded. This argument does not assert its continuity.

Put theta_epsilon=theta+epsilon h and q_epsilon=P_theta_epsilon f. The actual ample theory makes q_epsilon continuous. As epsilon decreases to zero, q_epsilon decreases pointwise. The common constant bounds make the limit generically finite, and the preceding genuine nef intersection proves that its limit is theta-psh. That limit is <=f. Conversely every theta-psh competitor is theta_epsilon-psh, so P_theta f<=q_epsilon. Therefore

$$q_\epsilon\downarrow q_0=P_\theta f.$$

All q_epsilon, for epsilon<=1, lie in the one actual ample cone PSH(theta+h) and have the same two constant bounds. Theorems7.14/7.18 and Proposition7.19 therefore apply to this decreasing family within their proper domain. Polynomial expansion, coefficient by coefficient, proves

$$E_{\theta_\epsilon}(q_\epsilon)\longrightarrow E_\theta(q_0),\qquad
M_{\theta_\epsilon}(q_\epsilon)\longrightarrow M_\theta(q_0).$$

Some individual theta-coefficients at positive epsilon may be signed; positivity of those coefficients is neither used nor asserted. Their continuous-test pairings converge by Proposition7.19(iv) in the common ample finite-energy cone. The full operators on the left and the final operator on the right are positive. For each signed measure coefficient, expand every factor (theta,u) as (theta+h,u)-(h,0). This is a fixed finite sum of positive mixed products, with their signs retained. Each positive summand has the fixed finite numerical mixed mass of its ample classes, independent of u and epsilon. The sum of those masses bounds total variation uniformly, so multiplying a coefficient by a positive power of epsilon makes it tend weakly to zero. The corresponding energy coefficients converge by the bounded published pairing theorem in the same common ample cone. This justifies the varying-epsilon polynomial passage with actual signed coefficients, rather than only PL test functions.

## 4. Uniform continuity needed for variation

Pointwise order and constants give the original envelope estimate

$$\|P_\theta f-P_\theta g\|_\infty\leq\|f-g\|_\infty.$$

For uniformly converging bounded genuine theta-psh potentials u_j -> u, their theta mixed measures and energy converge. Here is a reduction to the published decreasing-net theorem that avoids any unproved nonample strong topology. From any sequence converging in sup norm, pass to a subsequence with errors at most $4^{-j}$; adding $3\cdot4^{-j}$ makes that subsequence decreasing pointwise to u. Constants do not change curvature products. Energy changes only by $3\cdot4^{-j}\theta^n$. The decreasing convergence in the common ample cone from Section2 therefore gives the desired convergence on that subsequence. Every subsequence admits such a further subsequence, so the entire sequence converges. For each fixed continuous measure test this proves continuity on the sup-norm metric space; consequently it also proves convergence for arbitrary sup-norm converging nets. It does not apply a false monotone convergence theorem to arbitrary Borel nets.

## 5. Both derivatives of continuous-obstacle energy

Define F_theta(f)=E_theta(P_theta f) for f in C0(X^an). It is finite, concave and translation equivariant with raw coefficient theta^n. Concavity follows from the genuine convex cone, envelope order and bounded energy concavity; alternatively it follows from the pointwise limit of the ample concave functionals in Section3.

We first give the needed ample supporting inequalities rather than importing an unproved assertion about arbitrary obstacles. For an ample class omega and continuous f, q=P_omega f is continuous genuine. If 0<=s<=1, monotonicity gives P_omega(q+s(f-q))=q. The actual ample G0 outer derivative at q in the continuous direction f-q therefore yields orthogonality:

$$\int(f-q)M_\omega(q)=0.$$

Energy concavity along genuine psh segments gives

$$E_\omega(r)\leq E_\omega(q)+\int(r-q)M_\omega(q),\qquad r=P_\omega g.$$ Since r<=g and M_omega(q) is positive, the displayed orthogonality proves the supporting inequality

$$F_\omega(g)-F_\omega(f)\leq\int(g-f)M_\omega(P_\omega f).$$

Use this inequality once at f and once at f+tg. For t>0 it bounds the quotient between integral g M_omega(P_omega(f+tg)) and integral g M_omega(P_omega f). Apply it with omega=theta_epsilon and pass epsilon down to zero using the actual energy and operator limits from Section3. Thus

$$\int gM_\theta(P_\theta(f+tg))\leq
\frac{F_\theta(f+tg)-F_\theta(f)}t\leq
\int gM_\theta(P_\theta f).$$

The envelope Lipschitz estimate and Section4 make the left integral converge to the right one as t down to zero. For t<0 the same two supporting inequalities reverse under division; the two bounds have the same limit as t increases to zero. Therefore both derivatives exist and agree:

$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}F_\theta(f+tg)=\int gM_\theta(P_\theta f),\qquad f,g\in C^0(X^{\rm an}).$$

In particular this proves both outer signs at every **continuous genuine** theta-psh base potential u, by taking f=u. It proves the continuous-obstacle formula for every nef actual L, without semiampleness and including zero raw top mass. It does not infer a derivative at an arbitrary unbounded member of the floor-defined E1(theta), or even at an arbitrary bounded discontinuous base, from this continuous-obstacle argument. For example, on the product of two projective lines with L pulled back from one factor, the raw top mass is zero and the present top-degree energy is constant. A different lower-degree mixed energy can have positive mass and is a different functional.
