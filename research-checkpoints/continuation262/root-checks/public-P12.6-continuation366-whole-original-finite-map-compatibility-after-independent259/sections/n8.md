Degrees, towers, the one-slot limitation, and independent gauges

For arbitrary finite towers, top generic degrees compose with weights: d_P(direct)=sum_{Q over P} [K_Q:K_P] d_Q(next). Full closed residue degrees also multiply. Applying (A) twice therefore gives exactly the direct unnormalized all-pulled formula, without a constant-degree assumption. Normal-domain traces compose by grouping all embeddings in a common normal splitting field; generic inseparable lengths multiply, and G331 choice independence proves equality on EVERY support.

For general conductor traces, a tower identity requires BOTH intermediate full admissibilities: H admissible from the last source to the middle, and its traced middle function admissible to the base. These are essential hypotheses, not inferred from direct admissibility. To verify composition normalize each minimal component. A minimal last-source component contracts to a minimal middle component exactly when it dominates it; for an integral extension incomparability makes this intermediate minimal component unique. On dominating chains, the integral closure of the base domain in the final fraction field is also the integral closure of the finite intermediate domain there: integrality is transitive, and coefficients in the base remain coefficients in the intermediate ring. Thus all normal centres and quotient-support trace diagrams group the same field embeddings. Chains vertical over an intermediate domain do not contribute its normal trace; lower and zero centres remain in both admissibility tests. After the two positive full descents, uniqueness in G331 identifies the composite with the direct trace. Direct fibre constancy alone supplies no intermediate positive descent.

Finite flat kernels compose on every Artinian valuation fibre. Filter a lower local factor by its composition series of length l. The next finite flat algebra tensors this series exactly; after splitting, each upper residue factor of length m_j appears l times. The direct lengths are l m_j. This retains nilpotence and inseparability and agrees with determinant transitivity (also on generic product fields and hence over a reduced base). Constant ranks multiply, so normalized flat averages compose.

For varying branch degrees normalized composition fails sharply over ANY field. Let A=k[[t]], B=A times A, F=A times k[[s]], with t=s^2 in the second branch. Laurent parity gives field degree two in every characteristic. On normalized links take H=(0,-1). The direct weighted average is -2/3; successive normalized averages give -1/2. Both unnormalized traces are -2. Comparing each branch coefficient proves that normalized averages compose exactly when the next generic degree is constant over each intermediate fibre. In characteristic two the same example retains the purely inseparable degree.

More than ONE arbitrary source slot is false even for flat maps. Take a three-dimensional regular R with a=m, B=R times R, H=(-1,0), V=(0,-1). The source mixed product is zero because each component has a zero input. Both traces are -1, so the target mixed product has reference mass e(m)=1; both normalized traces are -1/2, giving mass 1/4. This exact contradiction prevents any all-arbitrary-slots trace formula. Only (C)--(D) are claimed.

For base primary b let t(v)=v_a(b)>0 and G(v)=v/t(v). Thus E_b(phi_b)(Gv)=E_a(phi_a)(v)/t(v), and positive domains correspond. G345 gives EXACTLY ONE measure factor:

This is raw reference mass, not probability normalization.

for these corresponding inputs. Together with rho_(aB) s=rho_c this gives the source weighted pushforward of (A) or (D). Omitting either function division or the ONE source measure factor is erroneous. Nonprimary sections can be noncompact and are not covered by these compact statements.
