# n1. Geometric domain, raw envelopes, and actual decreasing closure

We use only the entire qualified G331 for the function classes. Let R be an excellent local ring, replaced by its reduction, and a a proper maximal-primary ideal. X={v:v(a)=1} is its compact Hausdorff centred semivaluation section. Every prime support below the maximal ideal is retained. C is its finite continuous uniform closure; D consists of infima of nonempty actual decreasing C-nets, including bottom. G331 proves comparison with unit-or-primary ideal profiles, directed primary models, finite maxima, addition, nonnegative scaling, signed-profile density, all-support divisorial density, the normal zero dichotomy, and full positive finite-injection/conductor descent. It grants no envelope theorem, arbitrary-model cofinality, or singular retraction. All its source and scope qualifications remain attached.

For finite continuous phi write p(phi)=sup{h in D:h<=phi}. Negative constants are available, so this is finite bounded. It equals the C supremum: if g_alpha decreases to h<=phi, the increasing open sets {g_alpha<phi+epsilon} cover X. A finite subcover and directedness select one common index. The C function g_alpha-epsilon is below phi and at least h-epsilon. Consequently p is lower semicontinuous. It is continuous iff it belongs to D, and then it belongs to C: continuous p is uniformly approximated from below by finite maxima of C minorants, using the compact cover {g>p-epsilon}. Conversely every D function is upper semicontinuous. For p*(x)=inf_{open U containing x}sup_U p, continuity of phi gives p<=p*<=min(phi,0). Thus p* belongs to D iff p*=p is continuous. A greatest D minorant exists exactly in that case. Downward constant shifts compare competitors and prove ||p(phi)-p(psi)||<=||phi-psi||. None of this assumes positivity of an increasing supremum.

There is an actual graded ideal formula. For k>=1 put J_k(phi)={f in R:v(f)>=-k phi(v) for every v in X}. The ultrametric inequality and nonnegative values make this an ideal. It contains a^ceil(kM), where M>=max(0,-min phi); when that exponent is zero it is the unit ideal. Units are excluded if phi is negative somewhere, so in that case it is proper primary. It is integrally closed: in an ideal-integrality equation for f violating one inequality, f^d has strictly smaller finite value than every other term. This is impossible. Passing first to R/support(v) justifies the least-term argument even at nonminimal supports. Noetherianity implies finite generation of each J_k, not of their graded algebra. J_k J_l is contained in J_{k+l}, and J_k^l in J_{kl}. Hence -v(J_{n!})/(n!) increases. Its supremum is p: approximate any C minorant by a rational single-ideal profile and subtract a small rational primary constant after synchronizing denominators. This produces an admissible profile arbitrarily close below that minorant. Its ideal lies in the corresponding J_k; sufficiently large factorials are divisible by k. The power inclusion gives domination by those factorial profiles. This proves only pointwise convergence.

D is closed under decreasing D-nets, by a new insertion argument rather than flattening nested nets. On a normal local domain let u=inf_i u_i for a decreasing D-net. If u is zero anywhere, every u_i is zero there and the granted normal zero dichotomy makes all terms zero. Otherwise choose a nonzero term u_i0. Upper semicontinuity and compactness give u_i0<=-c for a finite c>0, including bottom. Given any finite continuous F>u, compact increasing open covers {u_i<F} choose a tail index i>=i0 valid everywhere. From its ONE actual C-representation choose g<F and g<-c/2 everywhere, again by compact covers. Choose 0<b<1 with b||g||<min(F-g). Then q=(1-b)g lies in C and u<q<F. At finite points the gap above u is at least bc/2; at bottom it is automatic.

All strict C majorants of u are therefore downward directed: apply insertion to the continuous minimum of two majorants, a test which need not be psh. Their infimum is u. Indeed at x and finite t>u(x), choose finite u(x)<ell<t, a neighbourhood where u<ell, and a Urysohn cutoff supported there; interpolate ell near x with a global strict upper bound elsewhere. This gives F>u with F(x)<t, also at bottom. Insertion gives a C majorant below t at x. Reverse pointwise order now supplies ONE actual decreasing C-net to u. Finite products treat semilocal normal centres. Pulling a decreasing D-net on a general reduced germ to its finite normalization, applying this argument centrewise, and using G331 full-fibre D descent proves the assertion downstairs. No increasing or arbitrary nondirected-infimum closure follows. In particular, only AFTER this membership proof may the E350 energy criterion be applied to an existing decreasing finite-energy net.
