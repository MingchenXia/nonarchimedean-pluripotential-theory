# Exact result and conventions

Let $k$ be an **algebraically closed, trivially valued field of characteristic zero**, let $X$ be a **reduced, irreducible, geometrically unibranch projective $k$-variety**, and let $n=\dim X$. Retain an **ample line bundle $L$**. More generally the entire result holds for any **ample real numerical Cartier class** $\omega\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)\subset N^1(X)_{\mathbb R}$. In the line bundle case $\omega=c_1(L)$. Put
$$V=(\omega^n)_X>0.$$
The reference potential is $0$, corresponding to the trivial test configuration and, for a line bundle, its trivial metric. All potentials and constants use real coefficients. A psh potential may take the value $-\infty$ on a pluripolar set; “finite energy” means its energy is a finite real number, not that its values are everywhere finite.

**Theorem.** There is a genuine positive mixed finite-energy Monge–Ampère operator, extending the test configuration intersection measures, and in particular an operator
$$\operatorname{MA}^{\rm raw}_\omega:\mathcal E^1(X,\omega)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Radon}_+(X^{\rm an}),\qquad
\operatorname{MA}^{\rm raw}_\omega(\varphi)=(\omega+dd^c\varphi)^n.$$
Its mass is $V$. Write $\operatorname{MA}_\omega=V^{-1}\operatorname{MA}^{\rm raw}_\omega$. For a positive Radon measure $\nu$ of mass $V$, set $\mu=\nu/V$. Then
$$\operatorname{MA}^{\rm raw}_\omega(\varphi)=\nu,\quad\varphi\in\mathcal E^1(X,\omega)$$
is solvable if and only if
$$E^\vee_\omega(\mu):=\sup_{\psi\in\mathcal E^1(X,\omega)}\left(E_\omega(\psi)-\int\psi\,d\mu\right)<\infty.$$
Equivalently, every $\psi\in\mathcal E^1(X,\omega)$ is $\mu$-integrable. The solution is unique modulo one additive real constant; $\sup_{X^{\rm an}}\varphi=0$ selects a unique solution. No restriction on the support of $\nu$ is imposed beyond this finite-energy condition.

The normalized psh space is compact for pointwise convergence on divisorial valuations. The space $\mathcal E^1/\mathbb R$ is complete for the energy quasimetric $I$, and normalized $\mathcal E^1$ is complete for the strong topology. Mixed measures are continuous along decreasing finite-energy nets and under strong convergence. Envelopes are continuous for continuous obstacles. Energy is differentiable in both signs of every continuous direction after taking the psh envelope. Finite-energy comparison and domination hold.

For $n=0$, the hypotheses force $X=\operatorname{Spec}k$, $V=1$, potentials are constants, $E(c)=c$, and the theorem is immediate. All estimates containing $1/n$ below concern $n\geq1$.

This is a solution of the original singular/unibranch axis with its retained field and polarization hypotheses explicitly stated. It includes all singular normal and all nonnormal geometrically unibranch varieties in this domain. It asserts no equation theorem over arbitrary valuations or positive residue characteristic, in nonample classes, or on nonalgebraizable spaces. Multibranch singular varieties cannot all be included in this same surjectivity statement: an actual finite-energy nonsolvable target on a nodal curve is given below.

# The psh cone and the model reference

Write $\operatorname{PL}(X)$ for rational model functions and $\operatorname{PL}(X)_{\mathbb R}$ for their real span. For a test configuration $\mathcal X$ dominating $X\times\mathbb A^1$, a vertical real Cartier divisor $D$ defines $\varphi_D$. [BJ], Lemma 4.3, gives
$$\varphi_D\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\theta)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \theta_{\mathcal X}+D\text{ is relatively nef}.$$
Its Definition 4.1 defines $\operatorname{PSH}(X,\theta)$ as generically finite usc decreasing limits of generically finite Fubini–Study functions whose rational numerical classes tend to $\theta$. “Generically finite” here means not identically $-\infty$ on a connected component; irreducibility makes this a single condition.

For an ample class, Theorem 4.15(iii) improves the approximants to $\mathcal H_{\rm dom}(\theta)$, consisting of rational model functions with $\theta_{\mathcal X}+D$ relatively ample on a configuration dominating the trivial one. Corollary 12.18(iii) gives decreasing **sequences**. This countable regularization is valid without an envelope hypothesis: its proof uses Theorem 12.16, decreasing/strong continuity and already convergent monotone nets, not Theorem 12.8's surjectivity. For nef classes the approximating classes may be chosen ample and converging from above. Finite maxima, positive scaling, addition and pullback preserve psh functions in their corresponding classes. A psh function is determined on all of $X^{\rm an}$ by its values on $X^{\rm div}$; it is the minimal usc extension of those values ([BJ], Theorem 4.22).

These facts identify [W]'s global psh cone with [BJ]'s cone for the **trivial reference form** associated to an ample rational line bundle. [W], Definition 2.7, uses decreasing sequences of nef model functions in $\omega+\epsilon_i\theta$ with $\epsilon_i\downarrow0$. Proposition 2.10(2) permits the fixed ample class. Proposition 2.11 approximates nef models by relatively ample ones, uniformly, and diagonally gives decreasing Fubini–Study approximants. Conversely [BJ], Corollary 12.18, supplies the needed sequences. The model-nef criterion is exactly [BJ], Lemma 4.3, for the trivial reference. A uniform approximation can be made decreasing by choosing successive uniform errors summable and adding the corresponding decreasing tails of constants. Thus neither a net-versus-sequence change nor local psh terminology changes the global cone being enveloped.

[W], Section 2.1.2, explicitly interprets models over a trivially valued field as test configurations, or their completion over $k[[t]]$. They are not arbitrary models obtained by normalizing away a nonnormal generic fiber. [BJ]'s integrally closed configurations, described below, make this distinction precise. The envelope argument only needs the trivial reference, so no identification with an arbitrary semipositive nontrivial reference is assumed.

For clarity about [W], Theorem 6.5's first reduction: in the application needed here, choose $m$ with $mL$ very ample, embed $X\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^r$ by its complete linear system, and multiply the potential and the trivial reference form by $m$. The trivial metric on $mL$ is exactly the pullback of the trivial Fubini–Study metric on $\mathcal O(1)$: at the center of any semivaluation, some basis section is a unit and all others have nonnegative vanishing. Dividing by $m$ restores $L$. This verifies the reduction for our reference. It would be unjustified simply to identify an arbitrary nef model metric with this trivial embedding metric; that broader reduction is not used here.

# Envelope property, including the removal of countability

## The countable case and the actual local-to-global input

First suppose $k$ is countable. This trivially valued complete field admits itself as a countable dense subfield. For $\omega=c_1(L)$ with $L$ ample rational, let $(\varphi_i)$ be a nonempty bounded-above family in the identified global psh cone. A projective resolution $\pi:Y\to X$ exists in characteristic zero. Since $\pi^*\omega$ is nef and $Y$ is smooth, [BJ], Theorem 5.20, applies to this **nef**, possibly nonample, pullback. Consequently
$$\widetilde\varphi=\left(\sup_i\pi^*\varphi_i\right)^*\in\operatorname{PSH}(Y,\pi^*\omega).$$
Geometric unibranchness gives connected geometric fibers of $\pi$; [W]'s proof of Theorem 7.1 uses precisely this condition. Proposition 2.24 descends $\widetilde\varphi$ to an usc function $\varphi$ on $X^{\rm an}$ and identifies it as the minimal usc extension of its divisorial values. At divisorial points, which are unchanged under this birational morphism, [BJ], Theorem 5.6 (also [W], Theorem 2.16), says the supremum agrees with its usc regularization. Thus $\varphi=(\sup_i\varphi_i)^*$.

The content of Proposition 2.24 is stronger than connectedness of $X$: a pullback-class psh function is constant on each geometric analytic fiber. Over divisorial points of each stratum, the completed residue field is discrete or trivial, and the restriction is $0$-psh on a connected proper fiber, hence constant (with the possible identically $-\infty$ restriction treated as constant). Generic-flatness strata and openness of flat analytification extend this fiber constancy to every point using minimal usc extension from divisorial points. Properness then makes the descended function usc. The signs in Lemma 2.25's last argument should use $m\varphi$ when $\varphi\leq0$: if $\varphi$ vanishes somewhere but is negative at a divisorial point, $m\varphi\downarrow$ gives a generically finite psh function taking $-\infty$ at that point, a contradiction. The displayed $-m\varphi$ is unnecessary and has the wrong sign.

The descended $\varphi$ is weakly $\omega$-psh. Its pullback is globally psh, hence locally psh, so local potentials pulled back by $\pi$ satisfy the defining weak condition in [W], Definition 6.4. Theorem 6.5 identifies it with the global psh cone. Here is the actual proof mechanism in the reference used above. On the standard affine charts $Y_j=X\cap\{z_j\ne0\}$, put
$$\rho_j=\max_{0\leq\ell\leq r}\log|z_\ell|-\log|z_j|.$$
Then $u_j=\varphi+\rho_j$ is weakly psh with logarithmic growth. Theorem 6.3 makes $u_j$ a decreasing limit of affine Fubini–Study functions of controlled logarithmic growth. For rational $c>1$, Lemma 3.25(1) makes the extension by $-\infty$ of
$$u_j-c\rho_j=\varphi-(c-1)\rho_j$$
globally $c\omega$-psh. Their finite maximum is $\varphi$, since $\min_j\rho_j=0$ at every point. Letting $c\downarrow1$ and using closure with respect to the numerical class proves $\varphi\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega)$.

Theorem 6.3 is not merely a claim of descent from a resolution. Its supplied proof in Section 6.5 inducts on successive singular loci. The smooth locus is Proposition 6.2(2). Lemma 6.16 supplies local finite projections to affine space that are isomorphisms on the current smooth singular stratum; Proposition 6.14, proved using norms of weak psh functions under finite maps and Rossi's principle, advances the induction. Corollary 6.9 passes from the affine cover to a Runge sublevel domain. Applied to $\log|w|+u$ on $Y\times\mathbb A^1$, Proposition 5.7 yields $u=-\log d_D$ as a regularizable logarithmic-growth psh function. The construction of strict supporting functions in Section 6.1 is the step that needs the countable dense subfield. This is the relevant proof chain invoked in Theorem 6.5, not an assumption of multiplier ideals on singular $X$.

## An explicit check of Section 7's field reduction

Now allow $k$ to have any cardinality. It suffices to prove continuity of $P_\omega(f)$ for $f\in\operatorname{PL}(X)$, then use the $1$-Lipschitz property of $P$ and uniform density of PL in $C^0$. Choose a countable **algebraically closed** subfield $k_1\subset k$ containing the coefficients defining $X,L$, the trivial reference, and the finite test configuration/divisor data defining $f$. Write these data as $X_1,L_1,\omega_1,f_1$ and let $r_{21}$ or $r_{k1}$ denote the corresponding analytic restriction maps.

The descended $X_1$ is geometrically unibranch. Indeed reducedness and irreducibility descend faithfully; normalization over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field remains normal after a field extension and is still the finite birational normalization. If a geometric fiber had more than one branch, it would still have more than one after extending to $k$, contrary to the hypothesis. Residue-field inseparability in geometric unibranchness is unchanged by this test. Ampleness descends as well. Thus the countable case applies to $X_1$.

Put $u_1=P_{\omega_1}(f_1)\in\operatorname{CPSH}(X_1,\omega_1)$ and $u=r_{k1}^*u_1$. Pullback of decreasing Fubini–Study approximants proves that $u$ is globally psh, and $u\leq f$; hence $u\leq P_\omega(f)$.

The contact property needed for the reverse inequality follows from **the genuine energy**, without assuming a new local measure. For $0\leq t\leq1$ the obstacle $u_1+t(f_1-u_1)$ lies between $u_1$ and $f_1$, so its psh envelope is $u_1$. Theorem 8.5/Corollary 8.6 of [BJ], already valid on a general variety, give
$$\int(f_1-u_1)\,d\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_1}(u_1)=0.$$
The integrand is nonnegative continuous. Therefore this measure is supported on $\{f_1=u_1\}$.

For a competitor $\psi\in\mathcal H_{\rm dom}(\omega)$ with $\psi\leq f$, enlarge $k_1$ to a countable algebraically closed $k_2\subset k$ containing its finite model data. Relative ampleness and the model psh condition descend under this field extension. In correctly typed notation,
$$\psi=r_{k2}^*\psi_2,\qquad f_2=r_{21}^*f_1,\qquad u_2=r_{21}^*u_1.$$
Restriction maps for complete field extensions are surjective (extend the point's seminorm to the scalar extension), so $\psi_2\leq f_2$.

The genuine measures satisfy
$$r_{21*}\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_2}(u_2)=\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_1}(u_1).$$
This can be checked directly on test configurations: the relatively integrally closed models remain vertically $R_1,S_2$ under these regular field extensions; their vertical components are geometrically integral over the algebraically closed field, their multiplicities are preserved, their intersection numbers are unchanged, and their divisorial points restrict to the original points. Thus the formula is precisely the model intersection formula. Approximate $u_1$ by model psh functions, uniformly since it is continuous, and use decreasing continuity of the genuine mixed operator to obtain the displayed identity. All reference volumes are unchanged. This check works for nonnormal generic fibers as well, using vertical integral closure rather than absolute normalization.

Since the contact set is closed, positivity and this pushforward identity give full mass on $r_{21}^{-1}\{f_1=u_1\}=\{f_2=u_2\}$. Hence $\psi_2\leq u_2$ almost everywhere for $\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_2}(u_2)$. The general-variety domination principle [BJ], Corollary 10.6, gives $\psi_2\leq u_2$ everywhere, and therefore $\psi\leq u$. Taking the supremum over model competitors ([BJ], Lemma 5.19) proves
$$P_\omega(f)=r_{k1}^*P_{\omega_1}(f_1)\in C^0(X^{\rm an}).$$
This verifies the needed Section 7 cardinality removal. It avoids needing the smooth theorem [BFJ15] for the singular $k_2$-variety in the proof of [W], Theorem 4.16: the global competitors needed here satisfy [BJ], Corollary 10.6, which does not require smoothness or envelope surjectivity. Orthogonality was independently deduced from [BJ]'s general energy differentiation above. There is therefore no circular use of the desired singular equation theorem in this field reduction.

## Ample real numerical classes

For any $\omega\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)$, choose rational ample classes $\omega_j\to\omega$ with $\omega_j-\omega$ ample. Such classes exist by density of rational numerical Cartier classes and openness of the ample cone, perturbing $\omega+\epsilon_j A$ inside a ball smaller than the distance to the boundary. Each is represented by an ample rational line bundle. For any bounded-above family in $\operatorname{PSH}(\omega)$, its usc supremum is $\omega_j$-psh because $\operatorname{PSH}(\omega)\subset\operatorname{PSH}(\omega_j)$. Closure with respect to the class ([BJ], Theorem 4.5, or Lemma 5.9) makes it $\omega$-psh. Thus the result extends to **all ample real numerical classes**, without descending real coefficients to a countable field.

# Genuine energy and the finite-energy operator on nonnormal varieties

## Vertical integral closure and intersection energy

For a nonnormal $X$, a test configuration must retain its given generic fiber $X$. [BJ], Definition 1.10, uses integral closure **in that generic fiber**, not normalization in the entire function field: on an affine chart $A$ this is closure in $A\otimes_{k[t]}k(t)$. This closure is finite, equivariant, and unchanged generically. Such configurations form a cofinal class. Absolute normalization instead has generic fiber $X^\nu$ and is not silently substituted for a model of $X$.

By Theorem 1.13 these integrally closed configurations are regular at the generic points of the vertical components and vertically $S_2$. For a component $E$ of $\mathcal X_0$, define
$$b_E=\operatorname{ord}_E(t),\qquad
v_E=b_E^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_E|_{k(X)}.$$
These DVR valuations exist even when the generic fiber is nonnormal. Remark 1.22 identifies the vertical valuations with those on the normalization, while distinguishing the two generic fibers.

For $n+1$ pairs $(\theta_i,\varphi_i)$ with $\varphi_i=\varphi_{D_i}$, the model energy pairing is
$$\langle(\theta_0,\varphi_0),\ldots,(\theta_n,\varphi_n)\rangle
=\prod_{i=0}^n(\theta_{i,\overline{\mathcal X}}+D_i)$$
on the compactification $\overline{\mathcal X}\to\mathbb P^1$, evaluated on its fundamental cycle. Projection formula makes it independent of a dominating model. It is symmetric, multilinear, zero on entirely trivial potentials, and translating the $i$th potential by $c$ adds
$$c(\theta_0\cdots\widehat\theta_i\cdots\theta_n)_X.$$
For a PL test function $h$, Proposition 3.14 gives the decisive identity
$$\langle(0,h),(\theta_1,\varphi_1),\ldots,(\theta_n,\varphi_n)\rangle
=\sum_E b_E h(v_E)\big((\theta_{1,\mathcal X}+D_1)|_E\cdots(\theta_{n,\mathcal X}+D_n)|_E\big).$$
Nefness of the restricted model classes makes the weights nonnegative. Flatness equates the total vertical and generic intersection numbers. This is the actual model Monge–Ampère construction.

## Extension and normalization of energy

For ample $\omega_i$ and psh $\varphi_i$, [BJ], Theorem 7.1, extends the pairing by
$$\langle(\omega_0,\varphi_0),\ldots,(\omega_n,\varphi_n)\rangle
=\inf_{\substack{\psi_i\in\mathcal H_{\rm dom}(\omega_i)\\\psi_i\geq\varphi_i}}
\langle(\omega_0,\psi_0),\ldots,(\omega_n,\psi_n)\rangle.$$
The extension is increasing and upper semicontinuous, and continuous along decreasing nets. Monotonicity follows from positive model weights. For upper semicontinuity choose an approximating tuple with energy below a threshold; continuity of $\varphi\mapsto\sup(\varphi-\psi_i)$ ([BJ], Proposition 4.28) bounds all nearby potentials by $\psi_i+\epsilon/C$, and the translation rule bounds the nearby pairing. This is a general-variety proof.

Define
$$E_\omega(\varphi)=\frac{\langle(\omega,\varphi)^{n+1}\rangle}{(n+1)V},\qquad
\mathcal E^1(X,\omega)=\{\varphi\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega):E_\omega(\varphi)>-\infty\}.$$
Then $E_\omega(0)=0$, $E_\omega(\varphi+c)=E_\omega(\varphi)+c$ exactly. Energy is concave, increasing and weakly usc, and decreasing-continuous. The coefficient $(n+1)V$ is essential: with it the differential is the **probability** measure $\operatorname{MA}_\omega$, not the raw measure. Bounded psh functions have finite energy. The space is convex, stable under constants, and upward closed inside the psh cone.

Theorem 7.8(vi) proves finiteness of every mixed pairing of finite-energy psh functions, with possibly different ample classes. The key reduction subtracts constants to make the functions nonpositive, compares their ample classes by $\omega_i\leq t\omega_j$, and bounds the energy of their sum from below by a finite constant times the sum of their diagonal energies (Lemmas 7.9–7.10). No envelope or smoothness assumption is used in this step.

Let $\widetilde{\mathcal E}^1$ be the real vector space of differences of finite-energy psh functions, initially viewed on valuations of linear growth. Theorem 7.14 extends the pairing symmetrically and multilinearly to $(N^1(X)\times\widetilde{\mathcal E}^1)^{n+1}$, compatibly with models and decreasing limits. This does not replace the psh cone by a convenient inverse parametrization.

## Definition, positivity, integrability and continuity of the measures

For $\theta_i\in N^1(X)$ and $\varphi_i\in\widetilde{\mathcal E}^1$, define the mixed signed Radon measure by
$$\int h\,\bigwedge_{i=1}^n(\theta_i+dd^c\varphi_i)
=\langle(0,h),(\theta_1,\varphi_1),\ldots,(\theta_n,\varphi_n)\rangle,
\qquad h\in\operatorname{PL}(X)_{\mathbb R}.$$
This is [BJ], Theorem 7.18, with its exact normalization. In the nef-psh case, increasingness of the pairing gives a positive linear functional in $h$. Its value at $1$ is $M=(\theta_1\cdots\theta_n)_X$, so positivity bounds its absolute value by $M\|h\|_\infty$. Density of PL in $C^0$ and Riesz representation supply the unique positive Radon measure. Multilinearity gives the signed case. This proves existence and uniqueness of the operator as a measure-valued construction, independently of solving equations.

Proposition 7.19(i)–(ii) gives mass $M$ and **exactly** the model atomic formula above whenever the potentials are PL. Thus the resulting operator agrees with all model intersection measures and, by decreasing approximation, on all of $\mathcal E^1$. The diagonal raw operator has mass $V$; the normalized diagonal operator has mass one and $\operatorname{MA}_\omega(0)=\delta_{v_{\rm triv}}$ for irreducible $X$.

Proposition 7.19(v) says every function in $\widetilde{\mathcal E}^1$ is integrable against the mixed measure and that the testing identity remains valid. For a positive mixed measure this follows by decreasing model approximants of a finite-energy test potential: mixed pairing finiteness bounds its limiting integral, while usc monotone convergence identifies it. A difference of two finite-energy tests is then integrable. For signed measures use their multilinear expression as a finite combination of positive mixed measures. Different representatives of the same difference agree almost everywhere because sums of psh functions agreeing on divisorial valuations agree globally wherever finite. Positive mixed measures put no mass on the remaining pluripolar singularity sets (Corollary 7.49).

Decreasing continuity follows by testing against PL and using decreasing continuity of the pairing, then density and the fixed masses. Proposition 7.21 supplies the stronger weighted continuity: if $\varphi_{ij}\downarrow\varphi_i$, including an integrable test potential $\varphi_0$, then
$$\varphi_{0j}\bigwedge_{i=1}^n(\omega_i+dd^c\varphi_{ij})
\longrightarrow
\varphi_0\bigwedge_{i=1}^n(\omega_i+dd^c\varphi_i)$$
weakly as signed measures. The integral is the pairing with $(\omega_0,\varphi_0)$ **minus** the pairing with $(\omega_0,0)$; the reverse order displayed in the source proof of Proposition 7.21(ii) has the wrong sign. Both pairings converge, and the positive weighted-measure argument of Lemma 7.22 then proves the assertion. Hölder continuity of the pairing (Theorem 7.34), or Proposition 12.7 on the diagonal, also proves continuity for the strong topology. For mixed tuples use Theorem 7.34 after normalizing their suprema; the constants converge separately.

# Compactness, differentiation, comparison and uniqueness without smoothness

## Normalized potential compactness

The envelope property just proved invokes [BJ], Theorem 5.11, on $X$ itself. Here is its relevant proof. For
$$\operatorname{PSH}_{\sup}(\omega)=\{\varphi\in\operatorname{PSH}(\omega):\sup\varphi=0\},$$
values at each divisorial valuation lie in a bounded interval. Tychonoff compactness gives a subnet converging on all divisorial points. The usc tail suprema
$$\psi_i=\left(\sup_{j\geq i}\varphi_j\right)^*$$
are psh by the envelope property and decrease. Their divisorial values are the ordinary tail suprema by nonnegligibility of divisorial points. Their limit is not identically $-\infty$, has the desired divisorial values, and is psh by decreasing closure. Continuity of the supremum functional preserves normalization. This is compactness of the genuine global psh cone, not compactness of a fixed skeleton or of potentials on a resolution only.

## Two-sided differentiation

On all functions use the extended energy
$$E(g)=\sup\{E(\psi):\psi\in\operatorname{PSH}(\omega),\ \psi\leq g\}$$
and its upper extension $E^\downarrow(g)=\inf\{E(h):h\in C^0(X),h\geq g\}$. [BJ], Proposition 8.3(iv), gives $E^\downarrow(g)=E(P_\omega(g))$ for bounded-above usc $g$ when the envelope property holds, including $g=\varphi+tf$ with $\varphi\in\mathcal E^1$ and $f$ continuous. Constants ensure this envelope has finite energy.

Theorem 8.5 proves, for $f\in\operatorname{PL}(X)_{\mathbb R}$,
$$E^\downarrow(\varphi+\epsilon f)=E(\varphi)+\epsilon\int f\,d\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)+O(\epsilon^2),$$
uniformly in $\varphi\in\mathcal E^1$. Its proof starts with continuous psh $\varphi$ and writes $f=\psi_1-\psi_2$ for model psh functions in an auxiliary ample class $\omega'$. Lemmas 8.7–8.8 use determinant-of-cohomology volumes, valid in the general projective scope, and the bound $V_{\omega+\epsilon\omega'}/V_\omega-1=O(\epsilon)$. The mixed expansion
$$V_\omega^{-1}(\omega+dd^c\varphi+\epsilon(\omega'+dd^c\psi_1))^n
=\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)+O(\epsilon)$$
when tested against bounded $f$ uses positive mixed measures of fixed mass. Multiplying by $\epsilon$ yields the uniform quadratic remainder. Decreasing approximation extends the formula to every $\mathcal E^1$ potential. Apply this argument to $-f$ for the other sign of $\epsilon$.

For $f\in C^0$, approximate it uniformly by $f_m\in\operatorname{PL}_{\mathbb R}$, with $\|f-f_m\|_\infty\leq\eta_m\downarrow0$. For either sign of $t$,
$$|E^\downarrow(\varphi+tf)-E^\downarrow(\varphi+tf_m)|\leq |t|\eta_m.$$
The correct right derivative squeeze has lower endpoint $\int f_m\,d\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)-\eta_m$ and upper endpoint $\int f_m\,d\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)+\eta_m$. (The source's displayed preliminary comparison and upper endpoint in Corollary 8.6 contain extraneous $t$ and a minus sign, respectively.) Let $m\to\infty$, and repeat with $-f$. Consequently
$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}E(P_\omega(\varphi+tf))=\int f\,d\operatorname{MA}_\omega(\varphi).$$
No multiplier ideals on singular $X$ or differentiability of an inverse measure map is assumed.

## Comparison and the energy separation of potentials

The genuine operator satisfies [BJ], Theorem 7.40,
$$\mathbf1_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\max\{\varphi,\psi\})
=\mathbf1_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\varphi),\qquad\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal E^1.$$
For model functions the proof is Lemma 7.43 and the intersection formula: on a vertical component where one potential is strictly larger, the maximum divisor agrees with that divisor on every adjacent component, hence near that component and in its intersection weights. This uses vertical integral closure, not a smooth model assumption. Weighted decreasing continuity from Proposition 7.21 extends the equality to $\mathcal E^1$, testing with $(\varphi-\psi)_+=\max\{\varphi,\psi\}-\psi$. The finite-energy measures ignore pluripolar sets where such differences need representatives.

With $\chi_\epsilon=\max\{\varphi,\psi-\epsilon\}$ and $A_\epsilon=\{\varphi<\psi-\epsilon\}$, $B_\epsilon=\{\varphi>\psi-\epsilon\}$, mass one and locality give
$$1\geq\operatorname{MA}(\psi)(A_\epsilon)+\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)(B_\epsilon),$$
hence
$$\operatorname{MA}(\psi)(A_\epsilon)\leq\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)(\{\varphi\leq\psi-\epsilon\}).$$
Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ proves the correctly signed comparison
$$\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\psi)
\leq\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\varphi).$$

The following quantities have the [BJ] normalization:
$$J_\psi(\varphi)=E(\psi)-E(\varphi)+\int(\varphi-\psi)\,d\operatorname{MA}(\psi),$$
$$I(\varphi,\psi)=\int(\varphi-\psi)\,d(\operatorname{MA}(\psi)-\operatorname{MA}(\varphi))\geq0,$$
$$J(\varphi)=J_0(\varphi)=\sup\varphi-E(\varphi).$$
Mixed pairing algebra gives
$$E(\varphi)-E(\psi)=\frac1{(n+1)V}\sum_{j=0}^n\int(\varphi-\psi)(\omega+dd^c\varphi)^j\wedge(\omega+dd^c\psi)^{n-j},$$
$$\int(\varphi-\psi)\,d\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)
\leq E(\varphi)-E(\psi)\leq\int(\varphi-\psi)\,d\operatorname{MA}(\psi),$$
and $I/(n+1)\leq J_\psi(\varphi)\leq nI/(n+1)$ ([BJ], Proposition 7.27). Hodge negativity for vertical intersection pairings and Cauchy–Schwarz give the quasi-triangle inequality for $I$, strict energy concavity measured by $I$, and the quantitative estimates of Theorem 10.3; these are general-variety results, extended by finite-energy approximation.

In particular [BJ], Corollary 10.4, says on an equidimensional variety
$$\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)=\operatorname{MA}(\psi)
\Longleftrightarrow I(\varphi,\psi)=0
\Longleftrightarrow \varphi-\psi\text{ is locally constant}.$$
To verify separation in our irreducible case, normalize both suprema to zero. Theorem 10.3 with the finite-energy point masses $\delta_v$ and $\delta_{v_{\rm triv}}$ implies $\varphi(v)=\psi(v)$ whenever $I=0$, for every divisorial $v$. Divisorial points have linear growth and their Dirac masses have finite dual energy (Proposition 11.1). Theorem 4.22 then gives $\varphi=\psi$ on all $X^{\rm an}$. For unnormalized potentials this is one real constant.

The domination principle, Corollary 10.6, also follows without smoothness. If $\varphi\in\operatorname{PSH}$, $\psi\in\mathcal E^1$ and $\varphi\leq\psi$ almost everywhere for $\operatorname{MA}(\psi)$, replace $\varphi$ by $\rho=\max\{\varphi,\psi\}\in\mathcal E^1$. Then
$$I(\rho,\psi)=-\int(\rho-\psi)\,d\operatorname{MA}(\rho)\leq0.$$
Nonnegativity gives equality, separation makes $\rho-\psi$ constant, and its almost-everywhere zero value makes that constant zero. This is also the domination input used earlier in the countability removal.

# Finite-dual-energy measures and the equation

## The exact energy condition and its necessity

For a probability measure $\mu$, define $E^\vee(\mu)$ as in the theorem, allowing value $+\infty$. Proposition 9.4 shows the same supremum can be taken over $\mathcal H_{\rm dom}(\omega)$; therefore it is a convex weakly lower semicontinuous functional on Radon probabilities. If it is finite, every finite-energy potential is integrable. Such measures give zero mass to pluripolar sets. The energy condition is meaningful for arbitrary support; it is not a prescribed finite atomic probability vector.

Conversely, [BJ], Theorem 9.20, in its irreducible general-variety scope, proves that integrating all of $\mathcal E^1$ implies finite dual energy. Its argument can be stated with the correct signs. On normalized $\{J\leq C\}$ the values $-\int\varphi\,d\mu$ are uniformly bounded: otherwise a sequence with integrals at most $-2^j$ would make $\sum_j2^{-j}\varphi_j$ finite-energy by concavity and decreasing continuity but have integral $-\infty$. The estimates of Proposition 7.27 and Lemma 7.33 give
$$J(t\varphi)\leq C_n t^2J(\varphi),\qquad0\leq t\leq1,$$
by the mixed expansion of $I(t\varphi,0)$. Choosing $t=J(\varphi)^{-1/2}$ when $J(\varphi)\geq1$ bounds the rescaled $J$, and gives
$$-\int\varphi\,d\mu\leq A J(\varphi)^{1/2}+B.$$
Since $E(\varphi)=-J(\varphi)$ after normalization, the supremum of $AJ^{1/2}+B-J$ is finite. This verifies the equivalence, including the minus sign missing from displayed estimates in the source proof.

If $\mu=\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)$ with $\varphi\in\mathcal E^1$, the energy tangent inequality gives
$$E(\psi)-\int\psi\,d\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)
\leq E(\varphi)-\int\varphi\,d\operatorname{MA}(\varphi).$$
Thus
$$E^\vee(\operatorname{MA}(\varphi))=E(\varphi)-\int\varphi\,d\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)<\infty,$$
which proves necessity. It is invariant under adding constants because the normalized mass is one. Equivalently the raw objective is $VE(\psi)-\int\psi\,d\nu$ and its supremum is $V E^\vee(\nu/V)$.

## Maximizers solve the genuine equation

Let $\mu$ have finite dual energy and put
$$F_\mu(\varphi)=E(\varphi)-\int\varphi\,d\mu,\qquad
J_\mu(\varphi)=E^\vee(\mu)-F_\mu(\varphi).$$
A maximizing sequence exists by the definition of a finite real supremum, and may be taken in model potentials by Proposition 9.4; normalize it by $\sup\varphi_i=0$. Strict concavity measured by $I$ yields ([BJ], Lemma 9.15)
$$J(\varphi)\leq C_n\big(J_\mu(\varphi)+E^\vee(\mu)\big).$$
Indeed for $\tau=(\varphi+0)/2$, $F_\mu(\tau)$ exceeds $\tfrac12F_\mu(\varphi)+\tfrac12F_\mu(0)$ by a positive multiple of $I(\varphi,0)$, while it is at most $E^\vee(\mu)$, and $J(\varphi)\asymp I(\varphi,0)$. Therefore the maximizing sequence has eventually bounded $J$.

By genuine normalized psh compactness, a subnet converges weakly to $\varphi\in\operatorname{PSH}_{\sup}$. Lower semicontinuity of $J$ gives $J(\varphi)<\infty$, hence $\varphi\in\mathcal E^1$.

The nontrivial integration input is **Proposition 9.19**, not weak convergence of measures on continuous functions. For every $\mu\in\mathcal M^1$, weak psh convergence with eventually bounded $J$ implies convergence in $L^1(\mu)$. Its proof approximates $\mu$ by genuine model Monge–Ampère measures of a maximizing sequence $\rho_j$. This approximation uses Theorem 9.11, whose independent proof below applies to any maximizing net without compactness or attainment; it does not assume our final surjectivity conclusion. Lemma 9.17 bounds
$$\left|\int\psi\,d(\mu-\operatorname{MA}(\rho_j))\right|\leq C\,J_\mu(\rho_j)^{1/2}$$
uniformly on a fixed bounded-$J$ set. Integration against each model measure is continuous on PSH since it is a finite weighted sum of divisorial evaluations. Their uniform limit is therefore continuous on that set. To obtain $L^1$ convergence, apply this to $\varphi_i$ and $\max\{\varphi_i,\varphi\}$, whose $J$ is bounded by the quasi-triangle and maximum identities. The correct identity is
$$|\varphi_i-\varphi|=2\max\{\varphi_i,\varphi\}-\varphi_i-\varphi,$$
not the plus sign in the displayed rearrangement in the source proof. Consequently $\int\varphi_i\,d\mu\to\int\varphi\,d\mu$.

Upper semicontinuity of $E$ now gives
$$F_\mu(\varphi)\geq\limsup_iF_\mu(\varphi_i)=E^\vee(\mu).$$
The reverse inequality is the definition of dual energy, so $\varphi$ is a maximizer.

For completeness, the maximizer-to-equation step tests the **constructed** operator. The Legendre inequality ([BJ], Proposition 9.9) is
$$E^\downarrow(\varphi+\epsilon f)-\int(\varphi+\epsilon f)\,d\mu\leq E^\vee(\mu).$$
For a maximizing net with deficit $\delta_i$, uniform differentiation gives
$$\epsilon\int f\,d(\operatorname{MA}(\varphi_i)-\mu)\leq\delta_i+C_f\epsilon^2,\qquad\epsilon>0.$$
Divide by $\epsilon$, first let $i$ tend to the limit, then $\epsilon\downarrow0$, and repeat with $-f$. PL density yields $\operatorname{MA}(\varphi_i)\to\mu$. Applied to the constant maximizing net at $\varphi$, this proves $\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)=\mu$. Equivalently one may differentiate $F_\mu(P(\varphi+tf))$ in both signs; the comparison $P(\varphi+tf)\leq\varphi+tf$ gives the same Legendre inequality. The signs above imply equality, not just an inequality against the target. Uniqueness was already proved by Corollary 10.4. Multiplication by $V$ proves the raw theorem.

# Strong completeness and the abstract theorem's application

The weak topology on $\mathcal E^1$ is divisorial pointwise convergence. The strong topology refines it by continuity of $E$. On the quotient it is defined by $I$; on the full space use
$$\widehat I(\varphi,\psi)=I(\varphi,\psi)+|\sup\varphi-\sup\psi|.$$
Theorem 12.4 proves this characterization using Proposition 9.19 to pass integrals against $\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)$, and Theorem 10.3 with divisorial Dirac masses to recover weak convergence from $I$.

Define
$$I^\vee(\mu,\mu')=\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal E^1}\big(J_\mu(\rho)+J_{\mu'}(\rho)\big).$$
Theorems 10.2–10.3 make this a quasimetric and Lemma 10.7 proves
$$I^\vee(\operatorname{MA}(\varphi),\operatorname{MA}(\psi))\asymp I(\varphi,\psi).$$
Theorem 10.14 proves completeness of $\mathcal M^1$: a Cauchy net has a weak probability-measure cluster point; its eventually bounded dual energy puts that point in $\mathcal M^1$, and the integral estimate of Theorem 10.3 makes convergence uniform on bounded-$J$ test sets, hence strong. The proof uses no envelope property and no smoothness. Our surjectivity therefore makes $\operatorname{MA}:\mathcal E^1/\mathbb R\to\mathcal M^1$ a bi-Lipschitz isomorphism of complete spaces, proving $I$-completeness. Adding the Cauchy real suprema proves completeness for $\widehat I$.

These are precisely the forward implications of the actual general-variety Theorem 12.8. Its reverse implication can also be checked without mistakenly putting arbitrary non-finite-energy increasing nets into $\mathcal E^1$. For $f\in C^0$, take the increasing net of finite maxima of model psh competitors below $f$, including a constant lower bound. Its potentials are uniformly bounded and thus in $\mathcal E^1$. If $i\leq j$, mixed positivity in the energy difference formula gives
$$0\leq\int(\varphi_j-\varphi_i)\,d\operatorname{MA}(\varphi_i)
\leq(n+1)(E(\varphi_j)-E(\varphi_i)).$$
Thus $J_{\varphi_i}(\varphi_j)\leq n(E(\varphi_j)-E(\varphi_i))\to0$. Completeness supplies a psh limit. Its divisorial values are those of $P(f)$; comparison with the continuous obstacle and all competitors identifies it with $P(f)$. Lemma 5.19 makes this envelope lsc, hence continuous. Lemma 5.17 gives the envelope property. This confirms the equivalence relevant here and the correct energy signs, without importing the smoothness of Theorem 12.9.

# Normalization at the psh, energy and measure levels

Let $q:X^\nu\to X$ be the normalization in our domain. It is **finite birational of degree one**, and geometric unibranchness makes it a universal homeomorphism: each normalization fiber is one geometric point with a purely inseparable residue extension, which in characteristic zero is an equality. Finite type over a field ensures finiteness. Its analytification is a homeomorphism, since seminorms over these unchanged residue fields lift uniquely, and the continuous bijection is between compact Hausdorff spaces. This topological fact alone does not prove psh descent.

For the actual psh descent use [BJ], Lemma 5.13, with the ample classes $\omega_m=(1+1/m)\omega>\omega$. All have the envelope property by the earlier result, so
$$q^*:\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{PSH}(X^\nu,q^*\omega)$$
is a homeomorphism for divisorial convergence. Its proof uses Theorem 4.32: an upstairs potential plus a small pulled-back logarithmic ideal potential descends in the slightly larger downstairs class; increasing convergence supplied by the envelope property and divisorial determination remove the logarithmic correction. Thus this is global psh descent, not just descent of set-theoretic functions.

Projection formula and degree one give $(q^*\omega)^n=\omega^n=V$. The model energy pairings agree under pullback ([BJ], Proposition 3.14(v)); approximation extends the identity in Proposition 7.2 and Theorem 7.14. Consequently
$$E_{q^*\omega}(q^*\varphi)=E_\omega(\varphi),\qquad
q^*:\mathcal E^1(X,\omega)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal E^1(X^\nu,q^*\omega).$$
The reference $0$ is pulled back to $0$; no degree factor or reference correction is hidden. For mixed measures, Proposition 7.19(iii), equation (7.18), is
$$q_*\bigwedge_i(q^*\omega_i+dd^cq^*\varphi_i)
=\bigwedge_i(\omega_i+dd^c\varphi_i).$$
It follows either from model projection formula or by testing PL functions and using the energy identity; density makes it a measure identity. Because $q^{\rm an}$ is a homeomorphism, this is also the unique pullback identification of measures. For $\widetilde\mu=(q^{\rm an})^{-1}_*\mu$, psh surjectivity and equal energies/integrals show
$$E^\vee_{q^*\omega}(\widetilde\mu)=E^\vee_\omega(\mu).$$
The same identities preserve $I$, suprema, strong convergence and the equation. This is the full natural normalization descent in the exact finite degree-one setting; no analogous claim for arbitrary finite maps is being made.

# Nonsmooth examples and an actual multibranch obstruction

## A singular normal surface

Let
$$X=\{xy=z^2\}\subset\mathbb P^3_k,\qquad L=\mathcal O_X(1).$$
The polynomial is irreducible, so $X$ is an integral surface. The sole singular point is $[0:0:0:1]$. A hypersurface is Cohen–Macaulay, hence $S_2$, and this singular locus has codimension two, so $R_1$ holds. Thus $X$ is normal, and geometrically unibranch. It is singular and $L$ is ample with $V=2$. Every positive Radon $\nu$ of mass two satisfying $E^\vee(\nu/2)<\infty$ has a unique normalized finite-energy solution. For example any divisorial $v$ supplies the admissible target $2\delta_v$, since $\delta_v$ has finite dual energy. Neither smoothness nor a torus invariance assumption is needed.

## A nonnormal unibranch curve

Let
$$C=\{y^2z=x^3\}\subset\mathbb P^2_k,\qquad L=\mathcal O_C(1).$$
Its normalization is
$$q:\mathbb P^1_k\longrightarrow C,\qquad [u:v]\longmapsto[u^2v:u^3:v^3].$$
Near the cusp $[0:0:1]$ the ring is $k[t^2,t^3]\subset k[t]$; $t$ is integral and absent from the first ring, proving nonnormality. The rational inverse on a dense open is $t=y/x$, so the map is birational of degree one. It is finite, with one point in every geometric fiber and equal residue fields. Away from the cusp it is an isomorphism, and its cusp fiber is the single point $u=0$. Thus $C$ is geometrically unibranch, although singular and nonnormal. Here $q^*L=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(3)$ and $V=3$.

The theorem applies to every admissible target of mass three on $C^{\rm an}$. An explicit genuine solution for $3\delta_{\operatorname{ord}_{u=0}}$ is obtained upstairs as follows. Blow up $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1$ at $(u=0,t=0)$, with exceptional component $E$. On this smooth integrally closed test configuration take the model class
$$p^*\mathcal O(3)+3\mathcal X_0-3E.$$
Its degrees on the strict-transform component and $E$ are respectively zero and three. Both multiplicities are one; Proposition 7.19(ii) gives raw measure $3\delta_{\operatorname{ord}_{u=0}}$. Its potential is $3\max\{1-s,0\}$ on the ray $s\operatorname{ord}_{u=0}$ and three on every other ray. It is a bounded continuous model psh function. Normalization descent proved above makes it a continuous finite-energy psh function on the nonnormal curve, with precisely that genuine raw measure; subtracting three gives supremum zero.

## Why the same onto theorem fails on a nodal curve

Let $C$ instead be an irreducible **rational nodal** projective curve with normalization $q:\mathbb P^1\to C$, and let $p_1\ne p_2$ be the two preimages of the node. Fix $d=\deg L>0$. The divisorial valuation $v=\operatorname{ord}_{p_1}$ on the common function field defines a point of $C^{\rm div}$, and $\delta_v$ has finite dual energy by the general-variety Proposition 11.1.

On $\mathbb P^1$ use the blowup of $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1$ at $(p_1,0)$ and the model class
$$p^*(q^*L)+d\mathcal X_0-dE.$$
It is relatively nef with degrees zero and $d$ on the two components, both of multiplicity one. Hence its **genuine intersection** measure is $d\delta_v$. Its continuous psh potential $g$ is
$$g(s\operatorname{ord}_{p_1})=d\max\{1-s,0\},\qquad
g(s\operatorname{ord}_p)=d\quad(p\ne p_1).$$
In particular $g$ takes values zero at the endpoint corresponding to $p_1$ and $d$ at the endpoint corresponding to $p_2$.

If a downstairs $\varphi\in\mathcal E^1(C,c_1(L))$ had raw measure $d\delta_v$, its pullback would be psh, finite-energy and would have raw measure $d\delta_v$ upstairs. For this last statement use the actual identity (7.18), together with positivity and the fact that the analytic preimage of the divisorial point $v$ is a singleton: a valuation of the common function field has one lift to the normalization. Uniqueness on the smooth irreducible normalization then forces $q^*\varphi=g+c$. But a pullback from $C$ must take the same value at the two endpoints mapping to the node, whereas $g+c$ takes values differing by $d>0$. This is a contradiction.

Thus $d\delta_v$ is an actual finite-energy nonsolvable target for the genuine source operator on a multibranch curve. This is more than failure of an envelope proof strategy. It does not assert that no measure has a solution on that curve, or that no different theory could be invented. [BJ], Theorem 5.12, separately proves the necessary unibranch restriction on the envelope property; connectedness or irreducibility alone cannot replace it.

# Component limits and disposition of P16.1

The theorem is stated for irreducible $X$, so the uniqueness conclusion is exactly one constant. If an equidimensional variety has several irreducible components, the genuine operator has raw component mass $(\omega^n)_{X_\alpha}$ and zero mass on their intersections, as follows from the component intersection formula and absence of mass on pluripolar sets ([BJ], Corollaries 7.49 and 9.13). Equality of measures only gives the locally constant ambiguity of Corollary 10.4. For a disjoint union of geometrically unibranch components the equation separates, each component must have its prescribed mass and finite dual energy, and the constants are independent on connected components. Nothing here asserts one-constant uniqueness on disconnected $X$ or surjectivity on arbitrary connected reducible varieties. Lower-dimensional components require the fundamental-cycle conventions and receive no mass from the top-degree operator; they are outside our theorem.

Every smoothness-dependent input in the retained P16.1 domain has now been identified and replaced: the envelope on $X$ is supplied by [W]'s local-to-global theorem and its Section 7 field reduction; compactness is the general envelope-to-compactness proof of [BJ], Theorem 5.11; two-sided energy differentiation is [BJ], Theorem 8.5/Corollary 8.6 with the corrected signs and envelope identification; comparison and domination use vertical integral closure, finite-energy weighted continuity, and [BJ], Theorem 7.44/Corollary 10.6. The actual general-variety maximizing proof of Theorem 12.8 then gives existence; finite-energy energy separation gives uniqueness. The operator, energy, mass and integrability are those constructed in [BJ] before smoothness, including nonnormal generic fibers.

Consequently there is no indispensable unresolved premise in this source-based proof for the stated algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero, geometrically unibranch irreducible projective, ample real-class domain. The broader mathematical family remains outside this conclusion. The result and the nodal obstruction assess the unchanged original singular/unibranch axis with explicit retained hypotheses, rather than silently changing its target or importing smoothness through Theorem 12.9.
