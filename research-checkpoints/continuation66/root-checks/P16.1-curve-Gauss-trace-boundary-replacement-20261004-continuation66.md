# A curve boundary trace replacement for the uncovered H(s) application

Unreviewed root proof proposal, 2026-10-04. The completed round56 author
correctly leaves Wu6.12 Step3's use of Wu4.19 over arbitrary H(s) uncovered.
The following route targets only the boundary equality actually needed in
that step. It does not assert arbitrary-field affine FS regularization.
No active worker receives this note and no original closure is recorded.

Let F be a complete nontrivially valued equicharacteristic-zero field with
residue characteristic zero. Let q:D->C be finite surjective between smooth
algebraic curves, possibly with D disconnected, and let s be an F-rational
closed point of C. Let w be the pullback of the source's weakly psh function
to D: the birational lifting argument must establish its actual local psh
membership. Suppose v on C is already locally psh and its value away from
a finite exceptional set is the trace

    v(t)=sum_(x in q^-1(t)) dim_(H(t)) A_(x,q) w(x).

The required conclusion is the same equality at s. In Wu6.12, v is already
globally affine-FS regularizable by the valid original-ground-field smooth
extension, and the normalized preimage curve supplies w. The new curve
argument does not need FS regularizability of w.

First extend scalars to the completed algebraic closure Fhat. Pullback of
Wu-local psh functions follows by pulling their common-neighborhood rational
tropical PL approximants. Finite local algebra dimensions preserve the
trace under scalar extension, purely algebraically, for any fiber-constant
function; this does not require Wu6.11's regularizable-function hypothesis.
The finite closed-point fibers split over Fhat with ramification lengths.
It suffices to prove the split-fiber assertion. Smooth affine curves admit
smooth projective compactifications, so Wanner1801.04713v3 applies on their
open subsets over this complete algebraically closed nontrivial field.

On each sufficiently small neighborhood, the finite continuous tropical
convex PL approximants have positive CLD curvature by convex smoothing.
Wanner Theorem2/4.7 makes each such approximant Thuillier-subharmonic.
Thuillier Proposition3.1.8(ii) passes a decreasing sequence through this
class, allowing an identically-minus-infinity component if it arises after
restriction. Thus the actual local psh definition supplies the disk maximum
principle; the two cones are not equated by terminology alone.

For an Fhat-rational closed point x choose a local analytic disk coordinate
z vanishing at x and small radii R in |Fhat^*|. The closed disk B(x,R) lies
in one such neighborhood and has boundary consisting of its Gauss point
zeta_(x,R). A constant harmonic majorant with boundary value w(zeta_(x,R))
and Thuillier Definition3.1.5 give

    w(x)<=w(zeta_(x,R)).

If the boundary value is -infinity, apply finite constants tending to
-infinity instead; if the restriction is identically -infinity the equality
is immediate. Upper semicontinuity and zeta_(x,R)->x give

    lim_(R->0) w(zeta_(x,R))=w(x),

also when w(x)=-infinity. No assertion of an ordinary punctured-neighborhood
limit is made: subharmonic functions can have nearby logarithmic poles.
The same radial statement holds for v at s.

Write the finitely many points over s as x_i. In local coordinates on their
small disks, q has expansion t=c_i z_i^(e_i)(1+O(z_i)), c_i nonzero, where
e_i is the finite-fiber ramification length. After making the disks small,
the unit factor has constant norm one. The characteristic-zero-residue
assumption also allows an analytic e_i-th root of this unit, reducing the
map to t=c_i y_i^(e_i). For small r in |Fhat^*| the preimage of the target
Gauss point zeta_(s,r) consists of the source Gauss points

    zeta_(x_i, R_i), R_i=(r/|c_i|)^(1/e_i),

with local degrees e_i. Properness excludes other preimages once the target
disk is small; the local finite disk map and its degree need to be checked
with the actual analytic/fiber-algebra conventions. Divisibility of the
algebraically closed value group keeps the radii strict. All these Gauss
points have generic algebraic support and lie outside the finite exceptional
closed set. Therefore the known identity there reads

    v(zeta_(s,r))=sum_i e_i w(zeta_(x_i,R_i)).

Let r decrease to zero. Every summand has the radial limit just proved;
local upper bounds rule out any plus-infinity cancellation. The finite sum
therefore tends to sum_i e_i w(x_i), even if that value is -infinity.
The left side tends to v(s). Descent restores the original local fiber
dimensions, including residue-field degrees, and proves the desired trace
boundary value.

For a trivially valued F the above nontrivial-field disk argument is not
applied. That case is already covered by the repaired smooth affine base;
alternatively its branch rays require a separate trivial-field maximum
argument. H(s) in the troublesome example is nontrivial with residue
characteristic zero because the original trivially valued characteristic-zero
field embeds into its residue field.

This supplies a concrete replacement route for Wu6.12 Step3, rather than
attempting the stronger arbitrary-H(s) affine regularization theorem.
