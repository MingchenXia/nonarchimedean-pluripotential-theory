# Exact assertion, conventions, and premises {#sec:scope}

Let $N$ be a lattice and $V=N\otimes_\mathbb Z\mathbb R$. Let
$Q_1,\ldots,Q_m$ be distinct complete rational affine $d$-planes,
$d\geq1$. For $m\geq2$ assume
$$Q_i\cap Q_j=H\quad(i\ne j),\qquad H\text{ rational affine},\quad \dim H=k<d.$$
The one-page affine result below does not require an intersection. Give
$X=\bigcup_iQ_i$ the actual cycle $[X]=\sum_i c_i[Q_i]$, with arbitrary
fixed $c_i>0$ real. Every spatial carrier and refinement is finite and
rational. Translated tangent lattices are
$N_\sigma=N\cap\operatorname{span}_\mathbb R(\sigma-\sigma)$; quotient
normals are primitive and inward. The actual reference $\gamma$ is
finite continuous real PA on this rational geometry, printed PC, and has
strictly positive slope in every nonzero recession direction. It is
never replaced by a rounded reference.

A printed polyhedral convex combination (PCC) has rational endpoints
$x_j$, nonnegative real weights $\alpha_j$ of sum one, center
$x=\sum_j\alpha_jx_j$, and faces $\tau\preceq\sigma_j$ on a rational
carrier with $x\in\tau$, $x_j\in\sigma_j$. Zero-weight terms may be
discarded from a constructed PCC. A finite-valued function in
$\operatorname{PC}_q$ satisfies its Jensen inequality for every such
PCC. Centers need not be rational. Continuity is an explicit hypothesis
in our new criterion, not a consequence asserted for arbitrary
printed-PC functions.

$\operatorname{PA}_\mathbb Z$ has integral tangent slopes and rational
constants. $\operatorname{PA}_\mathbb R$ is the real scalar extension on
finite rational carriers. Lemma [11](#lem:scalar){reference-type="ref"
reference="lem:scalar"} proves that arbitrary real affine coefficients
satisfying the rational matching equations belong to this genuine
extension. Irrational locations of maximum walls do not automatically
belong. The actual envelope is
$$P_\gamma(u)=\sup\{q\in\operatorname{PC}_q:q\leq\gamma+u,\ q\leq\gamma+C_q\text{ globally for some real }C_q\},\quad u\in C_b(X).$$
Competitors may be discontinuous and are unrestricted by either PA
coefficient convention. The real completion
$\mathcal D_{\mathbb R}(\gamma)$ is the global uniform closure of
genuine relative $\operatorname{PA}_\mathbb R\cap\operatorname{PC}_q$
functions, inside continuous functions of bounded difference from this
same $\gamma$. We construct decreasing approximants explicitly, rather
than defining continuous-PC functions into the domain.

::: {#thm:main .theorem}
**Theorem 1** (Universal common-$H$ real regularization). *Under these
hypotheses every $f\in C(X)\cap\operatorname{PC}_q$ with
$\|f-\gamma\|_\infty<\infty$ admits genuine finite rational-spatial real
PA PC functions $P_n$ globally uniformly decreasing to $f$. Each has the
actual page recession of $\gamma$ and one common uniform relative bound.
In particular every actual $P_\gamma(u)$, $u\in C_b(X)$, belongs to
$\mathcal D_{\mathbb R}(\gamma)$ before the completed raw operator is
applied.*
:::

# Elementary finite geometry and separation {#sec:foundations}

Ordinary real completeness, finite-dimensional linear algebra,
compactness of closed bounded sets, and the elementary properties of
convex secants are the background foundations. Here we provide the
additional finite convex and polyhedral arguments used, without an
unavailable source theorem.

::: {#lem:density .lemma}
**Lemma 2** (Feasible rational density). *Every point of a nonempty
rational polyhedron is a limit of rational feasible points in its
minimal face. A continuous printed-PC function satisfies every
real-endpoint PCC with the same rational face-incidence constraints.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* At a given point collect the rational equations and currently
active inequalities as equalities. Gaussian elimination gives a rational
particular solution and a rational basis of the homogeneous solution
space. Approximate its real coordinates by rational ones. The finitely
many inactive inequalities have strictly positive slack and remain
satisfied. This proves density, including boundary and lower-dimensional
faces.

For the PCC claim discard zero weights, write each endpoint cell as
$A_jx_j\geq b_j$ with equations included, and put $t_j=\alpha_jx_j$. The
constraints
$$\alpha_j\geq0,\quad\sum\alpha_j=1,\quad A_jt_j\geq b_j\alpha_j,\quad\sum t_j\in\tau$$
are rational linear constraints on $(\alpha_j,t_j)$. Rational feasible
approximants have positive $\alpha_j$ eventually. Their endpoints
$t_j/\alpha_j$ and centers $\sum t_j$ are rational and stay in their
assigned cells and center face. Apply the printed inequalities and take
limits by continuity. No new carrier or coarsening is used. ◻
:::

::: {#lem:hull .lemma}
**Lemma 3** (Finite hull and cone elimination). *In a real space of
dimension $a$, the convex hull of a compact set $K$ is compact. A cone
generated by a finite list is closed. A pointed rational polyhedron is
the hull of finitely many rational vertices plus its finitely generated
rational recession cone. Analogous statements with real generators hold
for real polyhedra.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* A convex combination with more than $a+1$ positive coefficients
has an affine dependency $\sum b_jz_j=0$, $\sum b_j=0$. It has both
coefficient signs. Subtract $t b_j$ from the coefficients, where
$t=\min_{b_j>0}\alpha_j/b_j$. Nonnegativity, total coefficient and
represented point are preserved, and at least one coefficient vanishes.
Iteration leaves at most $a+1$ points. The hull is therefore the image
of $K^{a+1}$ times the compact standard simplex under the continuous sum
map, allowing repetition. This proves compactness, rather than assuming
that a compact set's hull is closed.

For a conic representation eliminate a nonzero dependency among active
columns in the same way, after choosing its sign to have a positive
entry. The surviving columns are independent. On any independent subset
its inverse column map bounds all coefficients by a constant times the
represented vector's norm. There are finitely many subsets. A convergent
sequence thus has a subsequence represented on one subset with bounded
convergent coefficients. The limit belongs to the cone.

For finite inequalities, lineality is their joint homogeneous kernel and
has a rational basis in the rational case. In the pointed quotient the
sum of the defining nonnegative linear forms is strictly positive on
nonzero cone vectors. Its section at one is bounded: otherwise
normalized vectors have a unit limit with zero sum, hence in the removed
kernel. A bounded polyhedron is its vertex hull. Indeed from a point
that is not a vertex move in both directions in the kernel of its active
constraints to endpoints where more independent constraints are active.
Boundedness supplies the endpoints. Recursing terminates in at most the
dimension many increases of active rank. Only finitely many active
subsets occur, so there are finitely many vertices; rational full-rank
systems give rational vertices.

Apply cone generation to the homogenized inequalities $(x,t)$, $t\geq0$.
For a pointed polyhedron this cone is pointed; its positive-$t$
generators normalize to vertices, and its zero-$t$ generators give
recession rays. At $t=1$ their coefficients yield a vertex convex
combination plus a recession combination. This proves the stated
decomposition. For real inequalities the same rank and compactness
arguments apply over $\mathbb R$. Pure cone representations and linear
images of finite cones/hulls are again finitely generated. They are also
finite polyhedra: eliminate representation variables from their finite
inequalities by pairing the upper and lower bounds on each variable
(Fourier--Motzkin elimination). Positive, negative and zero coefficients
give a finite list at each step, including equality elimination. This
proves the needed finite-inequality description without a polyhedral
duality citation. ◻
:::

::: {#lem:support .lemma}
**Lemma 4** (Nearest-point separation and supporting planes). *A point
$z$ outside a nonempty closed convex set $C$ has a nearest point $w$ and
the inward normal $n=w-z$ satisfies
$$n\cdot(c-z)\geq\|n\|^2>0\quad(c\in C).$$ A finite continuous convex
function on an entire real vector space has an affine support at every
point and equals its biconjugate.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* A minimizing sequence for distance may be restricted to a
closed bounded ball, so compactness attains its minimum. Differentiate
the squared distance along the segment from $w$ to $c$:
$n\cdot(c-w)\geq0$, which gives the displayed strict separation.

For $F$ and $z_\epsilon=(x,F(x)-\epsilon)$, its closed convex epigraph
has distance at most $\epsilon$; nearest points converge to $(x,F(x))$.
Take a convergent subsequence of inward unit normals. Its limit
$(a,\beta)$ is a unit supporting normal at this graph point and
$\beta\geq0$, because the epigraph is upward closed. If $\beta=0$,
projection of the epigraph onto the whole space forces
$a\cdot(y-x)\geq0$ for every $y$, hence $a=0$, contradicting unit
length. Dividing by $\beta>0$ gives an affine minorant touching at $x$.
For $F^*(p)=\sup_y(p(y)-F(y))$, that support gives $F^*(p)=p(x)-F(x)$.
Thus $F^{**}(x)\geq F(x)$; the defining inequality gives the reverse
bound. ◻
:::

::: {#lem:derivative .lemma}
**Lemma 5** (Full convex local Lipschitzness and derivatives). *A finite
convex function on an entire finite-dimensional real space is locally
Lipschitz and continuous. At a point $y$ its directional derivative
$$h(w)=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{F(y+tw)-F(y)}t$$ is finite continuous
sublinear. Its subdifferential $K=\partial F(y)$ is nonempty compact
convex and $h(w)=\max_{\ell\in K}\ell(w)$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* On a cube around $y$, convexity bounds the function above by
the maximum of its finitely many vertex values. Reflect a point of the
half-sized cube through $y$ into the larger cube; the midpoint
inequality gives a finite lower bound. On a smaller ball the absolute
values are therefore bounded by a finite $M$. For two points in a
concentric ball of half the radius, extend the ray from the first
through the second by at least a fixed radius inside the larger ball.
Convex secants bound the positive difference quotient by $2M$ divided by
that radius. Reverse the points for the negative bound. This is local
Lipschitzness and implies continuity; no continuity theorem for printed
PC is being used.

Secant monotonicity gives the derivative limit; the Lipschitz bound
gives $|h(w)|\leq L\|w\|$ and $|h(v)-h(w)|\leq L\|v-w\|$. Rescaling
proves positive homogeneity. The inequality
$F(y+t(v+w))\leq\tfrac12F(y+2tv)+\tfrac12F(y+2tw)$ proves subadditivity
after subtraction and passage to the limit. Thus $h$ is continuous
convex sublinear.

Apply Lemma [4](#lem:support){reference-type="ref"
reference="lem:support"} to $h$ at any $w$. If its touching affine
support is $\ell(z)+b$, testing $z=0$ gives $b\leq0$; testing $z=2w$ and
using equality at $w$ gives $b\geq0$. Hence $b=0$, $\ell\leq h$, and
$\ell(w)=h(w)$. Forms below $h$ are exactly subgradients of $F$ at $y$:
convex secants give $h(v)\leq F(y+v)-F(y)$; the opposite implication
follows by differentiation of a support of $F$. Both signs of the norm
bound make these forms bounded. Their defining inequalities make the set
closed convex. It is nonempty by the support just constructed and
compact by finite-dimensional compactness. This also proves its exact
maximum representation in every direction. ◻
:::

::: {#lem:boundary .lemma}
**Lemma 6** (Continuity on the whole compact polytope). *A finite lower
semicontinuous convex function $a$ on a compact polytope $K$ is
continuous on all of $K$, including every boundary point, in its
relative topology. The polytope need not be rational.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Its upper bound is the largest of the finitely many vertex
values, by the vertex-hull representation. Fix $x\in K$ and work in its
affine span. Present $K$ by finitely many inequalities. Choose a
positive radius smaller than every inactive inequality's slack at $x$
divided by its normal's norm. For nearby $z\in K$, the direction
$v=(z-x)/\|z-x\|$ satisfies each active inequality homogeneously.
Consequently $x+rv\in K$ for a single fixed sufficiently small $r>0$.
With $t=\|z-x\|/r$,
$$a(z)\leq(1-t)a(x)+t\max_{\mathrm{vertices}\ v}a(v).$$ This proves
upper semicontinuity at $x$. Lower semicontinuity proves the other
limiting inequality. The singleton case is immediate. Compactness then
gives uniform continuity. ◻
:::

::: {#lem:biconj .lemma}
**Lemma 7** (Closed compact-domain biconjugacy). *Extend a finite lower
semicontinuous convex $a$ on a nonempty compact convex $K$ by $+\infty$
outside $K$. Then $a=a^{**}$ on the entire ambient space. In particular
the assertion includes its boundary.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The epigraph is closed, convex, nonempty and upward closed. For
$q\in K$ and $t<a(q)$, strictly separate $(q,t)$ by
Lemma [4](#lem:support){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:support"}.
The normal $(A,\beta)$ has $\beta\geq0$. Evaluating the strict
separation at $(q,a(q))$ gives $\beta(a(q)-t)\geq\|n\|^2>0$, so
*strictly* $\beta>0$. Thus
$$a(s)\geq t+\|n\|^2/\beta-A\cdot(s-q)/\beta\quad(s\in K).$$ This affine
minorant has value greater than $t$ at $q$. It yields $a^{**}(q)>t$, and
letting $t\uparrow a(q)$ gives equality with the elementary bound
$a^{**}\leq a$. If $q\notin K$, spatial nearest-point separation gives a
linear $u$ with $u(q)>\max_Ku$. Since $\min_Ka$ is finite,
$$(tu)(q)-a^*(tu)\geq t(u(q)-\max_Ku)+\min_Ka\longrightarrow+\infty.$$
This proves equality outside the compact domain as well. A separator
with possibly zero vertical component is insufficient for the
within-domain argument; the displayed same-fiber test establishes
positivity. ◻
:::

# Continuous PC on dependent complete pages {#sec:criterion}

Translate a rational point of $H$ to zero and write $L_i=Q_i-Q_i$, $H$
now linear, and $E=\sum_iL_i$. Its rational lattice is saturated in $N$
and it is the actual ambient space used henceforth.

::: {#thm:criterion .theorem}
**Theorem 8** (Common ambient support criterion). *For a continuous real
function $f$ on this book, printed PC is equivalent to ordinary
convexity of every full-page $f_i=f|_{L_i}$ and the following condition:
for every real $y\in H$ there is one $p_y\in E^*$ satisfying
$$f_i(x)\geq f_H(y)+p_y(x-y)\quad(x\in L_i,\ \text{all }i).                 \tag{CS}$$
Agreement of convex traces alone is insufficient for dependent pages.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Fix an inherited rational carrier, refined to include $H$ if
needed. Each convex cell is contained in one page: its relative interior
cannot lie in a finite union of proper affine subspaces of its own
affine hull. For example the product of nonzero affine equations for
those subspaces is a nonzero polynomial and cannot vanish on an open
set, as follows by repeated one-variable polynomial uniqueness. Thus
each page receives an inherited rational subdivision; we do not discard
its walls.

At any $x\in L_i$ let $\tau$ be its minimal center face. There is a
neighborhood meeting only cells containing $\tau$, because all other
finitely many closed cells avoid $x$. For all sufficiently small
$u\in L_i$, choose assigned page cells $\sigma_+,\sigma_-$ containing
$x+u,x-u$. The constraints
$$z\in\tau,\quad z+v\in\sigma_+,\quad z-v\in\sigma_-,\quad z,v\in L_i$$
form a rational feasible polyhedron.
Lemma [2](#lem:density){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:density"}
approximates $(x,u)$ by rational feasible midpoint triples. Their Jensen
inequalities and continuity give the local midpoint inequality at $x$
with the original incidences.

To get entire-page convexity restrict to any real segment, subtract its
chord and call the resulting continuous function $q$ on $[0,1]$. If
$q(t_0)>0$ internally, choose $\epsilon>0$ sufficiently small that
$q(t)+\epsilon t^2$ has a maximum at some interior $t_*$. At nearby
symmetric arguments the local midpoint inequality for $q$ implies
$$\tfrac12[(q+\epsilon t^2)(t_*+s)+(q+\epsilon t^2)(t_*-s)]
 \geq(q+\epsilon t^2)(t_*)+\epsilon s^2,$$ contradicting maximality.
Thus $q\leq0$ on every segment. This argument covers arbitrary inherited
faces, including those in $H$, and never coarsens to a rational box.

At $y\in H$, Lemma [5](#lem:derivative){reference-type="ref"
reference="lem:derivative"} supplies compact convex page
subdifferentials $K_i=\partial f_i(y)$ and finite continuous sublinear
$h_i(w)=\max_{K_i}\ell(w)$. Put $K=\prod_iK_i$ and
$S=\operatorname{image}(E^*\to\prod_iL_i^*)$. The image is a closed
linear subspace by finite-dimensional linear algebra, even when the
pages overlap tangentially. If $K\cap S=\varnothing$, minimize
$\|\operatorname{pr}_{S^\perp}z\|^2$ over compact $K$. Let $z_0$
minimize and $a=\operatorname{pr}_{S^\perp}z_0\ne0$. Differentiating on
$[z_0,z]$ gives $$\langle a,z\rangle\geq\|a\|^2\quad(z\in K).$$ Identify
the product dual with $\prod_iL_i$ using auxiliary inner products, and
let $(w_i)$ represent $-a$. Orthogonality to $S$ means $\sum_iw_i=0$ in
$E$. The product maximum is
$$\sum_ih_i(w_i)=\max_{(\ell_i)\in K}\sum_i\ell_i(w_i)
 \leq-\|a\|^2<0.$$ For sufficiently small $t>0$, derivative convergence
makes $\sum_i[f_i(y+tw_i)-f_H(y)]<0$. These endpoints have equal-weight
barycenter $y$. They lie in cells incident to its minimal face once $t$
is small. At real $y$ and real endpoints the perspective density proof
in Lemma [2](#lem:density){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:density"}
supplies exactly the same legal real-endpoint inequality. It forbids
this negative cost. Hence $K$ meets $S$, and an ambient preimage gives
(CS). Compact-product separation does not assume that restriction
coordinates are independent; their entire compatibility is encoded by
$S$.

Conversely, outside $H$ a center has a neighborhood in its unique page.
Every incident endpoint cell is in that page: its segment to the center
has an initial interval in the page; a cell contained in another page
cannot have that interval. Ordinary Jensen applies. At a center
$y\in H$, use the same $p_y$ in (CS) at every endpoint, assigning
endpoints in $H$ to any page. Multiplying and summing cancels
$p_y(\sum\alpha_j(x_j-y))$. This proves every printed PCC on every
rational refinement.
Diagnostic [8](#sec:diagnostic){reference-type="ref"
reference="sec:diagnostic"} verifies the final insufficiency
assertion. ◻
:::

# Entire full-affine primal foundation {#sec:primal}

This section independently reconstructs the old289 full-affine
reasoning, including exact finitely many contact samples. It is not an
application of an unreviewed label.

Let $\gamma$ be convex finite real PA on a rational carrier of a
complete rational affine space $L$, translated rationally to zero. Its
affine top-cell expressions are global supports: on an interior point an
affine derivative equals its convex supporting slope, and convex secants
propagate it across the full space. Therefore
$$\gamma(x)=\max_j(a_j(x)+b_j),\qquad h(v)=\max_ja_j(v),\qquad\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{a_j\}.$$
All $a_j,b_j$ are the actual real data. The finitely many constants give
a global bound on $\gamma-h$. On each recession cone of its rational
carrier, $h$ equals the linear part of that cell. The cones cover $L$:
along $tv$, $t\to\infty$, a finite carrier has a cell containing an
unbounded subsequence; dividing its defining inequalities by $t$ puts
$v$ in its recession cone. Taking its base point and its ray shows that
the cell slope equals $h(v)$ by the bounded difference. Strict growth
gives $h(v)>0$ for $v\ne0$, uniformly positive on the unit sphere. Thus
$\Delta$ is a compact full-dimensional polytope containing zero in its
interior. Otherwise separation supplies a nonzero direction on which its
support function is nonpositive. Every carrying cell is pointed: a
lineality $v$ would give $h(v)=a(v)$ and $h(-v)=-a(v)$, inconsistent
with both being positive.

Choose the finite rational ray generators of these recession cones using
Lemma [3](#lem:hull){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:hull"}. They
give the exact identity
$$C_h:=\operatorname{epi}h=\operatorname{cone}\{(r,h(r)),(0,1):r\text{ in the finite ray list}\}.            \tag{RC}$$
On each cone, conic spatial combinations lift with equality because $h$
is linear there. Convexity and homogeneity of $h$ prove the converse
inclusion. This is a real polyhedral epigraph with rational *spatial*
generators; irrational heights are allowed.

::: {#lem:dual .lemma}
**Lemma 9** (Actual whole dual domain). *For continuous convex $f$ with
$|f-\gamma|$ globally bounded, $\phi=f^*$ has domain exactly $\Delta$
and is finite continuous on the entire actual polytope $\Delta$,
including its boundary. Moreover $f(x+v)\leq f(x)+h(v)$, and $f$ has
exact recession $h$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Choose $C$ with $|f-h|\leq C$. For $p\in\Delta$,
$p(x)\leq h(x)$ gives $\phi(p)\leq C$; testing $x=0$ gives
$\phi(p)\geq-f(0)$. For $p\notin\Delta$, strict separation gives $v$
with $p(v)>h(v)$, so $p(tv)-f(tv)\to+\infty$. Thus the domain is exactly
$\Delta$. A supremum of continuous affine functions is convex lower
semicontinuous, and Lemma [6](#lem:boundary){reference-type="ref"
reference="lem:boundary"} gives whole-boundary continuity, even for an
irrational polytope. Lemma [4](#lem:support){reference-type="ref"
reference="lem:support"} gives $f=\phi^*$. Since $p(v)\leq h(v)$ on
$\Delta$, this formula gives $f(x+v)\leq f(x)+h(v)$. Bounded difference
and homogeneity imply the actual recession limit along every $x+tv$ is
$h(v)$. One can also derive the recession inequality directly: an excess
positive secant would force all subsequent secants above $h(v)$,
contradicting bounded difference. ◻
:::

::: {#thm:primal .theorem}
**Theorem 10** (Global rational-spatial primal majorants). *For every
$\eta>0$ and every finite prescribed rational sample set $B\subset L$,
there is a convex $F_A\in\operatorname{PA}_\mathbb R$ on a finite
rational carrier, with exact recession $h$, such that
$$f\leq F_A\leq f+\eta\text{ on the whole }L,\qquad F_A(b)=f(b)\quad(b\in B).$$*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Continuity gives $\phi(p)=\sup_{x\in L_\mathbb Q}(p(x)-f(x))$.
At each $p\in\Delta$ choose a rational $x$ achieving error less than
$\eta/2$. Continuity of $\phi$ makes its affine sample achieve error
less than $\eta$ on a neighborhood in $\Delta$. Compactness selects
finitely many samples $A$ covering *all* of $\Delta$; append $B$ and one
sample if necessary. Thus
$$\phi-\eta\leq g_A(p):=\max_{a\in A}(p(a)-f(a))\leq\phi\quad(p\in\Delta).$$
Form
$$E_A=\operatorname{conv}\{(a,f(a)):a\in A\}+C_h.                                      \tag{PH}$$
Its first term is compact and its cone is closed by
Lemma [3](#lem:hull){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:hull"}; a
convergent sum has a convergent compact component and hence a cone
limit, proving closedness. Its projection is the whole $L$. Convexity of
$f$ and its recession inequality put $E_A\subset\operatorname{epi}f$.
Every vertical fiber is a closed nonempty upward ray bounded below by
$f$, so it has a finite minimum. Call the lower boundary $F_A$. It is
finite convex, hence continuous by
Lemma [5](#lem:derivative){reference-type="ref"
reference="lem:derivative"}. Direct maximization of $p(x)-t$ on (PH)
gives $F_A^*=g_A$ on $\Delta$; outside $\Delta$ a recession vector makes
it infinite. Biconjugacy of finite continuous convex functions reverses
the preceding bounds to give the stated global error. Each prescribed
lift belongs to $E_A$ while $F_A\geq f$, proving exact sample values.
The bounded global error implies unchanged actual recession and bounded
difference from the original $\gamma$.

Lemma [3](#lem:hull){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:hull"} also
proves that (PH) has finitely many real affine inequalities and faces.
Because its projection is all $L$, a nontrivial defining inequality
cannot be purely spatial. Its lower facets therefore have positive
vertical coefficient, and $F_A$ is the maximum of finitely many real
affine forms. A lower face is generated by precisely the sampled
vertices and cone generators on which its support attains equality; the
upward ray is excluded. Its projection is a hull of rational positions
plus a cone of rational directions, hence a rational polyhedron by
rational elimination. Projection on a graph face is affine and
injective. Intersections of graph faces project to common faces; face
inclusions and intersections are preserved by this affine isomorphism.
The finitely many projected lower faces and all their faces cover $L$
and form a genuine rational spatial complex. No irrational coefficient
wall is declared rational. Lemma [11](#lem:scalar){reference-type="ref"
reference="lem:scalar"} now gives primitive real scalar membership. ◻
:::

::: {#lem:scalar .lemma}
**Lemma 11** (Rational matching gives real scalar membership). *A
continuous real piecewise affine function on a finite rational carrier
belongs to the real scalar extension of literal PA on that carrier.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Use rational affine origins and rational tangent bases on each
cell. Equality of affine restrictions on shared faces is tested at
finitely many rational affine basis points, giving a finite rational
homogeneous linear system in the slopes and constants. Its real kernel
has a rational basis by Gaussian elimination. Express the actual
coefficient vector as a finite real combination of that basis. Each
basis vector describes a continuous rational-affine function. A common
positive integer multiple clears its finitely many slope denominators in
the saturated cell bases, giving integral tangent slopes and rational
constants; division by that integer is absorbed into its real scalar
coefficient. This proves the assertion without rounding any actual
slope. ◻
:::

# The joint dual and simultaneous boundary fibers {#sec:joint}

Let $f$ satisfy Theorem [1](#thm:main){reference-type="ref"
reference="thm:main"}. Theorem [8](#thm:criterion){reference-type="ref"
reference="thm:criterion"} gives page convexity and common supports.

compact polytope, possibly irrational; $\phi$ is finite continuous

::: proof
*Proof.* Each $\Delta_i$ has finitely many real inequalities, so $D$
does too. It is closed. If it were unbounded, normalized unbounded $p_n$
have a unit subsequential limit $p$. Bounded restrictions imply $r_ip=0$
for every $i$. Since $E=\sum_iL_i$, this forces $p=0$, a contradiction.

$$\phi_i(r_ip_y)=p_y(y)-f_H(y),$$ so its restrictions lie in every

of $D$ from rationality of the spatial geometry.

extends $f$ elsewhere on $X$.

its minimum. Mixing minimizers proves convexity. For $q_n\to q$ take
minimizers on a subsequence realizing the lower limit; compactness gives

reference="lem:boundary"} gives entire boundary continuity. Directly

conjugate has domain exactly $\Delta_H$ by

consequently $T(D)=\Delta_H$, including every boundary fiber. In
dimension zero these are the same compact scalar identities. ◻

# Simultaneous trace lowering with preserved supports {#sec:lowering}

::: {#thm:extension .theorem}
**Theorem 14** (Entire trace extension). *Let $f_H\leq g\leq f_H+\eta$
be any finite convex function on $H$ with the exact trace recession,
$\eta>0$. There are finite continuous convex functions $F_i$ on the
entire pages with $$f_i\leq F_i\leq f_i+\eta,\qquad F_i|_H=g,$$ exact
page recession $h_i$, and at every $y\in H$ one common ambient form
supporting all $F_i$. These intermediate functions need not be PA and
receive no MA.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Let $b=g^*$. Conjugating the two bounds and applying
Lemmas [9](#lem:dual){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:dual"} and
[6](#lem:boundary){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:boundary"} gives
its entire domain $\Delta_H$, whole continuity there, and
$$k-\eta\leq b\leq k.$$ For each page use the continuous compact image
$$S_i=\{(r_ip,b(Tp)):p\in M\},\quad
 E_i=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\operatorname{graph}\phi_i\cup S_i\bigr)+\mathbb R_{\geq0}(0,1).$$
The graph and $S_i$ are compact.
Lemma [3](#lem:hull){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:hull"}'s finite
affine-dependence elimination proves their hull compact, so adding the
closed vertical ray gives a closed set. It is convex, upward closed and
projects onto all $\Delta_i$. The source heights are bounded below and
include the graph of $\phi_i$, so its fibers have finite attained lower
values $\psi_i$. This lower boundary is closed convex and finite on the
whole $\Delta_i$; whole continuity follows from
Lemma [6](#lem:boundary){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:boundary"}.

At a graph source the height is $\phi_i$; at a source from $M$, (SM)
gives $\phi_i(r_ip)=k(Tp)$ and $b(Tp)\geq\phi_i(r_ip)-\eta$. Convexity
of $\operatorname{epi}(\phi_i-\eta)$ therefore gives
$$\phi_i-\eta\leq\psi_i\leq\phi_i.$$ Every graph source also has height
$\phi_i(a)\geq k(U_ia)\geq b(U_ia)$, and each added source has equality
with $b$. Since $b\circ U_i$ is convex on $\Delta_i$, all of $E_i$ lies
above its epigraph lower bound. Thus $\psi_i\geq b\circ U_i$. For every
$q$ choose one joint minimizer $p\in M$ over it. The added source yields
$$\psi_i(r_ip)=b(q)\quad\text{for all }i,\qquad
 \min_{U_ia=q}\psi_i(a)=b(q).                                          \tag{LF}$$
These are attained identities, including boundary $q$.

Set $F_i=\psi_i^*$. Compact dual domains with bounded values make these
finite convex functions; Lemma [5](#lem:derivative){reference-type="ref"
reference="lem:derivative"} gives continuity. Conjugating the bounds
gives the claimed global error. The exact dual domain and bounded values
give recession support function $h_i$. Grouping the supremum by the
attained fibers in (LF) gives $F_i|_H=b^*=g$ by finite convex
biconjugacy.

For any real $y\in H$, continuity of $b$ on compact $\Delta_H$ attains
$g(y)=q(y)-b(q)$. Choose *one* joint $p\in M$ with $Tp=q$. By (LF), for
every page and every $x\in L_i$,
$$F_i(x)\geq (r_ip)(x)-\psi_i(r_ip)=p(x)-b(q)=g(y)+p(x-y).$$ The same
ambient $p$ supports all pages at $y$.
Theorem [8](#thm:criterion){reference-type="ref"
reference="thm:criterion"} proves PC of their continuous gluing. The
construction allows arbitrary continuous nonseparable convex trace data
and uses the one shared $H$ throughout. ◻

# Genuine raw cycles, completion, and crossing contact {#sec:raw}

For a balanced real cycle $c$ and a PA factor $a$ on a common rational
carrier use
$$D_ac(\tau)=\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}c(\sigma)a_\sigma(n_{\sigma/\tau})
 -a_\tau\left(\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}c(\sigma)n_{\sigma/\tau}\right).       \tag{RAW}$$
Linear parts occur, with the tangent correction shown. No normalization
is appended to $D_a^d[X]$.

Each complete page cycle is balanced on every rational refinement. At a
facet in a selected page its two top cells have the same weight and
opposite primitive quotient classes in the same saturated page lattice.
Their sum is tangent to the facet. At facets lying in $H$ this
cancellation occurs separately in each page that has top cells there.
Lower-dimensional overlaps cannot become top cells. Summing the actual
positive weights therefore gives a positive balanced cycle on $X$.
Subtracting one complete positive page leaves the explicitly effective
balanced residual $\sum_{i\ne j}c_i[L_i]$. Support inclusion alone would
not prove this residual condition.

The new PA approximants have ambient local supports at every point:
ordinary full-page supports away from $H$ and preserved common ambient
supports on $H$. On a relative interior point of any face $\tau$, its
support has the same derivative as $a_\tau$ in both tangent directions.
Subtract this form $p$ in (RAW). Balancing cancels its tangent
correction and leaves
$$D_ac(\tau)=\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}c(\sigma)(a_\sigma-p)(n_{\sigma/\tau})\geq0$$
for *every* nonnegative balanced local cycle, in every dimension and on
every common carrying refinement. Tangent changes of a normal lift
cancel because the derivatives agree on $\tau$. This proves genuine
primitive-sector positivity, not merely an abstract convex surrogate.

For the remaining finite recursion, the whole S262/S272 raw foundations
are available. We also checked their immediate primary proof chain and
give the complete calibration. Let $a_\sigma$ be the Euclidean covolume
of the saturated tangent lattice, $a_v=1$, and $\nu_{\sigma/\tau}$ the
inward unit conormal. A facet lattice basis followed by its primitive
complement gives perpendicular height $a_\sigma/a_\tau$, so
$$\operatorname{pr}_{N_{\tau,\mathbb R}^\perp}n_{\sigma/\tau}
   =(a_\sigma/a_\tau)\nu_{\sigma/\tau}.$$ Weights $a_\sigma c(\sigma)$
have zero Euclidean conormal sum. Embed $x$ as $(x,1)$ to satisfy BBGS's
hyperplane convention; all tangents retain their original metric.
Subtract an ambient extension of the face derivative. Tangent agreement
and balancing then give the exact negative-unit-normal BBGS product
conversion $$B_{-f}(ac)(\tau)=a_\tau D_fc(\tau).$$ It holds at every
recursive dimension, including signed or zero weights, ending with
covolume one at vertices.

In the complete BBGS subdivision proof, inherited facets keep their
data, a new facet inside an old affine weighted cell has opposite equal
contributions, and strata in old higher-dimensional cells inherit zero.
For commutation at a codimension-two face, each top-cell quotient wedge
has exactly two boundary rays $u,v$. Their inward unit perpendiculars
are $a u+b v$ and $a v+b u$, by reflection in the bisector. Expanding
the double product gives the symmetric diagonal sum $a f(u)g(u)$ and
cross terms $b f(u)g(v)$; interchanging the two boundary facets pairs
the cross terms. This proves commutation, also for nonsimplicial cells.
Pairing conormal sums with every ambient linear form characterizes
balancing. Subdivision and commutation then preserve balance. The
source's printed Proposition 3.19 commutation citation to 3.12 uses the
actual equality in 3.15. This is a numbering correction, not an extra
premise. The conversion proves the same finite raw properties for real
coefficients and real weights. No AR10 delegated proof or Alexandrov
measure is substituted.

On a finite balanced one-cycle, bounded PA $b,c$ have symmetric
bounded-edge contribution $Z(e)(b(v)-b(w))(c(w)-c(v))/L_e$ to
$\int bD_cZ$, where $L_e$ is primitive lattice length. Unbounded affine
bounded functions have zero slope. Thus bounded IBP holds and
$\deg D_bZ=0$. Replacing mixed relative factors by $\gamma$ one at a
time proves exact raw mass $\deg\gamma$. These finite calculations
precede all completions.

Theorem [1](#thm:main){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:main"} now
establishes domain membership. Whole S272 may therefore be applied: on

and $J_0=\int H_0\operatorname{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(\gamma)$.
Every completed tuple in each fixed family $\max_i\|f_i-\gamma\|\leq C$

$$\int H_0\operatorname{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(\mathbf f)\leq(2d/\epsilon)CM+J_0,
 \qquad\operatorname{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(\mathbf f)\{H_0>T\}\leq((2d/\epsilon)CM+J_0)/T.$$
The map is globally uniformly continuous for vague compact-test
