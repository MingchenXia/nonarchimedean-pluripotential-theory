# Primary-model numerical nefness and uniform ideal approximation

ROOT working proof, unreviewed. No caller grant, original closure or accepted-paper count follows from this document. This is not fed to either active frozen native campaign231/232. The conclusion below is a model-geometric comparison; the full normalization image and its relation to other singular analytic notions remain distinct questions.

Let (R,m) be a reduced positive-dimensional excellent Noetherian local ring. Nilpotents are removed before this construction. For an m-primary ideal a, let V_a(R) be the compact section of centered real semivaluations with v(a)=1. A centered semivaluation has support p strictly smaller than m, since v(m) is finite. An ideal value is the minimum over a finite generating set. If m^r is contained in a and a is contained in m^s, then s v(m)<=v(a)<=r v(m). Consequently the value of every primary ideal is a finite continuous bounded positive function on V_a. The topology is pointwise convergence with compact extended-real coordinate intervals.

Admissible models here are the actual blowups Y_I=Bl_I(Spec R) of primary ideals I. They are reduced and projective and are isomorphisms off the closed point: the Rees algebra is a subring of the reduced ring R[t], and I is the unit ideal off m. The tautological ideal I O(Y_I)=O(-E_I) is invertible, effective and relatively ample. We use the usual precise geometric meanings of proper coherence, relative ampleness, relative global generation for high powers of an ample line bundle, and nefness on contracted proper integral curves. In particular, nef plus an ample positive real perturbation is ample in the finite-dimensional numerical space, and ampleness is open in a fixed finite-dimensional real Cartier space. These are ordinary algebraic-geometric background inputs with projective Noetherian base hypotheses, not source claims extending BFJ to singular rings.

The model system is directed. On Y_(IJ), the product of the pulled-back ideals I and J is invertible. Each factor is invertible. Locally, write their product as the regular principal ideal (f), and put J'=f^(-1)J in the total quotient ring. Then IJ'=A. A finite expression 1=sum x_j y_j with x_j in I and y_j in J' gives a dual basis for I, because every y_j I is contained in A. Thus I is a finite projective module; its rank is one on every generic component, hence locally one. The same holds for J. These ideals contain a regular element, because the primary ideals of the reduced positive-dimensional local ring avoid all its finitely many minimal primes. The universal property of the blowup supplies the two domination maps. Pullback of an exceptional Cartier divisor is legitimate: the maps dominate every generic component, which lies outside the exceptional support. Numerical nefness is preserved under these maps, since the degree on a contracted integral curve is either zero when its image is a point or its positive generic degree times the degree on the image curve.

An integral exceptional Cartier divisor F is supplied with its canonical trivialization away from the closed fiber. For every such F on Y_I, there is N with -N E_I<=F<=N E_I in actual Cartier-divisor order. Indeed O(F)+O_Y and O(-F)+O_Y are coherent fractional subsheaves. Their quotients by O_Y are supported on the closed fiber. A sufficiently large power of I annihilates each quotient, by Noetherianity and a finite affine cover. Since I O_Y=O(-E_I), this gives the two asserted sheaf inclusions and divisor inequalities. Thus E_I is an order unit on every fixed finite-dimensional exceptional Cartier space. For real divisors, Cartier order is the real effective order; rational approximation after a strictly negative E_I perturbation is controlled by these integral order bounds.

Define f_F(v) geometrically. First quotient by the support p of v. Its valuation field is Frac(R/p), with a real valuation ring. The generic map to Y_I is uniquely defined off the closed point. Properness extends it uniquely to the valuation ring. Pull back the line bundle O(F), retaining its canonical generic trivialization. It is a rank-one lattice in the valuation field. Its generator has a finite value, and set f_F to the negative of that value. This is the usual Cartier value: for an effective E with O(-E) generated by e, f_E(v)=v(e). Changing a local generator multiplies it by a unit of the valuation ring and changes no value. No infinity-minus-infinity expression is used. The order bounds give |f_F(v)|<=N v(I). Values are additive in divisors, homogeneous in v, independent of further determination, and nonpositive for a negative divisor. Define real Cartier values by finite real linear combinations.

Continuity follows from an exact ideal expression, which also exposes the nonnormal correction. Write T=H0(Y_I,O_Y). Proper coherence makes T a finite R-module and an R-algebra. The injection R->T is an isomorphism off m, since the model is. Hence T/R is killed by some primary ideal c, and cT is contained in R. If T=R, choose any primary c. This uses the intermediate algebra of this actual model; it does not claim that the full normalization of R is an isomorphism off m.

Suppose L is a rational negative exceptional Cartier divisor with O(L) relatively ample. For sufficiently divisible large k, O(kL) is globally generated relative to the affine base. Its section module F_k=H0(Y_I,O(kL)) is contained in T. Put J_k=cF_k, a finite ideal of R. It is the unit ideal off m. It is proper: c is contained in m, and every maximal ideal of the finite integral R-algebra T lies over m, so cT contains no unit from R. It follows that J_k is primary. Its pullback generating ideal is exactly

    J_k O_Y = (c O_Y) O(kL).

At the valuation-ring center, global generation means that among the coefficient sections generating O(kL) at least one is a unit. Taking minimum values therefore gives the exact identity

    v(J_k)=v(c)-k f_L(v),
    -(1/k)v(J_k)=f_L(v)-(1/k)v(c).

The product c O_Y need not be invertible; only its finite ideal value is used. This is not the false assertion that the uncorrected F_k is an ideal of R, and not an assertion that J_k pulls back to O(kL) without the conductor factor.

For a general integral exceptional F, choose N large enough that L=F-N E_I is negative and relatively ample. Choose one permissible k in the preceding construction. Then, exactly,

    f_F(v)=-(1/k)v(J_k)+N v(I)+(1/k)v(c).

All three primary ideal values on the right are finite continuous bounded functions. This proves continuity and boundedness of every geometric exceptional Cartier value, without assuming a topology on the lifted centers or deriving continuity from a formal ratio of infinities. It also proves that its evaluation agrees with its pullback value on every dominating model. Real linear combinations retain these properties.

Now let D<=0 be a real exceptional Cartier divisor on Y_I which is numerically nef over Spec R. Given eta>0, choose delta>0 small enough that delta ||v(I)||_infinity<eta/3. The real divisor D-delta E_I is relatively ample. Rationally approximate it in a fixed finite-dimensional exceptional Cartier space, retaining relative ampleness and changing it by at most (delta/2)E_I in Cartier order and by less than eta/3 in uniform value. This is possible by the preceding order-unit bounds, the continuity of evaluation in its finitely many coefficients and openness of ampleness. Denote the rational result by D'. In particular

    D'<=D-(delta/2)E_I<=0.

For sufficiently divisible large k, apply the corrected section construction to D'. It gives the primary ideal J_k=cH0(Y_I,O(kD')) with

    g_k(v):=-(1/k)v(J_k)=f_D'(v)-(1/k)v(c)<=f_D(v).

Choose k so ||v(c)||_infinity/k<eta/3. The three errors give ||f_D-g_k||_infinity<eta. Thus every geometric numerical-nef model function is uniformly approximated by primary ideal functions, from below, on the entire compact section, including semivaluations with nonminimal support. Neither a resolution nor normality, pure dimension, residue-field closure or characteristic zero entered this comparison.

Conversely -(1/k)v(J) for primary J is the value of the negative real Cartier divisor -(1/k)E_J on Y_J. Its line bundle is globally generated in the rational sense, hence relatively nef. The uniform closure C_a of these primary ideal functions therefore equals the uniform closure of the geometrically defined nonpositive numerical-nef exceptional Cartier model functions. Allowing all real positive scales does not change this equality. Zero is in the closure by sending the scale of any fixed primary ideal to zero. Products and sums of ideals, with common rational scales and uniform approximation of real coefficients, show that C_a is a closed convex cone stable under finite maximum. This is a proved geometric comparison, rather than defining numerical nefness to mean an ideal closure.

The result is a UNIFORM closure theorem. It does not prove decreasing primary-ideal approximation from above on a singular link. On a multibranch curve a mixed-zero profile is a uniform limit of strictly negative primary profiles, but cannot be their decreasing limit. In particular an approximation from below cannot be relabeled decreasing regularization. One may subsequently define a geometric singular class by nontrivial decreasing nets in C_a; that is a specified additional completion, not an extra theorem about all raw weak b-divisor limits.

Primary gauges cause no change to the intrinsic homogeneous model construction. For H_(a,b)(v)=v/v(b), the exact value rule is

    f_D,b(H_(a,b)(v))=f_D,a(v)/v(b).

Both v(b) and its reciprocal are bounded on V_a by the primary inclusions. Thus this map preserves uniform closure and pointwise decreasing nets, and obeys the exact multiplicative cocycle. It is independent of generators and determines the same homogeneous function on each centered ray.

The smooth complex specialization can be stated precisely using the actual BFJ source. BFJ's formal psh class is defined using a nef WEIL divisor, strong continuity on every closed dual-complex face, and the canonical all-semivaluation extension of Definitions2.5/2.8. It is not the class of arbitrary nef traces on one model. Proposition3.13 gives a decreasing SEQUENCE of primary ideal functions for every formal psh function, on ALL of V, and its proof uses Theorems3.9/3.10. For a formal psh function continuous on compact V, Dini gives uniform convergence of that sequence. Conversely, a uniform limit of primary formal psh functions has a weak nef Weil divisor; Proposition3.11, convexity on simplices and equality on the rational divisorial points identify it with the corresponding formal psh function. The bounded uniform closure above is consequently the CONTINUOUS BFJ class in the smooth complex setting, not every bounded usc formal psh function by a change of terminology.

There is also a precise route for the decreasing completion in that setting. Let a decreasing net in this continuous class have a non-identically-minus-infinity limit g. Proposition3.14 and the order bound g_i(v)<=g_i(v_m) show that the values at v_m stay finite below; otherwise every point would tend to minus infinity. The Izumi bound makes the limit finite at every quasimonomial point. Its weak divisorial limit is nef. On each rational closed dual-complex face its pointwise limit is finite and convex; at the relative interior of each minimal rational face it is continuous. Equality on dense rational divisorial points of that face identifies it with the continuous function supplied by Proposition3.11, including all lower-dimensional faces. Finally each formal g_i satisfies g_i(v)=inf_pi g_i(r_pi(v)). The two infima commute, so g(v)=inf_pi g(r_pi(v)); this is the exact canonical extension from Definition2.8. Together with Proposition3.13 this identifies the nontrivial decreasing completion with the actual BFJ formal psh class, on every semivaluation, in its smooth complex hypotheses. The identically-minus-infinity element may be adjoined separately. No Izumi/dual-complex/resolution assertion is thereby made for an arbitrary excellent singular ring.

For normalization, downward models give a safe direction. On each local normalization branch above m, the blowup of the extended primary ideal maps to the downstairs primary blowup by its universal property. Pullback of a nonpositive numerical-nef Cartier divisor remains such a divisor: its degree on each contracted curve is zero or a positive degree times a downstairs nonnegative degree. The pulled-back value is exactly f_D(w|R). Uniform closure and decreasing completion retain this pullback rule. All such functions are constant on the fibers of the known compact normalized-boundary quotient. On the finite-conductor stratum these fibers are singletons; on its infinite-value stratum equality of restrictions to the conductor quotient is the existing boundary identification rule. Thus the construction does not discard that stratum or replace its quotient by a separated branch set.

The reverse geometric assertion is not proved here: a fiber-constant normalization-side geometric function need not descend as a nef Cartier divisor or be uniformly approximable by downstairs nef data merely because its underlying continuous function descends. In particular the normalization conductor can have infinite value, and dividing its value by k does not uniformly vanish there. The primary correction above works because T/R for a primary model is supported only at m; it cannot silently be reused for the whole normalization. A full image theorem or model/conductor gluing argument remains a gate. This is also related to the separate original P12.6 geometric descent target.

The nodal curve illustrates the distinction from radial analytic psh. On the two branches of R=k[[x,y]]/(xy), x^A+y^B is a regular primary element with orders(A,B). On the m-normalized section, taking A_N=max(1,floor(N alpha)) and B_N=max(1,floor(N beta)) and scaling by1/N gives every profile(-alpha,-beta), alpha,beta>=0, as a uniform limit. For another primary gauge with branch orders d_1,d_2, use floor(N alpha d_1), floor(N beta d_2) instead, retaining the lower bound one. Thus C_a contains a mixed-zero profile with the exact gauge scaling. The corresponding separated finite model has no contracted integral curves, so this is consistent with numerical nefness. It does not claim that this profile has a classical/CLD logarithmic radial extension on a conductor-controlled closed-disc node: that established extension theorem excludes mixed-zero profiles. The two notions have different explicit comparison domains. The smooth one-branch curve unit test has no such distinction.
