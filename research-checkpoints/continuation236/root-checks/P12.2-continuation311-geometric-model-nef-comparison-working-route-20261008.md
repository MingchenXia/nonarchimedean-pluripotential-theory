# Unreviewed working route for original P12.2

This is ROOT mathematical research DATA, not an accepted theorem or a supporting grant. The original target concerns an independently geometric psh cone on the normalized boundary of an excellent singular or nonnormal local germ, its smooth specialization, its primary-gauge rule and its relation to the ideal-generated cone. A definition of an order completion alone is insufficient. P12.3--P12.6 remain separate targets. Every step below needs proof or rejection; this note prescribes no solver strategy.

Let R be an excellent reduced positive-dimensional Noetherian local ring and let a be primary. Consider projective models obtained from primary-ideal blowups, with no assumption of resolution, normality, pure dimension, characteristic zero or an algebraically closed residue field. A possible genuinely geometric starting object is an exceptional real Cartier divisor D, trivialized off the closed fiber, with D<=0 and O(D) relatively nef in the sense of nonnegative degree on every contracted integral proper curve. This condition uses actual algebraic geometry rather than an ideal function as its definition. Values on centered semivaluations must be well-defined, including semivaluations with nonminimal support. Values are homogeneous on rays; their restriction to v(a)=1 supplies a normalized function.

The promising comparison is a relative-ample perturbation argument. After moving to a model admitting an effective exceptional Cartier E with O(-E) relatively ample, D-epsilon E should be relatively ample. A sufficiently small rational perturbation should preserve effectivity and ampleness. For a rational divisor D', some O(kD') is relatively globally generated over the affine base. Its pushforward should be a genuine downstairs primary ideal I with I O(Y)=O(kD'), so its valuative function is -(1/k)v(I), with the sign checked against the convention D'<=0. If epsilon v(E) is uniformly bounded on the primary section, this proves uniform ideal approximation of the geometric model function. Conversely, a globally generated negative divisor from a primary ideal is relatively nef. A complete comparison of the appropriate completions would then be more than an unproved identification of two definitions.

There is a possible repair of the nonnormal pushforward gate, rather than assuming that pushforward lies in R. Put T=H^0(Y,O_Y). For a projective birational model which is an isomorphism off the closed point, proper coherence suggests that T is a finite R-algebra with T/R supported at the closed point. Choose a primary ideal c annihilating T/R; then cT is contained in R. If F_k=H^0(Y,O(kD')) is contained in T and generates O(kD'), the product cF_k is a downstairs ideal I_k. Its pulled-back generating ideal is (cO_Y) O(kD'), so its value should be v(I_k)=v(c)-k v(D') on every centered semivaluation. Consequently -(1/k)v(I_k) differs from the geometric model function v(D') by only -(1/k)v(c), uniformly bounded on any primary section. Off the closed point this ideal is the unit ideal, and c may force it to be proper, so it should be primary. This does not give the initially proposed exact pullback equality, but may suffice for uniform approximation. Prove every premise, including semivaluation evaluation and properness, instead of declaring nonnormal descent exact. If T=R, c may simply be any primary ideal. The correction is independent of a model resolution and may retain arbitrary characteristic.

Several essential interfaces are unresolved in this note:

1. Prove the chosen model system is directed/cofinal for the geometric objects actually used. The blowup of a product of primary ideals is a candidate common domination, but individual invertibility from invertibility of the product must be justified over reduced rings with several components. A claimed relatively ample exceptional divisor on an arbitrary projective modification needs proof; resolution or smooth-source models cannot silently be imported in positive characteristic.
2. Define the exceptional divisor and its valuation at a semivaluation with kernel. Ratios with two infinite values do not define a value. Proper valuative lifting after quotienting by the support, and bounds by a pulled-back primary ideal, may provide the correct definition. Well-definedness, independence of determination and continuity are substantive gates.
3. Justify the uniform divisor bounds. A model dominating the a-blowup has an invertible primary pullback. Coherent quotients supported on the closed fiber suggest bounds -N E_a <= D <= N E_a. This should make all values finite and bounded on v(a)=1, but the scheme-theoretic argument must handle nonnormal models and local trivializations.
4. Relatively nef plus relatively ample is relatively ample, rational approximation and relative global generation are ordinary algebraic-geometric inputs only with their exact hypotheses. Show that pushforward sits inside R, is primary, has no lost components and pulls back to exactly the prescribed line bundle. Nonnormality can make an unjustified ideal/divisor identification false.
5. Specify the topology and completion being compared. Uniform closure of bounded model functions, decreasing nets, decreasing sequences, weak b-divisor limits and usc extension from divisorial points are different constructions. The general ring may have no resolution or usable dual complexes. A proof must identify the actual ideal-generated cone being compared, establish its geometric meaning, and prove the exact smooth BFJ specialization rather than cite a superficial analogy.
6. Gauge changes act on homogeneous functions by positive rescaling along each ray. Prove their exact cocycle and that changing a does not change the intrinsic geometric cone. For normalization and conductor descent, distinguish functions defined by downstairs models from independent branchwise nef data; function descent and descent of a line bundle/divisor need not be equivalent.

The curve test is especially informative. For a reduced nodal local curve, the primary blowup separates its two branches and contracted integral curves are absent. Exceptional Cartier coefficients (0,-1) can therefore be numerically nef on the separated model, whereas the known classical/CLD logarithmic radial extension on the conductor-controlled closed-disc node excludes a mixed-zero profile. Thus a numerical-nef boundary cone must not be identified with that radial extension cone without an additional theorem. If the numerical construction instead yields the full nonpositive ideal cone, explicitly prove and explain this distinction; decide whether it meets the original geometric target, rather than asserting that the word nef closes the problem. On a smooth one-branch curve both theories have the nonpositive single-coordinate unit test.

The available primary BFJ paper supplies the actual smooth complex framework, not its extension to excellent nonnormal rings: Definition2.1 constructs its weak nef Weil cone from ideals; Proposition2.2 identifies its Cartier part with actual relative numerical nefness. Definitions2.5/2.8 and Proposition3.11 add continuity on closed dual-complex faces and the extension to all semivaluations. In higher dimension a trace or retraction of a nef Weil divisor need not be nef. These statements cannot be collapsed into a claim that arbitrary nef model traces already form a decreasing psh regularization. The singular multiplier-ideal source concerns normal complex varieties and is likewise not a positive-characteristic or conductor-descent theorem.
