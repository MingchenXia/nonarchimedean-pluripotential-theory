I reconstruct the continuous-PC criterion without coarsening the inherited rational carrier. It is: each complete branch restriction is ordinary convex, and at every actual crossing v there is one ambient real form p_v whose restriction supports every incident branch at the shared value f(v). Different crossings may require different forms. Shared values alone do not supply these forms.

First, let f be continuous printed-PC. For an actual real point x on a branch, let tau be its minimal inherited face. There are finitely many closed cells. Those not containing x have positive distance from x, so a sufficiently small ball meets only cells containing tau as a face. For a short branch displacement w, choose incident cells sigma+ and sigma- containing x+w and x-w. The simultaneous feasible set (z,u) with z in tau and z+u in sigma+, z-u in sigma- is a rational polyhedron. The active-face density proof in n1 approximates (x,w) within that SAME feasible set by rational pairs. These give legal printed midpoint triples with exact barycenter z and rational endpoints z+-u, both endpoint cells incident to the actual center face. Continuity gives the local midpoint inequality at x, including irrational centers and crossings. No cell coarsening or uniform local radius over a segment has been assumed.

Pointwise local midpoint inequalities imply entire branch convexity. On any parameterized segment subtract its chord, obtaining continuous q on [0,1] with endpoints zero. If q is positive somewhere, take epsilon>0 small enough that q(t)+epsilon t^2 exceeds both endpoint values at an interior point. Its maximum occurs in the interior. At that maximum the local midpoint inequality for q and the strict quadratic increment imply that the average at t+-s is at least the maximum plus epsilon s^2 for some sufficiently small positive s. This contradicts maximality. Hence q<=0 on every segment. This strict-maximum argument handles varying local radii; a hidden finite-cover radius argument is unnecessary.

I check the convex support facts needed for the crossing test. A finite convex function F on the whole finite-dimensional affine branch is bounded above on a cube by its finitely many vertex values. Reflection through the center bounds it below on a smaller cube. On a still smaller ball, extend the direction joining two points by a fixed radius inside the bounded region. Convex secant monotonicity bounds the difference by the oscillation times their distance divided by that radius. Reverse the points to get the other bound. Thus F is locally Lipschitz and continuous. At a, h(v)=lim_{t down to0}(F(a+tv)-F(a))/t exists by monotone secants, is finite by local Lipschitzness and obeys a norm bound. Rescaling gives positive homogeneity. Applying convexity at the midpoint of a+2tv and a+2tw yields h(v+w)<=h(v)+h(w). Both sublinear difference inequalities imply Lipschitz continuity of h.

For a touching support of any finite continuous convex H, project (x,H(x)-1/n) to its closed epigraph. Existence follows by restricting distance minimization to a bounded closed ball. First variation along every feasible segment supplies a supporting unit normal at the nearest point. Its distance from (x,H(x)) tends to zero, and a subsequence of normals has a unit supporting limit at the graph point. The height coefficient is nonpositive because the epigraph is upward closed. It cannot be zero: a unit horizontal normal would bound its full spatial projection, which is the entire affine space. Consequently its vertical coefficient is strictly negative; division gives an affine support minorant. This NONZERO limiting vertical coefficient is essential.

Apply the construction to h at each v. If ell(x)+b<=h(x) touches at v, evaluating at tv for all t>=0 gives ell(v)<=h(v) from t to infinity, hence b>=0 by touching. At zero b<=0, so b=0 and ell(v)=h(v). The forms ell<=h are exactly K={ell:F(a+x)>=F(a)+ell(x) for every x}: one direction takes derivatives of the global inequality; the other uses h(x)<=F(a+x)-F(a). Thus K is nonempty closed convex, bounded by the norm bound on h applied to both signs, and compact. Moreover h(v)=max_{ell in K}ell(v) for every v. This proves the exact compact support representation, not merely existence of one subgradient.

At a crossing translate v to zero. Let K_i be the compact branch subdifferentials and h_i their support functions. In W=product L_i^*, K=product K_i is compact convex. Let S be the image of the ambient restriction map E^* -> W. It is a closed finite-dimensional linear subspace, although it need not be all of W. Suppose K and S are disjoint. Choose k0 in K minimizing squared distance to S, and q=projection_{S-perp}(k0), nonzero. For k in K, first variation toward k gives <q,k-k0>>=0, hence <q,k>>=||q||^2. Set z=-q. It annihilates S and max_K <z,k><= -||q||^2<0. Using the dual identification with vectors w_i in L_i, annihilation is the ACTUAL ambient equality sum_i w_i=0, and the strict bound is sum_i h_i(w_i)<0. The signs of this alternative are therefore correct.

The branch embeddings are rational, so the exact kernel of the ambient sum map has a rational basis. Approximate the separating tuple INSIDE that kernel by rational tuples. Continuity of h_i preserves strict negative cost. Drop zero entries. A common sufficiently small rational t>0 makes every endpoint v+t w_i lie in a genuine inherited cell incident to v; finiteness excludes all nonincident cells near v. For this fixed finite tuple, the derivative expansion gives sum_i(f_i(v+t w_i)-f(v))=t sum_i h_i(w_i)+o(t)<0. Equal weights 1/m give exactly barycenter v. The crossing is a rational vertex of the carrier, so these are legal finite printed endpoints. This contradicts PC. Thus K intersects S and an actual ambient preimage gives p_v supporting ALL incident complete branches. No direct-sum grouping of dependent branch barycenters occurs.

For sufficiency, a positive-dimensional convex cell contained in the finite union of affine branches is contained in one branch. If no branch contains its affine hull, choose a nonzero defining affine equation for each proper branch intersection with that hull. Their product is a nonzero polynomial and cannot vanish on an open relative interior, contradicting coverage. Once a branch contains the hull, it contains the cell. Cells on different branches therefore meet only at empty sets or the actual singleton branch intersections. Whenever cells from two branches contain that singleton, the complex common-face axiom makes it a vertex. Such cells exist at every crossing because each complete branch is covered by its cells. This proves that crossings are vertices on every rational carrier; it is not an extra chosen geometry assumption.

For a PCC whose center is away from crossings, all incident endpoint cells are on the unique branch, so ordinary convexity proves Jensen even for real endpoints. At a crossing v, use f(x_j)>=f(v)+p_v(x_j-v) for each endpoint, on whichever incident branch carries its cell. Summing with the actual nonnegative weights cancels the ambient form by the barycenter equality. These arguments hold on every rational carrier/refinement and for real center and endpoints. They also show why no global ambient convex extension is needed. This proves both directions of the criterion in the stated continuous class. The proofs supply the unnumbered incidence, local-Lipschitz, derivative, separation, rational-kernel and geometry claims of the paper as well as its numbered results.
