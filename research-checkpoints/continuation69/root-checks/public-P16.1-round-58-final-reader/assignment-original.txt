## 2.2. Intersections, energy, and Radon measures

$$

$$
 \langle(0,h),(\theta_1,\varphi_{D_1}),\ldots,(\theta_n,\varphi_{D_n})\rangle

 \tag{5}
$$

intersection measures.

$$

$$

convergence of the weighted measures. This supplies the sign needed
in its printed proof. The Hölder pairing estimate of Theorem 7.34
also gives strong continuity of mixed measures. On irreducible $X$,
$\operatorname{MA}_\omega(0)=\delta_{v_{\rm triv}}$.

## 2.3. Comparison and uniqueness before envelopes

For $\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal E^1$, the model formula, locality on
vertical components, and weighted decreasing continuity give BJ
Theorem 7.40:
$$
 \mathbf1_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\max(\varphi,\psi))
 =\mathbf1_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\varphi).
 \tag{8}
$$
On a model the maximum agrees near a component where its value is
strictly larger, and hence has the same intersection weight there.
The weighted limiting test is
$(\varphi-\psi)_+=\max(\varphi,\psi)-\psi$; pluripolar sets have no mass.
Apply (8) to $\max(\varphi,\psi-\epsilon)$ and use equal total masses:
$$
 \operatorname{MA}(\psi)(\{\varphi<\psi-\epsilon\})
 \leq\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)(\{\varphi\leq\psi-\epsilon\}).
$$
Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives the correctly signed comparison
$$\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\psi)
 \leq\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\varphi). \tag{9}$$

Set
$$
 I(\varphi,\psi)=\int(\varphi-\psi)(\operatorname{MA}(\psi)-\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)),
$$
$$
 J_\psi(\varphi)=E(\psi)-E(\varphi)+\int(\varphi-\psi)\operatorname{MA}(\psi),
 \qquad J(\varphi)=\sup\varphi-E(\varphi).
$$
Mixed pairing algebra and vertical intersection negativity imply
$I\geq0$, $I/(n+1)\leq J_\psi\leq nI/(n+1)$, strict energy concavity,
and the quantitative pairing estimates of BJ Sections 7 and 10.
The energy difference is
$$
 E(\varphi)-E(\psi)=\frac1{(n+1)V}\sum_{j=0}^n
 \int(\varphi-\psi)(\omega+dd^c\varphi)^j\wedge(\omega+dd^c\psi)^{n-j}.
 \tag{10}
$$
It lies between the integrals against $\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)$
and $\operatorname{MA}(\psi)$, in that order.

BJ Corollary 10.4 is in the equidimensional general-variety scope.
Equal MA implies $I=0$. Normalize equal suprema. Theorem 10.3 tested
against $\delta_v$ and $\delta_{v_{\rm triv}}$ gives equality at every
divisorial $v$: these Dirac measures have finite dual energy by
Proposition 11.1 and linear growth. Minimal usc extension gives global
equality. Irreducibility therefore leaves one additive constant.

The global domination principle is also unconditional: if
$\varphi\in\operatorname{PSH}$, $\psi\in\mathcal E^1$ and
$\varphi\leq\psi$ almost everywhere for $\operatorname{MA}(\psi)$,
put $\rho=\max(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathcal E^1$. Then
$$I(\rho,\psi)=-\int(\rho-\psi)\operatorname{MA}(\rho)\leq0.$$
Separation and its a.e. zero difference give $\rho=\psi$. This is BJ
Corollary 10.6 for **global** potentials. It is not a substitute for
proving a merely weak/local singular potential is global.

# 3. A noncircular smooth affine repair

## 3.1. Correct growth and the printed counterexample

W Definition 3.15(1) requires
$$
 \operatorname{FS}(Y)\ni h=max\{c_i\log|f_i|+C_i,\lambda\},
$$
where the $f_i$ generate $k[Y]$, $c_i>0$ are rational, and
$C_i,\lambda$ are rational. Its printed Definition 3.15(2) requires
$h\leq u+O(1)$, a **lower** growth condition. Definition 6.1 instead
requires $u\leq h+O(1)$, an upper condition. We explicitly use (2)
and write $\operatorname{PSH}^{\log,+}$ and
$\operatorname{PSH}^{\log,+}_{\rm reg}$ for this corrected convention.
This does not make the printed assertions true under their printed
definitions.

Indeed take $k=\overline{\mathbb Q}$, $Y=\mathbb A^1$, and
$a=\log|z|$. The functions $a_m=\max(\log|z|,-m)$ are actual affine
FS functions and decrease to $a$. They are finite rational convex PL
functions of $-\log|z|$; identity pullback proves weak pshness.
Also $a\leq\max(\log|z|,0)$. But every FS function has a finite floor
and $a(0)=-\infty$, so no FS function can satisfy the printed lower
bound. Thus W's printed Proposition 6.2(2) and Theorem 6.3 are false
with that membership requirement. The corrected convention permits
logarithmic poles.

W Proposition 4.19 independently invokes Lemma 3.25(1), whose explicit
antecedent is affine regularizability. That antecedent is its desired
conclusion. The following proof replaces this invocation rather than
just changing the growth sign.

## 3.2. Independent smooth local-to-global input

We use the smooth specialization of the local-to-global mechanism
preceding W Proposition 4.19. Its logical inputs are as follows; none
is the disputed affine regularization lemma.

First, a **global PL** locally psh metric is a nef model metric.
For a continuous PL limit, the local decreasing tropical PL
approximation is uniformly convergent on compact neighborhoods by
Dini. Locally, finite rational convex PL functions have convex
smoothing approximants on their finite-coordinate charts; at infinite
coordinates the finite PL expression is independent of those
coordinates on a smaller neighborhood (W Remark 3.2). It therefore
gives a CLD psh function. CLD Definition 8.1.1 is the sheaf generated
from smooth positive-Hessian functions by decreasing limits, not W's
definition by name. CLD Proposition 8.3.1 gives positive curvature for
these locally integrable functions. For an integral model metric,
CLD Theorem 18.8.4 and Corollary 18.8.8 identify positive curvature
with numerical nefness of its residual bundle. Rational metrics are
handled by a tensor power. Applied to model charts this is W Theorem
3.23, hence (3). This direction does not claim equality of all CLD
and W local psh functions.

Second, a bounded locally regularizable function can be embedded
on a smaller neighborhood into a bounded genuine global psh potential.
Here is the useful construction of W Proposition 3.28(2). Choose
nested compact rational tropical domains $P\Subset Q\Subset U$
around the point, with an ample FS potential $v$ such that $v<0$ on
$P$, $v\leq0$ on $Q$, and $Q=\{v\leq0\}$. Clearing poles by a high
power of an ample line bundle realizes the finitely many inequalities
as ratios of sections; add a complete very ample system. Let
$|u|\leq M$ there, with rational $M$ enlarged if necessary. Choose a
rational $A>(2M+1)/\epsilon$ so that
$h=Av+M+1<-M$ on $P$ and $h>M$ on a collar of $\partial Q$.
For the given common-neighborhood decreasing PL approximants $u_i$,
compactness and the strict collar margin ensure $u_i<h$ on that
collar for all sufficiently large $i$. Extend
$\max(u_i-h,0)$ by zero. This is globally PL and locally $A\theta$-psh,
so the preceding model criterion makes it globally psh. These
extensions decrease, are uniformly bounded below, and limit to the
same extension for $u$. This proves the needed genuine globalization
using local PL approximants only. It also proves divisorial
determination for bounded local potentials by BJ Theorem 4.22;
truncation gives the general local assertion used below.

The FS reference just constructed need not equal a previously fixed
model reference, even if their underlying line bundle agrees. This is
accounted for explicitly: write $\theta=\theta_0+dd^c\eta$ with a
global rational PL model potential $\eta$. Replace the glued
$A\theta$-potential $\psi$ by $\psi+A\eta$, and replace its local
potential $h$ by $h-A\eta$. Their sum on the local neighborhood is
unchanged, and the new pair represents $A\theta_0$. Both $\eta$ and
the glued potential are bounded on the compact space. This reference
change, rather than simply identifying different model forms, is
used whenever a prescribed form is required in the next construction.

Third, the bounded local mixed measure can be defined using these
globalizations and BJ's genuine mixed operator. On a small neighborhood
write $C_i u_i=\phi_i+g_i$, with $\phi_i$ global bounded psh and
$g_i$ a model potential. Define the local mixed measure by the
corresponding scaled global mixed measure. For PL inputs CLD Theorem
18.4.7 identifies its intersection weights with the local model
measure. Weighted decreasing continuity in BJ Proposition 7.21 proves
independence of choices on overlaps and localizes the measure. This
is the actual construction in W Theorem 4.2, whose proof uses
Proposition 3.28(2), not smooth affine regularization. It works with
decreasing approximants because the measure is transported from BJ;
mere current positivity is not being asserted to supply that continuity.

Local logarithms of units are pluriharmonic. A finite PL partition of
unity subordinate to a compact finite cover permits integration by
parts, first for local PL approximants and then by weighted continuity.
One can refine the finitely many tropical inequalities of the cover
by a rational simplicial subdivision and use its affine hat functions;
only their restrictions to the compact tropical image matter. Local
PL functions in this calculation need extension only on a neighborhood
of the compact support. The identity
$$\int_X dd^c\phi_1\wedge\bigwedge_{i=2}^n(\theta_i+dd^c\phi_i)=0$$
then gives total local mass $(\theta_1\cdots\theta_n)$. Locality for
maxima is transferred from BJ by the same globalization. Its correct
restriction on $\{u>v\}$ is the measure of $u$, not the measure of $v$
printed in W 4.11(1). Mass and locality give bounded local comparison
with the sign in (9), as in W 4.12--4.15.

On a **smooth** projective irreducible $\bar Y$ over our field, BJ
Theorem 5.20 gives the envelope property for its nef reference class.
For an ample rational trivial reference $\theta$, BJ Theorems 12.8,
12.12 and Proposition 11.1 give a continuous genuine global psh
solution $h_v$ to $\operatorname{MA}_\theta(h_v)=\delta_v$ for every
divisorial point $v$. This replaces the BFJ input in the smooth use of
W 4.16 entirely within its valid scope. For bounded local
$\alpha,\beta$ with $\alpha\leq\beta$ a.e. for the local measure of
$\beta$, subtract a constant from $h_v$ so $h_v<\alpha$. Then
$\alpha_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)\alpha+\epsilon h_v<\alpha$ and its
positive local mixed expansion has an atom at least $\epsilon^n V$
at $v$. Comparison and equal masses force all its mass onto
$\{\alpha_\epsilon<\beta\}$, hence
$\alpha_\epsilon(v)<\beta(v)$. Let $\epsilon\downarrow0$ and use
local divisorial determination. This proves local domination on this
smooth compactification. No singular Dirac existence is used.

Finally let $\alpha$ be bounded locally $\theta$-psh on this smooth
projective space. Take a decreasing **net** of continuous majorants
$f_i\downarrow\alpha$. Such a net exists on a compact Hausdorff space
for every bounded usc function, by normality and finite minima;
countability is unnecessary. Smooth EP gives continuous global
$p_i=P_\theta(f_i)$. The envelope of
$p_i+t(f_i-p_i)$ is $p_i$ for $0\leq t\leq1$. BJ's genuine continuous
energy derivative gives
$$\int(f_i-p_i)\operatorname{MA}_\theta(p_i)=0.$$
Thus the measure is on the closed contact set. Since
$\alpha\leq f_i=p_i$ there, the local domination just proved gives
$\alpha\leq p_i\leq f_i$. The net $p_i$ decreases to $\alpha$ and
has a uniform lower bound, so BJ decreasing closure proves global
pshness. This is the needed smooth instance of W Theorem 4.17, with
its hypotheses established noncircularly. General singular local
domination has not been used.

## 3.3. Smooth compactification and rational parameters

Take an affine embedding $Y\hookrightarrow\mathbb A^r$ and its
projective closure. Characteristic-zero resolution by boundary
blowups gives a smooth projective compactification $\bar Y$ that is
unchanged on $Y$. There is an effective ample boundary divisor: start
with the hyperplane divisor, and at a boundary blowup replace its
pullback by $m$ times that pullback minus the effective exceptional
divisor, for sufficiently large $m$. The negative of that exceptional
divisor is relatively ample. Enlarging $m$ makes the divisor effective
with precisely the inverse-image boundary support. Iteration proves
the assertion; this is the sign-correct form of W Lemma 3.32.

Choose a very ample multiple $L_0$ of that boundary divisor and its
canonical section $s$, so $Y=\bar Y\setminus Z(s)$. Give $L_0$ its
trivial semipositive model metric, with rational form $\theta$.
Including $s$ in a very ample system yields
$$g=-\log|s|_\theta\in\operatorname{FS}(Y),\qquad g\geq0.$$
The ratios of the complete system by a power of $s$ generate $k[Y]$,
as the affine chart of a closed projective embedding. If (2) holds,
polynomial degree estimates between coordinate generators give
$h\leq A g+B'$ for a positive rational $A$ chosen large enough.
Thus assume $u\leq Ag+B$. Choose **rational** $c>1$ and
$\theta'=cA\theta$. This is a rational ample semipositive model form,
not an arbitrary real model coefficient. Choose rational $C$.

## 3.4. Direct truncation and the boundary neighborhood

On $Y^{\rm an}$ set
$$\phi=u-cAg,\qquad\phi_C=\max(u-cAg,C),$$
and set $\phi=-\infty$, $\phi_C=C$ on
$D^{\rm an}=\bar Y^{\rm an}\setminus Y^{\rm an}$.
The upper bound gives
$$\phi\leq B-(c-1)Ag.\tag{11}$$
Choose $R$ with $B-(c-1)AR<C$. The continuous norm $|s|_\theta$
vanishes precisely on $D^{\rm an}$. Its open sublevel set
$\{|s|_\theta<e^{-R}\}$ is a neighborhood of that whole boundary,
and $g>R$ on its intersection with $Y^{\rm an}$. By (11),
$\phi_C=C$ there. This is an actual common boundary neighborhood,
not just a pointwise assertion of limiting values. It proves usc
gluing, and $C\leq\phi_C\leq\max(B,C)$ everywhere.

On $Y$, $cAg$ is a genuine model potential of $\theta'$ in W
Definition 3.10: clear denominators in $cA$ and use the corresponding
power of $s$. Hence
$$\phi_C+cAg=\max(u,C+cAg)$$
is locally psh by the local definition and finite-max closure. To
verify that closure, put the two local decreasing PL sequences over
the same smaller neighborhood and combine their tropicalizations;
their maxima are rational convex PL and decrease to the maximum.
Near the boundary $\phi_C=C$, and every local potential of the
semipositive form $\theta'$ is locally psh; thus $C$ is locally
$\theta'$-psh. Changes of trivialization add a rational multiple of
the logarithm of an invertible section ratio. Refining the
tropicalization adds an affine function in a finite unit coordinate,
which preserves local convexity and decreasing approximation. The
same local definition therefore holds across charts.

It follows that $\phi_C$ is a bounded locally $\theta'$-psh function
on the **smooth** compactification. Section 3.2 makes it genuine
global $\theta'$-psh. Let $C=-m\downarrow-\infty$. The limit $\phi$
is finite wherever $u$ is finite and $g$ is finite. Since $Y$ is
irreducible and $u$ is generically finite, there is such a point, so
the global limit is not identically $-\infty$. BJ decreasing closure
gives $\phi\in\operatorname{PSH}(\bar Y,\theta')$. The identically
$-\infty$ function is outside this generically finite theorem; if
an extended cone includes it, $g/m-m$ is an explicit decreasing FS
sequence tending to it. No generically finite assertion is attached
to that endpoint.

## 3.5. Actual affine FS correspondence

Take a decreasing global rational FS sequence $\phi_i\downarrow\phi$
in the rational class $\theta'$, using BJ Corollary 12.18 followed
by ample FS approximation with summable error tails. Set
$u_i=\phi_i+cAg$ on $Y$. Its FS metric on a sufficiently divisible
power $m cA L_0$ is described by a finite basepoint-free system with
rational weights. Add the complete very ample system on a further
power, including the distinguished section $s^{mcA}$. Assign rational
weights low enough that all added terms are dominated by the
original metric: their relative log norms have finite suprema on the
compact projective space. The metric is unchanged.

On $Y$ these terms are
$$m^{-1}\log|\sigma/s^{mcA}|+\lambda_\sigma.$$
The complete-system ratios generate $k[Y]$; the distinguished section
supplies a rational constant floor. Thus every $u_i$ is in the actual
FS class of Definition 3.15(1), with positive rational coefficients,
rational constants, and coordinate-algebra generators. The sequence
decreases pointwise to $u$. The upper growth (2) was assumed and
proved compatible with this construction. A lower bound at logarithmic
poles was neither assumed nor concluded. This proves Theorem C's
locally psh part.

## 3.6. Smooth weakly psh base

The repaired base needed in W 6.2(2) also starts with a weakly psh
function. Proper birational pullback is local psh by definition; the
proper map is closed, so the descended function is usc. Local
globalization and divisorial determination above show it is the
minimal usc extension of its divisorial values. On a dense affine
principal open $Y_f$ where the birational map is an isomorphism,
the function is locally psh and has the upper growth bound. The
locally psh part of Theorem C gives actual affine regularization there.

For completeness the upper-growth removable extension used here
does not need the disputed base. Suppose $v$ is already regularizable
on $Y_f$ and $v\leq h+B$ for $h\in\operatorname{FS}(Y)$. For
positive rational $\epsilon$, extend $v+\epsilon\log|f|$ by
$-\infty$ across $f=0$. Controlled approximants of $v$, obtained
from a projective compactification by the **legitimate** regularizable
case of W Lemma 3.25, are bounded above by
$2\max(h,-\epsilon\log|f|/3)+O(1)$. Their sums with
$\epsilon\log|f|$ tend to $-\infty$ near $f=0$, uniformly for
each fixed approximant. Truncate at rational floors. The truncations
are PL and locally psh, constant on a neighborhood of $f=0$;
compact model gluing and (3) give global affine FS regularization.
No regularization of $v$ is being proved by assuming it here.

Let $\epsilon_i\downarrow0$. Subtract a common large multiple of an
ample boundary potential $g$ on a smooth compactification. The
resulting genuine global potentials are uniformly bounded above,
equal $v+\epsilon_i\log|f|-2Ag$ on $Y_f$, and $-\infty$ on the
complement. They need not be monotone where $\log|f|>0$. Smooth EP
applied to their tail suprema shows that the usc regularization of
their pointwise limit is global psh. On $Y_f$ this limit is
$v-2Ag$ and is already usc; there the regularization is unchanged.
Section 3.5 supplies a regularizable extension on $Y$. Divisorial
determination makes it unique and identifies it with the original
weakly psh function. This reconstructs the smooth upper-growth base
without using the singular theorem 6.3 or its conclusion.

# 4. An elementary supporting barrier, with its precise extent

Let $\mathbb T=\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$ with extended order topology,
and $r\geq1$. Choose $a>0$ with $\log(r+1)/a\leq1$. Define
$$\chi(t)=a^{-1}\log\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^r e^{-a t_i}\right),
\qquad e^{-a\infty}=0.$$
Then $\chi$ is finite continuous and
$$\max(0,-t_1,\ldots,-t_r)\leq\chi(t)
\leq\max(0,-t_1,\ldots,-t_r)+1.\tag{12}$$
The inequalities bound a sum of $r+1$ nonnegative terms by its
largest term and $(r+1)$ times that term.

Fix $t$, put $Z=1+\sum_i e^{-at_i}$,
$p_0=1/Z$, $p_i=e^{-at_i}/Z$, and $I=\{i:p_i>0\}$. Define
$$C=\chi(t)+\sum_{i\in I}p_i t_i
 =-a^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^r p_i\log p_i,\qquad
 \ell_t(t')=C-\sum_{i\in I}p_i t'_i.$$
Terms with zero coefficient are omitted; if $t'_i=\infty$ for
$i\in I$, the affine expression is $-\infty$. It is finite on a
neighborhood of the contact point and has nonpositive slopes. Write
$p'$ for the probabilities obtained from $t'$. For finite values of
the affine expression,
$$a(\chi(t')-\ell_t(t'))=\sum_{i=0}^r p_i\log(p_i/p'_i)\geq0.$$
Gibbs' inequality follows directly from $\log x\leq x-1$ on the
positive support. Equality also requires no $p'$ mass outside that
support, hence $p'=p$. Since $p_0,p'_0>0$ and
$p_i/p_0=e^{-at_i}$, this means $t'=t$. Thus contact is unique,
including infinite-coordinate strata. The expression must be
evaluated at $t'$, correcting the variable in W Lemma 6.6.

Pointwise strictness is insufficient for uniform separation on this
noncompact space. Put $D(t')=\chi(t')-\ell_t(t')$. If
$m=\min_i t'_i<0$, then $\chi(t')\geq-m$ and
$\ell_t(t')\leq C-(1-p_0)m$, so
$$D(t')\geq-p_0m-C.$$
Consequently $K_b=\{D\leq b\}$ has all coordinates at least
$-B$, $B=(b+C)/p_0$. Since $\chi\geq0$,
$\sum_{i\in I}p_i t'_i\leq b+C$, so for each $i\in I$,
$$t'_i\leq\frac{b+C+B\sum_{j\in I,\ j\ne i}p_j}{p_i}.$$
Coordinates outside $I$ need no upper bound. This puts $K_b$ in a
compact product of finite intervals and intervals $[-B,\infty]$.
The extended nonnegative $D$ is continuous there, so $K_b$ is
compact. Its zero set is $\{t\}$. If $U$ is any neighborhood of $t$,
compactness of $K_1$ and the finite-intersection property give
$K_b\subset U$ for some $b>0$. Hence
$$\ell_t<\chi-b\quad\hbox{on }\mathbb T^r\setminus U.\tag{13}$$
This proves the exact supporting and separation assertions independently
of the cited APW supporting results.

There is also an explicit rational local polyhedral approximation for
this particular function. Its entropy variational formula is
$$\chi(t)=\sup_{q\in\Delta_r}
 \left(-\sum_i q_i t_i-a^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^r q_i\log q_i\right).\tag{14}$$
Rational $q$ give the same supremum, also at infinity, by approximating
inside the face with zero coefficients on infinite coordinates. On
the compact box $Q_B=[-B,\infty]^r$, enumerate rational vectors with
$q_0=1$ and the simplex vertices included. Their finite maxima are
continuous and increase to $\chi$. Dini gives uniform approximation.
Round entropy constants down by rational errors. For rational
$\epsilon>0$, a finite rational convex PL maximum $h_\epsilon$
then satisfies $\chi-\epsilon\leq h_\epsilon\leq\chi$ on $Q_B$.
With $\epsilon_j=4^{-j}$,
$$g_j=h_{\epsilon_j}+2\epsilon_j,\qquad
 \chi+\epsilon_j\leq g_j\leq\chi+2\epsilon_j$$
gives $g_{j+1}<g_j$ and uniform convergence to $\chi$ there.
The same bound holds on the open box $t_i>-B$. At an infinite
coordinate, terms involving it tend to $-\infty$ and are inactive
on a smaller neighborhood; the constant term remains. This checks
the actual finite rational PL definition, rather than treating an
extended affine expression as a finite function everywhere.

Pullback along regular functions $h_1,\ldots,h_r$ yields decreasing
local rational tropical PL approximants wherever those functions
have bounded absolute values. It does **not immediately** give
decreasing global affine FS approximants. Rational slopes can be
cleared to logarithms of monomials. The generator and floor
conditions require the compact gluing/complete-system procedure of
Section 3.5, or W Lemma 3.26's independent PL-exhaustion procedure:
on each compact exhaustion domain, truncate a function minus a
larger ample FS potential; the growth margin makes the boundary
collar constant; glue its given PL approximants; apply (3); then
apply global FS regularization. The sequence's errors are made
summable before diagonalization. This procedure is valid whenever
the **common-domain** local PL sequences and upper growth bound
have actually been established. It is not automatic closure under
every decreasing sequence of merely locally psh functions; W
Remark 4.6 explicitly distinguishes that issue.

We spell out the common-exhaustion step, including real slopes and
the infinite series, so that the barrier does lie in the actual
corrected affine FS regularization class. This step uses the model
criterion (3), rather than a singular envelope theorem.

**PL-exhaustion gluing lemma.** Suppose $Y$ is irreducible affine,
$u\leq g+B$ for an actual affine FS $g$, and on the interior of
each compact exhaustion domain there is a single decreasing rational
convex tropical PL sequence to $u$. Assume $u$ is generically finite.
Then $u\in\operatorname{PSH}^{\log,+}_{\rm reg}(Y)$.
To prove this, choose the projective compactification and rational
ample model potential associated with $g$ by the explicit
affine embedding of W 3.17(2). Rational rescaling and a constant
turn $g$ into that model potential. Fix rational $c>1$ and a rational
floor $C$. The upper growth margin puts
$\{u-cg\geq C-1\}$ in a compact set $E\Subset Y^{\rm an}$.
Take a compact neighborhood of $E$ inside one exhaustion interior.
On its boundary collar $u-cg<C-1$; compactness of the collar and
decrease of the given continuous PL sequence imply that every
sufficiently late approximant is below $cg+C$. Thus its maximum
with the floor, after subtracting $cg$, extends as the constant $C$
outside that neighborhood. It is globally PL and locally
$c\theta$-psh; the local model criterion makes it globally psh.
It decreases to $\max(u-cg,C)$, which is genuine global psh by
decreasing closure. Letting $C\downarrow-\infty$ preserves generic
finiteness and gives the global potential $u-cg$, with boundary
value $-\infty$. Global sequential FS regularization and the
complete-system generator/floor argument of Section 3.5 then give
actual decreasing affine FS approximants for $u$. This proves the
lemma without the disputed 4.19 application or singular EP. It
reconstructs the required upper-growth instance of W 3.26.

For the strict barrier on a countable trivially valued $k$, enumerate
all regular functions $f_j$ and fix coordinate generators $z_i$.
Use $\rho_j=\chi_{r+j}(-\log|z_i|,-\log|f_1|,\ldots,-\log|f_j|)$.
Let $v=\max(0,\log|z_i|)+1$, and choose positive rational
$\gamma_j$ so small that
$0\leq\gamma_j\rho_j\leq2^{-j}(v+1)$. Polynomial degree bounds
and (12) permit this choice. The series $\rho=\sum_j\gamma_j\rho_j$
is continuous, converges locally uniformly, and has upper growth
$\rho\leq v+1$.

Its upper approximants
$R_N=\sum_{j\leq N}\gamma_j\rho_j+2^{-N}(v+1)$ decrease to
$\rho$, because the next term is at most the decrease in the tail
bound. On a **fixed** compact exhaustion neighborhood, all the
regular functions in each finite list have rational log-norm upper
bounds. Formula (14) therefore approximates each $R_N$ uniformly
by a finite rational convex tropical PL $G_N$, with error at most
$\epsilon_N=4^{-N}$. The constants and slopes are rational, since
the $\gamma_j$ are rational. Set
$\widetilde G_N=G_N+2\sum_{m\geq N}\epsilon_m$; this tail is
rational. On that neighborhood,
$$\widetilde G_N-\widetilde G_{N+1}
 \geq R_N-R_{N+1}+\epsilon_N-\epsilon_{N+1}\geq0.$$
The sequence decreases to $\rho$. The gluing lemma proves genuine
affine FS regularizability with its generator requirement.

Fix a contact point $x$ and an open neighborhood $U$. The initial
topology of regular-function norms gives a finite-list tropical
neighborhood $\operatorname{Trop}_j^{-1}(W)$ containing $x$ and
contained in $U$; the enumeration includes that finite list. Use the
supporting expression $\ell$ at $\operatorname{Trop}_j(x)$ from
(13), and put
$$\rho'=\gamma_j\ell\circ\operatorname{Trop}_j
                 +\sum_{i\ne j}\gamma_i\rho_i.$$
It has upper growth, is finite at $x$, agrees there with $\rho$,
and is at most $\rho-\delta$ outside $U$ for some $\delta>0$.
There is no $\infty-\infty$ operation: the continuous series is
finite and the supporting term is either finite or $-\infty$.

To verify actual regularizability of $\rho'$, write its supporting
term as $C+\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_i\log|h_i|$ with real
$\alpha_i>0$. On a fixed exhaustion neighborhood choose rational
$B_i$ with $\log|h_i|\leq B_i$. Choose rational
$\alpha_{i,m}\uparrow\alpha_i$, all positive, and rational
$q_m\downarrow C+\sum_i\alpha_i B_i$. Then
$$L_m=\max\left(q_m+\sum_i\alpha_{i,m}(\log|h_i|-B_i),-m\right)$$
is finite rational convex tropical PL and decreases to that real
logarithmic expression. This monotonicity uses
$\log|h_i|-B_i\leq0$; at a zero of a positive-coefficient $h_i$
the untruncated expression is $-\infty$ and the floor gives a
finite approximant. The remainder series, omitting the fixed $j$,
has decreasing local PL approximants by the preceding tail
construction. Add the two sequences on the same exhaustion
neighborhood. They decrease to $\rho'$, which is generically finite
since it is finite at $x$. The gluing lemma supplies decreasing
**global affine FS** approximants, with rational constants,
rational slopes, and a generating coordinate list. This proves
the supporting replacement used in W 6.7 under upper growth,
including real coefficients, logarithmic poles, and the infinite
series. It does not assert the norm theorem 6.12 or the full
singular Runge chain.

# 5. Rechecking the singular induction: a concrete uncovered field

W Theorem 6.3 uses the smooth weakly psh base 6.2(2), then supporting
functions, Rossi's principle, finite norms 6.12, the local projection
step 6.14--6.16, and the product-domain distance argument 5.7. The
smooth base is repaired above under upper growth. It is insufficient
to validate the next norm application.

In Proposition 6.12 Step 1, for $s\in S^{\rm an}$ the proof sets
$F=H(s)$, takes a line through its $F$-rational lift in $S_F$, and
normalizes the line's preimage. The normalized curve $C_s^\nu$ is
smooth over $F$. Step 3, W LF2331--2334, states that the pulled-back
local psh function on this curve is affine FS regularizable **by
Proposition 4.19**. But 4.19 explicitly assumes a discretely or
trivially valued field. The following instance shows that $F$ need
not satisfy that antecedent.

Take $k=\overline{\mathbb Q}$ trivially valued and
$$
 Y=\operatorname{Spec} k[a,b,y,z]/(b^2-a^3),\qquad
 S=\operatorname{Spec} k[a,y,z],\qquad \pi:Y\longrightarrow S.
 \tag{15}
$$
The source is integral, nonnormal and unibranch, the product of a
cusp and two affine lines. The map is finite flat of degree two:
its algebra has basis $1,b$ over $k[a,y,z]$. It is étale on $a\ne0$;
there $b$ is invertible and the derivative $2b$ is invertible.
This affine source is a chart of
$C_{\rm cusp}\times\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, a projective
geometrically unibranch threefold in the retained domain. Its
normalization is $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$;
the cusp normalization has one point with unchanged residue field
in each geometric fiber, and taking its product preserves that
universal-homeomorphism property. The exterior tensor product of
$\mathcal O_{C_{\rm cusp}}(1)$ and the two $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$
is ample. Thus this field issue arises internally in the proposed
proof on the retained projective domain. It is not a request to
extend the task to a different ground-field axis.

On $S$, let $s$ have kernel $(a)$ and monomial valuation
$$v(y)=1,\qquad v(z)=\sqrt2,$$
with a nonzero polynomial in $y,z$ valued by its least monomial
weight. Distinct monomials have distinct weights, so products have
the sum of their least weights. This defines a multiplicative
seminorm $\exp(-v)$ and an analytic point. Its completed residue
field is the completion of $k(y,z)$ for this valuation. It contains
values $m+n\sqrt2$ for all integers $m,n$, from rational monomials.
This subgroup is nondiscrete: by the pigeonhole principle applied
to fractional parts of $j\sqrt2$, there are nonzero such values
arbitrarily close to zero. Its absolute value is nontrivial.
Completion cannot remove those elements or their values.

The $F$-rational lift of $s$ has coordinates $(0,y_s,z_s)$. The line
$a=\tau$, $y=y_s$, $z=z_s$ is generically in the étale locus. Its
preimage is $b^2=\tau^3$, and its normalization is $\mathbb A^1_F$
with $\tau=t^2$, $b=t^3$. Thus the smooth curve used by Step 3
really occurs over a nondiscrete field, in dimension three, with an
algebraically closed trivial original field and unibranch source.
This is a mathematical witness to the failed application antecedent;
it is not a finite computer search or a counterexample to norm
regularization itself.

Section 6.3.1 and Proposition 6.11 of W do allow **any** complete

A possible repair is to replace Step 3 by an independently proved
one-dimensional pushforward/subharmonic boundary-limit argument,
including compatibility with W's local definition and multiplicities.
Another is to prove upper-growth affine regularization on these
smooth curves over every $H(s)$. Neither has been proved here. This
is a specific mathematical obligation, not an objection based on
v1 status or missing bibliographic identifiers.

With the supporting-function closure supplied by Section 4,
this gap still occurs in 6.12(2), then in 6.15 Step 5 and the

# 6. Conditional envelope passage and removal of cardinality

This section checks the useful forward route with its antecedents
visible. It does not claim the missing countable case.

For a rational ample trivial reference, a very ample multiple
embeds $X$ into $\mathbb P^r$. At the center of a valuation one basis
section is a unit and the others have nonnegative order; the trivial
metric on that multiple is the pullback of the trivial projective
FS metric. This verifies the needed reference reduction. It does
not identify an arbitrary nef model metric with the embedding metric.
On chart $Y_j=\{z_j\ne0\}\cap X$, the potential
$$\rho_j=\max_i\log|z_i|-\log|z_j|$$
is nonnegative and $\min_j\rho_j=0$ everywhere. If the missing
singular upper-growth regularization theorem were available, it
would regularize $\varphi+\rho_j$. The legitimate regularizable
extension lemma then makes $\varphi-(c-1)\rho_j$, extended by
$-\infty$, globally $c\omega$-psh for rational $c>1$. Taking its
finite maximum and then letting $c\downarrow1$ proves that weakly
psh chart data are genuinely global. The upper chart bound follows
from bounded-above usc $\varphi$ on the compact projective space.
This is W 6.5 with precisely its currently missing input exposed.

For a resolution $p:\widetilde X\to X$ of a geometrically unibranch
$X$, geometric fibers are connected: factor through the finite
normalization; the latter is a universal homeomorphism in this
domain, and a proper birational map to a normal target has connected
geometric fibers by Stein factorization. BJ 5.20 applies on the
smooth resolution to the **nef**, possibly nonample, $p^*\omega$.
Its envelope descends to an usc function constant on fibers by W
2.24. That proposition first proves constancy over divisorial
points of strata, where residue fields are discrete or trivial,
then uses flat-stratum analytic openness and divisorial determination
to pass to all points. In its zero-psh argument, $m\varphi$ is the
decreasing sequence for $\varphi\leq0$; the printed $-m\varphi$
has the wrong sign. Divisorial nonnegligibility identifies the
descended function with the downstairs usc supremum. It is weakly
psh. Only the missing weak/global identification would now make it
genuinely global downstairs. Fiberwise usc descent alone does not.

Suppose henceforth that the rational ample envelope theorem on
countable algebraically closed subfields **has been proved**. Let
the original $k$ have arbitrary cardinality. Descend the finite
data of $X$, $L$ and a rational PL obstacle $f$ to a countable
algebraically closed $k_1\subset k$. Each such trivial field is
complete. Reducedness and irreducibility descend by faithful field
extension. Over these perfect characteristic-zero fields, normal
finite-type algebras are geometrically normal; one can test $R_1$
and $S_2$ after a separable extension, whose local fibers are
regular. Consequently finite normalization commutes with the field
extension: the extended normalization is finite normal birational
and hence is the normalization. Multiple geometric branches would
persist after extension, so geometric unibranchness descends.
Ampleness descends by the same faithful flat test.

Set $u_1=P_{\omega_1}(f_1)$, continuous and global by the assumed
countable theorem. Pull back to $u=r_{k1}^*u_1$. Model approximants
show $u$ is global psh and $u\leq f$. For $0\leq t\leq1$, the
envelope of $u_1+t(f_1-u_1)$ is $u_1$: the obstacle lies between
$u_1$ and $f_1$. The genuine BJ continuous energy derivative gives
$$\int(f_1-u_1)\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_1}(u_1)=0.\tag{16}$$
Thus the measure is supported on the closed contact set.

A global model psh competitor $\psi\leq f$ descends to a larger
countable algebraically closed $k_2$, with $\psi=r_{k2}^*\psi_2$.
Relative ampleness and nefness descend by faithfully flat base
change. The restriction map $r_{21}$ is surjective: a multiplicative
seminorm extends to a scalar extension by passing to a completed
residue field extension, equivalently using BJ's restriction-map
section. Thus $\psi_2\leq f_2=r_{21}^*f_1$.

The genuine mixed measures satisfy
$$r_{21*}\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_2}(r_{21}^*u_1)
      =\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_1}(u_1).\tag{17}$$
For model tuples this is checked in (5). A regular field extension
preserves vertical $R_1$ and $S_2$: at vertical codimension-one
points regularity is stable under these separable extensions, and
depth with regular fibers preserves $S_2$. BJ's vertical criterion
therefore preserves relative integral closure while retaining the
given nonnormal generic fiber. Over an algebraically closed base,
vertical irreducible components are geometrically integral, so no
component splits. DVR multiplicities of $t$, divisorial restrictions,
and degrees of intersections are unchanged. Flat extension of
cycles gives identical weights in (5). Decrease model psh
approximants of $u_1$ and test continuous functions to obtain (17);
volumes also stay $V$. This argument never absolutely normalizes
away the generic fiber.

Positivity and (17) put the pulled-back measure on
$\{f_2=r_{21}^*u_1\}$. Hence $\psi_2\leq r_{21}^*u_1$ almost
everywhere for that measure. BJ Corollary 10.6 applies to this
**global** competitor on the singular irreducible variety and gives
the inequality everywhere. Supremizing model competitors, as in BJ
Lemma 5.19, proves $P_\omega(f)=r_{k1}^*P_{\omega_1}(f_1)$, hence
continuity. PL density and the envelope's sup-norm contraction
give all continuous obstacles. No BFJ Dirac equation is invoked on
a singular $k_2$-variety.

If this rational envelope antecedent were established, ample real
classes would follow by choosing rational ample $\omega_j\to\omega$
with $\omega_j-\omega$ ample. This is possible by openness of the
ample cone and rational density, perturbing $\omega+\epsilon_j A$.
The inclusions $\operatorname{PSH}(\omega)\subset
\operatorname{PSH}(\omega_j)$ and BJ's actual class closure make
the usc supremum $\omega$-psh. Thus real-class approximation and
cardinality removal are valid conditional passages, not replacements
for the failed rational countable base.

# 7. The exact envelope-to-equation proof

We prove Theorem B and keep its antecedent throughout.

## 7.1. Energy condition and necessity

For a Radon probability $\mu$, (1) is a convex weakly lsc functional;
BJ Proposition 9.4 computes the same supremum on genuine model
potentials. Finite dual energy makes every finite-energy test
integrable and excludes pluripolar mass. Conversely, if every such
test is integrable, integration is uniformly bounded on normalized
$J$-bounded sets. Otherwise choose $J(\psi_j)\leq C$ with
$\int\psi_j\,d\mu\leq-2^j$. The decreasing partial sums
$\sum_{j\leq m}2^{-j}\psi_j$, completed by a zero term to a convex
combination, have energy at least $-C$, so their limit is in
$\mathcal E^1$ but has integral $-\infty$, a contradiction.

For $0\leq t\leq1$, the actual mixed expansion yields
$$I(t\psi,0)\leq t[1-(1-t)^n]I(\psi,0)\leq nt^2I(\psi,0).$$
Together with $J\asymp I(\cdot,0)$ this gives
$J(t\psi)\leq C_n t^2J(\psi)$. Normalize $\sup\psi=0$ and
take $t=J(\psi)^{-1/2}$ when $J\geq1$. Uniform integration on
the rescaled bounded-$J$ set gives
$-\int\psi\,d\mu\leq A\sqrt{J(\psi)}+B$. Subtracting $J(\psi)$
proves finite dual energy. This is BJ Theorem 9.20 with the
necessary minus signs supplied by its own weighted-sum argument.
It does not require EP.

If $\mu=\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)$, mixed integrability and the
tangent inequality following (10) give
$$E(\psi)-\int\psi\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)
 \leq E(\varphi)-\int\varphi\operatorname{MA}(\varphi).$$
Equality holds at $\psi=\varphi$, so the right side is the finite
dual energy. For raw mass $V$, the correct objective is
$VE(\psi)-\int\psi\,d\nu=V(E(\psi)-\int\psi\,d\mu)$.
Translation invariance uses mass one after normalization.

## 7.2. Compactness and differentiation

By BJ Theorem 5.11, EP gives compactness of
$\operatorname{PSH}_{\sup}(\omega)$ for divisorial convergence.
Bounded divisorial values give a Tychonoff subnet; its usc tail
suprema are psh by EP. Divisorial nonnegligibility identifies those
values with ordinary tail suprema. Their decreasing limit has the
desired finite divisorial values and is globally psh. Continuity
of the supremum preserves normalization. This is compactness of
the genuine cone on $X$, not on a fixed skeleton or only upstairs.

BJ Theorem 8.5 proves on a general reduced projective variety,
uniformly in $\varphi\in\mathcal E^1$ and for $f\in\operatorname{PL}_{\mathbb R}$,
$$E^\downarrow(\varphi+\epsilon f)
 =E(\varphi)+\epsilon\int f\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)+O(\epsilon^2).\tag{18}$$
Its proof writes $f$ as a difference of auxiliary ample model
potentials, uses determinant-of-cohomology/sup-norm volumes,
and the mixed positive expansion with fixed masses. The volume
ratio $V_{\omega+\epsilon\omega'}/V_\omega-1$ is $O(\epsilon)$;
multiplication by $\epsilon$ gives the uniform quadratic error.
Decreasing approximation extends from continuous psh to every
finite-energy potential. Apply to $f$ and $-f$.

For continuous $f$, approximate uniformly by $f_m\in\operatorname{PL}_{\mathbb R}$,
with error $\eta_m$. The correct estimate is
$$|E^\downarrow(\varphi+tf)-E^\downarrow(\varphi+tf_m)|\leq|t|\eta_m.$$
The derivative is squeezed between
$\int f_m\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)-\eta_m$ and
$\int f_m\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)+\eta_m$, for either sign by
also using $-f$. Let $m\to\infty$. EP is used in BJ Proposition
8.3 to identify the upper extension with
$E(P_\omega(\varphi+tf))$ for unbounded $\varphi$; bounded shifts
ensure this envelope has finite energy. Thus
$$\left.\frac d{dt}\right|_{0}E(P_\omega(\varphi+tf))
       =\int f\operatorname{MA}_\omega(\varphi).\tag{19}$$
No multiplier ideals on singular $X$ are assumed in (18). The
envelope identity in (19) is conditional, as required.

## 7.3. Attainment and the actual equation

Put $F_\mu=E-\int\varphi\,d\mu$ and
$J_\mu=E^\vee(\mu)-F_\mu$. Choose a model maximizing sequence,
normalized by $\sup\varphi_i=0$. Strict concavity at the midpoint
with $0$ gives BJ Lemma 9.15:
$$J(\varphi_i)\leq C_n(J_\mu(\varphi_i)+E^\vee(\mu)).$$
It has eventually bounded $J$. Compactness gives a weakly
converging subnet. Lower semicontinuity of $J$ puts the limit in
$\mathcal E^1$.

The fixed target integration step is BJ Proposition 9.19, not weak
testing on continuous functions: weak psh convergence with bounded
$J$ implies $L^1(\mu)$ convergence. Its noncircular proof chooses
maximizing model potentials $\rho_j$; Legendre duality and (18)
give $\operatorname{MA}(\rho_j)\to\mu$ **before** attainment.
Lemma 9.17 bounds the target integration error uniformly on a
bounded-$J$ set by a constant times $J_\mu(\rho_j)^{1/2}$.
Each model measure is a finite sum of divisorial evaluations, so
its integration is weakly continuous there. Uniform limits give
target-integral convergence. Apply this also to
$\max(\varphi_i,\varphi)$, whose $J$ stays bounded. The exact
identity is
$$|\varphi_i-\varphi|=2\max(\varphi_i,\varphi)-\varphi_i-\varphi.$$
This proves $L^1$ convergence. Energy usc then gives
$F_\mu(\varphi)\geq\limsup F_\mu(\varphi_i)=E^\vee(\mu)$,
and hence attainment.

For any maximizing net with deficit $\delta_i$, the Legendre
inequality and (18) give for $\epsilon>0$
$$\epsilon\int f(\operatorname{MA}(\varphi_i)-\mu)
       \leq\delta_i+C_f\epsilon^2.$$
Divide by $\epsilon$, pass to the net limit, let $\epsilon\downarrow0$,
and repeat with $-f$. PL density proves convergence of the
**constructed genuine** measures to $\mu$; a constant maximizing
net at the attained potential proves $\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)=\mu$.
This is BJ 9.11--9.12. Necessity and uniqueness were proved before
EP. Multiplying by $V$ gives the raw equation.

## 7.4. Completeness and why the antecedent is essential

BJ Theorem 12.4 identifies strong convergence with divisorial weak
convergence plus convergence of energy, and $I$ defines it modulo
constants. On the full space add
$|\sup\varphi-\sup\psi|$. Theorem 10.14 proves completeness of
the finite-dual-energy target space with quasimetric
$$I^\vee(\mu,\mu')=\inf_\rho(J_\mu(\rho)+J_{\mu'}(\rho))$$
without EP. Proposition 12.7 gives a bi-Lipschitz embedding of
$(\mathcal E^1/\mathbb R,I)$ into that space. Surjectivity just
proved makes it an isomorphism of complete spaces. Cauchy suprema
give completeness of normalized/full potentials.

For the converse, completeness makes the bounded increasing net
of finite maxima of model competitors below a continuous obstacle
$f$ converge: include a constant lower bound, so all competitors
are uniformly bounded and genuinely in $\mathcal E^1$. For
$\varphi_i\leq\varphi_j$, (10) gives
$$0\leq\int(\varphi_j-\varphi_i)\operatorname{MA}(\varphi_i)
 \leq(n+1)(E(\varphi_j)-E(\varphi_i)).$$
Hence $J_{\varphi_i}(\varphi_j)\leq n(E(\varphi_j)-E(\varphi_i))\to0$.
Completeness gives a psh limit. Its divisorial values and the
continuous obstacle identify it with the model supremum envelope.
BJ Lemma 5.19 supplies its lower semicontinuity, so it is continuous;
the continuous-obstacle characterization gives EP. These are the
actual general-variety implications of BJ Theorem 12.8. In the
forward implication its printed Corollary 5.21 citation must be
the general Theorem 5.11 once EP is assumed. The smooth sufficient
theorem 12.9 is not an unconditional singular theorem.

# 8. Nonnormal unibranch normalization and a nodal obstruction

For a reduced irreducible geometrically unibranch finite-type variety,
normalization $q:X^\nu\to X$ is finite birational of degree one.
The geometric unibranch condition makes every geometric fiber a
single point with purely inseparable residue extension; in
characteristic zero it is an equality. Thus it is a universal
homeomorphism, and its analytic seminorms lift uniquely, so
$q^{\rm an}$ is a homeomorphism. This topological assertion does
not prove global psh descent.

One-way pullback is unconditional. Projection formula gives
$(q^*\omega)^n=V$, and BJ Proposition 7.2 gives
$$E_{q^*\omega}(q^*\varphi)=E_\omega(\varphi).$$
Consequently downstairs $\mathcal E^1$ pulls into upstairs
$\mathcal E^1$. BJ Proposition 7.19 gives the actual mixed identity
$$q_*\bigwedge_i(q^*\omega_i+dd^cq^*\varphi_i)
       =\bigwedge_i(\omega_i+dd^c\varphi_i).\tag{20}$$
It follows from model projection formula and decreasing pairing
continuity, or from (7) and PL density. These do not require EP.

Actual **onto** pullback needs more: BJ Lemma 5.13 assumes a sequence
$\omega_m>\omega$ tending to $\omega$, each with EP. Under that
antecedent it uses small pulled-back logarithmic ideal corrections,
increasing closure in the larger classes, and divisorial determination
to descend every upstairs global psh function. Equal energies then
give onto $\mathcal E^1$. Combined with the analytic homeomorphism,
onto pullback would also give equal dual energies and correspondence
of every target. These onto assertions remain conditional here;
the missing singular envelope premise cannot be replaced by the
homeomorphism.

There is an unconditional obstruction outside the unibranch domain.
Let $C$ be an irreducible rational projective nodal curve, $q:\mathbb P^1\to C$
its normalization, and $p_1\ne p_2$ the preimages of the node.
Let $d=\deg L>0$ and $v=\operatorname{ord}_{p_1}$ on the common
function field. It is a divisorial point downstairs, and
$\delta_v$ has finite dual energy by BJ Proposition 11.1.

Blow up $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1$ at $(p_1,0)$, with exceptional
$E$. The model class
$$p^*(q^*L)+d\mathcal X_0-dE$$
has degrees $0$ and $d$ on the two central components, both with
multiplicity one. It is relatively nef: every vertical curve is a
central component and has the indicated nonnegative degree. Its
genuine raw measure by (5) is $d\delta_v$. Its bounded continuous
psh model potential is
$$g(s\operatorname{ord}_{p_1})=d\max(1-s,0),\qquad
g(s\operatorname{ord}_p)=d\quad(p\ne p_1).$$
In particular its two endpoints at $p_1,p_2$ have values $0,d$.

If a downstairs finite-energy solution to $d\delta_v$ existed,
its pullback would be finite-energy by the unconditional energy
identity. By (20), positivity and equal mass force its upstairs
measure to be $d\delta_v$, since a valuation of the common function
field has a singleton analytic lift. Upstairs uniqueness on the
smooth irreducible curve makes its pullback $g+c$. A pullback from
$C$ has the same value at the two endpoints mapping to the node;
$g+c$ differs by $d>0$. Contradiction. This is an actual finite-energy
nonsolvable target for the genuine source operator, also reflected
in BJ Example 12.11. It does not depend on the missing unibranch EP.

For comparison, the cuspidal cubic $y^2z=x^3$ has normalization
$[u:v]\mapsto[u^2v:u^3:v^3]$, finite birational with one geometric
point in every fiber, and $q^*\mathcal O_C(1)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(3)$.
The same upstairs blowup has central degrees $0,3$ and genuine
mass $3\delta_{\operatorname{ord}_{u=0}}$. This upstairs computation
does not prove onto psh descent downstairs. No general cuspidal
existence theorem is inferred from it in this paper.

# 9. Exact disposition and reproducibility

The global model/intersection finite-energy theory, necessary target
energy condition, and uniqueness/comparison replacements are
established in the retained projective ample domain by BJ. The
full existence-and-uniqueness theorem is established here only
with its actual EP antecedent, and unconditionally in the smooth
domain where BJ proves EP. The direct smooth extension has been
repaired noncircularly under the corrected upper-growth convention.
The entropy barrier and its local rational PL approximation are
independently proved. The singular norm step over $H(s)$ still
has the concrete gap in Section 5. The supporting replacement now
includes common-exhaustion and actual affine FS regularization,
but does not remove that norm antecedent. Therefore the rational
countable-field singular EP,
then unconditional arbitrary-cardinality/real-class singular
existence and onto normalization, remain unproved in this assignment.

No claim is made about other valuations, positive characteristic,
nonample classes, nonprojective proper spaces, or nonalgebraizable
analytic spaces. Arbitrary support in Theorem B is genuine arbitrary
support with (1), not replacement by a skeleton. Irreducibility gives
one constant; in the broader equidimensional source scope the
ambiguity is locally constant, and component masses are their
intersection volumes. Disconnected or multibranch varieties do
not acquire an asserted one-constant onto theorem.

## Explicit conventions, rather than literal faulty formulas

Definition3.1(3), actual page14, writes f(x)=l(x)+C but only f(x')>=l(x').
Read literally this holds locally for every continuous real function: choose
a constant lower bound l and set C=f(x)-l. The intended supporting-affine
condition must instead be f(x')>=l(x')+C with equality at x. This correction
is necessary whenever using the genuine convex/psh classes. A proof using
literal membership in the faulty class cannot establish global psh membership.
The corrected condition on finite coordinate charts is ordinary convexity.
At a coordinate +infinity the supporting affine function has coefficient
zero in that coordinate, as required for real-valued continuous affine data.
Every use of a boundary support or rational PL approximation still needs its
actual proof; the correction alone does not supply it.

Proposition3.28's actual page22 defines a metric maximum containing log|s0|,
then identifies v=-log||s0|| with a maximum lacking the corresponding zero
term and concludes v(x)<0. With that unshifted term v>=0, so the displayed
construction cannot be literal. It also changes the semipositive model form
from omega to theta but subsequently calls a theta potential an omega
potential. The following bookkeeping repairs both defects.

## Proposed corrected bounded local construction

Take rational M>0 with |u|<=M on the chosen compact neighborhood. Select
rational coordinate inequalities log|f_i|<C_i, with f_i=s_i/s0 on an affine
chart and x strictly inside every inequality, and add enough sections to
define a globally generated ample mL. A generating list suffices; it need
not simultaneously be a prescribed basis. Use an actual FS metric whose
local potential is

    v=(1/m) max(-mC0, log|f_1|-mC_1,...,log|f_r|-mC_r),

with C0>0 and all thresholds chosen so v(x)<-epsilon<0. The constant floor
is negative, not zero. Make Q={v<=0} compact and contained in the original
open set, and P={v<=-epsilon}. The ample class is unchanged, but its
semipositive model form is theta. Write theta=omega+ddc p for the actual
global rational model-metric difference p, retaining this difference below.

Choose rational A>(2M+1)/epsilon and put h=A v+M+1, a local potential of
A theta up to its rational additive constant. Define

    Psi=max(u-h,0) on Q;  Psi=0 outside Q.

On P, h<-M and Psi=u-h. Near the boundary of Q, h>M and Psi=0. Thus the
local definition and finite maxima give Psi in bounded LPSH_(A theta).
Set Phi_theta=Psi/A. Then the correctly referenced functions are

    Phi_omega=Phi_theta+p,
    g_omega=v-p+(M+1)/A.

Here g_omega is an actual local omega potential and Phi_omega belongs to
bounded LPSH_omega. On int(P) the exact identity is

    Phi_omega+g_omega=u/A.

This accounts for both the metric change and the scalar factor. It does not
identify theta with omega merely because their line bundles agree.

If u_i decreases to u through local tropically convex PL functions, the
same construction for Psi_i can be made zero on one fixed boundary shell
for all sufficiently large i. Indeed on the compact shell where
v>=-1/(2A), h>=M+1/2; the decreasing upper-semicontinuous differences
u_i-h have a strictly negative limit there. Their nested nonnegative level
sets are compact with empty intersection, so one is empty. This proves the
needed eventual strict inequality without uniform convergence to a singular
limit. Psi_i then decreases globally to Psi.

The indispensable remaining step is to verify that these globally glued PL
functions are genuine model-psh for A theta, using the actual local/model
criterion or an explicit FS/model construction. That step cannot be inferred
just from a positive CLD Hessian without checking the model positivity
criterion. Once proved, the global decreasing theorem gives Phi_theta in
PSH_theta and the exact background transformation gives Phi_omega in
PSH_omega. This establishes the regularizable version of the local extension
needed for the local mixed-MA theorem. The source Theorem4.2 does provide a
full reduction of local bounded decreasing continuity to the genuine global

reference to an unretrieved APW measure theorem. Its extension and PL/global

Unreviewed root continuation of the frozen continuation66 curve boundary
proposal. This checks two additional obligations identified while reading
the completed round56 draft. It is not an independent correctness verdict
or a completed original solution, and no active worker receives it.

## 1. A nontrivially valued extension also handles trivial H(s)

The curve boundary argument need not split into trivial and nontrivial
F cases. If F is trivially valued, extend to F((t)) with |t|=exp(-1),
then take its completed algebraic closure Omega. If F is nontrivially
valued, take its completed algebraic closure directly. In the intended
applications F=H(s) extends a trivially valued characteristic-zero field,
so its residue field has characteristic zero; Omega retains that property.
Omega is complete, algebraically closed and nontrivially valued, exactly
the standing scope of Wanner1801.04713v3 LF168--174.

The actual common-neighborhood rational tropical PL approximation of a
locally psh function pulls back under this field extension. Continuous
convex PL weights have positive CLD curvature by local convex smoothing;
Wanner4.7 gives the subharmonic disk comparison. Thuillier3.1.8(ii) passes
through the decreasing sequence. Thus the boundary trace proof of
continuation66 applies over Omega in either case. Trace base change is
the finite Artinian fiber-dimension identity proved in the determinant
note of continuation67. It does not require already regularizable w.

For clarity, the tropical convex convention here includes the corrected
support inequality f(x')>=l(x')+C when f(x)=l(x)+C. The source's literal
Definition3.1(3), omitting C from that inequality, does not establish
convexity and is not used to infer current positivity. The correction
and its effects must be stated in the integrated proof, as in continuation65.

After splitting a finite closed-point fiber over Omega, choose small local
disk coordinates z_i at its smooth source points and t at the target.
The finite curve map has t=c_i z_i^(e_i) times a unit. For a sufficiently
small disk the unit is analytically 1-close to a nonzero constant. Since
|e_i|=1, the convergent binomial e_i-th root changes coordinates to give
t=c_i y_i^(e_i). A target Gauss point of radius r in |Omega^*| has exactly
one source Gauss point on each such disk, of radius
(r/|c_i|)^(1/e_i) and fiber degree e_i. The residue extension at that
Gauss point has degree e_i (the reduction equation Y^(e_i)=T is
irreducible over the rational-function residue field), so this is the
dimension in the actual analytic fiber algebra, not an unweighted count.
Properness excludes other preimages for sufficiently small target disks.

On each closed disk the subharmonic maximum principle bounds the value
at its center by its value at the single boundary Gauss point. Upper
semicontinuity gives the reverse limiting inequality. This proves radial
convergence to the center value even when it is -infinity; identically
-infinity restricted components are handled separately. Taking the finite
weighted sum and using local upper bounds proves the trace equality at
the target center. Ordinary punctured-neighborhood convergence is neither
needed nor generally true. The entire proof is to be independently checked.

## 2. A fixed curve may fail to lift to a given birational model

Wu6.12 takes a slicing line first and a proper birational model witnessing
weak pshness later. Its assertion that the normalized slicing curve
automatically maps to the proper transform must be checked: a curve can
be contained in the image of the model's exceptional locus. Merely being
generically in the finite map's etale locus does not prevent this.

The following test-function argument can make the slicing multiplicities
independent of the chosen dense open and line before using a specially
adapted line for a particular weakly psh function.

Keep the original algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero
ground field k, an integral affine Y and finite surjective pi:Y->S, with S
affine open in A^n. Choose any dense principal open W in S such that
pi^-1(W)->W is finite etale. At s take F=H(s), its F-rational lift s',
and a line through s' generically contained in W_F. Reduce the scheme
preimage and normalize to obtain the smooth finite-flat curve q:D->C.
Each component of the scheme preimage has dimension one by the height
bound and finiteness; the normalization therefore covers all points over
s'. The etale generic fiber is reduced of degree d=deg(pi), so q has
degree d. It supplies a positive integer weight m_(y,C) at each original
analytic fiber point y, by summing the actual q-fiber dimensions over
the normalization points above y. The total is d.

Fix a globally affine FS test h on Y. On U=pi^-1(W), choose an actual
nonnegative affine FS exhaustion v_U including its localized algebra
generators. Then max(h,v_U/m-m) decreases to h|U and is actual affine FS
on U: the extra branches include the inverse defining function and the
original generators, with a rational floor. Thus h|U is already globally
FS-regularizable. The direct finite-flat determinant lemma proves that
N_pi(h)|W is globally FS-regularizable on W.

Because pi is finite, every source coordinate satisfies a monic equation
with base coefficients. The non-Archimedean root bound gives
h<=A g_S+B on all of Y for a fixed base affine FS exhaustion g_S and
some rational A>0. Thus its trace over W satisfies the same upper-growth
bound, multiplied by d. The legitimate already-regularizable removable
extension theorem on the **smooth original-ground-field** S gives a unique
regularizable v_h on S extending that trace. This is the repaired smooth
extension, not arbitrary-H(s) regularization.

The pullback of h to every normalized slicing curve is locally psh directly,
without choosing a birational witness. Apply the radial boundary trace
argument above to v_h and h on that curve, and obtain

    v_h(s)=sum_(y over s) m_(y,C) h(y).

Different W choices give the same v_h by uniqueness of smooth removable
extension, since their traces agree on their common dense open. Hence every
allowed slicing curve gives the same weighted sum for every global affine
FS test h.

These tests determine a measure on the finite fiber. Differences of affine
FS functions form a rational vector lattice: sums and maxima are closed,
and max(h_1-g_1,h_2-g_2)=max(h_1+g_2,h_2+g_1)-g_1-g_2.
Constants belong to this lattice as differences. Distinct analytic points
have distinct regular-function seminorms. A truncated log norm, with very
low added coordinate-generator branches, separates any prescribed pair
on the finite fiber. Thus the lattice separates these points. The elementary
finite-set lattice approximation argument, or real closure followed by
Stone--Weierstrass, makes its restrictions dense in all real functions on
that finite set. Equal FS test integrals therefore force equal individual
weights. This proves the proposed independence of m_(y,C) from W and C.

Now let u be weakly psh, witnessed by a proper birational mu:Ytilde->Y.
Shrink W further so mu is an isomorphism over pi^-1(W), as well as pi
etale there; finiteness sends the proper exceptional-image subset to a
proper closed subset of S. Choose a line through s' generically in this
smaller W_F. The generic inverse to mu lifts its normalized preimage curve;
properness and the valuative criterion extend the lift across each closed
point of the smooth curve. Pulling back u o mu gives the required actual
local psh function w on D. The radial boundary argument proves the trace
formula for u at s. The test-function independence just proved makes its
weights the fixed multiplicities of pi, independent of this u-specific
choice. This removes the hidden curve-lifting dependency from the proposed
repair and targets exactly Wu6.12 Step3.

Pending independent reconstruction: the smooth removable extension with
its corrected upper-growth antecedent, the FS restriction and finite-set
lattice argument, the generic degree/dimension assertions, and the disk
ramification computation. The singular Runge induction, localization hull
repair and final envelope passage remain separate obligations. This note
does not turn the already published round56 partial draft into a PASS.


## Unreviewed explicit algebraic model-PL collar formula

# Explicit model PL membership for the affine FS collar gluing

Unreviewed root proof proposal, 2026-10-05. This targets a representation
obligation retained in continuation66 and in the completed round56 draft.
It applies to collar gluing of **global affine FS expressions** and does
not identify every analytic PL function on a singular variety with a model
PL function. No original problem is closed and no active worker receives it.

Let X be projective, possibly singular and nonnormal, over algebraically
closed trivially valued k. Let L be ample with a rational basepoint-free FS
reference metric theta. Let s_0 be a section of L such that Y=X\Z(s_0) is
affine, and suppose its actual reference potential

    g=-log||s_0||_theta>=0

is a proper affine FS exhaustion. We can arrange this with the projective
embedding associated to an affine FS function, adding the distinguished
section s_0 with weight zero. Fix c>0 rational and C rational.

Let a_i be an **actual global affine FS** function on Y. Express each of
its finitely many regular functions as f_j=F_j/s_0^(d_j), with
F_j in H^0(X,d_j L), using the polynomial expression in the affine
embedding coordinates. Write

    a_i=max(lambda_i, q_j log|f_j|+A_j), q_j>0 rational.

Suppose that on a fixed collar R<=g<=R+1 in X^an one has
a_i-cg<=C, with R>0 rational. Define psi_i to be max(a_i-cg,C) on
g<=R, and C on g>=R, including the boundary divisor. Because the
expressions agree on the collar, this is a continuous glued function.
If a_i is locally psh, then psi_i is locally c theta-psh: on Y add the
model potential cg, and near the boundary use the semipositive constant.

We now establish psi_i in the **genuine global BJ PL(X)** algebraically.
Set

    G=min(g,R+1)=-max(log||s_0||_theta,-R-1).

The maximum on the right is a bounded global model PL function. Indeed
it is the difference between the FS metric obtained by adding s_0 and
the reference theta; taking their finite maximum includes a complete
basepoint-free reference system. Hence G is in PL(X), including at the
zeros of s_0 where G=R+1.

Similarly choose positive rational M_j and put

    H_j=max(log||F_j||_(d_j theta),-M_j).

This is bounded global model PL: relative to the canonical trivial
metric, if phi_theta is the rational FS reference potential, the formula
is max(log||F_j||_triv,d_j phi_theta-M_j)-d_j phi_theta. The first term
is an actual bounded FS metric on d_j L because the second branch
contains a complete basepoint-free system; the last is its model reference.
Thus H_j is a difference of genuine global bounded FS/model functions.
It does not require a local-analytic-PL representation theorem.

Choose M_j so large that for every G in [0,R+1],

    -q_j M_j+(q_j d_j-c)G+A_j<=C.

This is possible because all coefficients and the interval endpoints are
rational. It ensures that clipping log||F_j|| at -M_j never changes the
maximum with floor C when g<=R+1. Define the global model PL function

    A=max(C,lambda_i-cG,
          max_j(q_j H_j+(q_j d_j-c)G+A_j)).

On g<=R+1 it equals max(a_i-cg,C). All its constituents are bounded
global model PL functions, with signed rational coefficients allowed.
By BJ1801.08229v4 Section2.1, PL(X) is a rational vector lattice closed
under finite max and min, so A is genuinely in PL(X).

Choose positive rational K with C+K>=sup_X A, which exists by compactness.
Set

    F=min(A,C+K(R+1-G)).

This is another genuine bounded global model PL function. On g<=R the
second argument is at least C+K, so F=A=max(a_i-cg,C). On the collar
R<=g<=R+1, the assumed inequality gives A=C, while the other argument
is >=C, so F=C. On g>=R+1, including the whole divisor, G=R+1 and
the other argument is C, so F=C because A>=C. Therefore F=psi_i on
all of X^an. This proves the desired **global model representation** by
an explicit finite lattice formula, without modifying the generic fiber.

Having actual model PL membership, the primary positivity bridge of
continuation66 applies on this singular X. Clear rational coefficients,
represent the metric by its equivariant flag-ideal model, and base change
to k((t)). The flag-ideal formula gives the actual pulled-back metric at
every analytic point, not only on the Gauss section. Local c theta-pshness
gives positive CLD curvature, preserved by CLD18.8.6. CLD17.7.10 and
18.8.4 applied at a point reducing to each central curve prove the model
bundle nef. BJ's genuine model nef criterion then gives global
c theta-pshness. No smooth-only BFJ equivalence is applied to singular X.
Every formal reduction, residual pre-model and positivity application
still needs independent reconstruction in an integrated proof.

For u globally affine-FS regularizable with u<=g+B, take a decreasing
actual FS sequence a_i. For rational c>1 and a rational floor C, the
growth margin makes u-cg<C-1 on a collar sufficiently far out. Compactness
of that collar and decrease of the continuous a_i show that all
sufficiently late i satisfy a_i-cg<C there. Use the same collar for
that entire tail. The above construction gives genuine global model psh
psi_i decreasing to max(u-cg,C), extended constantly at the divisor.
Letting C decrease to -infinity yields u-cg as a genuine global psh
potential if it is generically finite. Global decreasing FS approximation
and the complete-system correspondence give controlled actual affine FS
approximants in this **already regularizable** case.

This supplies an explicit route for the legitimate source3.25 antecedent
and related singular collar steps using globally algebraic FS input. It
does not prove arbitrary singular weak/local regularization by assuming
that conclusion, and it does not automatically handle a local PL sequence
with no global algebraic expression. Smooth local gluing can still use the
separate nontrivially valued GM plus smooth BJ route. The exact Hartogs/
Runge use of finite polynomial coefficient expressions must be checked
separately before applying the present formula there.
