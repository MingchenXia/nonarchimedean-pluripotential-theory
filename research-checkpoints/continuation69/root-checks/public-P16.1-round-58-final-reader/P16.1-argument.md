# 1. Exact disposition

The requested theorem concerns every reduced irreducible geometrically
unibranch projective variety over every algebraically closed trivially valued
field of characteristic zero, in every dimension, with every ample line
bundle. That theorem is **not established in this paper**. The original
problem and completion criteria are preserved unchanged. In particular,
none of the conditional statements below replaces the required singular
envelope theorem by an assumption in a purported solution.

There are three sorts of results here. The global finite-energy operator and
its precise envelope-to-equation equivalence are source theorems of
Boucksom--Jonsson [BJ], in their actual general-variety scope. The determinant,
Artinian base-change, algebraic collar representation, entropy approximation,
pole-order, and curve boundary arguments are reconstructed below, without
using an earlier generated argument as a premise. Finally, certain local
measure and smooth weak-regularization obligations remain unproved here.
They are stated concretely in Section 11. No inference about novelty or
independent mathematical certification is made.

The main new partial statements are the following.

**Theorem 1 (finite traces).** Let $A$ be an integral affine algebra over a
complete non-Archimedean field, and $B$ a reduced finite locally free
$A$-algebra of rank $d$. The trace, weighted by the dimensions of the actual
local Artinian analytic fiber algebras, of an actual affine FS function is
an actual affine FS function. Its determinant formula, positive rational
coefficients, rational floor, and algebra-generator condition are all
explicit. Decreasing regularization, whenever already supplied, is
preserved; generic finiteness of the limit is a separate condition.
Base change of the weighted trace holds for **every** pulled-back function,
without a regularizability antecedent.

In addition, let $k$ be algebraically closed and trivially valued of
characteristic zero, let $Y$ be integral affine, and let $\pi:Y\to S$ be
finite surjective, with $S$ affine open in $\mathbb A_k^n$. There are positive
integer weights at every analytic fiber point, totaling $\deg\pi$,
independent of a slicing line, an auxiliary etale open, or a tested function.
For globally affine FS functions their trace is the FS function given by
the generic field-norm polynomial. The same holds for a supplied decreasing
FS sequence, subject to generic finiteness of its trace limit. For an input whose curve restrictions, after the complete algebraically
closed extension in Section 5, have common-neighborhood decreasing
continuous CLD-psh approximants, and a global regularizable extension of its
trace from an etale open, the curve boundary argument proves equality with
that fixed weighted trace everywhere. It supplies the missing **equality**
in Wu Proposition 6.12 Step 3 under these explicit antecedents; it does not
supply the extension, the smooth weak base, or general compatibility of a
convex function defined only on a tropical image with CLD currents.

**Theorem 2 (algebraic collar).** In the projective compactification of an
affine embedding, a collar truncation of an actual global affine FS
expression has a finite formula in BJ's rational model-PL lattice, including
on the whole boundary divisor. If the glued metric is locally psh in the
corrected convex sense specified below, CLD's residual-degree criterion
makes this actual model metric nef, also on singular and nonnormal spaces.
Consequently the already-regularizable upper-growth instance of Wu 3.25
has a noncircular proof. The same formula applies to the explicit finite
polynomial coefficient expressions in the Hartogs distance calculation,
and proves its upper-growth regularization statement.

**Theorem 3 (support and localization).** The finite entropy barriers and
their countable positive rational series, as well as their real logarithmic
supports, have direct global decreasing approximations in the actual affine
FS class. No singular analytic-PL representation assertion is needed.
The pole-order localization inequality with error $2cM$ holds for every
integral affine algebra over a trivially valued field, including nonnormal
algebras; it uses integral closure and an $f$-power conductor, rather than
counting a chosen denominator power.

These partial results concern proof inputs. They do not constitute a
singular existence theorem. Only the genuine global operator, necessary
and sufficient *energy condition for admissible targets*, necessity for
solutions, uniqueness, and the conditional equation theorem are asserted
in the full requested projective domain. Unconditional arbitrary-support
existence, singular envelopes, and onto normalization remain unresolved
in this reconstruction. The ample-real-class global assertions are
supported by BJ; the new local algebraic formulas use rational ample data.

Only the six specified primary caches are read: [BJ] 1801.08229v4;
[W] 2609.27610v1; [CLD] 1204.6277v2; [GM] 1608.08030v3;
[Wa] 1801.04713v3; and [Th] tel-00010990v1. Wu is a public v1 preprint;
no independent correctness verdict is assumed. The exact PDF and literal
text bytes are preserved in `sources/`. `source-extracts/source-provenance.json`
gives hashes, versions, inclusive one-based LF ranges calculated with
`split('\n')`, literal statements and relevant proof bodies, applications,
and limitations. Whole-body inspection of these six papers is not claimed.
No separate APW or BE primary body is imported.

On an affine algebra $R$, write
$$\operatorname{AFS}(R)=\left\{\max\{\lambda,c_i\log|f_i|+C_i\}_i\right\},$$
where the $f_i$ generate $R$ as a ground-field algebra, all $c_i$ are
positive rational, and $\lambda,C_i$ are rational. This is precisely W
Definition 3.15(1). It is a real-valued continuous function: logarithmic
zeros are absorbed by the finite floor. A corrected regularizable
upper-growth function has a supplied decreasing AFS sequence, is
nontrivial on the relevant components, and satisfies $u\le h+O(1)$.
We never replace BJ's genuine global PSH cone by this auxiliary definition.

Two corrections are indispensable when interpreting local convexity.
W Definition 3.15(2) prints $h\le u+O(1)$, whereas Definition 6.1 and the
extension arguments use $u\le h+O(1)$. The former excludes $\log|z|$ on
$\mathbb A^1$, even though $\max(\log|z|,-m)$ is a decreasing sequence
of actual AFS functions. A finite floor cannot be below $-\infty$ at zero.
Thus the literal printed equality in 6.2(2) cannot be retained. Every local
repair here uses the upper condition, allowing logarithmic poles.

W Definition 3.1(3) prints contact $f(x)=\ell(x)+C$ but the inequality
$f(x')\ge\ell(x')$, omitting $C$. Literally a continuous function bounded
below near $x$ satisfies this by taking $\ell$ constant. We require instead
$f(x')\ge\ell(x')+C$ with contact at $x$. For the finite-coordinate PL
functions used here this is local ordinary convexity. At an infinite
coordinate, a finite PL expression is independent of that coordinate on
a smaller neighborhood, as in W Remark 3.2. All current-positivity uses
below refer to this corrected condition. No assertion is proved about
the overly large class obtained from the literal defective inequality.

Positive rational sums and maxima of AFS functions are AFS. Distribute the
finite maxima; clear rational coefficients into monomials; and retain,
without rewriting, each single-generator branch obtained by choosing the
constant floors in the other summands. Those branches still contain the
original generators with positive rational coefficients. The sum of floors
is rational. Differences form a rational vector lattice, since
$$\max(a-b,c-d)=\max(a+d,c+b)-(b+d).$$
This elementary observation will also be used on finite analytic fibers.

# 3. Genuine global theory and the exact conditional equation

Let $k$ be algebraically closed trivially valued, $X$ reduced irreducible
projective of dimension $n$, and $\omega\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)$ an
ample real numerical Cartier class. These are BJ's general conventions;
Section 7 returns to general varieties after the smooth-only subsection
6.5. Characteristic zero is retained in the task, but not needed for this
operator construction. Put $V=(\omega^n)>0$.

For a nonnormal generic fiber, models are integrally closed **in that
generic fiber**. On an affine chart the closure is in $A[1/t]$, not the
absolute normalization of $A$ in its total fraction field. Absolute
normalization would change $X$. BJ 1.10--1.13 prove finiteness, cofinality,
and the vertical $R_1/S_2$ criterion. The generic point of a vertical
component $E$ has a DVR; let
$$b_E=\operatorname{ord}_E(t),\qquad v_E=b_E^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_E|_{k(X)}.$$
For model potentials represented by vertical real Cartier divisors $D_i$
on a common dominating configuration, the genuine mixed raw measure is
$$M^{\rm raw}=\sum_E b_E\left(\prod_{i=1}^n
                (\omega_{i,\mathcal X}+D_i)|_E\right)\delta_{v_E}. \tag{3.1}$$
Relative nefness gives nonnegative intersection weights. Intersecting the
central-fiber cycle shows total mass $(\omega_1\cdots\omega_n)$.
BJ Lemma 4.3 is the actual model-nef/PSH criterion.

Compactify the configuration over $\mathbb P^1$ and set
$$\langle(\theta_0,\phi_0),\ldots,(\theta_n,\phi_n)\rangle
       =\prod_i(\theta_{i,\overline{\mathcal X}}+D_i).$$
Projection formula proves independence of the common model and normalization
pullback. Translation in entry $i$ adds $c(\prod_{j\ne i}\theta_j)$.
BJ Theorem 7.1 extends this pairing to ample psh tuples by the infimum of
pairings of dominating model majorants. Its proof uses model monotonicity,
continuity of $\sup(\phi-\psi)$ for a model $\psi$, and the translation
rule to prove usc. It also gives decreasing-net continuity. Theorems
7.8 and 7.14 give finite mixed pairings for finite-energy tuples and a
symmetric multilinear extension to their differences. None assumes EP.

The actual normalized energy is
$$E_\omega(\phi)=\frac{\langle(\omega,\phi)^{n+1}\rangle}{(n+1)V},\qquad
 E_\omega(0)=0,\quad E_\omega(\phi+c)=E_\omega(\phi)+c,$$
$$\mathcal E^1(X,\omega)=\{\phi\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega):E_\omega(\phi)>-\infty\}.$$
It is the trivial-reference energy of BJ, not an energy normalized by a
supremum or by a changed background metric. When a collar uses another
reference $\theta$, its potential is transferred to this cone by adding
the actual model-metric difference.

For a rational model-PL test $h$, BJ 7.18 defines the mixed measure by
$$\int h\,dM^{\rm raw}
 =\langle(0,h),(\omega_1,\phi_1),\ldots,(\omega_n,\phi_n)\rangle.\tag{3.2}$$
Positivity bounds this functional by its mass times $\|h\|_\infty$.
BJ 2.2--2.3 give PL density in $C^0$; Radon representation supplies the
unique measure. Multilinearity handles signed differences. Proposition
7.19 proves agreement with (3.1), exact mass, integrability of every
finite-energy test, decreasing continuity, and normalization pushforward.
Weighted decreasing continuity is 7.21; the weighted pairing is the
pairing containing the test potential **minus** the one containing zero.
The estimates of 7.34 give strong continuity. Thus this is the genuine
mixed model/intersection operator, constructed before equation existence.
For the diagonal, $\operatorname{MA}_\omega=M^{\rm raw}/V$ is a probability.
For irreducible $X$, $\operatorname{MA}_\omega(0)=\delta_{v_{\rm triv}}$.

Model locality for maxima, followed by weighted decreasing continuity,
gives locality on $\{\phi>\psi\}$ with the measure of $\phi$.
The equal-mass argument then gives
$$\int_{\{\phi<\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\psi)
   \le\int_{\{\phi<\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\phi).$$
BJ 10.4 and 10.6 give uniqueness modulo one constant and global domination
in this general irreducible scope, without EP. In particular,
$$I(\phi,\psi)=\int(\phi-\psi)(\operatorname{MA}(\psi)-\operatorname{MA}(\phi))\ge0.$$
If the measures coincide, $I=0$. The quantitative separation theorem
10.3 tested against finite-dual-energy divisorial Diracs gives equality
of sup-normalized potentials at all divisorial points. BJ 4.22 supplies
their minimal usc extension, proving global equality. Domination follows
by applying this to $\max(\phi,\psi)$: its $I$ with $\psi$ is nonpositive
under the a.e. inequality, hence zero. These are global statements, not
local/global identification on singular analytic charts.

For a raw positive Radon target $\nu$, its prescribed mass is exactly $V$.
Put $\mu=\nu/V$. The finite-dual-energy condition is
$$E_\omega^\vee(\mu)=\sup_{\psi\in\mathcal E^1}
       \left(E_\omega(\psi)-\int\psi\,d\mu\right)<\infty.\tag{3.3}$$
The supremum can be computed on genuine model potentials. BJ 9.20, in
its irreducible scope, proves equivalence with integrability of every
$\mathcal E^1$ potential. A sign in its printed integration bound must
be negative: sup-normalized potentials are nonpositive. To check the
proof, unbounded integration on a $J$-bounded set would give
$J(\psi_j)\le C$ and $\int\psi_j\,d\mu\le-2^j$. The weighted decreasing
sum $\sum2^{-j}\psi_j$ has energy at least $-C$ by concavity but integral
$-\infty$, a contradiction. The mixed expansion gives, for $0\le t\le1$,
$$I(t\psi,0)\le t[1-(1-t)^n]I(\psi,0)\le nt^2 I(\psi,0).$$
Since $J$ and $I(\cdot,0)$ are comparable, scaling by $t=J(\psi)^{-1/2}$
yields $-\int\psi\,d\mu\le A\sqrt{J(\psi)}+B$ and (3.3).
For a solution, the mixed energy tangent inequality gives attainment of
(3.3) at the solution, so necessity is unconditional. This characterization
of targets does not assert surjectivity.

**Conditional equation theorem.** Assume the genuine envelope property for
$\omega$: usc suprema of nonempty bounded-above global psh families are
psh. Then every target (3.3), with arbitrary support, is the MA of an
$\mathcal E^1$ potential, unique modulo one constant. Conversely,
surjectivity for every such target implies EP. This is BJ Theorem 12.8,
not its smooth sufficient corollary 12.9.

Here are the operative steps. General Theorem 5.11 supplies compactness of
sup-zero PSH for divisorial convergence. The uniform energy derivative,
Theorem 8.5, is
$$E^\downarrow(\phi+\epsilon f)
 =E(\phi)+\epsilon\int f\operatorname{MA}(\phi)+O_f(\epsilon^2),\quad f\in\operatorname{PL}_{\mathbb R},$$
uniformly for $\phi\in\mathcal E^1$. The source uses model-volume
calculations and mixed positive expansions, not smoothness of $X$.
Apply it to both signs. Uniform approximation of a continuous direction
by PL directions changes energy by at most $|t|\eta$, so the two-sided
derivative is $\int f\operatorname{MA}(\phi)$. EP is required in 8.3 to
identify this with $E(P_\omega(\phi+tf))$ for an unbounded potential.

Normalize a maximizing sequence for (3.3) by $\sup\phi_j=0$. Strict
concavity and BJ 9.15 bound $J$; compactness gives a weak subnet and
lower semicontinuity puts its limit in $\mathcal E^1$. Proposition 9.19
supplies $L^1(\mu)$ convergence on bounded-$J$ sets. Its proof first
approximates $\mu$ by maximizing **model** MA measures using 9.11, before
attainment: the Legendre inequality and uniform derivative imply their
weak convergence. The uniform target-integration estimate 9.17 then
makes integration a uniform limit of divisorial finite-sum evaluations.
For absolute values the correct identity is
$$|\phi_j-\phi|=2\max(\phi_j,\phi)-\phi_j-\phi.$$
Energy usc gives attainment. At an attained maximum, the Legendre
inequality, the derivative, and tests $f,-f$ force the actual constructed
MA measure to be $\mu$. The raw objective is $VE-\int\phi\,d\nu$;
translation invariance uses precisely mass $V$.

BJ 10.14 supplies target-space completeness before EP; 12.7 supplies the
MA embedding. Surjectivity makes the embedding onto and supplies strong
$\mathcal E^1$ completeness. Conversely completeness applied to bounded
increasing finite maxima of model competitors gives continuous envelopes:
for $\phi_i\le\phi_j$, the mixed energy formula bounds the relevant
$J_{\phi_i}(\phi_j)$ by $n(E(\phi_j)-E(\phi_i))$. The net is Cauchy,
and divisorial values identify its psh limit with the model supremum.
This explains why solving only EP-to-equation cannot complete the task.

For $n=0$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}k$, $V=1$, potentials are constants and
$E(c)=c$; the equation is immediate. No $n\ge1$ estimate is applied there.

# 4. Determinants and the exact affine FS trace

Let $B$ be finite locally free of rank $d$ over integral affine $A$.
For an analytic base point $s$, $F=H(s)$ and
$$B_s=B\otimes_A F=\prod_{y\mid s}B_{s,y},\qquad
 m_y=\dim_F B_{s,y},\qquad \sum m_y=d.$$
The residue field of $B_{s,y}$ is $H(y)$, finite over $F$, with its unique
extended absolute value. Nilpotents are retained. Set $N(u)(s)=\sum m_yu(y)$.

Take $u=\max(\lambda,c_i\log|f_i|+C_i)\in\operatorname{AFS}(B)$.
Choose an integer $D$ clearing all $c_i$, and put
$$b_0=1,\quad b_i=f_i^{Dc_i},\quad w_0=D\lambda,\quad w_i=DC_i.$$
The determinant of multiplication by $\sum_iT_i b_i$ on $B$ is a scalar
endomorphism of the invertible module $\det_A B$. It is therefore a
basis-independent homogeneous polynomial
$$P(T)=\det(\operatorname{mult}_{\sum T_i b_i})
       =\sum_{|\beta|=d}a_\beta T^\beta\in A[T],\qquad a_{(d,0,\ldots,0)}=1.$$
No global free basis is required.

Extend $F$ to a complete algebraically closed valued extension. The finite
algebra becomes a product of local Artinian algebras with residue field
the extension. On a local factor of length $\ell$, a composition series
has $\ell$ residue-field quotients, and multiplication acts on each by
$\sum T_i b_i(x)$. Thus
$$P_s(T)=\prod_x\left(\sum_i T_i b_i(x)\right)^{\ell_x}.$$
The sum of geometric lengths above an original $y$ is $m_y$, including
its residue degree. In characteristic zero this also follows by splitting
the separable residue field into its $[H(y):F]$ embeddings. The characteristic
zero case is all that is required here.

For arbitrary positive real radii $r_i$, the weighted Gauss norm
$\|\sum a_\beta T^\beta\|_r=\max|a_\beta|r^\beta$ is multiplicative.
One elementary proof chooses the lexicographically largest exponent among
terms attaining each maximum. At the sum of those exponents, no other
pair of maximal terms can contribute: in the additive lexicographic
order each is at most the chosen term, and equality of the sum forces
both equalities. Contributions of smaller norm cannot cancel the unique
maximal contribution. The resulting coefficient has the product norm;
the opposite inequality is the non-Archimedean triangle inequality.
This proof requires no value-group restriction on the $r_i$. Isometric
scalar extension preserves the norm term by term.

Applying this with $r_i=e^{w_i}$ gives the exact identity
$$DN(u)(s)=\log\max_\beta e^{\sum_i\beta_iw_i}|a_\beta(s)|,$$
$$N(u)=\max_\beta\left\{D^{-1}\log|a_\beta|
                         +D^{-1}\sum_i\beta_iw_i\right\}.\tag{4.1}$$
The $T_0^d$ coefficient supplies the rational floor $d\lambda$.
All intercepts and positive log coefficients are rational; zero
coefficients can be discarded. Cancellation of determinant coefficients
has already been handled by Gauss multiplicativity.

The coefficients $a_\beta$ need not generate $A$. Choose generators $g_j$
of $A$ over the ground field and write their images as polynomials in the
$f_i$. If $c_* =\min c_i$ and $C_* =\min C_i$, then, with
$Q=\max(0,\log|f_i|)$, one has $u\ge c_* Q-M_0$ for a rational $M_0$
large enough to cover the floor and constants. A polynomial expression
of degree $e_j$ gives
$$\log|g_j\circ\pi|\le e_j Q+L_j,$$
where $L_j$ is the logarithm of a coefficient bound. Choose positive
rational $\eta_j$ with $\eta_je_j\le c_*$, and rational $M_j$ large
enough that $u\ge\eta_j\log|g_j\circ\pi|-M_j$ globally. Coefficients
of a nontrivially valued field merely change $M_j$. Summing on a fiber,
$$N(u)\ge d\eta_j\log|g_j|-dM_j.$$
Add these dominated branches to (4.1). Its value is unchanged and its
list now generates $A$. This proves actual AFS membership, not just
local tropical-PL membership.

For $u_j\downarrow u$, a fixed finite fiber sum gives $N(u_j)\downarrow
N(u)$, including a $-\infty$ summand. Every sequence is locally bounded
above by its first AFS term, so no $+\infty-\infty$ occurs. For an upper
growth bound on a finite map, the source coordinates are integral over
$A$. A monic equation gives the root estimate
$$\log|f_i(y)|\le\max_{j<m}\frac{\log|a_j(s)|}{m-j}$$
(the zero root is harmless). Otherwise the leading power would strictly
dominate all other terms and could not sum to zero. Polynomial bounds
in base generators therefore give an upper base-FS growth bound. To
call the trace limit generically finite, one must verify that it is finite
on the required base component; for example, it suffices that it be finite
at all points of one fiber. We do not infer this merely from a formal sum.

If $u$ is finite continuous and $u_j$ are continuous AFS approximants,
Dini on each compact inverse image gives uniform convergence there.
The trace error is at most $d$ times that error. Formula (4.1) shows the
approximant traces continuous, so the trace is continuous. Such compact
sets exist locally on the analytic base, and the finite map is proper.
Dini is used only for a continuous finite limit.

**Trace base change without approximation.** At any scalar-extended base
point $s'$ above $s$, the fiber algebra is $B_s\otimes_FH(s')$.
Its decomposition is obtained by splitting the original idempotents and
then its local factors. For each original factor, dimensions over
$H(s')$ sum to $\dim_F B_{s,y}$. Every refined analytic point restricts
to $y$, since the residue-field norm extension is unique. Hence for any
function $a$ on the original fiber,
$$\sum_{x\mapsto y}\dim_{H(s')}B'_{s',x}=m_y,\qquad
 N_{\pi'}(a\circ r)(s')=N_\pi(a)(s).\tag{4.2}$$
This is an identity of finite Artinian algebras, not a consequence of
regularizable-function base change. It also applies to a finite map
without flatness when using its actual fiber dimensions at that point.


**Worked Artinian-fiber check.** For $A=k[a]$, $B=k[z]$, $a=z^2$,
let $u=\max(\log|z|,-1)$. Its norm polynomial is
$P=T_0^2-aT_1^2$, with radii $(e^{-1},1)$, and formula (4.1) gives
$N(u)=\max(-2,\log|a|)$. At $a=0$ the fiber is $k[z]/(z^2)$,
one point with Artinian dimension two; $2u(0)=-2$ agrees. If the fiber
splits away from zero, two dimension-one points give the same formula;
if it stays a degree-two residue field, its single dimension-two point
also gives it because $|z|=|a|^{1/2}$. Thus neither nilpotent lengths nor
residue degrees can be replaced by an unweighted point count. This is
an exact algebraic example, not an exhaustive finite computation.

# 5. Radial curve boundary equality

Let $F$ be complete with residue characteristic zero, $C$ a smooth curve,
and $q:D\to C$ a finite map from a disjoint union of smooth curves, every
component dominating $C$. It is flat: over each target DVR its finite
module is torsion-free. Let $a\in C(F)$. Let $v$ and $w$ be usc functions
on neighborhoods of $a$ and $q^{-1}(a)$, respectively, with common-neighborhood
decreasing continuous CLD-psh approximants **after the scalar extension
used below**, allowing an identically
$-\infty$ restriction to a component. A sufficient explicit condition is
a decreasing sequence of rational convex PL weights on **ambient tropical
neighborhoods**, pulled back by regular functions. This includes every
actual AFS function and its supplied decreasing sequences. Suppose, on a punctured algebraic
neighborhood of $a$,
$$v(t)=\sum_{x\mid t}\dim_{H(t)}(\mathcal O(D)\otimes H(t))_x\,w(x).\tag{5.1}$$
Then the same equality holds at $a$.

To handle **all** $F$, first choose a complete algebraically closed
nontrivially valued extension $\Omega$. If $F$ is trivial, embed it in
$F((T))$, $|T|=e^{-1}$, then take the completed algebraic closure. If
$F$ is nontrivial, take its completed algebraic closure directly. Residue
characteristic remains zero. Wanner's terminology, LF168--174, is exactly
complete, algebraically closed, nontrivially valued; normalizing its
valuation scale to contain $\mathbb Q$ does not affect the assertions.
The geometric fiber now consists of rational points, and (4.2) transports
(5.1) without any assumption of FS regularizability of $w$.

For the stated sufficient tropical condition, the common-neighborhood PL
sequences pull back under scalar extension and under algebraic maps: pull
back the regular functions defining the tropical chart and retain the same
convex PL expression on its ambient neighborhood. Near a zero coordinate a
finite PL expression is independent of it on a smaller neighborhood.
On the remaining finite coordinates its finite convex affine maximum has
smooth convex regularized maxima converging uniformly on compact subsets.
The $d'd''$ Hessian is positive. This proves continuous PL approximants
are CLD-psh in Wanner Definition 3.16; continuity supplies local
integrability. Wanner Section 4, LF800--805, additionally fixes a **smooth proper**
algebraic curve. Complete each component of the smooth $\Omega$-curves
by the normal projective model of its function field; a regular curve
over the perfect $\Omega$ is smooth, and the original smooth curve is
an open subcurve (its local DVR points identify this open immersion).
Thus the local neighborhoods are open subsets of smooth proper curves,
exactly the scope of Theorem 4.7. No extension of the functions across
the added boundary is necessary. The theorem applies to each continuous
approximant on those open subsets and makes it Thuillier-subharmonic.
Thuillier 3.1.8(ii) passes to the decreasing limit on each connected
neighborhood: it is subharmonic or identically $-\infty$. This use does
not assert a general arbitrary-$F$ affine regularization theorem. For a
convex PL function specified only on a tropical image with local supports,
an ambient convex smoothing theorem is an extra antecedent: a local affine
support alone need not furnish such an extension. That full compatibility
of W's corrected local class is not proved here, and is a concrete remaining
condition for applying this sufficient boundary theorem to every weak input.

For a locally subharmonic $b$ at a rational center with disk coordinate $z$,
take sufficiently small closed disks, contained with a neighborhood in its
domain. Their boundary is the single Gauss point $\xi_R$ for
$R\in|\Omega^*|$. The constant harmonic comparison of Th 3.1.5 gives
$$b(0)\le b(\xi_R).$$
For a nontrivial subharmonic function the latter is finite by Th 3.1.9.
Since $\xi_R\to0$, usc gives $\limsup_{R\to0}b(\xi_R)\le b(0)$.
Thus $b(\xi_R)\to b(0)$, also if $b(0)=-\infty$. The identically
$-\infty$ case is immediate. This is convergence along these boundary
Gauss points, **not** an ordinary punctured-neighborhood limit. For example,
logarithmic singularities at other closed points obstruct such ordinary
limits even for otherwise well-behaved subharmonic functions.

Choose a target coordinate $t$ at $a$ and coordinates $z_i$ at the finitely
many source centers. On sufficiently small disks a finite curve map has
$$t=c_i z_i^{e_i}U_i(z_i),\quad c_i\ne0,\quad U_i(0)=1,$$
where $e_i$ is the order of $q^*t$ in the source DVR. Shrink until
$|U_i-1|<1$. Because residue characteristic is zero, $|e_i|=1$ and
all rational binomial coefficients have norm at most one. The convergent
binomial series $U_i^{1/e_i}$ gives $y_i=z_iU_i^{1/e_i}$.
This is an invertible coordinate on a smaller disk: its linear term is
one, and its higher coefficients become strictly smaller than the
linear term after shrinking; successive substitution is a contraction.
Thus $t=c_i y_i^{e_i}$ there.

For a target Gauss radius $r\in|\Omega^*|$ small enough, each such disk
has exactly one preimage Gauss point of radius
$R_i=(r/|c_i|)^{1/e_i}\in|\Omega^*|$. Rescale both coordinates to unit
radius. The residue equation is $Y^{e_i}=\alpha T$, $\alpha\ne0$,
which is irreducible over $\widetilde\Omega(T)$ by Eisenstein at $T$.
Consequently the Gauss norm on $\Omega(y_i)$ lies above that on
$\Omega(t)$ with residue degree $e_i$. Since the entire map on these
disks has degree $e_i$, the actual analytic fiber algebra is a single
field of dimension $e_i$; there are neither additional points nor
hidden nilpotent lengths at this generic Gauss point. At $t=0$ its
local Artinian dimension is also $e_i$.

Properness is essential to exclude preimages outside the chosen disks.
Take open disks inside the coordinate neighborhoods; the complement is
closed and its image under the finite map is closed, avoiding $a$.
A small target neighborhood therefore has all preimages in these disks.
Choose $r$ further small so its Gauss point avoids the finite algebraic
exceptional set in (5.1). Then
$$v(\xi_r)=\sum_i e_i w(\xi_{R_i}).$$
The separate radial limits give the assertion at $a$. If a summand tends
to $-\infty$, usc bounds the others above near their centers, so the
finite sum tends to $-\infty$. There is no subtraction of infinities.
Finally aggregate the geometric Artinian dimensions by (4.2) to return
to the original $F$-fiber. This proves the boundary equality used here.

# 6. Slicing weights, an independent norm extension, and proper lifting

In this section $k$ is algebraically closed trivially valued of
characteristic zero, $Y$ integral affine of dimension $n$, and $S$ affine
open in $\mathbb A^n$, with a finite surjection $\pi$. Write $A=k[S]$,
$B=k[Y]$, $K=\operatorname{Frac}A$, $L=\operatorname{Frac}B$, and $d=[L:K]$.
Choose a dense principal etale open $W\subset S$.

For $s\in S^{\rm an}$ set $F=H(s)$ and take its rational lift $s'$ in
$S_F$. Choose a line through $s'$ generically in $W_F$, intersected with
$S_F$. Such a line exists because $F$ is infinite and finitely many
nonzero polynomial conditions cannot vanish on every direction. In the
scheme preimage, the line is cut by $n-1$ linear equations. $Y_F$ is
integral of dimension $n$ because $Y/k$ is geometrically integral.
The principal-ideal height bound and the dimension formula for affine
finite-type algebras give every component dimension at least one;
finiteness gives dimension at most one. There are no zero-dimensional
minimal components. Reduce this preimage and normalize it to $D$.
Every component dominates the smooth line $C$, so $q:D\to C$ is finite
flat. Normal curves over the perfect characteristic-zero $F$ are smooth.
Its generic fiber lies in the etale locus, where neither reduction nor
normalization changes it; hence its rank is $d$. Every point over $s'$
is covered by normalization. Summing its positive Artinian dimensions
over points restricting to an original analytic $y\mid s$ defines positive
integer weights $m_{y,C}$ with total $d$.

We now prove independence without invoking an unproved smooth removable
extension. For any global $h\in\operatorname{AFS}(B)$, form the homogeneous
polynomial of Section 4 as the field norm $N_{L/K}(\sum_iT_i b_i)$.
Each $b_i$ is integral over $A$, so $\sum T_i b_i$ is integral over
$A[T]$. All coefficients of its characteristic polynomial are integral
over $A[T]$. The norm polynomial belongs to $K[T]$, and $A[T]$ is
normal because $A$ is a localization of a polynomial algebra over $k$.
Thus its coefficients are in $A[T]$. This algebraic argument extends
(4.1) across the nonflat locus. The base-generator bound of Section 4
makes the resulting maximum $v_h$ an actual global AFS function on $S$.
On $W$ it is the determinant trace of $h$.

Pull $v_h$ and $h$ to the normalized slicing curve. They are continuous
locally convex PL, and (4.2) gives their trace equality on $C\cap W_F$.
The radial theorem of Section 5 yields
$$v_h(s)=\sum_{y\mid s}m_{y,C}h(y).\tag{6.1}$$
The field-norm polynomial depends only on $\pi$ and $h$, not $W$ or $C$.
Consequently all slicing choices have identical integrals against every
actual global AFS test.

These tests determine each individual weight. Their differences form the
rational lattice of Section 2 and contain rational constants. Distinct
analytic fiber points have different regular-function seminorms; choose
$f$ distinguishing them and truncate $\log|f|$ below a rational floor
small enough to retain the distinction. Add low generator branches so
that they are dominated at both points. This is an actual AFS function
separating the pair. On a finite set the closure of this rational lattice
is all real-valued functions: for a pair of points, affine rescaling of a
separating function interpolates two prescribed values in the closure;
finite minima and maxima then give a function with every prescribed
finite-set value. Real rescaling is obtained as a limit of rational
rescalings. Equality of integrals therefore implies equality against
each indicator function. Hence $m_{y,C}$ is independent of both choices;
write it $m_y(\pi)$. On $W$ it agrees with (6.1)'s actual finite-flat
fiber dimension. At a nonflat fiber these weights need **not** be called
the dimensions of the original nonflat fiber algebra; they are the fixed
normalized slicing degrees, totaling the generic degree $d$.

For a supplied decreasing AFS sequence $h_j\downarrow h$, $v_{h_j}$ are
actual AFS functions and their fixed finite-fiber sums decrease to
$\sum m_yh(y)$. Thus finite-surjective trace regularization is proved for
an already globally regularizable input, provided its limit is generically
finite. If the input is finite continuous, the compact uniform convergence
argument of Section 4 proves continuity of this trace too. This is a new
partial strengthening of the finite-flat calculation in the original
normal-target situation; it proves no weak-input approximation by itself.

Now suppose $u$ is weakly local psh, witnessed by a proper birational
$\mu:\widetilde Y\to Y$, and suppose that a genuine globally regularizable
$v$ on $S$ has already been constructed agreeing with its trace on a
suitable etale open. Shrink that open so $\mu$ is an isomorphism over its
preimage. This is possible: the bad image is a proper closed subset of
$Y$; its image under finite $\pi$ is closed of dimension less than $n$.
Choose the slicing line **after** this shrink. Each normalized curve
component then has a generic lift through the inverse of $\mu$. Properness
extends this generic lift across every closed point of the smooth curve
by the valuative criterion for its DVRs; separatedness glues the extensions.
Its pullback $w$ is now an actual decreasing local convex-PL limit, or
identically $-\infty$ on a restricted component. If these restrictions
have the continuous CLD-psh approximants of Section 5 (in particular the
ambient-convex sufficient condition), the radial theorem proves
$$v(s)=\sum_{y\mid s}m_y(\pi)u(y).$$
The preceding independence proof ensures these weights did not depend on
this $u$-specific choice of line. A curve chosen before the witness need
not lift; no such assertion is used.

In Wu 6.12 the preceding extension depends on a smooth weakly-psh
upper-growth base and its removable extension. Those antecedents are
not reconstructed in full here. The missing arbitrary-$H(s)$ affine
regularization in its printed Step 3 can be avoided for this equality under
the explicit curve-positivity condition,
without asserting a stronger arbitrary-field theorem. For instance,
$Y=\operatorname{Spec}k[a,b,y,z]/(b^2-a^3)\to\operatorname{Spec}k[a,y,z]$
is finite flat of degree two. At a point with kernel $(a)$ and weights
$v(y)=1,v(z)=\sqrt2$, $H(s)$ contains the nondiscrete group
$\mathbb Z+\sqrt2\mathbb Z$. The line $a=\tau$ has normalized preimage
$\tau=z_0^2,b=z_0^3$ over that field. Sections 5--6 apply to its
boundary equality when the stated curve-positivity antecedent holds; they do not prove arbitrary-$H(s)$ FS regularization.

# 7. Explicit model-PL collars and their positivity

Let $X$ be the projective closure of an affine embedding $Y$, $L$ a very
ample rationally weighted basepoint-free FS reference with metric $\theta$,
and $s_0$ the distinguished section such that $Y=X\setminus Z(s_0)$.
Assume $g=-\log\|s_0\|_\theta\ge0$ is an actual proper affine FS
exhaustion. The standard coordinate closure with the trivial reference
has $g=\max(0,\log|z_1|,\ldots,\log|z_r|)$. More general rational FS
embedding references work with the same formulas.

Take a global actual affine FS expression
$$a=\max(\lambda,q_j\log|f_j|+A_j),\qquad f_j=F_j/s_0^{d_j},\quad
 F_j\in H^0(X,d_jL).$$
The homogeneous expression is obtained from a polynomial in the affine
embedding coordinates; no arbitrary analytic function is homogenized.
Fix rational $c>0,C,R>0$ and assume $a-cg\le C$ on the entire shell
$R\le g\le R+1$. Define $\psi=\max(a-cg,C)$ for $g\le R$, and $C$
for $g\ge R$, including $Z(s_0)^{\rm an}$.

Here is its finite **algebraic** model-PL formula. Set
$$G=\min(g,R+1)=-\max(\log\|s_0\|_\theta,-R-1),$$
$$H_j=\max(\log\|F_j\|_{d_j\theta},-M_j).$$
These are genuine BJ model-PL functions. For example, if $\phi_\theta$ is
the FS metric's global potential relative to the trivial metric, then
$$H_j=\max(\log\|F_j\|_{\rm triv},d_j\phi_\theta-M_j)-d_j\phi_\theta.$$
After clearing rational weights and taking a suitable power, the first
maximum is an actual basepoint-free FS metric: its second branch contains
the complete basepoint-free system of the reference. The same argument
applies to $G$ with $s_0$. A lone untruncated section norm is not claimed
to be bounded PL. BJ Section 2.1 says the **rational** PL space is a vector
lattice. Its real span need not be closed under max, and is not used here.

Choose rational $M_j>0$ so that
$$-q_jM_j+(q_jd_j-c)G+A_j\le C\quad(0\le G\le R+1).$$
It suffices to check the two endpoints of this rational affine expression.
This is a uniform clipping threshold, including all zeros of $F_j$.
Set
$$A_* =\max\left(C,\lambda-cG,
              \{q_jH_j+(q_jd_j-c)G+A_j\}_j\right).$$
Signed rational coefficients are permitted by the rational vector space.
On $g\le R+1$, clipping changes no branch above the floor, hence
$A_*=\max(a-cg,C)$. Choose rational $K_*>0$ with
$C+K_*\ge\sup_X A_*$, and put
$$F_* =\min\{A_*,\ C+K_*(R+1-G)\}.\tag{7.1}$$
On $g\le R$ the second branch is at least $C+K_*$ and is inactive.
On the shell $A_*=C$ by hypothesis. On $g\ge R+1$, including the whole
boundary divisor, $G=R+1$ and the cap is exactly $C$, while $A_*\ge C$.
Thus $F_*=\psi$ everywhere. This checks each signed coefficient and every
boundary value. It proves model representation for this algebraic input;
it does not identify arbitrary analytic PL with equivariant model PL.

Locally on $Y$, adding $cg$ to $\psi$ gives $\max(a,C+cg)$ where the
inner expression is used. This is corrected locally convex PL. On the
shell it equals $C+cg$, and near the boundary the glued potential is
constant relative to the semipositive reference. Hence the metric is
locally $c\theta$-psh, including the seams. Changes of frame add a
logarithm of an invertible section ratio, an affine harmonic term.

We check that this local assertion makes the represented model metric nef
without a smooth-only equivalence. Clearing rational powers makes the
metric integral PL. For the collar metrics here, the local formulas are explicit finite
maxima of algebraic logarithms on ambient tropical neighborhoods. They
have smooth convex approximants; their uniform convergence on smaller compact
charts gives positive curvature currents. CLD 18.8.4 and 18.8.8 apply to
boundaryless separated analytifications; algebraic projective
analytifications have these properties, also when singular or nonnormal.
To read actual central curve degrees, represent the metric on a dominating
equivariant configuration and extend to $k((t))$, $|t|=e^{-1}$.

This extension really is the pulled-back metric at **every** analytic
point. BJ 2.1 expresses model PL as differences of rational flag-ideal
functions. An equivariant ideal has monomial generators $t^{-\ell}f$;
at any extended analytic $x$ its ideal norm is
$\max e^\ell|f(p(x))|$. This is precisely the pullback of (2.3), without
restricting $x$ to the Gauss section. Integral closure of a flag ideal
does not change this norm: a monic integral equation and the ultrametric
root bound give the inequality for every added integral element, and the
opposite inequality follows from containment. Tensor powers handle the
reference metric and differences. The formal completion of the
projective flat configuration over $k[[t]]$ is an admissible model of
the original generic fiber $X_{k((t))}$; nonnormality of that fiber has
not been normalized away.

CLD 18.8.6 transports positive curvature to this nontrivially valued field;
it imposes no nontriviality restriction on the original field. At each
integral central-fiber curve $Z$, formal reduction is surjective, so take
an analytic point reducing to its generic point. The closure of that
reduction point is $Z$. CLD 17.7.10 identifies $Z$ with the restricted
model line bundle as a residual pre-model. The $d=1$ case of 18.8.4
therefore gives $\deg(\mathcal L|_Z)\ge0$. If one passes to an
algebraically closed complete extension to obtain a reduction point,
projection formula multiplies the degree by a positive field/component
degree and yields the same inequality on $Z$. Nefness concerns reduced
integral curves; nilpotent structure in the special fiber does not change
the test. Nonreduced generic fibers are not introduced. All vertical
curves have nonnegative degree, so the actual model bundle is relatively
nef. BJ Lemma 4.3 returns the metric to the genuine global PSH cone.
No BFJ smooth equivalence on a singular quotient is used.

For comparison, [GM] Theorem 1.1 identifies algebraic, formal and piecewise
linear metrics on proper schemes, including semipositivity, but fixes a
nontrivially valued field throughout. It cannot be applied directly over
$k$. On a **smooth** compactification, a rational analytic PL metric can
instead be pulled to $k((t))$, made strict integral PL by a tensor power
and powers of $t$, and treated by GM, then by residual degrees and BJ
Remark 4.19. That remark is expressly smooth and connected, with
characteristic zero and an ample class. This route proves the model-psh
claim for individual smooth gluing metrics; by itself it does not prove
the later bounded local mixed-measure construction or its comparison.

**Already-regularizable extension.** Suppose $a_i\downarrow u$ is a
supplied sequence of global AFS functions and $u\le g+B$.
For rational $c>1$ and floor $C$, choose $R$ with
$B-(c-1)R<C-1$. The compact shell $R\le g\le R+1$ has
$u-cg<C-1$. The nested closed sets
$\{a_i-cg\ge C\}$ on that shell have empty intersection, so one is
empty. This uses compactness, not Dini for a singular limit. A whole tail
therefore satisfies the same collar inequality. Formula (7.1) yields
genuine global model-psh $\psi_i$ decreasing to
$\max(u-cg,C)$, extended constantly at the boundary. Global decreasing
closure makes that truncation genuine psh. Let $C\downarrow-\infty$.
If $u$ is finite at a point of $Y$, the limit is not identically
$-\infty$ and is genuine global psh in class $c\theta$.

BJ 12.18 supplies decreasing sequential global model approximation
without EP; uniform approximation of nef models by ample FS metrics,
with summable error tails, gives decreasing rational FS approximation
in the rational class. Restrict to $Y$ and add $cg$. Add a complete very
ample system with sufficiently low rational weights so each metric is
unchanged; compactness bounds the relative norms of the added sections.
The ratios by the distinguished power of $s_0$ generate $k[Y]$, and its
own section gives a constant floor. Thus the restrictions are actual AFS.
This proves the legitimate already-regularizable 3.25 antecedent and
controlled approximants with $|u_i-cg|=O(1)$ individually. It proves no
arbitrary weak-input approximation by assuming such a sequence.

# 8. Hartogs coefficient expressions, with their exact extent

Let $Y$ be integral affine over the original $k$, with coordinate
exhaustion $g\ge0$, and let $D\subset(Y\times\mathbb A^1)^{\rm an}$ be
Runge and contain the zero section. Use the definition that the polynomial
hull of every compact subset of $D$ is contained in $D$. The hull is
compact: bound all coordinate norms by a compact polydisk, and intersect
the closed polynomial inequalities there. A compact polynomially convex
set inside an open set is contained, with a strict margin, in a compact
finite polynomial-inequality polytope inside that open set. To see this,
separate each point of the complement in a larger bounding polydisk by
one polynomial with rational threshold strictly between its value and the
compact supremum; use a finite subcover. The bounding coordinates provide
compactness. This also handles a compact supremum of $-\infty$.

A countable exhaustion, with hulls at each step, gives nested compact
polytopes $P_r$ and strict polynomial-inequality opens $\Omega_r$ with
$P_r\subset\Omega_{r+1}\subset P_{r+1}\subset D$ and union $D$.
We use **strict inequalities**, not the generally false identification
$\operatorname{int}\{|f|\le a\}=\{|f|<a\}$: a constant constraint can
make that identification fail. This avoids an additional literal defect
in the distance calculation. Write
$$\Omega_r=\{|G_j(y,w)|<e^{c_j}\}_j,\qquad
 G_j=\sum_{i=0}^{k_j}f_{j,i}(y)w^i,\quad c_j\in\mathbb Q.$$
On the zero-section domain $O_r=\{|f_{j,0}|<e^{c_j}\}_j$, the distance
radius of the fiber disk is determined by the Gauss sup norm, so
$$H_r(y):=-\log d_{\Omega_r}(y)
     =\max_{j,\ i\ge1}\frac{\log|f_{j,i}(y)|-c_j}{i}.\tag{8.1}$$
Indeed all disk points of radius $R$ satisfy the inequalities exactly when
the Gauss bounds $|f_{j,i}|R^i\le e^{c_j}$ permit their open-disk limits;
Gauss points show the converse for any larger radius. The constant terms
are already strictly below threshold. An absent nonconstant coefficient
contributes $-\infty$. Compactness of $P_r$ prevents all such coefficients
from vanishing on a zero-section fiber, since otherwise the entire
noncompact affine line fiber would lie in $P_r$.

The $O_r$ exhaust $Y^{\rm an}$. On any fixed compact coordinate sublevel,
a whole tail is contained in $O_r$, and the $H_r$ decrease there to
$u=-\log d_D$. Compactness of a smaller closed fiber disk inside $D$
justifies convergence of the radii, even when $d_D=\infty$ and $u=-\infty$.
Formula (8.1) is a finite maximum of rational positive log coefficients
of **global regular functions on $Y$**. It has no generator or floor
condition yet. On the whole affine space set
$$a_r=\max\{H_r,\epsilon_r g-r\},\qquad
       \epsilon_r\in\mathbb Q_{>0},\quad \epsilon_r\downarrow0.$$
Expanding the second branch supplies the original coordinates with positive
rational coefficients and the rational floor $-r$. Thus $a_r$ is actual
AFS globally. On any fixed compact tail domain these functions decrease
to $u$; no global monotonicity of the raw $H_r$ is asserted.

Suppose $u\le Ag+B$, with rational $A>0$, and $u$ is finite somewhere.
Fix rational $c>A,C$ and choose $R$ so $B-(c-A)R<C-1$. On the compact
sublevel $g\le R+1$, a tail of the $a_r$ is defined by (8.1), decreasing
to $u$. On its outer shell the compact nested-level-set argument of
Section 7 yields $a_r-cg<C$ for a whole tail. Formula (7.1) and the
verified model-nef criterion give genuine decreasing global potentials
whose limit is $\max(u-cg,C)$ on the inner sublevel and $C$ outside.
Since $u-cg<C-1$ for $g\ge R$, this is the desired whole-space
truncation. Decrease $C$ and use the nontrivial finite point, then the
actual global FS/generator correspondence of Section 7. This proves
$u$ has a global decreasing AFS regularization. It also proves local
corrected pshness directly from the common-tail local sequences (8.1).

This checks the precise polynomial coefficient use in W 5.7 and in the
Hartogs step of 6.14. It does **not** assert that the Hartogs domain of an
arbitrary weakly psh $u$ is Runge. W 6.15--6.16 is needed for that input;
representation of (8.1) alone cannot prove it.

# 9. Direct entropy supports and their global FS sequences

For $x_i=\log|f_i|\in\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}$, including an algebra-
generating list, fix positive rational $a$ with $\log(r+1)/a\le1$ and set
$$\chi(x)=a^{-1}\log(1+\sum_i e^{ax_i}).$$
It lies between $\max(0,x_i)$ and that maximum plus one. For $x_0=0$,
let $\Delta$ be the probability simplex and
$F(p)=a^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^r p_i\log p_i$, $0\log0=0$. Gibbs' inequality,
derived from $\log t\le t-1$, gives
$$\chi(x)=\max_{p\in\Delta}\{\sum_{i\ge1}p_ix_i-F(p)\}.$$
For an infinite negative coordinate restrict to its zero-coordinate face.

Take rational triangulations of $\Delta$, respecting every face and
including all vertices, with mesh tending to zero. The lower convex
polyhedral envelope of the lifted vertex values lies above $F$ by
convexity, and at most $\eta$ above it by uniform continuity and the
barycentric representation in a small simplex. Its dual is the finite
maximum of $\sum p_ix_i-F(p)$ at these vertices. Hence the error is
at most $\eta$ for **all finite $x$**, without a compact coordinate bound.
Round intercepts downward to rational values with error at most $\eta$;
keep the zero vertex intercepts unchanged. The resulting $h$ satisfies
$\chi-2\eta\le h\le\chi$ globally. The same inequalities hold on every
infinite-coordinate face by taking the face subcomplex or finite limits.
Clearing denominators converts branches to log norms of regular monomials.
Vertex branches retain zero and each original $\log|f_i|$ separately.
Thus $h$ is actual AFS. Choose error $e_m=4^{-m}$ and take
$g_m=h_m+2e_m$. Then $g_m\ge\chi+e_m$ while
$g_{m+1}\le\chi+2e_{m+1}<\chi+e_m$; it is a decreasing global AFS
sequence tending to $\chi$.

At a contact point $x$, put $p_0=1/Z$, $p_i=e^{ax_i}/Z$, $Z=1+\sum e^{ax_i}$,
and omit zero coefficients. The support is
$$\ell(x')=-a^{-1}\sum p_i\log p_i+\sum_{p_i>0}p_ix_i'.$$
It is finite at contact. The gap multiplied by $a$ is
$\sum p_i\log(p_i/p'_i)\ge0$, with equality only at $x'=x$, including
the infinite faces. To verify uniform separation, change signs to
$t_i=-x_i$. With $C=-a^{-1}\sum p_i\log p_i$ and
$D(t')=\chi(-t')-[C-\sum p_it_i']$, $D\le b$ implies
$\min t_i'\ge-(b+C)/p_0$, using $\chi\ge-\min t_i'$ when that minimum
is negative. It also gives $\sum_{p_i>0}p_it_i'\le b+C$ since $\chi\ge0$.
Thus all coordinates are bounded below and positive-support coordinates
bounded above. The remaining coordinates lie in compact intervals
$[-B,+\infty]$. The extended gap is lower semicontinuous on this compact
product (it is $+\infty$ when a positive-support coordinate is infinite).
Its sublevels are compact, with zero set precisely the contact point.
Nested sublevels therefore lie inside any prescribed contact neighborhood
for some positive $b$. This yields the needed uniform strict gap. No
claim that the gap is finite continuous at such pole coordinates is needed.

For a countable coordinate algebra enumerate its nonzero regular functions;
zero functions can be omitted, since their norms distinguish no points.
Use barriers $\rho_j$ incorporating the coordinate generators and the first
$j$ functions. Polynomial degree bounds give
$\rho_j\le C_j(v+1)$ for $v=\max(0,\log|z_i|)$.
Choose positive rational $\gamma_j$ with
$\gamma_j\rho_j\le2^{-j}(v+1)$. The series converges locally uniformly,
and
$$U_N=\sum_{j\le N}\gamma_j\rho_j+2^{-N}(v+1)$$
decreases to it. Finite positive rational combinations have global uniform
AFS approximants $W_N$ with $U_N-e_N\le W_N\le U_N$, $e_N=4^{-N}$.
Then $W_N+2e_N$ decreases to the series by the same error comparison.
Omitting one fixed summand has exactly the same tail argument.

A support has real positive logarithmic coefficients. Here is its direct
global rational sequence, including all poles. For nonzero $f_i$, choose
integers $d_i>0$ with $\log|f_i|\le d_iv$ globally. Set
$s=C+\sum\alpha_i\log|f_i|$, $\alpha_i>0$, and
$A=\sum d_i\alpha_i$. Choose positive rational $q_{i,m}\uparrow\alpha_i$,
rational $B_m\downarrow A$ strictly above $A$, and rational $C_m\downarrow C$.
Put $b_m=B_m-\sum d_iq_{i,m}>0$ and
$$s_m=C_m+\sum q_{i,m}\log|f_i|+b_mv.$$
Away from poles,
$$s_m-s=(C_m-C)+\sum(\alpha_i-q_{i,m})(d_iv-\log|f_i|)+(B_m-A)v\ge0,$$
$$s_m-s_{m+1}=(C_m-C_{m+1})+(B_m-B_{m+1})v
             +\sum(q_{i,m+1}-q_{i,m})(d_iv-\log|f_i|)\ge0.$$
At a pole both have value $-\infty$; no subtraction is used there.
With rational $\epsilon_m\downarrow0$, define
$$H_m=\max(s_m,\epsilon_mv-m).$$
Both branches decrease to $s$, including at a pole. Expanding $v$ and
clearing rational powers gives regular monomial log branches, while the
second branch explicitly retains each original coordinate generator and
the floor $-m$. Thus $H_m$ is actual AFS. The empty support case is a
constant and has the same floor/generator construction.

Combine this support sequence with the decreasing AFS sequence of the
remaining series, retaining single-generator branches under rational sums.
The resulting $\rho'$ is global upper-growth regularizable, finite at
contact, agrees there with $\rho$, and satisfies $\rho'\le\rho-\delta$
outside a prescribed neighborhood. The initial seminorm topology supplies
a finite-list tropical neighborhood at contact; the enumeration includes
it. This proves the exact supporting construction of W 6.7 independently
of its unidentified APW supporting antecedents and independently of a
singular PL-exhaustion theorem.

# 10. Pole-order localization and the nodal obstruction

Let $R$ be integral affine over trivially valued $k$, $f\ne0$, $L$ a
nonempty compact subset of $Y_f^{\rm an}$, and $x$ its $R$-polynomial
hull point in $Y_f^{\rm an}$. Choose $M$ with $|\log|f||\le M$ on
$L\cup\{x\}$. Let $u\in\operatorname{AFS}(R)$, $h\in R_f$, and suppose
$$a\log|h|+b\le\max(u,-c\log|f|)+O(1),\qquad a,c>0.$$
Then
$$a\log|h(x)|+b\le\sup_L(a\log|h|+b)+2cM.\tag{10.1}$$

Take the finite normalization $\bar R$ in the common fraction field.
At every prime divisor $D$ of $\operatorname{Spec}\bar R$, evaluate the
growth inequality on $\tau\operatorname{ord}_D$, $\tau>0$. Elements of
$R$ have nonnegative order, so $u$ stays between its finite floor and a
fixed finite upper bound. All these field valuations belong to $Y_f^{\rm an}$.
Dividing by $\tau$ gives
$$a\max(-\operatorname{ord}_D h,0)\le c\operatorname{ord}_D f$$
when there is a pole; no pole is possible if the right side is zero.
Choose positive integers $p,q$ with $q/p>c/a$. Then $z=h^pf^q$ has
nonnegative order at every divisor of the normal affine algebra $\bar R$,
so its codimension-one intersection description gives $z\in\bar R$.
It is integral over $R$ and belongs to $R_f$. The finite algebra
$B=R[z]$ has $B_f=R_f$; the finite module $B/R$ is killed by one $f^N$.
Consequently for every $\ell\ge1$,
$$z^\ell f^N=h^{p\ell}f^{q\ell+N}\in R.$$
Apply the genuine $R$-polynomial hull inequality to this regular function,
then multiply logarithms by $a/(p\ell)$ and use the two bounds by $M$:
$$a\log|h(x)|+b\le\sup_L(a\log|h|+b)
                         +2a(q/p+N/(p\ell))M.$$
Let $\ell\to\infty$, then rational $q/p\downarrow c/a$.
If $h(x)=0$ the conclusion is immediate; if $h=0$ on all of $L$, the
same polynomial-hull inequality forces $h(x)=0$. This includes the
$-\infty$ endpoint. Normalization is only used to count actual poles;
the hull is always tested on functions of the original nonnormal $R$.
Taking a finite maximum gives the FS version of (10.1).

The printed denominator claim in W 5.11 is false: take
$f=z^2,g=z,h=g/f=z^{-1},a=2,c=1,u=0$. Then
$\max(2\log|h|,0)=\max(-\log|f|,0)$, but $ar=2>1$ although
$g\notin(f)$. The conclusion (10.1) is not refuted; actual pole orders
repair its proof. To deduce W 5.10 for a regularizable $v\le u+O(1)$,
one additionally needs controlled approximants with growth bounded by
$2\max(u,-c\log|f|)+O(1)$. For such a supplied sequence the error is
$4cM$, and the decreasing usc compact suprema converge to $\sup_Lv$:
if their suprema stayed above a larger level, their nested closed
superlevel sets would have nonempty intersection. Let $c\downarrow0$.
This compact argument uses no invalid uniform Dini convergence to a
singular function. The required controlled extension is a separate input
in a full Runge reconstruction.

The genuine nodal obstruction is unconditional. Let $C$ be an irreducible
rational projective nodal curve and $q:\mathbb P^1\to C$ its normalization,
with node preimages $p_1\ne p_2$. For $d=\deg L>0$, the divisorial
$v=\operatorname{ord}_{p_1}$ downstairs has finite dual energy by BJ 11.1.
Blow up $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1$ at $(p_1,0)$, with exceptional $E$.
The model bundle
$$p^*q^*L+d\mathcal X_0-dE$$
has degree zero on the strict central component and degree $d$ on the
exceptional component, both of multiplicity one. These are all vertical
curves, so the bundle is relatively nef; (3.1) gives raw MA $d\delta_v$.
The bounded model potential is $d\max(1-s,0)$ on the $p_1$ ray and
$d$ on other rays. Its two node-preimage endpoints have values $0,d$.
If a downstairs finite-energy solution existed, normalization pullback
would preserve energy and genuine pushforward would preserve its measure.
The function-field valuation $v$ has one analytic lift; positivity and
fixed mass therefore force the upstairs target to be the same Dirac.
Upstairs uniqueness gives the displayed potential plus a constant.
A downstairs pullback has equal values at $p_1,p_2$, a contradiction.
This is an actual nonsolvable finite-dual-energy target for the genuine
operator outside unibranchness, not a change of the problem's target.

# 11. Remaining dependencies and normalization

The preceding repairs do not complete the new singular envelope source.
The following are indispensable unresolved steps in this reconstruction.

1. **Local positivity and smooth weak upper-growth base.** The GM/CLD/smooth-BJ route in
   Section 7 handles individual continuous rational PL metrics on a
   smooth compactification when their local curvature positivity has
   actually been established. Section 5 proves ambient-convex PL
   positivity and applies Wanner in its exact field scope; the full
   corrected local class, defined using functions only on tropical images,
   still needs a compatible current-positivity argument or an independent
   curve slope proof. The finite norm tests themselves are actual FS and
   satisfy the stronger sufficient condition. To deduce the arbitrary locally psh smooth
   affine theorem, direct truncation $\max(u-cAg,C)$ gives a common
   constant boundary neighborhood and local pshness. It still needs a
   complete proof that this bounded locally psh function is global.
   Wu 4.17 reaches that conclusion through 4.2 and 4.16. Here the exact
   bounded-local mixed-measure independence on overlaps, compatibility
   with CLD 18.4.7, total mass, localization, integration by parts, and
   local comparison have not all been reconstructed. Positive currents
   and a model-nef criterion alone do not prove them. The corrected
   negative-floor local extension must also retain the actual background
   difference: if $\theta=\omega+dd^cp$, then the local formula is
   $\Phi_\omega=\Psi/A+p$, $g_\omega=v-p+(M+1)/A$, with
   $\Phi_\omega+g_\omega=u/A$. Merely equating $\theta$ and $\omega$
   is invalid. Accordingly arbitrary smooth **weak** upper-growth
   regularization and the principal-open removable extension are not
   asserted as unconditional new theorems here.

2. **Rossi and the Runge induction.** Section 8 proves the regularization
   consequence when the Hartogs domain is already Runge. W 5.6's Rossi
   proof and 6.15's splitting argument also glue on arbitrary analytic
   neighborhoods or on disjoint analytic pieces $W_1,W_2$. Their inputs
   are not the single global algebraic FS expression of Section 7.
   A separate rational model-PL bump/gluing argument, or an independent
   polynomial maximum proof, must verify those applications. The
   continuous integer trace in W 6.15 Step 3 is locally constant, but a
   single $\gamma$ on the whole chosen open needs connectedness or an
   explicit localization; continuity alone does not imply it. The
   $\gamma=1$ denominators in its Step 4 need the zero-other-weight case
   treated separately. These are repairable-looking obligations, not
   counterexamples to the desired theorem. No complete replacement of
   Rossi, local projection 6.16, or the full 6.14 induction is claimed.
   The norm equality of Section 6 cannot construct its own smooth weak
   regularization antecedent.

3. **Actual singular envelopes.** W 7.1 descends a smooth-resolution
   envelope using connected fibers and W 2.24, then invokes weak/global
   identification 6.5, which invokes the unreconstructed 6.3 induction.
   The zero-psh multiplier in the proof of W 2.25 must be $m\phi$ for
   $\phi\le0$, not $-m\phi$. Resolution connectedness and usc descent
   by themselves do not prove global psh descent. Therefore the
   countable rational singular envelope base remains unproved here.

4. **Onto normalization and cardinality passage.** For the requested
   geometrically unibranch $X$, finite normalization is degree-one
   birational with singleton geometric fibers and purely inseparable
   residue extensions. In characteristic zero those are equalities.
   It is a universal homeomorphism and its analytification is a
   homeomorphism: at each point the unchanged residue field has exactly
   one extended seminorm. These topological facts do not give onto PSH.
   The unconditional identities are
   $$E_{q^*\omega}(q^*\phi)=E_\omega(\phi),\qquad
   q_*\bigwedge_i(q^*\omega_i+dd^cq^*\phi_i)
      =\bigwedge_i(\omega_i+dd^c\phi_i),$$
   by BJ 7.2 and 7.19, hence one-way $\mathcal E^1$ preservation.
   BJ 5.13 makes PSH pullback onto only when larger classes approaching
   $\omega$ have EP. Under that antecedent the logarithmic-ideal
   corrections and increasing closure descend every upstairs potential;
   equal energies then give onto $\mathcal E^1$. The operator and all
   dual-energy targets correspond after onto descent. Its EP antecedent
   has not been proved here. Neither analytical homeomorphism nor
   pullback identities remove it.

If the countable rational envelope base is proved, the conceivable
arbitrary-cardinality passage uses finite model/obstacle/competitor data
over countable algebraically closed subfields, faithful descent, relative
integral closure in the **given** nonnormal generic fiber, vertical
$R_1/S_2$, component and multiplicity preservation, genuine mixed-measure
pushforward, contact orthogonality, and BJ global domination for genuine
global competitors. No singular BFJ Dirac equation may replace these.
Real ample classes require approximation from above and BJ's actual class
closure. This paper has not reconstructed all those passages as an
unconditional singular theorem; the missing base already blocks them.

The conditional theorem in Section 3 retains all target fields and ample
line bundles, and BJ's ample real numerical classes. Its arbitrary-support
targets are the actual Radon measures (3.3), with mass $V$, not skeleton
inverse measures. Existence in the smooth domain is supplied by BJ 5.20
and 12.9. The unconditional singular/unibranch theorem, global envelopes,
and onto normalization required by P16.1 remain unproved. No unrelated
valuation, characteristic, nonample, proper-nonprojective, or genuinely
analytic equation axis is inferred from the field-general algebraic
lemmas in this paper.
