\begin{abstract}
We propose a direct extension of the genuine continuous mixed
Monge--Ampere operator to actual finite-energy psh metrics on a smooth
projective variety over any complete characteristic-zero
non-Archimedean field. The dimension is arbitrary and trivial,
discrete and nondiscrete valuations are included. Ordered continuous
energy gaps control dense scalar tests; polarization then gives a
positive mixed limit. An averaging estimate proves integrability of
every finite-energy test. A second averaging estimate controls the
error of unbounded tests uniformly along decreasing nets, yielding
joint weighted continuity and the genuine extended-energy formula.
All potentials are actual all-point psh endpoints. The paper supplies
no general-field equation existence theorem, strict locality or
comparison, and assumes no envelope continuity. Every new proof
awaits independent verification.
\end{abstract}

\section{The actual cone and the proposed operator theorem}

Let $K$ be a complete characteristic-zero non-Archimedean real-valued
field, $X/K$ smooth, geometrically integral and projective of dimension
$n\geq1$, and $L$ an actual ample rational line bundle. Write
$T=X^{\an}$ and $V=(L^n)>0$. The space $T$ is compact Hausdorff.
Fix a continuous psh metric $\psi$ on $L$. The term psh means exactly
the decreasing-Fubini--Study-net definition of
\cite[Definition 7.2]{BE} on the fixed rational bundle. We exclude the
identically $-\infty$ metric. Scalar potentials $u$ mean absolute
metrics $\psi+u$ evaluated at every point, including supported points;
$u=-\infty$ is a literal value. Such potentials are upper
semicontinuous and bounded above. Denote the continuous ones by
$\CPSH$ and the actual singular cone by $\PSH$.

For continuous potentials put $\theta_u=\ddc(\psi+u)$ and use the
genuine continuous mixed measures of \cite[Theorem 8.4]{BE}. Wedges
below are measures on $T$ with the fundamental class of $X$ implicit.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \begin{split}
 E(u)&=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^n
             \int_T u\,\theta_u^j\theta_0^{n-j}
                     \quad(u\in\CPSH),\\
 E(u)&=\inf\{E(v):v\in\CPSH,\ v\geq u\}
                     \quad(u\in\PSH),\\
 \E^1&=\{u\in\PSH:E(u)>-\infty\}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Continuous majorants exist by the defining approximation. The
extension takes values in $[-\infty,\infty)$ and retains the actual
potential as its argument. It does not define any measure by naming
a prescribed target.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For $u_1,\ldots,u_n\in\E^1$ there is a canonical positive Radon
measure $\MA(u_1,\ldots,u_n)$ of raw mass $V$. It agrees with the
genuine continuous mixed operator, is symmetric and affine in the
curvature of each slot, and is the weak limit along every simultaneous
continuous psh decreasing approximation of the actual endpoints.
No support restriction is imposed. Write $\MA(u)=\MA(u,\ldots,u)$.

Every $w\in\E^1$ is integrable against every such mixed output. On
any common directed index set, if $u_{r,i}\downarrow u_r$ for
$1\leq r\leq n$ and $w_i\downarrow w$, all in $\E^1$ with the
indicated endpoints in $\E^1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted}
 \begin{split}
 \MA(u_{1,i},\ldots,u_{n,i})&\longrightarrow
                   \MA(u_1,\ldots,u_n)\quad\hbox{weakly},\\
 \int_T w_i\,\MA(u_{1,i},\ldots,u_{n,i})&\longrightarrow
                   \int_T w\,\MA(u_1,\ldots,u_n).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The genuine energy identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sing-energy}
 E(u)=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^n
        \int_Tu\,\MA(\underbrace{u,\ldots,u}_{j},
                       \underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n-j}).
\end{equation}
For $u,v\in\E^1$, the actual segment $u_t=(1-t)u+tv$ belongs to
$\E^1$, its energy is a polynomial of degree at most $n+1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derivative}
 \frac{d}{dt}E(u_t)=\int_T(v-u)\,\MA(u_t)
                      \qquad(0\leq t\leq1).
\end{equation}
At endpoints the derivative has its one-sided meaning. The difference
in this integral is evaluated on a common finite-value full-measure
set, without subtracting infinite values.

Changing the continuous psh reference leaves the absolute finite-energy
class and operator unchanged. For every complete valued extension
$F/K$, pullback preserves energy and finite energy, and the projection
$p:X_F^{\an}\to T$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:basechange}
 p_*\MA(u_{1,F},\ldots,u_{n,F})=\MA(u_1,\ldots,u_n).
\end{equation}
The raw mass remains $V$; no field-degree factor is introduced.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is an operator statement. It asserts neither attainment
for arbitrary finite-energy targets nor uniqueness of solutions. Its
original P16.4 scope audit is given at the end.

\section{Continuous estimates and compact Radon facts}

The precise continuous inputs are the actual psh closure and smooth
regularization in \cite[Section 7]{BE}, the complete continuous
mixed-operator construction in \cite[Section 8.1]{BE}, and continuous
energy in \cite[Definition 9.13 and Proposition 9.14]{BE}.
In particular the relative cocycle gives, for continuous $u,v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-gap}
 E(v)-E(u)=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^n
                    \int_T(v-u)\,\theta_v^j\theta_u^{n-j}.
\end{equation}
Continuous energy is monotone, satisfies $E(u+c)=E(u)+cV$, and is
Lipschitz in uniform distance with constant $V$.

Here and below positive real convex combinations of continuous fixed
bundle psh metrics are allowed. For rational coefficients the tensor
product of defining FS systems gives the assertion, followed by
uniform closure. Approximating real coefficients by rationals gives
the continuous assertion. The continuous mixed operator extends the
same affine curvature identity by uniform continuity.

For any product $Q$ of $n-1$ continuous psh curvatures and for scalar
differences of continuous psh metrics, define
\[
 D_Q(f,g)=-\int_T f\,\ddc g\,Q.
\]
The differences may lie on other semiample rational bundles. This is
the signed dpsh calculus of \cite[Section 8.1]{BE}. The form is
symmetric, positive semidefinite and satisfies Cauchy--Schwarz.
To check the latter assertions, uniformly regularize the finitely
many metrics by smooth semipositive metrics using Theorem 7.14 of
that source. For smooth data Stokes and positivity give
$D_Q(f,f)=\int df\wedge d^cf\,Q\geq0$ and symmetry. Analytification
is boundaryless and projectivity supplies compact support for this
Stokes application. The continuous mixed signed measures have
uniformly bounded total variation, bounded by the sum of their
positive intersection masses. Thus for $f_a\to f$ uniformly and
signed measures $\lambda_a\to\lambda$ weakly,
\[
 \left|\int(f_a-f)\lambda_a\right|
       \leq\|f_a-f\|_\infty\|\lambda_a\|_{\rm TV}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \int f(\lambda_a-\lambda)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This passes symmetry and positivity to $D_Q$. Positivity of
$D_Q(f+sg,f+sg)$ for real $s$ gives Cauchy--Schwarz. All expressions
are the representation-independent continuous dpsh operator; no
singular measure is used here.

It also proves continuous energy concavity. Along a continuous psh
segment with difference $d=v-u$, its second derivative is
\[
 \frac{d^2}{dt^2}E((1-t)u+tv)
       =-nD_{\theta_{(1-t)u+tv}^{n-1}}(d,d)\leq0.
\]
The first derivative and the polynomial identity follow from the
continuous energy calculus and multilinearity. Thus concavity is
derived from smooth positivity, rather than attributed to a property
not listed in Proposition 9.14.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:weak-gap}
For every $f\in\mathrm{PL}_{\mathbb R}(T)$ there is $C_f\geq0$,
independent of continuous fixed-$L$ psh $u,v$, such that $u\leq v$
implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-gap}
 \left|\int_T f\,(\theta_v^n-\theta_u^n)\right|
       \leq\sqrt{n(n+1)C_f\,[E(v)-E(u)]}.
\end{equation}
These real PL functions are uniformly dense in $C(T)$, also at a
trivial valuation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cite[Lemma 5.17 and Theorem 5.18]{BE}, write $f=h-k$ for two
continuous FS metrics on a common ample bundle $A$; their theorem
with real weights gives the stated density without requiring a
nontrivial absolute value. Put
$C_f=2\|f\|_\infty(A\cdot L^{n-1})$.
Let $\delta=v-u\geq0$ and
$Q_j=\theta_u^j\theta_v^{n-1-j}$ for $0\leq j<n$.
The two positive measures $\ddc h\,Q_j$ and $\ddc k\,Q_j$ have
mass $(A\cdot L^{n-1})$, so
\[
 0\leq D_{Q_j}(f,f)\leq C_f.
\]
Factoring the curvature difference and integrating by parts gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}D_{Q_j}(\delta,\delta)
   &=\int_T\delta(\theta_u^n-\theta_v^n)\\
   &\leq\int_T\delta\theta_u^n
    \leq(n+1)[E(v)-E(u)],
 \end{split}
\]
where the final inequality is the $j=0$ term of
\eqref{eq:energy-gap}; every term there is nonnegative.
Consequently the absolute value of
$\sum_jD_{Q_j}(f,\delta)=-\int f(\theta_v^n-\theta_u^n)$
is at most
\[
 \sum_j\sqrt{D_{Q_j}(f,f)D_{Q_j}(\delta,\delta)}
 \leq\sqrt{nC_f\sum_jD_{Q_j}(\delta,\delta)},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:weak-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:energy-net}
If actual psh $u_i\downarrow u$ and $u$ is actual psh, then
$E(u_i)\downarrow E(u)$, including the value $-\infty$.
Finite positive convex combinations of actual finite-energy
potentials are actual finite-energy potentials, and extended energy
is concave on this cone.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Monotonicity gives a decreasing limit at least $E(u)$. For any
continuous majorant $v\geq u$ and $\epsilon>0$, the closed compact
sets $\{u_i\geq v+\epsilon\}$ decrease with empty intersection.
The finite intersection property gives an index after which
$u_i<v+\epsilon$ everywhere. Hence $E(u_i)\leq E(v)+\epsilon V$
on this tail. Infimizing over $v$ and then $\epsilon$ gives the reverse
inequality, including $E(u)=-\infty$.

The same compact argument applies when a decreasing net has
identically $-\infty$ limit: each constant potential $-M$ is then
a continuous majorant of that limit, and the tail lies below
$-M+\epsilon$. Its energies consequently tend to $-\infty$ as
$M\to\infty$.

For finitely many actual endpoints choose their continuous decreasing
psh nets. Positive convex combinations form a product-directed
continuous decreasing net with the asserted all-point limit.
The actual psh decreasing-closure property in
\cite[Lemma 7.4]{BE} puts that limit in the possibly identically
$-\infty$ decreasing-closure cone. Continuous concavity bounds its
approximating energies below by a finite convex combination of the
endpoint energies. The preceding constant-majorant argument excludes
an identically $-\infty$ limit. It is therefore in the actual cone.
Continuous concavity and the just-proved energy limit give
$E(\sum_r a_ru_r)\geq\sum_r a_rE(u_r)>-\infty$ for
$a_r\geq0$, $\sum a_r=1$. The same passage proves concavity.
Zero coefficients are omitted, so $0\cdot(-\infty)$ is never used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:radon}
Let $\mu$ be a finite Radon measure on a compact Hausdorff space.
For a directed increasing net of nonnegative lower semicontinuous
functions $g_i$ with supremum $g$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lsc-net}
 \int g\,d\mu=\sup_i\int g_i\,d\mu.
\end{equation}
If finite positive Radon measures $\mu_a\to\mu$ weakly, then for
every nonnegative lower semicontinuous $g$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:portmanteau}
 \int g\,d\mu\leq\liminf_a\int g\,d\mu_a.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each $t\geq0$, the open set $\{g>t\}$ is the union of the
directed open sets $\{g_i>t\}$. Inner regularity and finite subcovers
of compact subsets imply
$\mu\{g>t\}=\sup_i\mu\{g_i>t\}$. Approximate the layer-cake
integral of $g$ from below by a finite lower step sum, truncating its
height first. For its finitely many levels choose one common index
making all corresponding open-set masses as close as desired to
their suprema. The same lower step sum for $g_i$ proves
\eqref{eq:lsc-net}, even if the integral is infinite.

For \eqref{eq:portmanteau}, compact Hausdorff normality and Radon
inner regularity give
$\int g\,d\mu=\sup\{\int h\,d\mu: h\in C(T),\ 0\leq h\leq g\}$.
One direct verification chooses inner compact subsets at finitely
many strict open levels of $g$ and continuous functions supported
in these open sets and equal to one on the chosen compacts. For
equally spaced levels of spacing $\eta$, their sum multiplied by
$\eta$ is at most $g$ at each point. Such sums approximate the
truncated layer integral, then the full integral. Each fixed
continuous minorant $h$ passes through weak convergence and is
bounded above by $g$ for every $\mu_a$. Supremizing gives
\eqref{eq:portmanteau}. No metrizability or arbitrary measurable-net
monotone convergence is used.
\end{proof}

\section{A canonical pure measure and positive polarization}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:pure}
For every actual $u\in\E^1$, any continuous psh net
$u_a\downarrow u$ has a unique weak curvature limit of raw mass
$V$, independent of the approximation. Denote it by $\MA(u)$.
The estimate \eqref{eq:weak-gap} extends to ordered $u\leq v$ in
$\E^1$, and $u_i\downarrow u$ in $\E^1$ implies
$\MA(u_i)\to\MA(u)$ weakly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The energies $E(u_a)$ decrease to the finite number $E(u)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-net}. On each fixed real PL test,
Lemma~\ref{lem:weak-gap} makes the continuous measures a Cauchy net:
for two tail indices use an index dominating both and the triangle
inequality. Positivity and their common mass $V$ bound these
functionals on $C(T)$ uniformly. PL density therefore gives a limit
on every continuous test. Its positive bounded linear functional
defines a positive Radon measure by Riesz, with mass $V$.

For two approximation nets $u_a$ and $v_b$ of the same actual
endpoint, $m_{a,b}=\max(u_a,v_b)$ is a continuous psh decreasing
product net with the same endpoint. Its energies also tend to $E(u)$.
The ordered estimate compares each original measure to that of
$m_{a,b}$ with an error tending to zero. Hence both limits agree.
When $u$ is continuous the constant approximation proves agreement
with its genuine continuous measure.

For ordered singular $u\leq v$, choose continuous approximations
$u_a\downarrow u$, $v_b\downarrow v$. Then
$\max(u_a,v_b)\downarrow v$. Apply the continuous ordered estimate
between $u_a$ and this maximum and pass to their weak limits and
finite energy limits. This gives \eqref{eq:weak-gap} for $u,v$.
For $u_i\downarrow u$ in $\E^1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-net}
and that singular estimate make all PL-test differences tend to zero.
Uniform mass and PL density give weak convergence on $C(T)$.
\end{proof}

For a nonempty subset $I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}$ set
$u_I=|I|^{-1}\sum_{r\in I}u_r$, which belongs to $\E^1$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-net}. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polarization}
 \MA(u_1,\ldots,u_n)=\frac1{n!}
    \sum_{\varnothing\ne I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}
       (-1)^{n-|I|}|I|^n\MA(u_I).
\end{equation}
For continuous inputs this is the genuine mixed measure: expand
$\theta_{u_I}=|I|^{-1}\sum_{r\in I}\theta_{u_r}$. Inclusion--exclusion
cancels every monomial missing one of the $n$ labels; the surviving
product has coefficient $n!$. This also proves
$\sum_{I\ne\varnothing}(-1)^{n-|I|}|I|^n=n!$.

For simultaneous continuous decreasing approximations of the actual
$u_r$, each subset-average measure tends weakly to $\MA(u_I)$.
At every stage the expression in \eqref{eq:polarization} is a positive
continuous mixed measure of mass $V$. Thus its limit is positive and
has mass $V$. This proves positivity, approximation independence
and continuous agreement without assuming positivity of a signed
formula by itself. Passing the continuous identities proves symmetry
and affine curvature dependence in every slot. For arbitrary singular
$u_{r,i}\downarrow u_r$ the subset averages decrease to their actual
finite-energy endpoints; Proposition~\ref{prop:pure} and the finite
formula give mixed weak convergence.

\section{Every finite-energy test is integrable}

A common constant change of reference makes any fixed finite family
of scalar potentials and tails of their defining continuous nets
nonpositive. We may therefore establish integrability under that
normalization; bounded constant changes do not affect it. Write
$B(u)=-E(u)\geq0$ for $u\leq0$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:integrability}
For nonpositive $w,u_1,\ldots,u_n\in\E^1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integrability-bound}
 \int_T(-w)\,\MA(u_1,\ldots,u_n)
   \leq \frac{(n+1)^{n+1}}{n!}
                      \left(B(w)+\sum_{r=1}^nB(u_r)\right)<\infty.
\end{equation}
In particular every mixed output gives zero mass to the pole set
of every actual finite-energy test.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First assume all potentials continuous. Put
$z=(w+u_1+\cdots+u_n)/(n+1)$. Concavity gives
$B(z)\leq[B(w)+\sum_rB(u_r)]/(n+1)$.
Every term in the formula for $B(z)$ is nonnegative. Its top term
$\frac1{n+1}\int(-z)\theta_z^n$ contains the term
\[
 \frac{n!}{(n+1)^{n+2}}
        \int_T(-w)\theta_{u_1}\cdots\theta_{u_n}
\]
with the displayed coefficient: one factor comes from $-z$, $n$
from the curvature average, and one from the energy denominator.
Retaining this nonnegative term proves
\eqref{eq:integrability-bound} for continuous data.

Fix a nonpositive continuous test $w_k$ and let continuous curvature
approximants of the actual $u_r$ decrease to them. Mixed weak
convergence and finite energy convergence give the same inequality
with $w_k$ and the singular $u_r$. Now take nonpositive continuous
$w_k\downarrow w$. Its costs satisfy $B(w_k)\leq B(w)$.
The functions $-w_k$ increase, are nonnegative and continuous,
and have supremum $-w$. Lemma~\ref{lem:radon} on the single mixed
limit measure gives \eqref{eq:integrability-bound}. Since $w$
is bounded above, this proves full absolute integrability. Its
$-\infty$ set has zero measure as asserted.
\end{proof}

No uncountable intersection of full-measure sets is asserted. Each
finite collection of tests has its own common finite-value set of
full measure, which is all that the ensuing formulas require.

\section{The uniform error of a singular test}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:test-error}
Let $u,w\in\E^1$ and let continuous psh $w_k\downarrow w$.
For every fixed $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:test-error}
 \int_T(w_k-w)\,\MA(u)
 \leq A_n\left[
 E\left(\frac{u+w_k}{2}\right)
       -E\left(\frac{u+w}{2}\right)\right],
 \qquad A_n=2^{n+1}(n+1).
\end{equation}
Both sides are defined and nonnegative. Differences of potentials
inside integrals mean their finite-value representatives.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First suppose $u$ is continuous and take a tail index $j\geq k$.
Put $h_k=(u+w_k)/2$, $h_j=(u+w_j)/2$; these are continuous psh
and $h_k\geq h_j$. The top term of \eqref{eq:energy-gap} gives
\[
 (n+1)[E(h_k)-E(h_j)]
       \geq\int_T(h_k-h_j)\MA(h_k).
\]
The positive curvature expansion of
$\MA(h_k)=((\theta_u+\theta_{w_k})/2)^n$
gives $\MA(h_k)\geq2^{-n}\MA(u)$. Since
$h_k-h_j=(w_k-w_j)/2\geq0$, this proves
\eqref{eq:test-error} with $w_j$ in place of $w$.
Let $j$ increase along its directed tail. The functions $w_k-w_j$
are increasing nonnegative continuous functions with supremum
$w_k-w$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:radon} passes the left side to its
integral. Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-net} passes $E(h_j)$ to
$E((u+w)/2)$, finite by convexity. This proves the claim for
continuous $u$.

For actual singular $u$, take continuous $u_a\downarrow u$.
The just-proved inequality applies to every $u_a$. The nonnegative
function $g=w_k-w$ is lower semicontinuous, possibly infinite on
the pole set of $w$. It is integrable against all measures involved
by Proposition~\ref{prop:integrability}. Weak convergence
$\MA(u_a)\to\MA(u)$ and \eqref{eq:portmanteau} give a lower bound
for the liminf of these left sides. Both energies on the right
decrease to their finite actual endpoint energies by
Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-net}, so their difference has the asserted
limit. This proves \eqref{eq:test-error} in general.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:weighted-pure}
If $u_i\downarrow u$ in $\E^1$ with $u\in\E^1$, then for
every fixed $w\in\E^1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-weight}
 \int_Tw\,\MA(u_i)\longrightarrow\int_Tw\,\MA(u).
\end{equation}
If also $w_i\downarrow w$ in $\E^1$ with $w\in\E^1$ on the
same index set, the same conclusion holds with $w_i$ on the left.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose continuous psh $w_k\downarrow w$. The product-directed
potentials $(u_i+w_k)/2$ decrease to $(u+w)/2\in\E^1$.
For any $\epsilon>0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-net} gives $i_0,k_0$
such that
\[
 0\leq E((u_i+w_k)/2)-E((u+w)/2)<\epsilon
               \quad(i\geq i_0,\ k\geq k_0).
\]
Also $E((u_i+w)/2)\geq E((u+w)/2)$. Hence
Lemma~\ref{lem:test-error} bounds
$\int(w_{k_0}-w)\MA(u_i)$ by $A_n\epsilon$ on the tail,
and gives the same bound for the limit measure. For the fixed
continuous test $w_{k_0}$, weak convergence gives convergence of
its integrals. The triangle inequality and these two error bounds
prove \eqref{eq:fixed-weight} as $\epsilon\downarrow0$.

For the moving weights, fix $k$ and $\eta>0$. The closed compact
sets $\{w_i\geq w_k+\eta\}$ decrease to an empty intersection
because $w\leq w_k$. Thus $w_i\leq w_k+\eta$ on a tail, and
\[
 0\leq\int_T(w_i-w)\MA(u_i)
       \leq\int_T(w_k-w)\MA(u_i)+\eta V.
\]
Choose $k=k_0$ in the preceding uniform bound, then take a common
tail with the compactness bound. The right side is at most
$A_n\epsilon+\eta V$. All differences are legitimate finite
integrals by Proposition~\ref{prop:integrability}. This proves
that the moving-weight error tends to zero, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

Apply this proposition to each subset-average net in
\eqref{eq:polarization}. Its finite signed coefficients are fixed,
and all test integrals exist. Summing therefore proves the full
mixed weighted law \eqref{eq:weighted}. The argument works for
arbitrary directed nets; sequences or a single skeleton were never
substituted for them.

\section{Energy, reference covariance and field extension}

Choose continuous $u_i\downarrow u\in\E^1$. In each term of the
continuous energy identity take $u_i$ as moving weight, $j$ slots
equal to $u_i$ and $n-j$ slots equal to the reference potential zero.
The mixed weighted law and Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-net} give
\eqref{eq:sing-energy}. This proves that the previously defined
majorant energy is the energy of the genuine limiting operators.

For the segment of two actual finite-energy potentials $u,v$,
convexity was proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-net}. Let continuous
$u_a\downarrow u$, $v_b\downarrow v$. At every product-directed
stage the continuous energy on their affine segment is a polynomial
of degree at most $n+1$, and its derivative is the integral of
$v_b-u_a$ against its pure measure. Each coefficient of this derivative
is a fixed binomial factor times an integral with weight $u_a$ or
$v_b$ and curvature slots chosen from $u_a,v_b$. The full mixed
weighted law passes each such coefficient to its finite singular
integral. The constant coefficient of the energy converges to
$E(u)$. There are finitely many coefficients, so integration of
these coefficient limits proves a limiting polynomial whose value
at any $t$ is $E(u_t)$, by decreasing energy continuity. Its derivative
is precisely the polarized integral in \eqref{eq:derivative}.
This is a finite polynomial argument, not an exchange of arbitrary
limits and derivatives. Proposition~\ref{prop:integrability} permits
$v-u$ on the common finite-value full-measure set of each output.

Let $\psi'$ be another continuous psh reference on $L$, and write
$b=\psi'-\psi$ and $u'=u-b$. The set of continuous absolute psh
majorants of $\psi+u=\psi'+u'$ is the same for both references.
The continuous cocycle gives
$E_{\psi'}(v-b)=E_\psi(v)-E_\psi(b)$ for every majorant.
Infimizing gives
\[
 E_{\psi'}(u')=E_\psi(u)-E_\psi(b).
\]
Thus the absolute finite-energy class is identical. Its absolute
continuous approximation nets are also identical; their genuine
curvature measures do not depend on reference. The canonical limits
and all mixed operators are therefore identical. This justifies the
constant normalizations used above as well as general reference
covariance.

For a complete valued extension $F/K$, the standard analytic
projection is continuous and surjective. Pulling back the defining
continuous FS nets gives the actual endpoint $u_F=u\circ p$.
The continuous energies agree under extension by
\cite[Proposition 9.14]{BE}. Decreasing energy continuity on both
spaces gives
\[
 E_{\psi_F}(u_F)=\lim_aE_{\psi_F}(u_{a,F})
              =\lim_aE_\psi(u_a)=E_\psi(u).
\]
In particular $u_F\in\E^1$. The genuine continuous mixed
pushforward law of \cite[Proposition 8.3 and Section 8.1]{BE}
passes to weak limits by testing with $f\circ p$ for $f\in C(T)$,
and proves \eqref{eq:basechange}. Intersection degree is unchanged
under extension. Neither an exact extension-supnorm identity nor
descent of arbitrary upstairs singular metrics was assumed.

\section{Zero dimension, imported scope and remaining equation inputs}

For $n=0$, the retained geometric-integrality hypothesis gives
$X=\operatorname{Spec}K$ and $T$ a single point. The actual nontrivial
psh scalars are finite constants, $V=1$, $E(u)=u$ and the operator
is its unit point mass. All weighted identities and field laws then
hold directly. The substantial argument above concerns $n\geq1$.

The imported continuous theory is used in its precise projective,
geometrically reduced, semiample rational-bundle domain; the present
smooth geometrically integral ample setting meets all those

The paper constructs a proposed arbitrary-field finite-energy mixed

\begin{abstract}
Let $K$ be any complete non-Archimedean field of characteristic zero,
including fields with nondiscrete value group, and let
$C=\widehat{\overline K}$. For a smooth geometrically integral projective
$K$-variety and an actual ample rational line bundle, we give an
all-point descent argument for Galois-invariant singular psh metrics
and their extended relative energy. The argument uses continuous
field-extension results and compact orbit maxima; it does not require
continuity of arbitrary psh envelopes. We also descend the
continuous-test energy dual by Haar averaging. These statements do not
construct the general unbounded mixed Monge--Ampere operator and do
not solve the entire original problem P16.4. All new statements in this
paper await independent verification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and the precise result}

Fix a complete non-Archimedean real-valued field $K$ of characteristic
zero. Its absolute value may be trivial, discrete, or nondiscrete.
Let $X/K$ be smooth, geometrically integral and projective of dimension
$n\geq 1$, and let $L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)\otimes\mathbb Q$ be an
actual ample rational line bundle. Set
\[
 T=X^{\an},\qquad C=\widehat{\overline K},\qquad
 Y=X_C^{\an},\qquad \pi:Y\longrightarrow T,\qquad
 G=\Gal(\overline K/K),\qquad V=(L^n)>0.
\]
The unique extension of the absolute value makes every element of $G$
an isometry of $\overline K$, hence of $C$. The induced action on $Y$
is semilinear. We use the profinite topology on $G$.

Choose a continuous psh reference metric $\psi$ on $L$, and put
$\psi_C=\pi^*\psi$. Such a reference exists: a generated positive
multiple of $L$ supplies a Fubini--Study metric. We write a metric as
$\psi+u$, so that $u$ is a scalar on the whole carrier. For brevity
$u\in\PSH_K(L;\psi)$ means that $\psi+u$ is a psh metric.
Here psh has exactly the definition in \cite[Definition 7.2]{BE}:
it is an all-point decreasing-net limit of continuous Fubini--Study
metrics on the fixed rational bundle. We exclude the identically
$-\infty$ metric; all other poles, including supported analytic points,
are retained. Constant additions are literal real constants. No torus,
skeleton, countability or support restriction is imposed.

For continuous psh $v$ define the raw relative energy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 E_K(v)=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}
 \int_T v\,(\ddc(\psi+v))^j\wedge(\ddc\psi)^{n-j}\wedge\delta_X.
\end{equation}
The continuous mixed measures here are the actual measures of
\cite[Theorem 8.4]{BE}. Their total mass is $V$. Define, for singular
$u\in\PSH_K(L;\psi)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extended}
 \begin{aligned}
 E_K(u)&=\inf\{E_K(v):v\in C(T)\cap\PSH_K(L;\psi),\ v\geq u\},\\
 \E_K^1&=\{u\in\PSH_K(L;\psi):E_K(u)>-\infty\}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
An approximating FS metric provides a continuous majorant, so the
infimum is well-defined in $[-\infty,\infty)$. Use the same definitions
over $C$ with reference $\psi_C$. This definition does not assume an
unbounded Monge--Ampere operator.

For a positive Radon measure $\mu$ on $T$ of raw mass $V$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 J_K(\mu)=\sup_{v\in C(T)\cap\PSH_K(L;\psi)}
 \left(E_K(v)-\int_T v\,d\mu\right).
\end{equation}
Constants cancel in this expression. We call this the
\emph{continuous-test dual}; no equivalence with an arbitrary-field
all-$\E^1$ integrability condition is built into the definition.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
In the stated setting the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The action $G\times Y\to Y$ is jointly continuous and
$\pi$ identifies $T$ with the compact Hausdorff quotient $Y/G$.
\item Pullback gives an order-preserving bijection
\[
 \PSH_K(L;\psi)\ \simeq\ \PSH_C(L_C;\psi_C)^G.
\]
It is a bijection of actual metrics at every point, including their
$-\infty$ values, and identifies the continuous subspaces.
\item For every $u\in\PSH_K(L;\psi)$,
\[
 E_C(\pi^*u)=E_K(u),
\]
including the value $-\infty$. Thus pullback identifies $\E_K^1$
with the invariant part of $\E_C^1$.
\item Every positive Radon measure $\mu$ on $T$ has a unique invariant
positive Radon lift $\widetilde\mu$ on $Y$. The lift preserves raw mass,
is continuous for weak convergence, and satisfies
\[
 J_C(\widetilde\mu)=J_K(\mu)\quad\text{if }\mu(T)=V.
\]
\item For continuous psh slots $v_1,\ldots,v_n$,
\[
 \pi_*\MA_{C,c}(\pi^*v_1,\ldots,\pi^*v_n)
 =\MA_{K,c}(v_1,\ldots,v_n).
\]
The upstairs measure is the unique invariant lift of the downstairs
measure. The mass on both sides is $V$, with no field-degree factor.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The theorem concerns fixed ample rational bundles and smooth
geometrically integral projective varieties. It makes no assertion for
arbitrary transcendental classes or nonprojective analytic spaces.
The energy equality is an extended scalar identity; it must not be
read as an unbounded mixed-product theorem.

\section{Continuous facts used, and their boundary}

We use the following exact continuous results of \cite{BE}.
Corollaries 7.5--7.6 give finite maximum, decreasing closure and uniform
closure of continuous psh metrics. Theorem 7.31 recognizes a
\emph{continuous} metric as psh after any complete scalar extension,
provided $X$ is geometrically reduced and the bundle is semiample.
Our $X,L$ satisfy these assumptions over both $K$ and $C$.
The complete proof of that theorem uses the $o(m)$ norm estimate in
Theorem 6.6 and the FS operator; it does not assume envelope continuity.
We do not replace it by an assertion of exact equality of extended
supnorms.

Theorem 7.14 supplies uniform smooth semipositive regularization of
continuous psh metrics. Proposition 8.3 and Theorem 8.4 supply the
actual continuous mixed measures, integration by parts, uniform-to-weak
continuity and scalar-extension pushforward. Definition 9.13 and
Proposition 9.14 supply continuous relative energy, monotonicity,
constant shift, the cocycle law, $V$-Lipschitz continuity and scalar
extension invariance. The normalization is raw, as in
\eqref{eq:energy}. The ordinary analytic facts used below are
projective analytification being compact Hausdorff, the seminorm
description on affine charts, and complete fields being henselian.

For completeness, real convexity of the continuous psh cone needs no
extra envelope theorem. A rational convex combination of two FS
metrics is an FS metric on the same rational bundle: clear
denominators, tensor all pairs of the generated section systems, and
add their weights. Uniform approximation by FS metrics proves the
same assertion for rational combinations of continuous psh metrics.
Approximating a real coefficient uniformly by rationals then gives
real convexity by Corollary 7.6.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:concave}
The continuous energy $v\mapsto E_K(v)$ is concave. The same holds
over $C$. It is invariant under the semilinear $G$-action upstairs.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We give the direct smooth argument to identify the scope of this
property. For two smooth semipositive metrics $\phi_0,\phi_1$ on $L$
put $h=\phi_1-\phi_0$ and $\phi_t=\phi_0+th$. Multilinearity of
curvatures and integration by parts give the energy polynomial and
its derivatives
\[
 \frac d{dt}E(\phi_t,\psi)=\int h(\ddc\phi_t)^n\wedge\delta_X,
 \qquad
 \frac {d^2}{dt^2}E(\phi_t,\psi)
 =n\int h\,\ddc h\wedge(\ddc\phi_t)^{n-1}\wedge\delta_X.
\]
These formulas remain valid when $\psi$ is first approximated by
smooth semipositive metrics. With $T_t=(\ddc\phi_t)^{n-1}\wedge
\delta_X$, the smooth Stokes rule used in
\cite[Proposition 8.3(iii)]{BE} gives
\[
 \int h\,\ddc h\wedge T_t
 =-\int dh\wedge d^ch\wedge T_t\leq0.
\]
Here $dh\wedge d^ch$ is a positive real $(1,1)$ form with the
standard $d^c$ convention; the closed semipositive curvature factors
preserve positivity. Compactness eliminates boundary terms. Hence the
energy polynomial is concave on $[0,1]$. Theorem 7.14 and the uniform
continuity of mixed measures and energy extend the inequality to
continuous psh metrics and a continuous psh reference. This is the
continuous concavity also stated in \cite[Section 1.6]{BGM}; we use no
singular concavity statement from that source.

Naturality under a valued-field automorphism identifies the curvature
forms and analytic fundamental classes after the induced semilinear
isomorphism. Integration on the isomorphic carrier is unchanged.
Consequently $E_C(g^*v,g^*\psi_C)=E_C(v,\psi_C)$ for smooth metrics,
and then for continuous psh metrics by uniform regularization. Since
$g^*\psi_C=\psi_C$, the claimed invariance follows. The same argument
gives the equivariance of the continuous mixed operator.
\end{proof}

\section{The completed algebraic closure really is an orbit quotient}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:quotient}
The action $G\times Y\to Y$ is jointly continuous. The fibers of
$\pi$ are nonempty and are exactly the $G$-orbits.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First let $E/K$ be finite Galois and $x\in T$. The analytic fiber over
$x$ is described by the multiplicative norms on
$\mathcal H(x)\otimes_K E$, where $\mathcal H(x)$ is the completed
residue field. This nonzero finite etale algebra is a product of finite
separable fields over the complete henselian field $\mathcal H(x)$.
Each field factor has a unique extension of its absolute value, so
the fiber is a finite nonempty set. The group $\Gal(E/K)$ acts
transitively on the factors: an orbit which were proper would give a
nontrivial invariant idempotent, whereas the invariant algebra is
$\mathcal H(x)$. Uniqueness of the absolute values also makes the
action transitive on the norm points. This includes nontrivial
valuations on $\mathcal H(x)$ and supported points of $T$.

We next justify the completion step, instead of assuming that every
coefficient in $C$ is algebraic. There is a natural map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
 Y\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{E/K\ \mathrm{finite\ Galois}}X_E^{\an}.
\end{equation}
For a compatible point family choose an affine $K$-chart containing
its common downstairs scheme support, with coordinate generators
$t_1,\ldots,t_r$. The family defines a multiplicative seminorm on
$A\otimes_K\overline K$. For an element of $A\otimes_K C$, express
it as a polynomial in the $t_i$ with finitely many coefficients in
$C$ and approximate those coefficients by elements of $\overline K$.
For a difference polynomial supported on finitely many monomials,
the ultrametric inequality bounds its seminorm by
\[
 \max_\alpha |c_\alpha-c'_\alpha|
                   \prod_{i=1}^r|t_i|^{\alpha_i}.
\]
Thus approximants are Cauchy in the seminorm. More explicitly,
$\big||a|-|b|\big|\leq|a-b|$ ensures convergence of their numerical
norms. The bound proves independence of the chosen approximants.
It also proves independence of polynomial representatives: a relation
in the finitely generated ideal of the chart can be approximated by
finite linear combinations of its defining $K$-relations; its
seminorm is zero. Addition, multiplication, the ultrametric inequality
and restriction to the given absolute value on $C$ pass to limits.
We obtain a unique point on the $C$-chart. These constructions agree
on overlaps, since localization at a function of nonzero seminorm is
unique.

This proves that \eqref{eq:inverse} is a continuous bijection.
Its source is compact and its target is Hausdorff, so it is a
homeomorphism. The finite fibers over $x$ form an inverse system
with surjective transition maps: at each later finite level the same
finite-etale, complete-field argument applied over the earlier level
extends each norm point. Their compact inverse limit is nonempty,
which proves surjectivity of $\pi$.

For joint continuity of the action, functions with coefficients in
$\overline K$ have a finite-index open stabilizer in $G$.
Their evaluations therefore vary jointly continuously in $(g,y)$.
For coefficients in $C$, approximate by algebraic coefficients.
On an affine coordinate neighborhood where the finitely many $|t_i|$
are bounded, the displayed estimate is uniform in both $y$ and $g$:
every $g$ acts isometrically on the coefficient field and fixes the
$K$-coordinates. Uniform limits of these evaluation functions are
continuous. Such neighborhoods give the pointwise seminorm topology,
so the full action is jointly continuous.

Finally, for $y,z\in Y$ with $\pi(y)=\pi(z)$, the sets
\[
 F_E=\{g\in G:(gy)_E=z_E\}
\]
are nonempty closed subsets of the compact group $G$. Any finite
intersection is nonempty by passing to a finite Galois compositum and
using finite-level transitivity. The finite intersection property
gives a $g$ matching every finite level. The uniqueness in
\eqref{eq:inverse} gives $gy=z$. Orbits are contained in fibers by
definition, proving equality.
\end{proof}

Since a quotient by a compact group on a compact Hausdorff space is
compact Hausdorff, the induced continuous bijection $Y/G\to T$ is a
homeomorphism. Invariant continuous scalars therefore descend
continuously. Invariant upper semicontinuous scalars with values in
$[-\infty,\infty)$ descend upper semicontinuously: each closed upper
level set is compact, and its image is closed. This is an all-point
statement, independent of the scheme support of a point.

\section{Compact orbit maxima replace finite coefficient descent}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:orbit}
For a continuous psh scalar $h$ on $Y$, the orbit maximum
\[
 h^G(y)=\max_{g\in G}h(gy)
\]
is continuous, invariant and psh. It is the pullback of a continuous
psh scalar on $T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Joint continuity and compactness of $G\times Y$ show that the maximum
exists and is continuous. To check psh, for every $\epsilon>0$ choose
a finite set $g_1,\ldots,g_N$ such that
\[
 \left\|h^G-\max_{1\leq j\leq N} h\circ g_j\right\|_\infty
 <\epsilon.
\]
Indeed uniform continuity gives, near each $g$, a group neighborhood
on which $|h(gy)-h(g'y)|<\epsilon$ uniformly in $y$; a finite
subcover suffices. Pullback by each semilinear isometry sends an FS
system to an FS system, and hence preserves psh. Finite maximum is
psh by Corollary 7.5 of \cite{BE}, and uniform closure of the
continuous cone proves that $h^G$ is psh.

The quotient identifies $h^G$ with a continuous downstairs scalar
$a$. The metric $\psi+a$ is continuous, and its pullback is psh.
Theorem 7.31 of \cite{BE}, with the actual complete extension $C/K$,
shows that $\psi+a$ is psh. No exact descent of individual weighted
FS section lists was required. In particular no assertion that
nonzero coefficients have norm one is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:singular}
Pullback gives the psh bijection in Theorem \ref{thm:main}(2).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pullback of FS metrics and their decreasing limits gives one
direction, and surjectivity of $\pi$ gives injectivity and order
reflection. For the converse let $u_C$ be an invariant upstairs psh
scalar and choose its defining decreasing net of continuous FS
scalars $h_i\downarrow u_C$. By Lemma \ref{lem:orbit}, every $h_i^G$
is the pullback of a continuous downstairs psh scalar $a_i$, and
$a_i$ decreases.

We prove the exact endpoint without interchanging an arbitrary
supremum and infimum. Fix $y\in Y$ and a finite threshold $b>u_C(y)$.
The compact sets
\[
 Q_i=\{g\in G:h_i(gy)\geq b\}
\]
are decreasing with empty intersection, since $u_C(gy)=u_C(y)<b$.
Compactness and directedness imply $Q_i=\varnothing$ on a tail.
Thus $h_i^G(y)<b$ on that tail. Since $h_i^G\geq u_C$, it follows
that $\inf_i h_i^G(y)=u_C(y)$. If $u_C(y)=-\infty$, apply the same
argument to each finite $b$.

The quotient descends $u_C$ to a scalar $u$ on $T$. The $a_i$
decrease to $u$ at every point, so Corollary 7.5 and Lemma 7.4 of
\cite{BE} show that $u$ is psh in the actual decreasing-FS-net
definition. It is not identically $-\infty$ because $u_C$ is not.
For a continuous endpoint, quotient descent is continuous and the
same Theorem 7.31 gives the continuous statement. Conversely a
continuous downstairs endpoint has continuous pullback.
\end{proof}

\section{Extended energy descends without an unbounded product}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:energy-net}
For any decreasing net of continuous psh scalars $v_i\downarrow u$
on $T$,
\[
 E_K(v_i)\downarrow E_K(u).
\]
The assertion includes the value $-\infty$ and holds over $C$ too.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By monotonicity and \eqref{eq:extended}, $E_K(v_i)\geq E_K(u)$.
Let $w$ be any continuous psh majorant of $u$, and fix
$\epsilon>0$. The compact sets
$\{x:v_i(x)-w(x)\geq\epsilon\}$ are decreasing with empty
intersection. On a tail $v_i\leq w+\epsilon$ everywhere.
Monotonicity and the constant-shift rule for continuous energy give
\[
 \inf_i E_K(v_i)\leq E_K(w)+V\epsilon.
\]
First let $\epsilon\downarrow0$, then take the infimum over all $w$.
This proves equality, even when that last infimum is $-\infty$.
There is no integration of a measurable directed net in this proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:energy}
For every downstairs psh scalar $u$,
$E_C(\pi^*u)=E_K(u)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take a defining continuous FS net $v_i\downarrow u$. Its pullback
decreases to $\pi^*u$. Continuous scalar-extension invariance gives
$E_C(\pi^*v_i)=E_K(v_i)$ for each $i$. Apply Lemma
\ref{lem:energy-net} on both carriers to these very nets.
\end{proof}

One may instead start with arbitrary upstairs continuous majorants
and orbit-maximize them; Proposition \ref{prop:singular} and Lemma
\ref{lem:energy-net} give the same conclusion. Exact field descent
of each original FS list is unnecessary. The energy class here is the
actual scalar class defined by \eqref{eq:extended}, not an abstract
completion whose elements may lack potentials.

For two continuous downstairs psh references $\psi,\psi'$, the cocycle
identity and taking the same majorant infimum give
\[
 E_{\psi'}(\phi)=E_\psi(\phi)-E_\psi(\psi')
\]
also for singular $\phi$. The difference of the references is
continuous and bounded on the compact carrier. Consequently membership
in the extended finite-energy class is reference-independent; all
descent formulas are covariant under an actual change of reference.

\section{Invariant Radon lifts and the continuous-test dual}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:radon}
Pushforward identifies invariant positive Radon measures on $Y$ with
positive Radon measures on $T$. Its inverse preserves mass and is
continuous for the weak topologies.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $dg$ be probability Haar measure on $G$. For $f\in C(Y)$ put
\[
 Af(y)=\int_G f(gy)\,dg.
\]
Joint continuity and compactness show that $Af$ is continuous and
invariant. It descends to a continuous function $\overline{Af}$ on
$T$. The positive bounded functional
$f\mapsto\int_T\overline{Af}\,d\mu$ has norm $\mu(T)$.
Riesz representation gives a positive Radon measure
$\widetilde\mu$ on $Y$. Haar invariance gives group invariance, and
$A(f\circ\pi)=f\circ\pi$ gives
$\pi_*\widetilde\mu=\mu$ and $\widetilde\mu(Y)=\mu(T)$.

To check uniqueness, approximate $Af$ uniformly in $y$ by finite
weighted group averages. Such approximation follows by choosing a
finite clopen partition of $G$ on each piece of which $f(gy)$ varies
by at most $\epsilon$, uniformly in $y$, and taking one representative
from each piece with its Haar weight. If $\lambda$ is invariant,
each such finite average has the same integral as $f$. Thus
$\int f\,d\lambda=\int Af\,d\lambda$, and the pushforward determines
the latter. The same formula against continuous tests proves weak
continuity of the lift. Weak continuity of pushforward is immediate.
\end{proof}

For a finite Galois level $E/K$ with a fiber of $r$ norm points, the
invariant fiber probability has weight $1/r$ at each point.
The number $r$ need not be $[E:K]$. This finite-fiber statement must
not be confused with a blanket division by a field degree. The
scalar-extension pushforward law for intersection measures retains
the geometric mass $V$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dual}
If $\mu(T)=V$, then $J_C(\widetilde\mu)=J_K(\mu)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pullback of continuous downstairs tests and continuous energy
invariance give $J_C(\widetilde\mu)\geq J_K(\mu)$.
For an arbitrary continuous upstairs psh scalar $h$, its Haar average
$Ah$ is a uniform limit of finite convex averages of the scalars
$h\circ g$. Real convexity and uniform closure of the continuous psh
cone show that $Ah$ is continuous psh. It is invariant, so Theorem
7.31 and the quotient descend it to a continuous psh scalar $a$.

Continuous concavity and $G$-invariance of energy give, for each
finite convex average, energy at least $E_C(h)$. Uniform continuity
of energy therefore gives
$E_C(Ah)\geq E_C(h)$. Invariance of the lift and the same finite
average approximation give
$\int Ah\,d\widetilde\mu=\int h\,d\widetilde\mu$.
Since $Ah=\pi^*a$, we obtain
\[
 E_C(h)-\int h\,d\widetilde\mu
 \leq E_K(a)-\int a\,d\mu\leq J_K(\mu).
\]
Taking the supremum over all continuous upstairs psh $h$ proves the
other inequality, including infinite dual values.
\end{proof}

For any single downstairs psh scalar $u$, the ordinary Radon
pushforward law gives
\[
 \int_Y |\pi^*u|\,d\widetilde\mu=
 \int_T |u|\,d\mu,
\]
as extended nonnegative integrals; these functions are Borel. Thus
the integrability of all downstairs $\E_K^1$ tests is equivalent to
integrability of all \emph{invariant} upstairs $\E_C^1$ tests.
We do not infer integrability of every noninvariant singular upstairs
test from this identity or from Proposition \ref{prop:dual}.

\section{Continuous measures and what remains unproved}

For continuous psh slots on the actual fixed bundle, Proposition
8.3(iv) and the uniform extension in Theorem 8.4 of \cite{BE} give
the pushforward statement in Theorem \ref{thm:main}(5). Naturality
under $g$ and invariance of the slots make the upstairs mixed measure
invariant. Lemma \ref{lem:radon} identifies it with the unique
invariant lift. Proposition 8.5 gives raw mass $V$ on each carrier.
These facts complete the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}.

There is a useful precise reduction for the remaining work.
\emph{If} an actual equivariant mixed Radon operator is constructed
on all of $(\E_C^1)^n$, its pushforward on pulled-back slots defines
an operator on $(\E_K^1)^n$. Genuine decreasing-continuity and
weighted integral identities for these slots transfer whenever they
are proved upstairs: pushforward is weakly continuous, and each
downstairs Borel test is its actual pullback. This conditional
observation is not the construction of that upstairs operator.

Likewise, if an upstairs equation has a solution for the invariant
lift and every upstairs solution is unique modulo constants, then
the normalized solution is invariant. For example impose
$\sup_Y u=0$ relative to $\psi_C$; $g^*u$ has the same normalization
and the same target. Equivariance and uniqueness imply $g^*u=u$.
Proposition \ref{prop:singular} then descends that actual solution.
This argument requires actual existence, equivariance and
every-solution uniqueness upstairs; it provides none of them.

The following original gaps therefore remain.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A genuine full unbounded mixed operator on arbitrary-field
finite-energy potentials, including approximation independence,
weighted limits, locality and comparison, has not been constructed.
\item Continuity of arbitrary envelopes over a nondiscrete field,
the required normalized potential compactness, orthogonality and
both signs of the outer first variation remain to be established
where needed for the full variational theorem.
\item The equivalence between the original intrinsic all-$\E^1$
target condition and a usable dual bound, actual variational
attainment and every-solution uniqueness are not proved here.
\end{enumerate}
A value group which is dense remains embedded and nondiscrete in
every valued extension. Passing to $C$ does not make it discrete.
The accepted trivial-valuation P16.5 theorem is not a theorem over
a nontrivially valued $C$, and was not used as a grant in this paper.
These valuation restrictions cannot be removed by renaming the
base field. No original problem is declared solved by continuation 214.

\begin{abstract}
Assuming the complete independently accepted all-field finite-energy
operator theorem, we prove an intrinsic target-energy criterion when
the continuous reference has a single norming Monge--Ampere atom.
All finite-energy potentials are integrable against a positive target
of the correct raw mass if and only if its continuous-test energy
dual is finite. A convex-series argument gives a uniform unit-energy
bound; the vanishing reference integral then upgrades it to a
square-root estimate and variational coercivity. The hypothesis holds
for standard projective space over every complete characteristic-zero
real-valued non-Archimedean field, including nondiscrete and trivial
valuations. Targets need not be invariant or supported on a skeleton.
No compactness, envelope differentiability or actual equation
attainment is asserted. These new proofs await independent review.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact prerequisite and norming reference}

Let $K$ be a complete characteristic-zero real-valued
non-Archimedean field, $X/K$ smooth, geometrically integral and
projective of dimension $n\geq1$, and $L$ an actual ample rational
line bundle. Put $T=X^{\mathrm{an}}$ and $V=(L^n)>0$.
Fix a continuous psh reference $\psi$. A scalar $u$ represents the
actual all-point metric $\psi+u$. Its psh definition is the
decreasing-Fubini--Study-net definition in
\cite[Definition 7.2]{BE}; the identically $-\infty$ metric is excluded.
Extended relative energy is the infimum of the continuous psh
majorant energies, with $E(0)=0$, and $\E^1$ consists of actual
potentials of finite extended energy.

Our sole caller mathematical prerequisite is the \emph{complete}
accepted operator manuscript \cite{O}, including its source-preserving
normalization clarifications. We use its actual convex $\E^1$ cone,
energy concavity and decreasing continuity, genuine positive mixed
measures of raw mass $V$, integrability of every $\E^1$ test against
every mixed output, the genuine singular energy identity, affine
curvature calculus, constant law and complete-field pushforward.
No equation, compactness, envelope or comparison result is granted.

We impose the following explicit additional reference hypothesis.
There is a point $\xi\in T$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchor}
 \MA(0)=V\delta_\xi,\qquad
 \sup_T u=u(\xi)\quad\hbox{for every scalar FS potential }u.
\end{equation}
These statements concern the reference on this actual bundle.
They are not claimed for a general projective variety or for an
arbitrary atomic measure with several support points.

The norming equality extends to every actual psh potential.
Indeed take an actual FS net $u_i\downarrow u$ and set
$c_i=u_i(\xi)=\sup_Tu_i$. The finite numbers $c_i$ decrease.
If their limit were $-\infty$, the inequalities $u_i\leq c_i$
would force $u$ to be identically $-\infty$. Otherwise their limit
$c$ is finite, $u\leq c$ at every point and $u(\xi)=c$.
Thus $c=\sup_Tu$ and every actual psh potential is finite at $\xi$.

We normalize potentials by $u(\xi)=\sup_Tu=0$, so $u\leq0$.
For such actual $u\in\E^1$ write
\[
                 B(u)=-E(u)\geq0.
\]
If $\mu$ is any positive Radon measure of raw mass $V$, put
\[
 A_\mu(u)=\int_T(-u)\,d\mu\in[0,\infty],\qquad
 J(\mu)=\sup_{v\in\CPSH}\left(E(v)-\int_Tv\,d\mu\right).
\]
Here $\CPSH$ means continuous actual fixed-$L$ potentials. The dual
is unchanged by adding a constant to its test because the two
constant contributions are $cV$. Its value is at least zero.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:criterion}
Under \eqref{eq:anchor} and the full prerequisite \cite{O}, the
following are equivalent for every positive Radon $\mu$ of mass $V$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every actual $u\in\E^1$ is absolutely $\mu$-integrable.
\item The unit-energy integral bound
\[
 U_\mu=\sup\{A_\mu(u):u\in\E^1,\ u(\xi)=0,\ B(u)\leq1\}
\]
is finite.
\item $J(\mu)<\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
If these hold, with $C_n=2^{n+1}$, every normalized $u\in\E^1$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sqrt}
 A_\mu(u)\leq U_\mu\max\{1,\sqrt{C_nB(u)}\}
              \leq U_\mu+U_\mu\sqrt{C_nB(u)}.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercive}
 E(u)-\int_Tu\,d\mu
       \leq-B(u)+U_\mu+U_\mu\sqrt{C_nB(u)},\qquad
 J(\mu)\leq U_\mu+\tfrac14 C_nU_\mu^2.
\end{equation}
The functional tends to $-\infty$ whenever the normalized energy
cost tends to infinity, and its supremum over $\E^1$ equals its
continuous-test supremum. None of these assertions says that the
supremum is attained by an actual potential.
\end{theorem}

\section{The quadratic scaling gain}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:scaling}
For every normalized actual $u\in\E^1$ and $0\leq t\leq1$,
\[
                         B(tu)\leq C_nt^2B(u).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The convex combination $tu+(1-t)0$ is an actual $\E^1$ potential.
For $0\leq k\leq n$ set
\[
 A_k=\int_T(-u)\,\MA(\underbrace{u,\ldots,u}_{k},
                           \underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n-k}).
\]
All numbers are finite and nonnegative by the prerequisite, and
$A_0=-Vu(\xi)=0$ by \eqref{eq:anchor}. The genuine energy identity
gives $B(u)=(n+1)^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^nA_k$.
For $0<t<1$ affine curvature calculus in each slot gives the
\emph{actual} mixed identity
\[
 B(tu)=\frac{t}{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^n\sum_{k=0}^j
       \binom{j}{k}t^k(1-t)^{j-k}A_k.
\]
Its finitely many terms are integrable; no infinite values are
subtracted. The $k=0$ terms vanish. Since $t^{k+1}\leq t^2$ for
$k\geq1$, $0\leq(1-t)^{j-k}\leq1$, and
$\sum_{j=k}^n\binom{j}{k}=\binom{n+1}{k+1}\leq2^{n+1}$,
the displayed expression is at most
$C_nt^2(n+1)^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^nA_k=C_nt^2B(u)$.
The endpoints $t=0,1$ are immediate, and at zero the actual
potential is the reference, without a zero multiple of a pole.
\end{proof}

The absent linear term is the decisive point. Energy concavity
alone would give only $B(tu)\leq tB(u)$ and would not establish the
finite-dual conclusion from a unit-energy bound.

\section{Uniformity from all-test integrability}

Suppose every actual $\E^1$ potential is $\mu$-integrable, but
$U_\mu=\infty$. Choose normalized actual $u_j\in\E^1$ with
$B(u_j)\leq1$ and $A_\mu(u_j)\geq4^j$, for $j\geq1$.
For $N\geq1$ form the actual convex combination
\[
 w_N=\sum_{j=1}^N2^{-j}u_j+2^{-N}0.
\]
Zero coefficients, if any, are omitted. Each $w_N$ belongs to
$\E^1$, $w_N\leq0$, $w_N(\xi)=0$, and $w_{N+1}\leq w_N$.
Concavity gives
\[
                 E(w_N)\geq\sum_{j=1}^N2^{-j}E(u_j)\geq-1.
\]
The decreasing actual psh closure puts the all-point limit $w$
in the psh cone; it is not identically $-\infty$ because
$w(\xi)=0$. Decreasing energy continuity gives $E(w)\geq-1$,
so $w\in\E^1$. Ordinary \emph{sequential} monotone convergence
for the single measure $\mu$ yields
\[
 \int_T(-w)\,d\mu
   =\sum_{j=1}^\infty2^{-j}A_\mu(u_j)
   \geq\sum_{j=1}^\infty2^j=\infty.
\]
This contradicts the hypothesized integrability. Hence $U_\mu$ is
finite. This proof needs no compactness of a family of normalized
potentials and no assertion about arbitrary measurable nets.

Now assume $U_\mu<\infty$. For any normalized $u\in\E^1$, take
$t=1$ if $C_nB(u)\leq1$, and
$t=(C_nB(u))^{-1/2}$ otherwise. In both cases $t>0$ and
Lemma~\ref{lem:scaling} gives $B(tu)\leq1$. Since $tu$ is still
normalized, $tA_\mu(u)=A_\mu(tu)\leq U_\mu$.
This proves \eqref{eq:sqrt}, even when the original integral was
initially allowed to be infinite. It therefore also proves
absolute integrability of every normalized potential. Every other
actual $\E^1$ potential differs by a finite constant, so all-test
integrability follows. For continuous normalized tests
$E(u)-\int u\,d\mu=-B(u)+A_\mu(u)$; maximizing the bound in
\eqref{eq:coercive} over $\sqrt{B(u)}\geq0$ proves finiteness of
$J$ and its asserted upper bound.

\section{Finite dual, singular tests and arbitrary nets}

Conversely let $J(\mu)<\infty$ and take any actual $u\in\E^1$.
Choose continuous psh $u_i\downarrow u$. A tail is bounded above
by a fixed finite constant $M$, because it lies below any one of
its continuous stages. On this tail the continuous nonnegative
functions $M-u_i$ increase to $M-u$. The dual inequality gives
\[
 \int_T(M-u_i)\,d\mu
   \leq MV+J(\mu)-E(u_i)
   \leq MV+J(\mu)-E(u)<\infty.
\]
We use the precise Radon-net integration fact here. The strict
superlevel sets of $M-u_i$ increase as directed open sets and cover
each strict superlevel set of $M-u$. Inner compact approximation
and a finite subcover identify the mass of each limiting superlevel
with the supremum of its stage masses. One common stage approximates
any finite lower layer sum. Truncating the height and refining the
layers therefore shows that the integrals increase to
$\int(M-u)\,d\mu$, including when that integral is infinite.
The uniform finite bound excludes infinity. Since $u$ is bounded
above, this proves absolute $\mu$-integrability.

The same argument, now with the endpoint integral known to be
finite, gives $\int u_i\,d\mu\to\int u\,d\mu$.
The prerequisite supplies $E(u_i)\downarrow E(u)$. Consequently
\[
 E(u)-\int_Tu\,d\mu
       =\lim_i\left(E(u_i)-\int_Tu_i\,d\mu\right)\leq J(\mu).
\]
Continuous tests are included in $\E^1$, proving equality of the
two suprema and completing Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}.
In particular every genuine output $\mu=\MA(v)$, $v\in\E^1$,
satisfies this target criterion by all-test integrability in
\cite{O}. The converse criterion does not construct an output.

\section{Standard projective space over every complete field}

Take $X=\mathbb P^n_K$, $L=\mathcal O(1)$ and its standard
Fubini--Study reference $\psi$. Let $\xi$ be the Gauss point on
the chart $T_0\ne0$, in coordinates $z_j=T_j/T_0$. For a section
$s$ of $mL$, its expression $s/T_0^m$ is a polynomial of degree
at most $m$. Its value at $\xi$ is the maximum absolute value of
its coefficients. The non-Archimedean triangle inequality in any
projective chart shows that its global $m\psi$-norm is at most
this coefficient maximum; the Gauss point attains equality.

For an actual FS metric defined by sections $s_\alpha$ and real
weights $b_\alpha$, its scalar relative to $\psi$ is
\[
 u=\frac1m\max_\alpha\bigl(\log|s_\alpha|_{m\psi}
                                      +b_\alpha\bigr).
\]
Every section term attains its own maximum at the same $\xi$.
Thus $\sup_Tu=u(\xi)$, including arbitrary real FS weights.
The preceding decreasing-net argument proves the norming identity
for all actual psh endpoints, with literal values at supported points.

The reference measure is $\delta_\xi$, with raw mass $V=1$.
Indeed the standard projective model has reduced irreducible special
fiber and the intersection number of $\mathcal O(1)$ on that fiber
is one. The model formula in \cite[Example 8.11, formula (8.3)]{BE}
therefore has exactly one atom. To apply that formula in its stated
source construction, first pass to a complete algebraically closed
nontrivially valued extension, also when the original valuation is
trivial. The standard model upstairs still has a single reduced
component and its Gauss point maps to $\xi$: Gauss evaluation on
coefficients from $K$ is unchanged. The complete-field pushforward
law gives the claimed original-field measure, without a field-degree
factor. This uses no envelope continuity or density of a value group
in a discretely valued one.

Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} applies on $\mathbb P^n_K$ over
every complete characteristic-zero real-valued non-Archimedean
field. Its targets are arbitrary positive Radon measures of mass
one subject to the equivalent energy conditions; there is no torus
invariance, skeletal support, carrier metrizability or discreteness
restriction. This explicit domain is a restricted geometric sector,
and is not the entire original arbitrary-variety equation axis.

sup-normalized potential need not vanish. The proof above therefore
does not upgrade the general linear scaling bound without an
additional proved reference estimate. Even in the present norming

actual maximizing potentials nor upper semicontinuity in a proved
compactness topology. Envelope orthogonality and both-sign energy
differentiation in arbitrary outer continuous directions, actual
equation attainment and uniqueness of every solution remain to be
established. No original P16.4 closure is claimed. The new
criterion and its complete prerequisite must remain separately
identified when reviewed or reused.

\subsection{Scope and result}\label{scope-and-result}

The entire original P16.4 is not solved in this paper. We prove a
ground-field extension of the supplied complete B theorem at the
\textbf{trivial-valuation boundary}. This result is in every dimension
and on the whole analytic carrier; it is not a projective-line or
skeletal statement. It does not give an operator over a nontrivially
valued nondiscrete field. Accordingly the submission has
\texttt{candidate=false}.

Throughout the positive theorem, \(k\) is any field of characteristic
zero, with its trivial absolute value. Such a field, and its algebraic
closure with the trivial absolute value, are complete. Let \(X\) be a
smooth geometrically integral projective \(k\)-variety of dimension
\(n\geq1\), and let \(L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)\otimes\mathbb Q\) be an
actual ample rational line bundle. Rational powers are cleared before
using sections. Put \[
 T=X^{\mathrm{an}},\qquad V=(L^n)>0.
\] Geometric integrality is retained to have one generic normalization
and uniqueness modulo one constant. No countability, algebraic
closedness, finite generation of the section ring beyond ampleness,
torus invariance, skeletal support, or restriction on measure support is
imposed. Smoothness, projectivity, algebraizability, ampleness, and
residue characteristic zero are retained. Neither P16.3 nor the nonample
part of P16.5 is asserted.

Use the canonical trivial reference metric on \(L\). Its scalar is
denoted by \(0\). Write \(o\) for the generic trivial valuation. A
basepoint-free Fubini--Study scalar on \(L\) is \[
 h=\frac1m\max_j\bigl(\log\|s_j\|_{\mathrm{triv}}+\lambda_j\bigr),
 \quad s_j\in H^0(X,mL),\quad\lambda_j\in\mathbb R.
 \tag{1}
\] The sections have no common zero. Define \(\operatorname{PSH}_k(L)\)
to consist of their all-point decreasing-net endpoints with finite value
at \(o\). This is the usual fixed ample-bundle psh cone. We prove below
that it equals the fixed-bundle version of the cone in grant B after
scalar extension. Supported points and values \(-\infty\) are included.
Constants mean literal real constants.

For continuous psh \(v\), let
\(\operatorname{MA}_{k,c}(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\) be the genuine continuous
mixed operator, with its raw intersection normalization. Define \[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_k(v)&=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^n
 \int_T v\,\operatorname{MA}_{k,c}(v^{[j]},0^{[n-j]}),\\
 E_k(u)&=\inf_{\substack{v\in C(T)\cap\operatorname{PSH}_k(L)\\v\geq u}}E_k(v),
 \qquad\mathcal E^1_k=\{u:E_k(u)>-\infty\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\] Thus \(E_k(0)=0\) and \(E_k(u+c)=E_k(u)+Vc\). All suprema and
inequalities are on the original full carrier.

\textbf{Theorem 1 (proved boundary theorem).} There are genuine positive
symmetric mixed Radon measures \(\operatorname{MA}_k(u_1,\ldots,u_n)\)
on \((\mathcal E^1_k)^n\), each of raw mass \(V\), extending the actual
continuous mixed operator. They are independent of decreasing continuous
psh approximations, weakly continuous along arbitrary decreasing
finite-energy nets, and satisfy the energy identity, integrability of
every finite-energy test, and its weighted decreasing continuity.

For a positive Radon measure \(\mu\) on \(T\), the exact target
condition is \[
 \mu(T)=V,\qquad
 J_k(\mu):=\sup_{v\in C(T)\cap\operatorname{PSH}_k(L)}
       \left(E_k(v)-\int_T v\,d\mu\right)<\infty.
 \tag{3}
\] It is equivalent to integrating every genuine \(\mathcal E^1_k\)
potential, after any constant normalization. Every such target admits an
actual \(u\in\mathcal E^1_k\) with \(\operatorname{MA}_k(u)=\mu\). Every
solution is unique modulo a real constant. The normalized psh cone and
its bounded energy-cost subsets are compact in divisorial topology.
All-point envelopes, both signs of obstacle energy differentiation,
strict Borel locality, comparison, and actual variational attainment
hold. No inverse construction is used to define the operator.

The proof is relative only to the \textbf{complete B body supplied in
the assignment}, in its algebraically closed trivially valued smooth
projective actual-big rational-bundle domain, specialized to ample
bundles. Its internal G0/G1 references are not separately imported.
Historical review labels, old notes and old source hashes are not
premises. In particular, the unreviewed round46 projective-line operator
is not assumed. Ordinary field algebra, intersection theory,
compact-space Radon measures, and the continuous-metric theory are used
where specified. All new descent statements are proved here.

\subsection{Exact use of B and the continuous
background}\label{exact-use-of-b-and-the-continuous-background}

Let \(\bar k\) be an algebraic closure, \(Y=X_{\bar k}^{\mathrm{an}}\),
\(G=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/k)\), and \(\pi:Y\to T\). The full B
theorem applies on \(X_{\bar k}\) with the actual ample \(L_{\bar k}\).
Its reference is \(R=0\): zero is the scalar of a generated power of
\(L_{\bar k}\), and every normalized section scalar has supremum zero at
the generic trivial valuation. Thus the B target condition has no
reference-pole exception and is \[
 D_{\bar\nu}=\sup_{u\in\mathcal E^1_{\bar k},\,u(o)=0}
       \left(E_{\bar k}(u)-\int_Yu\,d\bar\nu\right)<\infty.
 \tag{4}
\] B supplies actual psh closure and envelopes, divisorial compactness
and all-point determination, scalar energy and its decreasing
continuity, genuine mixed products and weighted continuity, obstacle
differentiation, comparison, intrinsic targets, attainment, and
uniqueness for every solution. No version of these assertions over \(k\)
or a nontrivially valued field is granted.

For the genuine continuous background we checked the fixed primary
version Boucksom--Eriksson, arXiv:1805.01016v3: Definition 7.2,
Corollary 7.6, Theorem 7.14, Proposition 8.3 including its proof,
Theorem 8.4 with Lemma 8.6 and the full extension proof, and Definition
9.13--Proposition 9.14 including the proof. These units allow complete
fields, including trivial valuation, and continuous psh metrics on ample
rational bundles. Their field-extension law is a \textbf{pushforward}
law with unchanged geometric raw mass, not multiplication by the degree
of the field extension. They supply no singular field-extension law;
that law is proved below. The compact Hausdorff and seminorm
descriptions of projective analytifications are standard analytic
background, also recorded in its Section 4.1.

Here is a useful bridge between B's varying-bundle cone and (1). Suppose
\(h_i\downarrow u\) is a defining net in B, on actual \(B_i\) with
\(c_1(B_i)\to c_1(L)\), and \(u(o)\) finite. On a tail take
\(h_i\leq C\). For rational \(0<t<1\) and late \(i\), \[
 L-(1-t)B_i\text{ is ample}.
\] Tensor the section system for \((1-t)h_i+tC\) with a weight-zero
generated power of this difference; the scalar is unchanged everywhere.
Indeed at the valuation-ring center of each analytic point some
generator is a unit. Such centers exist by projectivity, including for
supported semivaluations. Append a generated power of \(L\) at scalar
\(C-N\). This gives the basepoint-free scalar \[
 H_{i,t,N}=\max\bigl((1-t)(h_i-C)+C,C-N\bigr)
 \quad\text{on the actual fixed }L.
 \tag{5}
\] Eligible triples are directed by later \(i\), smaller \(t\), and
larger \(N\). A common later \(i\) fits the prescribed positive ample
differences. Since \(h_i-C\leq0\), the displayed functions decrease in
that order. Their infimum is \(u\) at every point: first hold \(t,N\)
and take \(i\), then let \(t\downarrow0\) and \(N\uparrow\infty\). At a
pole this is a literal limit of finite upper bounds, with no subtraction
of infinities. Conversely a fixed-\(L\) net is already a defining net in
B. This proves equality of the cones in the ample subcase.

B's genuine operator agrees there with the continuous operator just
described. Rational weighted FS systems have actual finite intersection
measures; these are the same finite coefficients in B. Real weights are
approximated uniformly by rational weights. A continuous psh metric is
uniformly approximated by such systems; if the error of the \(j\)th is
at most \(4^{-j}\), adding \(2\cdot4^{-j}\) makes them decrease to the
metric. B's decreasing continuity and the continuous operator's uniform
continuity identify both limits. This establishes agreement without
postulating a new unbounded product.

\subsection{The analytic quotient, including all
fibers}\label{the-analytic-quotient-including-all-fibers}

\textbf{Lemma 2 (quotient).} The action of the profinite group \(G\) on
\(Y\) is jointly continuous; \(\pi\) is surjective and its fibers are
exactly the \(G\)-orbits. It induces a homeomorphism \(Y/G\simeq T\).
The point over \(o\) is the generic trivial valuation of \(X_{\bar k}\).

\textbf{Proof.} On an affine \(k\)-chart, an analytic point is a
multiplicative seminorm on its coordinate algebra. Every function after
scalar extension has coefficients in a finite extension. Its conjugates
therefore vary locally constantly with \(g\in G\), proving joint
continuity of the action in the pointwise seminorm topology.

For a finite Galois extension \(E/k\) and \(x\in T\), its analytic fiber
is the set of multiplicative norms on the finite étale algebra
\(\mathcal H(x)\otimes_k E\). Here \(\mathcal H(x)\) is the completed
residue field; it is complete and hence henselian. The algebra is a
nonzero product of finite separable fields, each with the unique
extension of the absolute value of \(\mathcal H(x)\). This also holds
when that absolute value is trivial. Thus the fiber is nonempty.
\(\operatorname{Gal}(E/k)\) acts transitively on its factors: otherwise
the sum of the idempotents in one orbit would be a nontrivial invariant
idempotent, whereas the invariant algebra is \(\mathcal H(x)\).

Analytifications over \(\bar k\) are the inverse limit of the
finite-extension analytifications. To check this directly, choose a
\(k\)-affine chart containing the scheme support of \(x\). A compatible
family of seminorms defines a seminorm on the union of its
scalar-extended algebras; all algebraic norm identities are
finite-coefficient identities. This is the required point over
\(\bar k\), and the seminorm topologies give the claimed inverse-limit
topology. Surjectivity follows from the nonempty finite fibers and
compactness of their inverse system. For two points \(y,z\) in the same
fiber, the sets \[
 \{g\in G:g y_E=z_E\}
\] are nonempty closed subsets of \(G\). Any finite intersection is
nonempty by passing to a finite Galois compositum. Compactness of \(G\)
gives one \(g\) matching every finite level. Consequently \(gy=z\).

Both \(Y\) and \(T\) are compact Hausdorff. The continuous bijection
\(Y/G\to T\) is therefore a homeomorphism. At the generic trivial
valuation, geometric integrality makes \(k(X)\otimes_k E\) a field for
each finite \(E\). Its only extension of the trivial absolute value is
trivial, so this fiber is a singleton. No points with nongeneric support
were omitted. \(\square\)

An invariant usc extended-real function on \(Y\) consequently descends
to an usc function on \(T\). For example, its closed upper level sets
have compact, hence closed, images. An invariant continuous function
descends continuously. This remains valid at \(-\infty\).

\subsection{Exact descent of actual FS systems and singular
potentials}\label{exact-descent-of-actual-fs-systems-and-singular-potentials}

\textbf{Lemma 3 (FS descent).} For any FS scalar \(h\) on
\(L_{\bar k}\), its orbit maximum \[
 h^G(y)=\max_{g\in G}h(gy)
 \tag{6}
\] is the pullback of an actual FS scalar on \(L\) with the same cleared
degree.

\textbf{Proof.} All sections in \(h\) are defined over one finite Galois
extension \(E/k\). In degree \(m\), take the finite union of all
conjugate section lists, with unchanged weights. This produces \(h^G\).
For each distinct weight \(a\), the subspace \(W_a\) spanned over \(E\)
by sections of weight at least \(a\) is semilinearly Galois stable. It
descends to a \(k\)-subspace \(U_a\): in a fixed \(k\)-basis of
\(H^0(X,mL)\), the unique reduced-row-echelon matrix of \(W_a\) is fixed
by every automorphism, so its entries lie in \(k\). This also proves
\(W_a=U_a\otimes_kE\).

Choose a \(k\)-basis adapted to the finite nested flag \(U_a\), and give
each basis vector its largest flag weight. The weighted maximum of this
basis equals the weighted maximum of the original conjugate generators.
For one inequality express an adapted vector in the generators of its
weight level; for the other express each generator in adapted vectors of
at least its weight. Every nonzero coefficient has absolute value one.
The ultrametric inequality proves both bounds on the section norms at
every analytic point, even when a section vanishes. Basepoint freeness
descends faithfully flatly. Hence this is an actual FS system over
\(k\), and not just a function on a quotient. \(\square\)

\textbf{Lemma 4 (psh descent).} Pullback gives a bijection \[
 \operatorname{PSH}_k(L)\ \simeq\
 \operatorname{PSH}_{\bar k}(L_{\bar k})^G
 \tag{7}
\] as actual functions on all points. It also identifies continuous psh
metrics.

\textbf{Proof.} Pulling back sections proves one direction. If an
invariant \(u\) is a B-psh endpoint, (5) supplies a decreasing net of
fixed-bundle basepoint-free FS scalars \(h_i\downarrow u\). Their orbit
maxima \(h_i^G\) decrease, and Lemma 3 descends each one. Their endpoint
is exactly \(u\). Indeed fix \(y\) and a finite real threshold
\(a>u(y)\). The compact sets \[
 \{g\in G:h_i(gy)\geq a\}
\] decrease to the empty set, since \(u(gy)=u(y)\). Finitely many
suffice to have empty intersection, and directedness supplies a whole
tail with empty set. This proves \(\inf_i h_i^G(y)\leq u(y)\); the
reverse inequality is immediate. If \(u(y)=-\infty\), apply this
argument at each finite threshold. There is no measurable-net
integration or interchange of a noncompact supremum and infimum.

The descended functions decrease all-point to the descended \(u\), whose
value at \(o\) is finite. If \(u\) is continuous, net Dini follows from
the same nested compact-superlevel argument on \(Y\); hence the
descended FS net converges uniformly. Conversely continuous pullback is
continuous. \(\square\)

This proof depends on the trivial coefficient absolute value and on
having an actual upstairs psh potential. It does not provide an upstairs
finite-energy operator for a nontrivially valued field.

\subsection{Radon measures: the unique invariant
lift}\label{radon-measures-the-unique-invariant-lift}

\textbf{Lemma 5.} Every positive Radon measure \(\mu\) on \(T\) has a
unique \(G\)-invariant positive Radon lift \(\widetilde\mu\) on \(Y\).
It satisfies \[
 \pi_*\widetilde\mu=\mu,\qquad \widetilde\mu(Y)=\mu(T),
 \quad\int_Y(f\circ\pi)d\widetilde\mu=\int_T f\,d\mu
 \tag{8}
\] for every nonnegative Borel \(f\), and for every integrable signed
\(f\). The lift and pushforward are inverse homeomorphisms between base
measures and invariant measures for the weak topologies.

\textbf{Proof.} The probability Haar functional on \(G\) can be
constructed by uniform averaging on finite quotient groups; locally
constant functions are uniformly dense on the profinite compact group.
For \(f\in C(Y)\) put \[
 Af(y)=\int_G f(gy)\,dg.
\] Joint continuity and compactness make \(Af\) continuous and
invariant. It therefore descends to a continuous function on \(T\). The
positive functional \(f\mapsto\int_T Af\,d\mu\) has norm \(\mu(T)\);
Riesz representation defines \(\widetilde\mu\). Averaging leaves a
pullback \(f\circ\pi\) unchanged, proving its pushforward and mass.
Group invariance follows from Haar invariance. If \(\lambda\) is another
invariant Radon measure, finite-average approximation, uniform in \(y\),
gives \(\lambda(f)=\lambda(Af)\). Its pushforward determines the latter,
so \(\lambda=\widetilde\mu\). The same continuous tests prove both weak
continuity statements. The Borel integral laws now follow from the usual
pushforward law for a single measure, with countable simple-function
approximation. No arbitrary measurable-net convergence is used.
\(\square\)

On a finite fiber with \(r\) points, its orbit probability puts mass
\(1/r\) at each point. In general \(r\) need not equal \([E:k]\). A
uniform division by the field degree at every point is wrong. We
preserve the original measure, with no mass discarded at supported
points.

\subsection{Raw energy and genuine mixed-operator
descent}\label{raw-energy-and-genuine-mixed-operator-descent}

The finite genuine model normalization deserves emphasis. For a rational
weighted FS test configuration over \(k[t]\), a central prime \(Q\) of
multiplicity \(b_Q\) contributes \[
 b_Q\bigl(\mathcal L_1|_Q\cdots\mathcal L_n|_Q\bigr)_k\,
       \delta_{b_Q^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_Q|_{k(X)}}.
 \tag{9}
\] Constant finite separable extension splits primes but does not ramify
their local uniformizers. A finite étale extension of a DVR is
unramified. Intersection degrees over \(E\) obey \[
 \deg_k(p_*z)=[E:k]\deg_E(z).
\] The projection formula for the finite map on the total model
consequently cancels this same factor \([E:k]\). Summing over split
components gives exactly (9). In particular \((L_E^n)_E=(L^n)_k=V\);
this is geometric base change, not a degree-\([E:k]\) map measured
everywhere over \(k\). Real weights pass by uniform approximation. For
continuous psh metrics the same pushforward formula follows from uniform
FS approximation and the continuous operator. Equivalently it is
Proposition 8.3(iv) passed through Theorem 8.4 in the checked version.
Therefore \[
 E_k(v)=E_{\bar k}(v\circ\pi)
 \quad(v\text{ continuous psh}).
 \tag{10}
\]

Both the continuous energy and operator over \(\bar k\) are invariant
under the semilinear \(G\)-action. At rational FS stages this follows by
transporting the actual section system and finite intersections; all
degrees and multiplicities are preserved. Uniform limits prove it on
continuous metrics. Decreasing approximants and B prove it on the entire
finite-energy space and its mixed products.

\textbf{Lemma 6 (extended energy).} For every actual
\(u\in\operatorname{PSH}_k(L)\), \[
 E_k(u)=E_{\bar k}(u\circ\pi).
 \tag{11}
\] This includes the value \(-\infty\). Thus (7) identifies
\(\mathcal E^1_k\) with the invariant part of \(\mathcal E^1_{\bar k}\).

\textbf{Proof.} Every continuous base majorant pulls back to a
continuous upstairs majorant, giving the inequality
\(E_k(u)\geq E_{\bar k}(u\circ\pi)\). Lemma 4 supplies actual descended
continuous FS majorants \(a_i\downarrow u\). Their energies equal the
upstairs energies by (10); B's decreasing energy continuity makes these
tend to \(E_{\bar k}(u\circ\pi)\). They are competitors in (2), proving
the reverse inequality. This is cofinality in energy, not an assertion
that averaging a majorant lowers its energy. \(\square\)

Define, before considering any target, \[
 \operatorname{MA}_k(u_1,\ldots,u_n)
 :=\pi_*\operatorname{MA}_{\bar k}(u_1\circ\pi,\ldots,u_n\circ\pi).
 \tag{12}
\] This uses B's genuine operator in its granted domain, on actual
pullback psh metrics. Its continuous restriction equals the genuine
continuous operator by (9)--(10), so it is an actual extension and not
an assigned inverse. Positivity, symmetry and raw mass \(V\) follow
immediately. For any independent decreasing continuous psh approximants
with finite-energy endpoints, their pullbacks are genuine approximants
upstairs, and B's independence and decreasing continuity prove
independence and continuity of (12) along the original directed nets.

The upstairs mixed measure in (12) is invariant and hence is precisely
the invariant lift of the base mixed measure, by Lemma 5. All genuine
integrability and weighted continuity therefore descend by the exact
pushforward integral law. In particular \[
 E_k(u)-E_k(v)=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^n
    \int_T(u-v)\,d\operatorname{MA}_k(u^{[j]},v^{[n-j]}).
 \tag{13}
\] The signed difference is taken on the common full-measure set where
both finite-energy tests are finite. No infinite arithmetic at supported
poles is used. Constant shifts preserve the operator and add \(Vc\) to
the energy.

The space and measure condition are independent of the chosen continuous

 \tag{15}

 \tag{16}

\subsection{Intrinsic targets, actual attainment, and every-solution
uniqueness}\label{intrinsic-targets-actual-attainment-and-every-solution-uniqueness}

The continuous-test definition (3) equals the supremum over all

finite subcover, and one directed index dominates that cover. Inner
regularity, finite level partitions and truncation give the integral

\(J_k\) is finite, the integral of every normalized \(-u\) is finite.
Continuous tests are already finite-energy tests, so the two suprema are
equal.

The same reasoning in the ample B domain identifies (4) with its
continuous-test supremum. The decisive equality is \[
 J_k(\mu)=J_{\bar k}(\widetilde\mu).
 \tag{18}

 Av=\int_G v\circ g\,dg.
\] This is a continuous psh function: Haar integrals are uniformly
approximated by finite convex combinations of conjugates, all on the

such uniform limits. Finite concavity, Galois invariance of energy, and

Invariance of \(\widetilde\mu\) gives
\(\int Av\,d\widetilde\mu=\int v\,d\widetilde\mu\). By Lemma 4, \(Av\)
descends to a continuous base psh test. Its base objective is at least
the original upstairs objective, proving (18). Only continuous functions
are averaged; no averaging or subtraction of unbounded poles occurs.

Consequently \(J_k(\mu)<\infty\) is exactly B's intrinsic target
condition for \(\widetilde\mu\). To check the converse intrinsic
characterization using only original base tests, suppose every
normalized base E1 potential is \(\mu\)-integrable. Write
\(B_k(u)=-E_k(u)\) and \(I_\mu(u)=\int(-u)\,d\mu\). Their integrals are

 p_m=\sum_{j=1}^m2^{-j}u_j+2^{-m}0.

 B_k(ru)\leq C_0r^2B_k(u),\qquad C_0=2^{n+1},\quad0\leq r\leq1.

proving \(J_k(\mu)<\infty\). This argument averages no singular
functions and uses only a countable convex series. No support
restriction is imposed by the lifting operation.

scale to radius one and use reduction of polynomials; products of
nonzero residue polynomials stay nonzero. Other radii follow by
limits, since $|C^*|$ is dense in $\mathbb R_{>0}$: roots of an
element of nonunit norm give a dense divisible logarithmic subgroup.

Here is the disk classification needed at possible endpoints.
For any seminorm point $x$ on the affine chart, put
$r=\inf_{a\in C}|T-a|_x$. If the infimum is attained at $a$,
ultrametricity and minimality give
$|T-b|_x=\max(r,|a-b|)$ for every $b$; factorization of polynomials
then identifies $x$ with that disk point, allowing $r=0$.
If it is not attained, choose $a_j$ with
$r_j=|T-a_j|_x\downarrow r$. The disks $B(a_j,r_j)$ are nested
after choosing the numbers strictly decreasing. Indeed
$|a_i-a_j|\leq\max(r_i,r_j)$. For every $b$,
\[
 |T-b|_x\leq\max(r_j,|a_j-b|)
                \leq\max(r_j,|T-b|_x),
\]
so their disk seminorms tend to $x$ on all polynomials.
The disks have empty intersection, since a point in their
intersection would attain the infimum. Their limiting radius is
positive by completeness: if it were zero their centers would
form a Cauchy sequence with a point in every disk.
These are the terminal type IV points. Radius-zero disks are
the terminal type I points; adjoining infinity completes the
projective line.

Ordered chains of disks give arcs: between two disk points go to
the least containing disk and then down the other chain. Its radius
is $\max(r,s,|a-b|)$ for disks $(a,r),(b,s)$. Nested-disk endpoints
give the corresponding limits; the increasing-radius chain gives
the arc to infinity. Strict disk inequalities separate the branches,
so these arcs are unique. Comparable positive-radius disks have
length $|\log r-\log s|$. This constructs the rooted tree, without
asserting that its full path metric defines the analytic topology.
Every terminal point is approached on its root arc by finite
positive-radius disk points. Convergence follows by evaluating
linear polynomials in the displayed disk formulas, factorization,
and the second affine chart at infinity. Density of $|C^*|$ also
allows type II points on every nondegenerate finite subarc.

Write $g(x,y)$ for the length of
$[\xi,x]\cap[\xi,y]$, permitting infinity on a diagonal terminal
pair. For a finite disk point $y=\zeta(a,r)$ set
$b_y=\log\max(1,|a|,r)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 u_y=\log\max\{|T_1-aT_0|,r|T_0|\}
              -\log\max\{|T_0|,|T_1|\}-b_y.
\end{equation}
This is a continuous relative FS scalar and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atom}
                 u_y=-g(\,\cdot\,,y),\qquad
                 u_y(\xi)=0,\qquad \MA(u_y)=\delta_y.
\end{equation}
The first identity follows directly from disk radii. Inside the
unit disk its two sides are
$\log\max\{|T-a|,r\}$ minus their Gauss value. On the outer
branch the denominator contributes $\log\max(1,|T|)$.
If $|a|>\max(1,r)$, the path goes first from radius one to
$|a|$ and then down toward $a$, with total length
$2\log|a|-\log r$. These three disk configurations, and their
limits at endpoints and infinity, give the identity everywhere.

For the measure identity, first let $r=|c|$ with $c\in C^*$.
Use the standard $\mathbb P^1_{C^\circ}$ model with generic
coordinate sections $cT_0,T_1-aT_0$. Its generic identification is
an invertible linear change of homogeneous coordinates. The
model reference is the FS metric in \eqref{eq:kernel}, up to a
finite constant. Its reduced special fiber is a single projective
line, with $\mathcal O(1)$ degree one, and its generic Shilov point
projects to $\zeta(a,r)$. The actual model formula
\cite[Example 8.11 and (8.3)]{BE} therefore gives exactly
$\delta_y$. No field-degree or logarithmic-base factor enters.
For an arbitrary radius choose $r_j\in|C^*|$ tending to $r$.
Formula~\eqref{eq:kernel} converges uniformly, and the points
$\zeta(a,r_j)$ converge analytically to $y$. Uniform continuity
of the genuine continuous operator gives the same identity.

\section{Finite-tree potentials with genuine target measures}

Let $\Gamma$ range over convex hulls of $\xi$ and finitely many
finite disk points. These are compact finite metric trees; their
edges consist of positive-radius disk points. The family is
directed by inclusion. Let $r_\Gamma$ take $x$ to the last point
of $[\xi,x]$ in $\Gamma$. For every $y\in\Gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:retraction}
 g(r_\Gamma x,y)=g(x,y),\qquad
 g_\Gamma(x,z):=g(r_\Gamma x,r_\Gamma z).
\end{equation}
The retraction is continuous in the analytic topology. To check
this directly, take the finite leaves $y_1,\ldots,y_m$ of the
rooted tree. The functions $g(\,\cdot\,,y_i)=-u_{y_i}$ are
continuous and separate its points: for two comparable points
take a leaf beyond the farther one; for points on different
branches take a leaf beyond either branch. They embed the compact
tree into $\mathbb R^m$. The coordinates of $r_\Gamma x$ equal
$g(x,y_i)$, proving continuity. The kernel on $\Gamma^2$ is
continuous and obeys
\[
 |g(x,y)-g(x,z)|\leq\rho(y,z)\quad(x\in Z,\ y,z\in\Gamma).
\]
This follows because the shared initial root length changes by
at most the length between $y$ and $z$. Consequently $g_\Gamma$
is bounded and jointly continuous on $Z^2$.

For a Radon probability $\mu$, let
$\mu_\Gamma=(r_\Gamma)_*\mu$, a genuine positive Radon
probability. It can be diffuse along edges. Define
\[
 u_\Gamma(x)=-\int_\Gamma g(x,y)\,d\mu_\Gamma(y)
            =-\int_Zg_\Gamma(x,z)\,d\mu(z).
\]
Approximate this integral uniformly by finite convex sums of
the $u_y$: partition the finite tree into finitely many Borel
sets of small metric diameter, choose one point in each set,
and use its actual measure as coefficient. The displayed
Lipschitz bound controls the uniform error. Each finite sum is
continuous actual psh by real convexity on the fixed bundle.
Uniform closure gives a continuous actual psh $u_\Gamma$.
Affine curvature calculus and \eqref{eq:atom}, followed by
uniform continuity, give the \emph{genuine} identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GammaMA}
                     \MA(u_\Gamma)=\mu_\Gamma.
\end{equation}
The atomic approximations converge weakly to $\mu_\Gamma$ on
the compact finite tree; its inclusion into $Z$ is continuous.
This establishes the target measure rather than defining a
Hessian by naming it.

The measures $\mu_\Gamma$ tend weakly to $\mu$. Indeed the
continuous functions $g(\,\cdot\,,y)$ for all finite disk points
$y$ separate all points of $Z$. If two points lie on different
root branches, choose a disk point on one branch past their
meeting point; if they are comparable, choose a disk point
strictly beyond the nearer point. Terminal points are handled
by the disk approximations above. Together with constants,
the real algebra generated by these functions is uniformly
dense in $C(Z)$ by Stone--Weierstrass. For each element of this
algebra, retraction to every sufficiently large $\Gamma$ leaves
it unchanged by \eqref{eq:retraction}. Uniform approximation
then proves convergence against every continuous test. This is
a directed-net argument; no cofinal sequence is asserted.

\section{Actual existence from the continuous-test dual}

As $\Gamma$ increases, the common-root kernel $g_\Gamma$
increases: on each pair of arcs its value is the smaller of
their retained shared initial lengths. Hence $u_\Gamma$ decreases,
and $u_\Gamma(\xi)=0$. Its all-point endpoint $u$ is actual psh
by decreasing closure \cite[Corollary 7.5]{BE}, or its
reconstruction in N. The anchor excludes the identically
$-\infty$ endpoint.

Put
\[
 I_\Gamma=\int_{Z^2}g_\Gamma(x,z)\,d\mu(x)d\mu(z).
\]
The bounded continuous kernel justifies Fubini. The curve energy
identity and \eqref{eq:GammaMA} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
 E(u_\Gamma)=-\tfrac12 I_\Gamma,\qquad
 \int_Zu_\Gamma\,d\mu=-I_\Gamma,\qquad
 E(u_\Gamma)-\int_Zu_\Gamma\,d\mu=\tfrac12 I_\Gamma.
\end{equation}
For the first equality use $u_\Gamma(\xi)=0$ and
$g_\Gamma(r_\Gamma x,r_\Gamma z)=g_\Gamma(x,z)$.
If $J(\mu)<\infty$, these continuous actual tests imply
$I_\Gamma\leq2J(\mu)$. Decreasing energy continuity therefore
gives $E(u)\geq-J(\mu)>-\infty$. Thus the endpoint is in the
\emph{actual} $\E^1$ cone. Grant O applies to its defining
decreasing continuous net and yields
\[
 \MA(u)=\lim_\Gamma\MA(u_\Gamma)
             =\lim_\Gamma\mu_\Gamma=\mu.
\]
No abstract Hilbert-space element is substituted for an actual
potential; no compactness or envelope theorem is used.

\section{Uniqueness at all points and the variational value}

For normalized actual curve potentials $p,q\in\E^1$, O gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetry}
                     \int_Zp\,\MA(q)=\int_Zq\,\MA(p).
\end{equation}
Here is a derivation from its exact granted statements.
$E(p)=\frac12\int p\,\MA(p)$ because $p(\xi)=0$.
Expand this identity on the actual segment $(1-t)p+tq$ using
affine curvature calculus. Its derivative at zero is
$-\int p\MA(p)+\frac12(\int p\MA(q)+\int q\MA(p))$.
The granted segment derivative is
$\int(q-p)\MA(p)$. Equality gives \eqref{eq:symmetry}.
Every integral is finite by O; common full-measure representatives
avoid subtraction of pole values.

If $\MA(p)=\mu$, apply \eqref{eq:symmetry} with $q=u_y$ for
any finite disk point. Since $\MA(u_y)=\delta_y$, O also
ensures $p(y)$ is finite, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:values}
                         p(y)=-\int_Zg(z,y)\,d\mu(z).
\end{equation}
Thus every normalized solution has the same values at all finite
disk points. We must still recover its values at every terminal
point.

Every relative FS scalar is nonincreasing on each path away
from $\xi$. To verify this for a homogeneous section, factor
it over $C$ into linear forms. A nonzero linear form with zero
$a\in\mathbb P^1(C)$ has normalized log norm equal to its
Gauss log norm minus $g(x,a)$; this is the radius-zero limit
of \eqref{eq:kernel}, and for $a=\infty$ follows from the
second chart. Each common-root length increases along a
root path. Products add log norms, real weights add constants,
and finite maxima preserve nonincrease. This proves the claim
for every weighted FS system, and taking decreasing FS-net
endpoints proves it for every actual psh scalar.

Let $x$ be terminal and let finite disk ancestors $y_i$ tend
to $x$ on $[\xi,x]$. The preceding monotonicity gives
$p(y_i)\downarrow\ell\geq p(x)$ in the extended reals.
An actual psh scalar is upper semicontinuous, as an infimum
of continuous FS scalars, so
$p(x)\geq\limsup_i p(y_i)=\ell$. Consequently
$p(x)=\ell$, even when the value is $-\infty$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:values} determines all these limits.
The finite disk points together with terminal points exhaust
$Z$, proving all-point uniqueness modulo constants.

For any actual solution $u\in\E^1$, O supplies integrability
of every $\E^1$ test against $\mu=\MA(u)$. Concavity and
the granted derivative at the initial point of the segment
from $u$ to $v$ give
\[
 E(v)-E(u)\leq\int_Z(v-u)\,d\mu\qquad(v\in\E^1).
\]
Thus $u$ maximizes the stated functional. Continuous psh
majorants decreasing to $u$ converge in energy and in their
integrals against the fixed output $\mu$ by O's weighted
continuity, with constant curvature slots. Hence the
continuous-test supremum equals its value at $u$.
For the constructed solution, the same weighted continuity
and \eqref{eq:cost} give
\[
 J(\mu)=\tfrac12\sup_\Gamma I_\Gamma,
 \qquad E(u)=-\tfrac12\sup_\Gamma I_\Gamma,
 \qquad \int_Zu\,d\mu=-\sup_\Gamma I_\Gamma.
\]
These equalities involve finite quantities once the target
condition is established.

\section{Arbitrary ground fields and actual rational bundles}
\label{sec:descent}

Suppose first that $K$ is nontrivially valued and let
$C=\widehat{\overline K}$. Let
$\pi:(\mathbb P^1_C)^\an\to Z$ and
$G=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K/K)$. All quotient and
invariance assertions in this paragraph are the full grant D,
including actual singular endpoints, rather than finite-level
or skeletal replacements. The unique invariant Radon lift
$\widetilde\mu$ has mass one and
\[
                  J_C(\widetilde\mu)=J_K(\mu)<\infty.
\]
The construction above gives an actual normalized upstairs
solution $\widetilde u\in\E_C^1$. The standard reference
and its Gauss point are $G$-invariant. For each $g\in G$,
naturality of the continuous operator and its defining
decreasing limit make $g^*\widetilde u$ a solution of the
same lifted target. Upstairs all-point uniqueness and the
common normalization imply $g^*\widetilde u=\widetilde u$.
Actual singular descent in D yields
$\widetilde u=\pi^*u$ with $u\in\E_K^1$. Genuine
covariance in O then gives $\MA(u)=\mu$.

If $v$ is any downstairs solution, its pullback is actual
finite energy by O. Its upstairs measure is invariant and
pushes to $\mu$; uniqueness of the invariant Radon lift in D
identifies it with $\widetilde\mu$. Upstairs uniqueness
therefore identifies the normalized pullback with
$\widetilde u$. Surjectivity of $\pi$ gives equality at
\emph{every} downstairs analytic point.
No averaging of singular potentials or of their pole values
has been performed. If $K$ is trivially valued, grant T
already establishes exactly this actual ample equation and
every-solution uniqueness; the nontrivial tree argument is
not applied to that case.

Finally $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1_K)=\mathbb Z$. Indeed a
generic trivialization expresses a line bundle on this smooth curve
by a divisor. An affine closed point cut out by an irreducible
polynomial of degree $d$ is linearly equivalent to $d[\infty]$
by that polynomial's divisor. Hence every divisor is equivalent to
its degree times $[\infty]$, and a principal divisor has degree
zero. Consequently an
actual ample rational bundle has degree $a\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$
and is identified with $\mathcal O(a)$. Choose reference
$a\psi$. Clearing denominators in FS systems and scaling
their weights shows that $u=av$ identifies its actual psh
cone with that of $\mathcal O(1)$. Continuous curvature
linearity and the continuous energy identity give
\[
 \MA_a(av)=a\MA_1(v),\qquad E_a(av)=a^2E_1(v).
\]
The correspondence of continuous majorants extends the
energy identity to actual singular endpoints, and O's
decreasing continuity extends the operator identity.
For a raw-mass-$a$ target, set $\nu=\mu/a$; then
$J_a(\mu)=a^2J_1(\nu)$ and all-test integrability is
equivalent under the same scalar correspondence. Existence
and uniqueness follow. A change to any continuous psh
reference changes test scalars by a bounded continuous
function and energy by the reference cocycle in O.
The dual changes by a finite constant, so its finiteness,
the intrinsic condition, the absolute actual equation and
every-solution uniqueness are reference-independent.

\begin{abstract}
We derive strict Borel winner locality, ordinary open-set locality
and mixed comparison for actual finite-energy non-Archimedean
potentials. The domain is a smooth geometrically integral projective
variety of any positive dimension over any complete characteristic-zero
real-valued non-Archimedean field, with an actual ample rational
bundle. The prerequisite is the companion direct finite-energy
mixed-operator and joint weighted-net theorem. Continuous locality
comes from the local uniformly approachable CLD construction.
A bounded Lipschitz test argument transports it to singular
endpoints without assuming convergence of arbitrary Borel tests
under weak convergence. Raw mass then gives comparison. The
companion prerequisite and the new proofs require independent
verification before this partial result can be accepted. No
prescribed-target equation, compactness or envelope theorem is
asserted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Actual potentials and the precise prerequisite}

Let $K$ be any complete characteristic-zero real-valued
non-Archimedean field, including trivial or nondiscrete valuation.
Let $X/K$ be smooth, geometrically integral and projective, with
$n=\dim X\geq1$, and $L$ an actual ample rational line bundle.
Fix a continuous psh reference $\psi$ and put
$T=X^{\mathrm{an}}$, $V=(L^n)>0$.
Every scalar psh potential $u$ represents the actual absolute
metric $\psi+u$, defined at all points as a decreasing-FS-net
endpoint in \cite[Definition 7.2]{BE}. Define its extended relative
energy by the infimum of continuous psh majorant energies, and
$\E^1$ by finiteness of that energy. No synthetic or abstract
potential is substituted for an endpoint.

We use exactly the following operator conclusions of the full
companion manuscript \cite{O}; until that complete prerequisite is
independently checked, the present new derivation is conditional
on it. It states that finite positive convex combinations and finite
maxima preserve the actual $\E^1$ cone, and that:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Actual $u_1,\ldots,u_n\in\E^1$ have a canonical positive
Radon mixed measure $\MA(u_1,\ldots,u_n)$ of raw mass $V$, agreeing
with the genuine continuous operator. Constants in a slot do not
change it. Continuous decreasing approximations and arbitrary
decreasing finite-energy slot nets with finite-energy endpoints give
its weak limit.
\item Every actual $w\in\E^1$ is absolutely integrable against
each such mixed output, which therefore gives zero mass to $\{w=-\infty\}$.
\item If every slot decreases to its actual $\E^1$ endpoint and
also $w_i\downarrow w$ in $\E^1$ with $w\in\E^1$, then on any
common directed index set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prerequisite}
 \int_T w_i\,\MA(u_{1,i},\ldots,u_{n,i})
       \longrightarrow\int_T w\,\MA(u_1,\ldots,u_n).
\end{equation}
Constant weight nets are included. No locality or comparison is part
of this prerequisite.
\end{enumerate}

The companion establishes its operator from ordered continuous
Dirichlet estimates, dense scalar tests and positive polarization,
before selecting any target. Its weighted theorem uses a uniform
unbounded-test energy bound, rather than a measurable-net
monotone-convergence assertion. That full body, including every
generated dependency, is part of the verification chain needed here.
We do not replace it by a claim that a finite-energy definition alone
constructs the operator.

Finite maxima lie in $\E^1$ for an elementary reason: the actual
psh maximum is given by \cite[Corollary 7.5]{BE}, is not identically
$-\infty$, and has energy at least that of either finite-energy
operand. Constants preserve the cone by the continuous constant
law followed by its majorant infimum. These facts justify all
maxima and shifted potentials below.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
With the complete prerequisite above established, let $u,v\in\E^1$,
$m=\max(u,v)$, and $r_1,\ldots,r_{n-1}\in\E^1$.
Write $R=(r_1,\ldots,r_{n-1})$, with $R$ empty when $n=1$.
The genuine mixed operator has strict Borel winner locality:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict}
 \begin{split}
 \mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}\MA(m,R)&=
                  \mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}\MA(u,R),\\
 \mathbf1_{\{v>u\}}\MA(m,R)&=
                  \mathbf1_{\{v>u\}}\MA(v,R).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
If $u=v$ at every point of an open subset $U\subset T$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:open-local}
                  \MA(u,R)|_U=\MA(v,R)|_U.
\end{equation}
The mixed comparison principle is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison}
 \int_{\{u<v\}}\MA(v,R)\leq
                    \int_{\{u<v\}}\MA(u,R).
\end{equation}
The pure finite-energy comparison principle also holds:
\[
                  \MA(v)\{u<v\}\leq\MA(u)\{u<v\}.
\]
Its separate slotwise derivation is given below.
\end{theorem}

All winner sets are Borel: the scalar potentials are upper
semicontinuous. Differences will only be evaluated where the finitely
many involved potentials are finite. Each relevant output gives
zero mass to the excluded pole sets by the prerequisite. The literal
strict sets in \eqref{eq:strict} and \eqref{eq:comparison} include
points where only one operand is infinite, but those have zero
mass for every measure used. No arithmetic at common infinities
is required, and no countable or uncountable common full-measure
intersection across the entire net is claimed.

\section{Locality of the genuine continuous operator}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:continuous}
For continuous fixed-$L$ psh $u,v$ and continuous remaining slots
$R$, the identities \eqref{eq:strict} hold on the ordinary open
winner sets.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The continuous psh metrics, including their maximum, are globally
uniform limits of smooth semipositive metrics by
\cite[Theorem 7.14]{BE}. On a local line-bundle trivialization their
weights are therefore locally psh-approachable in precisely
\cite[Definition 5.6.3 and Section 6.3.1]{CLD}, without needing an
assertion that all continuous CLD-psh functions are approachable.

The positive local product in \cite[Corollaries 5.6.5--5.6.6]{CLD}
is defined by uniform smooth approximation and is a morphism of
sheaves. Its complete construction checks that products on two
open sets agree on their intersection. Curvature weights transform
by the logarithm of an invertible analytic section; its Hessian
vanishes, as in \cite[Section 6.4.1]{CLD}. Hence this is the same
local metric curvature product in different trivializations.

It also agrees with the genuine global continuous measure used
here. A global uniform smooth regularization converges to the BE
measure weakly on continuous tests, by \cite[Theorem 8.4]{BE}.
On each trivializing open set the same sequence converges to the
CLD local current on compactly supported smooth tests by
Corollary 5.6.5. Every such smooth function, extended by zero,
is a global continuous test. Thus the global restriction and the
local top current have the same smooth-test values. The measure
injectivity in \cite[Proposition 5.4.6]{CLD}, proved there by local
smooth density with compact support, makes them the same Radon
measure. Analytification is boundaryless, so its entire carrier is
in the interior domain of this local calculus.

On the open set $\{u>v\}$, the local absolute weight of the maximum
equals that of $u$. The sheaf property therefore identifies the
mixed products there. Interchanging $u,v$ proves the second identity.
\end{proof}

\section{Nonlinear tests along moving finite-energy measures}

The following lemma is the passage from weak convergence and the
weighted prerequisite to the Borel cutoffs needed for locality.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lipschitz}
Let $\rho_i$ be finite-energy mixed outputs whose slots decrease
to actual finite-energy endpoints, and let $\rho$ be their weak
mixed limit. For $a_1,\ldots,a_q\in\E^1$, every bounded Lipschitz
$F:\mathbb R^q\to\mathbb R$ and $f\in C(T)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-nonlinear}
 \int_T fF(a_1,\ldots,a_q)\,d\rho_i
       \longrightarrow\int_T fF(a_1,\ldots,a_q)\,d\rho.
\end{equation}
If also $a_{s,i}\downarrow a_s$ in $\E^1$ on the same index set,
then \eqref{eq:fixed-nonlinear} holds with $a_{s,i}$ on the left.
Define $F$ to be zero outside the finite-value domain of its
arguments; this changes none of the integrals.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose continuous psh $a_{s,k}\downarrow a_s$ for each $s$.
For the single Radon measure $\rho$,
\[
               \int_T(a_{s,k}-a_s)\,d\rho\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here is the exact net justification. On a tail all continuous
$a_{s,k}$ have a common bound $C$, and $C-a_{s,k}$ are increasing
nonnegative continuous functions. Their strict superlevel sets
increase through a directed open cover of each superlevel of
$C-a_s$. Inner compact approximation and finite subcovers give
the equality of superlevel masses with their suprema. A common
index approximates any finite lower layer sum; truncating height
and refining layers proves convergence of the integrals, including
infinite integrals. Integrability of $a_s$ supplied by the
prerequisite makes the present integrals finite. Subtraction then
gives the displayed error limit. No arbitrary measurable-net
monotone convergence is invoked.

For each fixed continuous stage $k$, the constant-weight case of
\eqref{eq:prerequisite} and ordinary weak convergence give
\[
 \int_T(a_{s,k}-a_s)\,d\rho_i
          \longrightarrow\int_T(a_{s,k}-a_s)\,d\rho.
\]
Choose one fixed stage for each of the finitely many $s$ so that
these limiting errors are as small as desired. They are then
uniformly small on a common $i$ tail. If $M_F$ is a Lipschitz
constant for the $\ell^1$ norm, the integral difference between
the actual nonlinear test and this fixed continuous-stage test
is bounded by
\[
       \|f\|_\infty M_F
            \sum_{s=1}^q\int_T(a_{s,k}-a_s)\,d\rho_i.
\]
The analogous bound holds for $\rho$. The fixed-stage test is
continuous, so its integrals pass through weak convergence.
The two error bounds prove \eqref{eq:fixed-nonlinear}.

For moving arguments the joint weighted law and its constant-weight
case give
\[
 \int_T(a_{s,i}-a_s)\,d\rho_i
     =\int_Ta_{s,i}\,d\rho_i-\int_Ta_s\,d\rho_i
             \longrightarrow0.
\]
Every difference is nonnegative and finite on its per-index
full-measure finite-value set. The Lipschitz bound again makes
the error in replacing all moving arguments by their fixed
endpoints tend to zero. Apply \eqref{eq:fixed-nonlinear}.
\end{proof}

This lemma uses the operator's weighted theorem; weak convergence
alone would not justify the asserted Borel-test convergence.

\section{Strict Borel winner locality}

Take independent continuous psh decreasing approximations of
$u,v,r_1,\ldots,r_{n-1}$, and use their common product index $i$.
Write the approximants as $u_i,v_i,R_i$ and put
$m_i=\max(u_i,v_i)\downarrow m=\max(u,v)$.
All endpoints lie in $\E^1$. Define
\[
 \rho_i=\MA(m_i,R_i),\quad \sigma_i=\MA(u_i,R_i),\qquad
 \rho=\MA(m,R),\quad \sigma=\MA(u,R).
\]
For $\epsilon>0$ let $\chi_\epsilon(d)$ equal zero for $d\leq0$,
$d/\epsilon$ for $0<d<\epsilon$, and one for $d\geq\epsilon$.
The function $F_\epsilon(a,b)=\chi_\epsilon(a-b)$ is bounded and
Lipschitz on $\mathbb R^2$. Continuous winner locality gives
\[
 \int_T f\chi_\epsilon(u_i-v_i)\,d\rho_i
             =\int_T f\chi_\epsilon(u_i-v_i)\,d\sigma_i
                         \qquad(f\in C(T)).
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:lipschitz} separately to these two mixed-output
nets, with moving arguments $u_i,v_i$. It passes this equality to
\[
 \int_T f\chi_\epsilon(u-v)\,d\rho
             =\int_T f\chi_\epsilon(u-v)\,d\sigma.
\]
Outside the finite-value domain define the cutoff to be zero.
The two measures of all excluded pole sets are zero.

Multiplying a finite Radon measure by a bounded nonnegative Borel
function gives another finite Radon measure on this compact
Hausdorff space. Indeed compact inner and open outer approximations
to any Borel set for the original measure have weighted error
bounded by the multiplier's supremum times the original error.
Thus equality on all continuous tests identifies the two weighted
Radon measures. Let $\epsilon$ decrease along the ordinary sequence
$1/j$. On the finite-value domain the cutoffs increase to
$\mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}$. Ordinary single-measure convergence for each
of the two finite measures gives the first identity in
\eqref{eq:strict}. On the excluded pole sets both sides are zero,
so it holds for the literal winner set. Interchanging the operands
gives the second identity. There is no need to assume continuity of
a singular winner set or convergence of its indicators under weak
measure convergence.

\section{Open locality and mixed comparison}

Suppose $u=v$ on an open set $U$. For $\epsilon>0$ put

 \MA(v,R)\{u<v-\epsilon\}

   &=V-\MA(u,R)\{u>v-\epsilon\}\\
   &=\MA(u,R)\{u\leq v-\epsilon\}\\
   &\leq\MA(u,R)\{u<v\}.
\end{align*}
All set comparisons hold on the common finite-value full-measure

repeating fixed slots in that inequality: those slots must also
change from $u$ to $v$. Apply strict winner locality in each slot
of $\MA(m_\epsilon,\ldots,m_\epsilon)$ in turn. On
$\{u<v-\epsilon\}$ it equals $\MA(v)$; on

calculation therefore gives
\[
             \MA(v)\{u<v\}\leq\MA(u)\{u<v\}.

Each intermediate slot replacement is justified by the already
proved one-slot locality with the other actual finite-energy slots
held fixed. No incorrect substitution of varying fixed slots into

\subsection{2. Actual algebraic splitting and bundle
identification}\label{actual-algebraic-splitting-and-bundle-identification}

\textbf{Lemma 2.} There is a finite Galois extension \(E/K\) and an
algebraic isomorphism \(X_E\simeq\mathbb P^1_E\). Over every extension
of \(E\), the actual pulled-back rational bundle is \(\mathcal O(a)\),
with its unchanged geometric degree \(a\).

\textbf{Proof.} Coherent cohomology of \(\mathcal O_X\) commutes with
any field extension here. A finite affine cover of the separated
projective scheme has affine intersections, and its Čech complex
computes this cohomology. Tensoring that complex with the extension
field is exact, so its cohomology tensors with that field. In particular
\(H^1(X_{\overline K},\mathcal O)=0\).

Global regular functions on a geometrically integral proper curve over
an algebraically closed field are constants: a nonconstant global
regular function would give a nonconstant proper morphism to the affine
line, whose image would be the affine line; the affine line is not
proper. The proper image of a proper variety is proper, whereas a proper
closed subset of the affine line is finite. Integrality then forces a
constant. Together with the same cohomology base change, this also gives
\(H^0(X_F,\mathcal O)=F\) for every field extension \(F/K\).

Choose a point \(P\in X(\overline K)\), defined over a finite extension
\(E_0/K\). On the smooth curve \(X_{E_0}\) it is an effective Cartier
divisor of degree one. The exact sequence \[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O\longrightarrow\mathcal O(P)
\longrightarrow\mathcal O(P)|_P\longrightarrow0
\] and the vanishing of \(H^1(\mathcal O)\) show that
\(H^0(\mathcal O(P))\) has dimension two and surjects onto the
one-dimensional fiber at \(P\). Its canonical section is nonzero away
from \(P\), and a section mapping to a nonzero fiber value generates at
\(P\). Thus these two sections have no common zero. They define a
nonconstant morphism to \(\mathbb P^1_{E_0}\) whose pullback of
\(\mathcal O(1)\) is \(\mathcal O(P)\).

Here is a direct proof that this morphism is an isomorphism, avoiding a
hidden finite-map or Riemann--Roch input. Write its affine coordinate as
\(f\). The chosen nonzero fiber value says that \(f\) has exactly one
pole, at \(P\), of order one. For each \(n\geq1\), the exact sequence
for \((n-1)P\) and \(nP\) gives, by induction, \(H^1(\mathcal O(nP))=0\)
and \(\dim H^0(\mathcal O(nP))=n+1\). The functions \(1,f,\ldots,f^n\)
have distinct pole orders, so form a basis. For any rational function
\(q\) on the curve, its poles form a finite set. At each pole
\(R\ne P\), \(f\) is regular and its residue is algebraic over \(E_0\).
A polynomial in \(f\) vanishing at that residue has positive vanishing
order at \(R\). A product \(h(f)\) of sufficiently high powers of these
polynomials cancels all poles of \(q\) away from \(P\). Therefore
\(h(f)q\) belongs to some \(H^0(\mathcal O(nP))\) and is a polynomial in
\(f\). Since \(f\) is nonconstant, \(h(f)\ne0\). Thus \(E_0(X)=E_0(f)\),
giving a rational inverse to the displayed morphism.

This inverse extends across every closed point of \(\mathbb P^1\): its
local ring is a discrete valuation ring, and the valuative criterion of
properness of \(X_{E_0}\) extends the generic inverse uniquely to its
spectrum. Finite presentation extends each such local morphism to a
neighborhood (choose an affine chart around its closed image and clear
the finitely many denominators); separatedness makes the extensions
agree. The two compositions agree with the identity generically and
hence everywhere on the integral curves. This proves splitting over
\(E_0\). Enlarge \(E_0\) to its finite Galois closure; characteristic
zero ensures separability.

For the bundle, \(\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1_E)=\mathbb Z\) by
degree. A generic trivialization writes any line bundle as a divisor; a
finite closed point defined by an irreducible polynomial of degree \(d\)
is equivalent to \(d[\infty]\) by that polynomial's divisor. Principal
divisors have degree zero. After clearing the rational denominator, the
pulled-back bundle is therefore \(\mathcal O(ma)\) for an integer
\(ma\), and the rational bundle is \(\mathcal O(a)\). Intersection
degree is unchanged by field extension. There is no factor \([E:K]\) in
\(a\). This is an identification of the actual bundle, not only its
numerical class. \(\square\)

The argument supplies the precise splitting antecedent needed to apply
Q. It agrees with the projective-line characterization in the Stacks

53.10.4, but reconstructs the needed degree-one implication above
without importing its other cases or its Riemann--Roch chain. Ordinary
Čech cohomology, elementary rational-function/divisor calculus, and the
valuative criterion of properness are algebraic foundations, not
unproved arbitrary-field pluripotential inputs.

generated by translations, nonzero scalings and inversion. Translations
preserve radii and least containing disks. Scalings multiply all radii
and all center differences by the same norm, leaving logarithmic path
lengths unchanged. For inversion, a disk with \(r<|b|\) is sent to the

\subsection{7. An explicit nonsplit nondiscrete
example}\label{an-explicit-nonsplit-nondiscrete-example}

Completeness can be checked directly. From a Cauchy sequence choose a
subsequence whose successive differences have valuation greater than the
positive integer index. The sum of the first term and these differences

difference supports occur, and a finite union of well-ordered subsets is
well ordered. Any nonempty subset of the full support has a least
element by restricting below one of its elements. Coefficients at each
exponent are locally finite. The resulting series is the limit; the
original Cauchy sequence has the same limit. This absolute value gives a
metric, so sequential completeness is completeness. The leading
coefficient order makes \(K\) an ordered field.

\qquad L=\mathcal O_X(1),\qquad a=2.
\tag{14}

actual bundle pulls back to \(\mathcal O(2)\). The nontrivial Galois

\] as analytic section norms: the upper bound is ultrametricity, and the
