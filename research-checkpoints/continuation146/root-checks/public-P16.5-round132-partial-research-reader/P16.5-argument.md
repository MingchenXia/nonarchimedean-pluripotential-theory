# Scope and exact outcome

This paper does **not** solve original P16.5. Its status is **PARTIAL**, and its canonical candidate flag is false. The results proved here are as follows. First, the supplied counterexample to the abstract decreasing-closure lemma is valid; we give a proof for arbitrary nets, rather than just sequences. # 1. Objects and allowed premises

Throughout the geometric results, $k$ is an arbitrary algebraically closed trivially valued field of characteristic zero, $X$ is a smooth integral projective $k$-variety of dimension $n\geq1$, and $H$ is an actual ample rational line bundle. No restriction on the cardinality of $k$ is imposed. Write $h=c_1(H)$, and let $x_0$ be the generic trivial valuation. Every potential is an actual function on the original compact Hausdorff Berkovich space $X^{\rm an}$. A rational line bundle means an element of $\operatorname{Pic}(X)\otimes\mathbb Q$, with an actual integral tensor power whenever sections are used.

For an actual rational bundle $B$, an actual generically finite Fubini–Study potential is
\[
 \phi=\frac1m\max_{1\leq a\leq r}\bigl(\log|s_a|+\lambda_a\bigr),
 \qquad 0\ne s_a\in H^0(X,mB),\quad \lambda_a\in\mathbb R.
 \tag{1.1}
\]
The canonical trivial metric is used. The integer $m>0$ clears the denominator of $B$. A base ideal is allowed. Thus (1.1) can take the value $-\infty$; we denote this family by $\operatorname{FS}^{\rm gf}(B)$. We reserve $\operatorname{FS}^{\rm fin}(B)$ for the potentials that are finite real-valued at **every** analytic point. They are continuous real functions; this is a stronger condition than having a finite number of sections.

The genuine numerical cone is precisely
\[
 \operatorname{PSH}(\theta)=
 \left\{u:\ u(x_0)>-\infty,\quad
 u=\inf_{i\in I}\phi_i\text{ on all of }X^{\rm an},\quad
 \phi_i\in\operatorname{FS}^{\rm gf}(B_i),\quad
 \phi_i\searrow,\quad c_1(B_i)\longrightarrow\theta\right\}.
 \tag{1.2}
\]
Every indexing set in this formula is an arbitrary directed set. An infimum of continuous extended-real functions is usc, so usc is automatic for these displayed defining nets. We do not replace (1.2) by its transfinite closure, an abstract completion, or a divisorial closure. The question is whether the **one-net** class (1.2) is already closed under decreasing nets.

On a proper variety the section norms in the canonical trivial metric are at most one: at an analytic point use its valuation-ring center, trivialize the bundle there, and evaluate the regular local section. At $x_0$, a nonzero section has norm one. Consequently
\[
 \sup\phi=\phi(x_0)=\max_a\lambda_a/m,
 \qquad \sup u=u(x_0)\text{ for every }u\text{ in (1.2)}.
 \tag{1.3}
\]
Write $\operatorname{PSH}_{\sup}(\theta)=\{u\in\operatorname{PSH}(\theta):u(x_0)=0\}$.

We use the finite-dimensional numerical space $N^1(X)_{\mathbb R}$, the numerical invariance and openness of the ample cone, closedness of the nef cone, and generation of sufficiently divisible powers of an actual ample rational bundle. These are the ordinary algebraic positivity facts used in the supplied complete proof of BJ Lemma 4.9. They do not imply a nonample psh closure theorem. Global generation can be taken from the defining projective characterization of ampleness and Serre generation. Numerical ampleness and openness are classical algebraic background, whose full independent foundational proofs are not newly supplied here. This limitation is recorded in the ledger.

# 2. The abstract defect, independently checked

**Proposition 2.1 (non-idempotence with poles).** Stability under real constant addition and continuity into $\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}$ do not make decreasing-net closure idempotent.

**Proof.** Give $K=\{0\}\cup\{1/r:r\geq1\}$ its subspace topology from $\mathbb R$. It is compact. For $i,j\geq1$, define
\[
 f_{ij}(1/i)=-\infty,\qquad
 f_{ij}(1/r)=-j\ (r\leq i,\ r\ne i),\qquad
 f_{ij}(0)=f_{ij}(1/r)=0\ (r>i).
 \tag{2.1}
\]
Each point $1/r$ is isolated; the tail at 0 is identically zero. Thus each $f_{ij}$ is continuous into the usual extended-real space. Put $F_i=\{f_{ij}+c:j\geq1,c\in\mathbb R\}$, and give all labels the same value in the one-point metrizable target $T=\{*\}$.

For fixed $i$, the sequence $f_{ij}$ decreases to $g_i$, which is $-\infty$ at $1,1/2,\ldots,1/i$ and zero elsewhere. Hence each $g_i$ is in the first decreasing closure. The $g_i$ themselves decrease to the usc function $g$ with $g(0)=0$ and $g(1/r)=-\infty$ for every $r\geq1$.

Suppose an arbitrary decreasing net $a_\alpha\in\bigcup_i F_i$ had limit $g$. If two of its members had different poles $1/i$ and $1/i'$, a common later member would have to be $-\infty$ at both. Every member of the available family has exactly one pole, a contradiction. Thus **every** member of this net has one common pole $1/i$. At 0 and at every $1/r$ with $r>i$, each member has the same value $c_\alpha$. If its limit at 0 is zero, its limit at each of these $1/r$ is also zero, contrary to $g$. Therefore $g$ is not in the first closure. This argument uses directedness only and rules out arbitrary-cardinality nets. $\square$

In the supplied proof of BJ Lemma 4.6, inequality (4.5) asserts
\[
 g_{i_3}+\varepsilon_3<
 \min(f_{i_1,j_1}+\varepsilon_1,f_{i_2,j_2}+\varepsilon_2).
 \tag{2.2}
\]
At a shared pole the two sides can both be $-\infty$; then (2.2) is false. After exponentiation the asserted strictly negative limiting difference is zero. A weak inequality does not cover such points by the open sets used in the compactness argument. Proposition 2.1 proves that this is not just a missing justification of a true abstract statement.

This defect is established directly from the literal supplied proof, BJv4 source LF2241–2292, with the surrounding definition and theorem in LF2180–2335. The failed displayed comparison is literal LF2265. It does **not** refute genuine closure for the full algebraic Fubini–Study families. Those families have additional section operations absent in (2.1).

# 3. A valid finite-valued flattening theorem

**Theorem 3.1 (finite-valued version).** Let $K$ be compact, $T$ metrizable, and $F_s\subset C(K,\mathbb R)$ be families stable under real constant addition, with labels $\rho:S\to T$. The first decreasing-net closure with converging labels is closed under another decreasing net with converging labels. The limits may have poles. The crucial hypothesis is that the *defining approximants*, rather than their limits, are finite real-valued.

**Proof.** Fix a metric $d$ on $T$. Suppose $g_i\searrow g$, the outer labels $t_i\to t$, and for each $i$
\[
 f_{i,j}\searrow g_i,\quad f_{i,j}\in F_{s_{i,j}},\quad
 t_{i,j}:=\rho(s_{i,j})\to t_i.
\]
Consider triples $a=(i,j,\varepsilon)$, $\varepsilon>0$, ordered by
\[
 a\succeq a'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 i\succeq i',\quad f_{i,j}+\varepsilon\leq f_{i',j'}+\varepsilon',\quad
 d(t_{i,j},t_i)+\varepsilon\leq d(t_{i',j'},t_{i'})+\varepsilon'.
 \tag{3.1}
\]
This is a preorder, which suffices to index a net.

Given two triples, choose $i_3\succeq i_1,i_2$ and $0<\varepsilon_3<\min(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)$. The function
\[
 R=\min(f_{i_1,j_1}+\varepsilon_1,f_{i_2,j_2}+\varepsilon_2)
\]
is **finite continuous**. At every $x\in K$, including where $g_{i_3}(x)=-\infty$,
\[
 g_{i_3}(x)+\varepsilon_3<R(x).
\]
The continuous real functions
\[
 e^{f_{i_3,j}+\varepsilon_3}-e^R
\]
decrease to a strictly negative function at every point: at a pole the limit is $-e^{R(x)}<0$. Their negative open sets cover $K$. A finite subcover and directedness yield one index $j_3$ at which the difference is negative everywhere. Increase $j_3$ so that
\[
 d(t_{i_3,j_3},t_{i_3})+\varepsilon_3<\min(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2).
\]
This triple is later than both original triples. Hence (3.1) is directed.

Set $q_a=f_{i,j}+\varepsilon\in F_{s_{i,j}}$. It is decreasing and is at least $g$. For any $x\in K$, any finite $b>g(x)$, and any label tolerance $\delta>0$, first choose $i_0$ with $g_{i_0}(x)<b$ and $d(t_i,t)<\delta$ for all $i\succeq i_0$. Choose $j_0,\varepsilon_0>0$ with $f_{i_0,j_0}(x)+\varepsilon_0<b$ and $d(t_{i_0,j_0},t_{i_0})+\varepsilon_0<\delta$. Every later triple has $q_a(x)<b$ and $d(t_{i,j},t)<2\delta$. Finite thresholds $b$ handle $g(x)=-\infty$ without the meaningless expression $-\infty+\delta>-\infty$. Thus $q_a\searrow g$ at all points and its labels converge to $t$. No countable subfamily, sequential compactness, or Borel monotone-convergence assertion is used. $\square$

This repairs the compact-space argument only on its stated finite-valued domain. It does not assert the original extended-real lemma.

# 4. Actual section operations independent of disputed closure

**Lemma 4.1 (zero sections and tensor products).** If $C$ is an actual ample rational bundle, there is a finite section system on a divisible power of $C$ whose weight-zero Fubini–Study potential is identically zero. Consequently an actual 

\[
 \phi\in\operatorname{FS}^{\rm gf}(B)
\]

is the same function in $\operatorname{FS}^{\rm gf}(B+C)$. Products of actual section systems also realize the sum of two Fubini–Study potentials on the sum of their actual bundles.

**Proof.** Choose a divisible $q$ for which $qC$ is globally generated, and generators $t_b$. At any valuation-ring center at least one generator is a unit, of norm one, and all are regular of norm at most one. Hence $q^{-1}\max_b\log|t_b|=0$ on every analytic point, including semivaluations of proper support. For two systems clear denominators to a common $M$; replace each system by all tensor products of the necessary number of its sections, with summed weights. This represents the same normalized maximum on the same actual rational bundle. Tensor the two resulting systems. The elementary identity
\[
 \max_{a,b}(A_a+B_b)=\max_a A_a+\max_b B_b
\]
holds also with $-\infty$, and proves the assertions. The local base ideals are the corresponding actual products. No numerical replacement of an actual tensor factor is made. $\square$

**Lemma 4.2 (direct ample embedding).** Let $u\in\operatorname{PSH}(\theta)$ have a defining net (1.2). If $\Omega$ is an actual ample rational bundle with $c_1(\Omega)-\theta$ ample, then $u$, as the same all-point function, belongs to the genuine actual ample cone $\operatorname{PSH}(\Omega)$.

**Proof.** On a tail, $\Omega-B_i$ is an actual ample rational bundle, by openness and numerical ampleness. Apply Lemma 4.1 separately to each stage. The unchanged functions $\phi_i$ are now actual Fubini–Study potentials on the one actual bundle $\Omega$, with their original decreasing order and all-point limit. This is already one defining net. There is no flattening of psh limits in this argument. $\square$

One can choose such an $\Omega$ for any fixed rational $\theta=c_1(L)$, by taking a sufficiently large ample multiple of $H$. Ample divisorial determination therefore gives all-point determination for the **already genuine** functions in (1.2), and finite divisorial coordinates. It does not show that a new nonample limit is in (1.2).

For clarity, addition, finite maxima and integer scaling can also be proved without BJ Lemma 4.6. Addition uses the tensor systems in Lemma 4.1 on the product of two original defining nets. Multiplication by a positive integer uses tensor powers. For a maximum of two genuine functions in the same numerical class, write their defining stages as $\phi_i$ on $A_i$ and $\psi_j$ on $B_j$. Choose rational $\eta_{i,j,r}>0$, tending to zero on the product of the original index sets and $r\to\infty$, such that $A_i-B_j+\eta_{i,j,r}H$ is ample. This is possible uniformly near zero in the finite-dimensional numerical space, by openness of the ample cone. Set $C_{i,j,r}=A_i+\eta_{i,j,r}H$. Both $C_{i,j,r}-A_i$ and $C_{i,j,r}-B_j$ are actual ample rational bundles. Lemma 4.1 embeds both unchanged functions into this same actual bundle. Taking their union of section systems after denominator clearing realizes their maximum. Its function values decrease on the product net, independently of the chosen $\eta$; its classes tend to $\theta$, and its limit is the literal all-point maximum. Constants are added to the weights. These are constructive one-net operations, not a closure citation.

# 5. Entire nef closure and normalized compactness

**Theorem 5.1 (nef intersection characterization).** If $L$ is any actual nef rational line bundle, not assumed semiample, and $\theta=c_1(L)$, then as sets of actual all-point functions
\[
 \operatorname{PSH}(\theta)
 =\bigcap_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb Q_{>0}}
       \operatorname{PSH}(L+\varepsilon H).
 \tag{5.1}
\]
Every genuine function on the left admits **one** decreasing net of finite continuous actual Fubini–Study potentials on bundles $L+\varepsilon_aH$ with $\varepsilon_a\to0$.

**Proof.** Each $L+\varepsilon H$ is an actual ample rational bundle. The forward inclusion follows from Lemma 4.2; $\varepsilon h$ is an ample difference. Conversely, take an all-point function $u$ in the intersection. For every positive rational $\varepsilon$, the granted actual ample regularization writes $u$ as an all-point decreasing net in $\operatorname{FS}^{\rm fin}(L+\varepsilon H)$. Apply Theorem 3.1 to the constant outer functions $g_\varepsilon=u$, indexed by positive rational $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, with numerical labels $c_1(L+\varepsilon H)\to\theta$. The families are actual finite continuous FS systems, stable under adding constants to section weights. The resulting single decreasing net is therefore made of actual section maxima, with numerical classes tending to $\theta$, and has all-point limit $u$. Its generic value is finite because $u$ already belongs to an actual ample cone. This proves (1.2), (5.1), and the additional regularization statement. $\square$

**Theorem 5.2 (entire genuine nef decreasing closure).** For any decreasing net $u_i\in\operatorname{PSH}(\theta)$, with $\theta$ as in Theorem 5.1 and finite limiting generic value, its all-point limit $u$ belongs to $\operatorname{PSH}(\theta)$. One actual defining decreasing finite-FS net witnesses this membership, with numerical classes tending to $\theta$.

**Proof.** Fix $\varepsilon>0$. Lemma 4.2 puts the entire original net into the one actual ample cone $\operatorname{PSH}(L+\varepsilon H)$ as unchanged functions. The given generic limit is finite. Ample decreasing closure, or Theorem 3.1 applied to its granted finite regularizers, shows that the same all-point limit $u$ is genuinely psh there. This holds for every positive rational $\varepsilon$. The constructive reverse direction of Theorem 5.1 then supplies the one defining net for $u$ in $\theta$. At poles, all limits are literal pointwise limits; no equality of divisorial coordinates substitutes for membership. $\square$

**Theorem 5.3 (entire genuine normalized nef compactness).** The whole normalized genuine cone $\operatorname{PSH}_{\sup}(\theta)$ is compact for pointwise convergence on divisorial valuations, without metrizability or countability.

**Proof.** Write $K_\varepsilon=\operatorname{PSH}_{\sup}(L+\varepsilon H)$ for rational $0<\varepsilon\leq1$. The actual ample grant makes each $K_\varepsilon$ compact. For $\varepsilon<1$, Lemma 4.2 includes its unchanged all-point functions into $K_1$; the inclusion is continuous in divisorial coordinates. The topology of $K_1$ is Hausdorff by ample all-point determination. Thus each compact image $K_\varepsilon$ is closed in $K_1$.

Their intersection is compact. A point of this intersection is an actual function in $K_1$. If membership in another compact image is first expressed by equality of divisorial coordinates, the representative from $K_\varepsilon$ and this function both lie in the same actual ample $K_1$; granted determination identifies them at all points. Thus membership in the intersection is all-point membership in every $K_\varepsilon$, rather than a claim about an abstract coordinate vector. Theorem 5.1 identifies the intersection precisely with $\operatorname{PSH}_{\sup}(\theta)$. The coordinate topology is the original one, so the identification is a homeomorphism. $\square$

Theorems 5.1–5.3 handle all nef rational classes, including big nef classes without a semiample or finite-generation restriction. Their field and original-carrier hypotheses are exactly those stated in §1. No nonample envelope theorem, BJ Theorem 5.11, Addendum Corollary 2.2, or general-big compactness assertion has been imported. This is a repair of the decreasing-closure and compactness gate **on the nef domain only**; it constructs no entire unbounded nef equation in positive volume.

# 6. Fixed algebraic poles: an actual translated subcone

Fix actual rational bundles $A,B$, with $A$ nef, and fix one actual
\[
 \sigma\in\operatorname{FS}^{\rm gf}(B),\qquad \sigma(x_0)=0.
\]
Set $L=A+B$, $\theta=c_1(L)$, $\alpha=c_1(A)$, and define the specified original-carrier subcone
\[
 \mathcal C_\sigma(\alpha)=
 \{\sigma+v:v\in\operatorname{PSH}(\alpha)\}.
 \tag{6.1}
\]
The addition is the literal extended-real addition; all functions in (6.1) equal $-\infty$ on the pole set of $\sigma$. An effective rational divisor with its canonical section is an important special case, but a finite section system with a nonprincipal base ideal is also allowed. $L$ may be big nonnef.

**Theorem 6.1 (actual common-gauge repair).** Every member of (6.1) is genuine in $\operatorname{PSH}(\theta)$, with one defining decreasing net of actual section maxima. The subcone is closed under arbitrary decreasing nets with finite limiting generic value, at all analytic points. Its normalized part is compact in the divisorial topology.

**Proof.** For $v\in\operatorname{PSH}(\alpha)$, take the single finite continuous net $p_a\searrow v$ from Theorem 5.1, on actual $A+\varepsilon_aH$. Lemma 4.1 realizes
\[
 \sigma+p_a\in\operatorname{FS}^{\rm gf}(A+B+\varepsilon_aH).
 \tag{6.2}
\]
This net decreases to $\sigma+v$ on the whole original space. At a pole of $\sigma$, all stages and the limit are $-\infty$. Elsewhere addition of its finite value commutes with the limit. The classes in (6.2) tend to the actual $\theta$, and the generic value is that of $v$, so (1.2) proves membership.

The denominators and ideals are actual. If $\sigma$ is given in degree $qB$ and $p_a$ in degree $m(A+\varepsilon_aH)$, choose $M$ divisible by both $q$ and $m$, and use **all** products of $M/q$ sections of the first system and $M/m$ sections of the second. The weights add. In local frames, the base ideal is $I_\sigma^{M/q}I_{p_a}^{M/m}=I_\sigma^{M/q}$, since the second system generates. Hence the entire defining net retains the fixed algebraic pole ideal with the exact normalized exponent of $\sigma$. It is not a small-order approximation to that pole ideal, and it is not a potential merely on its blowup.

Now let $u_i=\sigma+v_i$ decrease, with finite limiting generic value. The $v_i$ are uniquely specified: $\sigma$ is finite at every divisorial valuation, so equality of two representations gives equality of their $v$-coordinates; direct ample embedding and granted ample determination give equality at all points. At every divisorial valuation, decrease of $u_i$ gives decrease of $v_i$. To promote the order to all points, place a pair $v_i,v_j$ into one actual ample cone by Lemma 4.2. Its genuine maximum equals the larger function on divisorial coordinates; ample determination makes this equality literal everywhere. Therefore $v_i$ is a decreasing all-point net. Its generic values are those of $u_i$. Theorem 5.2 gives a genuine $v=\inf_i v_i$ in the entire nef cone. The literal all-point limit is $u=\sigma+v$, and (6.2) supplies one genuine defining net.

Finally, normalization of $u$ is normalization of $v$. On every divisorial coordinate the map $v\mapsto\sigma+v$ is translation by a fixed finite number, hence continuous and injective. Theorem 5.3 makes its normalized domain compact. Its image is exactly the normalized subcone (6.1), with the original coordinate topology. It is thus compact, and is closed in any fixed dominating actual ample cone. No membership of other $\theta$-psh functions in this image has been asserted. $\square$

The class $\theta$, gauge $\sigma$, and source nef cone are all retained in (6.1). This theorem does not replace the full genuine cone by an arbitrary choice of divisor or movable datum. In particular, a decomposition of a big bundle as ample plus effective does **not** show that every genuine function can be divided by that effective section and remain in the nef cone. That missing implication would require a separate proof and is not used.

# 7. Actual algebraic comparisons expose the remaining difficulty

**Proposition 7.1 (bounded-stage barrier).** If the genuine cone in (1.2) contains any bounded function, its numerical class is nef. In particular a big nonnef class cannot be treated by globally finite FS defining stages with classes tending to that class.

**Proof.** If $u$ is bounded below and $\phi_i\geq u$ is a defining stage, $\phi_i$ has no pole anywhere. At the trivial seminorm of any scheme point a common zero of all its sections would be a pole. Therefore the section system has no common zero and generates its actual bundle power. This makes $B_i$ semiample, hence nef: a generated bundle has nonnegative degree on every integral curve, since its pullback to the curve is generated. The limiting class is nef because each curve degree is continuous. The same reasoning applies if all defining stages are finite even without a bounded endpoint. $\square$

For nonnef $\theta$, choose an integral curve $C$ with $\theta\cdot C<0$. Then $(\theta+\varepsilon h)\cdot C<0$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, since $h\cdot C>0$. Thus small ample perturbations still admit no globally finite FS systems in these classes. The finite-valued repair of §3 cannot simply be applied to the nonnef cone. An absolute cap $\max(u,-j)$ can be a useful continuous obstacle, but it cannot be a genuine $\theta$-psh bounded potential in this situation.

**Proposition 7.2 (pole-set correction is not an order correction).** Actual section-ideal multiplication by an arbitrarily small coefficient does not in general repair the all-point comparison at a singular obstacle.

**Proof.** On $X=\mathbb P^1_k$, let $s,t$ be the standard generating sections of $\mathcal O(1)$, with $s$ vanishing at $p$. Put
\[
 \tau=\log|s|,\qquad
 f_j=\max(\log|s|,\log|t|-j)=\max(\tau,-j).
 \tag{7.1}
\]
The last equality holds at every analytic point because $\max(\log|s|,\log|t|)=0$. Each $f_j$ is a finite continuous actual FS potential on $\mathcal O(1)$. The sequence decreases on the whole carrier to the actual one-section potential $\tau$, including its pole. No $f_j+c$ is at most $\tau$, because at the trivial seminorm of $p$ it is finite whereas $\tau=-\infty$.

Even after restoring the pole set, order can fail. For rational $0<\delta<1$, the product construction makes
\[
 f_j+\delta\tau+c
\]
an actual FS potential on the actual rational bundle $\mathcal O(1+\delta)$. It has the pole of $\tau$. At the original valuation $v_a=a\,\operatorname{ord}_p$, $a>0$, the canonical values are
\[
 \tau(v_a)=-a,\qquad f_j(v_a)=-\min(a,j).
\]
For $a\geq j$, comparison with $\tau$ would require
\[
 -j-\delta a+c\leq-a,
 \quad\text{equivalently}\quad (1-\delta)a\leq j-c.
 \tag{7.2}
\]
This fails at every sufficiently large $a$, for any real $c$. Denominators are cleared by taking a tensor power for $\delta$; the calculation concerns actual sections and original analytic valuations, not a hypothetical abstract family. $\square$

More generally, along $v_a=a\,v$ for a fixed divisorial valuation and an actual system (1.1),
\[
 \lim_{a\to\infty}\frac{\phi(v_a)}a
 =-\min_b\frac{v(s_b)}m.
 \tag{7.3}
\]
This follows directly by dividing the finite maximum of affine functions by $a$. Constants and weights do not alter this recession. Thus an all-point inequality $\phi\leq\psi$ necessarily requires the normalized minimum vanishing order of $\phi$ to be at least that of $\psi$, at every such divisor. Matching the pole **set** is weaker than matching the required orders. In (7.2) the former is restored but the latter is only $\delta<1$.

The projective-line example is not a counterexample to genuine decreasing closure: $\tau$ itself is an available actual FS potential, so its constant defining net already gives genuine membership. It is a counterexample to the proposed *comparison mechanism* that takes a capped stage and adds a vanishingly small multiple of the pole gauge. Finite maxima, small constant shifts, and this particular small ideal multiplication do not justify the required simultaneous lower FS stage in a general big nonnef class.

To repair the full gate, one must prove an actual algebraic regularization preserving the necessary singular orders and all-point upper comparisons while its numerical error tends to zero. A multiplier-ideal regularization of one model obstacle, or divisorial coordinate agreement in a fixed ample cone, does not establish this assertion for an arbitrary decreasing net of genuine potentials. No such general regularization theorem is proved in this paper.

# 8. Separate zero-volume equation: the entire numerical zero class

**Theorem 8.1 (numerically trivial range).** For every actual numerically trivial rational bundle $L$ on the above $X,k$, including nontorsion bundles in $\operatorname{Pic}^0$,
\[
 \operatorname{PSH}(c_1(L))=\mathbb R.
 \tag{8.1}
\]
With canonical reference zero, the actual entire finite-energy space is $\mathbb R$, raw energy is zero, and the genuine raw top-degree operator is zero. The only positive target of the required raw mass is the zero Radon measure. Its equation is unique modulo constants.

**Proof.** Constants belong to the cone by (5.1): they are finite FS potentials on every actual ample $L+\varepsilon H$, using a generating power and constant weights. This argument does not require a section of the original numerically trivial bundle.

Let $u\in\operatorname{PSH}(0)$, and normalize $u(x_0)=0$. Integer tensor powers of a defining FS net show directly that $m u\in\operatorname{PSH}(0)$ for each positive integer $m$; this is a one-net construction as in §4. Lemma 4.2 embeds each of these normalized functions, unchanged, into the one actual ample cone for $H$. At a fixed divisorial valuation $z$, the finite evaluation on its compact normalized cone has a finite lower bound $-C_z$. Hence
\[
 -C_z\leq m u(z)\leq0\quad\text{for every }m\geq1.
\]
It follows that $u(z)=0$. Ample all-point determination, applied to $u$ and zero in the same actual ample cone, gives $u=0$ on every analytic point. Undoing the normalization proves (8.1).
