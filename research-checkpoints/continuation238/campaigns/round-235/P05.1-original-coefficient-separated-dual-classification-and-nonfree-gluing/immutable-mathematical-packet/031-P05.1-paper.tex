\begin{abstract}
We prove a geometric sufficient condition for global real-PA regularization that permits several distinct positive-dimensional overlaps. Start with one complete rational affine page. At each of finitely many steps attach a group of complete equal-dimensional pages whose pairwise intersections are one affine subspace contained in an earlier page. The affine span of the new group must meet the earlier affine span exactly in that subspace. Quotient directions inside a group may be dependent. Every continuous printed-PC target bounded relative to its actual admitted real-PA PC positive-growth reference has finite rational-spatial real-PA PC above-approximants with arbitrary global uniform error and exact page recession. The construction extends a fixed whole parent trace and lifts every one of its supporting forms to simultaneous supports on the new group. Previously constructed pages are never modified. A finite error budget proves termination of this assembly, including all intersections among attachment subspaces. The actual unrestricted envelope for every bounded continuous, possibly nonseparable obstacle enters the resulting decreasing uniform domain by the qualified grant R230. An explicit seven-plane example has two different unbounded line overlaps and dependent directions at each. Proper-cell geometries and arrangements without the free-span condition remain outside the theorem. This is new scoped progress, not a classification of the original residual: original P05.1 remains OPEN and candidate=false.
\end{abstract}

\section{Controlling problem, exact coefficients, and the new scope}
The controlling \texttt{original-task.json} has atom P05.1, a 523-character statement, a 44,807-character common-notation/source-family body, and a 710-character completion criterion. Its residual asks for the coefficient-separated classification of balanced rational polyhedral pairs with a PC PA reference positive at infinity, retaining higher-dimensional real regularity, arbitrary nonseparable obstacles, and the compact higher-dimensional literal branch. A new sufficient geometric class does not complete that classification.

Fix a lattice $N$, a finite-dimensional ambient $N_\R$, and a finite pure rational complex containing all its closed faces. Rational affine equations and inequalities can be made integral by clearing denominators. A tangent lattice is always the saturated
\[
N_\sigma=N\cap\operatorname{span}_\R(\sigma-\sigma).
\]
Facet normals point inward and represent primitive classes in $N_\sigma/N_\rho$. Top weights are actual strictly positive real numbers; balancing means that their weighted normal sum is tangent to the facet. The formal weights in the frozen BMPS Definition 2.19 are rational. Real balancing in this paper is proved directly with the same primitive classes, rather than attributed to that formal definition.

A printed PCC has rational endpoints $x_i$, real $\alpha_i\geq0$ summing to one, and center $x=\sum\alpha_i x_i$. There must be a rational carrying structure with faces $\tau\preceq\sigma_i$, $x\in\tau$, $x_i\in\sigma_i$. The center need not be rational. The class $\PC$ satisfies all these Jensen inequalities. Every use of continuity below is an explicit hypothesis. Literal PA has integral tangent slopes and rational constants. The class $\PA_\R$ is its finite real scalar extension on finite rational spatial carriers. We will prove that the real coefficient tuples we construct belong to this extension. An arbitrary maximum of irrational affine coefficients is not a rational-carrier assertion.

The actual reference $\gamma$ is continuous, $\PA_\R$, printed PC, and positive at infinity: each actual affine piece has strictly positive slope on every nonzero direction in that cell's recession cone. It is never rounded, averaged, replaced, or altered off a selected subset. The target is
\[
f\in C(X)\cap\PC,\qquad \|f-\gamma\|_\infty\leq C<\infty.
\]
No separability assumption is imposed. A uniform decreasing completion is constructed before any operator could be applied; this manuscript assigns no Monge--Ampere measure to an intermediate or to a completed endpoint.

\begin{definition}[Finite free attachment presentation]\label{def:free}
All pages below are distinct complete rational affine $d$-planes, $d\geq1$. Begin with a page $Q_0$. Having formed $X_{j-1}$, let $A_{j-1}=\aff X_{j-1}$. Attach a nonempty finite group
\[
B_j=\bigcup_{a=1}^{m_j}Q_{j,a},\qquad E_j=\aff B_j,
\]
along a nonempty rational affine $H_j$ of dimension less than $d$. Require:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $H_j$ is contained in one earlier page, called its parent;
\item every new page contains $H_j$, and distinct pages in $B_j$ have intersection exactly $H_j$;
\item $E_j\cap A_{j-1}=H_j$.
\end{enumerate}
For $m_j=1$, condition (2) just means $H_j\subset Q_{j,1}$. Put $X_j=X_{j-1}\cup B_j$, and stop at $j=r<\infty$.
\end{definition}
This is a rational geometric condition, checkable by affine Gaussian elimination. It makes no claim about feasibility of an unknown target on an unknown PA carrier. It allows $H_j$ and $H_l$ to be different, parallel, nested, or intersecting as permitted by the stated affine intersections. Dependence inside any new group is unrestricted. It excludes an attachment whose span already meets the earlier span in extra directions, even if its support meets the earlier support only in the desired trace.

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous approximation on the new class]\label{thm:main}
Let $X=X_r$ have a presentation in Definition~\ref{def:free}. Give its complete pages arbitrary specified positive real constant weights, and retain any fixed finite rational original carrier of $X$. For every admitted actual $\gamma$, every continuous printed-PC $f$ with bounded difference from $\gamma$, and every $\epsilon>0$, there is a genuine finite rational-spatial $P\in\PA_\R\cap\PC$ such that
\[
f\leq P\leq f+\epsilon\quad\hbox{on all }X.
\]
It has the exact page recession of $\gamma$. At every real point of every original face there is one ambient affine form supporting $P$ on the entire original incident cells. In fact it supports on every entire complete page containing that point. There is a globally strictly decreasing uniform approximating sequence with one uniform bound relative to this same $\gamma$.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is an existence and finite-construction theorem, with samples selected by compactness from actual dual data. It is not an exact computational oracle for arbitrary continuous functions or arbitrary real heights. It does not assert that every $P$ comes from restriction of one globally ambient convex function.

\section{Elementary foundations and precise real duality}
We use ordinary real completeness, finite-dimensional compactness of closed bounded sets, inverse linear algebra, and convex secant inequalities. The additional facts needed in the construction are proved here.

\begin{lemma}[Finite generation, compact hulls, and elimination]\label{lem:finite}
Finite conic hulls are closed. Compact sets in finite-dimensional space have compact convex hulls. Every nonempty polyhedron is a finite point hull plus a finite recession cone, including both signs of lineality generators. Rational polyhedra admit rational spatial generators. Such finite point/cone expressions are polyhedra, and rational linear images of rational polyhedra are rational polyhedra.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a conic representation with dependent positive columns, choose a nonzero relation $\sum b_i v_i=0$, with a positive $b_i$ after changing sign. Subtract $t b_i$ from its coefficients, where $t=\min_{b_i>0}a_i/b_i$. The representation stays nonnegative and loses a positive column. Iterate to an independent subset. Each independent column map has an inverse on its image, bounding coefficients by a constant times the represented vector's norm. There are finitely many subsets. A convergent vector sequence thus has a subsequence with one subset and bounded convergent coefficients; its limit lies in the cone.

For affine combinations, the analogous elimination uses $\sum b_i=0$ as well. In dimension $s$ it leaves at most $s+1$ points. The hull of a compact $K$ is the image of $K^{s+1}$ times the compact standard simplex under the continuous sum map, with repetitions allowed, hence compact.

For a cone given by finite homogeneous inequalities, remove their joint kernel as its lineality. Choose a rational complement for rational inequalities. In the pointed complement the sum of the defining nonnegative forms is strictly positive on nonzero cone vectors. The section at value one is bounded: an unbounded sequence, normalized and passed to a unit limit, would give a nonzero joint-kernel vector. A bounded polyhedron is the hull of finitely many vertices. At a nonvertex move in both directions of the kernel of active constraints until more independent constraints become active. Boundedness gives the endpoints; the original point is their convex combination. Induct on active rank. Vertices solve finitely many full-rank systems, rational for rational coefficients. Restoring both signs of a lineality basis proves finite generation of a cone. Homogenize a polyhedron $Ax\leq b$ as $Au\leq tb$, $t\geq0$: positive-$t$ generators yield points, and zero-$t$ generators yield recession directions. Section $t=1$ proves its stated decomposition.

Conversely introduce nonnegative hull/cone coefficients, sum-one for the hull, and the coordinate equations for their image. Eliminate each variable by pairing every lower bound with every upper bound, retaining rows independent of it. A variable with bounds of only one sign imposes no additional pair condition. These finite weak inequalities exactly assert a nonempty interval for that variable. Repetition proves the finite projected description, rational when the data are rational. Closedness of compact hull plus finite cone also follows by taking a convergent compact summand subsequence. These arguments include all relative affine hulls and zero-dimensional cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Supports and biconjugacy]\label{lem:support}
A finite convex function on a complete real affine space is continuous, locally Lipschitz, and has an affine support at every point. Its directional derivative at $y$ is finite continuous sublinear, and is the support function of a nonempty compact convex subdifferential. It equals its biconjugate. A finite lower semicontinuous convex function on a compact polytope is continuous on the whole polytope. Extended by infinity off that polytope, it too equals its biconjugate, including the boundary.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a cube about $y$, convexity bounds values above by the largest vertex value. Reflection in $y$ gives a lower bound on a smaller cube. Extend any segment in a still smaller cube a fixed distance in both signs; convex secants bound its slope by oscillation divided by that distance. This proves local Lipschitzness and continuity.

A point outside a nonempty closed convex set has a nearest point: restrict a minimizing sequence to a large closed ball and use compactness. For nearest $w$ to outside $z$, first variation along $[w,c]$ gives $(w-z)\cdot(c-w)\geq0$. Thus $(w-z)\cdot(c-z)\geq\|w-z\|^2>0$. Apply this to $(y,F(y)-t)$ below a continuous convex epigraph. Normalize the inward normals and let $t\downarrow0$ along a subsequence. Their unit limit supports at $(y,F(y))$ and has nonnegative vertical component. This component cannot be zero: projection onto the entire spatial space would force its horizontal part to vanish. Division gives an affine support. Every such touching support provides equality in double conjugation at its touching point; the defining transform inequality supplies the reverse inequality.

Convex secants give the directional limit $h(v)$; the local Lipschitz bound makes it finite, bounded by $L\|v\|$, and Lipschitz in $v$. Scaling proves positive homogeneity. The midpoint inequality at $y+2tv,y+2tw$ proves $h(v+w)\leq h(v)+h(w)$. Apply the full-space support argument to $h$ at $v$. Its affine support has zero constant: testing zero and $2v$, using homogeneity and contact at $v$, gives both signs of the constant bound. Forms below $h$ are exactly supports of $F$ at $y$, by differentiation in one direction and convex secants in the other. Testing both signs of $|h|\leq L\|\cdot\|$ bounds those forms; their set is closed, convex, nonempty, and compact. The support at $v$ attains $h(v)$.

For a finite lsc convex $a$ on a compact polytope $D$, its upper bound is the largest vertex value $M$. At $p\in D$ work in its relative affine hull and choose a fixed $u>0$ below each inactive facet slack divided by its normal norm. For nearby $q\in D$, active rows are respected along $(q-p)/\|q-p\|$, and inactive rows retain their margin up to distance $u$. Thus $p+u(q-p)/\|q-p\|\in D$. Convexity yields
\[
a(q)\leq a(p)+\frac{\|q-p\|}{u}(M-a(p)).
\]
Together with lsc this proves continuity at all boundary points. If there are no inactive rows any sufficiently small $u$ works; a singleton is immediate.

For biconjugacy on $D$, strictly separate $(p,t)$ with $p\in D,t<a(p)$ from its closed epigraph by nearest-point variation. The inward normal $(A,\beta)$ has $\beta\geq0$. Testing the point $(p,a(p))$ gives $\beta(a(p)-t)>0$, hence $\beta>0$. Division produces an affine minorant whose value at $p$ exceeds $t$. Let $t\uparrow a(p)$. Outside $D$, nearest-point spatial separation gives $v(p)>\max_D v$; scaling $v$ and using $\min_D a> -\infty$ makes the double transform infinite there. This proves the entire assertion without an external convex-duality theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Whole-page convexity and supports with the printed endpoints}
\begin{lemma}[Rational feasible approximation]\label{lem:density}
Continuous printed-PC functions satisfy every real-endpoint PCC with the same rational incidences.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At any point of a rational polyhedron, impose its active rows as equalities. Gaussian elimination gives a rational particular solution and a rational kernel basis; rational coordinates approximate the given coordinates. All inactive finite slacks remain strict. For a PCC delete zero coefficients and introduce $t_i=\alpha_i x_i$. Its endpoint-cell constraints become $A_i t_i\leq\alpha_i b_i$, together with $\alpha_i\geq0$, $\sum\alpha_i=1$, and $\sum t_i\in\tau$. This is a rational feasible polyhedron. Close feasible rational approximants retain $\alpha_i>0$, and their endpoints $t_i/\alpha_i$ and centers are rational. Apply printed PC and pass to limits using continuity. The argument covers irrational centers in lower-dimensional faces without changing the carrier.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Support criterion on any finite complete-page arrangement]\label{prop:criterion}
For a continuous function on a finite union of distinct complete rational affine pages, printed PC is equivalent to ordinary convexity on every whole page and, at each point $y$, existence of one ambient form supporting every entire page containing $y$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A convex carrier cell lies in one page. Otherwise its relative interior would be contained in finitely many proper affine subspaces of its affine hull. The product of a nonzero affine equation for each such subspace is a nonzero polynomial and cannot vanish on an open set, by successive one-variable polynomial uniqueness.

Fix a carrier and a point $y$. A sufficiently small neighborhood meets only closed cells containing $y$: the other finitely many closed cells avoid it. Its minimal face is a face of every cell containing it, by the common-face axiom. For short symmetric endpoints $y\pm tw$ in one complete page, their assigned cells are therefore incident to this same minimal face. Lemma~\ref{lem:density} proves the local midpoint inequality. To obtain convexity on a whole real segment subtract its chord, obtaining $q$ with endpoint values zero. If $q(t_0)>0$, choose sufficiently small $\delta>0$ so $q(t)+\delta t^2$ has an interior global maximum. At sufficiently short symmetric parameters around this maximum, the local midpoint inequality for $q$ makes the mean of the two perturbed values at least the maximum plus $\delta s^2$, a contradiction. Thus every segment satisfies convexity. This uses actual short incident PCCs, not a distant PCC or illegal whole-page coarsening.

Let $L_i$ be the tangent spaces of the pages containing $y$, and $K_i=\partial f_i(y)$ their compact subdifferentials from Lemma~\ref{lem:support}. Let $S$ be the image of restriction from $(\sum_iL_i)^*$ into $\prod_iL_i^*$, a closed linear subspace. If $K=\prod_iK_i$ misses $S$, minimize the squared norm of the orthogonal projection onto $S^\perp$ over compact $K$. For a minimizer $z_0$ put $a=\operatorname{pr}_{S^\perp}z_0\ne0$. First variation gives $\langle a,z\rangle\geq\|a\|^2$ for $z\in K$. Represent $-a$ by directions $w_i\in L_i$ using auxiliary Euclidean duality. Orthogonality to the restriction image means $\sum_iw_i=0$, and
\[
\sum_i f_i'(y;w_i)=\max_{(p_i)\in K}\sum_i p_i(w_i)\leq-\|a\|^2<0.
\]
For sufficiently small $t>0$, endpoints $y+tw_i$ have equal-weight barycenter $y$, lie in actually incident cells, and have negative Jensen cost. Lemma~\ref{lem:density} forbids it. Thus $K$ meets $S$, giving one ambient support on whole pages.

Conversely a legal endpoint cell lies in a page containing its center. The common entire-page support at the center bounds each endpoint from below. Multiply by the PCC coefficients and sum; the affine terms cancel. This proves every printed PCC on every permitted structure.
\end{proof}

\section{A finite rational primal construction with actual real heights}
Let $L$ be a complete rational affine space, translated by a rational point. If $\gamma$ is convex finite real PA on a rational carrier, its full-dimensional affine pieces are global supports by convex secants. Thus
\[
\gamma(x)=\max_\nu(a_\nu(x)+b_\nu),\quad
h(v)=\max_\nu a_\nu(v),\quad \Delta=\conv\{a_\nu\}.
\]
These are its actual coefficients; $\Delta$ need not be rational. The function $\gamma-h$ is bounded. On each recession cone of its rational carrier, $h$ equals that cell's linear part. Those cones cover $L$: on $tv$ some finite cell contains an unbounded subsequence, and division of its inequalities by $t$ puts $v$ in its recession cone. Bounded difference identifies its slope with $h(v)$. Positive growth gives $h(v)>0$ for $v\ne0$, uniformly on the unit sphere. In particular a carrying cell cannot have nonzero lineality, since its affine slope would have to be positive on both signs. The zero-dimensional case has a singleton dual domain and a vertical epigraph ray.

\begin{lemma}[Entire actual dual domain and rational recession lifts]\label{lem:dual}
For continuous convex $f$ bounded relative to $\gamma$, $\phi=f^*$ is finite continuous exactly on $\Delta$, including its entire boundary. Its extension is infinity elsewhere. The recession of $f$ is exactly $h$ and $f(x+v)\leq f(x)+h(v)$. There is a finite exact generating expression for $\epi h$ using rational spatial rays and their actual real heights.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $D$ with $|f-h|\leq D$. For $p\in\Delta$, $p(x)\leq h(x)$ bounds $\phi(p)$ above by $D$, and evaluation at zero bounds it below by $-f(0)$. For $p\notin\Delta$, nearest-point separation gives $v$ with $p(v)>h(v)$; $x=tv$ makes the transform infinite. The transform is convex lsc as a supremum of affine functions. Lemma~\ref{lem:support} gives whole-boundary continuity and $f=\phi^*$. The latter and $p(v)\leq h(v)$ imply the recession inequality. Bounded difference on rays gives exact recession.

Generate each rational recession cone of the carrier, including signed lineality generators if necessary. On each cone $h$ is linear. The union of the lifted generators $(u,h(u))$, together with $(0,1)$, generates $\epi h$: a conic representation inside the cone of $v$ gives exact height $h(v)$, and the vertical ray gives higher points. Convexity and homogeneity prove the reverse inclusion. Spatial directions are rational even when the heights are irrational.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rational graph carriers and real scalar extension]\label{lem:graph}
Suppose a finite full-domain epigraph has the form
\[
E=\conv\{(s,c_s)\}+\cone\{(u,d_u),(0,1)\},
\]
where all spatial points and directions are rational, all heights are real, and lower fibers are finite. Its lower function belongs to $\PA_\R$ on a finite rational spatial complex.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} makes $E$ a closed polyhedron; each fiber is a nonempty closed upward ray bounded below, with attained bottom. Its convex lower function has full domain, hence is continuous and has supports. In a finite inequality description, upward stability gives nonnegative vertical coefficient on each inward inequality. Full spatial projection rules out nontrivial purely spatial inequalities. Dividing the remaining positive vertical coefficients makes the lower function a finite affine maximum.

A face exposed by a lower support is generated exactly by the point generators with zero slack and recession generators with zero supporting value. Every positive coefficient must have zero slack. The vertical ray has positive slack and is excluded. The face lies on an affine graph, so spatial projection is an affine isomorphism onto a hull of rational points plus rational rays. It is a rational polyhedron by Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}. Every subface of a lower graph face is also lower exposed: add a sufficiently small exposing functional of the subface to the original one, preserving its positive vertical coefficient and all nonzero finite-generator slacks. There are finitely many such faces. If their projections meet at $x$, their graph heights are the same bottom, so the intersection is the projection of their common face, a face on each graph. These projected faces cover the domain and give a finite rational complex.

Continuous affine tuples on this carrier satisfy rational matching equations, evaluated on rational affine bases of shared faces. Their real solution space has a rational basis by Gaussian elimination. Clear finitely many slope denominators in saturated tangent bases and constant denominators in each basis tuple. The resulting basis functions are literal PA. Expressing the actual real tuple in this rational basis proves finite real scalar-extension membership. This argument does not compare irrational formulas to create new spatial walls.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform full-page primal majorants]\label{prop:primal}
For every $\delta>0$ and every finite prescribed rational sample set, there is a convex genuine rational-spatial real-PA majorant $Q$ with
\[
f\leq Q\leq f+\delta\quad\hbox{globally on }L,
\]
exact recession $h$, and equality at those unshifted samples.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Continuity of $f$ permits rational spatial samples in $\phi(p)=\sup_x(p(x)-f(x))$. For each $p\in\Delta$ choose one rational sample achieving error less than $\delta/2$. Whole continuity of $\phi$ gives a neighborhood where the same sample has error less than $\delta$. Compactness supplies a finite set $S$ covering all of $\Delta$; append prescribed samples. Thus
\[
\phi-\delta\leq k_S(p):=\max_{s\in S}(p(s)-f(s))\leq\phi\quad(p\in\Delta).
\]
Form $E_S=\conv\{(s,f(s))\}+\epi h$. It is closed, full spatial domain, and lies in $\epi f$ by convexity and the recession inequality. Fibers have finite attained bottoms. Its lower function $Q$ has transform $k_S$ on $\Delta$ and infinity outside: maximize the functional over the point hull, then the exact recession cone; outside $\Delta$ a separating ray gives infinity. Biconjugacy reverses the displayed transform bounds to give $f\leq Q\leq f+\delta$ everywhere. Sample lifts give equality there. Lemma~\ref{lem:graph} proves genuine rational-spatial real PA. The compact hull plus exact recession cone has exactly that cone as recession: divide a forward-ray sequence by its parameter, the bounded hull disappears, and closedness of the cone retains the direction. This proves exact recession rather than a rounded asymptotic slope.
\end{proof}

\section{Extension of a whole trace with every supporting form lifted}
This section supplies the mechanism for one new group. It independently proves the required compact dual identities and strengthens the support conclusion to an arbitrary trace subgradient. Its common-$H$ mechanism is consistent with the supplied S298 scope, but no S298 result or unavailable references are used as premises here.

Let $Q_i$ be complete rational pages with every pairwise intersection the same rational affine $H$. Translate a rational point of $H$ to zero; write $L_i$ for their tangent spaces and $E=\sum_iL_i$. Let continuous convex $f_i$ agree on $H$, be bounded relative to their actual page references $\gamma_i$, and have one common ambient support at each $y\in H$. These hypotheses follow from Proposition~\ref{prop:criterion} for the restriction of an original target. Write $h_i,\Delta_i,\phi_i=f_i^*$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}. On $H$ the actual reference trace is convex rational-spatial real PA, positive in every nonzero recession direction. Its actual recession $h_H=h_i|_H$ and compact gradient polytope $\Delta_H$ give the conjugate domain of $f_H$. If $H$ is a point use $\Delta_H=\{0\}$.

Let $r_i:E^*\to L_i^*$, $T:E^*\to H^*$, and $U_i:L_i^*\to H^*$ be restriction. Define
\[
D=\{p:r_i p\in\Delta_i\text{ for every }i\},\qquad
\phi(p)=\max_i\phi_i(r_i p),\qquad G=\phi^*.
\]
\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous whole-boundary fibers]\label{lem:fibers}
$D$ is nonempty compact polyhedral, $\phi$ is finite continuous convex on it, $G|_{L_i}\leq f_i$ and $G|_H=f_H$. The attained fiber minimum
\[
k(q)=\min_{p\in D,\ Tp=q}\phi(p)
\]
has domain exactly $\Delta_H$ and equals $f_H^*$ there. The separate minimum of $\phi_i$ on $U_i^{-1}(q)\cap\Delta_i$ equals the same $k(q)$. Every joint minimizer consequently satisfies $\phi_i(r_i p)=k(q)$ simultaneously for all pages. The set
\[
M=\{p\in D:\phi(p)=k(Tp)\}
\]
is compact and maps onto all of $\Delta_H$, including boundary fibers.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$D$ is closed with finite real inequalities. It is bounded: if $p_n$ were unbounded, normalize and pass to a unit limit; bounded $r_i p_n$ would force the limit to vanish on every $L_i$, hence on $E$, impossible. Common support at any $y\in H$ has simultaneous Fenchel equalities
\[
\phi_i(r_i p_y)=p_y(y)-f_H(y),
\]
so $p_y\in D$ and $D$ is nonempty. Finite maxima give whole continuity and convexity of $\phi$. From $\phi\geq\phi_i\circ r_i$ get $G|_{L_i}\leq f_i$. Fenchel equalities at each $y$ give the reverse inequality on $H$.

Compact fibers attain minima. Mixing minimizers proves convexity of $k$. For a convergent sequence of $q$ values, pass to a convergent subsequence of minimizing $p$ values along the lower limit; this proves lsc, also for its infinity extension because $T(D)$ is closed. Its domain $T(D)$ is a compact polytope, so Lemma~\ref{lem:support} proves whole continuity there and biconjugacy. Grouping the transform supremum by fibers gives $k^*=G|_H=f_H$; hence $k=f_H^*$, including entire domains, and $T(D)=\Delta_H$.

Repeat this argument for each single-page restriction $U_i$ and $\phi_i$. Its fiber minimum has conjugate $f_i|_H=f_H$, so biconjugacy gives the same function $k$ with the same whole domain. Therefore every $\phi_i(r_i p)\geq k(Tp)$. At a joint minimizer their maximum equals $k(q)$, forcing equality for every page. Continuity of $k$ and $\phi$ makes $M$ closed in compact $D$, and attainment gives surjectivity. No independence of restriction coordinates and no rationality of the actual dual polytopes has been assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Prescribed trace, arbitrary trace support, finite PA extension]\label{thm:trace}
Suppose $g$ is a finite convex genuine rational-spatial real-PA function on the entire $H$ such that
\[
f_H\leq g\leq f_H+a,
\]
for some finite $a\geq0$. For every $b>0$ there are convex genuine rational-spatial real-PA functions $Q_i$ on the whole new pages with
\[
f_i\leq Q_i\leq f_i+a+b,
\quad Q_i|_H=g,
\quad Q_i^\infty=h_i.
\]
For every $y\in H$ and \emph{every} $q\in\partial g(y)$ there is one $p\in E^*$ with $Tp=q$ supporting all $Q_i$ on their entire pages at $y$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $c=g^*$. Since $g$ is bounded relative to the actual trace reference, Lemma~\ref{lem:dual} gives entire domain $\Delta_H$, whole continuity, and $k-a\leq c\leq k$. For each page form the closed convex upward set
\[
K_i=\conv\bigl(\operatorname{graph}\phi_i\ \cup\
\{(r_i p,c(Tp)):p\in M\}\bigr)+\R_{\geq0}(0,1).
\]
Both generating sets are compact; Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} makes their hull compact, so $K_i$ is closed. Its projection is the whole compact $\Delta_i$, and all heights are uniformly bounded below. Fibers have finite attained bottoms $\psi_i$. Convexity and closedness give a finite lsc convex function, continuous on the entire polytope by Lemma~\ref{lem:support}.

At original graph sources $\psi_i$ has sources at $\phi_i$; at added sources Lemma~\ref{lem:fibers} gives $\phi_i(r_i p)=k(Tp)$ and $c(Tp)\geq\phi_i(r_i p)-a$. Thus convexity of $\epi(\phi_i-a)$ implies
\[
\phi_i-a\leq\psi_i\leq\phi_i.
\]
