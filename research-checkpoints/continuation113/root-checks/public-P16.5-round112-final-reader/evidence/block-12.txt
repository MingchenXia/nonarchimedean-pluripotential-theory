## 2. The finite family's single-stage decreasing closure is stable

Let D_A consist of generically finite pointwise limits of decreasing nets
from F_A. Addition of constants and finite maxima preserve D_A; for
maxima take the product of the two directed indices. We prove directly
that decreasing limits of elements of D_A stay in D_A. This uses the
compact flattening argument behind BJ4.6 only with finite continuous
inner functions.

Suppose psi_i decreases to a generically finite psi, and each psi_i is
the decreasing limit of f_{i,j} in F_A. Use indices alpha=(i,j,epsilon),
epsilon>0, and order them by

    alpha>=alpha' iff i>=i' and
                     f_{i,j}+epsilon <= f_{i',j'}+epsilon' everywhere.

This is directed. For two indices, choose i3 above both outer indices
and epsilon3 smaller than both positive epsilons. We have

    psi_i3+epsilon3 < min(f_i1,j1+epsilon1, f_i2,j2+epsilon2)

at EVERY point. The upper functions are finite, so this remains a
strict inequality where psi_i3 is minus infinity. The continuous
real-valued functions

    exp(f_i3,j+epsilon3)
       -exp(min(f_i1,j1+epsilon1, f_i2,j2+epsilon2))

decrease to a strictly negative function pointwise. Their negative open
sets increase to a cover of compact Y^an. A finite subcover and
directedness of j give one j3 for which the displayed inequality holds
globally. This provides an index above both original indices.

Now g_alpha=f_{i,j}+epsilon lies in F_A and is a decreasing net, bounded
below pointwise by psi. At any point with finite psi value, first choose
an outer i0 whose psi_i0 value is close enough to psi, then an inner j0
and epsilon0 whose g_alpha0 value is arbitrarily close from above.
Every subsequent index remains below it. At a point with psi=-infinity,
use an arbitrary real threshold instead. Thus g_alpha decreases to psi
at every point, proving stability of D_A under decreasing limits.

The restriction to finite inner approximants is useful: if both upper
approximants have a common minus-infinity value, the attempted strict
inequality -infinity+epsilon < -infinity+epsilon' is false. We do not
use that step on original L-section maxima with their common D poles.
This argument is a direct restricted proof and makes no claim that all
extended-valued families satisfy a flattening argument without further
care at their common poles.
