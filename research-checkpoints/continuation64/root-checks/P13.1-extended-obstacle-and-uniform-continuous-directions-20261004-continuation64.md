## Roots of bounded equivariant obstacles

Let phi be a bounded CPSH(xi) potential with sup_K phi=0. Let

    f=sum_l c_l (g_l-h_l),   g_l,h_l in H(a_l xi), a_l>0,
    A=sum_l |c_l|/a_l.

Every difference g_l-h_l is xi-invariant on the punctured cone. Thus the
obstacle psi=phi+t f is xi-equivariant, even though it need not be psh.
If l<=q_zeta/q_xi<=u holds on all weights, the oscillation of a difference
along a zeta orbit over parameter s is at most (u-l)|s|/a_l. Consequently
psi decreases along that orbit with slope at least

    l-|t| A(u-l).

If this is positive, psi has the required range and a unique root on every
orbit. It defines C_(xi,zeta)(psi), a continuous zeta-equivariant obstacle.
The root-slope proof applies to every support stratum because it uses the
global equivariant functions, not an assumed representation of psi as FS.

Order equivalence follows from zero sublevel sets. For any xi-equivariant
functions with strict zeta-orbit slopes, ordering is equivalent to inclusion
of their zero sublevel sets: move any point along the xi orbit to the zero
level of one function. Taking the zeta root preserves these sets. Conversely
the analogous argument applies to the zeta-equivariant images. In particular,
for an actual PSH(xi) competitor theta,

    theta<=psi iff C(theta)<=C(psi).

The accepted all-Reeb PSH order bijection then identifies the competitor
sets. For the genuine bounded envelopes whose regularity has been established,

    C(P_xi psi)=P_zeta(C psi).

For clarity, root transport also commutes with the relevant pointwise
supremum: all competitor orbit functions have the same uniform positive
slope bounds, and the zero set of their supremum is the intersection of
their zero sublevel sets. If an upper-semicontinuous regularization is used,
its commutation must be justified with the same slope bounds. For continuous
bounded obstacles and continuous envelopes, the genuine raw supremum already
is continuous, so no unproved regularization exchange is necessary.

## Quantitative convergence of the transported obstacle

On K, let |phi|<=B and |f|<=M. If zeta->xi, then l,u->1. For fixed t,
the positive lower slope above is at least1/4 for all sufficiently close
zeta, and the root parameter r of psi satisfies

    |r|<=4(B+|t|M).

The difference between f(v) and f(zeta_r v) is at most A(u-l)|r|. The
transported component differences f_zeta tend uniformly to f on K by
the finite-presentation root bounds. The root of phi has slopes between
l and u. Applying these estimates to the two root equations gives

    ||C(phi+t f)-(C(phi)+t f_zeta)||_K ->0.

The convergence is proved for each fixed bounded phi and t; its error may
depend on B. This does not prevent passing a quadratic inequality whose
constant is independent of phi through that limit. It is unnecessary to
claim uniform convergence of the transport error on all bounded potentials
with unbounded B.

Actual envelopes are1-Lipschitz in a bounded obstacle. Therefore

    ||C(P_xi(phi+t f))-P_zeta(C(phi)+t f_zeta)||_K ->0.

The genuine energy comparison and constant-shift correction give convergence
of these energies as zeta->xi. The normalization issue cannot be omitted:
for a psh theta with sup_K theta=c, the root slopes give

    |C(theta)-C(theta-c)-c|<=max(|1/l-1|,|1/u-1|)|c|.

Combine this with the normalized two-sided energy comparison. For theta=
P_xi(phi+t f), |c|<=|t|M, and |E(theta)-E(phi)|<=|t|M. Hence the energy
error tends to zero for each fixed phi,t, including obstacles of either sign.
No false raw commutation with additive constants is assumed.

## The rational estimate has constants independent of the quotient

For rational zeta the quotient has dimension d=n-1 and ample rational
class omega_zeta. The lifted f_zeta is a difference of continuous psh
potentials at the auxiliary class A omega_zeta. Signs of the coefficients
are handled by swapping the two potentials before summing. Positive real
coefficients and the same-ray sum use CPSH closedness; an arbitrary real
FS breakpoint is not simply declared a model PL function.

The actual BJv4 Lemmas8.7--8.8 imply, with M_zeta=||f_zeta||_K,

    |E(P(phi_zeta+t f_zeta))-E(phi_zeta)
       -t integral f_zeta MA_zeta(phi_zeta)|
      <=2 |t| M_zeta ((1+A|t|)^d-1)
      <=2 M_zeta d A 2^(d-1) t^2

for A|t|<=1, d>=1. The d=0 case is exactly linear. The same-volume expansion
must be applied to the actual mixed measures. The background envelope
property identifies the source's extended energy with E composed with P.

Extending the stated PL version to a CPSH auxiliary pair is legitimate
by uniform global FS/model approximation at the ample auxiliary class:
both approximants remain in PSH(A omega_zeta), the extended energy is
1-Lipschitz in the obstacle, and the mixed-MA pairings converge. The same
mass ratio is used at every approximation, so the constant is unchanged.
Real ample-class approximation must retain positivity and the relevant
PSH inclusion; it cannot be inferred merely from a coefficient label.

The mixed measures for bounded phi_zeta converge to the actual cone measure
at xi by the simultaneous CLD continuity and the common compact carrier.
Now pass through rational zeta->xi using the preceding obstacle identity.
For each fixed bounded phi, all terms converge and the constant tends to
2 M d A 2^(d-1). Thus the resulting inequality has this same constant for
every bounded phi, despite the phi-dependent convergence error.

## Finite-energy potentials require only a fixed-field decreasing passage

Take phi_j in CPSH(xi) decreasing to phi in E1(xi). For bounded f the
envelopes of phi_j+t f decrease to the envelope of phi+t f: the decreasing
psh limit is below the limiting obstacle, and every limiting competitor
lies below all earlier envelopes. Nontriviality and finite energy follow
from the bounds phi_j-|t|M <=P(phi_j+t f)<=phi_j+|t|M and their limits.

Genuine decreasing energy continuity passes the energy terms. Genuine
fixed-field mixed-MA continuity passes integral f MA(phi_j). The same
quadratic constant therefore survives for every phi in E1(xi). This route
does not integrate an unbounded moving-Reeb test function against a weak
limit and does not need such a tail assertion as an unproved substitute.

## Uniformity for a single continuous direction over a compact Reeb set

The dense direction class depends on the Reeb field. Density separately
at every field alone does not prove uniform differentiation for a fixed
f in C(K) as the field varies. The missing uniform approximation can be
obtained with a finite cover, as follows.

Let C be a compact Reeb slice and fix epsilon>0. At each xi0 in C choose a
finite FS-difference direction g_(xi0,xi0) with

    ||g_(xi0,xi0)-f||_K <epsilon/2.

Transport its individual FS components from a_l xi0 to a_l xi. The
finite-generator root bounds show uniform continuity of the resulting
g_(xi0,xi) on K as xi->xi0. Thus it is within epsilon of f on a neighborhood
U_xi0. Select a finite subcover U_1,...,U_r and a continuous partition of
unity alpha_i on the compact parameter space C. Set

    g_xi=sum_i alpha_i(xi) g_(xi_i,xi).

Then ||g_xi-f||_K<=epsilon simultaneously for all xi in C. It is a
finite sum of CPSH differences with an auxiliary multiple

    A_xi=sum_i alpha_i(xi) A_i <=max_i A_i.

Its sup norm is at most ||f||+epsilon. Hence its quadratic remainder
constant has one finite upper bound C_epsilon independent of xi and phi.
If A_xi=0, the direction is constant and the expansion is exactly linear;
one may also enlarge the auxiliary class harmlessly to a positive multiple.

Probability normalization and obstacle1-Lipschitzness now imply, for both
signs of t,

    sup_(xi in C,phi in E1(xi))
      |[E_xi(P_xi(phi+t f))-E_xi(phi)]/t
          -integral f MA_xi(phi)|
       <=2 epsilon+C_epsilon |t|.

First let t->0 for fixed epsilon, then epsilon->0. This proves the desired
uniform continuous-direction derivative if all preceding genuine-energy,
envelope and source applications have been independently established.
It does not assert a quadratic bound with one constant for every C0
direction. For the finite transport-defined direction class, the explicit
quadratic constant remains available.

The source bridge, genuine rational quotient calibration, global envelope
