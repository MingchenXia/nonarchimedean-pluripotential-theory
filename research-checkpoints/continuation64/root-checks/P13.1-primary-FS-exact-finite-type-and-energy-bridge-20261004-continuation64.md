## Primary FS lists can be completed without changing the function

Fix a normal affine cone R with homogeneous maximal ideal m and effective
torus action. Write K=X0^T, a compact subset of the punctured analytification.
Let f_i with real weights lambda_i define a genuine FS function at xi; the
f_i generate an m-primary ideal. Fix positive homogeneous algebra generators
g_j of R and an integral positive cocharacter eta0. For any Reeb eta set

    phi_eta = max_i (log|f_i|+lambda_i)/q_eta(alpha_i),
    h_(C,eta) = max_j (log|g_j|-C q_eta0(beta_j))/q_eta(beta_j).

On K all log|g_j|<=0. The continuous finite function phi_eta has a finite
minimum on K. For a fixed finite list of Reeb fields, choose C so large that
h_(C,eta)<=phi_eta on K for every field in the list. Thus adding the g_j with
the indicated weights does not alter any of those FS functions on K.

There is also a uniform choice for eta in a compact Reeb slice. A direct
primary-ideal proof avoids presupposing continuity in eta: some power m^r
lies in (f_i). For each generator g_j, express g_j^r as sum_i a_ij f_i.
At v in K at least one g_j has |g_j(v)|=1, whereas all |a_ij(v)|<=1.
Consequently at least one f_i has |f_i(v)|=1. For lambda_i<=0 this gives

    phi_eta(v)>=min_i lambda_i/q_eta(alpha_i).

The right side has a common finite lower bound on a compact Reeb slice.
The extra generator maxima have a common upper bound -C b with b>0 there,
so a single C suffices. Without the nonpositive normalization one can use
the same finite lower bound with the finite real coefficients.

Equality on K implies equality everywhere on the punctured cone. Both
functions factor through torification and have the same eta-equivariance.
For each invariant punctured v, the function

    s |-> min_j(v(g_j)+s q_eta(beta_j))

has a unique zero, because at least one generator has finite value and all
q_eta(beta_j)>0. That orbit point is in K. Transporting equality back along
this orbit proves equality at v, and torification proves it at all points.
Hence the completed algebra-generating presentation represents the original
primary FS function exactly. No C-to-infinity analytic limit is necessary.

Normalize phi_xi(v_triv)=0. At v_triv the displayed FS maximum is
max_i lambda_i/q_xi(alpha_i), so every lambda_i<=0. The added weights are
also nonpositive. This sign is simultaneous for every other Reeb field.

## Quotient norm and saturation

For the completed homogeneous algebra-generating list h_l with weights w_l,
define chi(f), for f in R_alpha, to be the maximum over homogeneous polynomial
representations of f of their minimum monomial weight. Positive integral
eta0 degrees leave only finitely many monomials of weight alpha. Thus the
maximum is attained. The construction is a T-invariant non-Archimedean
submultiplicative norm in the convention chi=-log||. It is linearly bounded
in every Reeb degree, and chi(f)<=0 if all assigned w_l<=0.

It is of finite type in Wu22 Definition3.6, including the issue that relations
may increase chi(h_l) above the originally assigned w_l. Reassign the finitely
many weights to chi(h_l). Multiplicativity and the ultrametric inequality
bound every minimum in every such representation above by chi(f). Conversely
an optimal original representation has each monomial's new weight at least
its old weight, so its minimum is at least chi(f). The quotient norm is
unchanged and an optimal representation uses the actual generator values.

For every Reeb eta,

    FS_eta(chi) = max_l (log|h_l|+w_l)/q_eta(weight h_l).

Indeed choose an optimal homogeneous representation of f. Its ultrametric
estimate and chi(f)<=each monomial weight give

    log|f|+chi(f)
       <= max_m sum_l e_(m,l)(log|h_l|+w_l).

Every monomial has q_eta degree q_eta(alpha); division yields a weighted
average bounded by the finite-list maximum. Conversely chi(h_l)>=w_l makes
the supremum over f at least that maximum. Saturation of chi therefore does
not change its FS metric. Power homogeneity of chi is not needed here.

If generator weights change by at most epsilon q_eta0(weight h_l), then
every homogeneous monomial of weight alpha changes by at most epsilon
q_eta0(alpha). Minima and their maximal representations have the same bound:

    |chi_epsilon(f)-chi(f)|<=epsilon q_eta0(alpha).

This quantitative estimate permits rational approximation of real assigned
weights in the normalized degree averages and FS functions.

## Signed cutoff identity and comparison

For a signed finite-type chi and fixed eta choose A so that all its shifted
generator weights w_l+A q_eta(weight h_l)>=0. Homogeneous polynomial
representations show exactly

    chi_A(f)=chi(f)+A q_eta(alpha),
    FS_eta(chi_A)=FS_eta(chi)+A.

The shifted norm remains multiplicative and has nonnegative jumps. A
homogeneous adapted basis gives the degree-shift identity for total jumps.
If N_eta(m)=dim R_eta(m), the total eta-degree D_eta(m) satisfies

    D_eta(m)=m N_eta(m)-integral_0^m N_eta(t) dt.

Since N_eta(m)~vol(eta)m^n/n!, including irrational eta, the normalized
average D_eta(m)/(m N_eta(m)) tends to n/(n+1). This follows directly by
rescaling the integral and dominated convergence; no rational-degree
Hilbert polynomial is assumed for irrational eta.

The rational finite-generated projective identity from the preceding
sources, Wu22 Lemma3.16(b), and the genuine cone/projective identity give
the corrected formula

    E_eta(FS_eta chi)=(n+1)/n * S_signed(chi,eta).

One may first prove it for the nonnegative shifted norm and subtract A:
both sides change by A since (n+1)/n times the degree-average shift is A.
The statement for irrational eta additionally requires the verified actual
simultaneous CLD continuity, not the erroneous last equality of Theorem5.15.

For nonpositive chi, let B_eta(m) be minus the total adapted jumping sum.
It is nonnegative and additive over full homogeneous weight spaces. If
l q_xi(alpha)<=q_zeta(alpha)<=u q_xi(alpha), then

    B_xi(m/u)<=B_zeta(m)<=B_xi(m/l).

This argument does not regard -chi as a multiplicative norm. Combining
the cutoffs with the volume bounds gives, under the just-checked identity,

    l^n/u^(n+1) (-E_xi phi_xi)
      <= -E_zeta phi_zeta
      <= u^n/l^(n+1) (-E_xi phi_xi).

The exact primary completion supplies one nonpositive chi representing
both original FS functions. Therefore no presentation-dependent constant
or additional C-limit enters this estimate.
