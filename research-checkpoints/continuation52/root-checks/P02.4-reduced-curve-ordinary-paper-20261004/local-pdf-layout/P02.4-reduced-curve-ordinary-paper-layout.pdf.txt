      Ordinary smooth regularization on reduced algebraic curves

                              Local research continuation for P02.4

                                           4 October 2026


Abstract
We give a fixed-neighborhood construction of decreasing ordinary Chambert–Loir–Ducros smooth
plurisubharmonic nets for arbitrary classical plurisubharmonic functions on reduced proper algebraic
curves over an algebraically closed, complete, nontrivially valued field. The construction allows
several normalization branches and values equal to minus infinity. It uses the explicitly stated
finite-tree smoothing theorem imported from the local manuscript. We prove the new directed-
selection and finite-branch descent arguments here. On a compact auxiliary domain, strict sublevels
being countable unions of closed sets is an exact functionwise condition for a decreasing continuous
sequence. In particular, countable density of the field gives ordinary smooth decreasing sequences
in the displayed curve scope. A saturated projective-bundle application is also proved. This is
an unreviewed local paper with an explicit imported foundation; it does not settle the original
classification of higher-dimensional analytic spaces in P02.4.


1. Domain, positivity and the imported foundation
Throughout, K is algebraically closed, complete and nontrivially valued. An ordinary open means
an open subset for the Berkovich topology. A classical psh function has values in R ∪ {−∞} and is
upper semicontinuous, with the classical curve-test property after analytic scalar extension. The
permitted identically minus-infinity case is handled explicitly. We never replace an arbitrary such
target by a finite continuous function.
An ordinary smooth function is smooth for analytic moment maps in the Chambert–Loir–Ducros
sense. Weak smoothness for harmonic tropicalizations is a different notion. Our positive approxi-
mants are ordinary smooth and have positive smooth curvature forms locally; consequently they are
classically psh. We use pullback stability of this actual ordinary smooth positive-form class. At
a singular point our descended approximants will be constant on an ordinary neighborhood, with
curvature zero there.

inequality gives |fℓ | = |fℓ (x)|. The finite tuple, and hence the smooth function of it, is constant
there.

2. Compact directed selection on a smooth branch
Lemma 2.1. Let Y be compact Hausdorff. Let (fi ) be a decreasing directed family of finite
continuous functions with infimum u. Let A ⊂ C(Y ) be stable under addition of real constants and
uniformly approximate each fi to every positive error. Then
                         Mu = {a ∈ A : for some δ > 0, a ≥ u + δ on Y }

                                                    2
is nonempty and downward directed, and its infimum is u. Its actual functions, indexed by reverse
pointwise order, form a decreasing net converging to u at every point. We interpret −∞ + δ = −∞.
Proof. If ∥g − fi ∥Y < ε, then g + 2ε ∈ Mu , since

                                    fi + ε ≤ g + 2ε ≤ fi + 3ε.

Given a, b ∈ Mu , choose δ > 0 no larger than their two available gaps. The function t =
min(a, b) − δ/2 is a finite continuous comparison function with t > u everywhere. The open sets
{fi < t} form an increasing directed cover of Y . Compactness and a dominating index give one i
with fi < t everywhere. Choose ε < δ/6 and set c = g + 2ε for a uniform approximant to fi . Then

                                 u + ε ≤ c ≤ fi + 3ε < min(a, b).

This proves downward directedness. The minimum is used solely as a comparison function; it is
not asserted to be psh. For a point y and a finite R > u(y), choose i with fi (y) < R and then ε
with fi (y) + 3ε < R. The shifted approximant is below R at y. This works for every finite R when
u(y) = −∞, and proves the claimed infimum. □
Lemma 2.2 (compact domination). If (qλ ) is a decreasing directed net of finite continuous
functions on a compact space A, with infimum u, and t ∈ C(A) satisfies t > u everywhere, then
some qλ < t on all of A. Finitely many additional strict pointwise inequalities compatible with the
limit can be imposed at the same time.
Proof. The sets {qλ < t} are an increasing directed open cover. Take a finite subcover and an
index dominating its indices. Each additional pointwise bound holds at some index by convergence;
dominate those finitely many indices too. □
Apply Lemma 2.1 on a fixed affinoid tree Y ⊂ W ⊂ C an with Y contained in a slightly larger
ordinary open on which u is defined. Thuillier provides the decreasing finite lisse family on that
larger open. Restrict it to the same Y and apply the imported finite-tree smoothing theorem. Let
A be the finite continuous functions on Y whose restrictions to Y ◦ are ordinary smooth psh. This
class is stable under constants. Lemma 2.1 gives an actual ordinary smooth psh decreasing net
on Y ◦ converging to the arbitrary u. There is no countability, residue-genus or residue-Picard
torsion assumption. A component where u is identically minus infinity can instead be treated by
the constant sequence −j.


3. Finite normalization and several branches

be ordinary open, and let u be an arbitrary classical psh function on W . At every x ∈ W there is
one compact strict affinoid neighborhood Y ⊂ W , with x ∈ U = Y ◦ , and a decreasing net of finite
continuous functions on Y , ordinary smooth and psh on the fixed U , converging pointwise to u|Y .
Multiple normalization branches and poles at the singularity are allowed. Each constructed net
pulls back with the same order and limit after every analytic scalar extension.

both v, w above, and fix y ∈ Y and a finite R > u(y). If a is finite and y = x, choose ε so small
that a + 2ε < R. The constructed common value is b + ε < a + 2ε < R. If a is finite and y ̸= x, the
point y has one normalization preimage ye. Choose ε with u(y) + 2ε < R when u(y) is finite, or any
sufficiently small positive ε when u(y) = −∞. Impose also gi (ye) < R − 2ε on its branch. Lemma
2.2 permits that simultaneous constraint and gives hi (ye) < R.
If a = −∞, choose in addition c < R − 2ε. For y = x the descended value is c + ε < R. For y ̸= x
impose gi (ye) < R − 2ε as well, taking ε so that this is compatible with its limit. The maximum
bound then gives hi (ye) < R. All choices involve finitely many branches and finitely many pointwise
conditions. Consequently inf Nu = u on all of Y , including poles. Indexing Nu by reverse pointwise
order proves the theorem’s net assertion.
The functions are actual local functions on U . Under an analytic morphism, analytic moment
maps and their positive curvature forms pull back; constants remain constant. Their restrictions to
overlapping charts remain equal. In particular the same functions pull back to UL for each analytic
extension L/K, and pointwise order and convergence to u ◦ p hold at each point of UL by evaluation
at its image. This is the pullback of the constructed base-field net, rather than descent of arbitrarily
chosen upstairs approximants. □


4. Exactly when a sequence can be extracted
Lemma 4.1. Let Y be compact Hausdorff and let (qλ ) be any decreasing directed net of finite
continuous functions with infimum u. There is a decreasing sequence of terms of this net converging
pointwise to u if every rational strict sublevel {u < r} is Fσ in Y . Conversely, the existence of any
pointwise decreasing continuous sequence with limit u implies that condition.

The resulting sequence decreases. If its infimum at a point exceeded u, an intermediate rational r
and a closed piece containing that point would contradict the eventual domination of λ(r, k). The
same argument works for u = −∞ using every rational r. □
Apply Lemma 4.1 to the actual net of Theorem 3.1 on its compact Y . Its terms are already ordinary
smooth psh on the fixed U ; selecting countably many dominating indices does not change that
domain or destroy branch alignment. Thus the Fσ condition is an exact functionwise criterion for a
decreasing sequence of finite continuous functions on this Y , ordinary smooth psh on U , with limit
u on all of Y .
If K has a countable dense subset, every strict affinoid Y is compact metrizable. To see this, a
countable family of Tate-polynomial evaluations densely approximates the elements of its affinoid
algebra and separates bounded seminorms. Those evaluations give an embedding into a countable
product of compact intervals; compactness and Hausdorffness give the stated metrizable topology.
Strict sublevels of the upper semicontinuous u are open, and every open subset of a metric space is
Fσ (use closed positive-distance inner layers). We obtain the following consequence.
Corollary 4.2. If K is also countably dense, every arbitrary classical psh function on an ordinary
open in a reduced proper algebraic K-curve has, locally on one fixed ordinary neighborhood, a
pointwise decreasing sequence of finite ordinary smooth psh approximants. This includes every
finite normalization branch count and every permitted minus-infinity target value. If the target is
finite continuous on the compact auxiliary Y , convergence there is uniform by Dini’s theorem.
The compact-domain qualifier matters. A sequence defined only on U need not continuously extend
to this particular Y . Necessity is therefore asserted for sequences on Y , or on a neighborhood of
a smaller compact domain Y ′ ⊂ U , rather than for an arbitrary unextended sequence on U . No
topology-only criterion for all higher-dimensional sheaf germs follows.


5. A saturated higher-dimensional application
Let p : X → Dan be an analytically locally trivial projective-space bundle of relative dimension
m ≥ 1. Let V ⊂ Dan be ordinary open and let v be an arbitrary classical psh function on the entire
saturated domain p−1 (V ). At a base point c, its restriction to the fiber is classical psh on Pm
                                                                                                H(c) .
The complete residue field H(c) need not be algebraically closed. That fiber is compact, connected
and boundaryless. If the restriction is not identically minus infinity, upper semicontinuity gives a
finite attained maximum. By the strong maximum principle [2, Theorem 6.1], its maximum locus is
open; it is also closed and nonempty. Connectedness makes the restriction constant. This proves
constancy on every fiber, including fibers over nonrigid base points.
There is consequently one function u on V with v = u ◦ p. Analytic local sections show that u is
upper semicontinuous and classically psh, and its sections agree on overlaps by fiber constancy.
Apply Theorem 3.1 at a base point and pull back its ordinary smooth psh net to p−1 (U ). Under
countable density apply Corollary 4.2 to obtain a sequence. Every term has the pulled-back actual
positive smooth curvature form, retains order and pointwise limit, and behaves likewise after scalar
extension.
This proves a theorem for the full classical class on saturated domains of these bundles, without any
torus invariance assumption. An arbitrary psh germ on a nonsaturated affine fiber chart can vary

along the fiber, so this argument makes no claim about that class.


6. Scope and remaining original obligations
The imported finite-tree smoothing is an explicit foundation from the authorized local manuscript.
The directed-selection, functionwise extraction and multibranch descent above are new local proof
applications. No independent verdict is claimed for this paper, and no frozen native draft, source or
canonical note has been altered. Neither active native equation author received this paper or its
imported premise.
P02.4 asks for the sharp geometric range of ordinary smooth decreasing psh regularization starting
with every classical psh function. The present theorem covers reduced proper algebraic curves over
the displayed algebraically closed field and a saturated bundle sector. It does not characterize
nonalgebraizable curve ranges, remove algebraic closedness in the ordinary construction, classify
general higher-dimensional local germs, or settle analytic-boundary cases. It therefore does not
close P02.4.
