# Canonical nonflat norm weights and a direct radial trace route

New root reconstruction, not independently reviewed or accepted. The frozen review74 and original author75 are unchanged; no hot feedback is supplied. Original P16.1 and P13.2 remain open. The algebra below concerns a finite dominant morphism of integral affine varieties over a characteristic-zero field, with normal target. The analytic formula concerns their actual Berkovich points over a complete ground field. The final weak-trace implication explicitly retains the smooth membership/removal antecedent; it is not an envelope or equation theorem.

## 1. A polynomial norm law exists without flatness

Let A be a normal finitely generated domain over a characteristic-zero field, let B be a finite integral A-algebra which is a domain, and put F=Frac(A), L=Frac(B), d=[L:F]. Finite dominance makes B torsion-free over A. Choose algebra generators b1,...,bm which also generate B as an A-module, adding 1 if needed. Form

    N(T1,...,Tm) = Norm_(L/F)(sum_i Ti bi).

This is homogeneous of degree d. Because L/F is separable, over an algebraic closure of F it is the product of the d conjugate linear forms. Every conjugate bi is integral over A. Every coefficient of the product is therefore integral over A, while it belongs to F by the determinant definition. Normality of A puts every coefficient in A. The same reasoning applies to the full characteristic polynomial of a universal element.

This defines a homogeneous multiplicative polynomial law on the actual algebra B, and not just on a free locus. Here is an explicit check of arbitrary scalar specialization. Fix a finite presentation A^ell -> A^m -> B -> 0, with relation columns rj. The identities

    N(T + sum_j Xj rj) = N(T)

hold over F and thus as polynomial identities over A. Multiplication and identity relations for the chosen generators similarly give N(bc)=N(b)N(c), N(1)=1 and N(a b)=a^d N(b). After any A-algebra extension R, these identities descend the polynomial to B tensor_A R, since tensoring the presentation is right exact. They also make the definition independent of the presentation. No determinant of the possibly larger-dimensional special-fiber regular representation is being substituted for this degree-d law.

The characteristic-polynomial identity holds in B[T] because it holds in L[T] and B injects into L. It therefore specializes as well. In particular, on any finite-dimensional fiber algebra C over a field H, with specialized law N_H,

    N_H(c) != 0  if and only if  c is a unit of C.                 (1)

One direction uses multiplicativity. In the other, the degree-d characteristic identity expresses c^-1 as a polynomial in c divided by the nonzero constant term N_H(c). This implication is the key positivity input at nonflat fibers.

## 2. Every actual fiber has canonical positive integer weights

First extend H to an algebraically closed field Omega. The finite algebra C_Omega decomposes into local Artinian algebras with residue field Omega. On its unit group the norm law ignores nilpotents. Indeed, for a nilpotent n,

    P(t)=N_Omega(exp(t n))

is a polynomial, since exp is a finite sum. It satisfies P(t+u)=P(t)P(u) and P(0)=1. If its degree were positive, the two sides would have different total degrees in (t,u); hence P=1. Every element of 1+rad(C_Omega) is such an exponential, by the finite logarithm formula. Thus the norm on units depends only on their nonzero residue coordinates lambda1,...,lambdar.

Its restriction to the semisimple subalgebra Omega^r is a polynomial P(lambda). Multiplicativity P(lambda mu)=P(lambda)P(mu) implies it is a single monomial: comparison of the two independent sets of variables excludes two distinct nonzero coefficients. N(1)=1 fixes the coefficient. Polynomiality makes the exponents nonnegative integers. Homogeneity gives

    N_Omega(c) = product_j residue_j(c)^gamma_j,
    gamma_j >= 0,                 sum_j gamma_j = d.              (2)

The identity, first obtained on units, holds everywhere by polynomial density. Set one residue coordinate to zero and the others to one. That element is a nonunit, so (1) forces its norm to be zero. Therefore every gamma_j is strictly positive. These exponents are unique and depend only on the specialized law, not on a test function, generators, a slicing curve or a flatness choice.

For a complete field H, write the finite fiber algebra as a product of local algebras C_x with residue fields H_x. All H_x/H are finite separable because the characteristic is zero. Conjugate embeddings into an algebraic closure have the same gamma, by the H-defined polynomial law. The complete-field absolute value has the same extension on every conjugate of H_x. Define

    Gamma_x = gamma_x [H_x:H] > 0.

Then sum_x Gamma_x=d. The x here are precisely the analytic points of the finite H-fiber: nilpotents do not create extra seminorms, and the absolute value on a finite extension of a complete field is unique. Thus these are positive integer weights on the actual fiber over every s in S^an, including nonflat fibers and arbitrary H(s).

On a flat fiber the specialized law is the determinant of multiplication on the d-dimensional regular representation. A composition series of each local Artinian factor shows that gamma_x is its length over the residue field. Consequently Gamma_x is its H-dimension, exactly the usual finite-flat analytic weight. This identifies the weights on the flat locus without claiming that the whole special-fiber algebra has dimension d at a nonflat point.

The weighted sum of a pulled-back function also commutes with complete field extension. A factor H_x splits after scalar extension into finite separable field factors whose dimensions over the new field sum to [H_x:H]; its common character exponent stays gamma_x. The new weights therefore sum above x to Gamma_x. Values of a function pulled back from the original analytic space are constant on those points. This verifies trace compatibility independently of any affine regularization theorem.

## 3. Exact whole-base Gauss formula for finite logarithmic maxima

Let f0,...,fq be elements of B and let c0,...,cq be arbitrary real constants. For an integer p>0 put

    u(y) = (1/p) max_i (log|fi(y)|+ci).

Zero terms have logarithm negative infinity. To obtain an everywhere finite continuous function, assume that one of the fi is 1, giving a constant floor. Form the actual polynomial

    Q(T) = Norm_(L/F)(sum_i Ti fi) = sum_|alpha|=d q_alpha T^alpha,
    q_alpha in A.

For any actual s in S^an, evaluate its coefficients in H=H(s). Equip the polynomial ring with the weighted Gauss norm |Ti|=exp(ci). This norm is multiplicative even when the radii are outside |H^*|: the initial forms multiply in a polynomial ring over the graded residue domain, so a product of two nonzero initial forms is nonzero. Only finitely many terms are involved. The statement also applies over trivially valued H.

Extend the coefficients isometrically to the completed algebraic closure of H and apply (2). The norm of each conjugate linear form is max_i exp(ci)|fi(x)|. Taking logarithms gives the exact identity

    sum_(x over s) Gamma_x u(x)
      = (1/p) max_|alpha|=d (log|q_alpha(s)|+sum_i alpha_i ci).    (3)

No generic-flatness limit or continuity assumption on a trace has entered this formula. It defines the trace of every such finite maximum on the entire base. The constant-floor coefficient supplies a nonzero constant lower bound for the Gauss norm, so the right side is a finite continuous logarithmic-polynomial maximum.

If the input also retains source algebra generators and has their positive lower growth, original base generators can be retained on the right. Express each base generator as a polynomial in the source generators. The non-Archimedean coefficient bound yields H_base(s) <= D H_source(x)+C for all x over s, after both exhaustions are given constant floors. Hence the trace has a lower bound a H_base-b with a>0, since sum Gamma_x=d. Append sufficiently small positive rational base-generator branches with sufficiently negative constants; they are dominated everywhere. A common denominator represents the existing q_alpha branches by powers and the new branches by powers of the original base generators. This changes neither the trace nor its carrier. In the trivially valued rational-weight setting, the constants can be chosen rational. With general real weights, retain the real constants explicitly rather than claiming rationality.

If v_j are continuous finite-max functions converging uniformly to v on the entire compact preimage pi^-1(K), then

    sup_K |Trace(v_j)-Trace(v)| <= d sup_(pi^-1 K) |v_j-v|.       (4)

Equation (3) and (4) prove continuity of the trace on K. The same proof works for directed decreasing nets whose limit is continuous: the compact finite-intersection argument gives uniform convergence. It uses whole compact preimages, and supplies no uniform approximation theorem for an arbitrary weak singular input. If the approximants and their limits are genuine base AFS functions in the specified target class, its actual uniform/decreasing closure gives membership. Such membership is a separate hypothesis, not a consequence of the norm algebra alone.

## 4. Compatibility with transverse curves and independence of slicing

In the original affine setting take S to be open in affine n-space over the ground field, with n>=1. The dimension-zero trace is already given by sections 1-3 and needs no radial argument. At an analytic s, extend to H(s), where s becomes a rational point. Choose a line C through that point meeting the dense etale/isomorphism open generically; the field is infinite. Remove any target boundary so C is a smooth affine curve in S_H. The source after this field extension is equidimensional of dimension n: a finite torsion-free A-module embeds in a finite free module after clearing denominators, and the injection survives the flat field extension, so its irreducible components still dominate S_H. Every irreducible component of the reduced scheme-theoretic preimage of C has dimension one: the n-1 equations give dimension at least one by the principal-ideal theorem, and finiteness gives dimension at most one. Every component therefore dominates C. Embedded zero-dimensional components and nilpotents are discarded only in this auxiliary curve construction, never in a subsequent MA fundamental cycle.

Normalize this reduced preimage. The resulting finite morphism q:Cn -> C is flat because its module is torsion-free over the regular one-dimensional base. Its generic algebra equals the original finite etale algebra over the generic point of C, of total degree d. Its norm polynomial therefore equals Q restricted to C: they agree generically and their coefficients are regular on the integral curve. The fiber character exponents in (2) are unique. Thus the sum of q's usual flat weights above each original fiber point is exactly Gamma_x. This proves that every such admissible line gives the same weights, and not merely one arbitrary choice of positive numbers.

## 5. Direct radial recovery and the remaining weak-trace antecedent

Here is a conditional application to a weak u supplied by ONE original proper witness rho:Z -> Y. Retain these precise analytic hypotheses: the pullback rho*u is locally a decreasing limit of the actual convex nondecreasing finite logarithmic expressions; these expressions and their extended zero-coordinate values pull back through complete field extension and algebraic curve morphisms; the already reconstructed smooth theorem regularizes u on the dense smooth affine open with the required UPPER-growth bound; and smooth principal-open removal produces a genuine base function v which equals the finite-flat trace on that open. None of these global membership/removal assertions is established by sections 1-4.

A component of Cn from section 4 lifts to the witness Z_H: on the generically isomorphic open it lifts uniquely, and properness extends the lift over the normal curve by the valuative criterion. This uses the properness over Y of the original witness. Pull the supplied local logarithmic expressions to this curve, rather than invoke a global affine regularization theorem over H(s).

After further complete algebraically closed extension, choose a local parameter z at a point above s. For sufficiently small radius r, the Gauss point z_r tests each convergent regular function g by its power series. Its norm is at least |g(0)|. Every supplied convex expression is nondecreasing in these log norms, including the allowed infinite coordinates, so its value at z_r is at least its value at the central point. Passing through the decreasing approximants gives u(z_r)>=u(0). Upper semicontinuity supplies the opposite limiting inequality. Hence

    lim_(r down to 0) u(z_r)=u(0),

also when that value is negative infinity. This is an actual value recovery argument; it does not exchange an unbounded moving test with a weak measure limit.

Near each point of Cn over the chosen closed point of C, write a base parameter t as z^e times an analytic unit. In characteristic zero over the complete algebraically closed extension, shrink the disk so the unit admits an analytic e-th root, and absorb it into z. Then t=z^e. Over a sufficiently small base disk, proper finiteness excludes other preimages. The preimage of its Gauss radius r consists of these local Gauss points at radii r^(1/e), with weights e. The finite character identity identifies their limiting sums with the canonical Gamma_x at s. A negative-infinity summand causes the sum to tend to negative infinity since all other summands are locally bounded above.

The genuine base v has the same central-value recovery along the base radius, from its actual local logarithmic approximants and scalar-extension compatibility. Every positive sufficiently small radius belongs to the etale/isomorphism open, since each excluded algebraic equation on C is a nonzero power series with nonzero Gauss norm. On those radii v equals the finite-flat trace of u. Taking the two radial limits and using the canonical weights proves

    v(s) = sum_(x over s) Gamma_x u(x).

Thus, under the explicitly retained smooth membership/removal and local-expression compatibility hypotheses, the genuine trace equals the weighted trace at every boundary point. No global affine regularization over an arbitrary H(s) was used. The algebraic weights and formula (3) are unconditional within their stated hypotheses; the extension to a general weak u still needs these analytic prerequisites. The original singular envelope and finite-energy equation consequently remain unresolved.
