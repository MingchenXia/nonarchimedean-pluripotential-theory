## 1. Original functions, targets and a unit-cost definition

Keep the original smooth integral projective variety X over an algebraically closed, trivially valued characteristic-zero field of arbitrary cardinality, and an actual big rational line bundle L. Let V=P_L(0) be its intrinsic minimal reference and m=vol(L)>0 its raw mass. E denotes the target-independent reconstructed raw relative energy with E(V)=0. Potentials and measures are actual objects on X^an; neither an inverse-defined operator nor a completion carrier is introduced.

Write E1_0 for the entire genuine finite-energy potentials with supremum zero and B(v)=-E(v). For a positive original Radon measure nu of mass m define, wherever finite,

\[
 H_v(\nu)=\int(V-v)\,d\nu,
 \qquad D(\nu)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal E^1_0}\{H_v(\nu)-B(v)\}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Let Z={V=-infinity} be the Borel reference-pole set. Let N be the intrinsic target space consisting of precisely such measures with nu(Z)=0 and finite D. The explicit reference-pole convention is necessary before evaluating the relative differences; finite D on the complement alone does not prove it. Own161 proves that this is equivalent to integrability of every relative genuine E1 test, and proves the finite uniform unit-cost bound

\[
 C(\nu):=\sup_{v\in\mathcal E^1_0,\ B(v)\le1}H_v(\nu)<\infty.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

All relative differences here are evaluated off the common reference-pole set, which is null by the explicitly retained target convention; the integrals are independent of any value chosen on that null set. Normalized potentials satisfy v<=V, so H_v is nonnegative. The numerical-zero case is excluded from this argument: m is strictly positive throughout.

For nu,eta in N put

\[
 d_*(\nu,\eta)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal E^1_0,\ B(v)\le1}
                |H_v(\nu)-H_v(\eta)|.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

This definition uses only the original intrinsic cone, its energy and positive target measures. Its value is finite by (1.2). It is symmetric and satisfies the triangle inequality. At this point separation has not been assumed.

## 2. Quadratic reference rescaling controls every test and solution cost

The entire quadratic reference contraction in own161 gives one K>=1, depending only on the retained dimension and its proved numerical estimate, with

\[
 B((1-r)V+rv)\le Kr^2B(v),\quad 0\le r\le1,
 \qquad H_{(1-r)V+rv}(\nu)=rH_v(\nu).
 \tag{2.1}
\]

These are genuine functions, since convexity of the numerical cone and finite energy were constructed before the target equation. Set

\[
 T(b)=\max\{1,\sqrt{Kb}\},\qquad b\ge0.
\]

Choose r=1/T(B(v)). Its reference contraction has cost at most one. Equations (1.2), (1.3) and (2.1) therefore give

\[
 H_v(\nu)\le C(\nu)T(B(v)),
 \qquad |H_v(\nu)-H_v(\eta)|\le d_*(\nu,\eta)T(B(v)).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

The case B(v)=0 causes no division: r=1 and the potential is V by the strict zero-cost statement. These estimates hold on the whole genuine E1 cone after normalization, rather than only on bounded or model potentials.

Let u_nu be the normalized actual solution furnished by own165 and167, equivalently the unique maximizer of F_nu(v)=H_v(nu)-B(v). Since V is feasible with F_nu(V)=0,

\[
 B(u_\nu)\le H_{u_\nu}(\nu)\le C(\nu)T(B(u_\nu)).
\]

If B(u_nu)>1/K this implies sqrt(B(u_nu))<=sqrt(K)C(nu). In all cases

\[
 B(u_\nu)\le\max\{1/K,\ KC(\nu)^2\}.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

This is a bound on an actually attained solution, not on a hypothetical maximizer in a completion. Also |C(nu)-C(eta)|<=d_*(nu,eta), directly from their common unit-cost supremum. Thus convergence in d_* itself implies a common normalized solution-cost bound. A separate uniform dual-energy hypothesis is unnecessary.

## 3. Separation of intrinsic targets

If d_*(nu,eta)=0, (2.2) gives H_v(nu)=H_v(eta) for every genuine normalized E1 potential. Thus their two genuine variational functionals are identical. By own165 each attains its maximum at an actual original potential; by own167 the maximizer is unique and every attained maximizer has the previously constructed metric output equal to its target. Hence their normalized maximizers agree, and applying that one actual operator gives nu=eta.

Consequently d_* is a metric on the intrinsic target space N. This proof of separation uses the previously reconstructed original equation; it does not define M as an inverse of d_* or as a chosen measure. In particular no unsupported claim that E1 tests linearly span all continuous original observables is needed.

## 4. Target convergence implies actual strong solution convergence

Let nu_i be an arbitrary net in N with d_*(nu_i,nu) tending to zero, and let u_i,u be their actual normalized solutions. Equation (2.3) puts u_i and u in a single cost sublevel B<=A on a tail, where A is finite. On this entire sublevel, (2.2) gives

\[
 \sup_{B(v)\le A}|F_{\nu_i}(v)-F_\nu(v)|
       \le d_*(\nu_i,\nu)T(A)=:\epsilon_i\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Every F here is evaluated on original genuine functions. Because u_i and u are maximizers for their own targets, evaluating the two optimality inequalities at each other's solution proves

\[
 |D(\nu_i)-D(\nu)|\le\epsilon_i,
 \qquad F_\nu(u_i)\longrightarrow D(\nu).
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Own145 proves that this normalized cost sublevel is compact and closed in the original divisorial topology. Own168 proves H_v(nu) continuous as a function of v on this sublevel for the fixed intrinsic nu. E is upper semicontinuous there, so F_nu is upper semicontinuous. If a subnet of u_i converges to a genuine potential u', (4.2) gives F_nu(u')>=D(nu). Equality must hold, and own167 gives u'=u. The compact Hausdorff uniqueness-of-cluster argument now proves convergence of the whole net to u, without sequential compactness or a metrizability assumption on the carrier.

The same fixed-target continuity and (4.1) show

\[
 H_{u_i}(\nu_i)=H_{u_i}(\nu)+o(1)\longrightarrow H_u(\nu).
\]

The actual solution identity E(u_i)=D(nu_i)-H_{u_i}(nu_i), together with (4.2), therefore gives E(u_i) to E(u). Divisorial plus energy convergence is the actual strong convergence criterion of own156. Hence u_i converges strongly to u, as genuine all-point functions, in the proved original metric d_R. The entire I convergence follows from own168.

This argument works for arbitrary target nets on the original nonmetrizable carrier. The compactness step is on genuine cost sublevels; it never manufactures an abstract limit and then asserts it has an original representative.

## 5. The converse and the resulting topology on targets

Suppose actual normalized solutions u_i converge strongly to u, and set nu_i=M(u_i), nu=M(u). Own173, equation (3.1) with A=1, gives exactly

\[
 d_*(\nu_i,\nu)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Thus the candidate equation gives a homeomorphism between the entire normalized genuine E1 cone with its actual strong topology and the intrinsic target space N with the metric d_*. This includes arbitrary target supports subject to the necessary reference-pole convention and finite intrinsic dual. Reference-pole nullness is retained explicitly; own158 proves nullness of other pluripolar sets from this convention and the finite dual. It does not impose atomic, divisorial, skeletal or countable support.

As another consequence, d_* convergence implies ordinary weak convergence against every original continuous observable: Section4 gives strong solution convergence, and own150 gives weak continuity of the preconstructed metric operator. We have not asserted that weak target convergence alone implies d_* convergence. The norm controls all cost-one relative tests simultaneously; ordinary weak convergence controls each continuous test separately.

The argument also proves continuity of D in d_*, using (4.2). For locally bounded C(nu), its modulus there is at most T(A)d_*, where A may be chosen by (2.3). This local bound has explicit genuine-cost content; it is not based on a probability normalization.

No completeness assertion for the metric d_* is made here. A homeomorphism from the complete genuine metric d_R does not itself imply completeness for a different compatible metric, and the estimates above do not supply a uniform reverse Cauchy bound.
