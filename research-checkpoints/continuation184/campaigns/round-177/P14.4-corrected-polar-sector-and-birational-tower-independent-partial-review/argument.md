\begin{lemma}[Old pole comparison]\label{pole}
If actual FS scalars satisfy $\psi\leq0$ and
$F\geq(1-\delta)\psi-k$, $0<\delta<1$, and usc $h\leq\psi+C$,
then $h-F$ off the poles of $F$, assigned $-\infty$ at those poles,
is usc. Decreasing such $h$ give a decreasing comparison net. If its
infimum is negative everywhere, an entire tail is negative everywhere.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}

makes that bound tend to $-\infty$. The assignment is a comparison
extension, not the value of $(-\infty)-(-\infty)$. Nested compact
nonnegative superlevels and directedness prove the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual decreasing closure]\label{closure}
A decreasing net $u_i\in\PSH(\theta)$ with finite generic infimum
has one all-point decreasing FS defining net with classes tending to
$\theta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

$T_\eta=L+\eta A$, choose $0<\epsilon<\eta/4$, $B=L+\epsilon A$,
and a fixed normalized G1 reference stage $\psi$ on $S$ so close to
$B$ that $T_\eta-S$ is ample. Choose small rational $\delta>0$ with
$T_\eta-(1-\delta)S$ ample. A special stage is an FS scalar on
$T_\eta$ with complete such data and
\[
 F\geq u+\kappa,\qquad F\geq(1-\delta)\psi-k,
 \qquad\kappa>0.
 \tag{3.1}
\]
It exists by taking the maximum of a shifted late defining majorant
of some $u_i$ and the floor, tensoring both into $T_\eta$.
For every fixed old $B_l,\psi_l$, the $u_i$ and late defining stages
$h$ of a fixed $u_i$ embed into $B_l$. Their generic values are at
most $M+1$, so first envelope maximality gives
$h\leq R_{B_l}+M+1\leq\psi_l+c_l+M+1$. This also bounds all $u_i$.

Given finitely many old stages $F_l$, choose the new $\eta$ arbitrarily
small below their $\epsilon_l$. Choose the new $S$ close enough to
$L+\epsilon A$ that every $B_l-S$ is ample. Then
$\psi\leq R_{B_l}\leq\psi_l+c_l$. Take
$0<\delta<\min_l\delta_l$. Where $F_l$ is finite, the new floor
$b=(1-\delta)\psi-k$ obeys
$b-F_l\leq(\delta_l-\delta)\psi_l+(1-\delta)c_l+k_l-k$.
One large $k$ makes it at most zero. At an old pole $b=-\infty$.
Choose $0<\kappa<\min_l\kappa_l/4$. Lemma~\ref{pole} first applied
to $u_i+2\kappa$ selects one late $i$ below every old strict guard.
Applied again to its literal defining tail, with the same old bounds,
it selects $h$ fitting the new target and with $h+\kappa\leq F_l$.
Thus $\max(h+\kappa,b)$ is a new special stage below all old ones.
Order all special stages by scalar and $\eta$ decrease. They are directed.

For $x$ and finite $t>u(x)$ choose a late $u_i(x)<t$ with margin,
then its target gap before a late defining $h$ fitting that gap, then
a small guard and large floor constant so both branches are below $t$
at $x$. This also works for every finite $t$ if $u(x)=-\infty$.
Subsequent directed refinements impose every old constraint. All stages
are above $u$, so their all-point infimum is exactly $u$. Their classes
are $\theta+\eta[A]\to\theta$ and their generic endpoint is finite.
\end{proof}

\[

\]

 T_\eta^{n+1}-T_\xi^{n+1}+(n+1)ST_\eta^n

\]

\]

$\min(t,1-t)/2$. Its integral is $1/8$; on $[1/4,3/4]$ the kernel
and each Bernstein monomial give the stated lower coefficient.

The finite slot-change calculation uses only (6.1), Cauchy--Schwarz and

       \max(J_A(\xi),J_A(\eta),J_A(\rho_l))^{1-\beta}.
 \tag{6.4}
\]
This reconstructs the primary finite calculation with the corrected

 \tag{6.5}

All multiplicities and raw volumes are present. For $n=1$ omit the

The terms with $k=0$ sum to at most $ra_0$; every other term has

\section{The original outer derivative and signed big margin}
For $f_D\leq0$, $\mathscr E(f_D)\geq-B$ and $c>B/V$, put
$g_D(c)=\vol(\rho_X^*L+D+cF)/(n+1)$.
Formula (4.2) makes $g_D$ convex, $V$-Lipschitz, zero at $c=0$ and
eventually $cV+\mathscr E(f_D)$, with $g_D(c)\geq cV-B>0$.
Thus the deficient class is genuinely big. Its positive derivative
measure $\mu_{D,c}$ has mass between $V-B/c$ and $V$.
There is coefficientwise domination
\[
 0\leq\mu_{D,c}\leq\mu_D,
 \quad\|\mu_D-\mu_{D,c}\|_{\rm TV}\leq B/c.
 \tag{7.1}
\]
Indeed the actual full-lattice trace
$\mathscr E(f_D)+cV-g_D(c)
=\lim n!m^{-n-1}\sum_j\min(w_{mj}+mc,0)$ is monotone under
adding any effective vertical divisor: inclusion of the full filtration
raises each ordered weight. Differentiate this monotonicity in each
effective $E$. The difference coefficient is nonnegative. Convexity
in $c$ gives its stated mass bound.

Fix one rational original model test $h=f_H$ on a fixed determination.
Choose ample $T$ there, $\gamma=\rho_X^*L+cF$, and
$\kappa=(n+1)(cV-B)>0$, $M=(n+1)\langle\gamma^n\rangle T$.
For every refinement carrying $D$, volume loss along $\alpha-rT$
while big is at most $rM$, since $\alpha=\rho_X^*L+D+cF\leq\gamma$.
Boundary continuity excludes a first exit before
$r_0=\kappa/[2(M+1)]$. Choose $a$ with $aT\pm H$ nef and put
$\lambda=a/r_0$. Then $\lambda\alpha\pm H$ are psef, so for

$\langle(\alpha+sH)^n\rangle$ between
$(1\pm\lambda|s|)^n\langle\alpha^n\rangle$.
Pair separately with the two fixed nef terms $aT+H$ and $aT$.
This bounds the derivative variation uniformly over all $D$ and refinements
and gives a fixed $K_c$ quadratic remainder for $g_{D+tH}(c)$.

For the full energy use the nonpositive obstacle
$f_D+sh-|s|H_0$, $H_0=\|h\|_\infty$, and parameter
$c_s=c+|s|H_0$. Its cost is at most $B_s=B+2V|s|H_0$.
Impose, before differentiating either sign,
\[
 |t|H_0<(c-B/V)/2,\qquad |t|<1.
 \tag{7.2}
\]
Then $c_s>B_s/V$ throughout the path, whose actual deficient class is
exactly $\alpha+sH$. The rough bound $B+2VH_0$ by itself does not
prove this when $c$ is just above $B/V$. Integrate the positive derivative

\[
 |\mathscr E(f_D+th)-\mathscr E(f_D)-t\mu_D(h)|
 \leq K_ct^2/2+|t|H_0(2B+2VH_0)/c.
 \tag{7.3}
\]
All order estimates use fixed nef tests. Both signed perturbations have
the verified margin (7.2).

For $u\in\EE$ use its canonical nonpositive model majorants $f_i$.
Their positive mass-$V$ measures have weak cluster points on original
compact $K$. At fixed $t$, their perturbed genuine envelopes decrease
exactly to $P_\theta(u+th)$, with decreasing energies. Pass the whole
increment (7.3) along a cluster, then divide with each sign, let $t\to0$
at fixed $c$, and finally $c\to\infty$. It gives a unique cluster $M(u)$
and
\[
 \left.\frac d{dt}\right|_0E(P_\theta(u+th))=\int h\,dM(u).
 \tag{7.4}
\]
Uniform model approximation changes each divided increment and integral
by at most $V$ times its sup error. Thus (7.4) holds for every original
continuous $h$. This is a target-independent outer-energy map, positive
of raw mass $V$, on actual functions on $K$.
The envelope and energy constant laws show directly from (7.4) that
$M(u+c)=M(u)$ for every real $c$; no target equation is used for this.
For ordered bounded-cost endpoints $a\geq b$, compare their perturbed
energy increments with (7.3); for fixed model $h$,
\[
 |M(a)(h)-M(b)(h)|\leq (E(a)-E(b))/t+K_ct+C_{h,B}/c.
 \tag{7.5}
\]
This proves decreasing weak continuity. At $u=P_\theta(f_D)$ apply
it to model majorants and their minima with $f_D$: the latter have
exactly the same envelope and energy as $f_D$. First (7.5) makes their
derivatives equal to $\mu_D$, then compares them to the canonical
majorants. Thus $M(P_\theta(f_D))=\mu_D$.

For an actual effective Cartier $S$, the vertical divisor moment of

part has nonnegative volume derivative. A fixed nef upper test gives

\[
 \int b_N\,dM(u)\leq C_{S,c}+NB/c.
 \tag{7.6}
\]
Divide by $N$, take $N\to\infty$, then $c\to\infty$.
Hence $M(u)(S^{\rm an})=0$, in particular $M(u)(Z)=0$.
This argument gives algebraic nullness and uniform algebraic collars.
It does not yet identify all unbounded genuine weighted products.

\section{Intrinsic targets and what pre-attainment estimates supply}
For a positive original Radon measure $\nu$ of raw mass $V$, missing
$\{R=-\infty\}$, set
\[
 I_\nu(u)=\int(R-u)d\nu,\qquad
 D_\nu=\sup_{u\in\EE,\ u(v_0)=0}\{I_\nu(u)-B(u)\}.
 \tag{8.1}
\]
The difference is taken where $R$ is finite; no absolute integrability
of $R$ is stipulated. Finite $D_\nu$ implies integrability of every
genuine relative E1 test directly. Conversely if all are integrable,
their integrals are uniformly bounded on $B(u)\leq1$: otherwise choose
$I_\nu(u_j)\geq4^j$ and take the genuine decreasing convex sums
$\sum_{j\leq m}2^{-j}u_j+2^{-m}R$ of bounded cost. Their actual E1
endpoint has infinite relative integral by ordinary countable monotone
convergence. Contraction (6.7), with $r=(2^{n+1}B(u))^{-1/2}$ for
large cost, then bounds $I_\nu(u)$ by a constant times $1+\sqrt{B(u)}$,
so $D_\nu<\infty$. Thus (8.1) is an intrinsic relative energy condition.
It implies $\nu(Z)=0$ using the actual E1 pole constructed above.

Writing $C_0=2^{n+1}$, contraction also gives
\[
 I_\nu(u)\leq\epsilon B(u)+C_0D_\nu/\epsilon,
 \qquad 0<\epsilon\leq C_0.
 \tag{8.2}
\]
Hence near-maximizers have bounded actual energy cost. Section density
on $U$ and directed continuous Radon integration show that model-envelope
objectives have exactly the same supremum. The Radon net assertion used
here is elementary: increasing continuous superlevels on $U$ cover each
compact subset of the limiting open superlevel by finitely many sets;
directedness gives one dominating index, inner regularity gives its mass,
and finite layer-cake sums give the integral. No measurable-net monotone
convergence assertion is imported.

Choose model near-maximizers $a_j$ within $\varepsilon_j\to0$ and bounded
cost. Their outputs are finite divisorial measures. For a fixed continuous
test $h$, $z_{j,t}=P_\theta(a_j+th)$ lies between
$a_j\pm|t|\|h\|$. Its objective and obstacle inequality give
$E(z_{j,t})-E(a_j)-t\nu(h)\leq\varepsilon_j$.
Both signs of the uniform fixed-increment bound (7.3), first at fixed
$c,t$, then $j\to\infty$, then $t\downarrow0$, then $c\to\infty$,
prove $M(a_j)\to\nu$ weakly. This uses no attained inverse.

For fixed model envelopes $a,b,w$, (5.5) applied to every fixed majorant
of $(a+b)/2$ gives
$\limsup_m\delta_{A_m}(\psi_{m,a},\psi_{m,b})\leq\delta(a,b)$,
where $\delta(a,b)=E((a+b)/2)-(E(a)+E(b))/2$.
This is only an upper midpoint estimate; it does not assume the bounded
barycenter identification proved later. Formula (6.5), compact-uniform
bounded quotient convergence on $U$, and weak endpoint output convergence
then give the absolute model estimate. On a fixed continuous cutoff in
$U$, bounded changing tests pass by uniform convergence; omitted mass
is bounded by the limiting measure's omitted mass, since it misses $Z$.
Canonical output approximation and increasing continuous relative costs
extend it to all genuine tests and outputs:
\[
 \int|a-b|dM(w)\leq
 C_n\delta(a,b)^\alpha B(w)^{1/2}
       \max(B(a),B(b),B(w))^{1/2-\alpha}.
 \tag{8.3}
\]
First use $a=R$, bounded majorants of $b$, and the bound
$\delta(R,b_i)\leq B(b)/2$ to prove every actual relative test
integrable at $M(w)$. Then pass two test nets separately in absolute
$L^1$; their midpoint energies decrease. The same model-pair passage
through the pre-attainment outputs $M(a_j)\to\nu$ gives (8.3) at a
target, with the output cost bound of that sequence. These arguments
retain all generic terms in (6.5) before the degree limit.

Every output consequently satisfies the intrinsic target condition.
For fixed relative tests, (8.3) bounds the ordered majorant errors
uniformly over any bounded-cost output family. Fixed bounded relative
tests pass weak convergence on a fixed $U$ cutoff; then those absolute
errors pass the unbounded test. Contact is already forced by (4.7): at
$a=P_\theta(f_D)$ the equality $E(a)=\mathscr E(f_D)$ and $a\leq f_D$
give $0\leq\int(a-f_D)d\mu_D\leq0$. Thus finite planes, these exact
contact integrals, and the fixed-test passage give the whole supporting plane
\[
 E(v)\leq E(u)+\int(v-u)dM(u),\qquad u,v\in\EE.
 \tag{8.4}
\]
This is an outer-map plane, not the entire mixed-operator identification.

\section{Exact quotient traces and bounded physical identification}
Fix an actual $p:Y\to X$ with $p^*L=A+T$, $A$ nef and generated in
cleared degree, $T\geq0$. Multiplication by $s_{\ell T}$ injects the
\emph{full} $H^0(Y,\ell A)$ as a fixed subspace $S_\ell$ of
$R_\ell=H^0(X,\ell L)$. Let $V_A=A^n$, $\varepsilon=V-V_A\geq0$.
Use the induced subspace and quotient flags of the original (4.5) flag.
Extend an adapted subspace basis to a full adapted basis. Its trace is
exactly the sum of subspace and quotient traces. Uniform shifts by
$\ell\|f-g\|$ also shift every ordered quotient weight by at most this
amount. Quotient rank asymptotics therefore give
\[
 |\mathscr Q_A(f)-\mathscr Q_A(g)|\leq\varepsilon\|f-g\|,
 \qquad\mathscr Q_A=\mathscr E-\mathscr H_A.
 \tag{9.1}
\]
The same finite flag argument gives monotonicity. Rounding error is at
most the quotient rank and vanishes in the trace scaling.

Here is the required actual restricted-lattice calculation. Take a smooth
common equivariant total determination \emph{mapping to}
$Y\times\mathbb P^1$ and carrying the original obstacle $f_D$.
Write the total pulled $T$ as $T^{\rm hor}+T^{\rm ver}$. A generic
section in the restricted space has the literal form $s_{\ell T}a$,
$a\in H^0(Y,\ell A)$. In an actual local frame, its original extension
order is the order of $a$ plus the order of $s_{\ell T}$ in the pulled
frame. Along every central prime this is the extension condition in
\[
 p^*L+D-T^{\rm hor}=p^*A+D+T^{\rm ver}.
 \tag{9.2}
\]
Along horizontal primes, regularity as a section of the latter bundle
is precisely regularity of $a$ as a section of $A$ on its fixed generic
model. Thus both implications hold at every prime: multiplying gives
the original section with the prescribed horizontal zeros; division
gives an actual $A$ section and the retained central orders.
Normality identifies sections with these orders. Consequently this is
the full relative lattice of (9.2), not a selected series. Its retained
vertical term is exactly the one used in the corrected complete moving
formula (5.2), before its effective residual base divisor is removed. This argument
requires the actual morphism to the specified auxiliary product; it
does not replace it by numerical equality. The example of blowing up
$(z,t)=(0,0)$ on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ has
$p_X^*\{z=0\}=T^{\rm hor}+E$; the vertical $E$ term in (9.2)
is essential, and its cancellation confirms rather than contradicts
the formula.

If $V_A>0$, the time-twisted bundle (9.2) is big for sufficiently
large $c$. Its full lattice diagonalizes, and (4.2)--(4.4) applied to
this actual bundle give the restricted intercept $\mathscr H_A(f)$,
and its positive finite divisorial derivative $\mu_{A,T;f}$ of mass
$V_A$. Original and restricted derivatives test the same pulled original
model direction. The derivative of (9.1) has norm at most $\varepsilon$
and value $\varepsilon$ on the constant one, by the two constant laws.
A real functional with these properties is positive: for $0\leq h\leq1$,
the norm bound on $1-h$ gives its value on $h$ at least zero.
Model density extends this to continuous tests. Hence
\[
 0\leq\mu_f-p_*\mu_{A,T;f},\qquad
 \|\mu_f-p_*\mu_{A,T;f}\|_{\rm TV}=V-V_A.
 \tag{9.3}
\]
If $V_A=0$, restricted rank is $o(\ell^n)$ and its fixed-obstacle
weights are bounded linearly in $\ell$, so its trace and derivative
are zero directly. No differential at a nonbig class is used. In the
moving setting a cofinal tail has $V_m>0$ by (5.4).

Suppose a bounded rational generated scalar $-C\leq\xi_m\leq0$
on $A_m$ has actual physical FS stage $v_m=q_{T_m}+\xi_m$, and
$f\geq v_m$ is an original model obstacle with $f(v_0)\leq0$.
All full restricted weights lie in $[-\ell C,0]$: the lower bound
uses $f\geq v_m$ and the canonical norms of auxiliary sections, and
the upper bound evaluates at $v_0$. Taking powers of the rational
weighted generators of $\xi_m$, after clearing their weights, shows
that the complete restricted stages $\sigma_{\ell,f}$ satisfy
$\xi_m\leq\sigma_{\ell,f}\leq0$ and
$\sigma_{\ell,f}\leq f-q_{T_m}$ at function-field divisorial points.
The fixed twist $c>C+1$ makes each compactified complete system
globally generated. Its full section bundle is the fixed big class
$\beta=p^*A_m+D+T_m^{\rm ver}+cF$.
Complete-base-system Fujita gives its nef moving top volumes tending
to $\vol(\beta)$. Subtracting the fiber term proves
\[
 e_m(\sigma_{\ell,f})\to\mathscr H_{A_m}(f).
 \tag{9.4}
\]

Volume convergence identifies every fixed derivative test as well.
Let $\Gamma_\ell\leq\beta$ be these complete nef models. They satisfy
$\Gamma_\ell\leq p^*L+cF$. For a fixed original model direction $H$,
choose a fixed globally nef big $\omega$ above this last class with
$\omega\pm H$ nef, on a fixed determination carrying $H$, and pull
it to the common refinements. The numerical upper bound suffices for
BFJ Corollary 3.4; the actual effective difference $\beta-\Gamma_\ell$
also gives volume order. For both signs of $t$ the independently read
Morse bound gives
\[
 \vol(\beta+tH)\geq\Gamma_\ell^{n+1}
             +(n+1)t\Gamma_\ell^nH-Kt^2.
 \tag{9.5}
\]
The same $K$ works for all $\ell$. Pass top volumes first; the positive
and negative signs bound limsup and liminf of $\Gamma_\ell^nH$.
Then let $t\to0$. BFJ differentiability gives
$\Gamma_\ell^nH\to\langle\beta^n\rangle H$.
For vertical tests this is exactly the finite restricted derivative.
Model density and its fixed raw mass therefore identify the weak limit
of its finite top measures with $\mu_{A_m,T_m;f}$. This proves the
complete two-sided derivative squeeze, without an unbounded auxiliary
operator or uniform Fujita rate across changing obstacles.

Finite concavity at $\xi_m$ and (9.4) give, with
$\sigma_m=\MA_{A_m,\rm raw}(\xi_m)$,
\[
 0\leq\mathscr H_{A_m}(f)-e_m(\xi_m)
 \leq\int\min(C,f-v_m)d\sigma_m.
 \tag{9.6}
\]
For a fixed original test $h$, ordered finite energy telescoping gives
$I\leq(n+1)$ times the ordered gap. A fixed-$C$ twist makes all
bounded auxiliary bundles nef and dominated by $p^*L+cF$.
Writing $H$ as two fixed nef tests bounds its quadratic forms in these
slots, independently of $m,f$ and refinements. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 |p_*\mu_{A_m,T_m;f}(h)-p_*\sigma_m(h)|
 \leq C_{h,C}\sqrt{\mathscr H_{A_m}(f)-e_m(\xi_m)}.
 \tag{9.7}
\]
The constant is fixed before the moving limit.
The same fixed twist bounds $\int v(S)d\sigma_m$ uniformly for
every fixed effective original divisor $S$: its vertical part is the
finite moment, its horizontal part is effective, and a fixed nef test
bounds the total intersection by a positive product of $p^*L+cF$.
Thus auxiliary collar masses are at most $C_{S,c}/H$.
Original bounded-cost outputs have the separate collar bound
$C_{S,c}/H+D/c$ from (7.6). Both collars must be kept distinct.

\begin{theorem}[Actual bounded generated quotient identification]\label{bounded}
Let $w\in\EE$ be normalized, continuous on $U$ and
$R-C\leq w\leq R$. Suppose $-C\leq\xi_m\leq0$ are rational
generated quotients on the common complete-system $A_m$, their generic
values tend to zero, their actual original FS stages $v_m$ are at most
$w$ on all lifts, and $\xi_m\to w-R$ uniformly on each compact subset
of $U$. Then $e_m(\xi_m)\to E(w)$ and $p_*\sigma_m\to M(w)$ weakly.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose fixed integer $N>C+2$ and genuine floors
$u_m=\max(v_m,R-N)\leq w$. They are actual E1 functions of bounded
normalized cost at most $NV$; their generic values $b_m\to0$ are
retained. On $U$, $0\leq w-u_m\leq N$, with uniform convergence
to zero on compact subsets. Original collars and the whole plane (8.4)
give $E(u_m)\leq E(w)\leq E(u_m)+o(1)$. Ordered energy variation
(7.5) gives $M(u_m)\to M(w)$.

At fixed $m$ take nonpositive canonical model majorants $f_j\downarrow
u_m$ with generic value $b_m$. Every original quotient weight is
nonpositive, so $\mathscr E(f_j)\leq\mathscr H_{A_m}(f_j)$.
Pass $j$ on the fixed finite support of $\sigma_m$ in (9.6):
\[
 E(u_m)\leq e_m(\xi_m)
       +C\sigma_m\{v_m<R-N\}.
 \tag{9.8}
\]
Auxiliary collars make this last mass tend to zero: on any fixed compact
subset of $U$, $v_m\to w\geq R-C>R-N$ with a fixed margin.
First fix the collar, then the moving index. The reverse bound is (5.5),
holding every finite original majorant of $w$ fixed through the degree
and moving limits before its outer infimum. Thus energies converge.

Combine (9.3), (9.7) and the same fixed-support limit to get
\[
 |M(u_m)(h)-p_*\sigma_m(h)|
 \leq(V-V_m)\|h\|+C_{h,C}\sqrt{C\sigma_m\{v_m<R-N\}}.
 \tag{9.9}
\]
Both terms vanish. The weak floor output limit identifies every model
test; density and raw mass identify all continuous tests. No original
E1 membership is assigned to the physical $v_m$.
\end{proof}

This theorem applies to maxima and rational convex combinations of any
fixed finite list of model envelopes. Tensor their actual generators
after clearing rational coefficients. Their physical horizontal factor
is exactly $T_m$ because the coefficients sum to one. Their compact
quotient limits and uniform boundedness follow from (5.3)--(5.4).
In particular it proves the exact bounded midpoint energy identification
needed for the atomic density argument, rather than only the upper estimate preceding (8.3).

\section{The genuine mixed operator and its entire energy identity}
Choose once a finite rational unisolvent set on the simplex for homogeneous
degree $n$ measure polynomials and degree $n+1$ energy polynomials.
It exists because a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on every rational
interior point; choose evaluations successively spanning the finite
dual space. At every such fixed weight Theorem~\ref{bounded} passes
the finite nef polynomial to the original diagonal $M$ and energy $E$.
Invert the fixed matrices before any moving limit. Every finite mixed
coefficient is a positive measure of raw mass $V_m$, retaining the
coefficient $b_E$ times its restriction intersections. The limits are
positive symmetric measures of mass $V$ and independent of the complete
system choice. Every fixed real simplex weight follows as well: bounded
relative order gives uniform parameter continuity of energy and its
perturbed secants, hence weak continuity of the diagonal output.

For arbitrary genuine endpoints choose independent canonical model
envelopes decreasing to them. At each fixed rational evaluation weight
their combinations decrease genuinely; (7.4) and the secant brackets at
$-t,0,t$ prove decreasing weak continuity of their diagonal outputs.
The same fixed inverse matrices give positive symmetric original mixed
measures, denoted $\MA_\theta(u_1,\ldots,u_n)$, with diagonal $M$.
Their expression using only diagonal outputs proves independence of
canonical choices and decreasing weak continuity along every genuine
finite-energy net, not just one canonical approximation. All fixed real
weights pass at the majorant limit without exchanging parameter and net.
Positive finite coefficients give
\[
 0\leq\MA_\theta(u_1,\ldots,u_n)
 \leq \frac{n^n}{n!}M\left(\frac{u_1+\cdots+u_n}{n}\right).
 \tag{10.1}
\]
Consequently every mixed measure integrates every actual relative E1
test and misses $Z$. This is the genuine operator: it is constructed
from finite actual intersection products, their bounded full-system
limits and genuine decreasing endpoints on the original carrier. It is
not assigned by an inverse and not an abstract completion.

For mixed decreasing endpoints with finite-energy limits, their average
costs are bounded on a tail. Domination (10.1) and (8.3) make the ordered
errors of any canonical test majorant uniformly small at all these mixed
outputs. At a fixed majorant its relative cost is bounded continuous on
$U$; hold an original continuous cutoff fixed in the weak limit, then

\begin{abstract}
The unchanged P14.4 construction in arbitrary big non-nef real
Bott--Chern classes is not solved here. We construct an independently
admissible cone of geometric measure data over the valuative carrier
for a separated-current sector on
$\operatorname{Bl}_{(\infty,\infty)}(\PP\times\PP)$.
Its inputs are diffuse positive measures with an explicit logarithmic
kernel condition, not complex potentials. They reconstruct positive
currents, their primitives and ordinary singularity envelopes in this
sector, with order, scaling, constants, actual decreasing approximation
and functorial pullback to every given smooth modification.
The construction retains a diffuse polar current that ideal data and
nonpluripolar products discard. We independently reconstruct the
mandatory equal-ideal/equal-product/equal-positive-mass example and
strengthen its equality to the entire tower of smooth modifications.

of a full non-Archimedean cone. No DXZ/BJ/MP operator identification,

\section{Independent geometric data on a curve}
Fix the unit Fubini--Study form $\omega$ on $\PP$ and its affine
coordinate $z$. For finite $t$ set
\[
 G(z,t)=\log\frac{|z-t|^2}{(1+|z|^2)(1+|t|^2)}\leq0,\qquad
 \ddc_zG(z,t)=\delta_t-\omega.
 \tag{2.1}
\]
The formula extends at infinity in coordinate $1/z$. Its sign follows
from $|z-t|^2\leq(1+|z|^2)(1+|t|^2)$.

\begin{definition}
In this paper an admissible measure is a finite positive Borel measure
$\nu$ on $\PP$, with no atoms, whose support $S$ is a compact subset
of the finite affine chart, and which satisfies
\[
 \int_\PP G(x,t)\,d\nu(t)=-\infty\quad\text{for every }x\in S.
 \tag{2.2}
\]
The zero measure is admissible, with empty support.
\end{definition}
This is a condition on a measure and a fixed geometric kernel.
It presupposes neither a complex psh input nor a $P$-envelope.
It is restrictive: arbitrary polar measures or arbitrary singularities
are not asserted to satisfy it. Positive sums and positive scalar
multiples are admissible. Indeed their supports are the finite union
of the incoming supports, and the nonpositive kernel makes an infinite
negative contribution at every point of that union.

\begin{lemma}\label{curvelemma}
For an admissible $\nu$ of mass $m$, the integral
$U_\nu(z)=\int G(z,t)d\nu(t)$ is nonpositive, locally integrable,
has the canonical quasi-psh representative, and
$\ddc U_\nu=\nu-m\omega$. It is smooth and finite off $S$,
and its pole set is precisely $S$. For every $h>0$,
$e^{-hU_\nu}$ is locally integrable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The local $L^1$ norms of logarithmic kernels with $t$ in a compact
set are uniformly bounded, by translating their singularities into
a fixed larger disc. Fubini proves integrability and the Hessian
identity. Fatou applied to $-G$ proves upper semicontinuity.
Integrating the submean inequalities for $\log|z-t|^2$ proves the
canonical local subharmonic condition after adding $m\log(1+|z|^2)$.
Away from the support all kernel derivatives are locally uniformly
bounded. Condition (2.2) identifies the poles. The function is finite
near infinity, hence nontrivial. In particular $S$ is polar and has
area zero, since it is contained in the infinite locus of a locally
integrable function.

At a point $z_0\in S$, atomlessness lets us choose a disc $B(z_0,r)$
with mass $\delta<1/h$. On the smaller disc $B(z_0,r/2)$ the
complementary potential and spherical terms are bounded.
If $\delta>0$, Jensen for the normalized restricted measure gives
\[
 \exp\left(-h\int_{B(z_0,r)}\log|z-t|^2\,d\nu(t)\right)
 \leq\frac1\delta\int_{B(z_0,r)}|z-t|^{-2h\delta}\,d\nu(t).
 \tag{2.3}
\]
Truncating the logarithm first justifies the extended-value formula.
The integrals of the right side on the smaller disc are uniformly
bounded by $\int_{|w|<2r}|w|^{-2h\delta}dA(w)<\infty$.
Fubini proves the assertion. The case $\delta=0$ and points off
the support are immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Pullback integrability without a birational grant]\label{pulllemma}
If $f$ is a nonconstant holomorphic map from a connected complex
manifold to $\PP$, then $U_\nu\circ f$ has its canonical quasi-psh
pullback representative and $e^{-hU_\nu\circ f}$ is locally
integrable for every $h>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Near a point with image outside $S$, including infinity, this is
smooth. At a point with image $t_0\in S$, choose a local complex
line direction in which $f(z,0)-t_0$ has a finite order $d\geq1$.
Such a direction exists by the first nonzero homogeneous term of
the holomorphic Taylor series. Choose a small circle $|z|=R$
on which this function has no zero, with all its $d$ zeros
strictly inside $R/2$. For nearby other coordinates $w$ and $t$,
the argument principle, or its homotopy proof, gives exactly $d$
zeros $r_i(w,t)$ inside $R/2$.
Factoring them, including multiplicities, gives
\[
 f(z,w)-t=u(z,w,t)\prod_{i=1}^d(z-r_i(w,t)).
 \tag{2.4}
\]
For each fixed $w,t$, $u$ is holomorphic and zero-free on the disc.
On the boundary its modulus is uniformly bounded below: the numerator
is bounded below and the product is bounded above.
The maximum principle for $1/u$ gives the same lower bound inside.
This argument does not require holomorphic choices of the roots.
H\"older therefore gives a uniform integral bound on a smaller disc
for $|f(z,w)-t|^{-2\epsilon}$ whenever $d\epsilon<1$, using
the $d$ factors and the bounds for
$\int|z-r|^{-2d\epsilon}dA(z)$.
Integrating over $w$ gives the corresponding local bound.

Choose the restricted measure near $t_0$ to have
$\delta<1/(hd)$, possible by atomlessness. Jensen as in (2.3),
now with $z$ replaced by $f(z,w)$, and the uniform bound just proved
give the required exponential integrability.
The complementary part is bounded after shrinking the image.
The same factorization bounds local logarithmic $L^1$ norms
uniformly in $t$, so Fubini defines the pullback primitive.
Alternatively its psh local primitives are compositions of psh
functions with $f$, verified by their restriction to holomorphic
discs and the submean inequality. They are nontrivial: a nonconstant
holomorphic scalar has open local image, whereas $S$ has no interior.
These arguments also establish the canonical representative.
\end{proof}

\section{An enriched carrier and a geometric sector cone}
Let $Y=\PP_z\times\PP_w$ and
$\pi:X=\operatorname{Bl}_{p}Y\to Y$, $p=(\infty,\infty)$,
with exceptional curve $E$. Put
$q=\operatorname{pr}_z\circ\pi$, $r=\operatorname{pr}_w\circ\pi$.
Let $h_E$ be a smooth metric, $\rho=c_1(\mathcal O(E),h_E)$,
$e=\log|\sigma_E|_{h_E}^2\leq0$, so $\ddc e=[E]-\rho$.
Define $H_z=\{q^*\omega_z\}$ and $H_w=\{r^*\omega_w\}$.

Let $\mathscr V_X$ be the centered coherent-ideal semivaluation
carrier appearing in the question, retaining supports and
$+\infty$. We use only this specified point set and evaluations
$v(\mathcal I_E)$, not a theorem identifying a topology or a
DXZ image. Let $v_0$ be the generic trivial semivaluation.
The precise enriched carrier used here is
\[
 \mathscr C_X=\mathscr V_X\times\PP(\mathbf C),\qquad
 \operatorname{pr}_1:\mathscr C_X\longrightarrow\mathscr V_X.
 \tag{3.1}
\]
The additional geometric datum is the positive measure $\nu$ on the
slice $\{v_0\}\times\PP$, or equivalently its positive functional
$F_\nu(g)=\int g\,d\nu$ on $C(\PP)$.
Using all continuous tests determines a finite Borel measure:
for a compact metric space, continuous cutoffs approximate indicators
of closed sets, and then regularity determines Borel sets.
No potential on $X$ is part of the input.
This deliberately adds complex geometry; it is not a claim that the
MP spectrum alone contains that geometry.

Define $\mathscr D$ by the independently checkable inputs
\[
 D=(a,b,c,\nu,\kappa),\quad a,b,c\geq0,\quad
 m:=\nu(\PP)\leq b,\quad \nu\text{ admissible},\quad\kappa\in\R.
 \tag{3.2}
\]
Addition adds all five entries, positive scaling scales all entries,
and the zero element has $\nu=0$.
For the big non-nef sector we require $a,b,c>0$.
The geometric output, not an input, is
\[
 \begin{split}
 \alpha_D&=bH_z+cH_w+a\{E\},\\
 \theta_D&=bq^*\omega_z+cr^*\omega_w+a\rho,\\
 \Phi_D&=ae+U_\nu\circ q+\kappa,\\
 T_D&=a[E]+(b-m)q^*\omega_z+cr^*\omega_w+q^*\nu.
 \end{split}\tag{3.3}
\]
Here $q^*\nu$ is defined by the local psh primitives of
Lemma~\ref{pulllemma}: $q^*\nu=\ddc(U_\nu\circ q)+m q^*\omega_z$.
It is positive, not an unjustified pullback of a distribution.

\begin{theorem}[Sector realization]\label{sectorthm}
The data (3.2) form a convex geometric cone with a constants action.
For $a,b,c>0$, $\alpha_D$ is big and non-nef and
$\Phi_D\in\PSH(X,\theta_D)$.
On a fixed $(a,b,c)$ leaf, the exact order is the kernel-test order
\[
 D\leq D'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 U_\nu(z)+\kappa\leq U_{\nu'}(z)+\kappa'
 \quad\text{for every }z\in\PP.
 \tag{3.4}
\]
It is equivalent to $\Phi_D\leq\Phi_{D'}$ on $X$.
The state determines its ordinary $P$-envelope by (1.1);
this does not characterize the image of that envelope operator.
Addition and positive scaling of data give the corresponding sums
and scalings of actual currents and primitives.
The measure is reconstructible from $T_D$ and the fixed geometric
parameters, independently of $\kappa$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The cone assertions follow from the sign of $G$ and support unions.
Lemma~\ref{curvelemma} and the local psh pullback prove (3.3).
All coefficients of the displayed positive current are nonnegative.

For the geometry, let $H=H_z+H_w$.
The class $A=2H-\{E\}$ is ample: the Segre embedding
$Y\hookrightarrow\mathbf P^3$ and the three hyperplanes through $p$
embed its blowup as the graph in $Y\times\mathbf P^2$.
The hyperplane pullbacks are $H$ and $H-\{E\}$, and their
Segre sum is $A$. Locally the three hyperplanes generate the
ideal of $p$, since their differentials generate its cotangent space;
outside $p$ they have no common zero. This verifies the graph
description by the local Rees algebra of that ideal.
For $t=\min(b,c)>0$,
\[
 \alpha_D=\frac t2 A+(b-t)H_z+(c-t)H_w+
                 (a+t/2)\{E\}.
 \tag{3.5}
\]
A K\"ahler representative of the first term, the semipositive
pullback forms, and the effective exceptional current give a
K\"ahler current in $\alpha_D$.
The exceptional normal bundle is $\mathcal O_{\PP}(-1)$, hence
$E^2=-1$, while $H_zE=H_wE=0$; thus $\alpha_DE=-a<0$.
Nef classes have nonnegative degrees on curves by their
K\"ahler approximants, so this class is non-nef.

Off $E$ the common term $ae$ is finite, and $q$ has points off $E$
above every point of $\PP$. This proves both directions in (3.4);
on $E$ both primitives are $-\infty$ when $a>0$.
If $a=0$, the term $ae$ is defined as zero, not $0(-\infty)$.
Linearity of kernel integrals proves the operation assertions
at finite points and with literal negative infinities at poles.
For zero scaling the primitive is zero, by convention.

On the product away from $p$, integrate $T_D-a[E]$ against
the unit form in the $w$ direction. The resulting measure in $z$
is $(b-m)\omega_z+\nu$.
Its singular part relative to area is exactly $\nu$, since the
support of $\nu$ has area zero. The computation is legitimate
on neighborhoods of $S\times\PP$, which avoid $p$.
Thus the geometric current recovers $\nu$.
In particular, on a fixed leaf different measures cannot give
the same current. The constants coordinate fixes the explicitly
reconstructed primitive, not merely its current.
\end{proof}

\section{All given smooth modifications and reference covariance}
\begin{theorem}\label{modthm}
For every given smooth connected proper bimeromorphic
$p:W\to X$ and every $D\in\mathscr D$ with $a,b,c>0$,
the actual pullback primitive is $\Phi_{D,W}=\Phi_D\circ p$ and
\[
 T_{D,W}=a[p^*E]+(b-m)(q\circ p)^*\omega_z+
 c(r\circ p)^*\omega_w+(q\circ p)^*\nu.
 \tag{4.1}
\]
These data are compatible with further smooth modifications.
No existence or projectivity of a resolution is needed for this
assertion: it quantifies over each modification already given.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The total pullback $p^*E$ is the zero divisor of the actual pulled
section. In local frames, $\ddc\log|p^*\sigma_E|^2=[p^*E]$
with its full multiplicities. Lemma~\ref{pulllemma} applies to
$q\circ p$, a nonconstant holomorphic map. Adding the pulled
smooth local potentials proves the canonical psh condition and
(4.1). For a second modification composition of functions,
pulled sections and smooth forms is literal.
The definition of the polar current by those same local primitives
then proves composition compatibility. There is no subtraction of
two infinite values and no replacement of $p^*E$ by its strict transform.
\end{proof}

At the point-set carrier level the compatible map is
$\mathscr C_W\to\mathscr C_X$, $(v,z)\mapsto(p_*v,z)$, where
$(p_*v)(\mathfrak a)=v(\mathfrak a\mathcal O_W)$.
Pullback preserves ideal sums, products and units, so this is a
centered semivaluation with its support retained by contraction.
It composes under further modifications, sends the generic trivial
point to the generic trivial point, and keeps the geometric measure
on that slice unchanged. Moreover
$v(\mathcal I_{p^*E})=(p_*v)(\mathcal I_E)$.
This is not a claim about a new identification of MP topologies.

The data are actual geometric data, so changing a smooth representative
by $\theta'=\theta+\ddc f$, $f$ smooth, transports the primitive
to $\Phi'=\Phi-f$ and keeps $T$ unchanged.
Changing the exceptional metric is a special case.
All multiplier ideals remain unchanged by this bounded perturbation.
For a transported reference obstacle $h-f$, rooftop competitors
give the exact formula
\[
 P_{\theta'}(\Phi-f+C,h-f)=P_\theta(\Phi+C,h)-f.
 \tag{4.2}
\]
Limits and upper semicontinuous regularization commute with the
smooth subtraction. Thus the singularity-envelope construction is
covariant with simultaneous transport of the reference.
Resetting the obstacle to zero after changing $\theta$ is a different
reference choice, not the equality in (4.2).
The cone was defined using fixed geometric forms and a fibration;
we do not assert independence of those actual geometric data,
or perturbation independence of a full real-class NA cone.
On this fixed sector no auxiliary ample perturbation enters (3.2)--(4.1).

\section{Projection, products, mass, and an actual approximation}
\begin{theorem}\label{shadowthm}
For $D$ in the big non-nef sector and $t>0$,
\[
 \cI(t\Phi_D)=\mathcal O_X(-\lfloor ta\rfloor E),\qquad
 Q_D(v):=-\lim_{\ell\to\infty}
   \ell^{-1}v(\cI(\ell\Phi_D))=-a\,v(\mathcal I_E).
 \tag{5.1}
\]
The formula includes supported semivaluations. The normalized
ideal shadow has $Q_D(v_0)=0$ and forgets $\nu$ and $\kappa$.
For any smooth modification in Theorem~\ref{modthm},
\[
 \cI(t\Phi_{D,W})=\cI(ta\,e\circ p).
 \tag{5.2}
\]
If $p^*E$ is simple normal crossing, the latter is
$\mathcal O_W(-\lfloor ta\,p^*E\rfloor)$, componentwise.
No such formula is asserted for a non-normal-crossing divisor.

With $S_D=(b-m)q^*\omega_z+cr^*\omega_w$, for $k=1,2$,
\[
 \langle T_D^k\rangle=S_D^k,\quad
 \langle T_{D,W}^k\rangle=(p^*S_D)^k,\quad
 M_{\theta_D}(\Phi_D)=2c(b-m).
 \tag{5.3}
\]
The modification has that same top raw mass.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Near $E$, $q$ is near infinity and $U_\nu\circ q$ is smooth.
For a defining coordinate $\zeta$, $\Phi_D=a\log|\zeta|^2+O(1)$.
A monomial $\zeta^j$ is integrable exactly when $j+1>ta$.
The least integer is $\lfloor ta\rfloor$, including integral $ta$.
For arbitrary holomorphic germs expand in $\zeta$: a first nonzero
coefficient below that degree is bounded below on an open subset
of the other coordinate, where the radial integral diverges.
Away from $E$, exponential integrability in
Lemma~\ref{curvelemma}, and Fubini, give the unit ideal.
This proves the sheaf equality.
For $w=v(\mathcal I_E)<\infty$, the ideal value is
$\lfloor\ell a\rfloor w$. For $w=\infty$, it is infinity for
all sufficiently large $\ell$. The unit ideal has value zero,
so no $0\cdot\infty$ is evaluated. This proves (5.1) on every
specified semivaluation, not by divisorial density.

On $W$, $p^{-1}E$ maps under $q\circ p$ to infinity.
The polar term is therefore bounded near that divisor.
Off it, Lemma~\ref{pulllemma} makes all exponentials of the
polar term locally integrable, and the exceptional term is smooth.
This proves (5.2) locally everywhere. In an SNC chart the
logarithmic divisor is a sum of coordinate logarithms, and
separate radial integrals give the componentwise floor formula.

The closed pole set on $W$ is
$Z_W=p^{-1}E\cup(q\circ p)^{-1}S$.
It is pluripolar by the actual quasi-psh primitive (when $\nu=0$
only the first set is required).
Off it the current is the smooth form $p^*S_D$.
BEGZ locality and noncharging give (5.3) if the smooth forms
have no coefficient mass on $Z_W$.
For the analytic divisor this is immediate from area zero.
For $(q\circ p)^{-1}S$, outside the critical locus use a
holomorphic submersion coordinate and Fubini; $S$ has area zero.
The critical locus has area zero: some derivative of the
nonconstant holomorphic scalar is not identically zero, so this
locus is contained in the zero set of a nonzero holomorphic function.
Such a zero set has area zero by slicing and the one-variable
isolated-zero theorem. This proves the assertion also at critical points.

The forms from either curve have square zero, so
$S_D^2=2c(b-m)\pi^*(\omega_z\wedge\omega_w)$.
The last volume form has integral one, by the isomorphism off
$E$ and $p$. A bimeromorphic map between smooth surfaces is
an isomorphism off proper analytic subsets; changing variables
there shows $\int_W(p^*S_D)^2=\int_XS_D^2$.
This proves both raw mass assertions. BEGZ applies on $W$:
one may use its local definition and finite mass directly here,
so no theorem that an arbitrary smooth modification is K\"ahler
is needed. The smooth extension calculation already proves
well-definedness and locality suffices.
\end{proof}

For fixed $a,b,c$ the ideal-shadow projection is constant, so it
preserves order trivially and cannot reflect it. Positive scaling
and addition descend to its asymptotic profiles $Q$, with the
literal extended-value conventions above. Exact multiplier ideals
do not have an additive floor law:
$\lfloor t(a+a')\rfloor$ need not be
$\lfloor ta\rfloor+\lfloor ta'\rfloor$.
Constants act trivially on ideals; the separate coordinate
$\kappa$ acts on the primitive by addition. There is no assertion
that $\kappa$ is the endpoint of a compatible DXZ test curve.
Neither the raw mass (5.3), the polar measure, nor the ordinary
$P$-envelope descends to the ideal shadow.

\begin{proposition}[Replacement topology and decreasing approximation]\label{approx}
For every fixed $D$, set
\[
 \nu_j=(1-1/j)\nu,\quad
 D_j=(a,b,c,\nu_j,\kappa),\qquad j\geq2.
 \tag{5.4}
\]
These are actual admissible states in the same class,
$\Phi_{D_j}\downarrow\Phi_D$ everywhere, and their raw products
and masses converge to (5.3). More generally, let
$D_j=(a_j,b_j,c_j,\nu_j,\kappa_j)$ and
$D=(a,b,c,\nu,\kappa)$ be admissible states whose measures have
support in a common compact affine set. If
\[
 (a_j,b_j,c_j,\kappa_j)\longrightarrow(a,b,c,\kappa),
 \qquad \|\nu_j-\nu\|_{\TV}\longrightarrow0,
\]
then their reconstructed primitives converge locally in $L^1$
on $X$ and every fixed smooth modification. This gives a product
topology on each fixed compact-support admissible sector;
no formal completion is used. The hypothesis on $a_j,\kappa_j$
is essential for a statement about actual primitives.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$G\leq0$ gives the pointwise decrease, including at all poles.
The measure error is $m/j$ in total variation. The smooth products
depend polynomially on $b-m_j,c$, where $m_j=(1-1/j)m$;
this proves product and mass convergence without a general
Monge--Amp\`ere continuity assertion.
Uniform local $L^1$ kernel bounds give
$\|U_\nu-U_{\nu'}\|_{L^1(K)}\leq C_K\|\nu-\nu'\|_{\TV}$
on the curve. On a fixed modification $p:W\to X$, the uniform
logarithmic bounds from Lemma~\ref{pulllemma} and a finite chart
cover give the corresponding estimate for the composed kernels.
Fix a compact coordinate region $K_0\Subset W$ and a smooth
volume form there. The divisor logarithm $e\circ p$ is locally
integrable: locally it is the logarithm of the squared norm of a
nonzero holomorphic defining function, plus a smooth function.
The exact primitive formula (3.3) therefore yields
\[
\begin{split}
 \|\Phi_{D_j}\circ p-\Phi_D\circ p\|_{L^1(K_0)}
 &\leq |a_j-a|\,\|e\circ p\|_{L^1(K_0)}\\
 &\quad+C_{K_0}\|\nu_j-\nu\|_{\TV}
       +|\kappa_j-\kappa|\,\operatorname{Vol}(K_0).
\end{split}
\]
Differences are taken almost everywhere; common pole sets have
volume zero. The three terms tend to zero under the stated
hypotheses. The identity modification proves the assertion on $X$.
The parameters $b,c$ determine the smooth background form and
do not occur in this primitive formula; their stated convergence
also transports that form continuously. No density of finite
ideals or ordinary PL models is asserted. Admissibility is not
asserted to be closed in arbitrary weak measure limits.

For clarity, convergence of $\nu_j$ alone is insufficient.
The admissible states $(1,1,1,0,\kappa_j)$, with $\kappa_j=0$
for even $j$ and $-1$ for odd $j$, have identical zero measures
in one big non-nef class. On a positive-volume compact region
outside $E$ their primitives alternate between $e$ and $e-1$,
so no $L^1$ limit exists. Actual primitives are not being
identified modulo constants.
\end{proof}

At positive $\lambda$, competitor scaling gives
$P_{\lambda\theta}[\lambda u]=\lambda P_\theta[u]$;
constant shifts of $u$ leave $P_\theta[u]$ unchanged by reindexing
$C$. Rooftop comparison proves monotonicity of this envelope
map. The current and raw mass scale by $\lambda$ and $\lambda^2$,
respectively. These are sector statements about actual complex
currents and ordinary envelopes.

\section{Explicit measures and separation on the entire birational tower}
Set $\ell_0=1$, $\ell_j=e^{-4^j}$ for $j\geq1$.
At every level retain the two closed intervals of the next length
at the endpoints of every retained interval, starting from $[0,1]$.
The inequality $2\ell_{j+1}<\ell_j$ makes the children disjoint.
The intersection is a compact set $C$.
The Bernoulli measure $\mu_0$ assigns each level-$j$ interval
mass $2^{-j}$; its singletons have mass at most $2^{-j}$ for
every $j$, so it is atomless. Let $D=C+2$ and $\mu_1$ be the
translated probability.
For $x$ in its level-$j$ interval,
\[
 U_{\mu_0}(x)\leq 2^{-j}\,2\log\ell_j=-2\,2^j\longrightarrow-\infty.
 \tag{6.1}
\]
This uses $G\leq0$ on the complement and denominators at least one.
The same proof holds on $D$. Thus both measures are admissible,
their supports are disjoint, and all curve and pullback
integrability assertions above apply.

\begin{theorem}[Mandatory test, strengthened]\label{mandatory}
Fix $a,b,c>0$ and $0<s<b$. In the fixed class
$\alpha=bH_z+cH_w+a\{E\}$ use the states
$D_i=(a,b,c,s\mu_i,0)$, $i=0,1$.
Their complete real-scale multiplier-ideal families coincide on
$X$ and on every given smooth modification $W\to X$.
Their entire first and second nonpluripolar currents coincide
on every such $W$, and their positive raw mass is
$2c(b-s)$.
Nevertheless their normalized ordinary $P$-envelopes on $X$ differ.
The enriched measure data distinguish them.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The equalities are (5.1)--(5.3), since the parameters and masses
of the two measures agree. The measures themselves differ,
as they have disjoint supports and positive mass.

The maximum of $U_{\mu_0}$ and $U_{\mu_1}$ is bounded:
near either compact support the other function is smooth and
bounded below, and on the remaining compact set both are smooth.
Thus
$W_0=\max(\Phi_{D_0},\Phi_{D_1})$
differs by a bounded function from $ae$.
It is $\theta$-psh by the local psh maximum rule.
Mass monotonicity (1.2) in both directions, and the $\nu=0$
case of (5.3), give $M_\theta(W_0)=2bc$.
Both inputs are nonpositive and have strictly positive mass.
If their ordinary envelopes were a common $P$, the DDL membership
assertion would give $\Phi_{D_i}\leq P$ and
$M_\theta(P)=2c(b-s)$.
Then $W_0\leq P$ and (1.2) gives
$2bc\leq2c(b-s)<2bc$, a contradiction.
Every DDL hypothesis is now checked: $X$ is compact K\"ahler,
the class is big, all competitors are $\theta$-psh,
and the two inputs have positive mass. No zero-mass envelope
assertion and no commutation of envelopes with maxima was used.
\end{proof}

In particular, $b=c=1$, $a=s=1/2$ gives the precise mandatory
class $\alpha=H+\frac12E$ with mass one,
$\cI(t\Phi_i)=\mathcal O_X(-\lfloor t/2\rfloor E)$,
first product $\frac12q^*\omega_z+r^*\omega_w$, and top measure
$\pi^*(\omega_z\wedge\omega_w)$.
The ordinary self-intersection is $2-a^2=7/4$, not the raw mass.
The strengthened conclusion concerns only the specified birational
ideal/product tower. It does not assert equality of every conceivable
movable datum, and is not a negative answer to original P14.4.
The full polar currents $(q\circ p)^*\nu$, retained here,
already escape this equality.

\section{The actual moving system permitted by B}\label{Bboundary}
For rational $a,b,c>0$, let
$L=\pi^*\mathcal O_Y(b,c)+aE$ on the complex projective surface,
viewed over the trivially valued field $\mathbf C$.
For cleared degrees $\ell$, every section of $\ell L$ restricts
to a line bundle of negative degree $-\ell a$ on $E$, so vanishes
there. Repeating this argument after each division gives
\[
 H^0(X,\ell L)=\sigma_E^{\ell a}
      H^0(X,\pi^*\mathcal O_Y(\ell b,\ell c)).
 \tag{7.1}
\]
The remaining space descends to $Y$.
One elementary justification is to view it as rational sections:
a pole downstairs along a prime divisor would remain a pole
upstairs, and a holomorphic section on $Y\setminus\{p\}$ extends
across the smooth surface point by the local Hartogs expansion.
The remaining bundle is generated since both degrees are positive.
Thus the actual full moving pair is
$A=\pi^*\mathcal O_Y(b,c)$, $T=aE$.
This is an actual positive part, not a relatively nef lift of $L$.

section system has the genuine all-point scalar
$q_T(v)=-a\,v(\mathcal I_E)$.

a unit, since the system is generated; the equality includes
supported points. Every normalized FS scalar on the fixed $L$

The remaining canonical section norms are at most one, and the
incoming generic constants tend to zero. At each fixed divisorial

 A^2=2bc,\qquad C^3=3d(2bc),\qquad
 e_A(0)=C^3/3-dA^2=0.
 \tag{7.2}
\]
Its sole central component has multiplicity one, valuation $v_0$,

\section{Precise unsolved requirements and evidence}
This construction is more than appending an original complex potential:
its admissibility is (2.2), its cone is (3.2), its geometric state is
a positive measure functional, and reconstruction and modification
compatibility have been proved. It is nonetheless only a sector
construction with prescribed fibration and geometric forms.
Its real coefficients lie in the real N\'eron--Severi space of
this projective surface; irrational coefficients do not establish
a genuinely transcendental case.
It is not the independently characterized full NA positivity theory
asked for in unchanged P14.4.

There is even a concrete missing cone operation in this sector:
the maximum of the two reconstructed curve weights in
Theorem~\ref{mandatory} is bounded and nonconstant.
It cannot be $U_\nu+\kappa$ for an admissible nonzero $\nu$,
which has poles, or for zero $\nu$, which is constant.
To see nonconstancy, near a support point one weight is very negative
while the other is smooth; on a nonempty ordinary open set the
winner has Hessian $-\omega$, hence cannot be constant.
Thus the sector is not closed under maxima in a fixed class.
Its decreasing approximation is (5.4), not a decreasing PL or
moving-model approximation theorem.

The following requirements remain unresolved here:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An intrinsic admissible geometric cone for every compact
K\"ahler manifold and every big non-nef real Bott--Chern class,
including genuinely transcendental and nonprojective classes.
\item Reconstruction of arbitrary prescribed ordinary $P$-types,
independent of fibration and kernel choices, with the requisite
maxima/envelopes and full approximation theory.
\item A projection from such states to compatible DXZ objects or
the actual MP potential cone, with all-point comparison and the
required endpoint and perturbation conventions.
\item Positive-volume operator comparison with DXZ/BJ/MP in
genuine common domains, retaining raw singularity-dependent mass
and the correct reference. No energy extension is attempted here.
\end{enumerate}

filtration maximality from the catalogue are not relisted as open;
