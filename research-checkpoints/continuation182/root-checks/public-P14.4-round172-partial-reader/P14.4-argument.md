## 2. Independent curve admissibility

On P¹ with unit Fubini–Study form ω set

    G(z,t)=log(|z−t|²/((1+|z|²)(1+|t|²))),
    dd^c_z G(z,t)=δ_t−ω,     G≤0.

The sign follows from (1+|z|²)(1+|t|²)−|z−t|²=|1+z conjugate(t)|². The formula extends at infinity.

An admissible measure ν is finite, positive, atomless, supported on a compact affine set S, and satisfies ∫G(x,t)dν(t)=−∞ at every x∈S. Zero is allowed with empty support. This is a condition on measures, not on a preexisting complex potential. Finite positive sums and scalings preserve it: supports unite and the nonpositive kernel preserves each infinite negative contribution.

Set m=ν(P¹), U_ν=∫G(z,t)dν(t). Translating logarithmic singularities into a fixed larger disc gives uniform local L¹ bounds. Fubini yields integrability and dd^c U_ν=ν−mω. Fatou for −G gives upper semicontinuity; integrated submean inequalities give the canonical representative. All derivatives are locally uniformly bounded off S, so U_ν is finite and smooth there, including infinity. Its poles are exactly S, by admissibility. Hence S is polar and area-null.

For every h>0, exp(−hU_ν) is locally integrable. At z_0∈S choose a disc of measure δ<1/h by atomlessness. Its complementary potential is bounded on the half-disc. Jensen for the normalized restricted measure gives

    exp(−h∫ log|z−t|²dν(t))
       ≤δ⁻¹∫|z−t|^(−2hδ)dν(t).

Truncate the logarithm first to justify extended values. Integrating in z is bounded by ∫_{|w|<2r}|w|^(−2hδ)dA(w), finite because hδ<1. If δ=0 the term is bounded.

## 3. Pullback integrability, proved locally

For every nonconstant holomorphic map f to P¹, exp(−hU_ν∘f) is locally integrable for every h>0. Outside S this is smooth. At an image t_0∈S, choose a complex line direction where f(z,0)−t_0 has finite order d≥1, using the first nonzero Taylor homogeneous term. Choose a small circle without boundary zeros. Under nearby other coordinates w and t, homotopy in the argument principle keeps exactly d zeros inside the half-disc. Factoring them gives

    f(z,w)−t=u(z,w,t) ∏_{i=1}^d(z−r_i(w,t)).

The zero-free unit is bounded below uniformly: the numerator is bounded below and the polynomial bounded above on the circle; apply the maximum principle to 1/u. Holomorphic choices of roots are unnecessary. Hölder with d factors bounds the integral of |f−t|^(−2ε) uniformly when dε<1, using the integrals of |z−r_i|^(−2dε). Fubini integrates the remaining coordinates.

Choose the restricted ν-mass δ<1/(hd) near t_0 and apply Jensen to log|f−t|². The complementary term is bounded after shrinking the image. This proves the assertion. The factorization also supplies uniform local logarithmic L¹ bounds, justifying the pulled integral.

The local psh primitives compose with f; their restriction to holomorphic discs verifies the submean condition. They are nontrivial because a nonconstant holomorphic scalar has open local image whereas S has no interior. Thus they have their canonical psh representatives. No projective resolution or pullback of an arbitrary distribution was assumed.
