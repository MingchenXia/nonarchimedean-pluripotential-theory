# P03.1: a noninvariant toric envelope argument, with explicit foundations

**Status: partial; not a solution of the original classification.** The new argument is a full-family upper bound, rather than an assertion about only invariant competitors. Its proved content is the conditional theorem below. The curve-calculus assumptions are stated explicitly because the primary potential-theory paper could not be retrieved. In particular, no claim from the supplied unpublished book, audit, hash, or previous draft is used as a proof.

The domain is a split analytic torus over a complete nontrivially valued field, with an ordinary bounded open convex tropical domain. The obstacle is tropical and has a finite convex minorant. Competitors in the upper-bound argument need not be invariant. The argument uses analytic extensions at essential steps. It does not replace scalar-stable positivity with ground-field curve tests.

## 1. Conventions and foundational gates

Let \(K\) be a complete nontrivially valued non-Archimedean field and let \(n\geq 1\). Write
\[
T_K=(\mathbf G_{m,K}^n)^{\mathrm{an}},\qquad
\tau(x)=(\log|Z_1(x)|,\ldots,\log|Z_n(x)|).
\]
This is the positive-log convention; changing signs everywhere gives the usual alternative tropical convention. Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbf R^n\) be nonempty, bounded, open, and convex, and put \(U_K=\tau^{-1}(\Omega)\). This is an ordinary open domain in a smooth torus. The theorem makes no assertion for a target domain with relative boundary. All its statements may be restricted to a good separated strict such domain, as in the original problem.

For \(t\in\mathbf R^n\), let \(\kappa_K(t)\) be the Gauss point given on Laurent polynomials by
\[
\left|\sum_{m\in\mathbf Z^n}c_m Z^m\right|_{\kappa_K(t)}
=\max_m |c_m|\exp\langle m,t\rangle.
\tag{1}
\]
Only finitely many coefficients occur in (1). The weighted Gauss norm is multiplicative: the initial forms of two nonzero Laurent polynomials have nonzero product in a Laurent polynomial ring over the graded residue field, which is an integral domain. Thus (1) defines a point of the torus. It has \(\tau(\kappa_K(t))=t\). The map \(\kappa_K\) is continuous: evaluation of the norm of every Laurent polynomial is a finite maximum of continuous functions; this is precisely the evaluation topology of algebraic analytification. The Laurent-polynomial identities below therefore suffice to identify torus points.

For any complete analytic extension \(E/K\), write \(\pi_{E/K}:U_E\to U_K\) for scalar projection. A *scalar-curve-positive* function here means a continuous finite function whose pullback along every curve map used in the classical curve definition, over every such \(E\), is subharmonic. This is the intended scalar-stable interpretation, not the weaker ground-field-only interpretation. The exact identification with the primary source's definition is a foundational gate, not inferred from its abstract.

The formal theorem uses the following explicit curve-calculus foundations.

**F1 (annular comparison).** If \(h\) is scalar-curve-positive, its pullback to any smooth strict open annulus over any complete analytic extension is subharmonic. On a closed strict annulus \(r\leq |u|\leq R\) contained in that open annulus, comparison against any harmonic function \(A+B\log|u|\) is valid using the two Gauss endpoints as boundary. On the unit-circle affinoid \(|u|=1\), comparison against a constant is valid using its one Gauss boundary point.

The boundary description is elementary. Laurent series on a closed annulus have spectral norm equal to the maximum of their endpoint Gauss norms. On \(|u|=1\), their spectral norm is the one Gauss norm. At non-Gauss points of either boundary-radius fiber, a branch neighborhood remains at the same modulus, so these points are ordinary interior points of the closed domain. The ordinary boundary in an open annulus consists of the indicated Gauss endpoints. Also \(A+B\log|u|\) is affine on the annular skeleton, with opposite slopes in the two directions, and constant on its disc branches. F1 is consequently the usual harmonic-comparison definition specialized to these domains. This specialization has not been checked against the unavailable exact primary definition.

**F2 (units and local curve comparison).** On every test curve, over every complete analytic extension, \(\log|g|\) is harmonic whenever \(g\) is an invertible analytic function. Real linear combinations and constants remain harmonic. Subharmonicity is local and is defined by harmonic comparison on admissible compact curve domains; harmonic functions obey that comparison principle. If the chosen classical definition includes singular test curves, F2 is required on those curves too; no unproved reduction from singular to smooth tests is inserted.

For clarity, two closure facts used below follow directly from this comparison calculus. A finite maximum \(v=\max_i H_i\) of harmonic functions is subharmonic: boundary domination \(v\leq H\) implies boundary domination of each \(H_i-H\), hence domination throughout the domain. A locally uniform limit of finite continuous subharmonic functions is subharmonic: if \(v\leq H\) on a compact comparison boundary, uniform error \(\epsilon\) implies \(v_j\leq H+\epsilon\) there; comparison and the limit give \(v\leq H+2\epsilon\) inside, and then \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). These are deductions from F2, not extra citations. The underlying unit-harmonic theorem and the applicability of this calculus to the exact original definition remain source-verification gaps.

**F3 (LFS presentation).** The LFS class in the conditional application has the conventional local presentation
\[
h=\frac1m\max_i\bigl(\lambda_i+\log|g_i|\bigr),
\quad m\in\mathbf Z_{>0},\quad\lambda_i\in\mathbf R,
\tag{2}
\]
where the \(g_i\) are analytic with no common zero. A local invertible denominator in a trivialization can be absorbed into the \(g_i\). In particular, single monomials with rational exponent vector in the function and arbitrary real additive constant, and their finite maxima with a common denominator, belong to the class. The theorem does not silently identify a differently defined LFS class with (2).

No exact published theorem asserting F2, F3, or the inclusion of every semipositive real-PL function in this scalar-curve-positive class was obtained in this run. They are explicit gates in the application. An abstract or a dossier statement is not used to fill them.

## 2. Scalar annuli dominate arbitrary competitors by Gauss values

**Lemma 1.** Assume F1. For every continuous finite scalar-curve-positive \(h\) on \(U_K\),
\[
h(x)\leq h\bigl(\kappa_K(\tau(x))\bigr)
\quad(x\in U_K).
\tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** Fix \(x\) and put \(t=\tau(x)\). Take \(E=\mathcal H(x)\), the completed residue field, and let \(a_i\in E^\times\) be the image of \(Z_i\); then \(|a_i|=e^{t_i}\). The \(E\)-rational point with coordinates \(a_i\) projects to \(x\).

Choose \(\epsilon>0\) such that \(t+se_j\in\Omega\) for every \(j\) and \(|s|<\epsilon\). If needed, enlarge \(E\) by adjoining a Gauss variable \(V\) of norm \(e^\epsilon\) and completing the resulting valued rational-function field. This is a complete analytic extension, and it makes the annuli with radii \(e^{\pm\epsilon}\) strict. This enlargement preserves the norms of all the \(a_i\) and of every original coefficient. Denote the resulting field again by \(E\).

Let \(E_j\) be the completion of \(E(S_1,\ldots,S_j)\) with the joint radius-one Gauss norm, and let \(x_j\in U_K\) be the seminorm obtained by substituting
\[
Z_i=a_iS_i\ (i\leq j),\qquad Z_i=a_i\ (i>j).
\]
Thus \(x_0=x\). Over \(E_{j-1}\), use the analytic curve map from \(e^{-\epsilon}<|u|<e^\epsilon\) to \(U_{E_{j-1}}\) with coordinates
\[
(a_1S_1,\ldots,a_{j-1}S_{j-1},a_ju,a_{j+1},\ldots,a_n).
\tag{4}
\]
Its entire unit-circle affinoid lies in \(U_{E_{j-1}}\), and its surrounding open annulus lies there by the choice of \(\epsilon\). The point \(u=1\) projects to \(x_{j-1}\); its radius-one Gauss point projects to \(x_j\). F1 applied to the pullback of \(h\), with the constant value at that Gauss point, gives \(h(x_{j-1})\leq h(x_j)\).

Finally, the joint Gauss norm in the independent variables \(S_i\) gives
\[
\left|\sum_m c_m\prod_i(a_iS_i)^{m_i}\right|
=\max_m|c_m|e^{\langle m,t\rangle}.
\]
Hence \(x_n=\kappa_K(t)\). Chaining the \(n\) inequalities proves (3). The use of completed residue and Gauss field extensions is essential; only testing curves over \(K\) would not justify this proof. \(\square\)

## 3. A generic scalar monomial curve proves convexity

**Lemma 2.** Assume F1. Under the hypotheses of Lemma 1, the function
\[
a_h(t)=h(\kappa_K(t))
\tag{5}
\]
is finite, continuous, and convex on \(\Omega\).

**Proof.** Finiteness and continuity follow from those of \(h\) and \(\kappa_K\). Fix \(t_0\) and an integer vector \(w\). Suppose a compact interval of points \(t_0+rw\) is contained in \(\Omega\). Its image can be enlarged slightly along this line while remaining in \(\Omega\).

Take \(E=\mathcal H(\kappa_K(t_0))\) and let \(a_i\) be the coordinate images. Over \(E\), consider the monomial curve
\[
\gamma(u)=(a_1u^{w_1},\ldots,a_nu^{w_n}).
\tag{6}
\]
For every Laurent polynomial \(P=\sum_m c_mZ^m\), collect terms in \(P(\gamma(u))\) according to \(k=\langle m,w\rangle\). Its coefficient of \(u^k\) is
\[
B_k=\sum_{\langle m,w\rangle=k}c_ma^m.
\]
The defining Gauss property of \(E\) implies
\[
|B_k|=\max_{\langle m,w\rangle=k}|c_m|e^{\langle m,t_0\rangle}.
\tag{7}
\]
Distinct monomials in the original \(Z_i\) remain distinct in this computation, so there is no cancellation between their initial forms. If \(\eta_{e^r}\) is the one-variable Gauss point of radius \(e^r\), (7) gives
\[
|P(\gamma(\eta_{e^r}))|
=\max_m|c_m|e^{\langle m,t_0+rw\rangle}.
\tag{8}
\]
The projection of this curve point to \(T_K\) is exactly \(\kappa_K(t_0+rw)\).

For endpoints \(r_0<r_1\), we may enlarge \(E\) further by independent weighted Gauss variables of norms \(e^{r_0},e^{r_1}\), and the norms of two slightly exterior radii. Completing the fraction field makes the closed annulus and its surrounding open annulus strict. Norms of the \(B_k\) remain unchanged under this isometric field extension. F1 compares the pulled-back function on the closed annulus to the harmonic function affine in \(\log|u|\) with its endpoint values. At \(r=(1-\theta)r_0+\theta r_1\), (8) therefore gives
\[
a_h(t_0+rw)\leq(1-\theta)a_h(t_0+r_0w)+\theta a_h(t_0+r_1w).
\tag{9}
\]
This proves convexity in every rational direction. For arbitrary \(t_0,t_1\in\Omega\), choose rational vectors \(d_j\to t_1-t_0\). For large \(j\), \(t_0+d_j\in\Omega\), and the entire segment is in \(\Omega\) by convexity. Each \(d_j\) is a real multiple of an integer vector, so (9) applies. Continuity in (5) permits the limit \(j\to\infty\), proving the general convex inequality. \(\square\)

**Why the generic scalar coefficients cannot be omitted.** In dimension two, the ground-field diagonal \(u\mapsto(u,u)\) annihilates \(Z_1-Z_2\). At the two-dimensional unit Gauss point, \(|Z_1-Z_2|=1\), even in residue characteristic two. Thus that diagonal does not contain the desired Gauss points. Equations (7) and (8), over the completed residue field of a multidimensional Gauss point, repair this exact cancellation defect. This is an algebraic observation, not a counterexample to psh envelopes.

## 4. Computing the full continuous scalar-psh envelope

Let \(\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\}\) be upper semicontinuous, and assume that it has a finite convex minorant \(a_0\). Put
\[
f=\varphi\circ\tau,\qquad
p(t)=\sup\{a(t):a:\Omega\to\mathbf R\text{ finite convex},\ a\leq\varphi\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The hypothesis forces \(\varphi\) to be finite everywhere: \(a_0(t)\leq\varphi(t)\) rules out \(-\infty\), and \(+\infty\) is not in the stated codomain. Consequently \(a_0\leq p\leq\varphi\), so \(p\) is finite. A pointwise supremum of convex functions is convex wherever finite, by taking the supremum of their convex inequalities.

Here is the required continuity argument. On a closed ball inside \(\Omega\), upper semicontinuity bounds \(\varphi\) above, and continuity of the finite convex \(a_0\) bounds it below. Thus \(m\leq p\leq M\) on that ball. The elementary convex chord argument gives local Lipschitz continuity: from either of two points in the concentric quarter-radius ball, extend the ray through the other point at least half the original radius inside the full ball. The convex inequality bounds the corresponding secant slope by \(2(M-m)/R\). Applying this in both orders bounds the absolute difference. The same finite-convex continuity fact for \(a_0\) follows from the identical argument after obtaining a local upper bound from convexity on a surrounding coordinate box and a lower bound by reflection about its center. This proof does not assume a common modulus for the original analytic competitor family.

Let \(\mathcal C_{K,f}\) be the **entire** family of finite continuous scalar-curve-positive functions on \(U_K\) dominated by \(f\). Under F1, for every \(h\in\mathcal C_{K,f}\), Lemmas 1 and 2 give
\[
h(x)\leq a_h(\tau(x))\leq p(\tau(x)),
\tag{11}
\]
because \(a_h\) is itself a convex minorant of \(\varphi\). No invariance of \(h\) is assumed.

Under F2, \(p\circ\tau\) is scalar-curve-positive. To see this for **every** analytic extension and every test curve map \(g:C\to U_E\), put \(G_i=Z_i\circ g\). Each \(G_i\) is an analytic unit, so \(\log|G_i|\) is harmonic by F2. Every affine function \(b+\langle q,t\rangle\) pulls back to the harmonic function \(b+\sum_i q_i\log|G_i|\).

A finite convex function on an open convex set has a supporting affine function at every point. For completeness, its one-sided directional derivative at an interior point is a finite sublinear function, as follows from local Lipschitz continuity and the convex inequality. A linear functional dominated by this sublinear function exists by the finite-dimensional sublinear extension argument: extending from a subspace by one vector permits an interval of values, whose lower bound is at most its upper bound by subadditivity; induction on dimension completes the extension. Convexity then gives a global supporting affine function. The collection of these supports represents \(p\) pointwise.

On each compact subset of \(\Omega\), finitely many supports approximate \(p\) uniformly from below: for each point take a support agreeing there, use continuity of its gap from \(p\), and take a finite cover. Their maximum pulls back to a finite maximum of harmonic functions. The closure deductions following F2 imply that the locally uniform limit \(p\circ\tau_E\circ g\) is subharmonic. Continuity and finiteness also hold. Thus \(p\circ\tau\in\mathcal C_{K,f}\), and (11) proves
\[
\boxed{\sup_{h\in\mathcal C_{K,f}}h=p\circ\tau.}
\tag{12}
\]
This is a full noninvariant-family conclusion, conditional on the specified curve foundations. It is not merely an invariant-competitor equality.

## 5. Full LFS equality and exact scalar compatibility

Assume F2 and F3. Every finite locally LFS function in (2) is scalar-curve-positive. Indeed, after any analytic extension and any test-curve pullback, the same local presentation remains valid. At a fixed curve point, some \(g_i\) has nonzero value because there is no common zero. All terms whose value is zero there are strictly below the finite maximum and can be discarded in a small neighborhood, by continuity of their norms. The remaining finitely many \(g_i\) are invertible on a smaller neighborhood. The function is therefore locally a finite maximum of harmonic functions, divided by a positive integer. Comparison gives subharmonicity, and locality gives it on the whole test curve. No extension of the regularized ground-field supremum is used in this argument.

Let \(\mathcal L_{K,f}\) denote the **entire** finite locally LFS minorant family on \(U_K\); it includes all noninvariant members. Its upper bound follows from (11). For the reverse inequality, fix \(t_0\) and a support
\[
\ell(t)=p(t_0)+\langle b,t-t_0\rangle\leq p(t).
\]
Let \(D=\sup_{t\in\Omega}\|t-t_0\|<\infty\). For \(q\in\mathbf Q^n\), set
\[
\ell_q(t)=p(t_0)+\langle q,t-t_0\rangle-D\|q-b\|.
\tag{13}
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz gives \(\ell_q\leq\ell\leq p\leq\varphi\) throughout \(\Omega\), while \(\ell_q(t_0)\to p(t_0)\) as \(q\to b\). If \(q=m^{-1}v\) with \(v\in\mathbf Z^n\), then
\[
\ell_q\circ\tau=c+\frac1m\log|Z^v|,
\tag{14}
\]
which is a global LFS function in F3 and is below \(f\). Thus
\[
\boxed{\sup_{h\in\mathcal L_{K,f}}h=p\circ\tau.}
\tag{15}
\]
Boundedness of \(\Omega\) is used specifically in (13). No corresponding full LFS assertion is made here on an unbounded tropical domain.

There is also a global increasing sequence in \(\mathcal L_{K,f}\) converging locally uniformly to (15). Take a compact exhaustion of \(\Omega\). The pointwise approximation (13), continuity, and a finite cover give a finite maximum of rational-slope affine minorants approximating \(p\) on each exhaustion set within \(2^{-j}\). Taking the cumulative maxima of these finite maxima makes the sequence increasing. If the slopes have different integer denominators, a common multiple writes each cumulative maximum in the form (2) using Laurent monomials. Every term stays below \(f\). This sequence proves the lower bound for the full family; the upper bound was already proved for **every** competitor, so the result is not a subfamily claim.

All arguments apply over any complete analytic extension \(E/K\), with the identical \(\Omega\), \(\varphi\), and \(p\). Since \(\tau_E=\tau_K\circ\pi_{E/K}\), equations (12) and (15) give the exact full-family identities
\[
\sup\mathcal L_{E,f\circ\pi_{E/K}}
=p\circ\tau_E
=(\sup\mathcal L_{K,f})\circ\pi_{E/K},
\tag{16}
\]
and the same formula for \(\mathcal C\). These are identities between the separately defined **full** competitor envelopes over the two fields. They do not assert descent of each upstairs competitor.

The raw envelopes in (12) and (15) are continuous, so their upper-semicontinuous regularizations equal themselves. Consequently
\[
(s_K\circ\pi_{E/K})^*=s_K^*\circ\pi_{E/K}=p\circ\tau_E.
\tag{17}
\]
This proves scalar regularization compatibility in this conditional toric sector without assuming scalar projection is open and without deriving upstairs regularization from ordinary ground-field curve tests.

## 6. The exact P03 union gate and original gaps

Let \(\mathcal E_{K,f}\) be the original union of all locally LFS and all semipositive real-PL minorants. If, in the exact original definitions and after every analytic extension, every one of those real-PL competitors is finite continuous scalar-curve-positive, then
\[
\mathcal L_{K,f}\subseteq\mathcal E_{K,f}\subseteq\mathcal C_{K,f}.
\]
Equations (12) and (15) squeeze its **entire** envelope to \(p\circ\tau\). The same squeeze over every \(E\) gives exact full-union pullback and (17). This implication does not require the Gauss radialization of an individual real-PL function to be real PL, or closure of the class under fiberwise maxima.

**The individual-real-PL inclusion is not proved here.** No exact primary source, definition, or full proof of that bridge was retrieved. In particular, the supplied claim that local PL and LFS functions are cofinal is not used. Thus the conditional squeeze is not advertised as an established result for the exact original union.

Even if this foundational bridge is supplied, the result addresses only tropical obstacles on the displayed bounded convex smooth-torus domains with a finite convex minorant. It supplies neither positivity for arbitrary noninvariant obstacles nor sharp normal/geometrically-unibranch higher-dimensional hypotheses. It reconstructs no branched-node, nonsplit-node, or codimension-two pinching necessity result; those supplied statements remain data. It proves nothing for target relative-boundary points or for singular normal targets. The nonempty convex-minorant condition excludes obstacles taking \(-\infty\); their full behavior and empty-family cases are not classified. No CLD-v2 assertion is made. Continuity of the explicitly computed toric function is used to settle scalar regularization in this sector, not to settle P03.3.
