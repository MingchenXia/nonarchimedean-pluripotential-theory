# Signed cone energy and a uniform outer-extension route

Unreviewed root proof proposal, 2026-10-05. No theorem in this note has
received independent reconstruction. It does not close P13.1 or P13.2.
The completed round55 proof, the continuation65 Veronese calculation and
continuation66 radial calculation are diagnostic inputs, not accepted

The main observation is the exact distinction in BJ1801.08229v4,
LF4843--4862: Theorem8.5 and Corollary8.6 give differentiation of
E-downarrow for every E1 potential. The envelope property is required

## 1. The representation gate, with actual scalar factors

Let R be a normal good torus cone of dimension n and eta a rational
Reeb field. Write eta=s*eta_e, where eta_e induces an effective integer
grading of gcd one, and s>0. Do not use a primitive vector in the ambient
torus lattice in place of this effective grading without checking gcd.
Let chi be the finite-type signed quotient norm constructed in round55,
first with rational assigned weights. Its rational Rees algebra Gamma is
finitely generated, with Reeb degree and filtration index as two gradings.

Choose a sufficiently divisible positive integer d so that R^(d) and the
corresponding Rees Veronese are generated in degree one. Put
V=Proj R, L_d=O_V(d), and Z=Spec R^(d) with its standard grading.
Normality of R and, for n>=2, Hartogs across the vertex must justify the
identification with the full section ring of L_d. Treat n=1 directly:
a normal one-dimensional good cone is the polynomial cone over a point.
The resulting finite map p:Spec R -> Z has generic degree d, by the
effective grading. It is not degree one merely because Proj is unchanged.

The restricted filtration gives an actual ample test configuration for
(V,L_d) by BHJ1504.06568v3 Proposition2.15, after the checked divisibility
and index clearing. Its metric must be identified with the norm's FS
supremum. Restricting to R^(d) does not change that supremum after the
degree conversion: powers g_i^d provide the lower inequality, and the
full norm/potential supremum provides the upper inequality even when
chi(g_i^d)>d*chi(g_i). Graph normalization changes neither the metric nor
the DH leading measure; check the actual BHJ normalization hypotheses.

Let Phi be the standard cone potential for this filtration on Z, and
rho_d its zero-weight reference. The exact equalities to establish are

    p*Phi=s*d*u_eta,       p*rho_d=s*d*rho_eta.

For the standard section cone, use the actual L_d^(-1) torsor over V.
Its local potentials are log|t_i|+h_i and log|t_i|+h_0,i. The top
horizontal mixed curvature pulled from V is a weighted integration
current over the actual H(v)-fibers (CLD18.6.1). Wedge it with
ddc max(log|t_i|+h_0,i,0). On each fiber this has one unit Gauss mass
at radius exp(-h_0,i(v)) (CLD18.6.2). Local smooth approximation and
the weighted-current continuity8.7.2 must identify the full Radon
measure against all continuous tests, not just base tests. This is the
radial bridge of continuation66 and still needs independent verification.

The finite-map projection formula must retain its degree d. With n-1
horizontal factors and one radial factor it gives

    V_eta=s^(-n)*d^(1-n)*(L_d^(n-1)),
    E_eta(u_eta)=E_Ld(phi)/(s*d).

Equivalently, the natural transverse projective class is
omega_eta=L_d/(s*d), and its actual energy agrees with cone energy.
The operator correspondence is the projective probability measure lifted
to the Gauss section and then through p to the torified carrier. Verify
uniqueness of the torified lift and the calibrated degree. Its raw mass
is V_eta; it is not the volume omega_eta^(n-1) without the radial factor.

BHJ's degree-wise signed mean equals its actual intersection energy.
Summing degrees then gives

    E_Ld(phi)=(n+1)/n*S_standard.

The remaining algebraic passage is

    S_chi(eta)=S_standard/(s*d).

Here the norm on the full R, rather than only R^(d), must be handled.
The finitely generated Rees algebra Gamma is finite over Gamma^(d):
every homogeneous generator has its d-th power in the latter algebra.
Its fraction-field rank is d. Choose homogeneous elements giving a free
rank-d submodule F inside Gamma over Gamma^(d); multiply denominators
to find one homogeneous c in Gamma^(d), nonzero, with c*Gamma contained
in F. Both embeddings are homogeneous for degree and filtration index.
They induce filtered injective maps on finite degree-cutoff spaces with
fixed degree/index shifts. The direct sum F consists of d shifted copies
of the restricted norm. A fixed degree shift changes cutoff dimensions
by O(m^(n-1)); the resulting unmatched spectral contributions have size
O(m^n) because all weights are linearly bounded. A fixed index shift
changes total weights by O(N(m))=O(m^n). All these errors vanish after
division by m*N(m). Formalize the spectral min-max inequalities for the
two filtered injections, rather than assuming that multiplication by c
is an isometry. This should give the stated mean conversion, including
signed weights. Nonstandard degrees and residues modulo d need checking.

The proposed final identity is therefore

    E_eta(u_chi,eta)=(n+1)/n*S_chi(eta).

No printed unmodified E=S formula is used. This identity implies
presentation independence only after the actual metric/current bridges
above are proved.

For irrational eta and arbitrary real weights, use round55's locally
uniform CPSH mixed-MA/energy continuity, not an unbounded limit theorem.
Continuity of the signed means follows by normalizing the finite list at
the trivial valuation. The normalized weights are nonpositive; the
uniform negative-mean comparison controls Reeb variation, and the
finite-type norm perturbation bound controls the changing weights.
Add back a(eta)=max_i(lambda_i/d_eta(beta_i)) using the *own-degree*
shift rule S_shift=S+a*n/(n+1). This avoids a fixed-reference degree
shift being incorrectly canceled at two nonproportional fields.

## 2. Genuine energy comparison and E1 preservation

Assuming the representation gate has actually been proved, the signed
cutoff estimate gives, for normalized FS u and its intrinsic root image,

    exp(-(2*n+1)*delta)*(-E_xi(u))
       <= -E_eta(R_xi,eta u)
       <= exp((2*n+1)*delta)*(-E_xi(u)),

where delta is the fixed-generator degree-ratio logarithmic distance.
These constants are independent of the primary list and its weights.
Uniform FS approximation gives the same estimate on normalized CPSH.

For u in normalized E1, do not silently assume a defining decreasing
CPSH net is normalized. Let u_i decrease to u and a_i=u_i(v_triv)
decrease to zero. N_i=u_i-a_i need not be decreasing, but each is
normalized CPSH and E_xi(N_i)=E_xi(u_i)-a_i. The root-shift slope
bounds give ||R(u_i)-R(N_i)||_K <= |a_i|/c. Monotonicity and the
unit translation rule bound the energy difference by the same amount.
The raw R(u_i) decrease to R(u). The quantitative lower energy bound
therefore proves target E1 membership; decreasing energy continuity
gives the displayed estimate for u. Applying the inverse proves onto
E1 on the normalized constant quotients.

This proves a useful uniform finite-energy comparison. It does not by
itself prove continuity for arbitrary strongly convergent nets, nor
uniform tails over an entire merely energy-bounded family. Those stronger
claims must not be asserted or made artificial prerequisites for the
following differentiation argument if it avoids them.

## 3. A fixed dense direction class with uniform positive domination

Fix one rational Reeb xi0. Take D to be the rational linear span of
differences of its actual FS potentials with rational weights, restricted
to the common torified compact carrier K. Allow arbitrary rational
positive equivariant slope in representations; differences of FS at
one fixed slope alone need not be dense. Verify the lattice/Stone--Weierstrass
density in C(K): primary padding, products, maxima and separation on K
must be established for this exact class. Sums and scalar multiples can
be written a-b with a,b having the same positive rational equivariant
slope, by padding with multiples of the zero-on-K reference. The formula
max(a-b,c-d)=max(a+d,c+b)-(b+d) proves lattice closure, with slope
allowed to increase. Rational constants and separation then give uniform
density by the real Stone--Weierstrass theorem applied to its closure.
For separation, distinct torified points differ on a T-homogeneous
regular function g. On K the actual primary FS expression obtained by
padding g with the algebra-generator reference is
max((lambda-v(g))/d_xi0(wt(g)),-M). Suitable rational lambda and M
distinguish the two values, including a finite versus infinite value.

For f=A0*(phi_xi0-psi_xi0) on K, with A0>0 rational, and eta in a
compact Reeb slice Q, extend
f by f_eta(v)=f(v+rho_eta(v)*eta), which is eta-homogeneous of degree
zero. Write phi_xi0=max_j((lambda_j-v(f_j))/d_xi0(alpha_j)) and put
r_j(eta)=A0*d_eta(alpha_j)/d_xi0(alpha_j). Choose one positive rational A
larger than every r_j(eta) for both finite lists and every eta in Q.
Then

    a_eta=A*rho_eta+A0*phi_xi0(v+rho_eta(v)*eta)
          =max_j[A0*(lambda_j-v(f_j))/d_xi0(alpha_j)
                      +(A-r_j(eta))*rho_eta(v)]

and the analogous b_eta satisfy a_eta-b_eta=f_eta. Each branch is a
positive sum of logarithmic regular functions; the total equivariant
slope is A. For rational eta the coefficients are rational, so after
clearing denominators and using the finite algebra-generator maximum
for rho_eta, this is an actual primary FS potential for eta/A. For
irrational eta prove actual CPSH(eta/A) membership via rational
approximation and the intrinsic root comparison back to the fixed
polarization. Merely showing CLD current positivity is insufficient.
The expressions vary locally uniformly. On K their difference is the
single fixed f, independent of eta.

On the rational projective quotient, a_eta and b_eta correspond to
semipositive metrics in the class A*omega_eta. Consequently the
projective mixed volumes are exactly

    V_(omega_eta+epsilon*A*omega_eta)/V_omega_eta
       =(1+epsilon*A)^(n-1).

BJ8.7--8.8 then gives a quadratic differentiation bound with a constant
depending only on n,A,||f||_K and a fixed small |epsilon| interval,
independent of eta, u and quotient denominators d. Check both signs by
interchanging a_eta and b_eta. This uses a *common* fixed direction f,
not changing test functions while claiming uniform differentiation.

## 4. The continuous-obstacle energy, without an envelope antecedent

For g in C(K) define an actual energy extremum

    F_eta(g)=sup{E_eta(w): w in CPSH(eta), w|K<=g}.

This is finite, monotone, translation equivariant and 1-Lipschitz. The
reference plus min(g) is a competitor; any competitor is bounded above
by the reference plus max(g). If g is the restriction of an actual CPSH
potential w, then F_eta(g)=E_eta(w) by energy monotonicity. In the
rational quotient verify equality with the precise BJ continuous-obstacle
energy. This comparison involves the existing genuine energy, not a
new inverse measure.

That equality must account for the residual torus: cone competitors
correspond initially to residual-torified projective competitors, whereas
BJ's supremum allows every projective competitor. Define the continuous
torification q on the entire quotient and identify K with its image via
the unique torified Gauss lift. The induced obstacle satisfies g=g*q.
For a projective FS metric psi, decomposing its finitely many sections
into residual-torus weight components gives psi*q as another actual FS
metric. The ultrametric inequality gives psi*q>=psi at every analytic
point. If psi<=g, then psi*q<=g as well. Pass through uniform FS
approximation to actual CPSH; monotonicity of the genuine energy then
shows the unrestricted and torified suprema agree. Verify linearization,
the canonical invariant reference, continuity and the identification
with K; do not equate the two suprema without this step.

For a fixed bounded obstacle |g|<=M, truncate competitors from below
by max(w,rho_eta-M). The supremum can thus be taken over potentials
bounded by M on K. Their intrinsic root changes are uniformly O(M*delta).
For a bounded w, put a=w(v_triv), Nw=w-a. The root-shift bound gives

    ||R(w)-R(Nw)-a||_K <= (exp(delta)-1)*|a|.

The normalized energy comparison and -E(Nw)<=2M then give a uniform
O(M*delta) energy change. Transfer competitors with R and subtract the
small uniform constraint error. Applying the reverse map proves
continuity of F_eta(g) in eta, locally uniformly for bounded obstacles.
Do not try to control arbitrary competitors by the unbounded pointwise
root estimate without this legitimate truncation.

For u in CPSH(xi), rational eta_l -> xi with u_l=R_xi,eta_l u have
locally uniform convergence. The continuous-obstacle extrema converge,
as do the actual probability measures by round55's CLD theorem. Passing
the *uniform* rational quadratic estimate to the limit supplies it for
irrational xi and bounded u, still with the same fixed f in D.

## 5. Outer extension and decreasing limits

For an upper-semicontinuous obstacle h on K, bounded above, set

    F_eta_down(h)=inf{F_eta(g): g in C(K), g>=h}.

The proposed canonical extension agrees with the genuine E_eta at every
actual PSH potential, including E1. To prove this, use u_i in CPSH
decreasing to u. For any continuous g>=u and epsilon>0, the open sets
{u_i<g+epsilon} eventually cover compact K; directedness gives
u_i<=g+epsilon everywhere for all sufficiently late i. Hence
E(u)<=F(g)+epsilon. Conversely g=u_i gives F_down(u)<=E(u_i).
Let i tend to its limit and epsilon to zero.

The same compactness argument, with u_i+t*f decreasing to u+t*f,
shows F_down(u+t*f)=lim_i F(u_i+t*f). This proves independence from
the defining net. Its finite value for u in E1 follows from monotonicity
and the translation bounds E(u)-|t|*||f|| <= F_down(u+t*f)
<=E(u)+|t|*||f||.

For fixed eta the existing genuine mixed operator's decreasing-net weak
continuity gives MA_eta(u_i)->MA_eta(u). Passing the bounded quadratic
estimate through this net proves

    |F_eta_down(u+t*f)-E_eta(u)-t*integral_K f MA_eta(u)|
       <= C_(Q,f)*t^2

for every eta in Q and every u in E1(eta), with both signs. The extension
is defined using the actual CPSH energy and agrees with it on E1; its
derivative is the actual cone probability operator. No equality with
E(P(u+t*f)) is asserted if an actual global envelope is unavailable.
Judge explicitly whether this is the differentiation intended by the
unchanged original task, rather than declaring an unproved envelope
equality or changing the target to obtain acceptance.

For any fixed continuous f on K choose f_m in D uniformly. Monotonicity,
the unit translation rule and probability mass bound the difference of
derivative remainders divided by |t| by

    2*||f-f_m||_K + C_(Q,f_m)*|t|.

Choose m before t. This is an explicit uniform differentiability modulus
over Q and all E1 potentials for each continuous direction. It does not
claim a quadratic bound for every continuous f or uniformity over the
entire unit ball of C(K).

## 6. Proof obligations and limits

All preceding proposed conclusions depend on the fully reconstructed
standard-cone, finite-cover, Rees-module and norm/test-configuration
bridges in Section1. The common-direction FS membership, carrier density,
quotient obstacle correspondence and actual operator applications must
also be proved with their exact hypotheses. Read the complete actual
Wu2406.13684v1 Definitions5.9/6.1, Proposition5.10 and Theorem6.3 proof;
some displayed source formulas have indexing or normalization errors and
must be corrected explicitly, rather than importing the printed formulas
as indisputable premises. Source hash and compile checks are not a proof.

If this route succeeds it may supply the unchanged P13.1 comparison and
uniform differentiation without a singular EP antecedent. It does not
by itself characterize the image in P13.2: variational attainment,
compactness and the necessary/sufficient target measure condition still
need their own exact argument. If any indispensable bridge fails, retain
the strongest true partial result and the concrete gate; do not declare
either original problem solved.
