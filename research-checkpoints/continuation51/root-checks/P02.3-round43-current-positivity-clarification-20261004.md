# Cellwise current positivity in the completed R43 draft

Fresh root-authored local clarification. It does not amend the frozen native proof or supply an independent acceptance verdict. It has not been sent to the author or a provider.

R43 Section 2.4 invokes coincidence of Lagerberg positivity cones in degree (d-1,d-1), where d is the analytic integration dimension. CD Corollary 1.2.10 states that coincidence for degree (N-1,N-1) in an N-dimensional vector space. A harmonic joint chart can have ambient dimension N>d. One therefore cannot infer that every ambient positive (d-1,d-1)-form is strongly positive from that corollary alone. The underlying current-positivity conclusion has a direct reconstruction which avoids this inference.

On a local joint harmonic chart, let alpha be the curvature preform coming from the positive semidefinite Hessian explicitly constructed in R43. It is a positive (indeed strongly positive) (1,1)-Lagerberg form in the actual ambient vector space. Let beta represent a positive test form of degree (d-1,d-1) on that chart. GJR Section 2.7 defines positive forms on a polyhedral support as restrictions of ambient positive forms and states that positive Lagerberg forms are closed under products. Thus alpha wedge beta is positive, without any equality of cones in ambient degree (d-1,d-1).

For each d-dimensional integration cell sigma, restrict both forms to its d-dimensional tangent space. CD Lemma 1.2.11 preserves positivity under this linear restriction. Either use positivity of the restricted product or apply CD Corollary 1.2.10 in THIS d-dimensional tangent space. In top degree (d,d), positivity means a nonnegative coefficient with the canonical Lagerberg orientation. Multiplying by the nonnegative tropical weight and integrating gives a nonnegative contribution from sigma. Cells of smaller dimension have zero contribution. Summing cells and localizing with nonnegative partitions of unity gives a nonnegative integral against the test form.

GJR Stokes on the stated Hausdorff boundaryless pure-dimensional domain then identifies this integral with the curvature-current pairing. For a construction first made over a finite Galois extension, GJR Proposition 14.5(1) gives equality of the upstairs and downstairs top-degree integrals, with no degree multiplier. The hypotheses of R43's current assertions retain boundarylessness and pure dimension; no such Stokes assertion is made at the actual boundary of the closed-disc example.
