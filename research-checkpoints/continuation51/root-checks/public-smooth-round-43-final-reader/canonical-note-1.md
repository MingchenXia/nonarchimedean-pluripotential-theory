GR, arXiv:2506.13548v2, Definition 4.1 defines classical psh using every analytic extension field and every analytic curve, with upper semicontinuous values in R union {-infinity}. GR 3.4.1 identifies its class on a smooth connected strict curve over a nontrivially valued field with Thuillier subharmonic functions together with the constant minus infinity. Proposition 4.2 gives ordinary sheafiness, maxima, positive linear combinations, scalar stability, analytic pullback and descent along finite surjective morphisms. Proposition 3.5(3) permits an upper semicontinuous function subharmonic on a curve's analytic interior to extend as subharmonic to the whole curve. Theorem 6.1 gives the local maximum principle at analytic interior points. Theorem 6.5 permits a pointwise limit of a net of classical psh functions when the limit is upper semicontinuous. Lemma 3.9 and its proof give finite normalization of reduced strict curves, rig-smoothness over perfect fields, exceptional discrete rigid points, the quotient property and local separation into finitely many branches.

GJR, arXiv:2111.05741, works over a complete nontrivially valued field on good strictly analytic spaces. Definition 5.1 uses strict affinoid G-coverings. Definition 8.1 uses integer-PL harmonic coordinates for harmonic tropicalizations. Proposition 7.5 expresses real harmonic germs through finitely many integer harmonic germs; 7.6 and Remark 8.2(4) preserve these coordinates under morphisms and every analytic scalar extension. Definitions 10.3 and 10.7 define weakly smooth functions by smooth Lagerberg functions in harmonic tropicalizations, sheafified on ordinary open sets. Section 10.8 gives pullback and Proposition 10.9 gives joint-chart equality. Ordinary CLD forms are included by Remark 10.10, but the reverse inclusion is not asserted. Proposition 14.2 gives finite Galois descent. Proposition 14.5(1) gives equality of integrals under finite Galois scalar extension, WITHOUT a degree multiplier. Sections 11–12 justify integration and current differentiation on Hausdorff boundaryless pure-dimensional spaces.

The actual GJR PDF, pages 80–81, was rendered and visually inspected. Proposition 15.11 states: for a smooth curve, meaning rig-smooth and boundaryless, over a nontrivially valued field, an arbitrary Thuillier-subharmonic function admits a decreasing NET of weakly smooth psh functions on each relatively compact ordinary open subset. The proof's final sequence concerns a single piecewise real-linear target. It does not establish a sequence for the initial arbitrary target. The proof expressly allows arbitrary real subdivisions without claiming that they are formal-model skeletons. Proposition 15.9 concerns the indicated strict subgraphs and piecewise (Z,Gamma)-linear harmonic functions. Proposition 15.7 identifies real harmonic functions with Thuillier harmonic functions. Remark 15.10 explicitly allows harmonic functions that are weakly smooth and not ordinary CLD smooth.

Thuillier's thesis, tel-00010990v1, distinguishes the all-domain site X_G and strict rigid site X_R in 2.1.1. Its Definition 3.2.1 of lisse uses all-domain affinoid coverings. Theorem 3.4.2 constructs a decreasing family indexed by finite subsets. No strict GJR real-PL membership or sequence is inferred merely from that theorem.

Wanner, arXiv:1801.04713v3, assumes an algebraically closed complete nontrivially valued field. Corollary 5.4 assumes a smooth proper algebraic curve, a residue field algebraic over a finite field or the projective-line/Mumford alternatives, and a finite continuous current-psh target on an open subset. Its compact Dini argument depends on that continuity. These restrictions are not silently applied to the original arbitrary classical class.

The actual saved CD arXiv:1204.6277v2 uses Definition 8.1.1, Lemma 8.1.4, Proposition 8.1.5 and Proposition 8.3.1 for ordinary smooth positive generators, transfinite decreasing-sequence stages, pullbacks and current positivity. Corollary 15.1.2 describes ordinary analytic smooth charts. Lemmas 1.2.10–1.2.11 give the relevant Lagerberg positivity cones and linear pullbacks. These results do not put every classical function in the first ordinary smooth stage or identify ordinary smoothness with weak smoothness. The other GR cache concerns strict real-PL semipositivity and is not used to identify arbitrary lisse functions as weakly smooth. Berkovich 1993 Proposition 1.5.5 supplies boundary composition, as restated in GR 2.11. No unpublished book premise is used.
