ID: n2

SUMMARY:
Draft sequence theorem for arbitrary classical psh germs on reduced boundaryless good strict curves over complete perfect nontrivially valued fields with a countable dense subset, including multibranch singularities. Normalization approximants are aligned on one fixed ordinary neighborhood, with harmonic weak smoothness, positivity and scalar compatibility proved.

DETAILED SUMMARY:
A countable extraction argument handles extended-real limits without replacing a general net silently. Finite normalization separates the branches at a singular rigid point. For a finite common target value, small upward shifts align approximant values; for a minus-infinity value, regularized maxima with a common level align them. Compact domination guarantees decrease on one fixed compact normalization neighborhood. Local constancy at rigid points gives actual constant neighborhoods downstairs. The result permits discontinuous targets, poles and identically minus-infinity branches, but remains a one-dimensional sufficient theorem under the stated field, reducedness and boundary assumptions.

TEXT:
Draft theorem. Let K be complete, perfect and nontrivially valued, with a countable dense subset. Let C be a reduced Hausdorff good strictly K-analytic curve with empty analytic boundary. For every ordinary open D in C, every arbitrary classical psh u:D→R union {-infinity}, and every x in D, there is one fixed ordinary open neighborhood V of x and finite weakly smooth classical psh functions v_j on V decreasing pointwise to u. Their strong curvature currents are positive. The same sequence pulls back with these properties after every analytic extension. For finite continuous u, convergence is locally uniform.

The following arguments establish the new applications of the premises audited in n1.

Countable extraction. Suppose a_i is a directed decreasing net of finite continuous functions on a second-countable space Z, with infimum a, possibly minus infinity. For each rational q, the open sets {a_i<q} cover {a<q}. Every open subspace of a second-countable space is Lindelof, so countably many indices suffice for each q. Enumerate all these indices and successively choose directed upper bounds i_j. Then a_{i_j} decreases to a: for each z every rational q>a(z) eventually bounds its selected values, and if a(z)=-infinity every rational does. This extracts indices sufficient by values; it does not claim countable cofinality of the original index set.

The field assumption supplies this topology locally. A strict affinoid algebra is a Banach quotient of a Tate algebra. Polynomials with coefficients from a countable dense subfield are dense. A norm-dense sequence b_n supplies countably many evaluations generating the spectrum topology, since ||b(x)|-|c(x)||≤|b-c|(x)≤||b-c|| uniformly. They separate points and embed the compact spectrum in a countable product of bounded real intervals. Thus its spectrum and ordinary open subsets are second countable.

Compact domination. If continuous a_i decrease to a, A is compact, and continuous finite b satisfies b>a on A, then some a_i<b throughout A. The open sets {a_i<b} cover A; use a finite subcover and one directed upper bound. Additional finite index or point-value requirements can be imposed simultaneously. No continuity of a and no Dini theorem are needed.

Regularized maximum. Choose an even nonnegative smooth probability density rho supported in (-1,1). Set theta_delta(t)=integral |t+delta s|rho(s)ds and M_delta(a,b)=(a+b+theta_delta(a-b))/2. Convolution makes theta smooth and convex, with derivative in [-1,1]. Jensen and the triangle inequality give max(a,b)≤M_delta(a,b)≤max(a,b)+delta/2. It equals max when |a-b|≥delta, and is convex and nondecreasing in each variable.

Classical positivity of such compositions is proved, rather than inferred from smoothness. For finite continuous classical psh g_i and a smooth convex coordinatewise nondecreasing F, supporting affine functions of F have nonnegative coefficients. Finite maxima of their evaluations on the g_i are classical psh by GR 4.2. Their directed net converges pointwise to the continuous composition, so GR 6.5 proves its classical positivity. The same argument applies to a convex nondecreasing function of log|P| that is constant on a negative half-line; the constant supporting line handles zeros.

The GJR construction has the specific local smoothness needed here. After a finite Galois extension, an individual lisse target is represented on a finite skeleton. At a curvature vertex with outgoing slopes s_e and positive weights w_e, let mu=sum w_e s_e>0. Replacing every slope by s_e-mu/(sum w_e) gives a harmonic star function G, with the same vertex value and strictly below the target F off that vertex. A sufficiently small M_delta(G+epsilon,F), delta<epsilon, equals G+epsilon near the vertex and F near the outer ends. The equalities hold on ordinary neighborhoods and permit ordinary gluing. Arbitrary real subdivisions are not identified with strict formal-model skeletons.

The resulting functions are harmonic on the unchanged regions and near the curvature vertices. GJR 15.7 and 7.5 represent each real harmonic piece as a smooth affine function of finitely many integer harmonic coordinates. On each intervening annulus the function is a smooth convex Phi(t) of an invertible logarithmic coordinate. These are exactly harmonic-tropicalization weakly smooth descriptions. Finite Galois translates may be averaged to make the construction invariant, preserving positivity, approximation bounds and decreasing order; GJR 14.2 descends the resulting functions. Harmonic pieces and convex annular pieces are subharmonic, and ordinary sheafiness together with GR 3.4.1 and 4.1.2 gives classical positivity. Identically minus-infinity components have approximants -j.

The curvature formulas are zero on harmonic pieces and Phi''(t)d't wedge d''t on annular pieces, with Phi''≥0. Regularized maxima and subsequent convex compositions have positive semidefinite Hessians in these harmonic coordinates. Integer harmonic coordinates remain integer harmonic under every analytic morphism and scalar extension, by GJR 7.6. Thus these curvature formulas pull back and remain positive. A positive semidefinite Hessian is a sum of nonnegative rank-one forms ell wedge J ell. Against positive compactly supported tests of degree (d-1,d-1), the relevant Lagerberg cones coincide by CD 1.2.10. On a common harmonic chart the resulting top-degree form is nonnegative, and the tropical integration weights are nonnegative. Stokes gives <d'd''[g],eta>=integral d'd''g wedge eta≥0 on Hausdorff boundaryless pure-dimensional spaces. If the description is obtained after a finite Galois extension, GJR 14.5 gives equality of these integrals, without a degree factor. This proves positive strong currents and therefore positive CLD currents. It is a construction-specific positivity proof, not a general comparison of classical and current positivity for every weakly smooth function.

After any analytic extension L/K the coordinates pull back. Compositing an auxiliary finite Galois field with L gives a finite Galois extension of L, so the same integration argument proves positivity there. Classical positivity independently follows from GR 4.2(5). One constructed sequence is pulled back; a new extraction over L is unnecessary.

Proof at a nonsingular point. Normalization is an isomorphism there and is rig-smooth because K is perfect. Apply GJR 15.11 on a relatively compact ordinary smooth neighborhood, treating an identically minus-infinity component by constants, and use the countable extraction just proved.

Proof at a singular point. It is rigid. GR 3.9 allows shrinking to U with no other singularity, whose finite normalization is a disjoint union of finitely many connected smooth branches U_i, each with exactly one point x_i over x and an isomorphism away from those points. Choose the U_i relatively compact in slightly larger smooth neighborhoods inside a normalized strict affinoid neighborhood. They are second countable. Choose a compact strict affinoid neighborhood Y⊂U of x and put V=ordinary interior of Y and A_i=nu^{-1}(Y)∩U_i. Each A_i is compact. Write u_i=u∘nu and a=u(x)=u_i(x_i).

On each branch, GJR and countable extraction provide finite weakly smooth classical psh sequences s_{i,j} decreasing to u_i, with the curvature descriptions above. Use s_{i,j}=-j for an identically minus-infinity branch.

A weakly smooth function on a curve is locally constant at a rigid point. Its local preform uses finitely many integer harmonic coordinates, each strict PL. The proof of GR 3.9(6) records that at a rigid point a strict affinoid G-cover has a member containing an ordinary neighborhood and that invertible analytic absolute values are locally constant there. Hence each coordinate, and its smooth composition, is locally constant. We only need this assertion on the smooth normalization branches.

Let epsilon_0=1 and epsilon_j=8^{-j}. Upper semicontinuity bounds u_i above on the compact branch closures; choose a common constant h_{i,0}=H≥u_i+1. Inductively construct h_{i,j}≥u_i+epsilon_j, decreasing on A_i and taking the same value at all x_i.

If a is finite, choose late terms g_i of the branch sequences with g_i≤s_{i,j}, g_i<h_{i,j-1}-4epsilon_j on A_i, and a≤b_i=g_i(x_i)<a+epsilon_j. Compact domination applies because 4epsilon_j<epsilon_{j-1}; the point-value requirement follows from convergence. Put b=max_i b_i and h_{i,j}=g_i+(b-b_i)+epsilon_j. The upward shifts lie in [0,epsilon_j), and all node values equal b+epsilon_j. Moreover,
  u_i+epsilon_j≤h_{i,j}≤s_{i,j}+2epsilon_j,
  h_{i,j}<h_{i,j-1}-2epsilon_j on A_i.
These bounds prove decrease and convergence, including any other minus-infinity values. Constant shifts preserve all smoothness and positivity assertions.

If a=-infinity, choose c_j≤-j with c_j<min_A h_{j-1}-4epsilon_j. Choose late g_i≤s_{i,j} satisfying g_i<h_{i,j-1}-4epsilon_j on A_i and g_i(x_i)<c_j-epsilon_j. Set h_{i,j}=M_{epsilon_j}(g_i,c_j)+epsilon_j. It equals c_j+epsilon_j on an ordinary neighborhood of every x_i. The maximum bounds give
  u_i+epsilon_j≤h_{i,j}≤max(s_{i,j},c_j)+3epsilon_j/2,
  h_{i,j}<h_{i,j-1}-5epsilon_j/2 on A_i.
Since c_j→-infinity, these prove pointwise convergence on every branch, including identically minus-infinity branches. The convex-composition argument and curvature formulas prove classical positivity and positive strong currents.

Descent and ordinary overlaps. The h_{i,j} have a common constant value on neighborhoods of all x_i. Away from x use the normalization isomorphism. Near x, finiteness makes normalization closed and a quotient: remove the image of the closed complement of these neighborhoods to obtain an ordinary neighborhood downstairs where the descended function is constant. Thus the functions v_j on the fixed V are finite continuous and weakly smooth on actual ordinary neighborhoods, with compatible overlaps. GR finite surjective descent proves their classical positivity. Curvature is zero near the singularity and positive by the displayed formulas elsewhere. Decrease holds on the fixed compact normalization preimage, hence on the fixed V; the pointwise bounds give limit u. Scalar pullbacks have the properties already proved. For a finite continuous target, Dini on compact subsets gives local uniform convergence; no such convergence is claimed for arbitrary singular targets.

This includes unibranch cusps and nodal multibranch curves. It does not assert general weak-smooth descent along arbitrary finite morphisms, or a theorem over nonperfect fields. It is a draft mathematical proof, not a consequence of successful execution. The complete argument is retained in P02.3-argument.md, Sections 2–3, and P02.3-paper.tex.
