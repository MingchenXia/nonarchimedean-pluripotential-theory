Equivalently in this scope u is in the classical curve-test class, by the precise comparison in Gubler--Rabinoff, arXiv:2506.13548v2, Proposition 4.1.2; no current criterion for a discontinuous u is substituted. On an identically -infinity connected starting component use the ordinary smooth psh constants -j directly; no imported nondegeneracy convention is needed for that case.

Choose one connected compact strict affinoid tree domain Y inside W with x in U=Int(Y), and an ordinary open Omega with Y contained in Omega relatively compact in W. The local-tree lemma in the exact imported book packet p063 supplies Y; algebraic properness, algebraically closed K, and boundarylessness at x are retained. Thuillier Theorem 3.4.2 gives on Omega a decreasing directed family f_i of finite continuous lisse subharmonic functions with infimum u. If u is identically -infinity on Omega, take f_i=-i instead. Restrict every f_i to the SAME compact Y.

The exact imported proposition `prop:p063-tree-lisse-ordinary-regularization` gives, for every i and every epsilon>0, a finite g in C(Y), ordinary CLD-smooth and psh on U, with ||g-f_i||_Y<epsilon. ## 1. Abstract compact-domain net lemma

Suppose Y is compact Hausdorff, u=inf_i f_i for a decreasing directed family of finite continuous functions, and a class A contained in C(Y) is stable under addition of constants and uniformly approximates each f_i to every positive error. Define

    M_u={a in A : for some delta>0, a(y)>=u(y)+delta for all y in Y}.

The convention -infinity+delta=-infinity is used. A uniform approximant g to f_i with error epsilon, shifted by 2epsilon, belongs to M_u: f_i+epsilon <= g+2epsilon <= f_i+3epsilon. Thus M_u is nonempty.

It is downward directed in the actual pointwise order. Given a,b in M_u, choose delta>0 no larger than either available positive gap. Set t=min(a,b)-delta/2, a continuous comparison function, so t>=u+delta/2. The open sets

    V_i={y in Y : f_i(y)<t(y)}

increase with the directed index and cover Y. A finite subcover and one dominating index give f_i<t everywhere on Y. Choose epsilon<delta/6 and let c=g+2epsilon be a shifted A-approximant to this f_i. Then

    u+epsilon <= c <= f_i+3epsilon < min(a,b).

In particular c is in M_u and is later than both a and b. The minimum here is only a continuous comparison function; it is NOT asserted to be psh.

Index M_u by reverse pointwise order, and use its actual functions as the net. It decreases and its infimum is u. Indeed each member majorizes u. For a fixed y and finite R>u(y), first choose i with f_i(y)<R, then choose epsilon so small that f_i(y)+3epsilon<R. The shifted approximant is below R at y. If u(y)=-infinity, the same argument applies to every finite R. This proves convergence at all points, including rigid poles and type-4 points, without any countability assumption.

Apply this lemma to the fixed Y and the ordinary smooth psh class above. Every arbitrary u in the displayed curve scope is a pointwise decreasing NET of finite ordinary CLD-smooth psh functions on the SAME ordinary U. No residue-genus restriction, finite-residue-field condition, continuity of u, or metrizable-base-field assumption occurs in this net conclusion.

## 2. An exact functionwise sequence criterion on the compact Y

In the same abstract setup, a decreasing SEQUENCE from A converging pointwise to u on all of Y exists if and only if every strict sublevel {u<r}, r rational, is F_sigma in Y.

Necessity: if a_j is such a sequence, then

    {u<r}=union_j {a_j<r}
           =union_{j,k>=1} {a_j<=r-1/k}.

Every set in the last union is closed in Y. This argument uses actual continuity on Y, not just on its interior.

Sufficiency: write {u<r}=union_k F_{r,k}, with F_{r,k} closed in Y, hence compact. Since {u<r}=union_i {f_i<r}, compactness gives for each nonempty F_{r,k} finitely many indices covering it, and directedness gives one dominating index i(r,k) with f_i<r on F_{r,k}. Choose one arbitrary initial index as well. Enumerate all the countably many indices chosen for rational r and integers k and recursively take a dominating sequence j_n. Then f_{j_n} decreases to u on Y: if the infimum at y exceeded u(y), an intermediate rational r and a closed piece F_{r,k} containing y would contradict the domination of i(r,k). At u(y)=-infinity the same argument works for every rational r.

Put epsilon_n=4^{-n}. Choose g_n in A with ||g_n-f_{j_n}||_Y<epsilon_n and set a_n=g_n+2epsilon_n. Then

    f_{j_n}+epsilon_n <= a_n <= f_{j_n}+3epsilon_n,
    a_{n+1} <= f_{j_n}+3epsilon_n/4 < a_n.

Thus a_n decreases to u at every point of Y. The argument never takes a minimum of psh functions or diagonalizes approximations on shrinking neighborhoods.

This is a descriptive-topological criterion for a specified u on this compact auxiliary domain. It is NOT a geometric classification of all higher-dimensional analytic spaces. A sequence defined only on U need not extend continuously to this particular Y, so its necessity is not asserted for that weaker statement. If a sequence is given on an ordinary neighborhood of a smaller compact tree domain Y' inside U, its restriction gives the necessary F_sigma condition on Y'; conversely the criterion on any eligible Y' supplies a local sequence there.

If K has a countable dense subset, each strict affinoid Y is compact metrizable: countably many dense Tate-polynomial evaluations generate and separate its bounded seminorms. Every open subset of a compact metric space is F_sigma. Since u is upper semicontinuous, the strict sublevels are open. Thus EVERY arbitrary u in the displayed curve scope has such a local decreasing ordinary smooth psh SEQUENCE over countably dense K. If u is finite continuous on Y, compact Dini convergence of f_{j_n} and the displayed errors also give uniform convergence; no such uniform claim is made for a discontinuous or -infinity-valued target.

## 3. Overlaps and scalar extension

All functions above are actual functions on the fixed ordinary U, so their restrictions agree on overlaps inside U. Local constructions at different starting points are not claimed to give identical approximating nets on the intersection; local regularization requires local sections, not a global approximating section.

An ordinary smooth psh function remains ordinary smooth and psh under analytic pullback and completed analytic scalar extension: local analytic moment maps pull back to moment maps, and the smooth positive (1,1)-form pulls back to a positive form. The same pointwise inequalities pull back. The scalar projection is surjective (GR Lemma 2.12), so the pullback of the net/sequence converges at each point to the pullback of this particular u. The scalar domain is the inverse image of U. This is not an equality between an upstairs regularization family and all downstairs families, nor a claim that the auxiliary Y has the same boundary under every extension.

The comparison theorem identifies the pulled-back smooth psh functions as classical psh. No assertion that all Thuillier harmonic functions are ordinary smooth is needed: the moving-divisor and cap construction is the additional smoothing step.

## 4. Higher-dimensional saturated proper-fiber application

Let p:X->C^an be an analytically locally trivial projective-space bundle of relative dimension m>=1, let V be an ordinary open subset of the base, and let v be an arbitrary classical psh function on the ENTIRE saturated domain p^{-1}(V). Its restriction to each projective-space fiber is classical psh. The curvewise maximum principle on the compact connected boundaryless projective fiber forces that restriction to be constant (including the identically -infinity case). An analytic local section then gives an upper semicontinuous classical psh base function u on V, and v=u composed with p. Use the local curve conclusion on a fixed U inside V and pull back its actual smooth psh net/sequence to p^{-1}(U). This treats the full classical class on this saturated domain, including discontinuous and -infinity-valued functions. Pointwise order, limit, ordinary smoothness and psh pullback persist after scalar extension.

More precisely, over a base point c the fiber is P^m over the complete residue field H(c), which need not be algebraically closed. It is compact, connected and boundaryless over H(c). GR Theorem 6.1 applies over that field to the pulled-back classical psh function. If the function is not identically -infinity, upper semicontinuity on the compact fiber supplies a finite maximum M. Its locus {v=M} is nonempty and closed by upper semicontinuity and open by Theorem 6.1, so connectedness gives the whole fiber. This also checks nonrigid base points; no argument using only the rigid locus or an algebraically closed residue-field assumption is substituted.

This remains a saturated-domain result. An arbitrary psh germ on a nonsaturated subdomain of X can vary along the fiber and is not covered. Therefore no original P02.4 higher-dimensional sharp characterization or catalogue closure follows.
