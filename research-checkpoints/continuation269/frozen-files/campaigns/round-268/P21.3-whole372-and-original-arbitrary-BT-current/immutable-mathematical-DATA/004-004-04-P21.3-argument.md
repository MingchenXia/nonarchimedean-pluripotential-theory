# Strict real-PL recognition and ordinary Bedford–Taylor multipliers

Source-proof repair and partial progress on P21.3. Research draft, 3 October 2026.
The unrestricted atom remains unresolved. No priority or novelty claim is made.

## 1. Exact result and target

The target allows an arbitrary complete non-Archimedean field K, and a good,
separated, strict, boundaryless K-analytic space X, pure of dimension n >= 2.
It quantifies over every ordinary open W, every positive d'- and d''-closed
ordinary CLD (p,p)-current T, 0 <= p <= n-1, and every finite continuous CLD
psh pair U,V on W. It asks whether

    dd[U] = dd[V]  =>  dd(UT|W) = dd(VT|W),   dd = d'd''.

Here [f]=f delta_X is the function current, with the canonical generic
component multiplicities, and fT is the ordinary order-zero product.
Positivity throughout means CLD's middle positivity: symmetry and
nonnegative pairing against positive complementary test forms. We never
replace this by tests against only strongly positive forms.

The repaired result restricts K to NONTRIVIAL valuation and the FUNCTION
class to Gubler–Jell–Rabinoff's actual R-PL class. An integral PL function
is continuous and G-locally log|u| for analytic units u, using the G-topology
generated by STRICT affinoid domains. In CLD terminology this is STRICTLY
integral parlinear (12.5.2), not merely integral parlinear with arbitrary
real constants on possibly nonstrict domains. An R-PL function is G-locally
a finite real combination of such logarithms (GJR Definition 5.1).

**Main partial theorem.** Under the target's ambient hypotheses and
nontrivial valuation, for every ordinary open W and every GJR R-PL h on W:

1. If dd[h] >= 0, then h belongs to CPsh(W), with CPsh defined solely by
   CLD Definition 8.1.7. Its residue is nef at every point.
2. Conversely, a semipositive GJR R-PL function belongs to CPsh, and a CPsh
   function has positive Hessian current. Thus on this precise R-PL class
   positive current, residual nefness, and CPsh membership are equivalent.
3. If dd[h]=0, both h and -h are CPsh, and dd(hT)=0 for EVERY target
   positive doubly closed ordinary current T. T is neither approximated
   nor lifted to a strong current.
4. The last identity persists if locally, on one fixed ordinary neighbourhood,
   h is a locally uniform limit of a NET of GJR R-PL zero-current functions.
   This asserts multiplier closure, not CPsh net closure or density.

Consequently the target identity holds for all finite continuous pairs
whose difference lies in this class or the displayed net closure; the
incoming pair need not itself be converted from Psh to CPsh.

We also give an actual strict boundaryless smooth analytic surface and a
strict PL function h which is not ordinary CLD-smooth, yet has both signs
in CPsh and satisfies the multiplier identity for all target currents.
This is a worked obstruction to a proposed counterexample, not a
counterexample to P21.3.

## 2. Primary inputs and their exact scopes

References [CLD] and [GJR] mean respectively arXiv:1204.6277v2 (25 July
2025) and arXiv:2111.05741v3 (7 March 2025). Both primary texts and PDFs
were read locally. Bibliographic theorem numbers below refer to these
versions. The book's Appendix M is an input to audit, not a granted proof.
Review receipts identify issues; none is used as a mathematical premise.

The indispensable geometric inputs are as follows.

**G1 (ordinary PL decomposition and formal models).** On a good strict
space over complete nontrivially valued K, a GJR R-PL function has at every
point an ORDINARY open neighbourhood on which it is a finite real sum of
integral PL functions (GJR Proposition 5.8). Integral PL functions on a
paracompact space are weights of formal models of the trivial bundle
(5.6); the formal models are filtered by dominance (2.4). Corollary 5.9
gives arbitrarily small compact strict neighbourhoods and one formal model
for a finite presentation. No reducedness, smoothness, algebraic closure,
or discretely valued hypothesis is present here.

For clarity, the nontrivial ordinary-local step in 5.8 works as follows.
Take a finite strict G-cover around x and group all coefficients into a
Q-independent basis, clearing its finite rational denominators. On two
overlapping domains, concatenate the logarithms of units. The germ of
their tropical image has rational tangent directions (GJR 3.2–3.3).
Rational displacement vectors are dense in each polytope of that germ.
Equality of the original real combinations and Q-independence force the
individual logarithms, after subtracting their values at x, to agree on
the overlaps. Their differences are constant there. Evaluate those
constants at rigid points: they belong to Q Gamma, Gamma=log|K^*|.
One integer multiple places all finitely many constants in Gamma;
multiply the units by constants from K^*, then glue. Dividing that integer
back into the real coefficients gives integral PL summands on one ordinary
neighbourhood. Thus this is not a promotion of a G-cover into an open cover
without the needed argument.

**G2 (residues, models, and nefness).** For a good strict germ, its ungraded
reduction red(X,x) is a quasi-compact open in the Riemann–Zariski space
of tilde H(x)/tilde K. On a formal model Y of a compact strict
neighbourhood, the closure V_Y,x of red_Y(x) is a model of this reduction;
the restriction of a formal line bundle to V_Y,x represents the residue
(GJR 6.13, 6.16; CLD Lemmas 11.9.2 and 17.7.10). Residues are additive,
and extend real-linearly to R-PL germs (GJR Lemma 6.18). They are intrinsic
to the germ. The Picard group of the reduction is the filtered colimit
of model Picard groups (GJR 6.6). A proper dominating morphism gives
another model (6.2), and models are filtered (Lemma 6.5).

A line bundle, or a real class, on a model is nef iff its degree on every
proper integral curve is nonnegative. Nefness on the reduction can be
checked on ANY representing model (GJR Lemma 6.11). To check the latter
assertion, take a common model of a nef representative and the given
representative, then a further model on which their pullbacks agree in
Pic tensor R. Proper pullback preserves nefness by the curve projection
formula. Proper surjective descent also holds: a proper integral curve
on the base is dominated by a proper curve upstairs, obtained by taking
the closure of a closed point over its generic point. The projection
formula gives the converse inequality. This proves the model-independence
needed here, without choosing a preferred model of all reductions.

**G3 (strict lifting and properness).** At an inner point of a compact
strict neighbourhood Y, V_Y,x is proper over tilde K (CLD Lemma 11.9.2(d)).
By Chow's lemma a proper integral variety V_0 admits a proper birational
morphism V -> V_0 with V projective. It is still a reduction model by
the valuative criterion (GJR 6.2). Every line bundle on red(X,x) is the
strict residue of a STRICTLY integral metrized analytic line-bundle germ
whose underlying analytic line bundle is trivial near x (CLD Theorem
17.5.7(b), or Proposition 17.5.6). A chosen analytic frame therefore
provides a genuine integral GJR PL weight a, with the prescribed residue.
Good implies almost good, as required by that lifting theorem; strictness
and separatedness are retained.

The strict lifting proof is the degree-one version of CLD Proposition
17.4.7: use a finite strict-domain cover trivializing the residual bundle,
lift the degree-one residual transition isomorphisms to analytic
isomorphisms near x, and shrink finitely often so they are isometries on
the corresponding domain intersections (strict Lemma 17.5.3 and
Corollary 17.5.5). With one domain fixed, form the other transitions by
u_beta,gamma=u_alpha,beta^(-1)u_alpha,gamma; these satisfy the cocycle
identities exactly, rather than just modulo reduction. Glue the metrics.
For rank one the underlying bundle can be trivialized by the same
transitions on the ordinary germ. Local orthonormal frames have norm 1,
so the resulting weight is G-strict-locally a logarithm of a unit with
NO arbitrary real additive constant. This explains why this lifting step
has the strict scope needed for the denominator argument.

**G4 (cofinality and semipositive locus).** Given any reduction model V,
some compact strict neighbourhood Y and formal model have V_Y,x
dominating V (GJR Lemma 6.14). If two line-bundle models give the same
residue, their pullbacks agree after a further dominating model, by the
filtered-colimit description of Pic. Formal models and inclusions of
compact neighbourhoods can be made compatible by Raynaud dominance
(GJR 2.4, and the proof of 7.10).

For an R-PL function g, nefness of its residue at x is equivalent to
existence near x of a nef real formal model of the trivial bundle
(GJR Proposition 7.1). In particular the semipositive locus is ordinary
open (Remark 7.3(4)). Here is the relevant argument. Represent g by a
formal model on a compact strict neighbourhood and restrict to the
closure V of the reduction of x. It is nef by G2. Take a compact strict
neighbourhood inside red^(-1)(V), which is open by anticontinuity;
after an admissible blowup it is the generic fibre of a formal open.
Each proper integral curve in its special fibre maps into V. The
projection formula makes the restricted formal class nef. This also
shows openness at every point of that neighbourhood. Neither a
whole-space property nor an unrelated cover is substituted here.

**G5 (fixed-neighbourhood LFS approximation).** If a GJR R-PL g is
semipositive at an inner point x of a good strict space over nontrivially
valued K, there is one strictly analytic neighbourhood U and a SEQUENCE
of R-PL locally Fubini–Study functions g_j on U converging UNIFORMLY
to g there (GJR Proposition 7.10). LFS means that each residual class is
semiample (Definition 7.8). Locally such functions are nonnegative real
combinations of finite logarithmic maxima of analytic units (7.9).
The complete fixed-neighbourhood mechanism is reconstructed in section 4.

**G6 (the only imported curvature-recognition direction).** On a
boundaryless, topologically separated analytic space, CLD Theorem 18.8.4,
with d=1, says that positivity near x of the first Chern current of an
integral metrized line bundle implies numerical effectiveness of its
residual line bundle. We apply ONLY this implication and ONLY to genuinely
STRICTLY integral metrics. In this strict scope the degree-one residual
bundle is the GJR residue (CLD 10.6.2, 17.5.1, 17.7.10; GJR 6.16), and
numerical effectiveness is precisely nonnegative degree on proper curves
(CLD 10.4.11, 10.7.14; GJR 6.7–6.11). For the trivial bundle of weight b,
the Chern current is dd[b] (CLD 18.1.2). No converse nef-to-Psh statement
from CLD 18.8.8 or 17.8.4 is used.

The positive-to-nef part of the proof of 18.8.4 is independent of the
problematic approximation proof in 17.8.4. It fixes a residual premodel
by a finite logarithmic domain cover. For an integral residual curve C,
the tube above its generic point accumulates at x. Hence it meets any
ordinary neighbourhood on which the Chern current is positive. At a
point of this tube the residual dimension of the metric is at most one;
the dimension-one current formula is a positive analytic multiplicity
times deg_C of the residual bundle. Positivity makes this degree
nonnegative. Repeating for every C is the desired nef implication.
This use preserves source multiplicities and does not classify T.

Standard algebraic inputs used in G3/G5 are stated explicitly: (a) Chow's
lemma as above; (b) for A ample and M a line bundle on a projective variety,
A^N tensor M is ample for all sufficiently large integers N; and (c) a
nef real class plus a positive multiple of an ample class on a projective
variety is ample. These hold over an arbitrary residue field, and are
used only on projective varieties, not arbitrary proper analytic spaces.
For (b), sufficiently high twists of M and M^(-1) by a very ample bundle
are generated by global sections (Serre); the tensor product of a very
ample bundle and a globally generated bundle is very ample, as seen by
the product morphism followed by the Segre embedding. Treat the finitely
many residue classes of the exponent of A to obtain all sufficiently
large N. Input (c) is the projective ample/nef cone theorem, cited in
GJR's actual proof of 7.10 to Lazarsfeld, Positivity I, Corollary 1.4.10.
Its real-Picard version follows by placing the finite coefficient vector
in a small rational cube of ample classes: each rational vertex clears
to an ample integral bundle, and the original vector is their convex
combination. Thus the semiample meaning of GJR 7.8 is respected, including
real, rather than just numerical, representatives.

**C1 (ordinary calculus).** Good separated pure spaces have the canonical
CLD level-n calibrated analytic tropical structure, with analytic boundary
as tropical boundary (CLD Theorem 15.7.1 and 15.7.2). Their boundaryless
ordinary opens are locally differentially separated. Smooth forms and
currents restrict and form ordinary sheaves; compact supports have smooth
cutoffs and finite smooth localization (CLD 6.2, Proposition 7.1.7).
The fundamental integration current is positive and doubly closed by
Stokes, and retains generic nilpotent multiplicities. Logarithms of
analytic units are ordinary CLD-smooth with zero smooth Hessian.

**C2 (middle positivity).** Every symmetric compactly supported ordinary
smooth test is a difference of two compactly supported positive tests
(CLD Proposition 6.5.2; GJR Lemma 7.12). Compact supports in the good
analytic scope satisfy its support hypothesis. Wedges of positive forms
are positive, including a positive smooth (1,1)-form wedged with any
positive complementary test (CLD Proposition 3.5.5, Lemma 7.2.7).
These are middle-positive statements. We supply the multiplication bound
and one-factor argument below, using no assertion that strongly positive
forms span all symmetric forms.

## 3. Direct minimal-sheaf proofs

CPsh is the smallest ORDINARY subsheaf of finite continuous functions
containing smooth functions of positive smooth Hessian and closed under
uniform sequential limits on a fixed open (CLD Definition 8.1.7).

**Lemma 1 (operations).** This definition alone makes CPsh closed under
finite nonnegative real combinations and locally uniform sequential limits.

**Proof.** For fixed c>=0, the sheaf of continuous f such that cf is CPsh
contains the smooth positive-Hessian generators and is uniform-sequentially
closed. Minimality gives scaling (c=0 is immediate). For a fixed smooth
positive-Hessian s on an open O, the sheaf on O of continuous f for which
f+s is CPsh has these same properties. Thus f+s is CPsh whenever f is.
Now on any O consider the sheaf of continuous g with this property:
for every subopen V and every f in CPsh(V), f+g|V is CPsh(V). It is a
sheaf, is closed under uniform sequential limits, and contains every
smooth generator by the preceding argument. Minimality gives addition
of arbitrary CPsh sections. Iteration gives finite nonnegative sums.
All these arguments can be run on an open O: the minimal construction
commutes with restriction, since the generators and the uniform-closure
condition are local sheaf conditions. For locally uniform convergence,
take a compact neighbourhood of a point inside the given open. Convergence
is uniform on its interior; use the definition there and then sheaf
locality. Such compact neighbourhoods exist in our good separated scope.
QED.

No transfinite-stage identity involving Psh was used. The harmless inclusion
CPsh subset Psh, when needed for the worked psh pair in section 7, follows
directly as well: Psh is the minimal sheaf generated by smooth psh functions
and decreasing sequential limits (CLD 8.1.1). It is stable under translation
by constants, by the same minimality argument. If finite Psh functions f_j
converge uniformly to f, then f_j+2||f_j-f|| has a decreasing subsequence
converging to f. Indeed choose successive positive errors with e_next<e/3;
then f_next+2e_next <= f+3e_next <= f_j+e+3e_next <= f_j+2e.
If infinitely many errors are zero, use the constant subsequence instead.
The continuous part of Psh is therefore a uniform-closed sheaf containing
the generators; it contains CPsh. The REVERSE inclusion is not asserted.

**Lemma 2 (logarithmic maxima).** Each real-PL LFS function is locally CPsh.

**Proof.** The local LFS presentation in GJR 7.9 reduces, with Lemma 1,
to L=max(ell_1,...,ell_r), ell_i=+-log|u_i|, possibly with smooth
logarithmic-linear terms and positive denominators. All ell_i are ordinary
smooth and have zero smooth Hessian. On this same ordinary presentation
open define

    S_tau = tau log(sum_i exp(ell_i/tau)),    tau>0.

This is a smooth function of the logarithms of analytic units, hence an
ORDINARY CLD-smooth function. Put w_i=exp(ell_i/tau)/sum_k exp(ell_k/tau).
Its Euclidean Hessian is (diag(w)-w w^t)/tau, and for every real z,

    z^t Hess(S_tau) z
      = (sum_i w_i z_i^2 - (sum_i w_i z_i)^2)/tau
      = sum_{i<j} w_i w_j (z_i-z_j)^2/tau >= 0.

Composing with the common moment of the u_i preserves this positive
(1,1)-form. Equivalently, it is a sum of positive squares of d'(ell_i-ell_j)
with the corresponding d'' terms. There are no scalar Hessian terms from
ell_i. Also

    0 <= S_tau-L <= tau log r

EVERYWHERE on that presentation open, independently of bounds for ell_i.
Taking tau=1/j, Definition 8.1.7 makes L CPsh. Finite positive combinations
and logarithmic-linear terms follow from Lemma 1; ordinary locality gives
the result. This neither asserts harmonicity of S_tau nor uses
Psh intersect C = CPsh. QED.

For completeness, the local expression in 7.9 has a strict proof. Lift the
integral semiample classes in the finite nonnegative real residue
presentation to strict PL germs by G3. An integer power of each residue is
globally generated; strict Corollary 17.5.10 gives the weight of that power
as a finite maximum of logarithms of analytic units after choice of a
normalized frame. The frame normalization is possible in the strict lift:
its fibre has a degree-one basis, which lifts to an analytic frame of norm
1 at x (strict Corollary 17.5.5). Subtracting the lifted real combination
from the original function leaves zero real residue and is real-linear in
ordinary analytic-unit logarithms by GJR Lemma 6.18. Add this smooth
zero-Hessian term to the finite maxima. All neighbourhoods used for this
ONE finite LFS presentation can be intersected once. No approximation
index enters this local expression.

**Lemma 3 (ordinary product bound and one-factor positivity).** For fixed
positive T, locally uniform nets f_a -> f satisfy f_aT -> fT weakly.
For f in CPsh and T positive doubly closed, dd(fT) is positive doubly
closed, in every target degree.

**Proof.** If beta is a fixed symmetric compact test, use C2 to write
beta=beta_1-beta_2 with positive compact tests. Pairing T with a positive
test times a smooth scalar defines a positive scalar distribution; a
smooth cutoff equal to 1 near a compact support bounds this distribution
by its sup norm. Smooth scalar approximation and the Riesz representation
theorem extend it to a positive Radon measure mu_i. This is exactly the
ordinary product construction of CLD 7.2.8 and 7.2.11–12, restricted to
middle-positive T. Thus with C containing both supports,

    |< (f_a-f)T,beta >|
      <= ||f_a-f||_C <T,beta_1+beta_2>.

Symmetry removes the antisymmetric part of any test. This proves the
assertion for the original net. It also justifies the ordinary order-zero
products and all uniform limits used here without relying on the
strongly-positive spanning assertion in the broader wording of 7.2.9.

For the second assertion, on every open consider continuous f for which
dd(fT) is positive. These sets form an ordinary sheaf: positivity and
currents are local, with nonnegative smooth partitions on compact supports.
For smooth f of positive Hessian, the ordinary Leibniz formula and both
closedness equations give dd(fT)=dd f wedge T, positive by C2. Uniform
sequential limits preserve this property by the displayed product bound
applied to beta=dd alpha. Minimality in Definition 8.1.7 includes all CPsh
functions. Distributional identities d'^2=d''^2=0 and d'd''=-d''d' give
double closedness. This is the valid CPsh proof in CLD Lemma 8.5.5;
it needs no Bedford–Taylor premise and no broader Psh recognition. QED.

The same argument with T=delta_X shows that a CPsh function has positive
Hessian current. Both signs of a current being middle-positive imply it
is zero: positive pairings vanish, C2 handles every symmetric test, and
symmetry handles the rest (CLD Remark 7.2.3).

## 4. Fixed-neighbourhood reconstruction of GJR 7.10

**Lemma 4.** A semipositive GJR R-PL germ at an inner point has CPsh
representatives near that point, by uniform LFS approximation on ONE fixed
ordinary open.

**Proof.** Represent g by a real formal class L on a compact strict
neighbourhood Z. The closure of the reduction of x is proper. Choose a
projective proper birational dominating model V, on which a real class M
represents L_g(x), and choose an ample integral line bundle A on V.
By G2, M is nef. Lift A to a strict PL germ b by G3 and represent b by a
formal line bundle B on a compact strict neighbourhood. Use G4 and a
common formal model, shrinking finitely many times, so on a final compact
strict Z both g=h_L and b=h_B, and the reduction closure V' dominates V.
The restrictions of L and B to V' can be made EXACTLY the pullbacks of
M and A. Equality of their residues gives equality after a model refinement;
G4 realizes that refinement as a formal reduction closure. This finite
preparation is done before choosing any approximation parameter.

Let

    O = Int_X(Z) intersect red_Z^(-1)(V').

Anticontinuity makes O ordinary open, and it contains x. For any rational
eta>0 put g_eta=g+eta b. On V its residue representative M+eta A is
ample by the projective nef/ample theorem, hence semiample in the real
meaning of GJR 7.8. Its pullback to V' is semiample. For every y in O,
the closure of red_Z(y) is a closed integral subvariety of V'; restriction
of a semiample real class to it is semiample. That restriction represents
L_(g_eta)(y). Consequently g_eta is LFS at EVERY y of this SAME O.

The function b is continuous on compact Z, hence bounded. With eta=1/j,

    sup_O |g_(1/j)-g| <= ||b||_Z/j -> 0.

Lemma 2 gives CPsh membership for every g_(1/j) on O by ordinary locality.
Definition 8.1.7 now gives g in CPsh(O). The auxiliary compact space may
have a boundary; the current and CPsh conclusion is made only on the
boundaryless ordinary open O. We have intersected with Int_X(Z)
explicitly. No sequence of shrinking O_j is used. QED.

This reconstructs the decisive mechanism of the actual primary
Proposition 7.10, not the incomplete fixed-neighbourhood inference in the
older merely integral metric proof.

## 5. One genuine integral order unit and rational recognition

**Lemma 5 (order unit).** Given finitely many GJR integral PL g_i near x,
there are one bounded ordinary neighbourhood B of x, a bounded GJR
integral PL a on B, and one integer N>=1 such that, as ordinary currents,

    A0=dd[a] >= 0,   N A0 + dd[g_i] >= 0,   N A0 - dd[g_i] >= 0.

**Proof.** Choose a compact strict neighbourhood Y of x inside the
common ordinary domain of all g_i, with x inner. Choose one formal model
dominating the finite models of the g_i. On its proper reduction closure
V_0, their residues are represented by integral line bundles M_i,0.
Use Chow's lemma to get a projective V -> V_0; set M_i to be their
pullbacks. Choose A ample on V and lift its residue to a STRICT integral
PL germ a by G3. After one finite shrinking all functions are defined on
the same ordinary neighbourhood.

By the ample-twist property, choose one N for which A^N tensor M_i and
A^N tensor M_i^(-1) are ample for every i. Thus L_a(x) and
L_(Na+-g_i)(x), represented respectively by A and N A+-M_i, are nef.
By G4 their semipositive loci are open. Intersect the finitely many loci;
Lemma 4 makes a and each Na+-g_i CPsh locally there, hence on that
ordinary intersection. Lemma 3 with delta_X gives the displayed current
inequalities throughout it. Finally choose a compact strict neighbourhood
Y_1 of x inside the intersection and let B=Int_X(Y_1). All functions
extend continuously to Y_1 and are bounded on B. The constant N and
the ordinary open B are fixed once for all. No real constant is appended
to a: it remains genuinely integral strict PL. QED.

**Proof of positive-current recognition.** Write h=sum_i lambda_i g_i on
an ordinary neighbourhood of x using G1, and assume H=dd[h]>=0. Restrict
to the B of Lemma 5. Put B_i=dd[g_i]. Choose epsilon_m=1/m and rational
q_i,m so close that, with r_i,m=q_i,m-lambda_i,

    N sum_i |r_i,m| <= epsilon_m.

Set f_m=sum_i q_i,m g_i+epsilon_m a. The exact current identity is

    dd[f_m] = H + (epsilon_m-N sum_i |r_i,m|) A0
                + sum_(r_i,m!=0) |r_i,m| (N A0+sign(r_i,m) B_i) >= 0.

Every coefficient and every current on the right is nonnegative. This is
an inequality on all positive ordinary tests on the FIXED B; it is not
an inequality after tropical pushforward.

Choose an integer d_m clearing all rational q_i,m and epsilon_m. Then
d_m f_m is strictly integral PL: finite integer sums of strict-domain
logarithms of analytic units have that property on the common strict
G-refinement. No nonstrict covering or arbitrary constant is introduced.
The trivial bundle of norm exp(-d_m f_m) therefore lies in the genuine
strict scope of G6. At every y of B, dd[d_m f_m]>=0 implies its residual
line bundle is nef. Scaling by 1/d_m gives nefness of L_(f_m)(y).

Apply Lemma 4 LOCALLY AT EACH y to f_m. Its LFS approximation takes place
on a fixed neighbourhood of that y; the neighbourhood may depend on m.
This is sufficient: ordinary locality gives f_m in CPsh(B) for every m.
The FINAL limit f_m -> h is on the original, m-INDEPENDENT B, since

    ||f_m-h||_B <= sum_i |r_i,m| ||g_i||_B + epsilon_m ||a||_B -> 0.

Definition 8.1.7 gives h in CPsh(B), then ordinary locality gives
h in CPsh(W). The empty presentation gives zero and is immediate.

To see the residual conclusion independently of any CPsh-to-nef
recognition, use the same projective V of Lemma 5 at x. The class
sum_i q_i,m M_i+epsilon_m A represents L_(f_m)(x); it is nef on V by G2.
For EVERY proper integral curve C in V,

    sum_i q_i,m deg_C(M_i)+epsilon_m deg_C(A) >= 0.

Pass to the limit for each fixed C. The resulting inequality says that
sum_i lambda_i M_i, representing L_h(x), is nef. No compactness of the
set of curves or finite curve test is claimed. Repeat at every x.

Conversely residual nefness gives CPsh by Lemma 4, and CPsh gives
dd[h]>=0 by Lemma 3. This proves statements 1–2 of the main theorem.
QED.

## 6. Zero current, arbitrary T, and nets

If dd[h]=0, apply the preceding recognition to both h and -h. Both are
CPsh locally, hence on W. For arbitrary positive doubly closed T set
R=dd(hT). Lemma 3 makes R and dd((-h)T)=-R middle-positive. The pointedness
argument after that lemma gives R=0. Its test degree is
(n-p-1,n-p-1); for p=n-1 these are scalar tests and for p=0 they are
(n-1,n-1)-tests. All endpoints are included. Locality gives the identity
on W, and UT-VT=(U-V)T gives the target identity for the displayed pair
class. Generic nonreduced multiplicities have not been discarded.

Suppose on one fixed ordinary O a net h_a of GJR R-PL zero-current
functions converges locally uniformly to h. The proved identity holds
for each h_a and any fixed T. For a compactly supported test alpha,
use Lemma 3 on beta=dd alpha (and its symmetric part if needed):

    |<dd((h_a-h)T),alpha>| <= C_(T,alpha) ||h_a-h||_C -> 0.

The compact C and constant are fixed independently of a. Passing to the
limit proves dd(hT)=0. The same estimate for delta_X also gives dd[h]=0,
but does NOT give a real-PL presentation or CPsh membership for h.
Nothing replaces the directed net by a sequence. Applying this locally
and then current locality proves statement 4.

## 7. A concrete nonsmooth analytic surface test

Let K=C((t)) with |t|=e^(-1). Let E be a smooth elliptic curve over C,
with origin o, and choose z in E(C) of infinite order. Such z exists
because E(C) is uncountable while its torsion subgroup is countable.
Let C=(E tensor_C K)^an, with its good-reduction smooth proper model
over C[[t]]. Let x be the divisorial/Gauss point of the special fibre;
the t-adic valuation of that fibre gives this analytic point, and
tilde H(x)=C(E). Its strict germ reduction is represented by the proper
smooth curve E (G2), and Pic(red(C,x))=Pic(E). Indeed the models with
function field C(E) are dominated by the normal proper curve E;
proper birational maps between normal proper curves are isomorphisms.
Thus filtered model Picard groups identify this reduction Picard group
with Pic(E).

The residual line bundle M=O_E(z-o) has degree zero and is non-torsion.
Strict lifting G3 supplies an integral strict PL germ g at x, with
residue M. Both M and M^(-1) are nef on E. G4 gives an ordinary open
B around x on which g and -g are semipositive. Lemma 4 gives both signs
in CPsh(B), hence dd[g]=0 by Lemma 3 and pointedness.

The function g is NOT ordinary CLD-smooth near x. One can use the strict
statement CLD Proposition 17.5.11 (smooth strict metric iff torsion
residue), but the needed direction also has this direct proof. For a
smooth parlinear function, the VALID FIRST PART of CLD Proposition
12.5.4 gives an integer k, an analytic unit u, and a real constant c
with kg=c+log|u| on an ordinary neighbourhood. This follows from a
smooth moment presentation: a smooth rational-PL function is locally
rational-affine, so one clears the slope denominators. Choose a rigid
point y there. Since g is STRICT integral PL and H(y)/K is finite,
g(y) and log|u(y)| are in Q log|K^*|=Q. Consequently c is rational,
and after an integer l, lc=log|b| for b in K^*. Therefore
lk g=log|b u^l| and its residue is zero by GJR Lemma 6.17. This
contradicts non-torsion of M. The argument uses strict values at the
rigid point; it does not accept the broader real-constant normalization
for merely integral functions.

Let D be the standard strict open unit disc and set X=B times_K D,
h=g composed with the projection pi:X->B. This is an ANALYTIC fibre
product, with no identification with a topological cartesian product.
It is good, separated, strict, smooth, boundaryless, and pure of
dimension two. Its section s(b)=(b,0) is analytic.

CPsh pulls back through analytic maps in this precise situation:
smooth positive-Hessian generators pull back as ordinary CLD forms,
uniform sequential limits pull back uniformly, and the condition is
local. Minimality therefore gives pi^*(CPsh_B) subset CPsh_X. Both
h and -h are CPsh. Thus dd[h]=0 and dd(hT)=0 for EVERY positive doubly
closed target current (p=0 or 1). This derives the Hessian equation
from positivity of both pullback functions; it does not pull back an
ordinary current equation. Also h cannot be ordinary CLD-smooth at
s(x), since s^*h=g and ordinary smooth forms pull back analytically.
The pair U=h,V=0 is finite continuous and CLD psh, by the safe inclusion
CPsh subset Psh proved in section 3. It has equal curvature and satisfies
Bedford–Taylor independence on every ordinary subopen of X for this
specified pair. There is no assertion for every other pair on X.

## 8. Source repair, comparison, and residual gap

The old broad class allowed c+log|u| on nonstrict G-domains. This paper
DROPS that class. Clearing rational slopes in it does not make its
metrics strict. For instance max(0,log|z|-c), with c irrational relative
to log|Q_p^*|, keeps an inadmissible strict value-group break after
denominator clearing. The present functions instead start with GJR
Definition 5.1, whose strict integral summands match CLD 12.5.2 exactly.

CLD Corollary 18.8.8's general integral recognition and Proposition
8.1.11's equality Psh intersect C=CPsh are NOT assumed, repaired, or
asserted here. The nef-to-psh citation mismatch and the discontinuous
successor approximants in 8.1.11 remain outside this argument. We have
used only the strict positive-to-nef direction of 18.8.4, direct minimal
CPsh operations, fixed-neighbourhood GJR 7.10, and explicit soft maxima.
The previous rational-cube argument is unnecessary for this repair.
The earlier source-side split audit does not resolve those broader gaps
and is not used to certify the current paper.

The mathematical advance over the prior two-sign criterion is recognition
for the entire ACTUAL GJR real-PL class, including sums with nonzero
individual Hessians that cancel. The order unit and rational corrections
handle positive current as well as zero current. The proof establishes
ordinary CPsh membership, not only classical curve-test psh or weak
smoothness. The source already contains a real-PL-to-residual-harmonic

The result retains every target current but restricts the pair difference
