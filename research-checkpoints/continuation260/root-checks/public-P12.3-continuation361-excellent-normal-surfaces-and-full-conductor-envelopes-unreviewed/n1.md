For a proper maximal-primary ideal a, X_a consists of ALL centred real semivaluations v with v(a)=1; prime supports below the maximal ideal are retained. This is compact Hausdorff. For finite continuous phi set p_C=sup{g in C:g<=phi} and p_D=sup{u in D:u<=phi}. A negative primary constant bounds both below. If u=inf g_i<=phi and epsilon>0, the increasing open sets {g_i<phi+epsilon} cover X. Compactness and directedness select one index globally. Then g_i-epsilon is a C-minorant and is at least u-epsilon. Therefore p_C=p_D=p, finite and lower semicontinuous. If p is continuous, the open sets {g>p-epsilon}, over its C-minorants, cover X; a finite maximum approximates p uniformly from below. Thus p is in C. Conversely p in D is upper semicontinuous and hence continuous. A greatest D-minorant exists exactly when p is continuous.

Define p*(x)=inf over open U containing x of sup_U p. Since phi is continuous, p<=p*<=min(phi,0). If p* is in D it is an admissible competitor, forcing p*=p; hence it is geometric exactly when p is continuous. Monotonicity and downward shifts of competitors give ||P phi-P psi||<=||phi-psi||. These are raw identities, without presuming output positivity.

There is an actual all-support graded ideal formula. For k>=1 put J_k(phi)={f in R:v(f)>=-k phi(v) for EVERY v in X}. Semivaluation axioms make this an ideal. With M>=max(0,-min phi), it contains a^ceil(kM); when the exponent is zero one can instead use a. If phi is negative somewhere, units are excluded and J_k is proper primary; if phi>=0 everywhere it is R. It is integrally closed: in a monic ideal-integrality equation for f, a violation v(f)<-k phi(v) gives a unique finite least term, namely the leading power of f, which cannot sum to zero. Quotient by the semivaluation support justifies this even for nonminimal supports. Noetherianity gives finite generation of each J_k, not of their graded algebra.

J_k J_l is contained in J_(k+l), and J_k^l in J_(kl). Consequently -v(J_(n!))/(n!) increases and has supremum p. To prove equality, approximate any C-minorant g rationally by -v(I)/s and subtract a small positive rational primary constant, realized by multiplying powers of I and a after a common denominator. This gives an admissible rational profile within any prescribed error below g. Its underlying ideal lies in the corresponding J_s; every sufficiently large factorial is divisible by s. The inclusion of powers proves its profile is below the factorial profile. Sending the error to zero proves the formula on ALL X. This pointwise increasing formula alone proves no continuity.
