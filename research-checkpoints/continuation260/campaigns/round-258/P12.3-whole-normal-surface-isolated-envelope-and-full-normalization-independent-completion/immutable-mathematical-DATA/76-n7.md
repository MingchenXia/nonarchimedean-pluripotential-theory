# n7. Full-fibre maxima and finite-cover envelopes over normal targets

Use G331 and n1--n6. Let A be an excellent normal local domain and B a finite reduced injection whose EVERY minimal component dominates A. All marked semilocal source centres and all semivaluation supports are included; no vertical component is assigned a generic degree. G331's coefficient norm construction gives a positive normalized trace for a dominating domain source, with a finite positive weight at EVERY point of the FULL restriction fibre. For several dominating components sum their generic-degree traces and normalize by the sum of their degrees. The result is continuous, preserves C and D, is a left inverse to pullback, and has positive finite weights on the full original B-fibre. These are conclusions inside G331, not a flat or defectless ramification formula.

A second, useful route explicitly constructs the fibre maximum. For one component B/P choose a finite normal field extension M/K containing its field and every embedding image, including inseparability, and let E be A's finite integral closure in M. Let G=Aut_K(M) and q=[M:K]/|G|. The coefficient norm on E, at ANY base semivaluation and ANY lift w, has measure q sum_(sigma in G) delta_(w composed with sigma). This follows by factorizing the norm polynomial, taking quotient-support Gauss coefficient minima, and then using the dense signed ideal lattice, exactly as proved in G331. Independence of that measure and its full-fibre support show that G is transitive on the ENTIRE E/A fibre, including nonminimal supports. For H in C_B, restrict to B/P, pull back to E, and take max_(sigma in G) sigma*H. It is in C_E and fibre constant over A, so positive descent gives a C_A function equal to the maximum of H on this component's full fibre. Finite maximum over the dominating components gives MH(v)=max_(rho(w)=v)H(w) in C_A. For H in D_B, use its actual decreasing C-net. Finite-fibre directedness gives max_w inf_alpha H_alpha(w)=inf_alpha max_w H_alpha(w), including bottom: simultaneous strict tests can be met at finitely many points. Therefore MH is in D_A by an actual C representation. This proof is specific to normal targets and dominating components.

For K=C or D and a finite bounded obstacle f on A, H<=rho*f implies MH<=f and H<=rho*(MH). Pullback gives the opposite inequality of competitor suprema. Consequently the UNRESTRICTED raw envelopes satisfy P_B^K(rho*f)=rho*P_A^K(f) on all supports, even before a regularity theorem. This is stronger than a merely fibre-constrained identity, within the stated normal/dominating scope.

Suppose now the marked source centres have the continuous-envelope property, proved for normal surfaces by n5 or for the exact smooth BFJ range by n6. For continuous f set U=P_B(rho*f) in C_B. Its normalized positive average p is in C_A and below f. Every target D-minorant u pulls back below U, so u<=p. Moreover rho*p is an admissible source minorant and hence rho*p<=U. On a fibre p is a positive weighted average of the finite values U(w), and is <= each of them. Each value must equal that average. Thus rho*p=U and p=P_A(f) is the greatest continuous geometric envelope. No extended-bottom average is used to force this equality. For bounded-above USC obstacles take ONE net of continuous strict majorants as in n5; its pullback infimum is a source D-envelope by greatestness against every source minorant. This proves pullback commutation and target positivity, together with the regularity, gauge and decreasing-net rules in n5.

There is a concrete NONFLAT example. For an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, A=k[U,V,W]_(m)/(UW-V^2) is covered by B=k[x,y]_(x,y), with U=x^2,V=xy,W=y^2. The ring A is normal by the parity-lattice intersection argument in n10; B is finite of generic degree two, with a single marked centre. The closed fibre has basis 1,x,y, since m B=(x,y)^2, and length three. A finite flat algebra of rank two would have fibre dimension two, so this cover is not flat. It still satisfies the theorem. The same surface cover works in every characteristic by n5. Adding any finite number of independent smooth variables gives higher-dimensional characteristic-zero examples by n6. These are finite-cover sectors, not a theorem that every normal excellent germ admits a suitable cover. The field/Galois argument itself is characteristic-independent; its higher-dimensional source regularity remains an explicit hypothesis.
