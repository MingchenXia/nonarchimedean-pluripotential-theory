# n8. Full conductor criterion and the all-characteristic nonnormal obstruction

Use G331, n1, n5 and n7. Let S be the finite normalization of excellent reduced R, split at ALL marked normal centres, and rho its full compact restriction quotient. At finite conductor value fibres are singleton; at infinite value their exact rule is restriction to the conductor quotient, including fibres internal to a single centre. Positive C/D descent is precisely constancy on these whole fibres. Therefore the downstairs raw envelope pulls back to the supremum of upstairs minorants which are fibre constant. An unrestricted upstairs envelope cannot be descended merely because its obstacle descends.

For finite continuous phi let p_S be the unrestricted upstairs raw envelope. The exact criterion is
\[
p_R^*\in D_R\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p_S\hbox{ is continuous and constant on every full fibre}.
\]
To prove necessity, first show equality of constrained and unrestricted raw suprema on the finite-conductor locus. Choose B>0 with -B<=phi, and clamp an upstairs C-minorant g into [-B,0]. The ACTUAL downstairs primary ideal K_N=c+a^N gives b_N=-(B/N)v(K_N), in [-B,0], equal to -B at conductor-infinite points. Then max(g+rho*b_N,-B) is in C_S, below rho*phi and constant on every fibre, since it is -B on the entire nontrivial-fibre stratum. It descends positively. At finite conductor value b_N tends to zero, proving the desired equality. This locus is dense in each normal centre: the conductor has a regular element, every fraction-field divisorial valuation has finite value on it, and G331 proves all-support divisorial density. If p_R* is in D, n1 makes p_R continuous. Its continuous pullback u<=p_S equals p_S on this dense locus. For a net from the locus converging to any point, lower semicontinuity of p_S gives p_S<=u. Thus equality holds everywhere and yields necessity. Conversely continuous p_S belongs to C_S by n1; full-fibre constancy and positive descent make it an admissible greatest downstairs envelope. Normal continuity is a CONDITION here; n5 and n7 supply it only in their proved sectors.

For ANY field let R=k[x,y,z]_(m)/(xy), with normalization A times B, where A=k[x,z]_(x,z), B=k[y,z]_(y,z). It is the fibre product over k[z]_(z): polynomial pairs with equal residue are represented by f+g-h, and local unit denominators can be cleared by a pair whose common residue is the product of their two unit residues. Its conductor is (x)A times(y)B. The only identified boundary pair is q_A killing x, and q_B killing y, both with z-order one. Their downstairs support (x,y) is nonminimal.

The continuous strictly negative obstacle phi=-1-1/v(y), with reciprocal infinity zero, is -1 on A and -1-1/w(y) on B. On B every centred normalized w satisfies w(f)>=ord_mB(f), hence every g in D_B satisfies g(w)<=g(o_B), first on ideal profiles, then by uniform and actual decreasing limits. Since phi(o_B)=-2, every minorant is <=-2 throughout B. The unrestricted normalization envelope is the continuous tuple (-1,-2), incompatible at q_A,q_B.

For n>=1 the A-primary ideal J_n=(x^n,xz^(n-1),z^(2n)), paired with constant -2 on B, gives a fibre-compatible C tuple and hence a positive downstairs C-minorant. Write a=w(x), b=w(z), min(a,b)=1. Its A profile is -1 when a<=b; for a>b finite it is -min(1+(a-1)/n,2); at q_A it is -2. For n=1 the middle generator is literally x, avoiding 0 times infinity. Their increasing supremum is -1 on A minus q_A and -2 at q and throughout B. The obstacle on A and the universal B inequality give these exact upper bounds for EVERY D-minorant. Monomial valuations with x-weight tending to infinity and z-weight one converge to q_A on every localized polynomial, so that raw envelope is not USC. N1 rules out a greatest geometric minorant or a geometric USC regularization, although the regularization stays below phi. In fact there is no maximal minorant: a maximal member would dominate every competitor by finite maxima, and equal the non-USC supremum. Thus nonnormal conductor descent can obstruct even a bounded strictly negative obstacle with smooth normalization.

If a normalization fibre meets TWO distinct positive-dimensional normal centres, there is also a general obstruction. Choose a centred divisorial point nu on one centre with finite conductor value; its downstairs singleton image is disjoint from the closed images of all other centres. A continuous obstacle in [-1,0], equal to -1 there and zero on those other images, exists by compact Hausdorff separation. On a second centre its unrestricted envelope is zero. On the first, G331's fixed-divisorial inequality g(nu)>=K g(w), K>0 independent of g and w, forces every minorant at the specified conductor-fibre point to be <=-1/K. Thus the separated raw envelope is not fibre constant, and the criterion excludes positive downstairs regularization. This does not classify internal fibres of one centre and does not prove anything impossible for the residual normal target.
