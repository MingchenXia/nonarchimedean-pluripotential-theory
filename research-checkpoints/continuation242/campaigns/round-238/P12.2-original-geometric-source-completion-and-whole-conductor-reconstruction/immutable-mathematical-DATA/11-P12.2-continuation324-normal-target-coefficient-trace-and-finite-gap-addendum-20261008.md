## 1. Exact domain and finite integral algebra

Let A be an excellent normal local domain, a a proper primary ideal, and A subset D a finite domain extension, with K=Frac(A), L=Frac(D), n=[L:K]. D need not be normal or local. On its finite disjoint closed-center section W=V_(aD)(D), use the product continuous uniform negative ideal cone C_D. Componentwise unit ideals are allowed. Every rational tuple of proper-primary/unit profiles is represented by a single global D-ideal J containing (aD)^N: contract each local primary ideal, intersect the contractions, and take a common power. Localization at a different maximal center makes such a contracted primary ideal the unit ideal. Common denominators synchronize rational tuple scales. Uniform closure gives the entire product cone. This argument uses closed centers, not a decomposition merely by irreducible components.

Choose a finite field extension M/K containing every K-embedding image of L and splitting the relevant algebraic conjugates. There are s separable embeddings sigma:L->M and a common generic inseparable multiplicity q with qs=n. Let E be the integral closure of A in M. Excellence/Nagata makes E finite over A. Every sigma(D) belongs to E because D is integral over A. We do NOT extend a semivaluation with nonzero support to K.

For a finite A-module generating tuple b_1,...,b_r of J define the coefficient ideal N(J) in A of

    Norm_(L(T)/K(T))(sum T_i b_i)
       = product_sigma (sum T_i sigma(b_i))^q.             (1)

Coefficients are in K and integral over A, so normality puts them in A. Replacing the generating tuple makes a linear substitution with A-coefficients in both directions, which gives the same coefficient ideal. Hence it is generator independent. Include A-module generators of a^N D among the tuple. For each generator c of a^N in A, the coefficient of its own variable's nth power is c^n. Thus N(J) contains a power of a and is either primary or the unit ideal. This is an actual finite ideal; no defectless valuation extension or flatness is asserted.

## 2. Extend the semivaluation to E, not to the original fraction field

Fix v in V_a(A), allowing its prime support P to be nonzero. Finite integral restriction gives a semivaluation w of E extending v and normalized by aE. For completeness choose a prime Q over P, form the finite injection A/P->E/Q and its finite fraction-field extension, extend the valuation induced by v there, and compose with E->E/Q. Integral elements have nonnegative values. Its center is a closed point above m because E is finite over local A and w(a)=1. This proves the needed lift while retaining its support.

Compose w with each sigma on D to get w_sigma. These are points of W. At every such v,

    v(N(J))=q sum_sigma w_sigma(J).                        (2)

The key is multiplicativity of the minimum coefficient value for polynomials under a semivaluation. Factor by its support to a domain carrying a valuation, then divide each polynomial by a coefficient of least finite value in that quotient's fraction field. The reduced polynomials over its residue field are nonzero; their product is nonzero. Therefore the product coefficient minimum is the sum of the factor minima. This fraction-field division takes place ONLY after quotienting by the support; it is not an extension of v to K. Every factor in (1) has a finite minimum because J contains a boundary power. The same proof works for q-fold factors and all characteristic. No infinity-minus-infinity occurs.

Since the coefficients in A restrict to v, their minima computed by w are exactly v(N(J)). Since b_i generate J over A, their minimum after each sigma is w_sigma(J). Equation (2) follows. It uses generic inseparable multiplicity and total degree n; no sum of ramification/residue degrees or valuation-defect shortcut is needed.

For each v and one chosen lift w this defines a finite positive measure mu_v=q sum_sigma delta_(w_sigma) on its fiber, of total mass n. Choice independence is established next, not inferred from a generic field norm at a nonminimal support.

## 3. Correct difference lattice and a continuous trace

The bounded ideal gauges w(J), with J containing boundary powers, separate points of W. If two semivaluations differ on a nonunit f, compare min(w(f),N)=w((f)+(aD)^N) with N large; this also distinguishes a finite value from infinity. Distinct closed centers are distinguished by an ideal primary at one center and unit at the others. Units and zero have their stipulated common values.

Their RATIONAL difference span is a vector lattice over Q. After common denominators each difference is (w(I)-w(J))/k. For two such differences min is obtained by the ideal-sum identity

    min(w(I)-w(J),w(K)-w(L))
        = w(IL+KJ)-w(JL).                                 (3)

Products/sums still contain boundary powers, with units allowed. Max follows by negation. Rational constants are present because w(aD)=1. The UNIFORM closure of this rational lattice is a REAL vector lattice containing all real constants, since the lattice operations and scalar approximation are uniformly continuous. Its point separation and the elementary lattice Stone--Weierstrass argument yield all C(W). Explicitly approximate a continuous function at two chosen points by an affine combination of a separating function, then take finite maxima/minima using compactness to control neighborhoods; arbitrary uniform errors follow. We do NOT claim that arbitrary real finite sums are exactly converted to integer ideal products.

For each ideal gauge the pairing with mu_v equals the fixed downstairs gauge v(N(J)) by (2), independent of the chosen lift w. Equal mass and density of the rational difference lattice make the pairings equal on every continuous function. Thus mu_v is choice independent in precisely the sense required for its operator, with no Radon representation theorem necessary for the finite measures.

Define Tf(v)=integral f dmu_v for f continuous on W. It is linear, positive and bounded with norm n. On each ideal gauge it is the continuous gauge of the primary/unit ideal N(J). Uniformly approximating arbitrary f by the difference lattice shows Tf is a uniform limit of continuous downstairs functions, hence continuous on V_a(A). Nonminimal supports remain included in this argument. For a negative profile -w(J)/k, T gives -v(N(J))/k; positive combinations and uniform closure prove T(C_D) subset C_A. Dividing by n is an allowed positive real scale. Choice independence and continuity use exact coefficient ideals, not a presumed unrestricted generic-degree trace on arbitrary nonnormal targets.

## 4. Completed functions and arbitrary reduced sources over a normal target

Indeed if a summand tends to minus infinity the sum does also; otherwise all finitely many limits are finite and directedness gives simultaneous approximation. Therefore

    inf_i Tf_i(v)/n = u_bar(v).
