\begin{abstract}
We prove an explicit necessary-and-sufficient infinite dual criterion for real envelope regularity in the finite globally embedded BMPS category. The criterion consists solely of rational cell incidence and point/ray data, the actual reference coefficients, rational matching spaces, finite support matrices and their multipliers, and a costed weak-star cone in the full dual of $C_b(X)$. It does not assume PA approximation, feasibility for a non-PA target, a refinement-uniform constant, or a free attachment presentation. We independently prove the proposed costed Farkas theorem and finite multiplier theorem. A new compatible, not necessarily PC, rational interpolation construction bridges the original finite real-PA obstacle property to the all-$C_b$ property; unrestricted competitors are treated using qualified R230. We also reconstruct an actual literal obstacle and nonregularizable envelope for every positive-dimensional admitted literal pair in this category. Qualified B225 excludes compact positive-dimensional embedded balanced supports. Zero-dimensional pairs have regularity in both coefficient categories. This is an exact source-domain classification by an infinite certificate, not a finite combinatorial test or a theorem of universal real regularity. No Monge--Ampere or contact conclusion follows. The catalogue's possible intended category beyond its stated BMPS definitions is discussed explicitly; no chart or compactification theorem is inferred from an embedded theorem.
\end{abstract}

 N_\sigma=N\cap\operatorname{span}_\R(\sigma-\sigma).

 \sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}w_\sigma n_{\sigma/\tau}\in N_{\tau,\R}

 E_\gamma(u)(x)=\sup\{q(x):q\in\PC,\ q\leq\gamma+u,\

 E_\gamma(u)\in\overline{\mathcal A_*(\gamma)}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}

\]

\[
 J_P(h)=h(y)-\sum_i\alpha_i h(x_i),\qquad
 b_P=\sum_i\alpha_i\gamma(x_i)-\gamma(y)\geq0.                        \tag{1.4}
\]

\[
 D_\gamma=\overline{\cone\{(J_P,b_P),(0,1):P\text{ legal}\}}
 ^{\sigma(B^*,B)\times\mathrm{usual}}.                              \tag{1.5}

\begin{definition}[Costed all-carrier certificate criterion]\label{d:criterion}
The pair satisfies $\mathfrak C(X,\gamma)$ if, for every $\ell\in B^*$ and $t\in\R$, the following implication holds:
\[
\begin{split}
 &\text{for every }\mathcal C\text{ there exists }\lambda_\mathcal C\geq0
 \text{ with}\quad A_\mathcal C^T\lambda_\mathcal C=a_\mathcal C(\ell),\quad
 N_\mathcal C^T\lambda_\mathcal C=0,\quad
 d_\mathcal C^T\lambda_\mathcal C\leq t\\
 &\hspace{30mm}\Longrightarrow (\ell,t)\in D_\gamma .
\end{split}\tag{1.6}
\]
\end{definition}
All objects in this definition are constructed from rational cells, their actual incidence, the lattice and matching equations, the actual $\gamma$, point evaluations and an explicitly specified bounded linear functional. No target approximation or envelope appears in the condition.

\begin{theorem}[Coefficient-separated source-domain characterization]\label{t:main}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Every zero-dimensional pair has (1.3) in both coefficient branches, and has the corresponding all-$C_b$ property.
\item Every positive-dimensional admitted literal pair fails (1.3). Its support is noncompact. A bounded literal obstacle with an actual nonregularizable envelope is constructed in Section~\ref{s:literal}.
\item For every admitted actual real reference, (1.3), its all-$C_b$ version, and $\mathfrak C(X,\gamma)$ are equivalent. All resulting envelopes have genuine strictly decreasing uniform real-PA PC approximants on finite rational carriers, with one relative bound and the actual recession.
\end{enumerate}
There are no positive-dimensional compact balanced supports in this category. Part (3) includes arbitrary proper support cells, nonfree arrangements, incidence loops and all bounded continuous obstacles. It gives an infinite dual classification, not universal satisfaction of the criterion.
\end{theorem}

\section{Finite polyhedral and separation foundations}
We use real completeness, finite-dimensional compactness, ordinary linear algebra and convex secants. To avoid an unspecified essential external separation or LP theorem, the needed forms are proved here. Zorn's lemma is the stated usual set-theoretic maximality principle.

\begin{lemma}[Finite generation, closed cones, and projection]\label{l:poly}
A finite conic hull is closed. Every nonempty polyhedron is a finite point hull plus a finite recession cone; rational rows give rational generators, including both signs for lineality. Projections and finite point/cone descriptions are polyhedra. Finite rational hyperplane subdivisions have common rational refinements.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Eliminate dependencies in a nonnegative generator representation: for $\sum_i b_i v_i=0$ with some $b_i>0$, subtract $s b_i$ from its active coefficients, where $s=\min_{b_i>0}a_i/b_i$. This preserves nonnegativity and removes a column. Repeat to an independent subset. Each independent column inverse bounds its coefficient sum by a constant times the represented vector's norm. There are finitely many subsets. A convergent represented sequence has a subsequence with one subset and bounded convergent coefficients, proving cone closedness.

For a cone described by homogeneous inequalities, remove their common kernel as lineality. In a complementary space the sum of its nonnegative defining forms is positive on every nonzero cone vector. Its section at one is bounded: normalize an unbounded sequence and pass to a unit limit to obtain a forbidden joint-kernel vector. A bounded polyhedron is its vertex hull: at a nonvertex move in both signs in the kernel of the active rows until another independent row is active, express the original point between those endpoints, and induct on active rank. Finitely many full-rank systems give the vertices, rational for rational rows. Restore a signed basis of lineality. Homogenize $Ax\leq b$ to $Au\leq sb$, $s\geq0$; positive-$s$ generators normalize to points and zero-$s$ generators are recession directions. This proves the asserted representation, including relative affine hulls and points. If the polyhedron is pointed, its homogenized cone is pointed as well. Choose its generators on extreme rays, obtained from vertices of the bounded section just constructed. A positive-$s$ extreme generator normalizes to a vertex of the original polyhedron: any nontrivial segment decomposition of that point would decompose its lifted ray into two nonproportional cone vectors. Hence a pointed polyhedron is the hull of its finite vertex list plus its recession cone, as used below.

Conversely introduce nonnegative representation variables, sum-one for point coefficients and coordinate equations for their image. Eliminate a variable by pairing every lower bound with every upper bound, retaining independent rows. One missing side imposes no paired condition. These finite weak inequalities exactly assert that the allowed interval is nonempty. Repetition proves projection and finite-description claims, preserving rationality. A finite rational ambient hyperplane arrangement subdivides every rational polyhedron whose wall equations are in the list. Extend equations on a rational affine hull to ambient rational equations by a rational basis. Taking the union of the lists gives the common refinement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Continuous separation with exact topologies]\label{l:hb}
If $D$ is a closed convex cone in a Hausdorff locally convex real space and $z\notin D$, a continuous linear $L$ satisfies $L(D)\leq0<L(z)$. If $L_0$ is a nonempty closed convex subset of a normed space containing zero and $z\notin L_0$, a bounded linear functional strictly separates $z$ from $L_0$, with a finite upper bound on $L_0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Here is the algebraic extension step. For a sublinear $p$, a linear $f$ on a subspace with $f\leq p$, and an extra vector $v$, an extension value $a=f(v)$ can be chosen in the interval
\[
 \sup_u(f(u)-p(u-v))\leq a\leq\inf_u(p(u+v)-f(u)).
\]
Subadditivity proves every left term is at most every right term. Each side is finite-bounded using the other side's value at zero. The extension remains dominated; Zorn's lemma extends to the whole space by unions of chains.

Choose a convex balanced open neighborhood $U$ of zero with $z\notin D+U$. The open convex set $O=D+U$ contains zero and is absorbing. Its gauge $p(x)=\inf\{r>0:x\in rO\}$ is finite sublinear, nonnegative, $O=\{p<1\}$, and $p(z)\geq1$. Sublinearity follows by rescaling two memberships and convexity; openness gives the displayed equality. On $\R z$, the functional $f(az)=a p(z)$ is dominated, also for $a<0$ since then it is nonpositive. Extend it by the preceding step. Domination on a balanced neighborhood in $O$ bounds both signs of $f$, so $f$ is continuous. For $d\in D$, all positive multiples lie in $O$, forcing $f(d)\leq0$, whereas $f(z)=p(z)\geq1$.

For the normed-space assertion use $O=L_0+\{\|v\|<\epsilon\}$ with $0<\epsilon<\operatorname{dist}(z,L_0)$. The same gauge argument gives a nonzero bounded functional with $f(z)\geq1$ and $f<1$ on $O$. Since every $l+v$ with $l\in L_0$, $\|v\|<\epsilon$ belongs to $O$, one has $f(l)\leq1-\epsilon\|f\|$. This is a finite strict separating bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rational matching and real scalar membership]\label{l:matching}
Real continuous affine tuples on a finite rational carrier are precisely the genuine finite real scalar extension of literal PA tuples. Their bounded subspace is the kernel of rational matching and recession equations. Its uniform norm is computed on finitely many rational point generators.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose rational affine origins and saturated tangent lattice bases. Equality on a shared face is equivalent to equality of affine values on a finite rational affine basis, hence is a rational homogeneous linear system in the tuples. Its real kernel has a rational basis by Gaussian elimination. Multiplying each rational basis tuple by an integer clears all intrinsic slope denominators; its constants remain rational. It is literal PA, and its real span is exactly the matching space.

For a cell $c=\conv V_c+\cone R_c$, an affine function is bounded on it exactly when its slope vanishes on every recession generator, with signed lineality generators included. Its values are then convex combinations of its values at $V_c$. The maximum absolute value over the finite point lists is therefore the global uniform norm. These are rational homogeneous boundedness equations, not a growth surrogate for $\gamma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Real endpoint passage and supports on segments]\label{l:segments}
A continuous printed-PC function is convex on every entire cell of every rational carrying structure. A continuous function with one ambient local affine lower support at every point of a finite embedded complex satisfies every printed PCC on every rational structure.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a real-endpoint PCC discard zero weights and put $t_i=\alpha_i x_i$. Constraints $A_i x_i\leq b_i$ become $A_i t_i\leq\alpha_i b_i$, together with $\alpha_i\geq0$, sum-one and $\sum_i t_i\in\tau$. This is a rational feasible polyhedron. At a given point impose all active rows as equalities. A rational particular solution and rational kernel basis approximate it by rational feasible points; inactive strict slacks persist and positive weights stay positive. The rational endpoints $t_i/\alpha_i$ and rational centers give actual printed tests. Continuity gives the original real inequality. Applying this with two endpoints in an entire original cell proves its convexity, without coarsening the carrier.

For the second assertion restrict to any segment lying in $X$. Near every point its local support applies in both segment directions, giving a local midpoint inequality. The restriction is globally convex: subtract its endpoint chord to obtain $q$, with zero endpoint values. If $q(t_0)>0$, choose sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$ so $q(t)+\epsilon t^2$ has an interior maximum. Its local midpoint inequality gives the mean at nearby symmetric parameters at least that maximum plus $\epsilon s^2$, a contradiction. At a PCC center choose its local common support. Each center-to-endpoint segment is in $X$; its initial supporting slope is a lower bound for the full secant by the just-proved convexity. Summing these endpoint inequalities cancels the covector. Finiteness of the original cells ensures the initial segment pieces belong to incident cells; every nonincident closed cell avoids a neighborhood of the center. This proves the assertion on every permitted structure, including irrational centers.
\end{proof}

\section{Local-cycle positivity and the actual PA class}\label{s:positivity}
\begin{proposition}[PC and source PAPC for finite matching real tuples]\label{p:papc}
For a continuous real-PA function of bounded difference from the admitted $\gamma$, printed PC is equivalent to nonnegative primitive tangent-corrected divisor coefficients against every nonnegative balanced local test cycle, as in BMPS Definition 3.1. Rational test weights suffice; allowing actual real test weights has the same conclusion. The analogous assertion holds in the literal subcategory.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $f$ is continuous PC and bounded relative to $\gamma$. On a rational refinement carrying $\gamma$ and $f$, qualified S306 gives one ambient support at each point on the entire incident refined top cells. Its hypotheses are checked in Section~\ref{s:matrix}. A support at $y\in\ri\rho$ agrees with the face derivative in both tangent signs. For a nonnegative balanced local cycle $c$ on a further common carrier the exact coefficient is
\[
 (f\cdot c)(\rho)=\sum_{\sigma\succ\rho}c(\sigma)f_\sigma(n_{\sigma/\rho})
 -f_\rho\!\left(\sum_{\sigma\succ\rho}c(\sigma)n_{\sigma/\rho}\right).
                                                                    \tag{3.1}
\]
Subtract the ambient support. Balancing cancels its contribution, and every residual inward slope is nonnegative. This works in every test-cycle dimension; its cells lie in incident support cells locally, and tangent changes of normal lifts cancel. It uses primitive saturated normals directly, no unit-normal substitution or factorial.

Conversely suppose the local-cycle positivity holds. At a rational point $y$ in a common rational carrier of $f$, choose rational generators $v_j$ for all incident tangent cones, including signed lineality, with cell assignments. Give each the real derivative cost $c_j=f'(y;v_j)$. The nonnegative relation cone $\{\lambda\geq0:\sum_j\lambda_jv_j=0\}$ is rational and finitely generated by rational vectors, by Lemma~\ref{l:poly}. Every rational such relation defines a rational nonnegative local one-cycle along the selected rational rays: scale each ray to its primitive vector and scale its weight inversely. Subdivide by rational equations through $y$ to include those rays, and restrict to a neighborhood without any other new vertices. Coincident rays are merged; their derivative costs agree by continuity on their shared direction. Formula (3.1) in dimension one and the positivity hypothesis give $\sum_j\lambda_j c_j\geq0$. Rational cone generation gives this for every real relation as well.

The finite cone generated by $(v_j,c_j)$ and $(0,1)$ therefore excludes $(0,-1)$. It is closed by Lemma~\ref{l:poly}. Separate it from that point, for example by nearest-point squared-distance variation. A separator $(a,b)$ has $b>0$: its value at $(0,-1)$ is strictly negative. Thus $p=-a/b$ satisfies $p(v_j)\leq c_j$. Nonnegative generation extends this to all incident tangent cones. On the incident affine pieces of $f$ it is a local lower support at $y$.

Every nonempty carrier face has a rational relative interior point. Within that relative interior its tangent cones and piece derivatives are unchanged. The same $p$ therefore gives a support, translated by the actual $f(y)$, at every real point in it. This includes every minimal face and hence every point of $X$. Lemma~\ref{l:segments} proves printed PC. No irrational point is inserted as a rational vertex, and no real cycle is relabeled rational.
\end{proof}

\section{Explicit finite carrier matrices and whole original cells}\label{s:matrix}
Let $\mathcal C$ range over all finite rational common refinements of $\Pi$ and the actual reference carrier. There is no fixed refinement-uniform bound or mesh parameter. Choose finite rational generators $V_c,R_c$ of every nonempty cell $c$, including signed lineality. Let $W_\mathcal C\subset B$ be the matching affine functions bounded on $X$. Use Lemma~\ref{l:matching} to compute a rational basis $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_m$. Write $h=\sum_j z_j\phi_j$. These coordinates are unrestricted real numbers.

For every original face $\tau\in\Pi$, and every $\rho\in\mathcal C$ contained in $\tau$, introduce an ambient affine support $s_{\tau\rho}(x)=p_{\tau\rho}(x)+e_{\tau\rho}$. The finite rows are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Equality with $\gamma+h$ at each point generator of $\rho$ and equality of slopes on each recession generator of $\rho$.
\item For every original top cell $\sigma\succeq\tau$, and \emph{every} carrier cell $c\subset\sigma$, impose
\[
 s_{\tau\rho}(v)\leq\gamma(v)+\sum_j z_j\phi_j(v)\quad(v\in V_c),\qquad
 p_{\tau\rho}(r)\leq\gamma_{c,\mathrm{lin}}(r)\quad(r\in R_c).
                                                                    \tag{4.1}
\]
\end{enumerate}
The bounded perturbation slopes vanish on $R_c$. Equality rows become paired inequalities. Listing the support coordinates as $u$ gives
\[
 A_\mathcal C z+N_\mathcal C u\leq d_\mathcal C.                       \tag{4.2}
\]
Both left matrices are rational: their entries are spatial coordinates and rational basis values. The right side contains the actual real values and slopes of $\gamma$. Cells and coefficients determine every row. No PC oracle or unknown non-PA function is an input. Include original top cells among the faces $\tau$ to control interior regions. Redundant rows and choices of intrinsic coordinates cause no change to the projection.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite matrix projection]\label{t:matrix}
Put
\[
 K=\{h\in B:J_P(h)\leq b_P\text{ for every legal }P\}.                \tag{4.3}
\]
The projection of (4.2) onto the represented $h$ is exactly $K\cap W_\mathcal C$. It is nonempty. Refinement inclusions preserve the represented function and its PC property. The union over all carriers is precisely the bounded-relative real-PA PC perturbations of $\gamma$ and is directed and convex.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From a feasible tuple, generator equalities give equality on the entire $\rho$, and point/ray inequalities give a minorant on every entire incident original top cell. Every real point $y$ in any original face belongs to at least one such $\rho$, so the same affine function is a common support there. This holds for irrational points, unbounded portions and boundaries, not only the sampled generators. Lemma~\ref{l:segments} proves every printed PCC in every rational structure. Thus $h\in K$. It is genuine bounded real PA by construction.

For necessity suppose $h\in K\cap W_\mathcal C$ and $f=\gamma+h$. Then $f$ is finite continuous printed PC and $|f-\gamma|\leq\|h\|_\infty$. Apply qualified S306 on a finite rational refinement carrying $\gamma$, with all original closed faces retained, actual strictly positive top weights balanced at every facet, and actual cellwise-affine real $\gamma$. Refinement preserves balancing: a new facet inside an old top cell has equal inherited weights and opposite saturated normals; a subdivided old facet retains its old equation. Its support theorem does not require $\gamma$ PC or positive at infinity, though both hold here. It gives at each point one common support on entire incident refined cells. This is within the exact accepted convex subject, not the abstract's concavity typo; the cellwise versus ambient-distance qualification is retained.

Lemma~\ref{l:segments} separately gives convexity of $f$ on each \emph{entire original} cell. At $y\in\ri\rho$ every segment to a point in an original incident top cell initially lies in a refined top cell containing $y$: intersect the segment with the finite closed refinement and use its first nonempty interval. The S306 support bounds that initial secant. Convexity of the whole original-cell restriction propagates the bound to the distant endpoint. Thus its support holds on the entire original incident cell, even when $\gamma$ was not affine on the coarse cell. If $y$ belongs to a smaller original face than $\tau$, its incident collection contains every cell incident to $\tau$.

Since $f$ is affine on $\rho$, testing both small tangent directions at $y$ forces the support to agree on the whole affine hull of $\rho$. For a point face only its value is needed. The chosen support therefore solves every point/ray equality and inequality row for this index. Finitely many choices give $u$ in (4.2). This proves necessity, including coarse-to-refined compatibility.

Zero is feasible because $\gamma$ itself is PC. Any finite rational carrier of a real-PA perturbation admits a common refinement with $\Pi$ and the reference. Conversely every represented function is a genuine real-PA perturbation. Two carriers have a common finite refinement; convex combinations of their represented PC functions are affine matching on it and retain every Jensen inequality. This proves directedness and convexity. No feasibility for a continuous completion was presumed: necessity here starts with an actual affine matching tuple.
\end{proof}

\section{Costed Farkas and finite multipliers}
\begin{theorem}[Costed infinite Farkas, including infinity]\label{t:farkas}
For $\ell\in B^*$ and $t\in\R$,
\[
 (\ell,t)\in D_\gamma\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \ell(h)\leq t\quad\text{for every }h\in K.                          \tag{5.1}
\]
No Slater assumption is needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The right inequality holds for each generator by (4.3), for $(0,1)$, for finite nonnegative sums and for weak-star/product limits, testing the fixed $h$. This proves one direction.

If $(\ell,t)$ is outside the closed convex cone, apply Lemma~\ref{l:hb} in the Hausdorff locally convex space $(B^*,\sigma(B^*,B))\times\R$. Every continuous linear functional there is a pairing with a single $(h,s)\in B\times\R$. Indeed continuity bounds it using finitely many evaluations on $B^*$; it vanishes on their common kernel, factors through their finite-dimensional image, and is a finite linear combination of those evaluations. Include the real coordinate and write the pairing as $\eta(h)-rs$. Separation yields
\[
 \ell(h)-ts>0,\qquad J_P(h)-b_Ps\leq0\ (\text{all }P),\qquad -s\leq0.
                                                                    \tag{5.2}
\]
If $s>0$, then $h/s\in K$ violates the proposed bound. If $s=0$, then $J_P(h)\leq0$ for every $P$. Since every $b_P\geq0$, $nh\in K$ for all $n\geq0$. Validity of $\ell(nh)\leq t$ forces $\ell(h)\leq0$, contradicting (5.2). This proves the converse and retains all nonzero slack costs.
\end{proof}

This proof uses the full dual $B^*$, not Radon measures of compact support or countably additive measures on $X$. For example on $X=\R$, the functional on the convergent bounded continuous functions given by their limit at $+\infty$ has norm one. Apply the algebraic dominated extension proof of Lemma~\ref{l:hb} with $p=\|\cdot\|_\infty$ to extend it to $C_b(\R)$. It annihilates $C_0(\R)$ and takes the constant one to one. It cannot be a finite signed Radon measure on $\R$: annihilation of all compactly supported continuous tests forces such a measure to be zero, whereas its total mass would be one. Thus replacing the dual by that measure subclass loses legitimate functionals. Definition (1.5) is the explicit dual data used here; no representation theorem is needed, and in a nonseparable predual its closure is not relabeled sequential.

Costs cannot be suppressed by homogenization. On the balanced line with $\gamma=|x|$, the PCC of $-1,1$ centered at zero has $b=1$. The valid PC target
\[
 f(x)=|x|+\tfrac12(1-|x|)_+
\]
has perturbation cost $J(h)=1/2$. The uncosted inequality $J(h)\leq0$ would incorrectly exclude it. Adjoining a homogeneous coordinate without retaining its fixed slice would have the same defect.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite multipliers, no LP grant]\label{t:mult}
For a fixed carrier, $\ell(h)\leq t$ on $K\cap W_\mathcal C$ if and only if a finite vector $\lambda\geq0$ satisfies
\[
 A_\mathcal C^T\lambda=a_\mathcal C(\ell),\qquad
 N_\mathcal C^T\lambda=0,\qquad d_\mathcal C^T\lambda\leq t.           \tag{5.3}
\]
Actual irrational right-hand sides are permitted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
One direction is multiplication of (4.2). For the other, in the finite coefficient space form the cone generated by its rows $(A_i,N_i,d_i)$ and $(0,0,1)$. It is closed by Lemma~\ref{l:poly}. If $(a_\mathcal C(\ell),0,t)$ is outside it, finite-dimensional cone separation gives $(z,u,s)$ with $s\geq0$,
\[
 A_i z+N_i u\leq d_i s,\qquad a_\mathcal C(\ell)\cdot z-ts>0.
\]
If $s>0$, division gives a feasible tuple violating the bound. If $s=0$, choose an existing feasible tuple $(z_0,u_0)$, which exists by Theorem~\ref{t:matrix}. Then $(z_0+nz,u_0+nu)$ is feasible for all $n\geq0$, and its objective exceeds $t$ for large $n$. Both contradict validity. Hence the point is in the finite cone. Its representation is (5.3), with the vertical generator absorbing the nonnegative excess $t-d^T\lambda$. This proves exact duality without strict feasibility, bounded auxiliary supports, or a theorem imported from an unavailable LP reference.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-carrier equivalence]\label{t:dual}
Criterion $\mathfrak C(X,\gamma)$ is equivalent to uniform density of bounded-relative genuine real-PA PC functions in the \emph{continuous} bounded-relative PC class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\section{A compatible rational interpolation bridge}\label{s:bridge}
The original finite-PA obstacle property cannot be substituted for the all-$C_b$ property merely by asserting PA density in $C_b(X)$; that broad assertion is false on noncompact spaces. We prove the narrower needed interpolation fact for continuous PC targets.

\begin{lemma}[Common rational generalized-simplex subdivision]\label{l:simplex}
Every finite rational polyhedral complex has a finite compatible rational subdivision whose cells have the form
\[
 c=\conv\{v_0,\ldots,v_l\}+\cone\{r_{l+1},\ldots,r_k\},
                                                                    \tag{6.1}
\]
with the augmented vectors $(v_i,1),(r_j,0)$ linearly independent. One may simultaneously refine any finite list of rational carriers.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Homogenize all cell inequalities in $V\times\R$ and include the hyperplanes $s=0$ and all coordinate hyperplanes. Their finite central rational arrangement is a fan of pointed cones: each cone lies in a coordinate orthant, which has no lineality. Its cones in $s\geq0$ refine the homogenized polyhedra on $s>0$. Faces at $s=0$ also acquire a common rational refinement, regardless of whether the original recession cones met in faces.

Triangulate this pointed fan compatibly. For every nonzero face choose one rational ray in its relative interior, for example a positive sum of its rational generating rays. By induction on face dimension, cone from this interior ray to the already triangulated boundary facets. In a bounded positive-linear-form section this is the elementary operation of coning an interior point to boundary simplices; it covers the section and meets along common faces. The same chosen rays on shared faces give compatibility. Flags of faces produce linearly independent ray generators, since at each step the new relative interior ray is outside the previous proper face's span. Normalize positive-$s$ rays to $(v,1)$ and keep zero-$s$ rays $(r,0)$. The section $s=1$ gives (6.1) and a finite common subdivision. Cones whose section is empty are ignored. A shared recession direction of disjoint cells is not assigned a shared function value at infinity by this construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite real-PA obstacle majorants without PC gluing]\label{t:interpolate}
For every continuous PC $f$ with $\|f-\gamma\|_\infty<\infty$ in the admitted real-reference category and every $\eta>0$, there is a continuous genuine finite rational-carrier real-PA $q$ such that
\[
 f\leq q\leq f+\eta\quad\text{on all }X,\qquad q-\gamma\in\PA_{\R,b}.
                                                                    \tag{6.2}
\]
The function $q$ is not asserted PC.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a finite refinement carrying $\gamma$ affinely; call its top cells $\sigma$. Qualified S306 applies to the continuous finite printed-PC $f$, the actual cellwise-affine $\gamma$, all closed faces and actual positive weights balanced at all facets. It supplies a finite global Lipschitz constant on each entire closed $\sigma$; it does not claim ambient-distance control across components. On each $\sigma$, Lemma~\ref{l:segments} gives convexity, $|f-\gamma|$ is bounded, and the actual affine restriction of $\gamma$ is strictly positive on every nonzero recession direction. These are precisely the extra hypotheses of S306's single-cell majorant theorem. It gives a convex rational-spatial real-PA roof $p_\sigma$ with $f\leq p_\sigma\leq f+\eta$ and exact recession $\gamma_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}$ on $\rec\sigma$. No common-support or trace gluing conclusion from S306 is presumed.

Take a common rational refinement of their spatial carriers and then Lemma~\ref{l:simplex}. On each cell (6.1) contained in $\sigma$, define the unique affine $q$ by $q(v_i)=f(v_i)$ and by recession slope $q_{\mathrm{lin}}(r_j)=\gamma_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}(r_j)$. Augmented independence gives existence and uniqueness on its affine hull. On any shared finite face the vertex values coincide and the recession slopes coincide, because $\gamma$ has the same affine restriction there. Thus the formulas match and are continuous on $X$. When two disjoint cells have a common direction at infinity but different reference slopes there, their ray prescriptions remain separate; matching is only on actual shared finite faces. Lemma~\ref{l:matching} proves genuine finite scalar-extension membership.

For a convex $f$ on $\sigma$ with upper bound $f\leq\gamma+C$, convex secants along a feasible recession ray imply
\[
 f(x+r)\leq f(x)+\gamma_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}(r).
                                                                    \tag{6.3}
\]
Indeed any larger unit secant would persist on longer secants and violate the upper affine bound as the ray parameter grows. For $x=\sum_i\alpha_i v_i+\sum_j\beta_j r_j$, with $\alpha_i,\beta_j\geq0$, $\sum_i\alpha_i=1$, ordinary convexity and repeated (6.3) give $f(x)\leq q(x)$. The roof $p_\sigma$ is affine on this cell and has precisely the same recession slopes there. Consequently
\[
 q(x)-p_\sigma(x)=\sum_i\alpha_i(f(v_i)-p_\sigma(v_i))\leq0.
\]
It follows that $f\leq q\leq p_\sigma\leq f+\eta$ on every entire cell. This proves (6.2), without needing any PC condition for the interpolant.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Original endpoint property, density and all obstacles]\label{t:bridge}
For an admitted actual real reference the following are equivalent: criterion (1.6); continuous relative-PC density; the original property (1.3) for all bounded genuine real-PA obstacles; and the all-$C_b$ version of (1.3).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first two are Theorem~\ref{t:dual}. Qualified R230 applies on the finite positive all-face balanced embedded support with continuous PC $\gamma$ and arbitrary $u\in C_b(X)$. It identifies the actual upper-only envelope (1.2) with a continuous PC function $e$ satisfying
\[
 \gamma+\inf_Xu\leq e\leq\gamma+u\leq\gamma+\sup_Xu.                 \tag{6.4}
\]
No separability, PA obstacle or PA density hypothesis is part of that grant. Density therefore gives uniform real-PA PC approximation of $e$, proving the all-$C_b$ property and hence the original property.

Conversely assume the original property, and take any continuous relative-PC $f$. Theorem~\ref{t:interpolate} gives $f\leq q\leq f+\eta$, with the legal bounded real-PA obstacle $u=q-\gamma$. The actual envelope has $f$ as an admissible competitor and is bounded above by $q$, so $f\leq E_\gamma(u)\leq f+\eta$. By the assumed property this endpoint lies in the uniform closure of $\mathcal A_\R(\gamma)$. Letting $\eta\downarrow0$ proves density by an explicit diagonal choice of PA approximants. Feasibility was not inferred from a non-PA continuous completion; the interpolant was constructed independently and only serves as an obstacle.

There is also a direct converse from the all-$C_b$ property: for $h\in K$ use obstacle $u=h$. Its envelope equals $\gamma+h$ because that function is itself a competitor and equals the obstacle. This covers every continuous relative-PC target, including nonseparable obstacles.

For strict decreasing approximation choose $a_n\in\mathcal A_\R(\gamma)$ with $\|a_n-e\|_\infty\leq\delta_n=2^{-n}$. Put $r_n=a_n+\delta_n$ and $g_n=r_n+4\delta_n$. Then
\[
 4\delta_n\leq g_n-e\leq6\delta_n,\qquad
 g_n-g_{n+1}\geq4\delta_n-6\delta_{n+1}=\delta_n>0.
                                                                    \tag{6.5}
\]
Rational common constants are literal PA and hence valid in the genuine real category. Each $g_n$ has its own finite rational carrier; no common finite carrier for the infinite sequence is claimed. Their relative bounds are at most $\|e-\gamma\|_\infty+3$ for $n\geq1$. On every feasible ray bounded differences give exactly the actual reference recession. The below-endpoint functions $a_n-\delta_n$ are legal PA competitors, have error at most $2\delta_n$, and prove equality of the actual envelope with the supremum of PA competitors. These shifts need not preserve contacts. No arbitrary real-affine maximum is used in this PA argument.
\end{proof}

\section{An actual literal obstruction, independently reconstructed}\label{s:literal}

\begin{lemma}[Coercivity and an arithmetic local gap]\label{l:literaltools}
A finite PA reference positive on every nonzero recession direction has bounded sublevel sets. If a continuous convex function is affine with slope $a\notin\Z$ on a rational line parameter interval $[-r,r]$, every literal PA PC approximant has uniform error there at least $r\operatorname{dist}(a,\Z)/4$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a reference-affine cell use finite point/ray generators. Positivity prohibits nonzero lineality. Choose $c>0$ smaller than the finitely many positive ratios of reference ray slopes to ray norms and bound its finitely many point values below by $c\|v\|-D$. The triangle inequality on a point/cone representation gives $\gamma(x)\geq c\|x\|-D$. Cells without rays are bounded and permit the same assertion with a larger $D$. Finiteness gives a global bound, so sublevels are bounded; they are closed by continuity.

For the gap, let $g$ be a literal PC approximant with error $e<\infty$. It is convex on the whole segment by Lemma~\ref{l:segments}. At any differentiability point $t\in[-r/2,r/2]$ avoiding its finitely many breakpoints, its derivative is an integer: the line direction is chosen in the saturated tangent lattice and every affine piece has integral slope there. Convex secants to $-r,r$ give
\[
 a-4e/r\leq g'(t)\leq a+4e/r.
\]
Thus $\operatorname{dist}(a,\Z)\leq4e/r$. Refinement cannot remove this positive error floor. No bound on the number or location of its PA pieces is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Literal bounded obstacle with nonregularizable actual endpoint]\label{t:literal}
Every noncompact finite positive balanced support with an admitted literal $\gamma$ has a bounded literal obstacle $u$ for which its actual envelope is a continuous rational-affine PC function $f$ having a positive uniform distance from every literal PA PC function. Thus literal regularity fails.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An unbounded reference cell has a nonzero recession vector. Its integral intrinsic gradient $m$ is nonzero by positive growth. Choose a primitive tangent lattice direction $v$ with $m(v)=k\ne0$, a rational point $x_0$ in that cell's relative interior, a rational constant $A>\gamma(x_0)$, and an integer $D>|k|$, $D\geq2$. Put $\lambda=1/D$ and
\[
 G=\max(\gamma,A),\qquad f=(1-\lambda)\gamma+\lambda G,\qquad
 H=(A-\gamma)_+,\qquad \delta=f-\gamma=\lambda H.                    \tag{7.1}
\]
$G$ is literal PA on rational comparison walls and printed PC by the maximum Jensen inequality. The convex combination $f$ is continuous PC and rational-affine on a finite rational carrier, belongs to the genuine real scalar extension, and is bounded relative to $\gamma$. The function $H$ is bounded and compactly supported by Lemma~\ref{l:literaltools}. Near $x_0$, $f=(1-\lambda)\gamma+\lambda A$, so its slope on $v$ is $a=k-k/D\notin\Z$. Choose a rational $r>0$ so $x_0+tv$, $|t|\leq r$, remains in that relative interior and below the level $A$. Lemma~\ref{l:literaltools} gives the positive error floor $r\operatorname{dist}(a,\Z)/4$.

It remains to construct an \emph{admissible literal obstacle} with this exact endpoint. Choose a common rational carrier of $\gamma,G,f,H$, refine by coordinate orthants if necessary to make its cells pointed, and let $S$ be the finite union of all its rational vertices. Their values $\delta(s)$ are rational. Choose an ambient integral polyhedral norm $\rho$, for instance a coordinate $\ell^1$ norm. There is an integer $M>0$ such that
\[
 \delta(x)\leq\delta(s)+M\rho(x-s)\quad(x\in X,\ s\in S).            \tag{7.2}
\]
To justify this globally, on every affine carrier cell containing $s$ its slope norm bounds the difference from $\delta(s)$ linearly. Every other closed cell avoids a neighborhood of $s$; finitely many such cells have a positive distance from $s$. Boundedness of $\delta$ supplies a finite bound for the ratio there. Norm equivalence and the finite list of $s$ give one integer $M$. This does not assume a global ambient-distance Lipschitz theorem for unrelated components.

Define
\[
 u(x)=\min\left\{H(x),\ \min_{s\in S}\bigl(\delta(s)+M\rho(x-s)\bigr)\right\}.
                                                                    \tag{7.3}
\]
Every displayed function is literal PA: its slopes are integral, constants rational, and its comparison walls have rational coefficients. The finite minimum is therefore literal on a finite common rational carrier. It is continuous and bounded, $0\leq u\leq H$, $u\geq\delta$ by (7.2), and $u(s)=\delta(s)$ for every vertex. It vanishes where $H=0$, in particular outside a compact set. Thus $f\leq\gamma+u$, with equality at every vertex.

The function $f$ is an admissible printed-PC competitor, so $E_\gamma(u)\geq f$. For any original unrestricted competitor $q$, qualified R230's completion theorem applies under positive all-face balancing. Its dominating continuous PC completion $F$ has the same rational values, stays below the continuous obstacle and below $\gamma+C_q$, and dominates $q$. Hence $F(s)\leq f(s)$ at every carrier vertex. On a fixed carrier cell $c$, $\gamma$ and $f$ are affine and have the same recession slope: bounded differences in (7.1) imply this, or check that an unbounded reference ray eventually has $G=\gamma$. Convexity and the upper bound $F\leq\gamma+C_q$ give the ray inequality (6.3) for $F$. For the point/ray representation of any $x\in c$, it follows that
\[
 F(x)\leq\sum_i\alpha_i F(s_i)+\sum_j\beta_j\gamma_{c,\mathrm{lin}}(r_j)
 \leq\sum_i\alpha_i f(s_i)+\sum_j\beta_j f_{c,\mathrm{lin}}(r_j)=f(x).
\]
Thus every $q\leq f$ everywhere, including irrational points, and the actual envelope is exactly $f$. The arithmetic error floor proves it is not a uniform literal PC PA limit. This is an actual bounded literal obstacle and envelope, not merely a density failure for a selected rational-affine function.
\end{proof}

For a checkable one-dimensional instance take $X=\R$, $\gamma=|x|$, $A=1$, $\lambda=1/2$, and vertices $-1,0,1$. Then $f=|x|+\tfrac12(1-|x|)_+$ and one possible literal obstacle is
\[
 u(x)=\begin{cases}\frac12+|x|,&|x|\leq\frac14,\\1-|x|,&\frac14\leq|x|\leq1,\\0,&|x|\geq1.\end{cases}            \tag{7.4}
\]
All slopes are integral and all breakpoints and constants rational. Its actual envelope is $f$. On the interval centered at $x=1/2$ with radius $1/4$, the slope is $1/2$, so the displayed error floor is $1/32$. We do not claim this particular floor is sharp.
