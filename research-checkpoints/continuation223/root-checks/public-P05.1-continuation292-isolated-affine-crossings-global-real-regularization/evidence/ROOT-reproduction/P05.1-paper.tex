\section{Exact domain and theorem}
Fix a lattice $N$ and $E=N\otimes\R$. Rational affine coordinates mean coordinates over $N\otimes\Q$. Clearing denominators in a finite list of affine constraints reconciles rational constants with the integral-constraint convention of BMPS Definition 2.1. Let
\[
 X=\bigcup_{i=1}^r Q_i,\qquad [X]=\sum_{i=1}^r c_i[Q_i],\qquad c_i\in\R_{>0},
\]
where each $Q_i$ is a distinct complete rational affine $d$-plane and, for $i\ne j$, $Q_i\cap Q_j$ is empty or a singleton. Completeness here means the entire affine plane, with no boundary truncation. All carriers are finite rational complexes in this single ambient space. Write $V$ for the finite set of intersection points, $I(v)=\{i:v\in Q_i\}$, and $L_i=Q_i-Q_i$. Gaussian elimination in rational equations shows that every $v\in V$ is rational. The sublattice $N_i=N\cap L_i$ is saturated and has rank $d$.

The cycle notation denotes the actual sum of complete-plane cycles. On a subdivision of a plane, a new facet has opposite primitive inward quotient classes and equal weights on its two sides, so its balance is zero modulo the facet tangent. When $d=1$, at a crossing the two directions of each complete line cancel separately. For $d>1$, an isolated crossing is not a top facet; all facet balances remain the branch balances. Hence the sum is an effective balanced cycle with strictly positive top weights. Subtracting any one summand leaves an effective balanced residual, even when branch directions have linear dependencies.

A printed polyhedral convex combination is an equality
\[
 x=\sum_{j=1}^m\alpha_jx_j,\quad \alpha_j\geq0,\quad
 \sum_j\alpha_j=1,\quad x_j\in X\cap N_\Q,
\]
on some rational carrier, with a center face $\tau$ and endpoint cells $\sigma_j$ satisfying $x\in\tau$, $x_j\in\sigma_j$, and $\tau\preceq\sigma_j$. Centers and coefficients may be real. The class $\PC$ consists of finite real-valued functions satisfying every such inequality, on all carriers in the rational equivalence class. We explicitly require continuity whenever it is needed. The class $\PA$ consists of continuous functions affine with arbitrary real coefficients on a finite rational spatial carrier. Real heights do not turn irrational spatial walls into rational walls.

An admitted actual reference is
\[
 \gamma\in\PA\cap\PC,\qquad
 \gamma_\sigma(v)>0\quad(0\ne v\in\rec(\sigma))
\]
on a finite rational carrier. Here $\gamma_\sigma$ is the linear part, and translated tangent spaces are $\operatorname{span}(\sigma-\sigma)$. Put $\mathcal D_\R(\gamma)$ equal to the uniform closure, within continuous functions at bounded distance from $\gamma$, of actual finite $\PA\cap\PC$ functions with bounded difference from this same $\gamma$.

\begin{theorem}[Scoped global regularization]\label{main}
For every admitted actual reference and every $f\in C(X)\cap\PC$ with $\|f-\gamma\|_\infty\leq C<\infty$, there are actual finite $G_n\in\PA\cap\PC$ on rational spatial carriers such that
\[
 G_n\downarrow f\ \hbox{everywhere},\qquad
 \|G_n-f\|_\infty\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \sup_n\|G_n-\gamma\|_\infty<\infty.
\]
Every $G_n$ has exactly the recession of the actual $\gamma$ on each complete branch. Consequently
\[
 \mathcal D_\R(\gamma)=\{f\in C(X)\cap\PC:\ f-\gamma\text{ is bounded}\}.
\]
For every $u\in C_b(X)$, its actual unrestricted envelope
\[
 P_\gamma(u)(x)=\sup\{q(x):q\in\PC,\ q\leq\gamma+u,
                      \ q\leq\gamma+C_q\text{ for some }C_q\in\R\}
\]
belongs to this genuine global decreasing domain. This includes non-PA and coupled obstacles.
\end{theorem}

The last assertion uses precisely the supplied R230 envelope theorem, stated below. It does not assume uniform PA approximation of arbitrary $C_b$ obstacles. Raw Monge--Amp\`ere is applied only after the theorem's approximation antecedent has been established.

\section{Rational incidence and ordinary convex facts}
\begin{lemma}[Rational feasible density and finite cones]\label{rational}
A point of a rational polyhedron is a limit of rational points in its minimal face. A cone defined by finitely many rational homogeneous constraints has a finite rational generating list, adding both signs of a rational basis for lineality. The same finite-generation and closedness statements hold over real coefficients, with real generators.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At the given point impose every active inequality as an equality together with the defining equations. Rational Gaussian elimination gives a rational affine particular solution and rational basis of its translation space. Approximate the actual coordinates in that basis by rationals. All inactive inequalities have strictly positive slack and stay satisfied. This proves density without moving off the actual feasible face.

For a cone remove its common kernel (lineality) using a rational complement. On the pointed quotient, the sum of a defining inequality list is a rational form positive off zero: its zero set in the cone would be the joint kernel. Its section at value one is bounded; an unbounded sequence divided by its norm would otherwise have a nonzero limit of section value zero. This bounded polyhedron is a convex hull of finitely many vertices. To see this, at a point not yet a vertex choose a nonzero direction in the kernel of its active constraints and move in both directions until an additional independent constraint becomes active. Boundedness supplies both endpoints; expressing the original point between them and repeating terminates when the active constraints have full rank. Only finitely many active lists exist. Their vertices solve rational full-rank systems. Lift the section vertices to cone generators and add both lineality signs.

A cone generated by finitely many vectors is closed. In any nonnegative representation, eliminate dependent active columns by a signed relation, changing its sign to have a positive entry and subtracting the relation up to the first vanishing coefficient. Repeat until the remaining columns are independent. On each independent subset the inverse column map bounds all coefficients by a constant times the represented vector's norm. A converging sequence thus has a subsequence using one of finitely many subsets with bounded, then convergent coefficients. This proves closedness and also works for real generators. Homogenizing a pointed polyhedron now gives a finite bounded vertex hull plus its recession cone by separating positive-height and zero-height generators. Finite hull-plus-cone sets are polyhedra: represent membership by convex and nonnegative coefficients and eliminate these variables one at a time. For each variable, pair each lower bound with each upper bound; zero-coefficient inequalities remain. This finite elimination proves the needed finite real half-space description.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Convex supports and directional subgradients]\label{convexfacts}
A finite convex function on a complete finite-dimensional affine space is continuous and locally Lipschitz. At an interior point $a$, its directional derivative $h(v)=\lim_{t\downarrow0}(f(a+tv)-f(a))/t$ is finite continuous sublinear. Its subdifferential
\[
 K=\{\ell:f(a+x)\geq f(a)+\ell(x)\text{ for all }x\}
\]
is nonempty compact convex, and $h(v)=\max_{\ell\in K}\ell(v)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A cube around a point bounds a finite convex function above by the largest value of its finitely many vertices. Reflection through the center and convexity give a lower bound on a smaller cube. On a still smaller ball, extend the direction between any two close points by a fixed positive radius within the bounded region. Convex secant monotonicity bounds the increment in that direction by the oscillation times distance divided by that radius. Reverse the two points for the other bound. This proves local Lipschitzness, and hence continuity, without an assumed differentiability theorem.

Secant monotonicity gives the derivative limit; the Lipschitz bound makes it finite and uniformly bounded by a constant times $\|v\|$. Positive homogeneity follows by rescaling $t$. For subadditivity use
$f(a+t(v+w))\leq\tfrac12 f(a+2tv)+\tfrac12f(a+2tw)$ and pass to derivatives. A finite sublinear function bounded on the unit sphere is Lipschitz: $h(v)-h(w)\leq h(v-w)$ and reverse. Thus $h$ is continuous.

Here is the finite supporting-hyperplane argument used in this paper. For a finite continuous convex $F$, its closed epigraph has a supporting plane at each graph point. Project the point $(x,F(x)-1/n)$ to that closed convex set. A nearest point exists by restricting distance minimization to a bounded closed ball. First variation of squared distance along every feasible segment gives a supporting unit normal at the nearest point. Their distance from $(x,F(x))$ tends to zero. A subsequence of normals converges to a unit supporting normal there. Its height coefficient is nonpositive since the epigraph is upward closed, and cannot be zero: its projection is the whole spatial space, so a nonzero horizontal form could not bound it. Dividing by the negative height coefficient gives an affine minorant touching $F$ at $x$.

Apply this to $h$ at each $v$. Homogeneity, by evaluating the minorant at $tv$ for all $t\geq0$, forces its intercept to be zero. Thus some $\ell\leq h$ has $\ell(v)=h(v)$. A form satisfies $\ell\leq h$ exactly when it is in $K$: one implication passes the support inequality to derivatives, and the other uses $h(x)\leq f(a+x)-f(a)$ by secant monotonicity. Hence the support representation follows, including nonemptiness. The uniform bound on $h$ and both signs of $v$ bound $\ell$ in norm. Closedness and convexity are immediate from the inequalities, so $K$ is compact.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Inherited-incidence branch convexity]\label{branchconvex}
If $f\in C(X)\cap\PC$, then every restriction $f_i=f|_{Q_i}$ is ordinary convex on its entire complete branch.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix any rational carrier, a point $x\in Q_i$, and its minimal face $\tau$. A sufficiently small ambient ball misses every cell not containing $x$, since finitely many such closed cells have positive distance from $x$. Every remaining cell contains $\tau$ as a face. For a sufficiently short branch displacement $w$, the two points $x\pm w$ belong to some incident cells $\sigma_\pm$. The feasible set
\[
 \{(z,u):z\in\tau,\ z+u\in\sigma_+,\ z-u\in\sigma_-\}
\]
is a rational polyhedron. By Lemma~\ref{rational}, $(x,w)$ is approximated inside it by rational $(z,u)$. The exact rational endpoints $z\pm u$ have barycenter $z$, with weights $1/2$, and their assigned cells contain the center face. The printed inequality is therefore legal on the inherited carrier. Passing to the limit by continuity gives the local midpoint inequality at $x$. This also covers irrational centers and an intersection vertex; no cell coarsening is used.

For clarity, this pointwise local midpoint property suffices for global segment convexity even without a uniform radius. Parameterize any branch segment by $t\in[0,1]$ and subtract its chord. If the continuous difference $q(t)$ is positive somewhere, choose a small $\epsilon>0$ so that $q(t)+\epsilon t^2$ still exceeds both endpoint values somewhere. It has an interior maximum. At that maximum, the local midpoint inequality for $q$, and the strict midpoint increment $\epsilon s^2$ of the added quadratic, imply that the average of its values at $t\pm s$ is at least its maximum plus $\epsilon s^2$. This contradicts maximality. Therefore every chord bounds $f_i$ above, proving convexity on the whole complete branch.
\end{proof}
\section{A common support at each dependent crossing}
\begin{theorem}[Exact continuous-PC criterion]\label{criterion}
A continuous function on $X$ is printed-PC if and only if its branch restrictions are ordinary convex and, at every $v\in V$, there is one ambient real linear form $p_v$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{support}
 f_i(x)\geq f(v)+p_v(x-v)\qquad(i\in I(v),\ x\in Q_i).
\end{equation}
Branch values agree at $v$ because the function is single-valued. The form may differ at different vertices. Linear independence of branch directions is not required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Necessity of branch convexity is Lemma~\ref{branchconvex}. Translate $v$ to zero on the incident branches and put $a=f(v)$. Their tangent spaces $L_i$ have pairwise zero intersection. Each convex branch has a compact nonempty subdifferential $K_i$ at zero and a finite continuous sublinear derivative $h_i$, with $h_i(w)=\max_{k_i\in K_i}k_i(w)$, by Lemma~\ref{convexfacts}.

Let $K=\prod_{i\in I(v)}K_i$ in $W=\prod L_i^*$, and let $S$ be the image of the ambient restriction map $E^*\to W$. It is a closed linear subspace: finite-dimensional elimination gives a basis and a complementary coordinate space. Suppose $K\cap S=\varnothing$. On the compact $K$, minimize the squared norm of projection onto $S^\perp$. Let $k_0$ be a minimizer and $q=\operatorname{pr}_{S^\perp}k_0\ne0$. First variation along the segment to any $k\in K$ gives
\[
 \langle q,k\rangle\geq\|q\|^2.
\]
The tuple $z=-q$ annihilates $S$ and has $\max_{k\in K}\langle z,k\rangle<0$. Identify $W^*$ with tuples $w_i\in L_i$. Annihilation means, as an actual ambient vector,
\[
 \sum_i w_i=0,qquad \sum_i h_i(w_i)<0.
\]
This is the compact product versus closed restriction-subspace alternative. It does not group branch barycenters as if their sum were direct.

The embeddings of $L_i$ are rational, so the exact kernel of $\sum_i w_i$ has a rational basis. Approximate the separating tuple within that kernel by rational tuples. Continuity of the $h_i$ preserves the strict negative sum. Remove zero entries. For a sufficiently small common positive rational $t$, every endpoint $v+tw_i$ lies in an actual cell incident to $v$, and
\[
 \sum_i(f_i(v+tw_i)-a)
       =t\sum_i h_i(w_i)+o(t)<0.
\]
Nonincident branches and nonincident cells have positive distance from $v$; finiteness supplies a common small radius. Equal weights $1/m$ on the remaining $m$ endpoints give exactly barycenter $v$. The carrier's center face is the rational vertex $v$. This is a genuine finite rational printed violation. Hence $K\cap S\ne\varnothing$, and any ambient preimage of a point in that intersection gives \eqref{support} on all complete incident branches.

For sufficiency, every positive-dimensional convex cell in the union is contained in one branch. Indeed its relative interior cannot be covered by finitely many proper affine subspaces: their defining nonzero linear equations have a product polynomial not identically zero on that affine hull. Thus some branch contains the hull, and then the whole cell. If two such cells on different branches share a positive-dimensional face, their planes would have a positive-dimensional intersection, contrary to the hypotheses. In any complex the point where branches meet is consequently a vertex: it is the common face of cells from different branches and their intersection is that singleton.

For a PCC center outside $V$, every cell incident to its center face is therefore in the unique branch containing the center. Branch Jensen applies. At a center $v\in V$, an endpoint cell lies in a branch incident to $v$; use \eqref{support} at every endpoint and sum. The ambient barycenter cancels $p_v$, giving Jensen. This holds on every rational carrier and refinement, and even for real endpoints. It proves sufficiency without an ambient-convex extension of $f$ on the whole union.
\end{proof}

\section{Global approximation on one full affine branch}
Work in saturated branch coordinates after a rational affine translation, so the branch is $\R^d$ with rational sample positions. The coordinate choice changes neither saturation nor the real coefficient convention.

\begin{lemma}[Actual recession geometry]\label{recession}
Let $\gamma$ be a finite continuous convex real PA positive-growth function on a rational carrier of the whole $\R^d$, and $|f-\gamma|\leq C$ for a finite convex $f$. There are finitely many actual real gradients $a_j$ and intercepts $b_j$ such that
\[
 \gamma(x)=\max_j(a_j\cdot x+b_j),\quad
 h(x)=\max_j a_j\cdot x,\quad \Delta=\conv\{a_j\}.
\]
The function $f-h$ is bounded globally, its exact recession is $h$, and $\Delta$ is a compact polytope containing zero in its interior. The cone $E_h=\epi h$ is generated by finitely many $(r,h(r))$ with rational spatial $r$, together with the upward vertical ray.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At an interior point of every full-dimensional carrying cell, its affine gradient supports the whole convex function. This follows directly from secants toward any other point, starting in the cell. Thus all cell affine expressions are global minorants; they attain equality on their own cells. Every point is a limit of such cell interiors, proving the finite maximum formula. It gives $|\gamma-h|\leq\max_j|b_j|$ and hence $|f-h|\leq D$ for some finite $D$.

The finitely many cell recession cones cover the whole space. For any direction $v$, a sequence $t_nv$ with $t_n\to\infty$ has a subsequence in one closed cell. Divide its affine constraints by $t_n$ to obtain the homogeneous recession constraints for $v$. On a recession cone of a cell with gradient $a$, the limit of $\gamma(x+tv)/t$ is $a\cdot v=h(v)$. The same limit for $f$, at any base point, is $h(v)$ because $|f-h|$ is bounded. Positive growth makes $h(v)>0$ for $v\ne0$. Continuity on the unit sphere gives $h(v)\geq b\|v\|$ with $b>0$. Finite convex separation (or the nearest-point argument) then shows that $\Delta$ contains the radius-$b$ ball about zero; otherwise a separating direction would contradict this support bound.

No carrying cell has lineality: along a lineality direction $v$, its linear part would be strictly positive both on $v$ and $-v$, an impossibility. Its rational recession cone therefore has finite rational ray generators by Lemma~\ref{rational}. On each such cone $h$ is the cell gradient, so a conic decomposition of a spatial vector by these generators lifts with exact equality of heights. Every $(x,t)$ with $t\geq h(x)$ can therefore be generated by the lifts of one cone's rays plus $(0,1)$. Conversely sublinearity of $h$ places every such sum in its epigraph. This proves the exact finite-generation assertion even when $\Delta$ has irrational vertices. It is not a rational polyhedron in $\R^{d+1}$ in general; only its spatial generators are rational.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Whole-boundary conjugate continuity]\label{boundary}
The conjugate $f^*(p)=\sup_x(p\cdot x-f(x))$ is finite on $\Delta$, infinite outside it, and continuous on the whole closed $\Delta$, including every boundary face.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $p\in\Delta$, $p\cdot x\leq h(x)$, so $-f(0)\leq f^*(p)\leq D$. Outside $\Delta$, nearest-point separation gives a direction $v$ with $p\cdot v>h(v)$, and evaluating at $tv$ makes the conjugate infinite. The supremum of affine continuous functions is convex and lower semicontinuous.

We prove the boundary fact used here. Any finite lower semicontinuous convex $g$ on a compact polytope is bounded above by the largest value at its finitely many vertices. Fix $p$ and describe that polytope by finite affine inequalities in its affine span. Choose $r>0$ smaller than every positive inactive slack at $p$ divided by the norm of its defining covector. For a nearby $q\ne p$ in the polytope, the point
\[
 z=p+r(q-p)/\|q-p\|
\]
is still feasible: active inequalities at $p$ hold along the direction to $q$ at every positive scale, and inactive ones hold by the slack choice. For $\|q-p\|<r$, write $q=(1-t)p+tz$, $t=\|q-p\|/r$. Then
\[
 g(q)\leq(1-t)g(p)+t\max_{\text{vertices }w}g(w).
\]
This proves $\limsup_{q\to p}g(q)\leq g(p)$. Lower semicontinuity gives the reverse lower limit. A zero-dimensional polytope is immediate; a lower-dimensional one uses its own affine span. Applying this proof to $f^*$ proves continuity on all of $\Delta$, rather than interior continuity alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Above approximation with every finite prescribed contact]\label{affine}
For any finite $B\subset\Q^d$ and any $\eta>0$ there is a finite convex $F\in\PA$ on rational spatial geometry such that
\[
 f\leq F\leq f+\eta\quad\hbox{on all of }\R^d,
 \qquad F(b)=f(b)\ (b\in B),\qquad F^\infty=h.
\]
It is printed-PC, boundedly relative to the same actual $\gamma$, and retains every affine support minorant of $f$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Continuity of $f$ permits the defining supremum of $f^*$ to be taken over rational $x$. At each $p\in\Delta$, choose one rational $x$ such that $p\cdot x-f(x)>f^*(p)-\eta/2$. Lemma~\ref{boundary} makes the error smaller than $\eta$ on a relative neighborhood of $p$ in the whole closed polytope. Compactness selects finitely many such samples $A_0$. Append $B$ and one rational point if necessary; call the resulting nonempty set $A$. Every appended term is still bounded above by $f^*$, so
\begin{equation}\label{dualerror}
 f^*(p)-\eta\leq g_A(p):=\max_{x\in A}(p\cdot x-f(x))\leq f^*(p)
                  \quad(p\in\Delta).
\end{equation}
All heights here are the actual real values of $f$.

Define the finite primal hull
\[
 E_A=\conv\{(x,f(x)):x\in A\}+E_h.
\]
The first summand is compact and the second is a closed finitely generated real polyhedral cone; hence the sum is closed (pass to a convergent subsequence of the bounded first summands). It is a finite real polyhedron by the elimination proof in Lemma~\ref{rational}. Its projection is the whole spatial space, since $E_h$ projects onto that space, and it is upward closed.

It lies inside $\epi f$. Indeed, convex secant monotonicity and the exact recession imply
\[
 f(y+v)\leq f(y)+h(v).
\]
To verify this, the secant increment at $t=1$ is no greater than its limit at $t\to\infty$, which is $h(v)$ by bounded distance from $h$ and the finite maximum expression for $h(y+tv)$. A convex combination of sample lifts lies above the value of $f$ at the combined spatial point. Adding $(v,t)\in E_h$ keeps it above $f$, by this inequality. Thus each vertical fiber of $E_A$ has a finite attained minimum bounded below by $f$. Write $E_A=\epi F_A$. Convexity and finite domain imply continuity by Lemma~\ref{convexfacts}. Each included sample lies on or above this minimum; since $F_A\geq f$, it follows that $F_A(b)=f(b)$ for every $b\in B$.

The conjugate of $F_A$ is $g_A$ on $\Delta$ and infinite outside it. Take the supremum of the linear functional $(p,-1)$ on the compact sample hull plus $E_h$. The compact part gives $g_A$; on the cone its supremum is zero for $p\in\Delta$ and infinite otherwise, by the polar inequalities $p\cdot v\leq h(v)$ for every $v$.

For a finite continuous convex function $H$, the supporting-plane construction in Lemma~\ref{convexfacts} proves
$H(x)=\sup_p(p\cdot x-H^*(p))$: a touching affine support gives equality from below and the conjugate definition gives the upper inequality. Apply this elementary biconjugacy to $f$ and $F_A$. Conjugating \eqref{dualerror} yields
\[
 f(x)\leq F_A(x)\leq f(x)+\eta
\]
globally, since both conjugates have precisely domain $\Delta$. There is no compact-set-only error estimate.

We check the spatial carrier rather than declaring irrational equality walls rational. Every exposed lower face of $E_A$, exposed by $(p,-1)$, is the sum of the face of the compact sample hull selected by that functional and the face of $E_h$ on which it vanishes. It is generated by subsets of the finitely many sample lifts and recession lifts. The vertical generator is excluded. Every generator has rational spatial position, although its height may be irrational. The projection of this face is therefore a rational convex hull plus a rational cone, hence a rational polyhedron. \end{proof}

\section{Primitive raw calibration and all recursive factors}\label{rawsection}
For a $k$-cell set $N_\sigma=N\cap\operatorname{span}(\sigma-\sigma)$. On a common finite PA carrier, the operator used throughout is
\begin{equation}\label{raw}
 D_fc(\tau)=\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}c(\sigma)f_\sigma(n_{\sigma/\tau})
 -f_\tau\left(\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}c(\sigma)n_{\sigma/\tau}\right),
\end{equation}
where $n_{\sigma/\tau}$ is a primitive inward quotient lift, and $f_\sigma$ denotes the linear part. Real coefficients, real signed cycle weights and zero intermediate weights are permitted. Replacing a lift by $n+t$, $t\in N_\tau$, changes the two terms by equal quantities because affine restrictions agree tangentially. Hence the coefficient is well-defined.

For completeness we give the source calibration and its finite proof chain, rather than appeal to BMPS's delegated Lemma 2.35. Choose a Euclidean metric and let $a_\sigma$ be the covolume of the saturated $N_\sigma$ in its tangent space, with $a_v=1$. A facet lattice basis extended by a primitive quotient lift is a basis of the cell lattice. Base times perpendicular height therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{pr}_{N_{\tau,\R}^\perp}n_{\sigma/\tau}
       =(a_\sigma/a_\tau)\nu_{\sigma/\tau},
\]
where $\nu$ is the inward unit conormal. Euclidean weights $C(\sigma)=a_\sigma c(\sigma)$ have zero conormal sum exactly when $c$ is lattice-balanced. Embed $x$ as $(x,1)$ in $E\oplus\R$ with product metric to satisfy BBGS's quasi-embedding convention. All tangents have last coordinate zero, so metrics and covolumes stay unchanged. Subtract an ambient extension of $f_\tau$ in \eqref{raw}; balance cancels the extension and continuity cancels tangential components. BBGS's negative unit-normal product $B$, evaluated at $-f$, then satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{calibration}
 B_{-f}(ac)(\tau)=a_\tau D_fc(\tau).
\end{equation}
This identity is applied at every successive dimension down to zero, where $a_v=1$. It is an identity for signed and zero weights as well as positive ones.

Here is the complete elementary commutation mechanism of BBGS Proposition 3.15. In the two-dimensional quotient at an actual codimension-two face of a top cell, exactly two facets remain. Let their inward unit boundary vectors be $v_1,v_2$ at angle $0<\theta<\pi$. In an oriented orthonormal basis they are $(1,0)$ and $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. The inward perpendiculars into the wedge are $(0,1)$ and $(\sin\theta,-\cos\theta)$. They have the paired expansions
\[
 v_1^\perp=a v_1+b v_2,\qquad v_2^\perp=a v_2+b v_1,
 \quad a=-\cot\theta,\quad b=\csc\theta.
\]
This follows also by reflection in the angle bisector. Expand the double product at the codimension-two face and group terms by the containing top cell. The $a$ terms are sums of $f_\tau(v_{\tau/\rho})g_\tau(v_{\tau/\rho})$, symmetric in the two functions. The $b$ terms are paired cross terms $f_\tau(v_{\tau/\rho})g_{\tau'}(v_{\tau'/\rho})$. Exchanging the two facets exchanges these cross terms and proves symmetry. A nonsimplicial top cell still has exactly two boundary facets after quotienting by that actual codimension-two face. This proves commutation for arbitrary real PA factors and arbitrary weights, without another source theorem.

The subdivision proof has three cases. A new facet inside an old weighted affine cell has equal weights and opposite normals, giving zero. A subdivided old facet retains its weights and conormal. A face whose minimal old face has larger dimension than the weighted cell receives zero weight. These exhaust the cases and give compatibility with refinement. An ambient linear form annihilates a Euclidean weight exactly when its conormal sum is zero, by pairing that sum with every form. Commutation therefore preserves balancing under PA intersection; refinement preserves it by the preceding cases. These are BBGS 3.12, 3.18 and 3.19. The commuting step printed in 3.19 refers to 3.12, but the used equality is 3.15. Conversion \eqref{calibration} transports all assertions to primitive raw recursion.

In the present sector, the criterion and ordinary branch supports give an ambient affine local support at every point. On the relative interior of a common PA face it agrees with $f_\tau$ on the tangent directions, since both signs along those directions are feasible. Subtract it in \eqref{raw}. For any nonnegative balanced intermediate cycle the residual inward slopes are nonnegative. Thus every finite PC mixed product is a nonnegative balanced cycle. Iteration gives the genuine finite raw measure $D_{f_1}\cdots D_{f_d}[X]$, with no appended factorial. A bounded PA function has zero slope on each unbounded edge; on a bounded primitive edge its two outgoing degree terms cancel. This also proves equality of all relative mixed masses by replacing factors with $\gamma$ one at a time. For bounded PA $b,c$ on a balanced one-cycle $Z$, the bounded-edge term in $\int bD_cZ$ is
\[
 \frac{Z(e)}{L_e}(b(v)-b(w))(c(w)-c(v)),
\]
where $L_e$ is primitive length. It is symmetric, proving finite bounded integration by parts. These signs and primitive units are precisely the finite operator underlying the granted completion.

\begin{lemma}[Saturated determinant recursion]\label{determinant}
On a complete saturated rank-$d$ plane with weight $c$, for rational forms $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_d$ having restriction matrix $A$ in a saturated basis,
\[
 D_{|\ell_1|}\cdots D_{|\ell_d|}[Q]
           =2^d|\det A|\delta_0.
\]
If $h=\sum_{j=1}^d|\ell_j|$, then
\[
 D_h^d[Q]=2^d d!|\det A|\delta_0.
\]
The first formula uses a unit-weight plane; multiply both formulas by $c$ for its actual weight. Singular matrices give zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Clear each row's denominator separately: $b_j=q_j\ell_j$ is integral with $q_j>0$. Let $\Lambda_0=N_Q$ and $\Lambda_j=\Lambda_0\cap\bigcap_{k\leq j}\ker b_k$. If the restriction of $b_j$ to $\Lambda_{j-1}$ is nonzero, its image is $g_j\Z$, $g_j>0$. Its kernel is saturated, and a primitive quotient lift $n_j$ has $b_j(n_j)=g_j$. Across the two halfspaces, the primitive recursion for $|b_j|$ gives exactly $2g_j[\Lambda_j]$. This is a complete subspace cycle, not a unit Euclidean normal calculation.

For independent rows, choose the $n_j$ successively and extend the saturated kernel bases. The columns $n_1,\ldots,n_d$ form a basis of $\Lambda_0$. In that basis the row matrix is triangular: $b_j(n_k)=0$ for $k>j$ and the diagonal is $g_j$. Therefore $\prod_jg_j=|\det B|$. Dividing the factors by the $q_j$ gives $2^d|\det A|$. For dependent rows some restriction is zero before rank $d$ and the corresponding divisor is zero, proving the singular case.

Expand $D_h^d$ multilinearly. Any word with a repeated factor is zero: commute the two equal factors next to each other, apply the first to obtain its complete kernel cycle, then the same form restricts identically to zero there. Among words of length $d$ in $d$ factors, exactly the $d!$ permutations contain every factor once. Each surviving word has the first mass just computed. Their sum supplies the factorial internally to the actual recursion; none is inserted afterwards. Commutation, zero factors, saturation and row denominators have all been accounted for.
\end{proof}

\section{Every crossing has an actual-reference negative-contact witness}\label{jets}
\begin{theorem}[Crossing obstruction at every actual reference]\label{contact}
If two branches meet at $v$, then for every admitted actual real reference there is a bounded continuous real PA obstacle on rational geometry whose actual unrestricted envelope is already finite relative real PA and has strictly negative raw contact integral.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose an incident branch with tangent $L_1$ and a nonzero rational line $T$ in another incident branch. Since the original planes meet only at $v$, $L_1\cap T=0$. Extend the saturated dual basis of $N_1$ to rational ambient forms $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_d$ which vanish on $T$. This is possible by prescribing their values on the rational direct sum $L_1\oplus T$, then extending a rational basis to the ambient space. This single two-space direct sum is valid; no direct-sum assumption on all incident branches is made. There is no requirement that these forms vanish on the other branches.

The ambient function $h=\sum_j|\ell_j|$ is homogeneous rational-affine convex, hence printed-PC on the actual tangent star. It has a finite rational hyperplane fan and vanishes on both directions of $T$, giving a genuine rational two-endpoint flat relation. The tangent cycle is exactly the sum of the complete planes through $v$; nonincident branches have positive distance and do not occur in the germ. It contains the positive complete subcycle $c_1[L_1]$, and its remaining actual branch summands form an effective balanced residual.

For each incident branch let $A_i$ be the restriction matrix of the $\ell_j$ in its saturated basis. By Lemma~\ref{determinant} and additivity in the cycle,
\[
 m_0(h)=2^d d!\sum_{i\in I(v)}c_i|\det A_i|
       \geq2^d d!c_1>0,
\]
with $A_1$ the identity. Singular restrictions contribute zero. All weights are their actual positive real values. The selected other branch has a kernel direction $T$ and hence zero determinant, while still supplying the flat PCC. Additional branches cannot cancel the atom because the residual is effective and their terms are nonnegative.

We check the actual-reference jet realization mechanism, rather than assuming that a maximum with an irrational reference has rational walls. On a specified finite rational carrier, continuous real PA coefficients form a finite-dimensional coordinate space with rational shared-face equations. The all-face support constraints are rational polyhedral: for each face use rational tangent generators $d_j$, including both lineality signs, and the nonnegative kernel relations of their matrix $U$. A zero-relation cone $\{\lambda\geq0:U\lambda=0\}$ is generated by finitely many rational minimal-support relations. Indeed a supported kernel of dimension greater than one allows two opposite perturbations up to a vanishing coordinate; decomposition into the two smaller supports and repetition terminates. A one-dimensional supported kernel has a rational generator by elimination. Relation costs are linear in the affine coefficients. They are equivalent to a support form by separation of the closed cone $\R_{\geq0}^m+\operatorname{image}U^\mathsf T$; its closedness is the finite independent-column argument of Lemma~\ref{rational}. A nearest point outside that cone supplies a nonnegative kernel tuple with negative cost. Supports along all face interiors imply all PCCs by propagation along contained segments, as in the criterion. Thus every face, including lineality and shared faces, is tested, without a simplicial hypothesis.

Let the actual $\gamma$ lie in the relative interior of its minimal face of this rational coefficient cone. The exact active equalities cut out a rational linear subspace $W$; inactive PC costs and the finitely many positive recession costs are strictly positive at $\gamma$. In rational coordinates on $W$, a small simplex with rational vertices inside this strict neighborhood can enclose $\gamma$ in its interior. For example use the vertices $b-r\sum e_j$ and $b+r e_j$ with small rational $r>0$ and rational $b$ sufficiently close to $\gamma$. \[
 \gamma=\sum_i t_i\gamma_i,\qquad t_i>0,\quad\sum_i t_i=1,
\]
where every $\gamma_i$ is rational-affine PC and positive-growth on the same rational carrier. This decomposition is of the actual finite reference coefficients, not of arbitrary continuous endpoints. Rational active equalities are chosen exactly, not by numerical tolerance. It remains legitimate with irrational $t_i$.

\[

\]
uses rational data, equals the norm entry near $v$, and equals $\gamma_i$ outside a compact set. \]
and leave it unchanged outside. On $r/2\leq\rho<r$ the norm entry dominates the inserted jet, giving an unchanged collar. Near $v$ the raised jet is active, so every $G_i$ has exactly the same germ $C_0+t+\delta h$.
