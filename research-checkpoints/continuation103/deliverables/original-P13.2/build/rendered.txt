2. The established projective envelope theorem and its precise use
We first state the projective background with its full domain. It is essential to distinguish this theorem from an
envelope assertion on a resolution.
Projective envelope lemma. Let Z be an original reduced irreducible geometrically unibranch projective
variety over any algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero field, of arbitrary cardinality. Let H be an
actual ample rational line bundle on Z, and let θ = c1 (H). For every bounded-above family (φi ) in the genuine
PSH(Z, θ) class, its upper semicontinuous upper envelope sup∗i φi belongs to that same class. Equivalently, for
every continuous obstacle f , Pθ (f ) is continuous and genuinely θ-psh. The normalized genuine PSH space is
compact for pointwise convergence on divisorial valuations.
This lemma, including first membership on the original target and its arbitrary-cardinality extension, is established
background here. The following proof route specifies the content being used. It is not a deduction from a smooth
quotient assumption, nor from a source headline alone.
Over a countable field, take a projective birational resolution π : Ze → Z with smooth projective source. Its
morphism is birational and projective; it need not be smooth. Set M = π ∗ H, an actual semiample nef rational
bundle. The first-membership argument descends every actual M -psh potential φ to an upper semicontinuous
scalar ψ on the original Z, with φ = ψ ◦ π at every analytic point, including values −∞. This all-point fiber
statement is part of the established descent input; equality on a dense open set and upper semicontinuity alone
would not prove it.

For an FS formula on the actual mLd , write each section as a finite sum of residual eigen-sections. Retain the
additive coefficient of the section for each of its components. The components still globally generate: simultaneous
vanishing of all components would force simultaneous vanishing of all original sections. In an eigenframe the
torified norm is the maximum of the component norms. The ultrametric inequality bounds the norm of the
original sum by that maximum. Thus the resulting actual FS formula is χ ◦ q ≥ χ, on the same mLd and reference.
