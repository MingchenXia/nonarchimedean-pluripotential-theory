Abstract and status
We reconstruct a proposed replacement polarization on a smooth integral proper variety over an arbitrary
algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero field. The retained datum is an actual rational-bundle
identity
M = (L, π : Y −→ X, A, D, ι),
ι : π ∗ L ≃ A + OY (D),
with Y smooth projective, A ample and D effective. The original proper carrier X is allowed to be nonprojective.
Choices are retained, and the proposed raw mass is An .
The reconstruction establishes the algebraic whole-fiber argument and a useful strengthening: the divisor is
saturated under π. It repairs the fixed-bundle approximation argument by using real-valued ample approximants,
and repairs divisor division by normalized ample compactness instead of a purported monotone divided net. It
gives an explicit actual ample finite-energy polar test, an intrinsic quotient-integrability criterion, and a complete
topological proof of the Radon inverse on the zero-mass complement. Finally it proves a conditional variational
transfer theorem, including all continuous original directions, actual attainment and uniqueness for the transported
measures.
This is not a complete solution of original P16.2. The required identification, for unbounded original
metrics, with a fully local intersection/nonpluripolar product remains an explicit gate. The continuous-stage
identification and a distinguished global approximation limit are proved below; these are not silently promoted to
the stronger local assertion. The source audit also lacks independently retained primary statement/proof units
for the proper analytification facts and the toric fan-to-scheme criteria. Public retrieval failed at the configured
network connection. These limitations are stated precisely in Section 12. No part of the granted ample theorem is
applied directly to a nonprojective carrier.

3.1. Explicit analytic gate A and the canonical reference
The following standard analytic facts have not been bound here to newly retained complete primary statement/proof
units. We keep their use visible:
A. A proper separated finite-type k-scheme has compact Hausdorff analytification; a proper surjective algebraic
map has continuous surjective analytification and is a closed quotient; every analytic point has a unique valuative
center on a proper algebraic carrier, compatible with proper morphisms and isometric extensions of completed
residue fields.
Parts of A have direct short explanations. For a finite affine cover Ui = Spec Ri of a proper carrier, a point
whose center lies in Ui gives a seminorm with all algebra generators bounded by one. The corresponding sets
of multiplicative seminorms are compact closed subsets of products of [0, 1]. Properness supplies centers, so
finitely many such compact sets cover the space. Separated analytification is Hausdorff. For surjectivity, the
proper fiber over H(x) is nonempty; a closed point of it has finite residue extension, to which the complete
absolute value extends, and gives an analytic lift. A continuous surjection from compact to Hausdorff is closed
and quotient. The valuative criterion supplies center compatibility. These observations clarify the hypotheses, but
do not replace the missing full primary binding for the analytic construction, separated-Hausdorff assertion, and
valuation-to-analytic-point correspondence.
Conditional on A, the canonical metric on the actual π ∗ L descends. Let x ∈ X an , and choose an L-frame e on a
Zariski neighborhood of its center cX (x). For any y over x, compatibility puts cY (y) in the pulled neighborhood, so
the canonical upstairs norm of π ∗ e is one there. If another frame differs by a unit a, its norm differs by the actual
scalar |a(x)|, identical at all lifts. This proves fiber equality of norms in an original frame, rather than equality
just at divisorial points. On an algebraic trivializing open, the pulled frame norm is continuous. Restriction of the
proper quotient to that open is still quotient, so this norm descends continuously. The descended metric will be
denoted ∥ · ∥0 .
This argument constructs a reference on an actual bundle on the proper original carrier. BJ Remark 1.24
characterizes the canonical metric on the projective source; it is not silently invoked as a projective theorem on
nonprojective X. Scalar potentials below are measured from this descended ∥ · ∥0 .
