L5@P0: Abstract. We prove that any reduced noetherian quasi-excellent scheme of
L6@P0: characteristic zero admits a strong desingularization which is functorial with
L7@P0: respect to all regular morphisms. As a main application, we deduce that any
L8@P0: reduced formal variety of characteristic zero admits a strong functorial desingularization. Also, we show that as an easy formal consequence of our main
L9@P0: result one obtains strong functorial desingularization for many other spaces
L10@P0: of characteristic zero including quasi-excellent stacks and formal schemes, and
L11@P0: complex or non-archimedean analytic spaces. Moreover, these functors easily
L12@P0: generalize to non-compact setting by use of generalized convergent blow up
L13@P0: sequences with regular centers.
L14@P0: 1. Introduction
L15@P0: 1.1. Motivation.
L16@P0: 1.1.1. Historical overview. Encouraged by Hironaka’s work [Hir] on resolution of
L17@P0: singularities, Grothendieck introduced quasi-excellent (or qe) schemes in [EGA,
L18@P0: IV2, §7.8] in order to provide a natural general framework for desingularization.
L19@P0: Grothendieck observed that the schemes studied by Hironaka were schemes of finite
L20@P0: type over a local qe scheme k, and proved that if any integral scheme of finite type
L21@P0: over a base scheme k admits a desingularization in the weakest possible sense then k
L22@P0: is quasi-excellent. Grothendieck conjectured in [EGA, IV2, 7.9.6] that the converse
L23@P0: is probably true, and thus any qe scheme admits a desingularization, and claimed
L24@P0: without proof that the conjecture holds true for noetherian qe schemes over Q
L25@P0: as can be proved by Hironaka’s method. The latter claim was never checked, so
L26@P0: desingularization of qe schemes of characteristic zero remained a conjecture until
L27@P0: the author’s work [Tem1] in 2008.
L28@P0: It was shown in [Tem1] that, indeed, desingularization of qe schemes over Q can
L29@P0: be obtained from Hironaka’s results relatively easily. Perhaps this is close to the
L30@P0: reduction Grothendieck had in mind, see Remark 1.3.3. However, the main result of
L31@P0: [Tem1] was that desingularization of qe schemes over Q follows from desingularization of varieties (by a more complicated argument). This is important, since many
L32@P0: relatively simple proofs for varieties are now available, and they establish a canonical resolution which is compatible with all smooth morphisms between varieties,
L33@P0: as opposed to the non-constructive original Hironaka’s method.
L34@P0: Key words and phrases. Resolution, singularities, quasi-excellent, functorial non-embedded
L35@P0: desingularization.

L39@P1: 1.1.2. New goals. The main disadvantage of the results from [Tem1] was that the
L40@P1: desingularization results for qe schemes were weaker than their analogs for varieties
L41@P1: in two aspects: (a) the centers of resolving blow ups were not regular, (b) no
L42@P1: functoriality/canonicity was achieved. The aim of this paper is to strengthen the
L43@P1: methods of [Tem1] in order to construct a desingularization by blowing up only
L44@P1: regular centers and so that the whole blow up sequence is functorial in all regular
L45@P1: morphisms (in particular, we cannot rely on the method of [Hir] anymore since
L46@P1: its resolution is not functorial). Our main results are Theorems 1.2.1 and 5.3.2
L47@P1: providing non-embedded desingularization of noetherian qe schemes over Q and
L48@P1: non-compact objects including all qe (formal) schemes over Q and analytic spaces in
L49@P1: characteristic zero. In comparison with the known non-embedded desingularization
L50@P1: of varieties our results give the strongest known desingularization with the exception
L51@P1: mentioned in Remark 1.2.2: the centers Vi֒→ Xi of our blow ups are not contained
L52@P1: in the maximum locus of the Hilbert-Samuel (or HS) function, moreover, Xi does
L53@P1: not have to be normally flat along Vi. As a drawback, our method does not treat
L54@P1: non-embedded desingularization of generically non-reduced schemes.
L55@P1: Although our method follows the method of [Tem1] closely, we tried to make
L56@P1: the paper as self-contained as possible. So, the familiarity with [Tem1] can be
L57@P1: useful but is not necessary. In particular, we give/recall all needed definitions.
L58@P1: As opposed to [Tem1], this time we split the exposition into two separate papers
L59@P1: concerning non-embedded and embedded desingularization. Note that unlike the
L60@P1: case of varieties, we have to deal with both cases because one cannot deduce the
L61@P1: non-embedded case from the embedded one – there are qe schemes that cannot be
L62@P1: locally embedded into regular ones. This paper deals only with the non-embedded
L63@P1: case (without boundaries), while the embedded case (with boundaries) is dealt with
L64@P1: in [Tem3].
