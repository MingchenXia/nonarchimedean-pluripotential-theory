   Let X be an analytic space and let x ∈ X . The infimum of the integers dimk {x}VZar
for V running through the set of analytic neighborhoods of x in X is called the central
dimension of x in X and is denoted centdim(X , x) [Duc18, Definition 3.2.2]. For any
analytic neighborhood V of x in X one has dimk {x}VZar ≥ dk (x), as noted in Remark 4.2;
it follows that centdim(X , x) ≥ dk (x).
   A point has central dimension zero if and only if it is a rig-point. The following Corol-
lary thus generalizes the fact that the rig-points are dense in an analytic space.

Corollary 4.4. Let X be a good, strictly k-analytic space of pure dimension n. Then for any
nonnegative integer d ≤ n, the set
                         
                           x ∈ X : centdim(X , x) = dk (x) = d

is dense in X .

Proof. Let x ∈ X , let V be a strictly affinoid neighborhood of x, and let W be the interior
of V in X ; this is an open neighborhood of x in X . Choose an admissible formal model V
of V . The special fiber Vs has dimension n. Let E ⊂ Vs be an irreducible d-dimensional
closed subset and let η be its generic point. We apply Theorem 4.1 to V and W to get a
neighborhood U of z in W , a point z ∈ W with reduction η, and a d-dimensional Zariski-
closed subspace Y ⊂ U with z ∈ Y . It follows that centdim(X , z) ≤ d, and equality holds
by Remark 4.2.                                                                            
