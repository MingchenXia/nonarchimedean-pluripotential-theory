# Locally bounded generated Psh: a multiplier-positivity induction

This is a new, unreviewed mathematical proof for the next P21.3 attempt.
It does not assert Bedford--Taylor independence or close the original.
Unlike recognition through CPsh, it works directly with the original
ordinary sheafified decreasing-limit construction.

## Statement

Retain every original hypothesis: K any complete non-Archimedean field,
including trivial valuation; X good separated strict boundaryless pure
n-dimensional, n>=2; W any ordinary open; T a middle-positive ordinary
CLD (p,p) current with d'T=d''T=0 and 0<=p<=n-1.

If f is a locally bounded real section of the generated CLD Psh sheaf on
W, its ordinary Borel order-zero product fT is defined, and

    d'd''(fT) is a positive, d'- and d''-closed ordinary current.

The assertion includes finite continuous generated-Psh functions but
does not require their recognition as CPsh. It also includes locally
bounded upper-semicontinuous sections.

## Proof

Use the original sheafified ordinal stages Psh_alpha. Prove, simultaneously
for every ordinary open, that every locally bounded real section f of
Psh_alpha has the asserted multiplier positivity. Upper semicontinuity
of every stage follows from the smooth base, decreasing infima, and
ordinary locality; it gives Borel measurability.

At rank zero f is smooth with positive smooth Hessian. Since T is doubly
closed, the smooth Leibniz identity gives

    d'd''(fT)=d'd''f wedge T,

which is middle-positive by the positive-factor rule.

At a successor, around any point of W take the ordinary open N on which
f is represented by a sequence f_j in Psh_alpha(N) decreasing to f.
The final f is locally bounded on N. Since f_j>=f, every predecessor
is finite and locally bounded below. Each f_j is upper semicontinuous
with values below +infinity, and hence bounded above on every compact
subset of N. Therefore EACH f_j is itself locally bounded on N.
The induction hypothesis legitimately applies to each predecessor;
continuity of these functions is never asserted.

Fix a compact smooth symmetric test alpha on N of the degree appropriate
for d'd''(fT). Decompose the compact symmetric complementary form
d'd''alpha as eta_1-eta_2, where the eta_i are compact middle-positive
smooth forms. Positivity of T gives finite positive Radon coefficient
measures mu_i for these two forms. Let C be the union of their supports,
a compact subset of N. On C the sequence satisfies

    inf_C f <= f_j <= sup_C f_1,

with finite endpoints. The f_j are Borel and converge pointwise to f at
every point. Bounded dominated convergence for each of the two actual
coefficient measures gives

    <f_j T,d'd''alpha> -> <fT,d'd''alpha>.

By the differential duality this is precisely

    <d'd''(f_jT),alpha> -> <d'd''(fT),alpha>.

For every middle-positive test alpha the left hand side is nonnegative
by the induction hypothesis. The right hand side is therefore
nonnegative. Symmetry of fT and compatibility of d'd'' with symmetry
give the other part of middle positivity. This proves positivity on N.
The ordinary product restricts canonically, and positivity of currents
is local; thus it proves positivity on W. No predecessor covers are
glued into one smooth potential. Each individual predecessor uses
the inductive theorem on its own ordinary sheaf.

At a limit ordinal, near any point f belongs to one earlier stage.
Local boundedness persists on that neighbourhood, so its earlier-rank
induction hypothesis proves positivity there. Ordinary locality
concludes the limit step. No single earlier rank over all of W is
needed.

The ordinal stages stabilize to the original generated Psh sheaf, so
the result follows for every locally bounded section of that sheaf.
Finally d'(d'd''S)=d''(d'd''S)=0 for every current S, by the differential
relations. Hence the resulting current is doubly closed.

## Precisely what this does and does not repair

The proof bypasses the successor gap in the printed CPsh recognition
argument: arbitrary possibly discontinuous predecessors are allowed,
and their local boundedness is supplied by the bounded FINAL function.
The compact domination argument uses coefficient measures of the
original fixed T, with their supports fixed before the sequence index.
It does not use Dini, shrinking common neighbourhoods, positive
partitions of unity, current density, strong lifts or curve restriction.

For a continuous locally bounded h, membership of BOTH h and -h in
generated Psh would consequently imply d'd''(hT)=0 by applying the lemma
to both signs and using pointedness of the positive-current cone.

For the actual problem h=U-V, however, U and V being generated Psh and
having equal canonical Hessians do not yet prove those two memberships.
The lemma only gives positivity of d'd''(UT) and d'd''(VT) separately.
Their equality, or an analytic pair/current/test violating it, remains
the essential original question. No subtraction closure of Psh is
asserted.
