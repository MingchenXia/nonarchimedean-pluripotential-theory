Continuous Psh pairs and finite curvature relations
An exact dual reduction and a generated-sheaf audit for P21.3
Research draft, 5 October 2026. The original P21.3 is unresolved in this draft. No analytic
counterexample is constructed. The result below is an exact reduction for the original continuous
generated-Psh pair class, rather than the stronger class of all continuous zero-Hessian functions. It
supplies a finite admissible-pair certificate if the reduced condition fails; it does not establish that
failure or the universal condition.

Premise 0. Analytic spaces have compact-neighbourhood bases, and ordinary opens are analytic

3. Cone operations survive every sheafified ordinal
Lemma 2. Every CLD Psh stage is an upper-semicontinuous convex cone containing constants.
Consequently PW is a convex cone and


                                    EW = PW − PW = spanR PW

is a real vector space.

Proof. At rank zero smooth functions have the asserted operations because D(af + bg) =
aDf + bDg ≥ 0 for a, b ≥ 0. Constants have zero Hessian.
At a successor, near any point choose one ordinary neighbourhood for a decreasing representation of
f and one for g. Their intersection is a common ordinary neighbourhood. Restrict both sequences
there. If fj , gj belong to the preceding cone, so does afj + bgj ; it decreases to af + bg. For strictly
positive coefficients this includes extended values; coefficient zero is handled by simply omitting
that summand, with zero supplied by a constant. Thus positive combinations belong locally to the
successor and hence belong to its sheaf. Limits of decreasing upper-semicontinuous functions are
upper semicontinuous, since {inf j fj < a} = j {fj < a}.
                                                S

At a limit ordinal λ, f and g belong near a point to stages ξ, ζ < λ. On the intersection both lie
in stage max(ξ, ζ) < λ, since the family is increasing. Its cone property gives the combination.
Ordinary sheaf locality places it in the limit stage. Constants persist by constant sequences and
inclusions. Upper semicontinuity is local, so it persists at limits as well.
For completeness, the family stabilizes: regard pairs consisting of an ordinary open and a section of
the ambient function sheaf as one set. An increasing chain cannot add a new such pair at every
successor beyond this set’s cardinal successor. Once a stage equals its successor, the construction
fixes it forever. The stationary stage contains the smooth generators and is closed under decreasing
sequences on each fixed open, because such a sequence defines a section at the next stage. It is
therefore the minimal Psh sheaf. This does not require an integrability restriction.
Taking finite continuous sections preserves the cone operations. Every real linear combination of
them splits into nonnegative and nonpositive coefficients, yielding a difference of two members of
PW . Conversely every such difference is in its real span. This proves the statement. □
This induction concerns operations, not a collapse of ranks and not continuity recognition. No
infinitely many shrinking neighbourhoods were intersected: successor addition uses two representa-
tions at a time, and limit addition uses two earlier ranks at a time. We do not assert that W 7→ EW
is a sheaf or that arbitrary local differences glue to a global difference on W . Each theorem below
uses global U, V on its specified W , exactly as the target does.

6.1. The safe inclusion and the successor gap
The inclusion CPsh ⊆ C ∩ Psh is provable directly. If Psh functions fj converge uniformly on an
ordinary open to a finite continuous f , put ej = ∥fj − f ∥. If infinitely many errors are zero, a
subsequence already equals f . Otherwise choose a subsequence with ejk+1 < ejk /3. Then


                         fjk+1 + 2ejk+1 ≤ f + 3ejk+1 ≤ f + ejk ≤ fjk + 2ejk .

The shifted functions are Psh by constants and the cone property, and decrease to f . Hence f is
Psh. The continuous part of Psh is an ordinary sheaf closed under uniform sequential limits and
containing the CPsh generators. Minimality proves the safe inclusion. Local uniform convergence
can be handled on interiors of compact neighbourhoods, with ordinary locality afterwards. This
proves no reverse inclusion.
The literal printed Proposition 8.1.11 asserts


                               CPshξ = C ∩ Pshξ        for every ordinal ξ.

Its base case is valid. At a limit, a continuous section that locally belongs to an earlier Psh rank
locally belongs to the corresponding CPsh rank if recognition at every earlier rank has already
been proved. Sheaf locality then handles the limit, without choosing one global earlier ordinal.
At a successor the hypothesis is instead


                                 u ∈ C(O),    uj ∈ Pshξ (O),      uj ↓ u.

The induction hypothesis identifies CPshξ only with the continuous members of Pshξ . It cannot be
applied to arbitrary, possibly discontinuous uj . The printed proof changes to a sequence of CPshξ
functions (CLD LF 9841–9845) without supplying that change. It therefore proves an implication
about decreasing continuous approximants, not the required successor implication about arbitrary
preceding-stage approximants.
This is a missing inference in the quoted proof, not a counterexample to Proposition 8.1.11. For a
purely topological diagnostic, on [0, 1] let fj = j −1 1{0} . The fj are upper semicontinuous, decreasing
and discontinuous, and converge uniformly to the continuous zero function. Thus even uniform
convergence to a continuous limit does not make the approximants continuous. No claim is made
that these functions are analytic Psh.
Indeed the compact upper-selection fact still holds for upper-semicontinuous approximants: if uj ↓ u
with continuous finite u and compact C ⊂ O, the open sets {uj − u < ϵ} are increasing and cover
C. A finite subcover gives one j with u ≤ uj < u + ϵ on C. This proves local uniform convergence
on compact sets, but provides no CPsh membership for uj and no smooth leaf on a common
neighbourhood.

6.2. Intermediate sheafification and positive gluing
At every successor, a preceding-stage section may itself only be represented on an ordinary cover.
Different members of a decreasing sequence can require different covers, different neighbourhoods

                                                   7
and different ranks of local representations. Selecting finitely many indices for a compact upper
obstacle does not glue their locally defined smooth leaves into one smooth positive-Hessian function
on one fixed ordinary neighbourhood. A countable intersection of chosen neighbourhoods need not
contain a neighbourhood.
At every limit, earlier ranks are local. Finitely many patches around a compact set have a maximum
rank below the limit, but replacing all their representations by one global fixed-domain representation
requires an additional gluing construction. The fixed-domain selection theorem supplied in the task
explicitly omits this intermediate sheafification and supplies no such construction.
Ordinary partitions of unity solve current localization, not automatic positive-Hessian gluing of
potentials. The elementary real example on (0, 1) is decisive for that proposed operation: the convex
functions x and 0, with positive smooth weights 1 − x and x summing to one, yield x − x2 , whose
second derivative is −2. This is only a diagnostic of the operation, not an analytic counterexample.
A different positive gluing method could succeed, but would have to prove its overlap inequalities
and fixed-neighbourhood conclusions.
Thus a full recognition proof would need, for every ordinal ξ, a justified replacement or positive
gluing procedure for a continuous decreasing limit of arbitrary Pshξ sections; it would also need to
handle the ordinary covers introduced at that rank. Neither compact Dini nor the source’s printed
induction supplies this.

6.3. Even repaired potential recognition leaves a multiplier gap
The proof of CLD Lemma 8.5.5 considers the ordinary sheaf of continuous f for which D(f T ) is
positive. Smooth generators belong to it by D(f T ) = Df ∧ T . Estimate (6) makes it stable under
uniform sequential limits; positivity is local and weakly closed. Hence the minimal CPsh sheaf
belongs to it. This independently establishes the proof’s exact CPsh conclusion. Extending it to all
continuous generated Psh requires the reverse recognition just audited.
If both h and −h are CPsh, the two currents D(hT ) and −D(hT ) are positive. A symmetric
compact test is a difference of two positive compact tests; therefore both signs positive implies zero.
This is a sound sufficient criterion. But recognizing U, V individually as CPsh would not by itself
recognize U − V and V − U as CPsh. No subtraction operation in the generating definition preserves
its cone. Equal upstairs Hessians of U, V do not provide equal Hessians for indexed approximants,
or a vanishing multiplier limit. A separate argument is still required.
GJR’s terminology does not fill this gap. Its 7.13 “psh” is current positivity. Its 10.17 begins with
weak smoothness and a zero weak form-Hessian, not just D[h] = 0 as an ordinary current. Once
the actual strict real-PL hypotheses in the task-granted positive theorem have been met over a
nontrivially valued field, that theorem already controls arbitrary target currents. The unproved new
step would be to recognize every actual pair difference in that class or its stated fixed-neighbourhood
zero-current closure. The new curve-recognition theorem also starts on an actual pure curve; it does
not restrict an arbitrary higher-dimensional ambient current equation to a curve.
Finally, GJR 12.1–2 provide strong-current restriction to ordinary tests. To use a strong product
for the present problem one needs a lift with the required closedness and the identity between its
restricted product and the ordinary order-zero product hT . Those assertions are not supplied by
the definition or restriction map. They are not premises here.

7. A consistency check, the residual scope and the precise missing condition
For the known smooth form-induced case T = δX ∧ γ, with γ smooth, symmetric, positive and
doubly closed of bidegree (p, p), the dual condition is direct membership, not just closure:


                                            νT,α = λγ∧α .                                           (8)

Indeed D(γ ∧ α) = γ ∧ Dα by both closedness equations and the Leibniz identities. The two
measures agree on all continuous f by canonical top-form integration. The test γ ∧ α is compact
and of degree (n − 1, n − 1). The associated current is middle-positive and doubly closed by the
canonical calculus and the smooth positive-factor rule. Thus this check includes generic lengths and
either valuation. It is an already known elementary range, not a solution of the residual atom and
not a density theorem for arbitrary currents.
The exact unproved condition in this draft is now:
     For every allowed K, X, p, T , every ordinary W ⊂ X, every compact smooth symmetric
     α of bidegree (n − p − 1, n − p − 1), and every finite tuple ui ∈ Psh(W ) ∩ C(W ), the
     vector (⟨D(ui T ), α⟩)i lies in the image of β 7→ (⟨D[ui ], β⟩)i for actual compact ordinary
     symmetric β of degree (n − 1, n − 1).
Equivalently, every exact signed curvature dependence must satisfy (5). Theorem 1 proves this is
exactly the original-pair problem, with every-open quantification retained. It has not been proved
for arbitrary positive doubly closed singular ordinary currents. No tuple, actual analytic current
and test violating it has been constructed.
The catalogue’s established curve, strict-polycylinder, specified-tower, invariant split-toric, real-PL
and explicitly weak-approximable ranges are not counted as new resolutions. The statement above
adds no field, smoothness, reducedness, normality, toric or approximability assumption to its formal
equivalence, but contributes no new analytic range where the equivalent universal condition is
verified. The residual higher-dimensional atom therefore remains unresolved, including its trivial-
valuation and singular/nonreduced scopes. No weighted cross-fan pushforward is used to decide the
upstairs equation.
