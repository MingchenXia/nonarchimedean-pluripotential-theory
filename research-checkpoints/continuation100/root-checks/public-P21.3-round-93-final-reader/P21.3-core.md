# Continuous Psh pairs and finite curvature relations

## An exact dual reduction and a generated-sheaf audit for P21.3

Research draft, 5 October 2026. **The original P21.3 is unresolved in this draft.** No analytic counterexample is constructed. The result below is an exact reduction for the original continuous generated-Psh pair class, rather than the stronger class of all continuous zero-Hessian functions. It supplies a finite admissible-pair certificate if the reduced condition fails; it does not establish that failure or the universal condition.

## 1. The unchanged target and the result proved here

Let $K$ be any complete non-Archimedean field, including one with trivial valuation. Let $X$ be good, separated, strict, boundaryless and pure of dimension $n\geq2$. Let $W\subset X$ be an arbitrary ordinary open. Fix a middle-positive ordinary CLD current $T=T|_W$ of bidegree $(p,p)$, with $0\leq p\leq n-1$, such that $\mathrm d'T=\mathrm d''T=0$. Write $D=\mathrm d'\mathrm d''$, and $[f]=f\delta_X|_W$. The fundamental integration current and every measure below use the canonical analytic calibration, including the actual generic Artinian component lengths. The question is

$$
U,V\in\operatorname{Psh}(W)\cap C(W),\quad D[U]=D[V]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D((U-V)T)=0. \tag{1}
$$

Here $C(W)$ means finite real continuous functions, not necessarily globally bounded. Psh means CLD Definition 8.1.1, the **generated ordinary sheaf**, and not merely positivity of the Hessian current. This distinction is essential.

Define

$$
\mathcal P_W=\operatorname{Psh}(W)\cap C(W),\qquad
\mathcal E_W=\mathcal P_W-\mathcal P_W.
$$

The first result proves that $\mathcal E_W$ is a vector space without any identification of Psh with CPsh. Let $\mathscr M_c(W)$ be finite signed Radon measures with compact support. For compact smooth symmetric forms $\beta$ and $\alpha$ of respective bidegrees $(n-1,n-1)$ and $(n-p-1,n-p-1)$, define

$$
\int f\,\mathrm d\lambda_\beta=\langle D[f],\beta\rangle,
\qquad
\int f\,\mathrm d\nu_{T,\alpha}=\langle D(fT),\alpha\rangle
\quad(f\in C(W)). \tag{2}
$$

These are actual ordinary signed measures, as proved below. Put

$$
\mathscr L_W=\{\lambda_\beta:\beta\text{ as above}\}.
$$

It is a vector subspace; there is no positivity restriction on $\beta$.

**Theorem 1 (exact original-pair criterion).** For this fixed $W,T$, the following are equivalent.

1. Equation (1) holds for every finite continuous generated-Psh pair on $W$.
2. For every $\alpha$ as above,
   $$\nu_{T,\alpha}\in\overline{\mathscr L_W}^{\,\sigma(\mathscr M_c(W),\mathcal E_W)}. \tag{3}$$
3. For every $\alpha$ and every finite tuple $u_1,\ldots,u_m\in\mathcal P_W$, there is **one** compact smooth symmetric $(n-1,n-1)$ test $\beta$, depending on that tuple and $\alpha,T,W$, such that
   $$\langle D[u_i],\beta\rangle=\langle D(u_iT),\alpha\rangle\qquad(1\leq i\leq m). \tag{4}$$
4. Every finite real curvature relation among actual continuous Psh functions is respected by the multiplier:
   $$\sum_{i=1}^m c_iD[u_i]=0
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   \sum_{i=1}^m c_i\langle D(u_iT),\alpha\rangle=0 \tag{5}$$
   for every such tuple, every real $c_i$, and every $\alpha$.

If (3) fails for a particular actual $W,T,\alpha$, the proof constructs finite continuous generated-Psh functions $U,V$ on that **same** analytic open, with upstairs $D[U]=D[V]$ and $\langle D((U-V)T),\alpha\rangle\ne0$. Thus an exhibited failure would refute P21.3 with the correct potentials, not merely with a continuous zero-Hessian function.

No such failure is exhibited here. Nor is (3), (4), or (5) proved for every allowed $T$. The theorem retains every target field, space, open, current degree and multiplicity, but is a reformulation, not a solution.

**Premise 4.** Ordinary forms have exterior products, differential Leibniz rules and compatible restriction [CLD, 3.3.10–12]. Ordinary currents restrict and form a sheaf on locally differentially separated spaces [CLD, 7.1.6–7]. The differential convention satisfies

**Premise 5.** Good Hausdorff analytic spaces are locally holomorphically and differentially separated. ## 3. Cone operations survive every sheafified ordinal

**Lemma 2.** Every CLD Psh stage is an upper-semicontinuous convex cone containing constants. Consequently $\mathcal P_W$ is a convex cone and

$$\mathcal E_W=\mathcal P_W-\mathcal P_W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\mathcal P_W$$

is a real vector space.

**Proof.** At rank zero smooth functions have the asserted operations because $D(af+bg)=aDf+bDg\geq0$ for $a,b\geq0$. Constants have zero Hessian.

At a successor, near any point choose one ordinary neighbourhood for a decreasing representation of $f$ and one for $g$. Their intersection is a common ordinary neighbourhood. Restrict both sequences there. If $f_j,g_j$ belong to the preceding cone, so does $af_j+bg_j$; it decreases to $af+bg$. For strictly positive coefficients this includes extended values; coefficient zero is handled by simply omitting that summand, with zero supplied by a constant. Thus positive combinations belong locally to the successor and hence belong to its sheaf. Limits of decreasing upper-semicontinuous functions are upper semicontinuous, since $\{\inf_j f_j<a\}=\bigcup_j\{f_j<a\}$.

At a limit ordinal $\lambda$, $f$ and $g$ belong near a point to stages $\xi,\zeta<\lambda$. On the intersection both lie in stage $\max(\xi,\zeta)<\lambda$, since the family is increasing. Its cone property gives the combination. Ordinary sheaf locality places it in the limit stage. Constants persist by constant sequences and inclusions. Upper semicontinuity is local, so it persists at limits as well.

For completeness, the family stabilizes: regard pairs consisting of an ordinary open and a section of the ambient function sheaf as one set. An increasing chain cannot add a new such pair at every successor beyond this set's cardinal successor. Once a stage equals its successor, the construction fixes it forever. The stationary stage contains the smooth generators and is closed under decreasing sequences on each fixed open, because such a sequence defines a section at the next stage. It is therefore the minimal Psh sheaf. This does not require an integrability restriction.

Taking finite continuous sections preserves the cone operations. Every real linear combination of them splits into nonnegative and nonpositive coefficients, yielding a difference of two members of $\mathcal P_W$. Conversely every such difference is in its real span. This proves the statement. $\square$

This induction concerns **operations**, not a collapse of ranks and not continuity recognition. No infinitely many shrinking neighbourhoods were intersected: successor addition uses two representations at a time, and limit addition uses two earlier ranks at a time. We do not assert that $W\mapsto\mathcal E_W$ is a sheaf or that arbitrary local differences glue to a global difference on $W$. Each theorem below uses global $U,V$ on its specified $W$, exactly as the target does.

## 4. Signed measures for ordinary multiplication

**Lemma 3.** The measures in (2) exist, are finite signed Radon measures, and have supports contained in $\operatorname{supp}\beta$ and $\operatorname{supp}\alpha$, respectively. Their definitions preserve the ordinary product and the canonical calibration.

**Proof.** Put $\eta=D\alpha$. It is a compact symmetric complementary test. By Premise 2 write $\eta=\eta_1-\eta_2$, where the $\eta_i$ are compact middle-positive tests. By Premise 3 the scalar functionals

$$g\longmapsto\langle T,g\eta_i\rangle$$

are positive Radon measures $\mu_i$. Their supports are compact, so their total masses are finite. The signed measure $\mu_1-\mu_2$ evaluates a continuous $f$ as $\langle fT,D\alpha\rangle$. Premise 4 identifies this with $\langle D(fT),\alpha\rangle$.

The measure is independent of the positive decomposition: the functionals agree on compact smooth scalars by linearity, hence on compact continuous scalars by the uniform density in Premise 5. A signed finite Radon measure is determined by those continuous integrals. If $g$ has compact smooth support disjoint from $\operatorname{supp}\eta$, its pairing is zero. Density inside that complementary open gives zero restriction there. Thus the signed measure's support is contained in $\operatorname{supp}\eta\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\alpha$, even if the positive decomposition used larger supports.

For $\lambda_\beta$, simply use the signed measure of the smooth compact top form $D\beta$ with the canonical calibration (Premises 0 and 5). Its integral against $f$ is $\langle[f],D\beta\rangle=\langle D[f],\beta\rangle$ by Premise 4, and its support is in $\operatorname{supp}\beta$. No reduced replacement or tropical pushforward is involved. $\square$

For later comparison the same decomposition proves the fixed-test estimate

$$
\left|\langle D((f-g)T),\alpha\rangle\right|
\leq \|f-g\|_{C}\,\langle T,\eta_1+\eta_2\rangle, \tag{6}
$$

where $C$ contains the two compact decomposition supports. This is a bound for the original middle-positive ordinary current. It holds for locally uniform nets of finite continuous functions. The compact $C$ and coefficient are fixed before the net index. It ensures convergence of differentiated products; it does not make their limit zero without an additional identity.

## 5. Proof of the exact pair criterion and its finite certificate

Give $\mathscr M_c(W)$ the topology defined by the seminorms $\mu\mapsto|\int e\,\mathrm d\mu|$, for $e\in\mathcal E_W$. It need not be asserted to be Hausdorff. Since $\mathcal E_W=\operatorname{span}\mathcal P_W$, the same topology is generated by evaluations against $\mathcal P_W$ alone: every finite family of differences is controlled by finitely many of their Psh summands, and the reverse inclusion is immediate from $u=u-0$.

Let

$$\mathcal H_W=\{h\in C(W):D[h]=0\}.$$

By (2) and symmetry, the annihilator of $\mathscr L_W$ in $C(W)$ is exactly $\mathcal H_W$. Thus its annihilator in $\mathcal E_W$ is $\mathcal E_W\cap\mathcal H_W$. These are precisely the equal-curvature pair differences, by Lemma 2 and the definition of $\mathcal E_W$.

Assume (3). For every $h\in\mathcal E_W\cap\mathcal H_W$, the continuous evaluation $\mu\mapsto\int h\,\mathrm d\mu$ vanishes on $\mathscr L_W$, hence on its closure. It therefore vanishes on every $\nu_{T,\alpha}$. This gives $\langle D(hT),\alpha\rangle=0$ for every symmetric $\alpha$. The current is symmetric because $T$ is symmetric, scalar multiplication preserves symmetry, and $DJ=JD$. Antisymmetric tests also pair to zero. This proves (1).

Conversely suppose that (3) fails for an actual $\nu=\nu_{T,\alpha}$. Its weak neighbourhood system is described by finitely many functions from $\mathcal P_W$. Choose $u_1,\ldots,u_m\in\mathcal P_W$ and a positive tolerance for which the corresponding neighbourhood of $\nu$ misses $\mathscr L_W$. Define

$$F(\mu)=\left(\int u_i\,\mathrm d\mu\right)_{i=1}^m\in\mathbb R^m.$$

The set $S=F(\mathscr L_W)$ is a vector subspace. Such a subspace is closed: extend a basis to a basis of $\mathbb R^m$; in those coordinates it is the common zero set of the remaining coordinates. The missing neighbourhood implies $F(\nu)\notin S$. Orthogonal projection onto $S^\perp$ gives a vector $c=(c_i)$ with

$$c\cdot S=0,\qquad c\cdot F(\nu)\ne0.$$

Put $c_i^+=\max(c_i,0)$, $c_i^-=\max(-c_i,0)$ and set

$$
U=\sum_i c_i^+u_i,\qquad V=\sum_i c_i^-u_i,\qquad h=U-V. \tag{7}
$$

Lemma 2 makes $U,V$ finite continuous Psh functions on exactly this $W$. For every $\beta$,

$$\langle D[h],\beta\rangle=\sum_i c_i\langle D[u_i],\beta\rangle=c\cdot F(\lambda_\beta)=0.$$

Symmetry gives $D[h]=0$ against all ordinary tests, not merely a coarse tropical test. Hence $D[U]=D[V]$. Meanwhile

$$\langle D((U-V)T),\alpha\rangle=c\cdot F(\nu)\ne0.$$

This contradicts (1) and proves equivalence of (1) and (3), with the promised admissible-pair certificate. This argument uses only finite-dimensional linear algebra and exact actual source functions; it imports no separation theorem for currents.

For a fixed tuple let

$$A:\beta\longmapsto(\langle D[u_i],\beta\rangle)_{i=1}^m,\qquad
v=(\langle D(u_iT),\alpha\rangle)_{i=1}^m.$$

Then $A$ is linear and its image is exactly $S$. The vector $c$ annihilates its image if and only if $\sum_i c_iD[u_i]=0$, because all symmetric compact tests are allowed and the sum is symmetric. Consequently (5) says $v$ annihilates $S^\perp$, equivalently $v\in S$. This is exactly existence of a $\beta$ in (4). It also follows that weak closure implies **exact** interpolation (4): finite evaluation images of the subspace are already closed. Finally, (4) supplies a member of $\mathscr L_W$ in every basic neighbourhood, so it implies (3). All four conditions are equivalent. $\square$

There is a useful precision in this result. When the criterion holds for a fixed $\alpha$, direct the finite subsets $F\subset\mathcal P_W$ by inclusion. Choose a test $\beta_F$ interpolating $F$ exactly. The resulting net $\lambda_{\beta_F}$ eventually agrees with $\nu_{T,\alpha}$ on each prescribed Psh function, and hence converges against every $e\in\mathcal E_W$. There is no assertion of common compact support or bounded total variation. This is not approximation by positive closed currents.

If the finitely many currents $D[u_i]$ are linearly independent, $S^\perp=0$ and $S=\mathbb R^m$; interpolation for this tuple is automatic for any $v$. Only exact signed **dependencies** of actual curvatures can supply a certificate. That finite fact does not establish (5) for dependent tuples.

The supplied stronger dual route tests all $C(W)$ and annihilates all $\mathcal H_W$. Since $\mathcal E_W\subset C(W)$, its topology is at least as strong as the topology in (3). Its closure condition is sufficient for (3). We make no assertion that the two topologies or closures agree. The refinement here makes failure of the **pair-class** closure, if exhibited, yield actual Psh potentials by (7). Failure of the stronger all-continuous closure alone would not have that consequence.

For a global current on $X$, the universal assertion is equivalent to Theorem 1's conditions for **every** ordinary $W\subset X$. Nothing in the proof extends functions from $W$ to $X$, or turns a cover of one open into an ordinary-open basis. The theorem is applied afresh on each $W$.

## 6. Audit of the generated-sheaf and multiplier routes

### 6.1. The safe inclusion and the successor gap

The inclusion $\mathrm{CPsh}\subseteq C\cap\mathrm{Psh}$ is provable directly. If Psh functions $f_j$ converge uniformly on an ordinary open to a finite continuous $f$, put $e_j=\|f_j-f\|$. If infinitely many errors are zero, a subsequence already equals $f$. Otherwise choose a subsequence with $e_{j_{k+1}}<e_{j_k}/3$. Then

$$
f_{j_{k+1}}+2e_{j_{k+1}}
\leq f+3e_{j_{k+1}}
\leq f+e_{j_k}
\leq f_{j_k}+2e_{j_k}.
$$

The shifted functions are Psh by constants and the cone property, and decrease to $f$. Hence $f$ is Psh. The continuous part of Psh is an ordinary sheaf closed under uniform sequential limits and containing the CPsh generators. Minimality proves the safe inclusion. Local uniform convergence can be handled on interiors of compact neighbourhoods, with ordinary locality afterwards. This proves no reverse inclusion.

The literal printed Proposition 8.1.11 asserts

$$\mathrm{CPsh}_\xi=C\cap\mathrm{Psh}_\xi\quad\text{for every ordinal }\xi.$$

Its base case is valid. At a limit, a continuous section that locally belongs to an earlier Psh rank locally belongs to the corresponding CPsh rank **if recognition at every earlier rank has already been proved**. Sheaf locality then handles the limit, without choosing one global earlier ordinal.

At a successor the hypothesis is instead

$$u\in C(O),\quad u_j\in\mathrm{Psh}_\xi(O),\quad u_j\downarrow u.$$

The induction hypothesis identifies $\mathrm{CPsh}_\xi$ only with the **continuous** members of $\mathrm{Psh}_\xi$. It cannot be applied to arbitrary, possibly discontinuous $u_j$. The printed proof changes to a sequence of $\mathrm{CPsh}_\xi$ functions (CLD LF 9841–9845) without supplying that change. It therefore proves an implication about decreasing **continuous** approximants, not the required successor implication about arbitrary preceding-stage approximants.

This is a missing inference in the quoted proof, not a counterexample to Proposition 8.1.11. For a purely topological diagnostic, on $[0,1]$ let $f_j=j^{-1}\mathbf1_{\{0\}}$. The $f_j$ are upper semicontinuous, decreasing and discontinuous, and converge uniformly to the continuous zero function. Thus even uniform convergence to a continuous limit does not make the approximants continuous. No claim is made that these functions are analytic Psh.

Indeed the compact upper-selection fact still holds for upper-semicontinuous approximants: if $u_j\downarrow u$ with continuous finite $u$ and compact $C\subset O$, the open sets $\{u_j-u<\epsilon\}$ are increasing and cover $C$. A finite subcover gives one $j$ with $u\leq u_j<u+\epsilon$ on $C$. This proves local uniform convergence on compact sets, but provides no CPsh membership for $u_j$ and no smooth leaf on a common neighbourhood.

### 6.2. Intermediate sheafification and positive gluing

At **every** successor, a preceding-stage section may itself only be represented on an ordinary cover. Different members of a decreasing sequence can require different covers, different neighbourhoods and different ranks of local representations. Selecting finitely many indices for a compact upper obstacle does not glue their locally defined smooth leaves into one smooth positive-Hessian function on one fixed ordinary neighbourhood. A countable intersection of chosen neighbourhoods need not contain a neighbourhood.

At **every** limit, earlier ranks are local. Finitely many patches around a compact set have a maximum rank below the limit, but replacing all their representations by one global fixed-domain representation requires an additional gluing construction. The fixed-domain selection theorem supplied in the task explicitly omits this intermediate sheafification and supplies no such construction.

Ordinary partitions of unity solve current localization, not automatic positive-Hessian gluing of potentials. The elementary real example on $(0,1)$ is decisive for that proposed operation: the convex functions $x$ and $0$, with positive smooth weights $1-x$ and $x$ summing to one, yield $x-x^2$, whose second derivative is $-2$. This is only a diagnostic of the operation, not an analytic counterexample. A different positive gluing method could succeed, but would have to prove its overlap inequalities and fixed-neighbourhood conclusions.

Thus a full recognition proof would need, for every ordinal $\xi$, a justified replacement or positive gluing procedure for a continuous decreasing limit of arbitrary $\mathrm{Psh}_\xi$ sections; it would also need to handle the ordinary covers introduced at that rank. Neither compact Dini nor the source's printed induction supplies this.

### 6.3. Even repaired potential recognition leaves a multiplier gap

The proof of CLD Lemma 8.5.5 considers the ordinary sheaf of continuous $f$ for which $D(fT)$ is positive. Smooth generators belong to it by $D(fT)=Df\wedge T$. Estimate (6) makes it stable under uniform sequential limits; positivity is local and weakly closed. Hence the minimal CPsh sheaf belongs to it. This independently establishes the proof's exact CPsh conclusion. Extending it to all continuous generated Psh requires the reverse recognition just audited.

If **both** $h$ and $-h$ are CPsh, the two currents $D(hT)$ and $-D(hT)$ are positive. A symmetric compact test is a difference of two positive compact tests; therefore both signs positive implies zero. This is a sound sufficient criterion. But recognizing $U,V$ individually as CPsh would not by itself recognize $U-V$ and $V-U$ as CPsh. No subtraction operation in the generating definition preserves its cone. Equal upstairs Hessians of $U,V$ do not provide equal Hessians for indexed approximants, or a vanishing multiplier limit. A separate argument is still required.

GJR's terminology does not fill this gap. Its 7.13 “psh” is current positivity. Its 10.17 begins with weak smoothness and a zero **weak form-Hessian**, not just $D[h]=0$ as an ordinary current. Once the actual strict real-PL hypotheses in the task-granted positive theorem have been met over a nontrivially valued field, that theorem already controls arbitrary target currents. The unproved new step would be to recognize every actual pair difference in that class or its stated fixed-neighbourhood zero-current closure. The new curve-recognition theorem also starts on an actual pure curve; it does not restrict an arbitrary higher-dimensional ambient current equation to a curve.

Finally, GJR 12.1–2 provide strong-current restriction to ordinary tests. To use a strong product for the present problem one needs a lift with the required closedness and the identity between its restricted product and the ordinary order-zero product $hT$. Those assertions are not supplied by the definition or restriction map. They are not premises here.

## 7. A consistency check, the residual scope and the precise missing condition

For the known smooth form-induced case $T=\delta_X\wedge\gamma$, with $\gamma$ smooth, symmetric, positive and doubly closed of bidegree $(p,p)$, the dual condition is direct membership, not just closure:

$$\nu_{T,\alpha}=\lambda_{\gamma\wedge\alpha}. \tag{8}$$

Indeed $D(\gamma\wedge\alpha)=\gamma\wedge D\alpha$ by both closedness equations and the Leibniz identities. The two measures agree on all continuous $f$ by canonical top-form integration. The test $\gamma\wedge\alpha$ is compact and of degree $(n-1,n-1)$. The associated current is middle-positive and doubly closed by the canonical calculus and the smooth positive-factor rule. Thus this check includes generic lengths and either valuation. It is an already known elementary range, not a solution of the residual atom and not a density theorem for arbitrary currents.

The exact unproved condition in this draft is now:

> For every allowed $K,X,p,T$, every ordinary $W\subset X$, every compact smooth symmetric $\alpha$ of bidegree $(n-p-1,n-p-1)$, and every finite tuple $u_i\in\operatorname{Psh}(W)\cap C(W)$, the vector $(\langle D(u_iT),\alpha\rangle)_i$ lies in the image of $\beta\mapsto(\langle D[u_i],\beta\rangle)_i$ for actual compact ordinary symmetric $\beta$ of degree $(n-1,n-1)$.

Equivalently, every exact signed curvature dependence must satisfy (5). Theorem 1 proves this is **exactly** the original-pair problem, with every-open quantification retained. It has not been proved for arbitrary positive doubly closed singular ordinary currents. No tuple, actual analytic current and test violating it has been constructed.

The catalogue's established curve, strict-polycylinder, specified-tower, invariant split-toric, real-PL and explicitly weak-approximable ranges are not counted as new resolutions. The statement above adds no field, smoothness, reducedness, normality, toric or approximability assumption to its formal equivalence, but contributes no new analytic range where the equivalent universal condition is verified. The residual higher-dimensional atom therefore remains unresolved, including its trivial-valuation and singular/nonreduced scopes. No weighted cross-fan pushforward is used to decide the upstairs equation.
