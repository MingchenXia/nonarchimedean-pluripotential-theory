Foundations for an exact original-pair reduction; unverified research draft.

The scope throughout is the unchanged P21.3: K any complete non-Archimedean field, including trivial valuation; X good separated strict boundaryless pure n-dimensional, n>=2; W any ordinary open; T middle-positive ordinary CLD (p,p), 0<=p<=n-1, d'T=d''T=0. D=d'd'', [f]=f delta_X with actual generic Artinian component lengths. This note proves two foundational facts for the new pairwise reduction; neither fact resolves the target.

Let P(W)=C(W) intersect generated CLD-Psh(W), where C(W) consists of finite real continuous functions and Psh is the smallest ordinary sheaf generated by smooth positive-Hessian functions and decreasing sequences, not the class merely defined by current positivity. Then P(W) is a cone containing constants, and E(W)=P(W)-P(W)=span_R P(W) is a vector space. This statement requires no Psh-to-CPsh recognition.

Here is a proof auditing every sheafified ordinal. Rank zero is the smooth positive-Hessian cone. At a successor, two sections are locally decreasing limits of previous-rank sequences. Intersect the two ordinary representation neighbourhoods at a given point, restrict both sequences, and add with fixed nonnegative coefficients. Previous-rank cone closure gives another decreasing representation; ordinary sheaf locality gives membership. Zero coefficients are omitted, avoiding any undefined zero times minus infinity; the constant zero handles the empty sum. A decreasing limit of upper-semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous, since its strict sublevel set is the union of theirs. At a limit ordinal lambda, two sections locally have earlier ranks xi,zeta<lambda; on an intersection they both have rank max(xi,zeta)<lambda. That rank's cone property and ordinary locality prove the limit case. Constants persist. Stabilization follows by considering the set of all pairs (ordinary open, ambient function section): a chain cannot add a new pair at every successor beyond that set's cardinal successor. An equal successor fixes all later stages. The stationary sheaf contains generators and is closed under decreasing sequences on each fixed open, so it is the minimal Psh sheaf. No integrability qualification is needed for this argument. Finally finite continuous sections preserve positive addition/scaling. Splitting any signed finite sum into its positive and negative coefficients proves E=span P. No global gluing theorem for E is asserted.

The second fact concerns actual signed measures. For a compact symmetric smooth beta of degree (n-1,n-1), the canonical top-form measure of D beta gives lambda_beta, with integral f d lambda_beta=<D[f],beta>. For a compact symmetric smooth alpha of degree (n-p-1,n-p-1), there is a finite signed compact Radon measure nu_(T,alpha) with integral f d nu_(T,alpha)=<D(fT),alpha> for every finite continuous f. Its support is contained in supp(alpha); lambda_beta is supported in supp(beta).

To prove the latter fact, decompose the compact symmetric complementary test eta=D alpha as eta1-eta2 with eta_i compact middle-positive. Middle positivity of T makes g -> <T,g eta_i> a positive scalar Radon measure mu_i, with finite mass because eta_i is compact. Their difference evaluates continuous f by the ordinary order-zero product as <fT,D alpha>=<D(fT),alpha>. Different decompositions agree on smooth scalar tests by linearity and then on continuous scalar tests by uniform smooth scalar density; they give the same signed measure. Outside supp(eta), the smooth scalar pairings vanish. Density in that open proves that the signed measure vanishes there. Thus its support lies in supp(eta), hence supp(alpha), even if the decomposition supports are larger. This uses middle-positive tests and does not use the faulty general strongly-positive-spanning shortcut. The analogous lambda_beta formula uses canonical top-form integration and the same dual D convention. Nilpotents and nonreduced generic lengths remain in delta_X.

In particular, on a compact C containing both decomposition supports,
|<D((f-g)T),alpha>| <= ||f-g||_C <T,eta1+eta2>.
This controls any locally uniform net with the original fixed T and test. It supplies convergence of products, not a vanishing limit for an unknown multiplier.
