Use the unchanged ambient/current scope and the cone and ordinary signed-measure results of n1. On the actual ordinary W put P=Psh(W) intersect C(W), E=P-P=span_R P, H={h in C(W):D[h]=0}, and L={lambda_beta: beta compact smooth symmetric of degree (n-1,n-1)}. The map beta -> lambda_beta is linear, so L already is a vector subspace, with no positive-test restriction. M_c(W) is the vector space of finite signed compact Radon measures. Give it the weak topology sigma(M_c,E) generated by evaluations integral e d mu for e in E. It need not be asserted Hausdorff. Because E=span P, it is also the topology generated by evaluations against P alone.

For fixed W,T, the exact original-pair assertion is equivalent to ALL the following statements:
(a) For every compact smooth symmetric alpha of degree (n-p-1,n-p-1), nu_(T,alpha) lies in the sigma(M_c,E) closure of L.
(b) For every such alpha and finite tuple u1,...,um in P, there is ONE compact smooth symmetric beta of degree (n-1,n-1), depending on the tuple, with <D[ui],beta>=<D(uiT),alpha> for every i.
(c) For every finite tuple as in (b) and every real signed curvature relation sum ci D[ui]=0, one has sum ci <D(uiT),alpha>=0 for every alpha.

Proof in full. By definition of lambda and symmetry, H is precisely L's annihilator in C(W), so E intersect H is its annihilator in E. These are exactly original equal-curvature pair differences: every E element is U-V with U,V actual finite continuous generated Psh, and D[U]=D[V] means D[U-V]=0. If (a) holds, evaluation against h in E intersect H vanishes on L and thus on its closure, hence on every nu_(T,alpha). This gives <D(hT),alpha>=0 on all symmetric tests. Since T, hT and D(hT) are symmetric (DJ=JD), antisymmetric tests also vanish; D(hT)=0.

Conversely if a particular nu=nu_(T,alpha) is outside that closure, a basic weak neighbourhood avoiding L is described by finitely many actual functions u1,...,um in P and a positive tolerance. Define F(mu)=(integral ui d mu)_i in R^m. Its subspace S=F(L) is closed: extend a basis to one of R^m, making S the zero locus of the remaining linear coordinates. Since the neighbourhood misses L, F(nu) is outside S. Orthogonal projection onto S-perp gives c=(ci) with c dot S=0 and c dot F(nu) nonzero. Put
U=sum max(ci,0) ui, V=sum max(-ci,0) ui, h=U-V.
n1's all-rank cone proof makes U and V finite continuous generated Psh on the SAME W, without CPsh conversion. For every ordinary compact symmetric beta, <D[h],beta>=c dot F(lambda_beta)=0. Hence the upstairs Hessian equation D[U]=D[V] holds against every ordinary test. But <D((U-V)T),alpha>=c dot F(nu) is nonzero. Thus an EXHIBITED failure of (a) would give the required analytic pair, current and smooth test without changing any field, space, degree or canonical length. No failure is exhibited here.

For a fixed tuple define A(beta)=(<D[ui],beta>)_i and v=(<D(uiT),alpha>)_i. The image S=A(tests) equals F(L). Its orthogonal complement consists exactly of those c for which sum ci D[ui]=0; all symmetric ordinary compact tests are available and the sum is symmetric. Therefore (c) says v is orthogonal to S-perp, equivalently v belongs to S. This is exactly (b). A point in the weak closure gives v in the closure of S; because this finite-dimensional subspace is already closed, approximation gives EXACT finite interpolation. Conversely exact interpolation puts a member of L in every finite-evaluation neighbourhood, proving (a). This proves all equivalences using only finite-dimensional linear algebra, with no current-density theorem.
