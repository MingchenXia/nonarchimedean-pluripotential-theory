## H: weighted solutions

Book VIII (6.5), LF21001–21026, gives the smooth weighted estimate on weakly pseudoconvex Kähler manifolds, with inverse smallest-curvature eigenvalue for q=1. LF21027–21054 explains the conversion to (0,q)-forms and the Ricci term; it vanishes for a Euclidean ball. Book VIII (6.9), complete statement/proof LF21061–21118, explicitly allows upper semicontinuous singular psh weights and gives the regularization/exhaustion/weak-limit method.

For the candidate's strict weight W with Hessian at least the identity, the same approximation method applied to (6.5) preserves that lower bound. Radial smoothings W_n decrease to W on smaller balls. Data norms for e^{-W_n} are bounded by the original weighted norm. On each fixed compact, upper bounds for W_n give ordinary local L2 bounds for solutions; diagonal weak limits solve the equation. Testing first against a fixed regularized weight, then monotone convergence of weights and exhaustion of the ball, gives the limiting estimate. Euclidean norm conventions must still be checked by the independent reader. Even the weaker geometry-dependent bounded-ball constant obtained directly from (6.9) suffices for every candidate application: replacing W by W-|z|² and using bounded factors yields C_D, independent of m and u; all subsequent overlap constants absorb it. Exact numeric one is not essential to R.

The data in both candidate uses are smooth cutoff derivatives supported away from the point pole, are dbar-closed, and have finite weighted norm. Their smoothness does not mean the psh weight is smooth. Fixed domains and fixed cutoffs make the geometric constants uniform in m. This validates the type of import and hypotheses, not the entire new transfer proof.

## O: a point with a uniform fixed-domain constant

OT article Corollary (4.11), complete statement/proof LF830–850, allows every psh weight on a bounded pseudoconvex domain. Remark (4.12), LF852–865, takes s(z)=(e diam Omega)^{-1}(z-z0) in the flat rank-n bundle and gives point extension. Physical PDF page15 was visually read to check all factors and the squared section/derivative norms. Theorem (6.1), whole statement/proof LF1106–1171, explicitly derives the evaluation-kernel lower bound with a constant depending only on n and the domain diameter.

One can safely obtain the ordinary unweighted-in-the-section L2 bound directly from (4.11): the function t^{2n}(-log t)^2 is bounded on 0<t<=e^{-1}, so its reciprocal controls the ordinary integrand; the determinant of ds is a fixed factor depending on the domain diameter and n, uniformly in z0. This avoids interpreting the isolated displayed C_n in (4.12) as a constant uniform under arbitrary domain rescaling. Only a fixed-domain constant independent of W=m(u+rho) and of z0 on inner balls is needed. At W(z0)=-infinity the candidate uses only the trivial nonnegative-kernel inequality. No positive-degree Manivel regularity assertion is used: the extension is of holomorphic functions, q=0.

## N: coherent multiplier ideals

OT article (7.1)–(7.3), LF1448–1495, includes the e^{-2 phi} definition and the whole Nadel coherence proof. Bourbaki (2.4.4), LF503–522, is a second complete proof; page11 was visually read because its OCR omits the formulas. It uses Hilbert sections, the strong Noetherian property, Krull and point-pole weighted solutions. Use phi=m(u+rho)/2 to match the candidate's e^{-m(u+rho)}. A smooth bounded rho on smaller balls does not change the ideal. The candidate reproves its own exact finite Hilbert-generation step; these sources validate coherence, not a grant of new R. Nonzero stalks can also be checked directly using O: on a connected ball choose a point at which the nontrivial psh weight is finite and extend the value1 with finite weighted norm. The resulting nonzero holomorphic function has a nonzero germ at every point of the ball by the identity theorem; each germ belongs to the local integrability ideal. Thus the ideal cannot have a zero stalk. The independent audit must check this reasoning, rather than read nonzeroness off a bibliography.

## C: closed sections and finite global division

Book IX (1.3)–(1.5), LF22508–22568, gives Krull separation, the analytic Krull topology and closedness of finitely generated submodules. Book IX section4.C, LF23507–23565, constructs natural Fréchet spaces of coherent sections using local quotient presentations. Entire Proposition (4.7), LF23607–23652, proves closed coherent subspaces and continuity of induced sheaf maps. A closed ideal's natural topology and its induced compact-open topology agree: the inclusion is continuous and bijective onto a closed Fréchet subspace, so the Fréchet open mapping theorem applies to its inverse.

Book IX Theorem (4.10) and Corollary (4.11), LF23714–23739, give vanishing on strongly q-complete spaces. The ensuing proof through LF23882 was read, including the bumping lemma and inverse-limit conclusion. In particular a product of balls is strongly1-complete (use the sum of the two strictly psh boundary exhaustions), so H1 of the coherent kernel of a finite generating morphism vanishes. The long exact sequence gives surjectivity O(V x conjugate V)^{s²} -> J(V x conjugate V). It is continuous between Fréchet spaces and therefore open.

At fixed m, the kernel tail tends to zero in the induced compact-open topology. It lies in J by closedness, hence converges in J's natural topology. Openness supplies coefficients with arbitrarily small sup on the fixed compact E x conjugate E. This supports the proposed relative finite reduction P<=K<=(1+delta)P including common zero sets. There is no uniform division bound as m varies; the finite truncation may depend arbitrarily on m. Whether all of the candidate's generation, product-ideal and normal-convergence premises hold is for the independent proof audit.
