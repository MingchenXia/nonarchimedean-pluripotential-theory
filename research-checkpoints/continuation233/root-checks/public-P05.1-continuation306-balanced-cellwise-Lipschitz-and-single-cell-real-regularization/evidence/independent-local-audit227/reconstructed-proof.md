## Domain and elementary foundations

Let X be a finite, pure d-dimensional, globally embedded rational polyhedral complex, including all closed faces. A fixed finite rational refinement carries the continuous actual real cellwise-affine gamma. At every codimension-one face, including any face that might otherwise be called boundary, strictly positive actual real top weights satisfy saturated-lattice balancing. Write G for the maximum intrinsic norm of gamma's affine gradients over its cells. Let f be finite, continuous, printed-PC and |f-gamma| <= C. There is no compactness, positive-growth or PC assumption on gamma in this theorem. The refinement inherits balance: a newly inserted facet has equal opposite contributions within its old cell; inherited facets retain the old equation after subdivision. Top weights are neither rounded nor rationalized.

Here and below an affine hull is supplied with its induced Euclidean metric. Ordinary foundations are real completeness, finite-dimensional compactness, linear algebra, elementary calculus and the divergence theorem on cones truncated by a sphere. The additional convex and polyhedral facts needed for the audit have the following finite proofs.

For fixed columns v_i, any nonnegative representation can be reduced to linearly independent active columns. Subtract a multiple of a nonzero dependence, chosen to make one positive coefficient vanish while keeping all coefficients nonnegative, and repeat. The inverses of the finitely many independent column maps bound the coefficient sum by K||v||. Consequently a finitely generated cone is closed: a convergent sequence has a subsequence with one independent active subset and bounded convergent coefficients. A nearest point to a closed convex set exists by restricting minimization to a large closed ball. First variation gives <w-k,z-k> <= 0. For a cone, scaling k gives <w-k,k>=0; this separates every point outside the cone without an imported separation theorem.

A cone given by finitely many inequalities has finitely many generators. Remove its lineality, the joint kernel of its rows. On the pointed quotient, with rows oriented nonnegative, their sum is positive on every nonzero cone vector. Its level-one section is bounded, since normalization of an unbounded sequence would give a unit vector with sum zero. A bounded polyhedron is a convex hull of finitely many vertices: at a nonvertex move in both signs of a direction annihilating the active rows until a new independent row becomes active, express the initial point as a combination of the endpoints, and induct on active rank. Homogenization then gives every nonempty polyhedron as a finite convex hull plus a finite recession cone, allowing both signs of lineality generators. Rational input gives rational points and rays by rational elimination. Conversely, introduce nonnegative hull/ray coefficients and eliminate them. Pairing each lower bound with each upper bound in a Fourier-Motzkin step gives exactly the remaining weak inequalities; a missing side means an unbounded feasible interval. Thus finite linear projections are polyhedra and closed; rational projections retain rational inequalities.

A finite convex function on a relative open neighborhood is locally Lipschitz. A cube's vertex values bound it above; reflection about its center bounds it below on a smaller cube. Extending a secant a fixed distance inside that cube bounds its slope by oscillation divided by distance. A relative affine lower support exists at an interior point q: project (q,f(q)-t) onto the closed epigraph over a smaller cube, normalize the outward normals and take a subsequential limit as t tends to zero. The limiting unit normal supports at (q,f(q)); its vertical component is negative. A zero vertical component would give a nonzero horizontal functional maximized at the center of a full spatial neighborhood. Divide by the negative vertical component to obtain a local support, and extend it to the entire convex domain by secant monotonicity. This proves support existence without assuming boundary subgradients. On full affine space it also proves extended biconjugacy: all affine supports appear among p.x-f*(p), while that expression is always below f. Hence f**=f for each finite convex full-space function used below.

## L0: continuous real PCC and closed-cell convexity

Discard zero coefficients from a feasible PCC tuple with endpoints x_i in rational incident cells sigma_i and center in their common face tau. Put t_i=alpha_i x_i. Its constraints are rational linear: alpha_i>0, sum alpha_i=1, A_i t_i <= alpha_i b_i, and sum t_i in tau. Impose the equalities of the minimal feasible face containing the tuple. Rational row reduction gives a rational particular solution and rational kernel basis; approximating its real coordinates by rational coordinates preserves all strict inactive inequalities and all positive alpha_i. The resulting rational endpoints t_i/alpha_i and rational centers approach the original ones. Apply the printed test to these rational tuples and use f's continuity. This proves every real-endpoint PCC, even when its limiting center is irrational. It never replaces an irrational point by a rational vertex in the same subdivision.

Inside a cell the common face may be that cell itself, as in the printed definition. The preceding argument gives ordinary convexity on each closed cell, including inherited lower-dimensional faces. Boundary values follow from the assumed continuity, not from an alleged automatic continuity theorem for printed PC.

## L1: distances, insets and simultaneous generators

Work in a rational affine hull and write P={x:A_i x<=b_i}. If x is outside P, let z be its nearest point and w=x-z. The active outward normals at z generate w. Otherwise separation of their finite closed cone gives a vector v with A_i v<=0 for every active row and <w,v>>0. All inactive rows have positive slack at z, so z+tv is feasible for small t>0, contradicting nearest-point first variation. Reduce the normal representation to independent columns, giving sum lambda_i <= H||w||. Therefore

    ||w||² = sum lambda_i(A_i x-b_i)
           <= H||w|| max_i(A_i x-b_i)_+.

This proves dist(x,P)<=H max violation, uniformly for all x, without boundedness of P. Zero rows are omitted; a whole affine hull has distance zero.

For a face tau, restrict to it every defining row of every incident top cell, and the rows defining tau itself. A valid affine inequality is either equality everywhere on tau or has strictly positive slack throughout its relative interior. There are finitely many nonidentical rows. Choose a rational relative-interior point z0 and a rational delta>0 smaller than all its slacks. Tighten precisely these rows by delta to define a nonempty rational inset. Applying the distance estimate to this inset shows that every y in tau is within D_tau=H_tau delta of some inset point z; the violation at y is at most delta. This includes every unbounded or boundary face. If the list is empty, use z=y and D_tau=0.

Each incident tangent cone is defined by rows active identically on tau and includes the tangential space T_tau. For an actual face tau of sigma its lineality is precisely T_tau: the joint kernel of all active rows is the direction space of that minimal face. The proof needs only the inclusion and also allows redundant cone descriptions. Choose finitely many rational generators, with both signs of lineality basis vectors, and assign each generator to an incident cell. At an inset point, the nonidentical rows have the fixed margin delta, while the identical rows obey their homogeneous directional constraints. One positive rational epsilon_tau, smaller than every needed margin/row-generator ratio, therefore makes z+epsilon_tau v_j feasible in its assigned cell for every inset point z and every assigned generator simultaneously. Let V_tau=max_j||v_j||. All of D, epsilon and V depend only on the fixed geometry.

## L2: actual real balance, stationary flux and both signs

For each saturated corank-one lattice inclusion N_rho in N_sigma, extend a basis of N_rho by a primitive inward quotient lift. It is a basis of N_sigma. Its perpendicular height is c_sigma/c_rho, where c denotes Euclidean covolume in the tangent space and c_point=1. Thus the projection of that primitive lift has length c_sigma/c_rho. Euclidean density mu_sigma=w_sigma c_sigma converts the lattice relation into the stationary unit-conormal relation sum mu_sigma nu_sigma,rho=0. This is an identity for the actual real weights; positivity is retained.

Translate the incident cones to y and let E be the span of their union. If their convex conical hull is proper in E, closedness and nearest-point separation give a nonzero linear form lambda nonnegative on the support and positive somewhere. Apply the divergence theorem to the tangent projection of its constant ambient gradient on each d-cone intersected with a ball. Its divergence is zero. Weighted planar-facet fluxes cancel by stationary balance. On the sphere its outward flux is lambda(x)/||x||, nonnegative everywhere and positive on a relatively open angular patch of some top cone. The positive densities make the total spherical flux strictly positive, a contradiction. In dimension one the same calculation is the weighted sum at the two sphere endpoints; dimension zero has E={0}. Cone lineality and all face tangent dimensions are allowed. Interfaces of lower codimension carry no facet flux. No omitted outside boundary equation is available under the hypotheses.

The generator cone therefore equals E. Independent-column reduction gives one finite constant K_tau such that every v in E, including both signs of every vector, has a nonnegative representation by the fixed assigned generators with coefficient sum <=K_tau||v||. The representations are fixed finite geometric choices, not finite samples of an infinite statement. A strictly positive zero-sum generator relation may also be obtained by summing representations of -v_j, but the full coefficient bound is what is needed below.

## L3: initially infinite derivatives and a support of every incident closed cell

Assume f on tau is globally Lipschitz with constant M_tau. Fix y in its relative interior and inset z, and put xi=z-y. For an assigned incident cell and feasible direction w let h_sigma(w) be the right directional derivative. Convex secants imply existence in [-infinity,infinity); there is no +infinity because at least one positive finite secant is available. It is homogeneous and subadditive on the incident cone. The subadditivity follows by applying convexity to the midpoint of y+2tu and y+2tv and passing to the right limit; all extended sums are legal since no +infinity occurs. Tangential derivatives in either sign have absolute value <=M_tau||xi||.

The secant at y+xi+epsilon v_j and |f-gamma|<=C in the same closed cell imply

    h_sigma(xi+epsilon v_j) <= 2C+G(D_tau+epsilon||v_j||).

Using epsilon v_j=(xi+epsilon v_j)+(-xi) and the tangential bound gives

    h_sigma(v_j) <= U_tau
       := [2C+(G+M_tau)D_tau]/epsilon_tau + G V_tau.

This upper bound is valid before excluding -infinity. For any finite zero-sum list of assigned incident directions, one sufficiently small common t makes all endpoints y+tw feasible. The real PCC centered at y gives sum [f(y+tw)-f(y)]/t >=0. If any incident h_sigma(w) were -infinity, append a fixed-generator representation of -w. All appended quotients have finite upper limits bounded by their scaled U_tau. The finite sum would tend to -infinity, contrary to PCC. Thus every incident derivative is finite. Passing to the limit in any finite zero-sum list now gives nonnegative total cost.

Define H_y(v) as the infimum of sum h_sigma_i(w_i) over ALL finite assigned incident directions with sum w_i=v. Fixed generators give H_y(v)<=K_tau U_tau||v||. Append a fixed-generator representation of -v to any such list; nonnegative zero-sum cost yields H_y(v)>=-K_tau U_tau||v||. Consequently H_y is finite everywhere in E, H_y(0)=0, positively homogeneous and subadditive. Concatenation proves subadditivity even when infima are not attained. Its two-sign bounds also show |H_y(v)-H_y(u)|<=K_tau U_tau||v-u||.

The finite convex support existence proved above applied at 0 gives a linear functional p on E with p.v<=H_y(v) for every v and ||p||<=K_tau U_tau; the norm bound follows by testing both unit signs. Extend it orthogonally to the full ambient space, without increasing its norm. For x in ANY incident closed cell, v=x-y is feasible, so

    p.(x-y) <= H_y(v) <= h_sigma(v) <= f(x)-f(y).

This supports f on the entire incident closed cells, including their distant unbounded parts. It is not a support derived in only one cell, a punctured-face derivative, or a sampled direction test.

## L4--L5: all faces, all subgradients and the recursion

Induct over every face dimension. Suppose supports of norm at most B have already been constructed at all points of proper faces of tau. Such supports hold on tau, since an incident top cell contains tau. At q in the relative interior, take ANY relative subgradient a. If a is nonzero, follow the ray q+t a/||a||. If it leaves tau at a finite endpoint z, closedness puts z in a proper face. The two support inequalities give

    t||a|| <= f(z)-f(q) <= t p_z.(a/||a||) <= tB.

If the ray never leaves, |f-gamma|<=C and gamma's affine restriction give its asymptotic secant slope <=G, so ||a||<=G. Therefore every relative subgradient has norm <=M_tau=max(B,G). Two interior supports prove Lipschitzness there. Mix any two closed-cell points with a fixed relative-interior point and let the mixing coefficient tend to zero; continuity yields the same global bound on the entire closed tau. Now L3 constructs its ambient incident-cell supports.

This induction does not start only at vertices. A minimal positive-dimensional face with no proper faces has every ray in question escape to infinity, so the recession argument starts it. An affine cell with lineality and no vertices is covered. For a zero-dimensional face the intrinsic Lipschitz constant is zero, but L3 can and generally does give a nonzero ambient support bound if its incident span is nonzero.

If proper-face support bounds are A_rho C+B_rho G, define a_tau=max A_rho and b_tau=1+max B_rho, with empty maxima zero. Then M_tau<=a_tau C+b_tau G. Valid next support coefficients are

    A_tau=K_tau(2/epsilon_tau+a_tau D_tau/epsilon_tau),
    B_tau=K_tau((1+b_tau)D_tau/epsilon_tau+V_tau).

For a point face one may use M=0 directly or retain these harmless upper coefficients. Take the maximum over the finite support and Lipschitz coefficient pairs. This proves a single A_X C+B_X G bound for every closed cell and every point's ambient incident-cell support. Summing along cell-contained segments proves |f(x)-f(y)|<=L d_polygonal(x,y) whenever that intrinsic distance is finite. Distinct nonincident components have no such path; for example parallel balanced lines can have different constant f values with gamma=0. This gives no false uniform ambient-distance estimate between them. L5 is precisely the cellwise input to the following construction, not a global PA gluing theorem.

## C3: exact extension without attainment or an operator

## B1--B3: the proper unbalanced orthant

On x,y>0, the Hessian quadratic form of sqrt(xy) equals -(y dx-x dy)²/[4(xy)^(3/2)], so the geometric mean is concave. Inward approximation and continuity extend the inequality to the closed orthant. Thus b=max(0,1-sqrt(xy)) is continuous convex, lies in [0,1], and f=x+y+b is continuous convex with bounded difference from gamma=x+y. Its actual recession is v_1+v_2, strictly positive on every nonzero orthant recession direction.

Every finite continuous REAL-PA function on a convex cell is globally Lipschitz: along any segment a finite rational carrier supplies a finite partition into affine pieces, whose intrinsic gradient norms have a finite maximum. Summing the segment lengths bounds the difference, including closed boundary segments by continuity. For any such a let e=||a-f||_infinity and let L be a Lipschitz constant of a-gamma. At z_n=(n²,0), w_n=(n²,n^-2), b(z_n)=1, b(w_n)=0 and distance=n^-2. Therefore 1<=2e+L/n². Passing to the continuum limit gives e>=1/2; infinite e is harmless. The affine actual REAL-PA gamma+1/2 attains precisely 1/2. The same pairs have f difference 1-n^-2 at distance tending to zero, proving failure of uniform continuity and global Lipschitzness.

At (x,0), x>0, the inward y derivative quotient is 1-sqrt(x)/sqrt(t), tending to -infinity. No finite relative subgradient can support there: the same inequality would bound that quotient below by its finite y component. A finite convex extension to an open neighborhood would be locally Lipschitz, also impossible. The other positive axis is symmetric. At the origin the finite subgradients are exactly p with 1-p_1>=0, 1-p_2>=0 and 4(1-p_1)(1-p_2)>=1: homogeneity near zero makes their support condition (1-p_1)x+(1-p_2)y>=sqrt(xy); AM-GM proves sufficiency, and minimization in x/y proves necessity. Since f>=1+x+y-sqrt(xy) everywhere, this local condition supports globally as well. This distinguishes the origin from the positive-axis failure.

A single positive orthant has just one inward primitive normal at either axis and cannot balance. Its star is in a proper halfspace, so L2's contradiction is unavailable. This is no balanced counterexample and supplies no MA statement.
