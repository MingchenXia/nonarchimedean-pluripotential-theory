Result and scope
P21.3 remains unresolved. We prove two reductions of its remaining arbitrary-current step. The
first gives an exact finite-test and dual criterion for weak positive exhaustion on one fixed ordinary
neighborhood. The second gives an exact scalar criterion, using a compact positive mass bound,
for relation preservation against all middle-positive doubly closed ordinary currents. The second
criterion does not require current exhaustion, smooth harmonic approximants, or generated-Psh
recognition of the difference. Neither geometric criterion is established for arbitrary analytic germs
or arbitrary equal-curvature pairs.
These are mathematical proofs of reductions, not a construction of a density net, a new geometric
special case, or an analytic counterexample. In particular a separator for the exhaustion cone
is not automatically a Bedford–Taylor detecting test of the prescribed form. All conclusions are
conditional on the explicitly stated ordinary-calculus premises. Execution and compilation are not
mathematical audits.

separated, strict, boundaryless, pure of dimension n ≥ 2, and let N ⊂ X be an ordinary open. Every
current and every test below is defined on this same N . Fix 0 ≤ p ≤ n − 1 and write q = n − p ≥ 1
and D = d′ d′′ . Positivity always means the middle cone. We keep the original canonical calibration
and its actual generic component lengths, including on singular and nonreduced spaces. We make

Exhaustion on one fixed ordinary neighborhood
Let G p (N ) ⊂ C be the degree-p part of the smallest system of actual current cones on ordinary opens
generated from the original δX by precisely the operations in G. Local gluing means gluing actual
currents agreeing on overlaps; it does not mean multiplying unrelated currents by a partition. All
weak limits are in a fixed degree and are required to be actual ordinary currents. One can also use
any smaller explicit construction cone closed under addition and nonnegative scaling. Everything
below holds for any such cone G ⊂ C; set G = G p (N ) for the proposed exhaustion.

Every compact ordinary smooth test has finitely many moment presentations after compact-support
localization. Including it in L therefore incorporates whatever analytic units that test uses, and the
directed set eventually includes every further refined test. The neighborhood N never shrinks with
L or m. Smooth partitions are used, if needed, to express a test; they do not glue approximating
currents. This is how the criterion retains all moment refinements and both differential equations
without introducing a coordinate kernel.
Corollary 3.1 (the exact conditional exhaustion route). If each original current T , near
every point of the open carrying the pair, has a fixed ordinary neighborhood N on which any of the
conditions in Theorem 3 holds with G = G p (N ), then (Q) holds for that current on every ordinary
open.
Proof. The grant G makes every approximant pair-compatible. For h = U − V continuous,
hSa → hT weakly by E3, whence D(hT ) = lim D(hSa ) = 0. On a compact symmetric test
ζ = η1 − η2 , approximate h by smooth g uniformly on the support of η1 + η2 . The error is bounded
by the uniform error times the tested positive mass. Weak convergence makes these masses eventually
bounded. For the fixed g, ⟨Sa − T, gζ⟩ → 0. Letting the scalar approximation error decrease proves
the asserted multiplier convergence for nets. Differential continuity gives the conclusion. Restrict the
pair to each N and use the ordinary current sheaf for equality on the given open. The fixed N may
depend on the point and on the restriction of the original current, but the proof uses neighborhoods
inside the open on which the pair is defined. It does not turn a chart cover into a basis. □
The unproved analytic statement is now the cone equality (9) on appropriate fixed neighborhoods.

not supply β = hDα, any continuous separately generated pair, or D[h] = 0. It is a failure of the
proposed exhaustion route, not a negative answer to P21.3.

What remains geometric
The first remaining statement is: for every original current and point, find one ordinary neighborhood
inside the pair’s domain on which the constructed-current polar satisfies (9), or at least every finite
test list satisfies (8) with actual candidates on that neighborhood. This would suffice by Corollary
3.1. Theorem 3 proves the direction from feasibility to a net only after feasibility has been
established; it supplies no chartwise construction or compatibility theorem.
The second remaining statement is: for every original continuous separately generated equalcanonical-curvature pair, prove the vanishing in Theorem 5 for all compact detecting tests, for
example by constructing (15) on one fixed neighborhood. This is a direct scalar route that would
control arbitrary T . Canonical nullity alone has not supplied the positive-plus-first-exact test
certificates.
