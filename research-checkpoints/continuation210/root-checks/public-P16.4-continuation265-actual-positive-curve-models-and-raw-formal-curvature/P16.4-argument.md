# A compatible étale-section argument over every quotient

Write $R=C^\circ$, $\mathfrak m=C^{\circ\circ}$, and $\kappa=R/\mathfrak m$. Choose $a\in R$ with $0<|a|<1$, and let $R_j=R/a^jR$. The $a$-adic and valuation topologies agree in rank one, so $R$ is separated and complete. Every $b\in\mathfrak m$ has some power in $a^jR$; hence $\mathfrak m/a^jR$ is a nil ideal and 
$\operatorname{Spec}R_j$ has exactly one point. No uniform exponent is claimed for this nil ideal. A finitely generated ideal consisting of nilpotents is nilpotent, but the entire maximal ideal need not be.

**Lemma 1 (section image).** Let $A\to E$ be an étale affine algebra and suppose a retraction $E\to A$ is given. Its associated section is an open and closed immersion. If $\operatorname{Spec}E$ has one point and the section hits that point, the section is an isomorphism.

**Proof.** An étale morphism has open diagonal; an affine morphism is separated and has closed diagonal. A section is the base change of that diagonal. Consequently its image is an open and closed subscheme and the section is an isomorphism onto that open subscheme. Under the stated topological condition its image is all of $\operatorname{Spec}E$. An open immersion with full underlying image is an isomorphism of schemes, including their structure sheaves. This argument does not use finiteness, a nilpotence bound, or an isomorphism deduced just from residue cardinality. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (actual formal Cartier section).** Let $B$ be an $a$-adically complete, $R$-flat admissible chart. Suppose an actual bounded $C$-rational point $p$ gives evaluation $\epsilon:B\to R$. Let $t\in B$ be an étale relative coordinate near its smooth residual point, in the sense that $B/a^jB$ is étale over a localization of $R_j[t]$ for every $j\geq1$. Put $b=\epsilon(t)$. Shrink the residual chart so that the zero fibre of $t-\bar b$ is the single reduced point $\bar p$. Then

\[
 \ker\epsilon=(t-b),\qquad B/(t-b)\simeq R.
\]

The ideal is closed and principal, its generator is a nonzerodivisor, its generic quotient is $C$ with the original evaluation, and its residual intersection has multiplicity one.

**Proof.** Evaluation of every restricted series converges at $p$, because each formal coordinate has absolute value at most one and the coefficients tend to zero. The algebraic relations hold because $p$ is an actual point of the given generic chart. Reduction gives compatible maps $\epsilon_j:B/a^jB\to R_j$, for **all** $j$, with $\epsilon_j(t)=b\bmod a^j$.

Let $E_j=(B/a^jB)/(t-b)$. This is the base change of the actual étale coordinate morphism along $t=b$, so it is étale over $R_j$. The evaluation is a retraction $E_j\to R_j$. The extended ideal $(\mathfrak m/a^j)E_j$ is nil: any one of its elements uses finitely many nil coefficients, which generate a nilpotent ideal. Thus its spectrum has the same points as its residual fibre. That fibre is exactly the chosen single reduced point $\operatorname{Spec}\kappa$. Lemma 1 proves $E_j\simeq R_j$, and the given evaluation fixes the isomorphism. These isomorphisms are automatically compatible in $j$.

Multiplication by the monic polynomial $t-b$ is injective on $R_j[t]$, even when $R_j$ has nilpotents. It remains injective after localization and then after flat étale base change to $B/a^jB$. Therefore $t-b$ is a nonzerodivisor at every level and in $B$, by separation. If $f\in\ker\epsilon$, at each level write uniquely $f=(t-b)q_j\bmod a^jB$. Uniqueness makes the $q_j$ compatible. Completeness gives $q\in B$ and $f=(t-b)q$. Conversely such a multiple evaluates to zero. This proves the exact ideal identity and its closedness, without assuming that completion is exact on an unknown ideal. Inverting $a$ gives the original $C$-point, and residual étaleness makes $t-\bar b$ a uniformizer at the smooth residual point. Its zero has length one. $\square$

This proof replaces working260's implicit formal-isomorphism assertion. It also avoids the finiteness-across-thickenings premise used in the earlier local route: the **given evaluation section** already supplies the stronger elementary argument.

# Positive approximation using shared heights

Let $G$ be the full finite connected metric graph of the actual model. Its edge lengths $\ell_e$ lie in $\Gamma=-\log|C^\times|$. This group is nonzero, divisible, and dense: root extraction gives divisibility, and it contains $\gamma\mathbb Q$ for a nonzero $\gamma$. Define the outgoing-slope Laplacian $\Delta_{\rm out}$; loops count both incident half-edges.

Choose arbitrary positive real numbers $q_v$ with $\sum q_v=D$. The vertex operator on affine edge functions has conductance $1/\ell_e$, negative diagonal and positive off-diagonal entries. For vertex values $h_v$,

\[
 -\sum_vh_v\Delta_{\rm out}h(v)
     =\sum_{e=vw}\frac{(h_w-h_v)^2}{\ell_e}.
\]

The kernel is precisely constants by connectivity. The matrix is symmetric with zero row sums, so its image is exactly the zero-sum subspace. Thus it has a solution, unique modulo constants, of

\[
 a_v+\Delta_{\rm out}h(v)=q_v.
\]

The connected one-vertex edgeless case has $a_v=q_v=D$ and requires no edge construction. For a self-loop the affine solution has equal endpoint heights and zero slope; both half-edges are retained.

Fix a small slope error $\delta>0$. Approximate **all shared vertex heights** $h_v$ by $g_v\in\Gamma$, with sufficiently small common error. On an oriented edge $v\to w$, let

\[
 s_e=(g_w-g_v)/\ell_e,\qquad
 p_e<s_e<r_e,
 \qquad p_e,r_e\in\mathbb Q,
\]

with $p_e,r_e$ within $\delta$ of the original slope. Strict bracketing is possible even at a rational or zero slope. Insert the bend

\[
 t_e=\frac{r_e\ell_e-(g_w-g_v)}{r_e-p_e},\qquad 0<t_e<\ell_e.
\]

Use slope $p_e$ up to $t_e$, and $r_e$ thereafter. Divisibility gives $t_e\in\Gamma$ and gives the bend height in $\Gamma$. The function reaches the **exact same global endpoint heights** on every incident edge. In particular no cycle period is approximated separately. The bend degree is $r_e-p_e>0$.

Put $q_*=\min q_v$ and $m=\max(1,\max\operatorname{valence}(v))$, counting loop incidences twice. Choosing outgoing-slope errors below $q_*/(4m)$ leaves every old degree at least $3q_*/4>0$. Every new bend has strictly positive degree. Uniform errors are bounded by the vertex-height error plus $\ell_e$ times the slope error. Hence these functions $g$ can approach $h$ uniformly. They may have a different refined vertex set at each stage. Positivity is verified on the **final** model, and no subsequent zero-degree vertex is silently added.

Every bend is an actual type-II point of its annulus because its distance is in $\Gamma$. BPR 3.13(2) and 4.11 give the actual dominating refined model $\mathfrak X_g$, with the same generic curve and pulled-back horizontal sections. The refinement is a subdivision, so it preserves every cycle and its length. Let $k>0$ clear all rational slope denominators. Choose constants $c_v\in C^\times$ with $-\log|c_v|=kg_v$.

# Cartier signs, unit ratios, and the entire denominator

Orient a refined edge from $v$ to $w$ and use its actual étale node chart $xy=b_e$, with $x$ a unit on the $v$-overlap and $y$ a unit on the $w$-overlap. Its length is $-\log|b_e|$. If $m_e$ is the integral slope of (kg), use meromorphic Cartier factors

\[
 f_v=c_v,\qquad f_e=c_vx^{m_e}.
\]

On the two overlaps the ratios are respectively

\[
 x^{m_e},\qquad
 \frac{c_vb_e^{m_e}}{c_w}\,y^{-m_e}.
\]

The exact endpoint-height identity gives

\[
 \left|c_vb_e^{m_e}/c_w\right|=1.
\]

This scalar belongs to $R^\times$; it need not equal one. These ratios are formal units. The chart is $R$-flat and $xy=b_e\ne0$, so (x,y), and nonzero scalars are nonzerodivisors and define legitimate meromorphic Cartier factors, also for negative exponents. Choose node opens avoiding all other nodes. Intersections of two such opens lie in smooth component opens, where both ratios to the same $c_v$ are units. Consequently the data give a genuine vertical Cartier divisor $V_g$; no scalar-equality cocycle is imposed around a cycle. Its canonical generic trivialization is the section (1) of the generically trivial line bundle.

In the local frame $f^{-1}$ of $\mathcal O(V_g)$, that canonical section is $f\cdot f^{-1}$ and has norm (|f|). Thus its weight is $-\log|f|$, which is $kg_v+m_e(-\log|x|)$ on the annulus and is constant $kg_v$ on the $v$-formal fibres. This proves the all-point equality of the vertical metric with $kg\circ r_G$, where the retraction is the actual BPR retraction. A formal unit has norm one on its whole generic chart, so this equality glues.

The degree sign follows from the same frames. At the $v$-branch, if $e_v=c_v^{-1}$ and $e_e=(c_vx^{m_e})^{-1}$, then $e_v=x^{m_e}e_e$. The residual meromorphic section $e_v$ has order $+m_e$ at the node on $C_v$. On $C_w$, the corresponding expression has order $-m_e$, with a nonzero residual unit scalar. Thus

\[
 \deg(\mathcal O(V_g)|_{C_v})
       =k\Delta_{\rm out}g(v).
\]

This sign uses positive outgoing slopes. It must not be changed to the opposite graph-divisor convention used elsewhere without changing the metric equation too. Reversing the edge changes $m_e$ to $-m_e$ and exchanges the endpoint contributions; the divisor and metric are unchanged.

Set

\[
 \mathfrak A_g=\mathfrak M_H^{\otimes k}|_{\mathfrak X_g}
               \otimes\mathcal O(V_g).
\]

Its specified generic identification is $M^{\otimes k}=kdL$. On old components its degree is $k(a_v+\Delta g(v))>0$; on a new bend component it is $k(r_e-p_e)>0$. All degrees are integers after multiplying by $k$. Paired slopes cancel, including both incidences of a loop, so their sum is (kD). Divide the **entire** metric weight by $kd$ to get a continuous formal rational metric on the original $L$. Its change from the horizontal metric on (M/d) is (g/d), not $g$ and not (kg/d). Its raw degree sum is

\[
 \frac{kD}{kd}=\frac Dd=\deg L=\lambda.
\]

This calculation fixes the scale for all subsequent operator applications.

# Canonical field identification without a singleton inference

**Lemma 4 (spectral residue field).** Let $B$ be a reduced strict $C$-affinoid algebra. Assume each nonzero spectral norm belongs to $|C^\times|$, and its canonical reduction $\widetilde B=B^\circ/B^{\circ\circ}$ is a domain. Then the spectral norm is multiplicative and gives a point $x$ with zero kernel. At the generic-reduction point,

\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal H(x)}=\operatorname{Frac}(\widetilde B).
\]

**Proof.** Normalize nonzero $f,g$ by scalars of their exact spectral norms. Both have nonzero reductions; their product has nonzero reduction in the domain. Its norm is therefore one. Scaling back proves multiplicativity and absence of zero divisors. The spectral seminorm defines an actual bounded multiplicative point. Its reduction kernel on $B^\circ$ is exactly $B^{\circ\circ}$, so it reduces to the generic point. Conversely an analytic point reducing to that generic point evaluates every normalized nonzero $f/c$ with norm one, so it evaluates every $f$ by the spectral norm. This proves uniqueness as a consequence of the norm hypotheses, not as a premise about residue fields.

By definition $\mathcal H(x)$ completes the honest field $\operatorname{Frac}(B)$. If $h\in\mathcal H(x)$ has norm at most one, choose $f/g$ with $|h-f/g|<1$. Then $|f/g|\leq1$. Choose $c\in C^\times$ with $|c|=\|g\|$. Both $f/c,g/c$ are powerbounded, the latter has nonzero canonical residue, and the residue of $h$ is

\[
  \overline{f/c}\,/\,\overline{g/c}.
\]

This gives surjectivity onto the actual completed residue field. Injectivity of the canonical fraction field follows from generic reduction. The argument includes residues of norm less than one (which are zero). It proves the whole field equality, not just a coordinate subfield statement. $\square$

# What would recognize these particular metrics as FS

The following two elementary lemmas delimit the remaining recognition application. They are included as proofs, not imported from the failed whole250 theorem.

**Lemma 6 (nonnegative flat Čech complex).** If $Z\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is proper, flat and finitely presented over a Noetherian ring, $B$ is invertible, and $H^i(Z,B)=0$ for all $i>0$, then $H^0(Z,B)$ is finite projective and arbitrary base change preserves this $H^0$ and the higher vanishing.

**Proof.** Choose a finite affine cover. Its finite intersections are affine because $Z$ is separated over the affine base. The bounded alternating Čech complex has degrees $0,\ldots,r-1$. Its terms are flat over $A$, since affine chart rings are $A$-flat and local line modules are projective of rank one over those rings. Base change of the cover gives precisely the tensor complex for any $A$-algebra. Starting at the top, higher exactness gives short exact sequences $0\to Z^{q-1}\to C^{q-1}\to Z^q\to0$. The top $Z^q=C^q$ is flat. Downward induction makes every kernel flat and each short sequence universally exact. In particular $Z^0=H^0$ is flat and tensoring preserves its kernel and all the higher exactness. Noetherian proper coherent cohomology makes $H^0$ finite, hence finitely presented; a finitely presented flat module is projective. This proves the claim. The argument would fail for a complex with terms in negative degrees, such as the degree (-1,0) presentation of $\mathbb Z/2$. $\square$

**Lemma 7 (actual compatible lifting and metric recognition).** For the actual complete flat formal pair of Theorem 3, suppose that for a tensor power $r>0$ of its bundle, the **whole** scheme $X_1$ has $H^1(X_1,A_1^r)=0$ and finitely many global sections generating $A_1^r$. If the original-field projective $H^0$ comparison is applied to the already specified original bundle $rkdL$, these are algebraic sections of that same bundle and their zero-weight FS metric is exactly $\phi_g$ at every point after division by (rkd).

**Proof.** Flatness and local freeness give, by multiplication by $a^j$, exact sequences on the common topological space

\[
 0\longrightarrow A_1^r\longrightarrow A_{j+1}^r
       \longrightarrow A_j^r\longrightarrow0.
\]

The indicated $H^1$ vanishing makes every $H^0$ reduction surjective. Lift the same finite generator list compatibly for all $j$. In complete local frames, the formal sheaf is the inverse limit of its reductions; sections are compatible local tuples, so global sections commute with this inverse limit. The lifts are actual formal global sections.
