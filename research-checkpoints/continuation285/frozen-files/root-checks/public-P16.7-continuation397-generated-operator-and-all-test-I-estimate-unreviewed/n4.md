# A genuine all-test strictness estimate and its remaining separation gate

The following is a new advance using n1--n3. It does not assert that degeneracy of $\theta$ has disappeared. For bounded continuous dpsh $f,h$ and a positive closed current $T$, write $Q_T(f,h)=-\int fDD(hT)$, with its positive Hilbert interpretation from n2. Extend the notation to differences of positive closed currents by linearity.

For $a,b,w\in\mathcal C$, put $f=a-b$, $g=(a+b)/2$, $h=w-g$ and
\[
 I(a,b)=\int(a-b)[MA(b)-MA(a)].
\]
The bounded product expansion and Green give
\[
 I(a,b)=Q_{\theta_a}(f,f)+Q_{\theta_b}(f,f)\ge0.
\]
The exact transgression identity, including its SIGN, is
\[
 Q_{\theta_w}(f,f)-Q_{\theta_g}(f,f)
 =Q_{\theta_a}(f,h)-Q_{\theta_b}(f,h).
\]
Indeed the left side is $-\int fDDf\wedge DDh$ by the recursive dpsh product rule. Symmetry of the product makes this $-\int fDDh\wedge DDf=Q_{DDf}(f,h)$, which is the right side because $DDf=\theta_a-\theta_b$. No arbitrary continuous coefficient is asserted closed, and no global Hessian domination is used. Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives
\[
 Q_{\theta_w}(f,f)\le\tfrac12 I(a,b)
 +\sqrt{I(a,b)\bigl(Q_{\theta_a}(h,h)+Q_{\theta_b}(h,h)\bigr)}.
\]

Here is an explicit uniform bound for the last factor. Normalize $a,b,w\le0$ by individual constants and assume their energies are at least $-C$, with $C\ge0$. Constants change neither the gradients nor $I$. By the finite-dual bound of n3, for $p,q$ among these potentials, $\int(-p)MA(q)\le3C$. Also $B(q,r)\le2MA((q+r)/2)$ and concavity bound its dual by $2C$, so $\int(-p)B(q,r)\le6C$. Since $|h|\le -w+(-a-b)/2$, it follows that
\[
 \int|h|MA(q)\le6C,\qquad\int|h|B(q,r)\le12C.
\]
Finally
\[
 Q_{\theta_a}(h,h)
 =-\int h\,[B(a,w)-(MA(a)+B(a,b))/2]\le21C,
\]
and the same bound holds with $b$. Thus the actual bounded all-direction estimate is
\[
 \boxed{Q_{\theta_w}(a-b,a-b)\le I(a,b)/2+\sqrt{42C\,I(a,b)}}.
\]
The numerical coefficient comes from $12C+3C+6C=21C$ in each current slot. This is a proved surface I-estimate for arbitrary bounded $\theta$-psh tests, not a proposed CLN bridge.

Now suppose $u,v\in\mathcal G$ and $MA(u)=MA(v)$. Take ANY bounded continuous decreasing presentations $u_j,v_j$, subtract fixed upper bounds from the whole presentations, and normalize a fixed $w\in\mathcal C$ below zero. Their energies have a common finite lower bound $-C$. All four terms of
\[
 I_j=\int u_jMA(v_j)-\int v_jMA(v_j)
 -\int u_jMA(u_j)+\int v_jMA(u_j)
\]
converge by the moving moments of n3. Equality of the limiting measures gives $I_j\to0$, and bounded positivity gives $I_j\ge0$. Consequently
\[
 Q_{\theta_w}(u_j-v_j,u_j-v_j)\longrightarrow0
\]
for EVERY fixed bounded test $w$. This conclusion is independent of both presentations. It is formulated as convergence of the actual bounded gradients; it does not define a gradient for an arbitrary discontinuous difference by fiat.

There is also a concrete local analytic consequence for bounded solutions. If $u,v\in\mathcal C$ have the same $MA$, the estimate gives $Q_{\theta_w}(u-v,u-v)=0$ for every $w\in\mathcal C$. Green and Cauchy--Schwarz with any compact smooth scalar test imply
\[
 DD((u-v)\theta_w)=0.
\]
This detects local divisors despite the vanishing of $\theta$ on residue bidiscs. To see it, let $P$ be a nonzero Laurent polynomial, and choose a lift chart $|x_i-a_i|<r$ centred at the tropical class of a point of its zero locus. Fix a smooth convex function $\chi$ on $\mathbf R$ with $\chi=0$ on $(-\infty,0]$, $0\le\chi'\le1$, and $\chi(t)=t-c$ for $t\ge1$. Set $\psi_N=\chi(\log|P|+N)$, extended by zero near its zeros. This is an ordinary smooth psh function: at a zero it is locally constant, and elsewhere it is a convex smooth function of a unit moment.

Choose $0<\delta<r^2/4$ and $\varepsilon_N>0$ so $\delta+\varepsilon_N\sup\psi_N<r^2/2$ on the closed chart. The global bounded potential
\[
 w_N=\max(\rho_a,\delta+\varepsilon_N\psi_N)-\rho_a,
\]
extended by zero, belongs to $\mathcal C$ by exactly the collar and soft-maximum proof of n1. On the FIXED smaller ordinary neighbourhood $\{\rho_a<\delta/2\}$, independent of $N$, its curvature is $\theta_{w_N}=\varepsilon_N DD\psi_N$. Hence $DD((u-v)DD\psi_N)=0$ there for every $N$.

The precise further source foundation is CLD v2 Theorem 16.7.3: on a boundaryless pure-dimensional analytic space, for a regular meromorphic function $P$, $DD[\log|P|]=\delta_{\operatorname{div}P}$. The complete statement and immediate proof, literal LF23468--23656, were read. Its proof uses compact tropical charts, the boundary product calibration of Theorem 14.7.2 and Stokes; those deeper inputs are conventional, not newly recursively audited. Our smooth torus chart and nonzero holomorphic $P$ satisfy regularity. The proof also explains why compact smooth top-test supports avoid the lower-dimensional zero locus. Thus $[\psi_N-N]\to[\log|P|-c]$ on those tests (indeed eventually exactly there), giving positive-current convergence $DD\psi_N\to\delta_{\operatorname{div}P}$. Applying the continuous-multiplier convergence of CLD 8.5.1 to the bounded continuous $u-v$, then differentiating, proves the new local identity
\[
 DD((u-v)\delta_{\operatorname{div}P})=0.
\]
It concerns actual analytic divisor currents, not an abstract tropical obstruction. It also shows exactly where bounded continuity was needed: multiplication through this divisor limit is not licensed for unbounded generated differences.

The remaining uniqueness gap is explicit. We have not proved that these vanishing gradient/current tests force the actual pointwise generated potentials to differ by a constant. In particular $Q_\theta=0$ alone cannot be used for that inference. Wanner's retained theorem, arXiv:1801.04713v3, assumes an algebraically closed complete nontrivially valued field and continuous functions on open subsets of smooth algebraic curves. Our $K=\mathbf C((s))$ is not algebraically closed. Its continuous curve recognition theorem cannot simply be cited here, and the generated unbounded divisor limit would require a separate restriction/integrability argument even after a justified field change. No higher-dimensional classical-psh recognition follows from one positive Hessian current. Artifact: n4.md, with the full proof in scoped-paper.tex and proof-companion.md. This strictness estimate and bounded divisor consequence are partial progress, not full uniqueness or P16.7 closure.
