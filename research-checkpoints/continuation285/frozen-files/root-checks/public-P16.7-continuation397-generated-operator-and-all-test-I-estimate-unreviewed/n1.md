# The analytic carrier, positivity datum, and separating tests

This is an independent proof draft relative to the conventional published foundations stated below. No supplied note or positive verdict is a premise. Put $K=\mathbf C((s))$, $|s|=e^{-1/8}$, $t=s^8$, $Y=(\mathbf G_m^2)^{\rm an}$ and $\Lambda=\langle(t,2),(3,t)\rangle$. On $Y$ let $x_i=-\log|z_i|$. Then $\Lambda$ translates $x$ by $\mathbf Z^2$. Let $X=Y/\Lambda$.

The following analytic foundations are retained. Conrad--Temkin, Non-archimedean analytification of algebraic spaces, author paper at https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/analgpaper.pdf, Lemma 4.2.3: an etale equivalence relation has an analytic quotient if its restrictions to an ordinary open cover do; the resulting local quotients are open subspaces covering the quotient. Lemma 4.2.4: pullback is an equivalence between coherent G-sheaves and coherent descent data for a flat quasi-finite analytic surjection. Its proof reduces locally to finite faithfully flat affinoid algebras, where completed tensor products are ordinary ones. Closed immersions descend by their coherent ideals. Theorem 2.3.1 reflects separatedness and smoothness under analytification, and properness for finite-type maps; Example 2.3.2 compares algebraic and analytic stalks at closed points. The applied statements and complete immediate arguments were read in the retained primary extracts. Deeper Berkovich descent is a conventional source foundation.

Inverse images of tropical rectangles of coordinate widths less than one have disjoint nonidentity translates. They furnish ordinary open quotient charts. On bounded tropical ranges the relation is a finite union of disjoint closed deck graphs. A compact fundamental polyannulus $x^{-1}([0,1]^2)$ surjects onto $X$. Choose a finite cover of this square by injective charts. Their disjoint union maps to $X$ by a flat quasi-finite surjection, with finite fibres; its product has the same properties. The relation ideals on this cover have the deck cocycle, so coherent descent proves that the quotient diagonal is closed. This does not apply quasi-finite descent to the infinite-sheeted map $Y\to X$. Rectangles with endpoints in $(1/8)\mathbf Z$ and nested widths below one give strict affinoid neighbourhoods. Thus $X$ is compact Hausdorff, separated, good, strict, smooth, pure two-dimensional, boundaryless and proper. Connectedness follows from the connected nested Laurent polyannuli covering $Y$ and surjectivity of $Y\to X$.

Here is an independent nonalgebraization argument. Gruson, Fibres vectoriels sur un polydisque ultrametrique (1968), IV.5 Theorem 1, applies to finite completed tensor products of disc algebras with radii in the divisible valuation group and unit-circle algebras. It gives $SK^0(H)=0$ (and $SK^1(H)=0$, unused here). A rank-one projective module has the class of $H$; stable equality followed by determinants trivializes it. Only disc/circle factors are inputs to this theorem. Wide annuli are handled separately: a line bundle on $A(r,R)\times Z$ can be trivialized on $C(r)\times Z$, then glued to the trivial bundle on $D(r)\times Z$. The finite rational cover $D(r)\cup A(r,R)=D(R)$ has intersection $C(r)$. Induction on the number of wide factors trivializes the resulting line bundle on $D(R)\times Z$ and hence the original one. This uses Tate--Kiehl coherent gluing, in Van der Put, Rigid analytic spaces (1975--76), 2.14--2.16.3: coherence is local on finite affinoid covers, coherent sheaves on an affinoid are finite modules and have vanishing higher cohomology, with the indicated descent equalizer. The complete relevant text and the J7-09 diagram were read, including its explicit appeal to Kiehl. Consequently every $P_m=\{|s|^m\le |z_i|\le |s|^{-m}\}$ has Picard group zero.

We also need compatibility on the noncompact torus, not merely this finite result. If $b=\sum b_\beta z^\beta$ is a unit on $P_m$, its Gauss log norm is convex; its inverse has the opposite convex norm. The common norm is therefore affine. A monomial maximizing it at an interior point agrees with this affine norm throughout the tropical box. Writing that exponent as $\alpha$ gives
\[
 |b_\beta/b_\alpha|e^{(m/8)|\beta-\alpha|_1}\le1.
\]
Thus $b=b_\alpha z^\alpha(1+g)$, with zero constant coefficient and $\|g\|_{P_j}\le |s|^{m-j}$ for $j<m$. A global unit on $Y$ is a scalar monomial: any different exponent would dominate along some tropical ray. Normalize successive trivializing frames by scalar monomials, so $e_{m+1}=(1+g_m)e_m$ on $P_m$. On each fixed $P_j$ the products $p_j=\prod_{m=j}^{\infty}(1+g_m)$ converge to units; the first finitely many factors are already units and the remaining factors and inverse tails converge by the displayed bound. The frames $e_jp_j$ agree on overlaps. They glue on the interior exhaustion, proving $\operatorname{Pic}(Y)=0$.

An equivariant line bundle frame then has scalar-monomial multipliers $c_i z^{h_i}$. Write $h_1=(A,B)$, $h_2=(C,D)$. Commutativity gives $t^C2^D=3^At^B$, hence $C=B$, $A=D=0$. Thus $h_1=(0,k)$ and $h_2=(k,0)$. For $k\ne0$, take $\lambda=\lambda_1\lambda_2^{\operatorname{sign}k}$ and $h=(k\operatorname{sign}k,k)$. Then $Q=v(\lambda^h)=2|k|>0$. Any nonzero analytic section satisfying $f(\lambda z)=cz^h f(z)$ has Laurent coefficients
\[
 a_{m+jh}=a_m c^j\lambda^{-jm-hj(j+1)/2}.
\]
Their valuations have leading term $-Qj^2/2$, contradicting Laurent convergence on the unit polyannulus. For $k=0$, two nonzero coefficient exponents must have identical pairings with both period valuations and hence coincide. Every nonzero equivariant section is a single nowhere-vanishing monomial, and two sections with the same multipliers are proportional.

For a meromorphic fraction $F=g/h$ on the smooth torus, its pole ideal $J=\{b:bF\in\mathcal O\}$ is the coherent kernel of multiplication by $g$ into $\mathcal O/(h)$. In regular local rings cancellation makes this ideal principal of rank one. Stacks Tag 0AG0 says a regular local ring is a UFD; its complete induction proof was read. Flat analytic restrictions commute with this kernel and glue the ideal. The exact regularity inputs are Ducros, Families of Berkovich spaces, 2.1.3(2),(5): affinoid-domain and analytic-local restriction maps are regular; and 2.1.4: for a scheme locally of finite type over an affinoid algebra the algebraic-to-analytic local map is regular. These are published foundations, with their cited deeper Ducros/Berkovich proofs disclosed rather than freshly reconstructed. A global frame of $J$ on $Y$ produces holomorphic $f_0$ and $f_1=Ff_0$ with the same deck multipliers. The preceding recurrence makes $F$ constant. Therefore $\Gamma(X,\mathcal M_X)=K$.

If $X=Z^{\rm an}$ for a separated finite-type $K$-algebraic space, the reflection theorem makes $Z$ smooth and proper of dimension two. It is connected, hence integral: regularity separates its finitely many irreducible components. Stacks Tags 086U, 0BB1, 0EN6, 0ENA and 0END supply a dense scheme locus and $\Gamma(Z,\mathcal K_Z)=K(Z)$, of transcendence degree two. Their precise hypotheses are: decency for the dense scheme locus; finitely many irreducible components on quasi-compact etale scheme charts; all weak associates of codimension zero; and generic points represented by field monomorphisms for 0ENA. Separated finite-type smooth $Z$ satisfies these conditions. The exact statements and immediate proofs were read. Regular algebraic-to-analytic local maps preserve nonzerodivisors, so local fractions analytify and descend. A closed point in a nonempty scheme open avoiding numerator and denominator zeros detects a nonzero rational function. Thus $K(Z)$ injects into $\Gamma(X,\mathcal M_X)=K$, a contradiction. This excludes separated finite-type algebraic-space algebraizations at the specified base field. No assertion after every field extension is made.

Put $\rho=|x|^2/2$ and $\theta=DD\rho$, where $DD=d'd''$. Deck changes of $\rho$ are smooth affine functions, so $\theta$ descends as an ordinary smooth positive closed form with local smooth psh potentials. The ordinary identity-torus integration convention has saturated lattice $\mathbf Z^2$, degree one, and measure $2\det(\operatorname{Hess}F)\,dx$ for a convex smooth weight $F$, supported on its Gauss section. This is the conventional CLD calibrated integration rule, with the factor $2!=2$ retained. Applied to $\rho$ and the unit fundamental square it gives $\theta^2=2i_*dx$ and raw mass $M=2$.

Let $\mathscr P$ be the minimal continuous psh sheaf of CLD Definition 8.1.7, closed under local uniform limits, and let $\mathcal C=\{u\in C(X):\rho+u\in\mathscr P\text{ locally}\}$. Its convex/max closure follows independently as follows: convex combinations of smooth psh weights are psh, and their smooth convex soft maxima converge uniformly to their maximum. Fixing one smooth weight, the other weights for which either operation belongs to $\mathscr P$ form a sheaf closed under uniform limits and containing the smooth weights. Minimality gives every weight; fix such a weight and repeat the argument in the other slot. Thus both operations preserve $\mathscr P$ and, after adding the common local $\rho$, preserve $\mathcal C$. Constants are included. This class separates every pair of analytic points. Different tropical classes are separated by a small smooth periodic function $\varepsilon f(x)$ with $I+\varepsilon\operatorname{Hess}f\ge0$. For points in one tropical fibre, lift their common tropical coordinate to $a$ and take a chart $|x_i-a_i|<r<1/2$. Distinct seminorms differ on a Laurent polynomial $P$. Label them so $|P(p)|>|P(q)|$, and set $b=\log|P(p)|$. On the closed polyannulus let $L$ bound $\log|P|$ from above. Choose $0<\delta<r^2/4$ and $\varepsilon>0$ with $\delta+\varepsilon(L-b)<r^2/2$. Set
\[
 F=\max(\rho_a,\delta+\varepsilon(\log|P|-b)),\quad
 \rho_a=|x-a|^2/2,\quad u=F-\rho_a.
\]
The strict boundary inequality gives a tropical collar on which $u=0$, so extension by zero is continuous. Near a zero of $P$, $F=\rho_a$ on an ordinary neighbourhood. Elsewhere a smooth convex soft maximum of the two ordinary smooth psh weights approximates $F$ locally uniformly. Thus this function belongs even to the first approximation stage, without identifying that stage with all of $\mathscr P$. It has $u(p)=\delta>u(q)$.

The span $\mathcal D=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbf R}\mathcal C$ is a vector lattice. Convexity writes its members as $A(p-q)$ with $p,q\in\mathcal C$. For a common $A>0$,
\[
 \max(A(p-q),A(r-s))=2A\max((p+s)/2,(r+q)/2)-2A(q+s)/2.
\]
Minima follow by negation. For any continuous $h$ and tolerance $\eta$, point separation and constants give $f_{xy}\in\mathcal D$ agreeing with $h$ at $x,y$. At fixed $x$, finitely many sets $\{f_{xy}>h-\eta\}$ cover $X$; their maximum $g_x$ is everywhere above $h-\eta$ and equals $h$ at $x$. Finitely many neighbourhoods on which $g_x<h+\eta$ cover $X$. Their minimum approximates $h$ uniformly within $\eta$. Hence $\mathcal D$ is dense in all of $C(X)$, including tests off the toric skeleton.

There is no global strictness assertion: on the residue bidisc $|z_i-1|<1$, $x_i=0$ and $\theta=0$. Curvature in moments $z_i-1$ cannot be bounded by a multiple of $\theta$ there. The later strictness argument uses other bounded potentials.
