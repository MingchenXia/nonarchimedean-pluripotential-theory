For $u,v\in\mathcal C$ put $\theta_u=\theta+DD[u]$ and
\[
 B(u,v)=\theta\wedge\theta_v+DD(u\theta_v),\qquad MA(u)=B(u,u).
\]
Locally this is the recursive product of the actual continuous psh weights. It is symmetric, positive and local; smooth affine changes of local potentials glue it. Testing exact terms by $1$ gives $B(u,v)(X)=M=2$. Convex and maximum closure of $\mathcal C$ follow from convex combinations and soft maxima of smooth weights, then two successive minimal-sheaf arguments, each closed under uniform limits. Thus they hold at every stage.

Here is the additional Green proof, not a citation to the supplied ROOT chain. Fix a positive closed $(1,1)$ current $T$. Complete compact smooth $(1,0)$ forms modulo the null space of $\int a\wedge Jb\wedge T$. Symmetry and positivity give a Hilbert pairing and Cauchy--Schwarz. Use local seminorms $Q_{T,\chi}(f,g)=\int\chi\,d'f\wedge d''g\wedge T$ with nonnegative compact smooth cutoffs. Let $\mathscr H_T$ consist of continuous functions locally approximable uniformly by smooth functions with strongly convergent gradients in these seminorms. Finite smooth partitions patch the approximations: the gradients of $\sum\chi_i f_{i,k}$ are $\sum\chi_i d'f_{i,k}+\sum f_{i,k}d'\chi_i$. The two sums converge strongly by gradient convergence and uniform convergence, respectively. Products and cutoffs obey the same argument. Continuous bounded coefficients act in the completion, using scalar smooth approximation and the norm bound $\|fa\|\le\|f\|_\infty\|a\|$.

Gradients are unique. If smooth $f_k\to0$ uniformly and their gradients tend strongly to $a$, closedness gives
\[
 \int d'f_k\wedge Jb\wedge T=-\int f_k\,d'(Jb)\wedge T\longrightarrow0
\]
for every compact smooth $b$. The right pairing is a finite signed measure. Smooth forms are dense by the definition of the completion, so $a=0$. On local patches use tests supported in a larger patch and cutoffs to obtain the same conclusion on a smaller one.

If $f\in\mathscr H_T$ and $L_Tf=DD(fT)$ is signed Radon, then for compactly supported $g\in\mathscr H_T$, $\int gL_Tf=-Q_T(f,g)$. First prove this for smooth $g$ by smooth approximation of $f$ and current differentiation; then approximate $g$ uniformly and strongly, with a fixed compact cutoff. This implies the exact identity
\[
 Q_{T,\chi}(w,w)=\tfrac12\int w^2DD\chi\wedge T-\int\chi wL_Tw.
\]
Consider the sheaf of members of $\mathscr H_T$ with $L_Tf\ge0$. It contains smooth psh functions. For a uniformly convergent sequence $f_j$, choose nested cutoffs $\chi,\eta$ inside the neighbourhood of uniform convergence, with $\eta=1$ near $\operatorname{supp}\chi$, and bound $|f_j|\le B$ on the whole support of $\eta$. For $\epsilon=\|f_j-f_k\|$ there,
\[
 0\le Q_{T,\chi}(f_j-f_k,f_j-f_k)
 \le\tfrac{\epsilon^2}{2}\|DD\chi\wedge T\|_{TV}
 +2B\epsilon\|\chi\|_\infty\|DD\eta\wedge T\|_{TV}.
\]
Indeed positivity bounds the mass of the two Laplacians by $2B\|\chi\|_\infty\|DD\eta\wedge T\|_{TV}$. Gradients are strongly Cauchy. On each smaller patch select smooth approximants with uniform and gradient errors below $1/j$ for a cutoff equal to one on that patch. This local selection proves uniform closure; it is not a flattening of unbounded presentations or a claim about a global uncountable family of seminorms. Positivity survives distributional limits. Minimality therefore puts every stage of $\mathscr P$ in this sheaf. Its vector span contains continuous dpsh functions. Finite patching on compact $X$ yields
\[
 \int fDD(gT)=\int gDD(fT)=-Q_T(f,g),\quad Q_T(f,f)\ge0.
\]

Define $E(0)=0$ and
\[
 E(u)=\tfrac13\int u\,[MA(0)+B(u,0)+MA(u)].
\]
For $u_t=(1-t)u+tv$, differentiation of this actual cubic and Green with $T=\theta,\theta_{u_t}$ give
\[
 E'(u_t)=\int(v-u)MA(u_t),\qquad E''(u_t)=-2Q_{\theta_{u_t}}(v-u,v-u)\le0.
\]
Consequently energy is monotone, concave, satisfies $E(u+c)=E(u)+Mc$, and obeys the tangent inequality. Integration of the quadratic Bernstein coefficients gives
\[
 E(u)-E(v)=\tfrac13\int(u-v)[MA(u)+B(u,v)+MA(v)].
\]
If $u\ge v$, each of the three nonnegative integrals is bounded by $3(E(u)-E(v))$. Bounded comparison follows from $w=\max(u,v-\epsilon)$ and locality: $MA(v)\{u<v-\epsilon\}\le MA(u)\{u\le v-\epsilon\}$. Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives $MA(v)\{u<v\}\le MA(u)\{u<v\}$, without omitting contact atoms at the intermediate stage.

Let $\mathcal G$ be the actual pointwise functions $u_j\downarrow u$, $u_j\in\mathcal C$, with $\inf E(u_j)>-\infty$. These are upper semicontinuous actual local CLD-generated psh potentials. Indeed the sheaf generated by smooth psh functions and decreasing limits is also closed under uniform limits with finite continuous limit: for uniform errors $\epsilon_j$, pass to a subsequence with $\epsilon_{j+1}<\epsilon_j/3$, then $f_j+2\epsilon_j$ decreases to the same limit. Zero errors are handled by a constant subsequence. Its continuous part therefore contains $\mathscr P$ by minimality, and $\rho+u_j\downarrow\rho+u$ belongs to that decreasing sheaf. This does not prove higher-dimensional classical curve-test recognition. The domain remains generated, not all intrinsic unbounded psh. Energy is canonical for EVERY such bounded continuous decreasing presentation. The decisive lemma is elementary: if $v_k\downarrow u$ are continuous and a continuous $a$ satisfies $u<a$ everywhere, the increasing open sets $\{v_k<a\}$ cover compact $X$, so one index works everywhere. Apply this with $a=u_j+\epsilon$, and reverse the roles of the presentations. Monotonicity and the constant rule give equality of both energy limits, including finiteness of the second. Order preservation follows by the same argument. An identically $-\infty$ limit would eventually lie below every constant $-A$, contradicting the energy bound.

Finite convex combinations are in $\mathcal G$ by bounded concavity. Energy on each finite simplex is a polynomial of degree at most three: bounded polynomial values converge at a finite unisolvent interpolation set inside the simplex, hence their coefficients converge. The resulting polynomial equals canonical energy at every simplex point. Such a set exists because no nonzero polynomial vanishes on an open simplex. Endpoints omit zero-weight functions, so there is no $0\cdot(-\infty)$ convention hidden in a sum.

One further continuity statement is crucial and has its exact restriction: if $u_k\in\mathcal G$ decrease to $u$ ALREADY IN $\mathcal G$, then $E(u_k)\downarrow E(u)$. For a continuous term $v_l$ presenting $u$, the open sets $\{u_k<v_l+\epsilon\}$ increase and cover $X$; here $u_k$ is upper semicontinuous. Apply order preservation, then let $l\to\infty$ and $\epsilon\downarrow0$. No automatic generation of the limit, net closure or global diagonal flattening follows.

For a positive finite Radon measure put $E^*(\mu)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal C}(E(v)-\int v\,d\mu)$. Constants force $\mu(X)=M$ when this is finite. Shift a presentation below zero, apply the bounded dual inequality and monotone convergence, and cancel the shift using mass $M$. This proves for every $u\in\mathcal G$ that $u\in L^1(\mu)$, its pole set is null, and
\[
 \int(-u)\,d\mu\le E^*(\mu)-E(u)\quad(u\le0),\qquad
 \int|u_j-u|\,d\mu\longrightarrow0.
\]
Bounded tangent gives $E^*(MA(v))=E(v)-\int vMA(v)$ for $v\in\mathcal C$.
