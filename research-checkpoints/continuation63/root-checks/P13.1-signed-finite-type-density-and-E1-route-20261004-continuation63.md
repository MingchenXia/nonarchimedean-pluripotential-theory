## Approximating a primary FS presentation by finite-type norms

Let R be the normal positively multigraded coordinate ring of the cone,
and choose an integral positive cocharacter eta0. Each homogeneous weight
space is finite dimensional, and all nonconstant generator degrees for
eta0 are positive. Take a finite FS presentation

    phi_xi=max_i (log|f_i|+lambda_i)/q_xi(alpha_i),

where the homogeneous f_i generate a primary ideal. Add a finite list of
positive-degree homogeneous algebra generators g_j of R, and give them
weights gamma_j(C)=-C q_eta0(beta_j). For C increasing, consider the FS
function associated with the entire algebra-generating list:

    phi_(C,xi)=max(phi_xi,
                  max_j(log|g_j|-C q_eta0(beta_j))/q_xi(beta_j)).

These decrease to phi_xi. Convergence is uniform on every compact subset
of the punctured cone: phi_xi is finite continuous there, log|g_j| are
bounded above, and there are finitely many g_j. The same statement holds
for a fixed second Reeb field zeta, with identical coefficients. There
is no need to claim that the original primary list generates R.

The generating list defines a signed finite-type quotient norm chi_C.
For f in a fixed homogeneous space R_alpha, define chi_C(f) as the maximum
over polynomial representations in the generators of the minimum of
the monomial weights occurring in that representation. This maximum is
attained: every representation may be made homogeneous, the positive
eta0 degree bounds every exponent, and only finitely many monomials of
weight alpha exist. Equivalently the finitely many distinct monomial
weights filter the finite dimensional space they span. Thus chi_C has
the non-Archimedean triangle inequality and the multiplicative filtration
inequality, is T-invariant, and is linearly bounded in homogeneous degree.
The maximal-representation definition is essential when there are
relations; assigning weights to generators alone is insufficient.

Its FS function is exactly the displayed generating-list maximum even
if saturation raises the weight of a generator. Indeed in an optimal
homogeneous representation, the ultrametric inequality gives

    log|f|+chi_C(f)
      <= max_m sum_l e_(m,l)(log|h_l|+lambda_l).

Since q_xi(alpha)=sum_l e_(m,l) q_xi(weight(h_l)), division bounds every
term by the generating-list FS maximum. Conversely chi_C(h_l)>=lambda_l,
so the supremum over all homogeneous f is at least that maximum. This
proves both inequalities. Power homogeneity of chi_C is not assumed;
one must check that the source's asymptotic norm/metric identity handles
the possible saturation, as expected for a finite-type quotient norm.

If phi_xi has supremum zero on the invariant carrier, the given FS
coefficients lambda_i are nonpositive: evaluate the maximum at the
trivial valuation, where all log|f_i|=0. Choose C>=0. All assigned
generator weights are then nonpositive, hence chi_C(f)<=0 for every
nonzero homogeneous f. This supplies the sign required for cutoff
comparison, without negating a multiplicative norm.

## The corrected signed energy identity

For a signed finite-type norm chi and a fixed Reeb field eta, choose A
large enough that every generator weight plus A q_eta is nonnegative.
All monomials in a homogeneous representation have the same q_eta
degree, so

    chi_A(f)=chi(f)+A q_eta(alpha)

is precisely the quotient norm obtained by shifting each generator.
It is nonnegative on every homogeneous space. Its FS function is
phi_(chi,eta)+A. Cutoff jumping sums are additive under this degree shift,
provided one uses a homogeneous adapted basis.

The source's actual rational quotient statements suggest the identity

    E_eta(phi_(chi,eta))=(n+1)/n * S_signed(chi,eta).

To prove it, first reconstruct the projective finite-type norm energy
identity, the genuine cone/projective measure identification of Wu22
Corollary5.14, and Lemma3.16(b). They give the factor (n+1)/n for the
nonnegative norm. The degree norm has cumulative average n/(n+1), so
subtracting the A translation proves the signed identity. The degree
average for irrational eta follows either from Hilbert cutoff asymptotics
or from continuity of the degree-norm S function and rational density.
An ample rational quotient may require a Veronese and a Reeb scale;
these factors must be checked rather than silently identifying its
projective mass with the raw n-dimensional cone mass.

For a fixed finite generating presentation, analytic energy continuity
as eta varies can be reconstructed using actual CLD/Bedford--Taylor
local-uniform continuity. The FS potentials and radial reference converge
locally uniformly. All mixed link measures are supported on the same
compact carrier K. Mixed-current continuity near K and uniform convergence
of the bounded energy integrands on K then give energy convergence.
This is a route to irrational eta, not an appeal to the erroneous final
equality of Wu22 Theorem5.15. The singular-variety CLD applicability and
the simultaneous reference/radial convergence must be verified in detail.

## Uniform comparison and passage to all normalized PSH

For eta=xi,zeta let

    l<=q_zeta(alpha)/q_xi(alpha)<=u

hold for all homogeneous weights. Such bounds follow from finitely many
algebra generators. Let B_eta(m) be minus the total jumping sum of a
nonpositive signed T-invariant norm on the cutoff R_eta(m). Homogeneous
adapted bases give

    B_xi(m/u)<=B_zeta(m)<=B_xi(m/l).

Combining this with u^(-n)V_xi<=V_zeta<=l^(-n)V_xi and the signed energy
identity gives

    l^n/u^(n+1) * (-E_xi phi_xi)
      <= -E_zeta phi_zeta
      <= u^n/l^(n+1) * (-E_xi phi_xi).

The constants do not depend on C in the algebra-generating approximation.
Consequently local uniform energy continuity first removes C, giving the
comparison for every normalized H presentation, and then gives it for
normalized CPSH. Intrinsic root transport is needed to show independence
of the presentation and to identify the two limits. That root map must
be reconstructed independently; this note does not import an unreviewed
canonical note as a premise.

For a normalized phi in PSH choose phi_j decreasing in CPSH with limit
phi, and put M_j=sup_K phi_j. Then M_j decreases to zero. The normalized
phi_j-M_j need not decrease, so one may not silently use decreasing
continuity on that sequence. Instead apply the CPSH comparison to it.
The root slope bounds give the global estimate

    0<=C_(xi,zeta)(phi_j)-C_(xi,zeta)(phi_j-M_j)<=M_j/l.

Energy monotonicity and translation therefore bound the energy error by
M_j/l. The unnormalized C(phi_j) do decrease to C(phi). Decreasing energy
continuity now passes the comparison, allowing infinite negative energy.
Applying the inverse root transport gives an E1 correspondence. This
step would prove finite-energy preservation and uniform normalized energy
bounds once every preceding source/norm/continuity obligation is settled.

## Ordered tails and moving MA

Suppose psi>=phi are potentials of the same Reeb class. In the energy
difference formula every mixed integral of psi-phi is nonnegative, and
the MA(phi) term occurs among the n terms. Hence

    integral(psi-phi) MA(phi)<=n(E(psi)-E(phi)),
    I(phi,psi)<=n(E(psi)-E(phi)).

For decreasing approximants phi_j>=phi, the two-sided cross-Reeb energy
bounds imply that the energy gap of their transported images is at most
the original gap plus O(delta)(-E_xi(phi)), where delta bounds |log l|
and |log u|. Thus first letting nearby Reeb fields converge and then
j increase supplies small ordered I tails. Genuine mixed Hölder estimates,
with uniformly controlled energies of a fixed dense direction class,
can then transfer weak convergence of model/CPSH moving MA measures to
finite-energy potentials. This needs a full verification of the precise
Hölder constants and test classes; a pointwise comparison against a
fixed measure is not a substitute.

## A route to uniform differentiation

Use a dense direction space of finite differences/sums of H(a xi)
potentials, initially with rational a, coefficients and FS constants.
For rational nearby zeta these give genuine projective PL directions
after the exact quotient/Veronese identification. General real FS
constants can be approximated; they should not simply be called PL_R,
since an irrational breakpoint need not come from a fixed model divisor.

For each fixed bounded CPSH phi, transport it to rational nearby fields.
Transport both potentials defining each direction f separately. On the
compact carrier their differences converge uniformly to f. Their auxiliary
projective classes are fixed multiples of the projective polarization;
the ratio and bounded potential norms, rather than a growing Veronese
degree, should control the BJ uniform-differentiation constants. Proving
that these constants depend only on such data is essential.

The transport of an obstacle phi+t f must also be checked. Along a zeta
orbit a difference of two a xi-equivariant potentials has slope bounded
by (u-l)/a, because its xi slope cancels. For sufficiently small t this
makes phi+t f strictly decreasing with the correct range. Its root
defines an extended obstacle transport. Comparing zero sublevel sets
shows the order equivalence needed for

    C(P_xi(phi+t f))=P_zeta(C(phi+t f)).

For bounded phi the root slope estimate and the small directional slope
give, for fixed t, uniform convergence of C(phi+t f) to C(phi)+t f_zeta
as zeta tends to xi. Envelopes are 1-Lipschitz in the obstacle. The energy
comparison can then transfer the rational expansion to xi, with the
same constant independent of phi: convergence may be proved for each
fixed phi; preserving a uniform inequality in that limit still gives a
uniform final constant. This requires actual envelope regularity on the
rational quotient and its bounded irrational transfer, using the checked
geometrically-unibranch source theorem or a proved replacement.

Finally remove boundedness through decreasing CPSH approximation.
The envelopes P_xi(phi_j+t f) decrease to P_xi(phi+t f) by psh closure,
obstacle order and the two competitor directions. They have finite
energy because phi_j-|t| ||f|| <= P <= phi_j+|t| ||f||. Decreasing energy
continuity and genuine weighted MA continuity pass the uniform expansion.
Uniform approximation of continuous directions gives their derivatives,
without falsely promising a common quadratic bound for all continuous f.

This route has substantive unresolved verification tasks: exact signed
finite-type norm/metric and rational energy identities; CLD continuity
on the singular cone; all local-uniform/dense-class passages; genuine
global envelope inputs and source growth convention; auxiliary-data
control of the BJ remainder; and the extended obstacle order calculation.
It must be reconstructed and reviewed as a whole. It is not an assertion
that P13.1 or the fixed-Reeb image problem is already solved.
