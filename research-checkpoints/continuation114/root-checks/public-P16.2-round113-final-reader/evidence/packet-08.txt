## 1. Attribution and exact fan

The seven-ray example below is the Miyake--Oda example, as reproduced in
Fujino--Sato, *Smooth projective toric varieties whose nontrivial nef line
bundles are big*, Proc. Japan Acad.85A(2009), Example4.4, printed pages92--93.
It is not a new example. The downloaded author's PDF was inspected on
PDF page4 to recover signs that its literal text extraction loses.

For any field k let N=Z^3 and put

    v1=(1,0,0), v2=(0,1,0), v3=(0,0,1),
    v4=(-1,-1,-1), v5=(0,-1,-1),
    v6=(-1,0,-1), v7=(-1,-1,0).

Take all faces of the maximal cones

    123, 125, 256, 456, 236, 367, 467, 137, 157, 457.

Here ijk means the nonnegative real span of vi,vj,vk. This agrees exactly
with the rays and ten maximal cones printed in Example4.4. The primary
source supplies attribution and an independent check of the example.
The verifications below avoid restricting its construction to C.

## 2. Completeness and smoothness

Start with the P^3 fan whose maximal cones are 123,124,234,134.
The first cone is unchanged. On each of the other three cones, place the
two rays along its edges from v4 and choose the following subdivisions:

    124 -> 125,256,456,
    234 -> 236,367,467,
    134 -> 137,157,457.

For a basis (a,b,c) of an original cone, the first pattern has new rays
a+c,b+c and consists of (a,b,a+c), (b,a+c,b+c), (c,a+c,b+c).
Its triangular cross-section is triangulated by these three triangles,
so they cover that cone and meet along faces. The next two patterns are
cyclic copies. On every shared face of the old P^3 fan, both subdivisions
use precisely the same new edge ray. They therefore glue to a fan with
the same support R^3. This proves completeness.

The determinants of the three displayed new basis matrices are,
respectively, 1,-1,1 times det(a,b,c). Every old maximal cone has
determinant +/-1. Thus each new maximal cone is generated by a Z-basis.
Its affine chart Spec k[sigma^vee intersect M] is A^3_k. These charts cover
X_Sigma(k), proving smoothness over any k directly. They contain the same
dense Laurent torus, so the glued scheme is integral and has dimension3.

For properness over any k, the primary input is Rohrer's dissertation,
ChapterIV, Lemma1.3.11 and Theorem1.3.12, with their entire printed
proofs. The theorem is over any ring R: a nonempty fan gives a proper
toric scheme if and only if the fan is complete (apart from R=0).
The proof of the sufficient direction is not a complex-topological
compactness argument. Given a DVR point whose generic point lies in
the orbit of a cone sigma, valuation of its character homomorphism is a
lattice vector in N/(N intersect span sigma). Completeness of the star
fan supplies a chart where all required character values have nonnegative
valuation, hence lie in the DVR. The valuative criterion first proves
properness over Z; base change proves it over every R.

The pertinent orbit-cover proof is IV1.3.1--1.3.9, also read. The star
completeness statement and printed proof are II2.2.5--2.2.7, read separately.
For clarity a direct proof of the only star-completeness implication used
is as follows. For z in relint(sigma) and any x in N_R, completeness gives
a cone containing z+epsilon_j x for a sequence epsilon_j decreasing to0.
Finiteness gives a single such cone tau for infinitely many j. Its closedness
gives z in tau, and the fan face property then gives sigma face of tau.
Projecting z+epsilon_j x shows that the image of x lies in tau/sigma.
Consequently the star fan covers the quotient real vector space.

The dissertation's IV1.2.1 gives separatedness, quasi-compactness and
finite presentation. Its printed proof cites earlier lemmas; reading those
earlier lemma proofs is not asserted here. The use of Theorem1.3.12 is
an exact external theorem with an inspected main proof and the listed
pertinent orbit/star units, not a claim that every foundation in the
249-page dissertation has been read. Rohrer1207.0605v2 and the survey
1107.2713v2 only refer elsewhere for properness; neither survey statement
is represented as a printed properness proof.

## 3. A direct nonprojectivity proof in every field

Any line bundle on X has a rational section trivializing its restriction
to the Laurent torus: the Laurent polynomial ring is a UFD, hence the
Picard group of its regular spectrum is zero. The section's divisor is
supported on the complement of that torus, so it has the form

    D=sum_{i=1}^7 a_i D_i,

with integer coefficients and invariant boundary prime divisors D_i.
Since X is smooth, this is a Cartier divisor. Thus checking the degree
of every invariant Cartier divisor suffices to exclude all ample bundles.

The walls 25,36,17 have the respective wall relations

    v1+v6=v2+v5,
    v2+v7=v3+v6,
    v3+v5=v1+v7.

The associated invariant orbit closures are P^1_k. To see this and the
degree formula without importing an intersection table, write adjacent
basis cones as (w1,w2,v) and (w1,w2,v') with v+v'=w1+w2. Quotienting
the orbit by the two wall coordinates glues A^1_z and A^1_{z^-1}.
Let e in M vanish on w1,w2 with e(v)=1, so e(v')=-1.
The monomial frames of O(D) on these two charts are chi^{m_sigma} and
chi^{m_sigma'}, where the pairings with chart rays equal minus their
divisor coefficients. Their difference vanishes on the wall and satisfies

    m_sigma'-m_sigma=(a_v+a_v'-a_w1-a_w2)e.

The transition of the second frame to the first is therefore z^d, with
d=a_v+a_v'-a_w1-a_w2. A rational section equal to the first frame has
order d at the other chart's origin, proving that its line bundle on
this orbit closure has degree d. Hence the three degrees above are

    d25=a1+a6-a2-a5,
    d36=a2+a7-a3-a6,
    d17=a3+a5-a1-a7.

Their sum is zero. An ample line bundle restricts to an ample bundle
on each of these P^1 curves, hence would have all three degrees positive.
This is impossible. Thus X is proper and smooth but nonprojective over
every field, including every algebraically closed trivially valued
characteristic-zero field retained by the original task.

## 4. Actual positivity data and role of this test

The refinement of the P^3 fan gives a toric morphism pi0:X->P^3 which
is identity on the dense torus. It is proper: X is proper over k and P^3
is separated over k, so its graph is closed and the projection is proper.
It is birational. Normality of P^3 and the actual proper birational
pushforward theorem (Stacks0AY8) give (pi0)_*O_X=O_P3. Projection formula
then gives

    H^0(X,pi0^*O(m))=H^0(P^3,O(m)),

whose dimension grows as binomial(m+3,3). Therefore pi0^*O(1) is an actual
big line bundle on this original nonprojective X. Proposal109 constructs
a retained smooth projective modification pi:Y->X and an ample/effective
decomposition pi^*L=A+D for this bundle, as for every big rational bundle
on every smooth integral proper original carrier.

This example tests the nonprojective part of that proposed general theorem.
No claim restricts the theorem to toric metrics, toric targets, atomic
measures, a fixed skeleton, or the particular projective contraction above.
No proof of the remaining analytic gates is supplied merely by this example.
Those gates, and the conditional continuation110 arguments, still require
their full original-domain reconstruction and independent review.
