## 1. The topological quotient can be proved without a properness black box

Berkovich1993 Proposition2.6.1 constructs good analytification and its
continuous functor, compatible with open subschemes and products. The
complete pertinent proof of2.6.7 establishes that a separated algebraic
morphism has separated analytification, by its closed diagonal. In
particular X^an is Hausdorff. Proposition2.6.8, with its printed proof,
says that the analytic image is the inverse image of the scheme-theoretic
set image under the analytic support map; the affine proof extends a
valuation from B/p to the fraction field of C/q. Thus the algebraically
surjective pi has surjective analytification.

Y^an is compact, as part of the actual projective ample background. A
continuous surjection from compact Y^an onto Hausdorff X^an makes X^an
compact and pi^an a closed quotient. Its restriction over any ordinary
open O of X^an is still a closed quotient relative to O: a closed subset
of pi^-1(O) is an intersection of a globally closed set with pi^-1(O),
and its image is the corresponding closed image intersected with O.
No reading of the unprinted cross-referenced proof of Berkovich2.6.9 is
claimed. That proper-analytification theorem is unnecessary for these
topological conclusions when an actual compact projective Y is retained.

## 2. Fiber maxima provide all original continuous majorants

For the genuine quotient psi put q(x)=max_{pi(y)=x} psi(y). Compactness and
upper semicontinuity attain this extended-valued maximum. For any real a,
{q>=a}=pi({psi>=a}) is closed. Thus q is upper semicontinuous and bounded
above. For original f continuous and real t set

    w_t=psi+t pi*f on Y,      q_t=q+t f on X.

The latter is the fiber maximum of w_t and is upper semicontinuous. The
family of real continuous original functions b>=q_t is nonempty, stable
under finite minima and directed in decreasing order. Its pointwise
infimum is exactly q_t, including where q_t=-infinity.

Here is a proof using only compact Hausdorff normality. Fix x and a>q_t(x).
Choose a global upper bound M>=a for q_t. The set O={q_t<a} is open and
contains x. A continuous bump lambda in[0,1], with lambda(x)=1 and support
in O, exists by normality. Then b=M-(M-a)lambda is at least q_t everywhere
and b(x)=a. Allowing every a at a minus-infinity point proves the same
infimum there. Minima make these choices a genuine decreasing net b_i,
with b_i down q_t at all original points. No countable approximation or
metrizability hypothesis is used.

The actual relative obstacle inequality

    u+t f <= tau+b

is equivalent to w_t<=pi*b everywhere on Y. In one direction pull it
back and subtract the finite sigma_D only outside D, hence on all
divisorial points. Apply BJ4.22 to the genuine psi with the continuous
obstacle pi*(b-t f) to extend the inequality to all of Y. The converse
is immediate after adding sigma_D and descending. This is in turn
equivalent to q_t<=b. In particular the majorants above are precisely
the relevant original relative continuous majorants.

## 3. Exact equality of outer energies in every original direction

Write theta_i=P_A(pi*b_i), the genuine ample envelope. It is genuine
finite energy because it is bounded below by psi-|t| ||f||. The envelopes
decrease as their continuous obstacles decrease. At the generic trivial
valuation their values are bounded below by a finite number, so their
pointwise decreasing limit theta is genuine. It satisfies theta>=P_A(w_t).
For every divisorial v of Y the map is an isomorphism at its analytic
support, and hence

    theta(v)<=lim_i(pi*b_i)(v)=w_t(v).

BJ4.22 extends this inequality to every point since w_t is usc. Thus
theta<=P_A(w_t), and consequently theta=P_A(w_t) on all of Y. This
argument needs neither psi nor theta_i to be fiber-constant on bad fibers.
It uses the actual divisor-finite divisorial values, not a choice of lift.

Equivalently, the PSH minorants of q_t composed with pi and of w_t agree:
one inclusion uses w_t<=q_t composed with pi; the other compares on all
divisorial points and then applies BJ4.22 with usc obstacle w_t. This
is an envelope equality, not equality of these two obstacle functions.

Define original outer energy for these specified obstacles by

    E_M^down(u+t f)
      =inf_{b continuous on X, u+t f<=tau+b} E_M(P_M(tau+b)).

The actual relative envelope identity gives
P_M(tau+b)=descent(sigma_D+P_A(pi*b)), whose energy is the raw ample
energy of its genuine quotient. Every such quotient dominates P_A(w_t),
so the displayed infimum is at least E_raw,A(P_A(w_t)). The particular
decreasing majorants from Section2 give the reverse inequality by genuine
decreasing ample energy continuity. Therefore

    E_M^down(u+t f)=E_raw,A(P_A(psi+t pi*f)).

This supplies the singular outer-energy equality that is not justified
merely by saying that a continuous original function pulls upstairs. Both
ample derivative signs now give the original derivative

    d/dt at0 E_M^down(u+t f)
      =integral_Y pi*f MA_raw,A(psi)=integral_X f MA_M(u).

The last identity presupposes the separately proved genuine local metric
product/pushforward identity. It is not an inverse target definition.

## 4. Static Radon lifting needs no point selection

Let mu be finite positive Radon on compact X^an with mu(P^an)=0. Restrict
it to W^an and transport by the actual isomorphism pi^-1(W)^an->W^an.
This is a Radon measure on that open. Extend it by zero to all of Y^an,
calling it nu. For any Borel E of Y^an, inner regularity of mu on the
corresponding Borel subset of W^an approximates its mass by compact K
contained in W^an. The isomorphism inverse of K is compact in Y^an and
contained in E. Thus nu is inner regular on every Borel set. It is finite;
on compact Hausdorff Y its inner regularity implies outer regularity by
taking complements. Therefore nu is positive Radon, pi_*nu=mu, and
nu(pi^-1(P)^an)=0. These conditions characterize it uniquely. No choice
of one point in a bad fiber has been made.

For this measure, every q_u agrees with its uniquely determined psi
on the full-mass isomorphism open. Hence

    integral_X q_u dmu=integral_Y psi dnu,
    E_M^dual(mu)=E_raw,A^dual(nu).

The explicit E1 polar test for D and the entire bad locus is a separate
proof dependency. Once established, finite original dual energy forces
mu(P)=0: its actual test quotient q is minus infinity there. Likewise
integrability of every actual E1 quotient forces mu(P)=0. With Section4
the ample intrinsic all-E1 integrability theorem proves both directions
of the original target criterion. Zero mass of P is a consequence of
that condition rather than an added arbitrary-support restriction.

## 5. Weak Radon lifting on the original carrier

For a net mu_i->mu of equal-mass positive Radon measures, all vanishing
on P, assume mu(P)=0. The corresponding zero-extended inverse nu_i
converges weakly to nu on Y. Let g be any continuous function on Y and
epsilon>0. Radon outer regularity and normality provide a continuous
0<=rho<=1 on X, equal to1 on a neighborhood of P, with integral rho dmu
small enough compared to epsilon/(1+||g||). Weak convergence gives the
same eventual bound for integral rho dmu_i. On W transport
(1-rho composed with pi)g down by the isomorphism. It is zero near P and
extends by zero to a continuous original h on X. Its mu_i pairings converge
to its mu pairing. The two removed rho-weighted g integrals are bounded
by ||g|| times the small rho masses. This proves convergence against g.
The construction works for nets and does not assume a globally selected
inverse point, metrizability, or an atomic/skeletal support class.
