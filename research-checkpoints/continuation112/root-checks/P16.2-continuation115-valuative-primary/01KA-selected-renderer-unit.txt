L31: 【19†Definition 26.20.3.】 Let $f : X \to S$ be a morphism of schemes. We say $f$ satisfies the existence part of the valuative criterion if given any commutative solid diagram
L32: \[ \xymatrix{ \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(K) \ar[r] \ar[d] & X \ar[d] \\ \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A) \ar[r] \ar@{-->}[ru] & S } \]
L33: 
L34: where $A$ is a valuation ring with field of fractions $K$, the dotted arrow exists. We say $f$ satisfies the uniqueness part of the valuative criterion if there is at most one dotted arrow given any diagram as above (without requiring existence of course).
L35: A valuation ring is a local domain maximal among the relation of domination in its fraction field, see Algebra, Definition 【20†10.50.1】. Hence the spectrum of a valuation ring has a unique generic point $\eta $ and a unique closed point $0$, and of course we have the specialization $\eta \leadsto 0$. The significance of valuation rings is that any specialization of points in any scheme is the image of $\eta \leadsto 0$ under some morphism from the spectrum of some valuation ring.
L36: Here is the precise result.

L39: 【21†Lemma 26.20.4.】 Let $S$ be a scheme. Let $s' \leadsto s$ be a specialization of points of $S$. Then
L40: 
L41:   1. there exists a valuation ring $A$ and a morphism $f : \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A) \to S$ such that the generic point $\eta $ of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)$ maps to $s'$ and the special point maps to $s$, and
L42: 
L43:   2. given a field extension $K/\kappa (s')$ we may arrange it so that the extension $\kappa (\eta )/\kappa (s')$ induced by $f$ is isomorphic to the given extension.
L44: Proof. Let $s' \leadsto s$ be a specialization in $S$, and let $K/\kappa (s')$ be an extension of fields. By Lemma 【22†26.13.2】 and the discussion following Lemma 【23†26.13.3】 this leads to ring maps $\mathcal{O}_{S, s} \to \kappa (s') \to K$. Let $A \subset K$ be any valuation ring whose field of fractions is $K$ and which dominates the image of $\mathcal{O}_{S, s} \to K$, see Algebra, Lemma 【24†10.50.2】.
L45: The ring map $\mathcal{O}_{S, s} \to A$ induces the morphism $f : \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A) \to S$, see Lemma 【25†26.13.1】. This morphism has all the desired properties by construction. $\square$
L46: 【26†Lemma 26.20.5.】 Let $f : X \to S$ be a morphism of schemes. The following are equivalent
L47: 
L48:   1. Specializations lift along any base change of $f$
L49: 
L50:   2. The morphism $f$ satisfies the existence part of the valuative criterion.
L51: Proof. Assume (1) holds. Let a solid diagram as in Definition 【19†26.20.3】 be given. In order to find the dotted arrow we may replace $X \to S$ by $X_{\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)} \to \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)$ since after all the assumption is stable under base change. Thus we may assume $S = \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)$. Let $x' \in X$ be the image of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(K) \to X$, so that we have $\kappa (x') \subset K$, see Lemma 【23†26.13.3】.
L52: By assumption there exists a specialization $x' \leadsto x$ in $X$ such that $x$ maps to the closed point of $S = \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)$. We get a local ring map $A \to \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ and a ring map $\mathcal{O}_{X, x} \to \kappa (x')$, see Lemma 【22†26.13.2】 and the discussion following Lemma 【23†26.13.3】. The composition $A \to \mathcal{O}_{X, x} \to \kappa (x') \to K$ is the given injection $A \to K$.
L53: Since $A \to \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is local, the image of $\mathcal{O}_{X, x} \to K$ dominates $A$ and hence is equal to $A$, by Algebra, Definition 【20†10.50.1】. Thus we obtain a ring map $\mathcal{O}_{X, x} \to A$ and hence a morphism $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A) \to X$ (see Lemma 【25†26.13.1】 and discussion following it). This proves (2).
L54: Conversely, assume (2) holds. It is immediate that the existence part of the valuative criterion holds for any base change $X_{S'} \to S'$ of $f$ by considering the following commutative diagram
L55: 
L56: \[ \xymatrix{ \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(K) \ar[r] \ar[d] & X_{S'} \ar[r] \ar[d] & X \ar[d] \\ \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A) \ar[r] \ar@{-->}[ru] \ar@{-->}[rru] & S' \ar[r] & S } \]
L57: Namely, the more horizontal dotted arrow will lead to the other one by definition of the fibre product. OK, so it clearly suffices to show that specializations lift along $f$. Let $s' \leadsto s$ be a specialization in $S$, and let $x' \in X$ be a point lying over $s'$. Apply Lemma 【21†26.20.4】 to $s' \leadsto s$ and the extension of fields $K = \kappa (x')/\kappa (s')$. We get a commutative diagram
L58: \[ \xymatrix{ \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(K) \ar[rr] \ar[d] & & X \ar[d] \\ \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A) \ar[r] \ar@{-->}[rru] & \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(\mathcal{O}_{S, s}) \ar[r] & S } \]
L59: 
L60: and by condition (2) we get the dotted arrow. The image $x$ of the closed point of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)$ in $X$ will be a solution to our problem, i.e., $x$ is a specialization of $x'$ and maps to $s$. $\square$
L61: 【27†Proposition 26.20.6 (Valuative criterion of universal closedness).】 Let $f$ be a quasi-compact morphism of schemes. Then $f$ is universally closed if and only if $f$ satisfies the existence part of the valuative criterion.

L63: Proof. This is a formal consequence of Lemmas 【14†26.20.2】 and 【26†26.20.5】 above. $\square$
