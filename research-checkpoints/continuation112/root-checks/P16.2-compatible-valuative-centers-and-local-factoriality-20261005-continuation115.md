## 2. Center of every analytic point on an arbitrary proper original X

Let X be a proper finite-type k-scheme, with k trivially valued, and
x in X^an. Its support and completed residue field define an actual
scheme morphism Spec H(x) -> X over k. Put R_x=H(x)^circ, the subring
of elements of absolute value at most1. For any nonzero element a,
either a or a^(-1) lies in R_x; hence R_x is a valuation ring with
fraction field H(x). The trivial norm on k makes k a subring of R_x.

Properness supplies a unique map Spec R_x -> X extending the given
generic map. Its closed-point image is the center c_X(x). This applies
to every analytic point, including points with nongeneric support, and
to every cardinality of k. No projectivity or rank-one discreteness
condition enters the valuation-ring criterion.

Let f:Y->X be a k-morphism between proper finite-type schemes and
y in Y^an, with x=f^an(y). The analytic functor gives an isometric
embedding H(x)->H(y). Thus R_x maps locally to R_y: the intersection
with R_x of the maximal ideal {a:|a|<1} of R_y is the maximal ideal
of R_x. Compose Spec R_y -> Y with f. It extends the same generic
map as Spec R_y -> Spec R_x -> X, so uniqueness on X gives equality.
In particular

    f(c_Y(y))=c_X(f^an(y)).

The identical proof works for any isometric valued-field extension
and an analytic point above x. It uses the actual residue-field map,
not a choice of a lift. It proves the compatible-center assertion
needed in original canonical metric pullback and section descent.

## 3. Literal canonical metric functoriality

For an actual line bundle L on proper X, choose a local algebraic frame
e at c_X(x). The canonical trivial norm assigns norm1 to that frame at
x; a vector a e has norm |a|. This does not depend on the frame: their
ratio is a unit of O_(X,c_X(x)), whose image and inverse both lie in
R_x, so its absolute value is1. A local regular coefficient has norm
at most1 for the same local-ring-map reason.

The compatible-center equality makes f^*e a frame at c_Y(y). It follows
directly that the canonical metric on f^*L is the pullback of this
canonical metric on L. Tensor products and divisible rational powers
are compatible by multiplying or taking the appropriate power of the
coefficient norms. For an actual section s of a cleared power, its
logarithmic norm therefore satisfies

    log||f^*s||(y)=log||s||(f^an(y)),

including the value minus infinity at a zero. This establishes the
actual metric/section pullback used in109/113 on nonprojective X.
It does not assert that the canonical metric of an arbitrary line
bundle is globally continuous or semipositive. Continuity of the
specified retained reference follows instead from its actual finite
FS representation and the compact quotient argument of110.

For a globally generating family of a cleared actual line bundle,
all its normalized section norms are at most1 at each center. Some
member is a unit coefficient in the local frame: otherwise the
family would not generate its stalk. Its norm is1. Hence the maximum
of logarithmic norms is identically0, also on a proper nonprojective
carrier. This is the generator multiplication identity used in114.
