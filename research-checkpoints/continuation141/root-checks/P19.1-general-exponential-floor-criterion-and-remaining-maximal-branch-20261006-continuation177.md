# Exponential floors, entropy divergence, and the remaining maximal-ray branch

**Status.** Root-authored supplement, unreviewed. It does not close original P19.1. The frozen candidate176 and prepared review130 are unchanged. This supplement records a criterion valid on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold and separates already established maximality/lower-bound inputs from the remaining upper recovery problem.

## 1. A criterion on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold

Fix a compact Kähler $(X,\omega)$ of dimension $n$, $V=\int\omega^n>0$, reference probability $\mu=\omega^n/V$, normalized Aubin--Mabuchi energy $E(0)=0$, and normalized Monge--Ampère probabilities. Let $u_t$ be an actual bounded weak geodesic ray from zero, with $u_t\leq0$. Assume that there is a fixed $\omega$-psh function $\psi$ such that
\[
 u_t\geq\psi\quad(t\geq0),\qquad
 \int_X e^{-b\psi}\,d\mu<\infty\quad\text{for every }b>0.
 \tag{1}
\]
Write $E(u_t)=-ct$. The exact affine-energy property and $u_t\leq0$ give $c\geq0$.

**Criterion.** If $c=0$, the ray is identically zero. If $c>0$, its entropy slope is $+\infty$ and its actual usual total-ray non-Archimedean datum is zero. In particular, any nonzero ray satisfying (1) has trivial datum and infinite entropy slope.

Proof: energy concavity, with the supporting plane at $u_t$ evaluated on zero, gives
\[
 \int_X(-u_t)\,\operatorname{MA}_\omega(u_t)\geq-E(u_t)=ct.
\]
For each fixed $b>0$, the probability entropy inequality with the bounded Borel test $-bu_t$ gives
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}(u_t)
 \geq bct-\log\int_Xe^{-bu_t}\,d\mu
 \geq bct-\log\int_Xe^{-b\psi}\,d\mu.
 \tag{2}
\]
The last logarithm is a finite constant independent of time. Let time tend to infinity first and then let $b$ tend to infinity. When $c>0$ this proves an infinite liminf, hence the extended-real limit is infinite.

The function $U(x,\zeta)=u_{-\log|\zeta|}(x)$ is $p_1^*\omega$-psh and nonpositive on $X\times D^*$, and extends psh across the central divisor. Fubini and (1) show that $e^{-bU}$ is locally integrable on $X\times D$ for every $b>0$. On a smooth modification, at a generic point of a prime divisor $F$, let $z_1$ cut out $F$ and let the Jacobian have order $k$. A positive generic divisorial coefficient $\lambda$ would give $U\circ\pi\leq\lambda\log|z_1|+C$. Change of variables would require integrability of $|z_1|^{-b\lambda+2k}$ for every $b$, which is impossible once $b\lambda\geq2(k+1)$. All these coefficients are zero. Thus the actual normalized values $-\nu_F(U)/\operatorname{ord}_F(\zeta)$ on vertical divisors are zero. Divisorial determination identifies the usual datum with zero.

When $c=0$, the normalized energy definition and negativity of $u_t$ imply
\[
 0=(n+1)V E(u_t)
   =\sum_{j=0}^n\int_Xu_t\,\omega_{u_t}^j\wedge\omega^{n-j}.
\]
Every term is nonpositive, so the $j=0$ term is zero. Hence $u_t=0$ almost everywhere for the smooth volume measure. Equality of psh representatives from almost-everywhere equality gives $u_t=0$ everywhere. This establishes the other branch. Only ordinary bounded pluripotential foundations enter this criterion.

Candidate176 supplies actual nonzero rays satisfying (1), for all products $\mathbf P^1\times Y$: one may take $\psi=a\,p_1^*\psi_{\rm Cantor}$ and $c=a^2/2$. The criterion itself is not limited to a product geometry; the existence construction in176 is.

## 2. Why entropy slopes exist in the extended sense

The following standard analytic inputs are used here: normalized energy is affine along genuine finite-energy geodesics; for a positive smooth closed $(1,1)$-form $\chi$, its twisted energy is convex along such geodesics and continuous under strong approximation; the extended Mabuchi functional is convex along these geodesics. They do not require a rational Kähler class. Chi Li's Lemma 2.35 and proof use exactly this analytic twisted-energy argument. We use those analytic foundations, not an algebraic approximation theorem in a transcendental class.

To check finiteness of the twisted slopes, normalize an arbitrary finite-energy ray from zero by its affine supremum so that $u_t\leq0$ and $E(u_t)=-ct$. Define
\[
 T_\chi(u)=V^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
              \int_Xu\,\chi\wedge\omega_u^j\wedge\omega^{n-1-j}.
\]
If $0\leq\chi\leq C\omega$, positivity gives
\[
 0\leq-T_\chi(u)
 \leq C V^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_X(-u)\omega_u^j\wedge\omega^{n-j}
 \leq C(n+1)(-E(u)).
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus convexity implies that $T_\chi(u_t)/t$ has a finite limit between $-C(n+1)c$ and zero. A general smooth closed real form is the difference of two positive smooth closed forms: choose $C$ large enough that $C\omega+\chi$ is positive and subtract $C\omega$. Hence its twisted slope also exists and is finite. Restoring the removed affine supremum adds a fixed linear term to each twisted energy; it does not affect finiteness.

The normalized Chen--Tian expression has the form
\[
 M(u)=\operatorname{Ent}(u)+T_{-\operatorname{Ric}(\omega)}(u)
                  +\overline S E(u),
\]
with the convention for Ricci matching $dd^c$ and $\overline S=n\int\operatorname{Ric}(\omega)\wedge\omega^{n-1}/V$. Constants at the zero reference are zero. Convexity of $M$ and its finite value at zero give an asymptotic slope in $\mathbf R\cup\{+\infty\}$. If it is infinite at every positive time, the claim is immediate; otherwise its secant slopes are bounded below by a finite secant slope. Subtracting the finite twisted slope and the affine energy slope shows that the entropy divided by time has an extended-real limit. Entropy is nonnegative in the probability convention, so this limit cannot be negative. This explains why the dichotomy below concerns limits, rather than an unsupported exchange of liminf and an approximation parameter.

## 3. Established inputs and the unresolved branch

The compact-Kähler maximality theorem states: a genuine finite-energy geodesic ray with finite Mabuchi slope is maximal relative to its usual non-Archimedean datum. Mesquita--Piccione, arXiv:2409.06221v2, Proposition 6.3.1 states this generalization of Chi Li's Theorem 1.2. The maximal-ray entropy lower bound is Proposition 6.2.2 of the same source: for $\phi\in E^{1,\rm NA}([\omega])$ and its maximal ray $r$, $H^{\rm NA}(\phi)\leq\operatorname{Ent}^{\infty}(r)$. These are established inputs with their stated scope, not new conclusions of this supplement. Root read the full lower-bound proof and the stated compact-Kähler maximality transfer; the latter source explicitly refers back to Li's local proof, whose full Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 1.2 proof were read.

By Section 2, a nonmaximal ray has infinite Mabuchi slope and therefore infinite entropy slope. For a maximal ray in the finite-energy NA domain with $H^{\rm NA}(\phi)=+\infty$, the source lower bound forces its entropy slope to be infinite as well. The comparison still needing an upper-recovery proof on arbitrary compact Kähler manifolds is therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}^{\infty}(r)\leq H^{\rm NA}(\phi)
 \quad\text{for maximal }r\text{ with }\phi\in E^{1,\rm NA}
             \text{ and }H^{\rm NA}(\phi)<\infty.
 \tag{4}
\]
No proof of (4) is asserted here. No assertion about arbitrary data outside the finite-energy NA domain is added. The recent projective maximal-ray equality cannot supply (4) for a transcendental nonprojective manifold just by changing terminology.

For the example176, the maximal ray of its datum zero is the zero ray, while its own normalized energy slope is negative. The energy missing from the usual datum is exactly $a^2/2$. This quantity is a useful diagnostic; adding it to the datum does not by itself prove a general transcendental entropy formula. The arbitrary-ray question and the maximal finite-entropy upper-recovery question remain logically distinct.
