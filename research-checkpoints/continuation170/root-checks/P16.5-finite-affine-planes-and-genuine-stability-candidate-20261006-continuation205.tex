\begin{abstract}
Under explicit genuine big-class foundations, finite divisorial
supporting planes extend the section energy to upper semicontinuous
obstacles and make perturbed envelope energies weakly upper
semicontinuous. Uniform variation then gives weak convergence of
the original output along every weakly convergent net whose energies
converge. Separately, uniform finite-atomic dual approximation before
attainment gives absolute target-integral convergence along every
weak net with uniformly bounded normalized energy cost.
The genuine four-point estimate further gives dual convergence
of outputs and simultaneous weighted convergence with independent
weak, cost-bounded testing potentials.
These are statements about actual genuine potentials. No fixed
original unbounded test at an auxiliary physical output is identified.
The nonample foundations remain explicit conditional antecedents.
\end{abstract}

\section{Domain and exact conditional inputs}
Let $X$ be a smooth integral projective $n$-fold, $n\geq1$, over
an algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero field.
Let $L$ be an actual big rational bundle, $\theta=c_1(L)$,
$K=X^{\mathrm{an}}$, and $V=\vol(L)>0$. Retain all supported
semivaluations and arbitrary directed nets. The genuine cone consists
of actual all-point single decreasing FS-net limits on varying
actual rational bundles; its weak topology is convergence at rational
divisorial points and the generic trivial valuation $o$.

The following are \emph{logical antecedents}, not assertions that
the actual nonample theory has been established.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Genuine convex, finite-max and decreasing closure, all-point
obstacle envelopes, and normalized weak compactness hold.
The intrinsic reference $R=P_\theta(0)$ has $R(o)=0$.
Every normalized genuine potential is at most $R$.
There are product-directed continuous rational model majorants
of every genuine potential, decreasing at all points, with the
prescribed generic value after normalization.
\item The continuous section energy $\mathscr E$ is concave,
monotone, $V$-Lipschitz and $V$-translation equivariant.
For each rational model obstacle $f_D$ its derivative is a positive
finite divisorial measure $\mu_D$ of raw mass $V$, compatible with
refinement. Its global affine plane is
\[
 A_D(g)=\mathscr E(f_D)+\int(g-f_D)\,d\mu_D,
 \qquad \mathscr E(f)=\inf_D A_D(f)\quad(f\in C(K)).
                                                        \tag{1}
\]
The index $D$ ranges over all rational model obstacles.
The actual genuine energy
$E(u)=\inf_{f\in C(K),\,f\geq u}\mathscr E(f)$ is decreasing-net
continuous, with $E(R)=0$ and $E(P_\theta(f))=\mathscr E(f)$.
Write $\EE=\{u\in\PSH(\theta):E(u)>-\infty\}$ and
$B(u)=-E(u-u(o))$.
\item There is an original, target-independent positive Radon
output $M(u)$ of raw mass $V$ for every $u\in\EE$,
constant invariant, with both continuous envelope derivatives.
For each fixed rational model direction $h$, each normalized
cost bound $B$, and arbitrarily large fixed $c$, there are
$K_{B,c,h}<\infty$, $a_{B,h}<\infty$ and $\tau_{B,c,h}>0$ such that
\[
\begin{split}
\big|E(P_\theta(u+th))-E(u)-tM(u)(h)\big|
 \leq K_{B,c,h}t^2+|t|a_{B,h}/c
\end{split}                                             \tag{2}
\]
for every normalized $u$ with $B(u)\leq B$ and
$0<|t|<\tau_{B,c,h}$. The constants do not depend on $u$.
The signed bigness margin is imposed before (2), as in
the corrected finite Taylor proof in whole159.
\item For every intrinsic target $\nu$ of raw mass $V$,
missing $\{R=-\infty\}$ and having finite intrinsic dual energy,
and every $D<\infty$, there are positive finite divisorial
measures $\nu_j$ and $\epsilon_j\to0$ such that
\[
 \sup_{\substack{z(o)=0\\B(z)\leq D}}
 \left|\int(R-z)\,d(\nu_j-\nu)\right|\leq\epsilon_j.
                                                        \tag{3}
\]
All genuine relative finite-energy tests are $\nu$-integrable.
This is precisely the pre-attainment conditional conclusion
of whole204, whose proof uses whole159 Sections6--8 and
whole202, without its later attained inverse.
\item For the final simultaneous weighted result only, every
output $M(u)$ is itself an intrinsic finite-dual target,
and the whole204 four-point estimate holds. In particular,
for normalized $a,b$ of cost at most $D\geq1$,
\[
 \sup_{\substack{z(o)=0\\B(z)\leq D}}
 \left|\int(R-z)\,d(M(a)-M(b))\right|
       \leq C_n\sqrt{D\,\delta(a,b)},                    \tag{4}
\]
where
$\delta(a,b)=E((a+b)/2)-(E(a)+E(b))/2\geq0$.
This is its full estimate with all costs bounded by $D$;
in dimension one the exponent-zero factor is omitted.
There is one actual Kodaira divisor $Z$ with $U=K\setminus Z$
such that all outputs miss $Z$, and each normalized
canonical model envelope $v_j\downarrow v$ has
$R-v_j$ bounded continuous on $U$, with $B(v_j)\leq B(v)$.
These are precisely the bounded-relative signatures used
in whole159 and whole202, not an assertion about an
unbounded auxiliary physical reference defect.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Upper semicontinuous obstacles and finite planes}
For a usc function $g$ bounded above and finite at every
divisorial point, set
\[
 \mathscr E^{\rm usc}(g)
       =\inf_{f\in C(K),\,f\geq g}\mathscr E(f).
\]
Continuous majorants decrease to $g$ on a product-directed
index. This follows from the usual continuous separation on
the compact Hausdorff carrier, and is also part of the stated
canonical-majorant input for $g=u+th$.

\begin{lemma}
For such $g$,
\[
             \mathscr E^{\rm usc}(g)=\inf_D A_D(g).       \tag{5}
\]
For every genuine finite-energy $u$ and $h\in C(K)$,
\[
 H_{t,h}(u):=E(P_\theta(u+th))
       =\inf_D\{A_D(u)+t\mu_D(h)\}.                      \tag{6}
\]
In particular $H_{t,h}$ is weakly upper semicontinuous
on the entire genuine finite-energy domain.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $f_i\downarrow g$ be continuous majorants. Since each
$\mu_D$ has finite divisorial support, all values of $g$
there are finite and
$\inf_i A_D(f_i)=A_D(g)$. Formula (1) and the interchange
of two infima give
\[
 \inf_i\mathscr E(f_i)=\inf_i\inf_D A_D(f_i)
                    =\inf_D\inf_i A_D(f_i)=\inf_D A_D(g).
\]
This uses a finite sum of coordinate values, not measurable-net
monotone convergence. Compactness makes these majorants
cofinal for energy: if $f\geq g$ is continuous and $\eta>0$,
a late stage satisfies $f_i<f+\eta$ everywhere. Thus (5) holds.

Now take canonical continuous model majorants $f_i\downarrow u$.
The envelopes $P_\theta(f_i+th)$ decrease to
$P_\theta(u+th)$. Indeed their genuine decreasing limit lies
below $u+th$, while each stage dominates every genuine
minorant of that obstacle. Their energies are finite because
$u-|t|\|h\|_\infty\leq P_\theta(u+th)\leq
u+|t|\|h\|_\infty$.
The envelope energy identity and decreasing continuity give
$H_{t,h}(u)=\inf_i\mathscr E(f_i+th)$.
Applying (5) proves (6).
Each plane in (6) is weakly continuous: its measure has
fixed finite divisorial support. An infimum of continuous
functions is upper semicontinuous.
\end{proof}

\section{Every genuine net with converging energies}
\begin{theorem}
Under inputs~1--3, if genuine $u_i,u\in\EE$ satisfy
\[
 u_i\longrightarrow u\ \hbox{weakly},\qquad
 E(u_i)\longrightarrow E(u),
\]
then $M(u_i)\longrightarrow M(u)$ weakly as original
Radon measures. No monotonicity of $u_i$ is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Their generic values converge. Subtract these constants;
the energies still converge by the raw $V$ constant law,
and the outputs do not change. The normalized costs are
bounded by one $B$ on a cofinal tail.
Positive mass-$V$ measures on the compact carrier have
weakly compact closure. Let $\lambda$ be any cluster measure
and restrict to its convergent subnet.

Fix a rational model $h$, a sufficiently large $c$, and
a nonzero signed $t$ allowed in (2). Upper semicontinuity
of (6), convergence of $E(u_i)$ and the lower half of (2)
give
\[
 t\lambda(h)\leq H_{t,h}(u)-E(u)
              +K_{B,c,h}t^2+|t|a_{B,h}/c.
\]
For positive $t$, divide by $t$ and send $t\downarrow0$.
For negative $t$, divide with reversal and send $t\uparrow0$.
The two original derivatives at $u$ give
$|\lambda(h)-M(u)(h)|\leq a_{B,h}/c$.
Finally $c\to\infty$ identifies those integrals.
Uniform density of rational model directions in $C(K)$
and the common raw mass $V$ identify $\lambda=M(u)$.
Every cluster is this same measure, so compactness gives
convergence of the whole net.
\end{proof}

\section{Absolute target integrals on weak energy sublevels}
\begin{lemma}
Under input~4, for each fixed intrinsic target $\nu$,
$z\mapsto\int(R-z)\,d\nu$ is weakly continuous on each
normalized cost sublevel.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a fixed $\nu_j$ in (3), the integral is a finite sum
of real divisorial coordinates and hence weakly continuous.
The uniform bound (3), applied at both endpoints of a
convergent net, proves continuity after $j$ tends to its endpoint.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
Under inputs~1,2,4, let $u_i,u\in\EE$ converge weakly
and have normalized costs uniformly bounded on a cofinal tail.
Then for every fixed intrinsic finite-dual target $\nu$,
\[
                       \int|u_i-u|\,d\nu\longrightarrow0. \tag{7}
\]
For each fixed index the relative differences are evaluated
on that pair's full-measure finite-cost set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First normalize the generic values to zero. Set
$w_i=\max(u_i,u)$. These are actual genuine functions of
finite energy, with normalized cost bounded by the same
bound enlarged to include $B(u)$. At every divisorial
coordinate $w_i\to u$, since those values are finite.
The lemma therefore gives continuity of the target costs
both for $u_i$ and for $w_i$. For each fixed index $i$,
on the full-measure set where its three relative costs are finite,
\[
 |u_i-u|=(R-u_i)+(R-u)-2(R-w_i).
\]
Integrate this identity and take the three limits to prove
(7) for normalized functions. No intersection of those sets
over an uncountable index is needed. For the original constants,
the additional absolute error is at most
$V|u_i(o)-u(o)|$, which tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\section{Independent changing tests at genuine outputs}
\begin{theorem}
Under inputs~1--5, suppose $u_i\to u$ weakly and
$E(u_i)\to E(u)$, while independently indexed genuine
$v_k\to v$ weakly have bounded normalized costs.
On the product-directed index,
\[
             (R-v_k)M(u_i)\longrightarrow(R-v)M(u)       \tag{8}
\]
weakly as finite original weighted Radon measures.
Each relative weight is evaluated on that measure's
full-measure finite-cost set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Normalize all generic values to zero first. Let
$\mu_i=M(u_i)$ and $\mu=M(u)$.
Choose $D\geq1$ containing the costs of $u_i,u,v_k,v$
on cofinal tails. The midpoints $(u_i+u)/2$ converge
weakly to $u$. Concavity bounds their energies below by
$(E(u_i)+E(u))/2$, while weak upper semicontinuity
bounds their limiting upper energies by $E(u)$.
Thus $\delta(u_i,u)\to0$. Formula (4) gives
\[
 \epsilon_i:=
 \sup_{\substack{z(o)=0\\B(z)\leq D}}
 \left|\int(R-z)\,d(\mu_i-\mu)\right|\longrightarrow0.
                                                        \tag{9}
\]

The fixed output $\mu$ is an intrinsic target, so
Theorem~4 gives $\int|v_k-v|\,d\mu\to0$.
Put $w_k=\max(v_k,v)$, which has cost at most $D$.
The three-cost identity in that theorem, now integrated
at $\mu_i$ and $\mu$, and (9) show
\[
 \int|v_k-v|\,d\mu_i
       \leq\int|v_k-v|\,d\mu+4\epsilon_i.               \tag{10}
\]
Every triple of relative costs in this calculation is
finite on a full-measure set for the fixed pair $(i,k)$;
no uncountable intersection is used.
Consequently replacing $v_k$ by $v$ in the left side
of (8) has error tending to zero against every $h\in C(K)$.

It remains to pass the fixed weight $R-v$.
Take normalized canonical model envelopes $v_j\downarrow v$.
At the fixed intrinsic target $\mu$, Lemma~3 gives
\[
       \int(v_j-v)\,d\mu\longrightarrow0.
\]
The same ordered tail at $\mu_i$ is at most this quantity
plus $2\epsilon_i$, by (9) applied to $v$ and $v_j$.
For fixed $j$, $R-v_j$ is bounded continuous on $U$.
Theorem~2 gives $\mu_i\to\mu$ weakly; a fixed continuous
cutoff supported in $U$, followed by Radon inner
regularity and $\mu(Z)=0$, therefore gives
$(R-v_j)\mu_i\to(R-v_j)\mu$.
First pass $i$ at fixed $j$ and fixed cutoff, then
remove the cutoff, and finally let $j$ tend to its endpoint.
The preceding ordered-tail bounds prove
$(R-v)\mu_i\to(R-v)\mu$.
Combining this with (10) proves (8) on the product index.

For nonnormalized testing potentials, write
$v_k=v_k^0+v_k(o)$. The restored weighted measure is
$(R-v_k^0)\mu_i-v_k(o)\mu_i$.
Weak convergence of $\mu_i$ and $v_k(o)\to v(o)$
passes the second term with its full raw mass $V$.
The base normalizations leave all outputs unchanged.
\end{proof}
