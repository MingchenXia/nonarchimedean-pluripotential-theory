### 4. Uniform continuity needed for variation

$$\|P_\theta f-P_\theta g\|_\infty\leq\|f-g\|_\infty.$$

### 5. Both derivatives of continuous-obstacle energy

Define F_theta(f)=E_theta(P_theta f) for f in C0(X^an). It is finite, concave and translation equivariant with raw coefficient theta^n. Concavity follows from the genuine convex cone, envelope order and bounded energy concavity; alternatively it follows from the pointwise limit of the ample concave functionals in Section3.

We first give the needed ample supporting inequalities rather than importing an unproved assertion about arbitrary obstacles. For an ample class omega and continuous f, q=P_omega f is continuous genuine. If 0<=s<=1, monotonicity gives P_omega(q+s(f-q))=q. The actual ample G0 outer derivative at q in the continuous direction f-q therefore yields orthogonality:

$$\int(f-q)M_\omega(q)=0.$$

Energy concavity along genuine psh segments gives

$$E_\omega(r)\leq E_\omega(q)+\int(r-q)M_\omega(q),\qquad r=P_\omega g.$$ Since r<=g and M_omega(q) is positive, the displayed orthogonality proves the supporting inequality

$$F_\omega(g)-F_\omega(f)\leq\int(g-f)M_\omega(P_\omega f).$$

Use this inequality once at f and once at f+tg. For t>0 it bounds the quotient between integral g M_omega(P_omega(f+tg)) and integral g M_omega(P_omega f). Apply it with omega=theta_epsilon and pass epsilon down to zero using the actual energy and operator limits from Section3. Thus

$$\int gM_\theta(P_\theta(f+tg))\leq
\frac{F_\theta(f+tg)-F_\theta(f)}t\leq
\int gM_\theta(P_\theta f).$$

The envelope Lipschitz estimate and Section4 make the left integral converge to the right one as t down to zero. For t<0 the same two supporting inequalities reverse under division; the two bounds have the same limit as t increases to zero. Therefore both derivatives exist and agree:

$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}F_\theta(f+tg)=\int gM_\theta(P_\theta f),\qquad f,g\in C^0(X^{\rm an}).$$

In particular this proves both outer signs at every **continuous genuine** theta-psh base potential u, by taking f=u. It proves the continuous-obstacle formula for every nef actual L, without semiampleness and including zero raw top mass. It does not infer a derivative at an arbitrary unbounded member of the floor-defined E1(theta), or even at an arbitrary bounded discontinuous base, from this continuous-obstacle argument. For example, on the product of two projective lines with L pulled back from one factor, the raw top mass is zero and the present top-degree energy is constant. A different lower-degree mixed energy can have positive mass and is a different functional.
