# A fixed-bundle rational metric bridge and an exact Gauss cutoff

New unreviewed root reconstruction. The frozen original author75 and partial review76 remain unchanged. No original problem or accepted dependency is closed by this document. The first two constructions concern an already supplied continuous rational PL metric on the ORIGINAL algebraic line bundle, and actual sections on that same bundle. They do not construct global rational approximants for an arbitrary continuous weak potential. The source-based semipositivity step retains the external formal/algebraic comparison and curve criteria with their exact hypotheses.

## 1. Construct the actual strict cover and frames first

Let k be algebraically closed and trivially valued of characteristic zero, X a reduced projective k-variety and L an ample line bundle. Fix its actual continuous reference metric. Suppose a continuous metric w on L is supplied with the following finite rational PL local presentation: in an ordinary analytic neighborhood of each point, its weight in a frame of L is F(log|g1|,...,log|gr|), where F is a supplied continuous rational PL function locally selecting from finitely many rational affine functions, with the affine terms finite on their charts. In particular, points with the same displayed log norms have the same F-value; this factorization is part of the hypothesis. At a zero coordinate every affine branch permitted on that chart has coefficient zero in that coordinate. The same actual local formula is pulled back to K=k((t)), with |t|=exp(-1). This is a hypothesis on the supplied metric's representation, not a claimed regularization theorem for all weak inputs.

For each point of X_K^an choose a compact strictly K-affinoid neighborhood V contained in one of the ordinary local charts and in a trivialization of L. Delete the zero coordinates which the chart does not use. Shrink the neighborhood so every remaining analytic function g_i is invertible; compactness gives positive lower and finite upper norm bounds, and integral t-power bounds can be chosen outside them. Such neighborhoods exist on the good strictly analytic projective space. Compactness gives finitely many of them. Retain the actual frame e of L and its transition functions on overlaps.

On V, the log-norm image of the units g_i is a finite union of rational polytopes. This is the compact invertible-function image theorem in the PUBLISHED Ducros paper, Theorem 3.2, LF2077-2150, not the different local-germ Theorem 3.4. Its hypotheses are compactness, strictness and invertibility, all checked here; it gives dimension at most dim(V), and no equality is needed. Its coefficient group for this discretely valued K has rational log constants.

Subdivide that image by all its faces and all hyperplanes on which two of the finitely many affine weight branches agree. On a relative open convex cell, the distinct affine values have a fixed strict order. Continuity of the supplied selection forces it to choose one of those values throughout the cell: the choice between distinct values is locally constant, and the cell is connected. Branches which agree identically there may be treated as one value. Continuity extends that chosen affine value to the closed cell. The finitely many closed cells cover the image.

Clear all denominators in these cells and in the weight branches, choosing one positive integer p for the finite cover. A cell inequality becomes an inequality between absolute values of invertible analytic Laurent monomials, with constants powers of |t|. Its inverse image in V is therefore a compact strictly analytic Laurent domain. These finitely many domains cover V and form a finite analytic G-cover; no identity with the topological interior of a weak polytope is asserted. Further finite strictly affinoid refinements may be taken.

On a cell where

    w = sum_i a_i log|g_i| + b,

put q=t^(-p b) product_i g_i^(p a_i). Every exponent is an integer and q is an analytic unit on the cell. Its norm satisfies log|q|=p w. Thus

    s=q e^p,             ||s||_(w^p)=1.

The s are literal frames of the fixed L_K^p. On overlaps their ratios are the actual original frame transitions times q/q', and have norm one because both frames have norm one for the same metric. They supply a real G-cover and a fixed-bundle unit cocycle before GM Proposition2.10 is invoked. No independently invented line bundle, absolute normalization, or merely numerical class is used. GM constructs a formal model of this SAME L_K^p from that cocycle. On the proper algebraic X_K, GM Theorem1.1 and Remark2.6 identify formal and algebraic model metrics. The latter comparison cites GK17 Proposition8.13; its full dependent proof has not been independently reconstructed in this root document.

For the basic finite maximum w=(1/p)max_j log|q_j|, with one constant nonzero q_j, this construction needs no polytope theorem. The winning domains |q_l|<=|q_j| are rational domains, q_j is invertible there because it dominates the constant, and q_j e^p is the unit frame. This elementary case also checks logarithmic zeros directly.

## 2. The positive curve test uses ordinary neighborhoods

Assume additionally that on the ordinary analytic local charts, the supplied weight is a decreasing limit of actual finite convex nondecreasing logarithmic expressions, and that these expressions pull back with their extended zero-coordinate convention through complete field extension and analytic curve morphisms. This is the precise local-expression compatibility needed for the application. It is stronger evidence than an assertion that an arbitrary positive current pulls back.

Over the completed algebraic closure of K, pull the expressions to any smooth analytic curve mapping into a chart. Each regular logarithm is subharmonic, allowing its isolated logarithmic poles. A finite affine term with nonnegative coefficients is subharmonic; finite maxima and the stated locally convex expressions preserve subharmonicity. Branches that become identically negative infinity are discarded; finiteness of the pulled-back continuous weight excludes an identically negative-infinity limit. Thuillier's decreasing-limit result consequently gives subharmonicity of the actual pulled-back weight. This concerns arbitrary local analytic curves, without requiring an algebraic global extension of such a curve.

The already constructed metric is Q-PL on the known bundle. In a trivialization its weight is therefore an R-PL function. GR Proposition5.1 and Corollary5.4 now give semipositivity from those curve tests, at every point of the ordinary analytic neighborhood. A frame change adds the logarithm of an analytic unit, hence a harmonic function; the assertion is independent of the frame. The curve criterion applies to already represented PL functions on a good strictly analytic space. GR Remark1.8's unknown general current converse is never used.

GJR7.3(3) warns that semipositivity is a sheaf in the ordinary analytic topology, NOT in the G-topology. Unit frames on separate Laurent cells by themselves prove no positivity at their boundaries. The ordinary-neighborhood curve tests above include every boundary point. GM Proposition3.11 then supplies global nefness of the formal model on the separated proper space. Its proof chooses an analytic point reducing to the generic point of each proper vertical curve, refines the local nef model and original model, and uses properness of that reduction closure to retain the entire curve. This is the required local-to-global step, not a gluing assertion based just on the winning cells.

The algebraic model metric on L_K is therefore nef. BE Theorem7.8 applies because K is NONTRIVIALLY valued, X_K is projective and L_K is ample; neither smoothness nor normalization of X_K is assumed there. It gives genuine continuous PSH membership on the same L_K, and Corollary7.6 supplies uniform approximation by same-class FS metrics. In its nef-to-FS proof, a nef model is perturbed on a dominating projective model toward an ample model of the SAME generic L_K. No class increase on the generic fiber is being canceled without proof.

## 3. Exact Gauss truncation retains the original class

All formulas in this section for phi are potentials relative to the fixed reference metric. Their equality is equivalent to equality of the actual metrics, because that reference is pulled back along the same Gauss map.

Let an actual FS metric on L_K be represented on mL_K by finitely many globally generating sections s_j and real additive weights c_j. Proper flat cohomology base change gives

    H0(X_K,mL_K) = H0(X,mL) tensor_k K.

Choose a k-basis b_l of H0(X,mL), and expand every coefficient in its Laurent series in t. Group by exponent:

    s_j = sum_(n>=N_j) t^n s_(j,n),       s_(j,n) in H0(X,mL).

All nonzero scalar coefficients of the s_(j,n) have k-norm one. In the chosen reference metric there is a uniform C with ||s_(j,n)(x)||<=C for every j,n,x: the finite basis b_l gives that bound by the non-Archimedean triangle inequality. The finitely many c_j are fixed.

At the Gauss lift sigma(x), the norm of this section is exactly

    ||s_j(sigma(x))|| = max_n exp(-n)||s_(j,n)(x)||.

This is the actual Gauss seminorm in H(x)((t)), with the reference on L pulled back. It is not an inequality derived from restriction alone.

The coefficient sections as a whole generate mL on X. If they all vanished at a geometric point, all the original K-sections would vanish at its scalar extension, contradicting their generation. Their k-linear span is finite-dimensional, so a finite initial collection spans it. Choose a cutoff N0 containing that finite generating collection. The finite maximum of their weighted reference norms is a positive continuous function on compact X^an, with a positive minimum a. For all sufficiently large N, the tail bound exp(-N)C max_j exp(c_j) is less than a. Therefore the infinite Gauss maximum equals EXACTLY its finite cutoff everywhere:

    phi_K(sigma(x))
      = (1/m) max_(j,n<=N) (log||s_(j,n)(x)||-n+c_j).

The right side is an actual FS metric on the original mL, with its finite coefficient-section generators and original reference. This proves the cutoff rather than just bounding a tail, and does not change the ample class. The argument handles zero evaluations through the generating minimum a. It permits real weights; a rational presentation requires rational weights or their separate uniform approximation.

Now let phi_i be same-class K-FS metrics converging uniformly to the supplied w_K. Their Gauss restrictions are actual k-FS metrics by the cutoff. Since w_K sigma=w and restriction does not increase the uniform error, these restrictions converge uniformly to w. BE Corollary7.6, valid for the original trivially valued field with a nonzero real weight subgroup, gives genuine CPSH(L) membership. If a decreasing sequence is required for this continuous w, select errors tending rapidly to zero and add decreasing error constants: for errors epsilon_i with epsilon_(i+1)<=epsilon_i/2, the constants 4epsilon_i make the corrected FS sequence decrease and still converge to w. The inequality uses the gap 4(epsilon_i-epsilon_(i+1))>=epsilon_i+epsilon_(i+1).

## 4. Exact remaining scope

This is a source-based bridge for a SUPPLIED global continuous rational PL metric on a fixed ample line bundle, with the stated local-expression compatibility. The strict-domain/unit-frame construction and exact same-class Gauss cutoff are new unreviewed detailed deductions. The primary statements and pertinent proof bodies in GM, GR, GJR, BE and Ducros were read; full proofs of their cited formal-algebraization dependencies were not.

Compact extensions in GM2.7 likewise do not automatically preserve nefness.
