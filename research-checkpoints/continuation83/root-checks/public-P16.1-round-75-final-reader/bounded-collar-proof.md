## 8. Bounded local products and the remaining smooth gate

Theorem 4.1 removes the representation and positivity circularity for its explicit continuous contact metrics. The following construction proves a bounded local product statement for that exact decreasing class. It does not accept the embedded round70 chain by citation.

The literal CLD Proposition 8.7.2 assumes continuous functions converging **locally uniformly**. Its proof multiplies by continuous functions using Lemma 8.5.1 and inducts after applying $d'd''$ (S3 LF10432--10449). A bounded decreasing local sequence with a discontinuous limit does not meet that premise. BE Theorem 8.4 is likewise a uniform extension on continuous psh-regularizable metrics, with exact fundamental-cycle mass in 8.5 (S7 LF2611--2779). The construction below uses genuine global decreasing convergence instead.

### 8.1. Common-collar bounded products and weighted limits

**Proposition 8.1.** On an analytic open of the original projective variety, let a finite tuple $u_1,\ldots,u_n$ be locally bounded decreasing limits of the contact weights of Section 2 on common neighborhoods. There is an intrinsic positive mixed local measure, agreeing with the continuous local product, obtained by limits of the supplied continuous products. The limit is independent of their supplied sequences, common collars and auxiliary ample references. The same conclusion holds for a bounded local contact test $u_0$ multiplying the measure, with its supplied decreasing approximation. No local comparison or IBP assertion is included.

We first construct actual collar data around any point inside the common neighborhood. Fix a very ample line bundle $H$ on $X$ and a section $s$ whose affine chart contains the point's algebraic support. Basic analytic neighborhoods there have finitely many regular constraints with finite rational strict margins. Each inverted nonvanishing function is $\tau_i/s^{d_i}$, by ordinary section localization; this uses no normality. The section $a=s\prod_i\tau_i$ of a positive power of $H$ gives the exact principal chart where all those inverses are regular. Each new constraint can be written $F_j/a^m$, after increasing a common $m$. Increase it until that power of $H$ is very ample and append a complete section system with sufficiently loose bounds. Its ratios by $a^m$ generate the original affine ring, by the standard chart of the closed projective embedding. A rational FS local weight
$$v=\frac1q\max\{q\lambda,\log|F_j/a^m|-C_j\}$$
with finite rational thresholds and negative floor $\lambda$ has compact $Q=\{v\le0\}$ inside the specified neighborhood. Choose a rational $\epsilon>0$ below all divided strict margins and with $\lambda<-\epsilon$. Then the interior of $P=\{v\le-\epsilon\}$ contains the point. Here $q$ is the common tensor exponent of $H$; the thresholds are $C_j$, not $qC_j$. This is an actual FS metric on $H$, with global model potential $p$ relative to its canonical trivial metric. Write $\theta=\omega+dd^cp$ for its curvature, where $\omega=c_1(H)$ denotes that fixed original reference.

The section-localization assertion follows by ordinary module localization on a finite affine trivializing cover and by its finite equalizers for matching sections: filtered localization commutes with those equalizers. Thus $\Gamma(D(s),\mathcal O_X)=\varinjlim_d H^0(X,H^d)/s^d$, retaining the original nonnormal ring.

Choose $M$ bounding all the local limits, including the test if present, on a common neighborhood of $Q$. Choose rational $A$ with $A\epsilon>2M+2$. Then $h=Av+M+1$ is below $-M$ on $P$ and at least $M+1/2$ on the compact strip $Q\cap\{v\ge-1/(2A)\}$. For each supplied decreasing continuous sequence, its closed superlevel sets $\{u_{i,j}\ge M+1/4\}$ on this strip decrease to the empty set. Compactness gives one common eventual tail for all finitely many sequences with $u_{i,j}<M+1/4<h$ on the **whole** strip. This invokes no Dini theorem for a discontinuous limit.

Set $\Psi_{i,j}=\max(u_{i,j}-h,0)$ on $Q$, zero outside. They agree with zero on the whole strip, hence on a neighborhood of the gluing boundary. Their metric weights are $\max(u_{i,j}-M-1,Av)$ inside and $Av$ on the reference-winning strip. These have the contact weights and whole collars required by Theorem 4.1. Their actual class is $A\theta$, on the same algebraic $H$ and original cocycle after clearing $A$; no desired weak membership is used. Their explicit bounded decreasing limits $\Psi_i$ are genuine by decreasing closure. On $P$ put
$$\Phi_i=\Psi_i/A+p,\qquad G=v-p+(M+1)/A,$$
so $u_i/A=\Phi_i+G$. The actual global metric with local weight $v+\Psi_i/A$ equals $u_i/A-(M+1)/A$ on $P$. Its canonical-reference potential is $\Phi_i$ in the original ample class $\omega$, not $\Psi_i/A$ alone. Every $\Phi_i$ is bounded genuine finite energy. Define the local mixed measure on the interior of $P$ by
$$A^n M^{\rm raw}_\omega(\Phi_1,\ldots,\Phi_n).$$
The factor $A^n$ accounts for scaling from $u_i/A$; no extra factor is inserted if one instead retains the unscaled global metrics in class $A\theta$.

For continuous inputs this is the continuous local product. Theorem 4.1 supplies genuine same-bundle FS approximation; BE7.14 smooths the FS metrics by smooth maxima (S7 LF2370--2379). BE8.18 identifies their model Deligne intersections, with fundamental cycles, by induction using a relatively regular section whose norm is one at every model Shilov point (LF3125--3202). Along with uniform continuity, this is BJ Remark7.23's genuine/CLD continuous compatibility (S1 LF4126--4129). Continuous locality is S1 Remark7.42 (LF4546--4551). These applications stay on the inner open, avoiding the boundaries of auxiliary affinoid domains; the represented-nef-to-positive-current implication is also stated and proved at S10 LF2547--2589.

Genuine decreasing continuity of the finite-energy mixed operator gives convergence for the supplied global truncations, hence for every compactly supported continuous test in the inner open. To prove independence, compare one supplied sequence in its original collar and in a third smaller shared collar about the tested point. At every finite stage their continuous local products agree there: both have the same actual local weights up to the stated constant and scale. Pass to both genuine decreasing limits against compact tests. For two different supplied sequences in that shared collar, their explicit global limits are the same function $\max(u_i-h,0)$ there, zero outside. The genuine global operator gives the same measure. Comparing each sequence with the shared collar proves independence of sequences and collars. Changes of reference use the same continuous equality and shared-collar argument, rather than comparing unequal numerical representatives. The identities hold near every point and therefore glue the locally finite positive measures on the analytic open.

For a bounded decreasing contact test use the same construction for $i=0$. On the inner open, $u_{0,j}=A\Phi_{0,j}+AG$ with fixed continuous $G$ in the chosen algebraic frame. BJ7.21 applies to the bounded genuine $\Phi_{0,j}$ and the other bounded genuine factors; the $G$ term passes by ordinary weak convergence against compact cutoffs. The sign in the scalar pairing identity is the pairing containing $\Phi_0$ **minus** the zero-test pairing, as printed correctly at LF4108--4113; LF4119--4123 reverse it. With the correct order both pairings converge. Translate the test below zero and apply the positive weighted-measure Lemma7.22 to its negative to get weighted weak convergence. This rederives the application without treating a difference of two decreasing functions as decreasing. The same shared-collar comparison establishes independence of the weighted limit. This proves Proposition 8.1.

### 8.2. What the bounded local product does not yet establish

To complete Gate B one must still transfer this bounded result through signed local PL extensions, construct compact PL extensions and subordinate rational PL partitions with controlled support, and prove compact IBP with support retained in a common collar. A telescoping sum must then prove **raw** global bounded-local mixed mass, and common-reference locality must give comparison and domination. These steps precede smooth local/global equality; domination of an auxiliary weak cone cannot be assumed from genuine global domination.
