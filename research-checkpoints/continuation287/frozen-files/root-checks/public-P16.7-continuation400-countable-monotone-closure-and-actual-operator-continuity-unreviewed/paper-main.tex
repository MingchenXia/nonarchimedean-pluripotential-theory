\begin{abstract}
We independently reconstruct the current generated-current calculus on a proper
nonalgebraizable analytic surface and prove a new countable closure theorem.
Every decreasing sequence of actual generated potentials with a common lower
energy bound has a single continuous decreasing presentation of its actual
pointwise limit. Energy continuity, ordered Monge--Ampere convergence and
summable convex-series closure follow. Full energy-sublevel compactness,
arbitrary-measure existence and pointwise uniqueness remain unresolved.
This is a scoped unverified draft, not a solution of the original P16.7.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Carrier, domain and precise new statement}
Retain $K=\mathbf C((s))$, $|s|=e^{-1/8}$, $t=s^8$, and
\[
 X=(\mathbf G_m^2)^{\mathrm{an}}/\langle(t,2),(3,t)\rangle,
 \qquad x_i=-\log|z_i|,\quad \rho=|x|^2/2,\quad \theta=\DD\rho.
\]
Affine changes of $\rho$ along periods give a global smooth closed curvature
datum of raw top mass $M=2$. The independent geometric argument and its exact
conventional foundations appear in the reconstruction below. Let $\C$ be the
continuous weights $a$ on $X$ for which $\rho+a$ belongs locally to the full
minimal continuous CLD psh sheaf. This uses all uniform-closure stages.
The actual positive bounded products are
\[
 B(a,b)=\theta\wedge\theta_b+\DD(a\theta_b),\qquad
 \theta_b=\theta+\DD[b],\qquad \MA(a)=B(a,a).
\]
The energy is the canonical cubic
\[
 E(a)=\frac13\int_X a\,[\MA(0)+B(a,0)+\MA(a)].
\]
It is monotone, concave, and satisfies $E(a+c)=E(a)+Mc$.
Define $\G$ to be the \emph{actual pointwise} limits $a_j\downarrow u$,
$a_j\in\C$, with $\inf_j E(a_j)>-\infty$. The reconstruction proves that
$E(u)=\lim_jE(a_j)$ is independent of every such presentation, preserves
order, and that $u$ is USC and is not identically $-\infty$.
Neither this definition nor the theorem assumes intrinsic recognition of
every unbounded psh function.

\begin{lemma}[Strict compact dominance]
Let $X$ be compact, let USC $g_k$ decrease pointwise to $g$, and let $a$ be
finite continuous with $g<a$ everywhere. Then $g_k<a$ everywhere for all
sufficiently large $k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The ordinary open sets $\{g_k<a\}$ increase and cover $X$.
A finite subcover has a largest index. The proof includes points where
$g=-\infty$ and does not require metrizability.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual countable monotone closure]
If $u_k\in\G$ decrease pointwise and $L=\inf_kE(u_k)>-\infty$, their actual
pointwise infimum $u$ belongs to $\G$, and $E(u_k)\downarrow E(u)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $f_{k,j}\in\C$ decreasing in $j$ to $u_k$ and enumerate every pair
$(k,j)$ as continuous functions $h_1,h_2,\ldots$. Then
$\inf_mh_m=\inf_ku_k=u$ at every point. Put
\[
 \epsilon_n=2^{-n},\qquad r_n=\sum_{\ell>n}\epsilon_\ell=2^{-n}.
\]
Each continuous term is bounded on compact $X$; no uniform lower bound on
the entire presentation is assumed. Initialize $k_0=0$.
Inductively choose $k_n\geq\max(n,k_{n-1})$ with
$u_{k_n}<h_m+\epsilon_n$ on all of $X$ for $m\leq n$.
The lemma applies to the finite continuous minimum of these bounds.
That minimum is used only as a bound, never as a psh potential.
For $n>1$, also $u_{k_n}\leq u_{k_{n-1}}\leq v_{n-1}$, where the preceding
stage has selected $v_{n-1}$. Apply the lemma to the presentation of $u_{k_n}$
to choose $j_n$ such that $v_n=f_{k_n,j_n}$ obeys
\[
 v_n<h_m+\epsilon_n\ (m\leq n),\qquad
 v_n<v_{n-1}+\epsilon_n\ (n>1).
\]
All bounds are strict and finite continuous, so a single index works.
Let $a_n=v_n+r_n$. Constant stability gives $a_n\in\C$, and
\[
 a_n-a_{n-1}<\epsilon_n+r_n-r_{n-1}=0\quad(n>1).
\]
Each $a_n\geq u$. Its decreasing limit $a$ satisfies $a\leq h_m$ for every
$m$, since $a_n<h_m+\epsilon_n+r_n$ for $n\geq m$.
Hence $a=u$ pointwise, including all poles.
Order preservation and the constant rule give
$E(a_n)\geq E(u_{k_n})\geq L$. This proves $u\in\G$ without presupposing
generation of the limit.

Order gives $E(u)\leq L$. For a presentation $b_\ell\downarrow u$ and any
$\eta>0$, the lemma gives $u_k<b_\ell+\eta$ globally eventually.
Thus $L\leq E(b_\ell)+M\eta$. Let $\ell\to\infty$ and then
$\eta\to0$ to obtain $L\leq E(u)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Countable directed infima]
A countable downward directed family in $\G$ with a common lower energy
bound has its actual pointwise infimum in $\G$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate the family and recursively choose a member below both the previous
choice and the next enumerated member. The resulting decreasing sequence is
coinitial. Apply the theorem. No uncountable coinitiality assertion follows.
\end{proof}

\section*{Actual operator and measure consequences}
The reconstructed generated operator is a limit of genuine bounded analytic
products. For $a_j\downarrow u$ and a bounded test $v\in\C$, the identity
\[
 \int_Xv\,\MA(a_j)
 =\left.\frac{d}{dt}E((1-t)a_j+tv)\right|_{t=0}
  +\left.\frac{d}{dt}E(ta_j)\right|_{t=1}
\]
and finite interpolation of cubic coefficients prove presentation independence.
The actual test lattice $\operatorname{span}\C$ is dense in $C(X)$, so the
limit is a positive Radon measure of raw mass two on the entire analytic space.
The reconstruction supplies mixed polarization, finite dual, moving moments,
comparison and exact contact-excluding locality before the new theorem is used.

\begin{corollary}[Ordered continuity without prior generation]
For the sequences in the theorem, $\MA(u_k)\to\MA(u)$ weakly against all
of $C(X)$. If also $v_k\downarrow v$ has a finite lower energy bound, then
$B(u_k,v_k)\to B(u,v)$ and
$\int|u_k-u|\MA(v_k)\to0$.
For every positive Radon $\mu$ of mass two and finite full bounded dual
\[
 E^*(\mu)=\sup_{a\in\C}\left(E(a)-\int a\,d\mu\right),
\]
one has $\int|u_k-u|\,d\mu\to0$, without any support restriction.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The theorem has now supplied actual generated limits. Cubic interpolation at
every finite convex combination gives the coefficient limits in the displayed
test identity; density and common mass give all-continuous-test convergence.
Polarization and the multivariate energy moments give the mixed and moving
conclusions, with the full moment proof recorded below. Finite dual makes
$u_1$ and $u$ integrable. Off their null pole sets,
$0\leq u_k-u\leq u_1-u\in L^1(\mu)$, so dominated convergence proves the
last conclusion. This is an ordered-family statement, not uniform
integrability of a nonordered energy sublevel.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Summable nonpositive convex series]
Let $u_i\in\G$, $u_i\leq0$, $E(u_i)\geq-C_i$, $C_i\geq0$, and let
$t_i\geq0$, $\sum_i t_i\leq1$, $\sum_it_iC_i<\infty$. Omitting zero
terms, the actual pointwise sum $u=\sum_it_iu_i$ belongs to $\G$ and
\[
 E(u)\geq-\sum_it_iC_i,\qquad
 E(s_N)\downarrow E(u),\qquad\MA(s_N)\to\MA(u),
 \quad s_N=\sum_{i\leq N}t_iu_i.
\]
For every finite-full-dual $\mu$ of mass two, with $D=E^*(\mu)$,
\[
 \int|s_N-u|\,d\mu
 \leq D\sum_{i>N}t_i+\sum_{i>N}t_iC_i\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The remaining coefficient at zero makes $s_N$ a finite convex combination.
Concavity gives $E(s_N)\geq-\sum_{i\leq N}t_iC_i$. Nonpositivity gives
$s_N\downarrow u$, so the countable closure theorem and ordered continuity
apply to the actual sum. The dual bound
$\int(-u_i)\,d\mu\leq D-E(u_i)\leq D+C_i$ and Tonelli give the tail
estimate. The countable union of pole sets and the pole set of $u$ are
$\mu$-null, so differences may be assigned arbitrary values there.
\end{proof}
