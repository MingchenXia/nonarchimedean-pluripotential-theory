# Ordered operator continuity and infinite convex sums

The following are new consequences of n1 and the actual countable presentation theorem n2. They impose no invariance or support restriction.

If u_k in G decrease and inf E(u_k)>-infinity, then u=inf u_k is generated by n2. The canonical energy continuity now applies with a proved actual limit. For every v in C, the coefficients of E((1-t)u_k+tv) and E(tu_k) converge by finite interpolation, and their boundary derivative identity from n1 gives integral v MA(u_k)->integral v MA(u). Density of span C and mass two give weak convergence against EVERY continuous analytic-space test. Polarization gives B(u_k,v_k)->B(u,v) if both decreasing families have finite lower energy bounds. This extends the previously conditional decreasing operator theorem to all such sequences; it does not assert general net convergence.

There is actual L1 convergence for arbitrary-support finite-full-dual measures. Let mu be positive Radon, mu(X)=2 and D=E*(mu)<infinity. The potentials u_k and u are integrable by n1, and u_1 dominates every u_k. Their decreasing differences u_k-u are nonnegative and bounded above mu-a.e. by the integrable u_1-u. Dominated convergence gives integral |u_k-u|mu->0. Moving MA moments and mixed moments follow exactly from the multivariate energy coefficients in n1, because n2 has now proved their limiting potentials generated. In particular, integral |u_k-u|MA(v_k)->0 for any second energy-bounded decreasing generated sequence v_k. These facts concern each ordered family and do not assert uniform integrability of a whole nonordered energy sublevel.

Here is a further actual analytic closure theorem. Suppose u_i in G are nonpositive, E(u_i)>=-C_i with C_i>=0, and t_i>=0 satisfy T=sum t_i<=1 and C_tot=sum t_i C_i<infinity. Omit zero coefficients, especially at poles. Define s_N=sum_(i<=N)t_i u_i and u=sum_(i>=1)t_i u_i pointwise, in R union {-infinity}. Each s_N is the finite convex combination of the u_i and zero with remaining coefficient 1-sum_(i<=N)t_i. Convexity gives s_N in G and concavity gives E(s_N)>=-sum_(i<=N)t_i C_i>=-C_tot. Because each u_i<=0, s_N decreases to the prescribed pointwise sum u. The theorem n2 supplies a single bounded continuous analytic decreasing presentation of that exact sum. Thus u in G, E(u)>=-C_tot, E(s_N) downarrow E(u), MA(s_N)->MA(u), and the sum is USC. It is not identically -infinity.

For every finite-full-dual mu as above, Tonelli and the n1 dual bound give an exact quantitative tail estimate:

integral |s_N-u|mu=sum_(i>N)t_i integral(-u_i)mu
<=D sum_(i>N)t_i+sum_(i>N)t_i C_i ->0.

All terms are nonnegative, so no undefined difference of infinite integrals occurs. On the common mu-null union of pole sets and the pole set of the sum, values of the differences may be assigned arbitrarily. This proves integrability and control for a fixed summable family, with the actual representative supplied independently by n2. It does not replace analytic potentials by an inverse Banach measure.

For completeness, the finite-dual variational objective over G equals its supremum over C: for any u in G its bounded continuous presentation satisfies E(a_j)-integral a_j mu->E(u)-integral u mu. Hence enlarging to these actual countable completions changes neither E*(mu) nor the eligible measure condition. Constants still fix the raw mass two. This is a noncircular necessary condition; none of the closure theorems proves that every such mu is MA(u).
