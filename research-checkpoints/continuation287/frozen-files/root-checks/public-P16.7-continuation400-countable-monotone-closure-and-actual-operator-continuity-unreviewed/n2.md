# Countable monotone closure with an actual analytic presentation

New theorem. On the analytic carrier, positivity datum and generated domain G reconstructed in n1, suppose u_k in G decrease pointwise and inf_k E(u_k)>-infinity. Then their ACTUAL pointwise limit u belongs to G and E(u_k) decreases to E(u). The hypothesis that the limit is already generated is unnecessary for sequences with this energy bound. No metrizability, countable point set, strictness of theta, or arbitrary-current base change is required. This is countable monotone closure, not compactness of an arbitrary sublevel net.

I give the diagonal construction explicitly; it is more than a choice of matching indices in the presentations. Choose presentations f_(k,j) in C, f_(k,j) downarrow u_k. Each continuous term is bounded on compact X; no uniform lower bound on the entire presentation is assumed. Enumerate ALL pairs (k,j) as a sequence, and denote the resulting continuous functions by h_1,h_2,... . Thus inf_m h_m=u pointwise. Every h_m dominates u. Fix epsilon_n=2^(-n), set r_n=sum_(ell>n) epsilon_ell=2^(-n), and initialize k_0=0.

The compactness lemma needed here holds even for USC outer terms: if g_k downarrow g, each g_k is USC, and a is finite continuous with g<a everywhere, then {g_k<a} is an increasing ordinary open cover of compact X. A finite subcover gives a global late index. This statement includes poles and uses strict inequality.

At stage n choose k_n>=max(n,k_(n-1)) so that

u_(k_n)<h_m+epsilon_n everywhere for all m<=n.

This is possible by the compactness lemma applied to the continuous minimum of the finitely many h_m+epsilon_n. That minimum is only a bound: it is NEVER inserted into the psh class. For n>1, u_(k_n)<=u_(k_(n-1))<=v_(n-1), so u_(k_n)<v_(n-1)+epsilon_n also holds. Apply the same lemma to the continuous presentation of u_(k_n). Select j_n such that v_n=f_(k_n,j_n) satisfies, everywhere,

v_n<h_m+epsilon_n (m<=n), and v_n<v_(n-1)+epsilon_n (n>1).

There are finitely many strict bounds at this stage, so one j_n works. Now put a_n=v_n+r_n. Constants preserve C, and

a_n-a_(n-1)<epsilon_n+r_n-r_(n-1)=0.

Thus a_n in C is decreasing. It dominates u. For fixed m and n>=m it is <h_m+epsilon_n+r_n, so its limit a is <=h_m for every m. Therefore a<=inf_m h_m=u. Combining both inequalities gives a=u pointwise, including every pole. This is a single genuine continuous analytic presentation of the original limit; it is not an abstract functional representative or an equality modulo a null set.

Let L=inf_k E(u_k)>-infinity. Since v_n>=u_(k_n), generated order preservation in n1 gives E(v_n)>=E(u_(k_n))>=L. The constant rule gives E(a_n)>=L. Hence the new presentation has finite lower energy and u belongs to G. Its upper energy is finite because its first term is continuous. The non-bottom conclusion of n1 applies, so u cannot be identically -infinity.

There is also an explicit check of the energy limit. Order gives E(u)<=E(u_k), so E(u)<=L. For any fixed continuous presentation term b_l downarrow u and eta>0, the open sets {u_k<b_l+eta} increase and cover X. The compactness lemma gives E(u_k)<=E(b_l)+2eta for large k. Taking k to infinity, then l to infinity and eta to zero, gives L<=E(u). Thus E(u_k) downarrow E(u), without circular use of continuity to prove generation.

The proof uses only compactness, USC outer terms, continuous presentations, constant stability, canonical monotone energy, and its positive mass constant rule. Its analytic applicability follows from the independent bounded-current/energy reconstruction in n1; no earlier supplied note is a premise. A naive diagonal would be wrong even on one point: f_(k,j)=0 for j<2^k and f_(k,j)=-1+1/k otherwise decreases in j to u_k=-1+1/k. The u_k decrease to -1 and have uniformly bounded energies for E(c)=2c, but f_(k,k) converges to0. The finitely many GLOBAL upper constraints above are exactly what prevents that failure.

Corollary. A countable downward directed family of members of G with a common lower energy bound has its pointwise infimum in G. Recursively choose a member below the previously chosen member and the next enumerated member. This produces a decreasing sequence coinitial in the family, to which the theorem applies. An uncountable decreasing net is covered only if it admits such a countable coinitial reduction (or another independently proved representation theorem). The construction does not provide one automatically.
