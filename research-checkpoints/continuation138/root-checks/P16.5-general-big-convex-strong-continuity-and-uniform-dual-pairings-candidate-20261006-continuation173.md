## 1. Domain, normalization and the uniform estimate available from own172

Retain the original smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed, trivially valued characteristic-zero field of arbitrary cardinality. Let L be an actual big rational line bundle, let V denote its intrinsic minimal reference, and write m=vol(L)>0. The energy E is raw, E(V)=0 and E(u+c)=E(u)+mc. For a normalized potential sup u=0 put B(u)=-E(u).

For a possibly unnormalized potential u write c(u)=sup u and u^0=u-c(u). Its normalized cost is B(u^0). Define

\[
 \mathscr M_A=\{M(w):\sup w=0,\ B(w)\le A\},\qquad A\ge0.
\]

Every element of this set is an actual constructed output of raw mass m. The entire moment estimate of own162 reads

\[
 \int (V-v)\,dM(w)\le B(v)+nB(w)
 \tag{1.1}
\]

for normalized v,w. The absolute strong-test estimate proved in own172 gives, for every fixed A and every normalized strongly convergent net v_i to v,

\[
 \sup_{\mu\in\mathscr M_A}\int |v_i-v|\,d\mu\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Here (1.2) uses genuine roofs r_i=R(v_i,v), the two ordered differences v_i-r_i and v-r_i, and the full Holder estimate. Its normalization error is controlled by the generic value of r_i; the original energy/divisorial strong criterion proves this value tends to zero. Thus (1.2) concerns actual functions and positive measures, without invoking an ambient signed finite-energy membership for the nonnef class. All integrals in it are legitimate by (1.1) and the roof decomposition.

For unnormalized strongly convergent v_i to v, the genuine criterion in own156 and the constant law imply c(v_i) to c(v) and v_i^0 to v^0 strongly. In particular

\[
 \sup_{\mu\in\mathscr M_A}\int |v_i-v|\,d\mu
 \le m|c(v_i)-c(v)|+
 \sup_{\mu\in\mathscr M_A}\int |v_i^0-v^0|\,d\mu
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

The statements apply to arbitrary nets, and to independent product indices when different nets occur. No countability restriction on the carrier or field is introduced.

## 2. Joint strong continuity of finite convex combinations

Fix a positive integer r. Suppose u_{a,i_a} converges strongly to u_a for a=1,...,r. Let lambda_{a,j} be nonnegative real numbers summing to one and converging to lambda_a. Use the directed product of j and all i_a. Set

\[
 g_{j,\mathbf i}=\sum_{a=1}^r\lambda_{a,j}u_{a,i_a},\qquad
 g=\sum_{a=1}^r\lambda_a u_a.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Both are genuine L-psh functions. Indeed scalar convexity in the numerical cone is proved by actual section tensors for rational coefficients and by closure in a common actual ample cone for real coefficients, as in own145. Concavity and finiteness of E prove they lie in the entire genuine E1 cone.

First normalize every input. Its generic value equals its supremum. The generic constant of g_{j,mathbf i} is exactly the convex combination of the input generic constants. These constants converge to the corresponding constant of g. Its normalized potential is consequently

\[
 g_{j,\mathbf i}^0=\sum_a\lambda_{a,j}u_{a,i_a}^0.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

By concavity,

\[
 B(g_{j,\mathbf i}^0)\le\sum_a\lambda_{a,j}B(u_{a,i_a}^0).
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Each input cost converges, so these costs and B(g^0) are bounded by one finite A on a product tail. Thus all the outputs M(g_{j,mathbf i}) and M(g) belong to mathscr M_A; the operator is unchanged by constants.

For mu in mathscr M_A, use the exact decomposition of the difference in (2.1) to obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int |g_{j,\mathbf i}-g|\,d\mu
 &\le\sum_a\lambda_{a,j}\int|u_{a,i_a}-u_a|\,d\mu\\
 &\quad+\sum_a|\lambda_{a,j}-\lambda_a|
       \int|u_a-V|\,d\mu.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{2.4}
\]

In the second term the subtraction of V is allowed because the differences of the coefficients sum to zero. Its moments are bounded, uniformly in mu, by (1.1) plus m|c(u_a)|. The first term tends to zero uniformly in mu by (1.3). Hence the left side tends to zero uniformly over mathscr M_A.

The entire supporting planes in own167 give both inequalities

\[
 E(g_{j,\mathbf i})-E(g)\le
       \int(g_{j,\mathbf i}-g)\,dM(g),
\]

\[
 E(g)-E(g_{j,\mathbf i})\le
       \int(g-g_{j,\mathbf i})\,dM(g_{j,\mathbf i}).
\]

Equation (2.4) therefore proves energy convergence. Every original divisorial value converges directly in (2.1), since the inputs are finite at each such valuation. Own156 identifies divisorial plus energy convergence with the actual strong topology. Thus g_{j,mathbf i} converges strongly to g. This includes vanishing limiting coefficients and independent input nets; no strictly positive lower bound on the coefficients was used.

Consequently the map taking a finite simplex of coefficients and a finite tuple of genuine E1 potentials to its convex combination is jointly strongly continuous. The constructed top operators of these combinations converge weakly on the entire original continuous test space by own150. This conclusion is about top operators; it does not define mixed nonnef products by polarization.

## 3. Uniform convergence of intrinsic target functionals on cost sublevels

Let w_i converge strongly to w, and put mu_i=M(w_i), mu=M(w). Fix A. Own168 gives bounded normalized costs for the outputs on a tail. We claim

\[
 \sup_{\substack{v\in\mathcal E^1(L,V)\\\sup v=0,\ B(v)\le A}}
 \left|\int(v-V)\,d(\mu_i-\mu)\right|\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Apply the full four-point inequality of own168 to the test pair v,V and the output pair w_i^0,w^0. With q=2^{-n}, its bound is

\[
 \left|\int(v-V)\,d(\mu_i-\mu)\right|
 \le C_n I(v,V)^q I(w_i^0,w^0)^{1/2}
          \max\{B(v),B(w_i^0),B(w^0)\}^{1/2-q}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

The ordered I/J estimate gives I(v,V) at most (n+1)B(v). The final maximum is bounded by a fixed number; its exponent is nonnegative for every n at least one. Strong convergence implies I(w_i^0,w^0) tends to zero. This proves the uniform assertion (3.1), including A=0 by the exact zero-cost separation statement. Raw constants add m times that constant to each integral and cancel in their difference, so no gauge issue remains.

For F_nu(v)=E(v)-integral(v-V)dnu, (3.1) says F_{mu_i} converges to F_mu uniformly on every normalized energy-cost sublevel. Each target here is intrinsic by own162. This is an estimate on the preconstructed original outputs, and does not replace the intrinsic target condition by the assumption that a target is an output.

We do not claim the converse implication from weak convergence of targets alone. Unbounded target dual costs can obstruct that converse, and (3.1) does not supply a uniform cost bound for arbitrary targets.
