# Abstract and exact theorem

Let $X$ be a smooth integral projective variety of dimension $n\ge1$ over an algebraically closed trivially valued field of characteristic zero and arbitrary cardinality. Let $L$ be any actual big rational line bundle. No nefness, finite generation or finite Zariski decomposition is assumed. The potentials are the literal genuine numerical psh functions on the whole original $X^{\mathrm{an}}$, defined by single decreasing nets of actual section metrics with numerical class tending to $c_1(L)$.

Put $V_L=P_L(0)$ and $v=\operatorname{vol}_X(L)>0$. This paper constructs a target-independent section/volume energy on the entire genuine cone, normalized by $E_L(V_L)=0$, with $E_L(u+c)=E_L(u)+cv$. The intrinsic relative domain is

$$\mathcal E^1(L,V_L)=\{u\in\operatorname{PSH}(L):E_L(u)>-\infty\}.$$

Its energy is characterized by the genuine reference floors $\max(u,V_L-k)$, is concave and upper semicontinuous in the original divisorial topology, and has compact normalized energy-cost sublevels. On that entire domain the construction gives a positive Radon metric operator $M_L(u)$ of raw mass $v$, agreeing with the actual continuous-obstacle section/volume operator. It is independent of decreasing model-majorant nets, including noncofinal nets with the same all-point endpoint. It satisfies both continuous-direction outer derivative signs:

$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}E_L(P_L(u+th))
   =\int h\,dM_L(u),\qquad h\in C(X^{\mathrm{an}}).$$

Every envelope here is an actual original genuine function. The operator is weakly continuous along arbitrary decreasing genuine nets with finite-energy endpoint, and under original divisorial convergence plus energy convergence. All measures and continuous observables remain on the original analytic carrier.

### 1. Exact objects and imported algebraic premises

Let X be smooth integral projective of dimension n over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic zero, trivially valued, and let L be an actual big rational line bundle. Put V=vol_X(L)>0 and d=n+1. No nefness, finite generation, or birational Zariski decomposition is assumed.

A model here is a smooth projective variety Y with a projective birational morphism rho:Y -> X times P^1, equivariant for the multiplicative group and an isomorphism away from the fiber over 0. Let pi be the map to P^1, F=pi^*(0), and D a rational Cartier divisor supported on F. Set B_D=rho^*p_X^*L+D and alpha_D(c)=B_D+cF. All tensor identifications use these actual line bundles. Degrees m below are divisible by a fixed denominator.

The algebraic background used explicitly is: full section asymptotics of a big rational bundle have a limit; volume is birationally invariant and monotone under adding effective divisors; volume is continuous; and on the big cone its differential is d times the positive intersection product. The volume root of degree 1/d is concave. These are imported algebraic premises, not additional grants of a non-Archimedean equation.

Primary evidence for the differential is Boucksom--Favre--Jonsson, arXiv:math/0608260v2, Theorem A and its complete proof in Section 3.2, pp.16--17. ### 2. Exact lattice calculation and eventual affine volume

Write F=sum b_i E_i, with the actual positive central multiplicities. Since D is supported on F, choose rational a,b with aF <= D <= bF coefficientwise. For each divisible m, let R_m=H^0(X,mL), of dimension r_m, and consider the lattice

    M_m=H^0(pi^(-1)(A^1),mB_D)
        inside R_m tensor k[t,t^(-1)].

Properness makes pi_*O_Y(mB_D) coherent. It is torsion free over the nonsingular base: multiplication by a nonzero base function is injective on sections of a line bundle on the integral Y. It is therefore locally free. Away from 0, rho identifies its restriction with R_m tensor O, and the chosen trivialization at infinity is the one coming from the actual product bundle. Birational normality gives rho_*O_Y=O and hence the same sections for the pullback product bundle. The divisor inequalities give actual lattice inclusions

    t^(-ma) R_m[t] <= M_m <= t^(-mb) R_m[t].

The equivariance makes M_m graded for the t degree in this fixed product trivialization. A finite filtration of the vector space R_m is obtained by taking the coefficients of its homogeneous pieces. Choose a basis adapted to this filtration. It gives integers w_m,j such that

    M_m = direct sum_j k[t] t^(-w_m,j) s_m,j,
    ma <= w_m,j <= mb.

Equivalently, glue this lattice to the fixed product lattice near infinity: the resulting vector bundle on P^1 is the direct sum of O(w_m,j). This also proves the splitting needed here without an assertion that the entire section algebra is finitely generated. Projection formula and the definition of direct image yield

    h^0(Y,m alpha_D(c)) = sum_j max(w_m,j+mc+1,0).

For rational c>-a and sufficiently divisible m every summand is positive. Consequently the exact formula is

    h^0(Y,m alpha_D(c)) = (mc+1)r_m + sum_j w_m,j.             (2.1)

It also shows that alpha_D(c) is big: its volume is at least d(c+a)V>0. For any rational h with c+h>-a, subtracting the two exact formulas and using the full-section asymptotic r_m/m^n -> V/n! gives

    vol_Y(alpha_D(c+h))-vol_Y(alpha_D(c)) = d h V.             (2.2)

Both big bundles have genuine asymptotic volume limits under the stated algebraic premise; there is no exchange of two uncontrolled limsups. Continuity extends (2.2) to real c,h in this half-line. In particular,

    vol_Y(alpha_D(c)) = d c V + A_Y(D)

for all sufficiently large c. The intercept is finite and is calculated from actual section lattices, including horizontal base loci of L. The argument never assumes that B_D is relatively generated or that its base locus is confined to the central fiber.

### 3. A well-defined algebraic energy of obstacles

For c large enough for both terms define

    E_L^alg(D) = [vol_Y(alpha_D(c))-vol_Y(alpha_0(c))]/d.

Equation (2.2) proves independence of c. On the product the exact Kunneth section count gives vol(alpha_0(c))=dcV; birational invariance gives the same equality on Y. Hence E_L^alg(D)=A_Y(D)/d and E_L^alg(0)=0.

If f:Y' -> Y is an equivariant birational morphism of such smooth models, replace D by f^*D and F by f^*F. Birational invariance of both volumes proves equality of energies on the two models. Any two permitted models have a common smooth equivariant resolution; thus the value is independent of the chosen representation of a Cartier vertical divisor on all models.

For real s, coefficientwise effective vertical H, and rational C satisfying -CF <= D-D' <= CF, the following hold:

    E_L^alg(D+sF)=E_L^alg(D)+sV,
    E_L^alg(D+H)>=E_L^alg(D),
    |E_L^alg(D)-E_L^alg(D')|<=CV.                             (3.1)

The first uses (2.2); the second is volume monotonicity; the third follows by sandwiching with the first two. Irrational coefficients are obtained by continuity on the fixed finite-dimensional vertical-divisor space. No ordering of residual section metrics is used.

The functional is concave. Fix D_0,D_1 and s in [0,1], set D_s=(1-s)D_0+sD_1, and choose a common sufficiently large c. Volume-root concavity gives

    [dcV+dE(D_s)]^(1/d)
      >= (1-s)[dcV+dE(D_0)]^(1/d)+s[dcV+dE(D_1)]^(1/d).

Divide by (dcV)^(1/d), expand (1+x/c)^(1/d) at zero, multiply by c and let c tend to infinity. Every intercept is fixed and finite. The result is E(D_s)>=(1-s)E(D_0)+sE(D_1). This passes from a root-concave (n+1)-dimensional volume to an ordinary concave vertical energy and is not a claim that ordinary volume itself is concave.

### 4. Actual positive central coefficients and their independence

For c large let

    q_i(D,c)=<alpha_D(c)^n> dot E_i.

This number is nonnegative. Indeed for r>=0, adding rE_i increases volume, and its right differential at zero is d q_i by the imported C1 theorem. Since F=sum b_i E_i, linearity of this differential and (2.2) give

    sum_i b_i q_i(D,c) = <alpha_D(c)^n> dot F = V.            (4.1)

Each individual coefficient is independent of sufficiently large c. For t in a fixed neighborhood of zero, the coefficient bounds aF<=D+tE_i<=bF can be chosen uniformly in t by enlarging a,b. Thus

    vol(alpha_D(c)+tE_i)=dcV+A_Y(D+tE_i)

for all c in one common half-line. Differentiating in t at zero proves that q_i(D,c)=q_i(D), independent of c. This argument is necessary: independence of the total mass alone would not prove independence of each summand.

Let x_i be the semivaluation on X obtained by restricting b_i^(-1)ord_Ei through the Gauss extension; repetitions, if any, are combined as measures. Define the actual Radon measure

    mu_D^alg = sum_i b_i q_i(D) delta_xi.                     (4.2)

It is positive, target independent, and has raw mass vol(L). It is not declared to be the Monge--Ampere measure of an arbitrary genuine psh function.

This atomic measure is invariant under replacing Y by a higher smooth model and D by its pullback. To see this, if G is an effective exceptional divisor of f:Y' -> Y, normality of Y gives f_*O_Y'(kG)=O_Y for all k>=0: a rational function with poles only over a locus of codimension at least two is regular on normal Y. Projection formula implies

    vol_Y'(f^*alpha+rG)=vol_Y(alpha), r>=0.

The C1 derivative at r=0 yields <f^*alpha^n> dot G=0. If E_i' is a strict transform, f^*E_i-E_i' is effective exceptional, so the same derivative and pullback invariance of volume give

    <f^*alpha^n> dot E_i'=<alpha^n> dot E_i.

Strict transforms retain their central multiplicities and restricted semivaluations; all new exceptional components have zero coefficient. Hence (4.2) gives the same measure after refinement. This is a proof of model invariance, not an assumption that every new central atom has positive weight.

For another vertical Cartier divisor H=sum h_i E_i on the same model, the exact two-sided derivative is

    derivative at t=0 of E_L^alg(D+tH)
        = <alpha_D(c)^n> dot H
        = sum_i h_i q_i(D).                                 (4.3)

Writing the usual vertical model function as f_H, with f_H(x_i)=h_i/b_i and constants f_(sF)=s, this is integral f_H d mu_D^alg. This convention is the additive metric of O(H) using its section on the punctured family; the effective-divisor value is positive. It is distinct from the function log|ideal|=-v(ideal).

If the full original-carrier uniform density and representation theorem for rational vertical model functions is supplied, (3.1) uniquely extends E_L^alg to a monotone concave V-Lipschitz functional on C^0(X^an). At every model obstacle D, (4.3) then gives its two-sided derivative in every original continuous direction: approximate f uniformly by a model f_H, bound the difference of difference quotients by V||f-f_H||_infinity, and bound the difference of integrals by the same quantity. First let t tend to zero for this fixed H, then improve the approximation. This last conclusion explicitly retains the density/representation theorem as a further premise; it does not supply differentiability at every continuous obstacle or every relative finite-energy base.

### 2. Genuine envelopes determine every actual section filtration

For an original continuous f and nonzero s in R_m=H^0(X,mL), set

    W_m(f;s)=inf over x in X^an of [mf(x)-log|s|(x)].

At poles of s the bracket is +infinity. Since log|s|<=0 and its generic value is zero, W_m(f;s) is finite and lies in [-m||f||_infinity,m||f||_infinity]. For every real lambda,

    W_m(f;s)>=lambda
      iff q_s+lambda/m <= f
      iff q_s+lambda/m <= P_L(f).                            (2.1)

The last equivalence is exact on the whole carrier. Its forward implication follows because q_s+lambda/m is itself a genuine psh minorant; the reverse follows from P_L(f)<=f. No density assertion about all psh functions being generated by sections of this fixed L is needed.

For a vertical model obstacle f_D, the condition for an integer lambda is equivalently that s t^(-lambda) is a regular section of mB_D on Y over A^1. The only potentially negative divisor coefficients of this rational section are central: s is already regular on the generic product, and horizontal pullback zero divisors are effective. At E_i the condition is

    ord_Ei(s)+m ord_Ei(D)-lambda b_i >= 0,

which is precisely (2.1) evaluated at x_i. Conversely, normality turns nonnegative coefficients at all prime divisors into regularity. A regular section has nonnegative value for every centered semivaluation, so the condition also holds for every point of X^an. Thus this is an equivalence, not merely an inequality checked at the central atoms.

Therefore the homogeneous lattice of own143, including each of its filtered coefficient subspaces, is determined by the whole genuine envelope P_L(f_D). If two model obstacles have the same envelope, all their actual filtered section spaces are identical in the fixed product trivialization, in every divisible degree. Their raw energies are equal.

More generally P_L(f_D)<=P_L(f_H) implies inclusion of the coefficient subspaces at every integer level, hence ordered weight sums, and hence

    E_L^alg(D)<=E_L^alg(H).                                  (2.2)

The weight-sum implication can be seen by choosing a common lower and upper integer bound for the weights and summing dimensions of the decreasing flags between those bounds. It does not require one basis to be simultaneously adapted to two flags. Adding a rational constant c gives

    P_L(f_D)<=P_L(f_H)+c implies E(D)<=E(H)+cV.

### 3. Actual continuous-obstacle section energy

For every real lambda, the set {0} union {s:W_m(f;s)>=lambda} is a k-vector subspace. Indeed the non-Archimedean triangle inequality for actual section norms gives W_m(f;s+s')>=min(W_m(f;s),W_m(f;s')), and multiplication by a nonzero scalar of the trivially valued k leaves W_m unchanged. This finite-dimensional decreasing flag admits an adapted basis. Its r_m weights, with multiplicities, are intrinsic; the weak inequalities in the definition retain their endpoints. Define

    e_m(f) = n! m^(-(n+1)) sum_j floor(W_m(f;s_m,j)).

For a rational model f_D these are exactly the integer lattice weights of own143, so e_m(f_D)->E(D). If ||f-g||_infinity<=epsilon, then every section's weights differ by at most m epsilon, and flag comparison gives

    |e_m(f)-e_m(g)| <= (n! r_m/m^n)(epsilon+1/m).             (3.1)

Uniform model approximation of this one continuous f and the full-section limit n!r_m/m^n -> V prove that e_m(f) has a limit. Explicitly fix a model g within epsilon of f, use convergence of e_m(g), and then use (3.1); the upper and lower limits for e_m(f) differ by at most 2V epsilon. Let epsilon decrease to zero. This uses sequences only for approximation of a single continuous function in the uniform norm, not to replace arbitrary psh nets on a nonmetrizable carrier.

Write E_L^obs(f)=lim e_m(f). It is the unique V-Lipschitz continuous extension of the model energy. It is finite, monotone, concave, has E(0)=0 and E(f+c)=E(f)+cV, by own143 and uniform approximation. Equations (2.1) and flag comparison now prove, for all continuous f,g,

    P_L(f)<=P_L(g) implies E_L^obs(f)<=E_L^obs(g),
    P_L(f)=P_L(g) implies E_L^obs(f)=E_L^obs(g).               (3.2)

Thus the energy of a genuine continuous-obstacle envelope is already well defined. It is computed from actual sections and canonical trivial norms before any target measure is chosen.

### 4. Uniform two-sided model derivatives on bounded obstacle sets

The following estimate supplies the missing continuity across models. Fix a rational vertical model direction h=f_H and C>0. Represent H on one fixed smooth model Z. On any common refinement, a model obstacle f_D with ||f_D||_infinity<=C has -CF<=D<=CF, by the model-function effectivity criterion. Choose a fixed sufficiently large rational c. On the Riemann--Zariski space of Z put

    alpha_- = pullback(L)+(c-C)F,
    alpha_+ = pullback(L)+(c+C)F,
    alpha_D = pullback(L)+D+cF.

The first is big by the exact lattice calculation of own143; and alpha_-<=alpha_D<=alpha_+ in the pseudoeffective order on every common refinement. Since alpha_- is big in the fixed finite-dimensional N1(Z), there exists a>0 with a alpha_- >= +/-H. For epsilon=a|t|<1,

    (1-epsilon)alpha_D <= alpha_D+tH <= (1+epsilon)alpha_D.

Let Q_D=<alpha_D^n>. Monotonicity and homogeneity of positive products yield

    (1-epsilon)^n Q_D <= <(alpha_D+tH)^n>
                           <= (1+epsilon)^n Q_D.            (4.1)

Choose fixed nef Cartier classes T_1,T_2 on Z with H=T_1-T_2, by adding a sufficiently ample class. Pairing a pseudoeffective Weil curve class with a nef Cartier class is nonnegative. Pair (4.1) with each T_i and use Q_D<=Q_+=<alpha_+^n>. This gives a finite constant K, depending only on the fixed data C,H,c and the positive products on Z, such that
