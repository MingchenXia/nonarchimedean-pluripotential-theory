# General big classes: weighted continuity for weak limits of bounded-cost model outputs

This unreviewed candidate uses capped complete-section obstacles to identify the actual relative integrals at weak measure limits. It fills the model-test identification gap without treating a Borel potential as an original continuous test. The limit is any intrinsic finite-dual target, and the approximating measures are original MODEL outputs with bounded potential costs. It subsequently treats all genuine E1 tests. Original P16.5 remains open pending the complete equation and continuity obligations and independent reconstruction.

## 1. Exact setting and proposed statement

Retain the general-big domain, genuine original energy and operator, raw volume V and reference V_theta from own143--165. Let normalized model-envelope w_j have B(w_j)<=B_0, and suppose their ACTUAL measures

    mu_j=M_theta(w_j) ->nu

weakly against all original C0, where nu is an intrinsic finite-dual original target of raw mass V. For every normalized genuine v in E1, the proposed conclusion is

    integral(V_theta-v)dmu_j -> integral(V_theta-v)dnu.  (1.1)

This holds for arbitrary nets as well as sequences. Full polar nullness of nu is own158; every relative integral is finite by its dual condition and own162 for outputs. The model four-point estimate is own164, whose published inputs were applied solely to bounded model potentials in actual auxiliary ample classes.

## 2. Two capped model envelopes approach a fixed model test

First take a fixed normalized model endpoint v=P_theta f, with f<=0 and f(v_triv)=0. Initially take rational f; the fixed-model real case follows by uniform approximation. Set M=-min f, and choose K>M+1. Let phi_m,f,phi_m,0 be the weighted and unweighted ACTUAL complete-section GF functions along factorial degrees. Their relative auxiliary potential satisfies

    -M-1/m<=phi_m,f-phi_m,0<=0

on the full-support carrier, as in own161/164. Define ORIGINAL continuous PL obstacles and their genuine MODEL envelopes

    F_m,K=max(phi_m,f,-K),       G_m,K=max(phi_m,0,-K),
    z_m,K=P_theta F_m,K,       y_m,K=P_theta G_m,K.

The caps remove each GF pole; these are actual original model functions, represented by the finite section-ideal flag with the constant branch. Neither the constant branch alone nor the resulting obstacle is assumed theta-psh. Since -K<=f and phi_m,f<=f,

    max(phi_m,f,V_theta-K)<=z_m,K<=v,
    max(phi_m,0,V_theta-K)<=y_m,K<=V_theta.             (2.1)

The energy transfer of own164 Section7 gives, with K FIXED before m tends to infinity,

    E_theta(z_m,K)->E_theta(v),
    E_theta(y_m,K)->0.

It uses the universal lower section weight -K: every actual normalized L-section metric lies below V_theta, so every continuous majorant of either lower floor in (2.1) gives all original weights at least -K times the degree. The missing-rank term is therefore -K(V-V_m), which vanishes for this fixed K.

The generic value of z_m,K equals a_m=max W_m(f)/m->0, while y_m,K has generic value zero. Normalize z_m,K when using own164's four-point estimate; its removed constant contributes at most V|a_m| in an integral. Ordered model support comparisons give

    I(z_m,K,v)<=(n+1)[E_theta(v)-E_theta(z_m,K)]->0,
    I(y_m,K,V_theta)<=(n+1)[-E_theta(y_m,K)]->0.

Costs of the normalized capped envelopes remain bounded on this m tail. Thus their model relative difference

    d_m,K=z_m,K-y_m,K

approaches v-V_theta in integral UNIFORMLY over all normalized MODEL outputs of cost at most B_0, by the two model four-point estimates with the reference measure V delta_vtriv. The small generic constant is retained. Against the FIXED intrinsic nu, the same convergence follows from own160's strong relative integration: (2.1) and the energy limits give actual rooftop convergence, with normalization constants tending to zero.

## 3. An original continuous test agrees outside one closed polar collar

Put the bounded original continuous test

    h_m,K=F_m,K-G_m,K.

Both h_m,K and d_m,K lie between -M-1 and zero. For the latter this follows from order and the original envelope sup-norm comparison applied to the two continuous capped obstacles. Fix once for all a nonempty complete degree ell, and its unweighted GF phi_ell,0. Its proper base-ideal zero set Z_ell is a genuine psh pole set, so nu(Z_ell)=0 by own158.

For m divisible by ell, phi_m,0>=phi_ell,0. On the complement of the closed collar

    C_K={phi_ell,0<=-K+M+1},

we have phi_m,0>-K+M+1 and phi_m,f>=phi_m,0-M-1/m>-K. Thus BOTH caps are inactive there. Each original GF function is genuine theta-psh and is a minorant of its capped obstacle. Its envelope is bounded above by that obstacle. Therefore at these points

    z_m,K=phi_m,f,
    y_m,K=phi_m,0,
    d_m,K=h_m,K.                                     (3.1)

The assigned source inequalities are on actual original functions; no birational potential is descended by fiat. On C_K the uniform bounds give

    |d_m,K-h_m,K|<=2(M+1)1_CK.                        (3.2)

The collar is closed because phi_ell,0 is continuous with values in [-infinity,0]. Its decreasing intersection as integer K tends to infinity is Z_ell. Hence nu(C_K)->0 by ordinary countable continuity of the finite measure. Weak convergence gives

    limsup_j mu_j(C_K)<=nu(C_K)

for each FIXED closed collar. This uses original compact-space Portmanteau, not an arbitrary monotone theorem for Borel nets.

## 4. The order of the three limits identifies the model test

Given a tolerance, first choose K>M+1 so that nu(C_K) is sufficiently small. This also makes the eventual mu_j collar errors small by Section3. Keep K fixed. Next choose m, divisible by ell and sufficiently large, so that Section2's uniform model-output integral error between d_m,K and v-V_theta is small, and so that their relative integral against the fixed nu is close. Keep this m,K fixed.

The function h_m,K is an ACTUAL original C0 test. Thus its mu_j integrals converge to its nu integral. Comparing it to d_m,K on each side by (3.2), and d_m,K to v-V_theta by Section2, proves

    integral(v-V_theta)dmu_j ->integral(v-V_theta)dnu

for the fixed model v. The choice K first, m second, j last is essential. The pole-valued fixed GF systems may have energy -infinity; only their capped envelope energies and the original continuous differences are used.

## 5. Uniform approximation of every genuine E1 test by model tests

Let v be any normalized genuine E1 endpoint, and v_i=P_theta f_i its cofinal normalized model envelopes decreasing to it. Their energies decrease to E_theta(v). For two ordered models v_i>=v_l, (3.1) of own164 gives

    I(v_i,v_l)<=(n+1)[E_theta(v_i)-E_theta(v_l)].

Apply own164's four-point estimate with test pair v_i,v_l and measure pair a normalized model w,V_theta. The generic test difference is zero, so it bounds the integral difference against M_theta(w) itself. For costs B(w)<=B_0, all constants are uniform on the given v_i tail. Let l tend through its cofinal net while i is FIXED. M_theta(w) is a finite sum of divisorial coordinates, so its actual integrals of v_l converge to those of v. This gives

    sup_(model w,B(w)<=B0)
      |H_vi(M_theta(w))-H_v(M_theta(w))|
        <=C_(n,B0,B(v)) [E_theta(v_i)-E_theta(v)]^q.   (5.1)

The right side tends to zero. Against nu, own160 applies to this genuine decreasing energy-convergent net, and gives H_nu(v_i)->H_nu(v). Section4 gives the mu_j-to-nu limit for each FIXED model v_i. Equation (5.1) and this fixed-target continuity let i tend last, proving (1.1) for every original genuine E1 test.

## 6. Immediate application to the original operator

For any normalized genuine w in E1, take its cofinal normalized model envelopes w_j decreasing to it.
