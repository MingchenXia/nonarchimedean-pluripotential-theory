Let $k$ be perfect of characteristic $p>0$, $A=k[[x_1,\ldots,x_d]]$, and $q=p^e$. Grouping exponents modulo $q$, with coefficient $q$th roots in $k$, proves uniquely
\[
 A^{1/q}=\bigoplus_{0\le\alpha_i<q} A\,x^{\alpha/q}.
\]
A series root is $\sum_\alpha x^{\alpha/q}\sum_\beta a_{q\beta+\alpha}^{1/q}x^\beta$; disjoint exponent classes give uniqueness. These are finite sums of formal series, so no unsupported interchange of infinite sums is involved. Rooting a finite $A$-module generating list for $R$ also makes $R^{1/q}$ finite over $A^{1/q}$ and hence finite over $R$. This proves actual Frobenius finiteness at the displayed hypotheses, not a Jacobian multiplier theorem.

Let $R$ be a complete normal domain finite over $A$, with $L=\operatorname{Frac}R$ finite separable over $K=\operatorname{Frac}A$. Choose a $K$-basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $L$ with $e_i\in R$. Let $G=(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(e_i e_j))$ and $\Delta=\det G$. The entries belong to $A$: they are in $K$ and integral over $A$, which is normal. The separable trace pairing is nonsingular, so $0\ne\Delta\in A$. This follows after extending to a splitting field, where the embedding matrix gives the nonsingular separable trace form; no nonzero value of $\operatorname{Tr}(1)$ is assumed.

Purely inseparable $K^{1/q}/K$ is linearly disjoint from the separable extension $L/K$. Hence $L\otimes_K K^{1/q}\simeq L^{1/q}$ and the same $e_i$ remain a basis over $K^{1/q}$. For $z\in R^{1/q}$, each trace $\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{1/q}/K^{1/q}}(ze_j)$ is integral over $A^{1/q}$ and in $K^{1/q}$, hence belongs to the normal $A^{1/q}$. Solving the linear trace equations with the adjugate matrix gives
\[
 \Delta R^{1/q}\subset\sum_i A^{1/q}e_i\subset A^{1/q}[R].
\]
Crucially $\Delta$ is independent of $q$. The multiplication map $P=A^{1/q}\otimes_A R\to L^{1/q}$ is injective: $A^{1/q}$ is finite free over $A$, $R$ is torsion-free over $A$, and its generic map is the preceding field isomorphism. Its image is $A^{1/q}[R]$. The displayed root decomposition makes $P$ a finite free R-module even if $R$ is not free over $A$ and is not Cohen–Macaulay.

For any $R$-linear map $\varphi:T=R^{1/q}\to R$, the containment gives an $R$-linear $T\to P$, $z\mapsto\Delta z$. Let $B=R\otimes_k T$ and $S=R\otimes_k R$. The module $P\otimes_k T$ is finite free over $B$. Multiplication $B\to T$ is a surjective $B$-linear map, with $T$ given its multiplication action. Thus multiplication $P\otimes_k T\to T$ lifts, by free-module lifting, to a $B$-linear map $\Psi:P\otimes_k T\to B$. Compose
\[
 T\otimes_k T\xrightarrow{\Delta\otimes1}P\otimes_k T
 \xrightarrow\Psi R\otimes_k T
 \xrightarrow{1\otimes\varphi}R\otimes_k R.
\]
Every arrow is $S$-linear and its diagonal restriction is $z\mapsto\varphi(\Delta z)=\Delta\varphi(z)$. Indeed multiplication after $1\otimes\varphi$ equals $\varphi$ after multiplication, by R-linearity. This proves a full compatible diagonal lift of $\Delta\varphi$, for every $e$. In the $F_*^eR$ notation the source precomposition is multiplication by $\Delta^q$, since $F_*r\leftrightarrow r^{1/q}$; it is not multiplication by $\Delta$.

For the completed-diagonal assertion additionally assume that $k$ maps isomorphically to the residue field of $R$, as in the intended coefficient-field setting. A finite set of maximal-ideal generators gives a presentation $R=k[[u]]/I$: successive homogeneous corrections represent every element modulo each maximal-ideal power, and completeness takes their series limit. The construction is continuous for the $(\mathfrak m\otimes1,1\otimes\mathfrak m)$ topology: each map is $S$-linear and sends the Nth adic submodule into the Nth one. The completion of $S$ is the Noetherian ring $k[[u,v]]/(I(u),I(v))$. Grouping exponents in the two variable blocks, then quotienting by the roots of the two defining ideals, identifies its root module with the completed tensor product of the two root modules. The original and root-adic topologies are cofinal, since a qth power multiplies each monomial degree by q. Multiplication and all maps extend uniquely; the commuting diagonal identity persists on the dense algebraic tensor submodules. The kernel of root multiplication is carried into the kernel of ordinary multiplication, so compatibility concerns the full root of the diagonal ideal, not just its ordinary extended ideal. No Noetherianity or general exactness of completion over the uncompleted tensor ring is assumed. Equivalently complete the finite free module and lift its basis directly along $\widehat B\to T$.

This theorem assumes an actual finite generically separable formal normalization. Cohen normalization over a coefficient field alone is not asserted to supply separability. No polynomial HH02 theorem was applied to power series.

The multiplier need not be primary. For $R=k[[t,y_2,\ldots,y_d]]$, $A=k[[t^n,y_2,\ldots,y_d]]$ with $n>1$ prime to $p$, the basis $1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1}$ has discriminant a unit times $(t^n)^{n-1}$. The finite map ramifies on $t=0$. A normalized semivaluation can kill t while another parameter supplies primary normalization, giving $v(\Delta)=\infty$. Therefore a single discriminant cannot supply the required uniform all-support primary bound. This is an obstruction to this inference, not a normal CE counterexample.

For a full isolated formal theorem one still needs a coherent ideal generated by compatible formal multipliers, unit on the full regular puncture of every actual primary model and hence containing a primary power in the isolated setting, and a BIG test-ideal product/restriction/subadditivity theorem on the appropriate completed diagonal with the exact all-map convention. Neither finite freeness nor the discriminant lift proves these statements. A continuous-Jacobian or Noether-different comparison would suffice only after its compatibility and BIG product/restriction statements are justified. Smolkin3.12 concerns reduced equidimensional algebras essentially of finite type over a perfect field; its tensor setting is not silently replaced. Its printed finite-field retraction is invalid and unused here.

CEMS Setting2.1 is broader: Noetherian F-finite normal schemes with dualizing complexes and compatible $F^!\omega\simeq\omega$, including essentially finite-type schemes over an F-finite local base. Complete equicharacteristic perfect-residue rings are F-finite by their power-series presentations and the root decomposition. Thus this source is not rejected merely for lack of field-finite-type hypotheses. Its big trace tails can apply once the actual dualizing data are chosen compatibly. Affine-base Koszul regularity also extends: on finite-dimensional projective Y over a Noetherian affine base, an ample globally generated A and vanishings $H^i(MA^{-i})=0$, $1\le i\le\dim Y$, propagate twists, give multiplication surjectivity and, by high-twist Serre generation, generate M. The generating Koszul complex is locally split, so no base-flatness is required. These facts locate the gap in the compatible primary multiplier and subadditivity mechanism.
