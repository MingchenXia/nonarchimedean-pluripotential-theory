$$E_\theta(u)\le\inf_i E_\theta(u_i)

## 4. Fixed and decreasing original E1 weights: full weighted weak convergence

Let u_i decrease to u in E1(theta), mu_i=M(u_i), mu=M(u), and let v be a fixed intrinsic E1(theta) weight. Subtract a fixed constant so v<=0; write v_k=max(v,-k). The transferred Lemma7.30 applied to v_k-0 and u_i-u gives

$$\int v_k\,d\mu_i\longrightarrow\int v_k\,d\mu$$

for every fixed k. Section2 gives mu_i->mu weakly on the full original continuous test space. Since -v_k is a bounded nonnegative lsc function, the actual primary Lemma7.22, with a constant net of weights, turns those two facts into weak convergence of (-v_k)mu_i to (-v_k)mu. This step handles a bounded discontinuous v_k; weak convergence alone would not.

The same Corollary7.31 argument as in133 supplies uniform weight tails. All normalized J-values of u_i on the tail are bounded by a common B, as are those of v and its floors. Set

$$\delta_k=(E_\theta(v_k)-E_\theta(v))/V\downarrow0.$$

For l>=k, apply the bounded or transferred relative-J inequality to v_k,v_l with reference u_i, and use the ordered I bound. Passing l to infinity by ordinary countable monotone convergence gives

$$\int(v_k-v)\,d\mu_i
\le V\left(\delta_k+C_n[n(n+1)\delta_k]^{2^{-n}}B^{1-2^{-n}}\right)=:Vr_k,$$

uniformly on the tail, with r_k->0. The same inequality holds for mu. For a continuous test f, the error replacing v by v_k is at most ||f|| Vr_k at either end. First choose k and then the original index. Hence v mu_i converges weakly to v mu against every original continuous f.

Now also let v_i decrease to v in intrinsic E1(theta), indexed by the same arbitrary directed set (a common refinement handles independently indexed nets). Their J-values are bounded and their raw energy difference tends to zero. The identical relative-J estimate applied to v_i,v, with reference u_i, gives

$$\int(v_i-v)\,d\mu_i\longrightarrow0.$$

The integrand is nonnegative; thus the total-variation norm of (v_i-v)mu_i tends to zero. Combining this with the fixed-v conclusion proves

$$v_i M(u_i)\longrightarrow vM(u)$$

## 5. Entire mixed products in one intrinsic nef class

For u_1,...,u_n in E1(theta), their every nonempty subset average a_I=|I|^{-1}sum_{i in I}u_i belongs to E1(theta), by genuine convexity and raw energy concavity. Define the mixed limit by polarization of the already constructed top operators:

$$M(u_1,\ldots,u_n)=\frac1{n!}
\sum_{\varnothing\ne I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}
(-1)^{n-|I|}|I|^nM(a_I).$$

This formula identifies an actual product limit; it does not choose a measure in response to a target. Indeed for bounded literal floors u_{r,k}, it is exactly the finite multilinear identity for the actual original bounded products. Each subset average of those floors decreases to a_I with intrinsic E1 endpoint. Section2 therefore shows that the actual positive mixed floor measures converge to the displayed expression. The limit is positive, has raw mass V, and is independent of common cofinal floor levels by the same argument.

We also identify its usual increasing winning-set construction. The positive mixed measure of the n floors is bounded above by n^n/n! times the top measure of their average, by its positive multinomial expansion. The increasing winning-set limit has full mass V and the full mixed floor measures converge to it in total variation, by the same complement estimate as Section1. The same domination and intrinsic integrability show that all original E1(theta) tests are integrable against this mixed operator, with no mass on any such test's poles.

For arbitrary simultaneous decreasing E1(theta) nets u_{r,i}->u_r, all subset averages decrease with E1 endpoints. Top continuity proves mixed weak continuity by the same finite polarization. If v_i decreases to v in E1(theta), Section4 applies to each subset average and gives

## 6. Both outer derivatives at every intrinsic E1 potential

For continuous h, let F_theta(h)=E_theta(P_theta h). Proposal132 reconstructs genuine bounded nef envelope membership and the supporting inequality, by passing actual ample supporting inequalities through bounded operator/energy limits:

$$F_\theta(h+t f)-F_\theta(h)
\le t\int f\,M(P_\theta h),$$

and its counterpart obtained by interchanging the two obstacles. This is valid for every continuous original f, both signs of t and every continuous h. It does not assume that the bounded nef envelope is continuous.

Let u be any intrinsic E1(theta) potential, and take continuous majorants h_i decreasing pointwise to u on compact X^an. Such a directed net exists for every upper-bounded usc function; finite minima make the family directed. The envelopes p_i=P_theta h_i decrease to u: their genuine endpoint lies between u and inf h_i=u. Energy and top continuity from Section2 give E_theta(p_i)->E_theta(u) and M(p_i)->M(u).

For a fixed real t, q_i=P_theta(h_i+t f) decreases to q_t=P_theta(u+t f). Genuine nef decreasing closure and all-point obstacle inequalities prove equality with this envelope. Its lower competitor u-|t|||f|| and its upper bound u+|t|||f|| show genuine intrinsic E1 membership. Apply the bounded supporting inequalities to h_i,h_i+t f and pass the original majorant net using energy and full-C0 operator continuity. For t>0 this gives

$$\int fM(q_t)\le
\frac{E_\theta(q_t)-E_\theta(u)}t
\le\int fM(u).$$

For t<0 the two integral bounds reverse after division. No continuity of the envelopes themselves was used.

Finally M(q_t)->M(u) weakly against every original continuous test as t->0. In fact |q_t-u|<=|t|||f|| wherever u is finite, and both have the same infinite locus. At every fixed k, the literal floors obey the global finite bound

$$\|\max(q_t,-k)-\max(u,-k)\|_\infty\le |t|\|f\|_\infty.$$

Proposal132's bounded uniform operator continuity proves convergence of their bounded floor measures. The suprema and normalized negative raw energies of q_t are bounded uniformly for small t, by the two order bounds. Section1's total-variation floor estimate is therefore uniform in t. First choose k, then let t->0. This proves the asserted full-C0 weak continuity. The two supporting inequalities now squeeze both signs to

$$\left.\frac d{dt}\right|_{t=0}E_\theta(P_\theta(u+t f))
=\int f\,M(u).$$

This is also the actual outer energy derivative: for an upper-bounded usc obstacle admitting a genuine intrinsic E1 minorant, the envelopes of decreasing continuous majorants decrease to its genuine envelope, and Section2 gives energy convergence. Thus the infimum defining the outer energy equals the envelope energy used above. At V=0 both sides vanish.
