L371: which in turn equals $\langle L\rangle^{n}$ by the proof of Proposition 【84†2.4】.
L372: 
L373: To prove the second equality, we again assume $\mathcal{L}-L$ is effective and $\mathcal{L}$ is big. For each $i$ it then follows that for any $k>0$
L374: 
L375:  | $$b_{i}\langle\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)\rangle^{n}\cdot E_{i}\geq b_{i}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle^{n}\cdot E_{i},$$  |
L376: by monotonicity of the positive intersection product. Since these quantities are nonnegative for all $i$ and sum to $\langle L\rangle^{n}$, it follows that they must be equal for all $k$.
L377: 
L378: ∎
L379: 
L380: This allows us to associate a divisorial measure to a test configuration.
L381: ###### Definition 3.4.
L382: 
L383: Given a test configuration $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})$ with $\mathcal{X}_{0}=\sum b_{i}E_{i}$ and $\mathcal{L}$ satisfying the conclusion of Lemma 【85†3.3】, we define its Monge–Ampère measure to be
L384: 
L385:  | $$\mathrm{MA}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}):=\{E_{i},\mathrm{vol}(L)^{-1}b_{i}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle^{n}\cdot E_{i}\},$$  |
L386: 
L387: which is a divisorial measure.
L388: ###### Remark 3.5.
L389: Provided one endows $X^{\mathrm{div}}$ with the structure of a topological space, the Monge–Ampère measure can be viewed as a (discrete) measure on $X^{\mathrm{div}}$, simply by defining it to be $\sum_{i}\mathrm{vol}(L)^{-1}(b_{i}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle^{n}\cdot E_{i})\delta_{E_{i}}$.
L390: Similarly, a divisorial measure can be viewed as a measure on $X^{\mathrm{div}}$, and we use this terminology (due to Boucksom–Jonsson), although we emphasise we do not require any topological aspects of $X^{\mathrm{div}}$.
L391: ### 3.2. The Calabi–Yau theorem
L392: 
L393: We next produce a test configuration with given Monge–Ampère measure.
L394: ###### Theorem 3.6.
L395: 
L396: Let $\mu$ be a divisorial measure, and suppose $\mathcal{X}$ is $\mu$-compatible and dominant. Then there exists $\mathcal{L}$ on $\mathcal{X}$ such that
L397: 
L398:  | $$\mathrm{MA}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=\mu.$$  |
L399: To prove Theorem 【86†3.6】, we adapt to the big case the ideas of Witt Nyström [【59†Nys25】] (who considered the setting of a Kähler class). The idea of the proof is similar to the Brouwer fixed point theorem, whereby we reduce the problem to a problem concerning maps between simplices, and conclude using topological properties of maps between spheres (or rather simplices in our setting).
L400: In order to produce a map to which this idea applies, we first combinatorially construct certain vertical divisors $D_{i}$ in $\mathcal{X}$.
L401: Fix a dominant, $\mu$-compatible degeneration $\mathcal{X}$, through Lemma 【87†3.2】. Define $\Ver(\mathcal{X})$ to be the vector space of $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisors on $\mathcal{X}$ with support in $\mathcal{X}_{0}$, modulo the addition of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)=k\mathcal{X}_{0}$ for all $k\in\mathbb{R}$.
L402: Thus for each $D\in\Ver(\mathcal{X})$, we obtain a test configuration $(\mathcal{X},L+D)$, and test configurations with underlying degeneration $\mathcal{X}$ precisely correspond to elements of $\Ver(\mathcal{X})$, up to the addition of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k).$
