L163: In this paper we further develop the non-Archimedean pluripotential theory for general $(X,\alpha)$, and as an application we are able to prove Theorem 【81†A】 in the general case.
L164: ### 1.3. A non-Archimedean Calabi-Yau Theorem
L165: 
L166: Key to proving Theorem 【81†A】 is our second main result, namely a non-Archimedean Calabi–Yau Theorem for general $(X,\alpha)$. A non-Archimedean Calabi–Yau Theorem for algebraic $(X,\alpha)$ was proved by Boucksom–Favre–Jonsson [【88†BFJ15】], subsequently followed by other versions (see e.g. [【71†BJ22】, 【92†BGM22】]). Before describing these results though it makes sense to briefly discuss the original Calabi–Yau Theorem.
L167: #### 1.3.1. The Calabi–Yau Theorem
L168: 
L169: The celebrated Calabi–Yau Theorem proved by Yau [【93†Yau77】, 【94†Yau78】] says that for any volume form $dV$ on $X$ such that $\int_{X}dV=\int_{X}\omega^{n}=\alpha^{n}$ there is a unique Kähler form $\omega^{\prime}$ in $\alpha$ such that $(\omega^{\prime})^{n}=dV$.
L170: Other versions have since been established by e.g. Kolodziej [【95†Koł98】] and Berman–Boucksom–Guedj–Zeriahi [【82†BBGZ13】]. To formulate this last version, which is of particular relevance to our paper, we first need to remind the reader about some basic definitions in (Archimedean) pluripotential theory.
L171: A smooth function $\varphi$ on $X$ is called a Kähler potential (with respect to $\omega$), written $\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\omega}(X)$, if $\omega+dd^{c}\varphi$ is Kähler. A decreasing limit $\psi$ of Kähler potentials not identically equal to $-\infty$ is said to be $\omega$-psh, written $\psi\in PSH_{\omega}(X)$.
L172: 
L173: The energy of a Kähler potential is defined as
L174: 
L175:  | $$\mathrm{E}_{\omega}(\varphi):=\frac{V^{-1}}{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\int_{X}\varphi(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{j}\wedge\omega^{n-j},$$  |
L176: where $V:=\alpha^{n}$. The energy of $\psi\in PSH_{\omega}(X)$ is defined as the infimum of the energy of all Kähler potentials $\varphi\geq\psi$, and the space of finite energy potentials is defined as
L177: 
L178:  | $$\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\omega}(X):=\{\psi\in PSH_{\omega}(X):\mathrm{E}_{\omega}(\psi)>-\infty\}.$$  |
L179: 
L180: We also let $\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\omega,\sup}(X):=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\omega}:\sup\varphi=0\}$.
L181: 
L182: The Monge-Ampère measure of a Kähler potential $\varphi$ is defined as
L183:  | $$\MA_{\omega}(\varphi):=V^{-1}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}$$  |
L184: 
L185: and there is a natural extension of the Monge-Ampère operator to $\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\omega}(X)$.
L186: 
L187: The (dual) energy $\mathrm{E}^{\vee}_{\omega}(\mu)$ of a Radon probabiltiy measure is defined as
L188: 
L189:  | $$\mathrm{E}^{\vee}_{\omega}(\mu):=\sup\left\{\mathrm{E}_{\omega}(\varphi)-\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu:\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\omega}(X)\right\},$$  |
L190: those with $\mathrm{E}^{\vee}_{\omega}(\mu)<\infty$ giving us the space of finite energy measures $\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}(X)$.
L191: 
L192: The Calabi–Yau Theorem proved in [【82†BBGZ13】] is the following:
L193: ###### Theorem 1.3.1.
L194: 
L195: The Monge-Ampère operator is a bijection between $\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\omega,\sup}(X)$ and
L196: $\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}(X)$.
L197: 
L198: To explain the non-Archimedean analogue we need to recall some basic definitions of non-Archimedean pluripotential theory.
L199: #### 1.3.2. Berkovich/tropical analytification
L200: In the algebraic case one would start with the Berkovich analytification $X^{an}$ of $X$ with respect to the trivial norm on $\mathbb{C}$ (see e.g. [【71†BJ22】]). However, if $X$ is non-projective $X^{an}$ might be trivial, so following [【68†MP24】] we instead consider the tropical analytification $X^{\NA}$ (denoted by $X^{\beth}$ in [【68†MP24】]) of $X$ whose points correspond to semivaluations on the set $\mathscr{I}_{X}$ of coherent ideal sheaves on $X$.
L201: Recall that a nonconstant function $v:\mathscr{I}_{X}\to[0,\infty]$ is called a semivaluation if for any $I,J\in\mathscr{I}_{X}$ we have that $v(IJ)=v(I)+v(J)$ and $v(I+J)=\min(v(I),v(J))$. Equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence $X^{\NA}$ becomes a compact Hausdorff space. When $X$ is projective there is a natural identification between $X^{an}$ and $X^{\NA}$. For details see [【68†MP24】].
L202: #### 1.3.3. Divisorial points
L203: If $E_{i}$ is an irreducible vertical divisor on a model $\mathcal{X}$, then there is an associated semivaluation $v_{E_{i}}(I):=\min\{b_{i}^{-1}\ord_{E_{i}}(f\circ\pi_{X}):f\in I(U),U\subseteq X\}$, where $b_{i}$ is the order of vanishing of $\mathcal{X}_{0}$ along $E_{i}$. Such valuations are called divisorial valuations and the set of divisorial points in $X^{\NA}$ is denoted by $X^{\divisorial}$. Importantly $X^{\divisorial}$ is dense in $X^{\NA}$ (see [【68†MP24】, Theorem B]).
L204: #### 1.3.4. Dual complexes
L205: To each SNC model $\mathcal{X}$ (i.e. a model such that $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0}^{red})$ is a SNC pair) one can associate a dual complex $\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}$ in the following way. Let $I$ be the index set for the irreducible components $E_{i}$ of $\mathcal{X}_{0}$.
L206: To each irreducible component $Z$ of an intersection $\bigcap_{i\in J}E_{i}$, where $J\subseteq I$, we associate the simplex $\Delta_{Z}:=\{w\in(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0})^{|J|}\mid\sum_{i\in J}w_{i}b_{i}\leq 1\},$ where as usual $b_{i}$ denotes the multiplicity of $\mathcal{X}_{0}$ along $E_{i}$. This collection then defines a simplicial complex that we denote by $\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}$.
L207: Morphisms between SNC models induce simplicial maps on the associated dual complexes, and one can show that there is a natural identification between $X^{\NA}$ and the projective limit of $\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}$. Using so-called monomial valuations there is also a natural injection $i_{\mathcal{X}}:\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\hookrightarrow X^{\NA}$, hence we can think of $\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}$ as a subset of $X^{\NA}$. For more details see [【68†MP24】].
L208: #### 1.3.5. Vertical divisors and PL functions
L209: Let $D$ be a (not necessarily irreducible) vertical divisor on a model $\mathcal{X}$. If $x\in X^{\divisorial}$ we let $\mathcal{X}^{\prime}$ be a SNC model that dominates $\mathcal{X}$ and with an irreducible vertical divisor $E_{i}$ such that $x=v_{E_{i}}$. We can then write $\mu^{*}(D)=a_{i}E_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}E_{j}$ where $\mu$ is the map from $\mathcal{X}^{\prime}$ to $\mathcal{X}$ and $(E_{j})_{j\neq i}$ are the other irreducible vertical divisors on $\mathcal{X}^{\prime}$.
L210: Then $f_{D}(x):=b_{i}^{-1}a_{i}$ defines a function $f_{D}$ on $X^{\divisorial}$ which can be seen to have a continuous extension to the whole of $X^{\NA}$, also denoted by $f_{D}$. Functions of this kind are called piecewise linear (PL), the set of PL functions being denoted by $\PL(X^{\NA})$.
L211: #### 1.3.6. Kähler potentials and $A$-psh functions
L212: 
L213: A PL function $f_{D}=\varphi_{D}$ is said to be a Kähler potential (with respect to $A$), written $\varphi_{D}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}(X^{\NA})$, if $A+D$ is relatively Kähler on some model $\mathcal{X}$ (relatively Kähler here means that $A+D+c\mathcal{X}_{0}$ is Kähler for large $c$). A decreasing limit $\varphi$ of Kähler potentials $\varphi_{D_{i}}$ is said to be $A$-psh (or just psh), written $\varphi\in PSH_{A}(X^{\NA})$.
L214: #### 1.3.7. Finite energy potentials
L215: 
L216: The energy $\mathrm{E}_{A}(\varphi_{D})$ of a Kähler potential $\varphi_{D}$ is defined as
L217: 
L218:  | $$\mathrm{E}_{A}(\varphi_{D}):=\frac{V^{-1}}{n+1}(A+D)^{n+1},$$  |
L219: 
L220: the energy $\mathrm{E}_{A}(\varphi)$ of an $A$-psh function $\varphi$ is defined as the infimum of the energy of all Kähler potentials $\varphi_{D}\geq\varphi$, and the space of finite energy potentials is defined as
L221:  | $$\mathcal{E}^{1}_{A}(X^{\NA}):=\{\varphi\in PSH_{A}(X^{\NA}):\mathrm{E}_{A}(\varphi)>-\infty\}.$$  |
L222: 
L223: We equip $\mathcal{E}^{1}_{A}(X^{\NA})$ with the strong topology, defined as the coursest topology, finer than the topology of pointwise convergence on $X^{\divisorial}$, such that $\mathrm{E}_{A}$ becomes continuous. We also let $\mathcal{E}^{1}_{A,\sup}(X^{\NA}):=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{1}_{A}:\sup\varphi=0\}$ and equip it with the subspace topology.
L224: #### 1.3.8. The non-Archimedean Monge-Ampère operator
L225: 
L226: The (non-Archimedean) Monge-Ampère measure of a Kähler potential $\varphi_{D}$ is defined as
L227: 
L228:  | $$\MA_{A}(\varphi_{D}):=V^{-1}\sum_{i}\left((A+D)^{n}\cdot(b_{i}E_{i})\right)\delta_{v_{E_{i}}},$$  |
L229: where $\mathcal{X}_{0}=\sum_{i}b_{i}E_{i}$ is the zero divisor on a SNC model $\mathcal{X}$ where $D$ is defined. This is easily seen to be a probability measure. There is also a natural extension of the non-Archimedean Monge-Ampère operator to $\mathcal{E}^{1}_{A}(X^{\NA})$ (see Section 【34†2.3.1】).
L230: #### 1.3.9. Finite energy measures
L231: 
L232: The (dual) energy $\mathrm{E}^{\vee}_{A}(\mu)$ of a Radon probability measure is defined as
L233: 
L234:  | $$\mathrm{E}^{\vee}_{A}(\mu):=\sup\left\{E_{A}(\varphi)-\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu:\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{1}_{A}(X^{\NA})\right\},$$  |
L235: those with $\mathrm{E}^{\vee}_{A}(\mu)<\infty$ giving us the space of finite energy measures $\mathcal{M}^{1}_{A}(X^{\NA})$. We endow $\mathcal{M}^{1}_{A}(X^{\NA})$ with the strong topology, defined as the coursest topology, finer than the weak topology of measures, that makes $\mathrm{E}^{\vee}_{A}$ continuous.
L236: #### 1.3.10. A non-Archimedean version of Theorem 【96†1.3.1】
L237: 
L238: Our second main result is a non-Archimedean Calabi–Yau Theorem, first proved in the algebraic case by Boucksom-Jonsson [【71†BJ22】, Theorem 12.8].
L239: ###### Theorem B.
L240: 
L241: The non-Archimedean Monge-Ampère operator is a homeomorphism between $\mathcal{E}^{1}_{A,\sup}(X^{\NA})$ and $\mathcal{M}^{1}_{A}(X^{\NA})$.
