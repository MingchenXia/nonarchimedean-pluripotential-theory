# Status and exact scope

**Original P21.3 remains unresolved in this draft.** We prove the proposed bounded-generated-Psh multiplier lemma, including its Borel multiplication and ordinary sheafification steps, without assuming continuous-Psh recognition. We also prove a consequence of primary classical pluriharmonic geometry: on a fixed compact affinoid, every linear subspace of classical pluriharmonic functions is closed under pointwise convergent nets, and such convergence is uniform. The second result diagnoses an approximation route; it gives no new Bedford--Taylor coverage. Neither result proves preservation of an arbitrary signed curvature relation, and no admissible analytic counterexample is constructed. These are unverified research proofs, with no priority claim.

Write $D=\mathrm d'\mathrm d''$. The unchanged target allows every complete non-Archimedean field $K$, including a trivially valued field. The space $X$ is good, separated, strict, boundaryless and pure of analytic dimension $n\geq2$. On **every ordinary open** $W\subset X$, the current $T$ has ordinary CLD bidegree $(p,p)$, $0\leq p\leq n-1$, is middle-positive, and satisfies both $\mathrm d'T=0$ and $\mathrm d''T=0$. For all finite continuous sections $U,V$ of the generated CLD Psh sheaf, the requested implication is

$$D[U]=D[V]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D((U-V)T|_W)=0. \tag{1}$$

Here $[f]=f\delta_X$ uses the canonical integration current. In particular, a generic component is weighted by its actual generic length. We do not replace $X$ by its reduction, pull back $T$, or identify the analytic fibre product with a topological product. Positivity means symmetry and nonnegative evaluation on **middle-positive** complementary smooth tests, including the interior degrees where positivity classes differ.

# Exact external premises

The following is the complete list of non-elementary external premises used. The indices agree with premises.json. Primary references mean [CLD], arXiv:1204.6277v2 (25 July 2025); [GJR], arXiv:2111.05741v3 (7 March 2025); and [GR], arXiv:2506.13548v2 (17 September 2025). The full texts were **not** read. A cited published theorem is an external premise, not an independently verified foundational theorem.

**Premise 0: analytic and ordinary calculus scope.** Analytic spaces have compact-neighbourhood bases; every ordinary open is an analytic domain with its canonical structure [CLD 11.1.7]. A topologically separated pure $n$-dimensional analytic space has the calibrated level-$n$ tropical structure, and analytic-domain calculus agrees with restriction [CLD 15.7.1--2]. A good Hausdorff analytic space is locally holomorphically and differentially separated; compact supports admit smooth cutoffs, compact smooth localization, and uniform approximation of compact continuous scalars by smooth scalars [CLD 15.2.2--9]. This includes either valuation regime. Goodness and separatedness of the target and its ordinary opens give precisely this scope, without a global paracompactness assumption.

**Premise 1: generated Psh.** Psh is the smallest ordinary subsheaf of functions valued in $\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}$ containing smooth functions with positive smooth Hessian and closed under decreasing sequential limits [CLD Definition 8.1.1]. Its sheafified ordinal stages are: smooth generators at rank zero; ordinary-locally decreasing sequential limits of the previous stage at a successor; ordinary-local earlier-stage membership at a limit [CLD Lemma 8.1.4]. The assertion that this construction exhausts Psh, upper semicontinuity, and its cone operations are reconstructed below. The printed stabilization proof's unnecessary locally-integrable qualification is not used to change Definition 8.1.1.

**Premise 2: middle positivity and compact decomposition.** Every compact symmetric smooth $(q,q)$ test is a difference of two compact middle-positive smooth tests, when its support lies in a paracompact locally differentially separated open [CLD 6.5.2]. The theorem explicitly includes compact support on an almost-good analytic space, so good suffices here. Middle-positive currents are symmetric functionals nonnegative on middle-positive tests; positivity is ordinary-local [CLD 7.2.1--4]. A positive smooth factor wedged with a middle-positive current is middle-positive [CLD 7.2.7(b)]. No spanning assertion about strongly positive forms is used.

**Premise 3: scalar coefficient measures.** On a locally differentially separated level-$n$ space, every positive scalar current is represented by a unique positive Radon measure [CLD 7.2.8]. For a middle-positive current $T$ and a smooth middle-positive complementary form $\eta$, its scalar coefficient measure $\mu_\eta$ satisfies $\int g\,d\mu_\eta=\langle T,g\eta\rangle$ for compact smooth scalars $g$, and is compactly supported if $\eta$ is compactly supported [CLD 7.2.11]. The source states locally bounded Borel multiplication in 7.2.12. We reconstruct its middle-positive order-zero justification rather than using the broader strongly-positive inference of 7.2.9.

**Premise 4: topology and the middle cone.** In Euclidean coordinates a symmetric $(q,q)$ superform is middle-positive exactly when its signed coefficient matrix on the exterior power is positive semidefinite [CLD 1.2.6--7]. Positivity is tested on each tangent face. Test-form topology is the inductive topology of fixed compact supports and fixed moment presentations, with the cellwise coefficient seminorms; continuity may be tested after compact smooth localization to one chart [CLD 7.1.1--3]. The image of a compact analytic domain under a moment is a finite polyhedral set [CLD 12.2.4; the theorem cites its primary proofs rather than providing one]. Only this polyhedrality assertion is used, not the printed dimension-equality clause, which fails for a constant moment. This is the finite-face input for the coefficient bound below. None of these facts classifies a singular current.

**Premise 5: restriction and differential identities.** Ordinary forms have compatible restriction, exterior products and Leibniz rules [CLD 3.3.10--12]. Ordinary currents form a sheaf; compact extension by zero defines restriction [CLD 7.1.6--7]. The current differentials obey

$$ (\mathrm d')^2=(\mathrm d'')^2=0,\qquad
\mathrm d''\mathrm d'=-\mathrm d'\mathrm d'',\qquad DJ=JD,\qquad
\langle DS,\alpha\rangle=\langle S,D\alpha\rangle $$

[CLD 7.7.1--5]. For smooth $s$ and a symmetric doubly closed $T$, $D(sT)=Ds\wedge T$ [CLD 8.5.3--4]. These identities concern the actual ordinary product on $W$.

**Premise 6: canonical integration.** The boundaryless canonical integration current $\delta_X=[1]$ is positive and doubly closed [CLD 15.7.1, 7.7.4]. For a pure closed analytic subspace $Y$, $\delta_Y=\delta_{[Y]}$, with generic component multiplicities [CLD 16.6.3]. Thus applying our arguments to $\delta_X$ includes singularities and nilpotents with their original calibration.

**Premise 7: classical pluriharmonic geometry.** For any complete non-Archimedean field, including trivial valuation, [GR Definition 7.1] defines classical pluriharmonicity by requiring both signs to be classically psh. These functions are continuous and form a real vector space [GR 7.2]. On any affinoid $A$ with finite Shilov boundary $\Gamma$, a classical pluriharmonic function attains its maximum and minimum on $\Gamma$, and evaluation $\operatorname{PH}(A)\to\mathbb R^\Gamma$ is injective [GR 7.3, using the global maximum principle 6.4]. There is no good/strict/smooth/nontrivial-valued hypothesis in 7.3. We use this published analytic theorem only for functions already in $\operatorname{PH}(A)$, never to recognize a zero ordinary Hessian.

**Premise 8: diagnostics, not recognition assumptions.** [GR 1.2] gives the inclusion of generated CLD Psh in classical psh, by functoriality, decreasing limits and the smooth-curve comparison. [GR 7.7--8] requires a nontrivially valued field and an **actual** strictly semistable formal model of a good quasicompact strict space to deduce real-PL regularity of already classically pluriharmonic functions. [GJR 10.17] instead starts with weak smoothness and a zero weak **form** Hessian, in its nontrivially valued good strict scope, and concludes harmonic real-PL regularity. [GJR 12.1--2] provides restriction of strong currents to ordinary tests, not a positive closed lift of every current and not product compatibility. These are used solely to audit the attempted routes; none supplies an assumption in Theorem 1 below.

We use bounded convergence for finite positive measures in its usual exact form: Borel $f_j\to f$ pointwise, with a common finite absolute bound on a finite-measure support, implies convergence of their integrals. A short proof is included in the successor argument, so it is not an additional external premise.

# Ordinary Borel multiplication for middle-positive currents

**Lemma 1.** Let $T$ be middle-positive on an allowed ordinary open. For each compact symmetric complementary test $\eta$ there is a canonical finite signed Radon measure $\mu_\eta$, linear in $\eta$, supported in $\operatorname{supp}\eta$, with

$$ \int g\,d\mu_\eta=\langle T,g\eta\rangle $$

for smooth $g$. Every locally bounded real Borel $f$ defines a symmetric ordinary order-zero current $fT$ by integration against these measures. It agrees with smooth multiplication and commutes with ordinary restriction. If $f_j$ have one finite common bound on every compact and converge pointwise to $f$, then $f_jT\to fT$ weakly.

**Proof.** Decompose $\eta=\eta_1-\eta_2$ using Premise 2. The coefficient measures of Premise 3 give $\mu_\eta=\mu_{\eta_1}-\mu_{\eta_2}$. Two decompositions give the same integrals against compact smooth scalars, hence against compact continuous scalars by smooth density, hence the same Radon measure. This also proves linearity. The functional vanishes on scalars supported away from $\operatorname{supp}\eta$. Density within that complementary open shows that $\mu_\eta$ is zero there. Thus the apparent larger supports of a positive decomposition do not enlarge the signed measure's intrinsic support. For an arbitrary test use its symmetric part: the antisymmetric part pairs to zero with $T$. This produces a linear symmetric functional

$$\langle fT,\eta\rangle=\int f\,d\mu_\eta. \tag{2}$$

We check that this functional is a current, including when $f$ is discontinuous. Work on a fixed chart $g:O\to E$, after multiplication by a compact nonnegative smooth scalar $\rho$. Choose one compact analytic domain $C\subset O$ containing $\operatorname{supp}\rho$ in its interior. The compact moment image $P=g_{\rm trop}(C)$ has finitely many tangent faces. On each face of dimension at least $q$, let $\Theta=\sum_i\mathrm d'x_i\wedge\mathrm d''x_i$ for fixed Euclidean coordinates on $E$. The coefficient matrix of $\Theta^q$ on $\bigwedge^q$ of that tangent space is positive definite. Consequently there is a constant $c$, for this finite presentation, such that for every symmetric presented test $a$,

$$ c\|a\|_{P,0}\Theta^q+a\geq0,\qquad
   c\|a\|_{P,0}\Theta^q-a\geq0. \tag{3}$$

Indeed on each of the finitely many tangent spaces the operator norm of the signed coefficient matrix of $a$ is bounded by a fixed multiple of its coefficient norm, whereas the fixed matrix of $\Theta^q$ has a positive minimum eigenvalue. Take the maximum of these finitely many constants. On faces of dimension below $q$ both restrictions vanish. For $q=0$, use $\Theta^0=1$ and scalar inequalities. This proof concerns the middle cone only.

Pull back (3) and multiply by $\rho$. The resulting compact forms are middle-positive. Their positive scalar coefficient measures imply the measure inequalities

$$-c\|a\|_{P,0}\mu_{\rho g^*\Theta^q}
  \leq\mu_{\rho g^*a}
  \leq c\|a\|_{P,0}\mu_{\rho g^*\Theta^q}.$$

The usual positive/negative decomposition of a signed measure gives a total variation bound by the measure on the right. Equivalently, integrate scalar simple functions of either sign and then bounded Borel limits. Thus

$$ |\langle fT,\rho g^*a\rangle|
 \leq c\|f\|_{\operatorname{supp}\rho}\|a\|_{P,0}
       \langle T,\rho g^*\Theta^q\rangle. \tag{4}$$

The factor involving $f$ is finite by local boundedness and compactness. The right hand side is a fixed $C^0$ seminorm bound on this test family. The symmetric projection is continuous, so the same conclusion holds for arbitrary tests. Finite smooth localization and Premise 4 prove ordinary order-zero continuity. This replaces the inappropriate use of strongly positive spanning in the broader source proof of 7.2.9.

For an ordinary inclusion $N\subset W$, extend a compact test on $N$ by zero. Equality of its scalar coefficient measures, checked on smooth scalars and then by density, proves $(fT)|_N=f|_N(T|_N)$. Smooth $f$ agrees with the original product by construction. Finally, for any fixed compact test, its two positive coefficient measures are finite. The stated common compact bounds allow bounded convergence separately for each measure in its positive decomposition. This proves weak convergence. $\square$

# Full sheafified ordinal reconstruction

We first reconstruct the stages and measurability. Let $\mathcal P_0$ be the ordinary sheaf of smooth positive-Hessian functions. Define $\mathcal P_{\xi+1}$ by ordinary-local decreasing limits of sequences from $\mathcal P_\xi$. At a limit $\lambda$, define membership by ordinary-local membership in some $\mathcal P_\xi$, $\xi<\lambda$. These are sheaves: the definitions are local conditions on sections of the ambient function sheaf. They are increasing because constant sequences retain a preceding section, and earlier stages embed in a limit.

Every stage consists of upper-semicontinuous functions with values in $\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}$. At a successor, for $f=\inf_j f_j$,

$$\{f<a\}=\bigcup_j\{f_j<a\}$$

is open. At a limit upper semicontinuity is ordinary-local. Hence every finite section is Borel. No second countability of the analytic space is needed: the preimages of the countable rational intervals generate the real Borel sigma-algebra.

The stages exhaust the minimal Psh sheaf. The collection of all pairs (ordinary open, section of the ambient function sheaf on that open) is a set. An increasing chain cannot introduce a distinct pair at every successor beyond its cardinal successor. At a stationary stage $\mathcal P_\kappa=\mathcal P_{\kappa+1}$, the construction is fixed at all subsequent successors and limits. This stage is closed under decreasing sequences on a fixed open: their limit belongs at rank $\kappa+1=\kappa$, without any integrability restriction. It contains the generators and is a sheaf, so minimality puts Psh inside it. Conversely every stage lies in Psh, by its two defining closure conditions and ordinary locality. This proves equality. Restricting these local constructions to an ordinary open gives the same local stages there; all induction statements below are made simultaneously on every ordinary open of $X$.

**Theorem 1 (bounded-generated multiplier positivity).** Under every original ambient hypothesis, let $f$ be a locally bounded real section of generated Psh on any ordinary $W$. Its Borel ordinary product is defined, and

$$D(fT|_W)\quad\text{is middle-positive and doubly closed}. \tag{5}$$

In particular this holds for every finite continuous generated-Psh potential, without recognizing it as CPsh.

**Proof.** Transfinitely, simultaneously for every ordinary open and every middle-positive doubly closed current in the specified degree, prove the assertion for every locally bounded real section of $\mathcal P_\xi$.

At rank zero, Premise 5 gives $D(fT)=Df\wedge T$. The smooth Hessian is positive; Premise 2 gives middle positivity.

At a successor fix a locally bounded real final section $f$. Around any point take an ordinary neighbourhood $N$ on which $f_j\in\mathcal P_\xi(N)$ decrease to $f$. Restricting $T$ to $N$ retains positivity and both closedness equations. At each point, $f_j\geq f$ implies that every $f_j$ is finite: the stages allow no $+\infty$, and $-\infty$ would contradict the lower bound. The final section bounds each $f_j$ locally below. Every finite upper-semicontinuous $f_j$ bounds itself above near each point, using the open set $\{f_j<f_j(x)+1\}$. Thus each $f_j$ is locally bounded on $N$. The inductive theorem applies to each, although it can be discontinuous and can itself have only sheaf-local preceding representations.

Fix a compact smooth symmetric test $\alpha$ of bidegree $(n-p-1,n-p-1)$ on $N$. The compact symmetric complementary form $D\alpha$ has a decomposition $\eta_1-\eta_2$ into compact middle-positive forms. Let $\mu_i$ be the associated finite positive coefficient measures of the **fixed original** $T|_N$. For the compact union $C$ of their supports, local boundedness of $f$ and upper semicontinuity of the finite $f_1$ give finite constants $a,b$ such that

$$ a\leq f\leq f_j\leq f_1\leq b\quad\hbox{on }C,\qquad j\geq1. \tag{6}$$

Upper boundedness of $f_1$ on $C$ follows by its local upper bounds and a finite subcover; boundedness of $f$ follows similarly. The support and bounds are fixed before $j$.

For completeness, convergence of the two integrals follows without a current regularization. Put $g_j=f_j-f$ on $C$; then $0\leq g_j\leq b-a$ and $g_j\downarrow0$. For $\epsilon>0$, the Borel sets $E_j=\{g_j\geq\epsilon\}$ decrease to the empty set. Finiteness of $\mu_i$ gives $\mu_i(E_j)\to0$: write the disjoint successive differences and use countable additivity. Therefore

$$0\leq\int g_j\,d\mu_i
  \leq\epsilon\mu_i(C)+(b-a)\mu_i(E_j).$$

First let $j$ tend to infinity and then $\epsilon$ tend to zero. Formula (2) and differential duality now give

$$ \langle D(f_jT),\alpha\rangle
     =\int f_j\,d\mu_1-\int f_j\,d\mu_2
     \longrightarrow
       \int f\,d\mu_1-\int f\,d\mu_2
     =\langle D(fT),\alpha\rangle. \tag{7}$$

For a middle-positive $\alpha$, every left hand side is nonnegative by induction. Its limit is therefore nonnegative. The current $fT$ is symmetric and $DJ=JD$, so its Hessian is symmetric. This proves middle positivity on $N$. Lemma 1's restriction compatibility and ordinary locality of positive currents prove it on $W$.

At a limit $\lambda$, around each point $f$ belongs to some earlier $\mathcal P_\xi$ with $\xi<\lambda$. Local boundedness restricts there. The inductive assertion gives positivity there, and locality gives it on $W$. There is no choice of one earlier rank for all of $W$, and no intersection of infinitely many neighbourhoods.

Stabilization gives the assertion for Psh. For any current $S$, $\mathrm d'DS=0$ by $(\mathrm d')^2=0$, and $\mathrm d''DS=-\mathrm d'(\mathrm d'')^2S=0$. Hence (5) is doubly closed. All degree endpoints are included: for $p=n-1$ the $\alpha$ tests are scalars; for $p=0$ their degree is $(n-1,n-1)$. The proof has not modified the calibration, assumed reducedness, or used a nontrivial valuation. $\square$

**Corollary 1.** Given locally bounded real generated-Psh sections $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ and $m\leq n-p$, the ordered recursion

$$T_0=T,\qquad T_k=D(f_kT_{k-1})\quad(1\leq k\leq m)$$

defines middle-positive doubly closed ordinary currents in degree $(p+k,p+k)$.

**Proof.** Apply Theorem 1 at each step while $p+k-1\leq n-1$. Products are the Borel order-zero products of Lemma 1 for the newly positive current. $\square$

This is an ordered construction. We prove neither independence of the order nor independence from curvature-equivalent potentials, so it must not be advertised as a canonical mixed Hessian product.

**Corollary 2.** If $h$ is locally bounded and both $h$ and $-h$ are generated Psh, then $D(hT)=0$ for every target $T$.

**Proof.** Theorem 1 makes $R=D(hT)$ and $-R=D((-h)T)$ middle-positive. Every middle-positive test pairs to zero with $R$. Symmetric-test decomposition handles arbitrary symmetric tests, and symmetry handles antisymmetric tests. Thus $R=0$. $\square$

For the actual $h=U-V$, only $U,V$ separately belong to Psh. Equation $D[h]=0$ has not supplied the two memberships in Corollary 2. In particular (5) gives two positive currents $D(UT)$ and $D(VT)$, not their equality.

# A consequence of actual affinoid pluriharmonic geometry

**Theorem 2 (closed subspaces under nets).** Let $A$ be any compact $K$-affinoid space over any complete non-Archimedean field. Let $L$ be any real linear subspace of the space $\operatorname{PH}(A)$ of classical pluriharmonic functions. If a net $(h_a)$ in $L$ converges pointwise on $A$ to a finite real function $h$, then $h\in L$ and the convergence is uniform on $A$. The limit need not be assumed continuous.

**Proof.** Let $\Gamma$ be the finite Shilov boundary. Premise 7 gives the linear injective evaluation map $E:\operatorname{PH}(A)\to\mathbb R^\Gamma$. For $v\in\operatorname{PH}(A)$, the maximum and minimum statements give

$$\|v\|_{C(A)}=\max_{x\in\Gamma}|v(x)|. \tag{8}$$

The image $E(L)$ is a linear subspace of the finite-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb R^\Gamma$, hence closed. To see this elementary fact without a topological separation theorem, take a basis of $E(L)$, extend it to a basis of $\mathbb R^\Gamma$, and observe that $E(L)$ is the common zero set of the remaining coordinate functionals. Pointwise convergence on the finite $\Gamma$ is convergence in $\mathbb R^\Gamma$, also for a directed net: for a fixed tolerance, take a common upper index for the finitely many evaluation thresholds. Its limit $(h(x))_{x\in\Gamma}$ therefore equals $E(v)$ for some $v\in L$. Equation (8) applied to $h_a-v$ gives uniform convergence to $v$. Pointwise uniqueness gives $h=v$. This proves the theorem. $\square$

On a compact space covered by finitely many affinoid domains, the same argument, using the union of their Shilov boundaries, proves the same assertion for any linear subspace of global classical pluriharmonic functions. Each function restricts classically pluriharmonically to these domains, so the supremum norm is determined by this finite union. This also reconstructs the net version of the sequence mechanism in [GR 7.4, 7.6].

A precise consequence for approximation is as follows. On such a fixed $A$, let

$$L_A=\{v\in\operatorname{PH}(A):v\text{ is ordinary CLD-smooth and }Dv=0\text{ as a smooth form}\}.$$

This is a linear subspace of $\operatorname{PH}(A)$. A pointwise or uniform net limit of its elements is **already** in $L_A$ by Theorem 2. In the usual good strict CLD scope, smooth functions with zero smooth Hessian have both signs among the smooth Psh generators, and the source inclusion [GR 1.2] places them in $\operatorname{PH}(A)$. Thus passing from sequences to nets of such harmonic functions on a fixed compact affinoid cannot create nonsmooth limits. Ordinary smoothness near a point and on a compact affinoid are distinct scopes; this conclusion requires the approximants on that same affinoid, and makes no statement about index-dependent shrinking domains.

This is a **diagnostic analytic consequence**, not a counterexample to (1). The actual pair difference has not been shown classically pluriharmonic. Even an already classically pluriharmonic nonsmooth function need not violate (1): the task-granted strict real-PL theorem handles many such differences over nontrivially valued fields. No residual Picard example is claimed as a new negative result.

# Attempts at the missing equality

The induction just proved cannot be recycled for a signed pair relation by replacing $f$ with $U-V$. The smooth base has exact relation preservation, but at a successor $U_j\downarrow U$ and $V_j\downarrow V$ are separate representations. Nothing makes $D[U_j]=D[V_j]$. Although (7) permits passing the products to their respective limits, it supplies no zero product difference to pass. At a limit the corresponding earlier ranks may also differ; taking their maximum puts the two sections in a common stage but does not create a curvature relation at the predecessor stage. Sheafification does not solve this defect.

One might try to perturb $U$ by subtracting $V$, thereby creating the two signs of $h$. The generated definition gives a cone, not a vector space. For completeness its finite nonnegative combinations do persist at every ordinal: at rank zero this follows by linearity of the smooth Hessian; at a successor intersect the two representation neighbourhoods and add the two decreasing sequences; at a limit move the two sections to the maximum of their two earlier ranks on the intersected neighbourhood. Upper semicontinuity and constants persist as above. Coefficient zero is handled by omitting that summand rather than by multiplying $-\infty$ by zero. This validates positive combinations but provides no subtraction operation. Equality of **canonical** current Hessians cannot be silently substituted for a generating construction of $h$ or $-h$.

The GR route has two distinct missing implications. The input $U,V$ is classically psh by the source inclusion, but their difference is not covered by that cone inclusion. To apply the Shilov and semistable statements to $h$, one needs both signs to pass **every** classical curve test, including after every complete analytic extension. The upstairs equation $D[U]=D[V]$ is an equation of ordinary ambient currents. It does not define a pullback of that current equation to a lower-dimensional curve, or ascent of it to a transcendental extension. No such operation has been constructed here. The finite-dimensional argument of Theorem 2 starts only after the classical membership is known.

Even that membership alone does not remove all scope restrictions. [GR 7.8] further assumes a good quasicompact strict domain with an actual strictly semistable formal model over a nontrivially valued field. Its simplex interpolation proof applies to those models; it neither asserts their existence on arbitrary analytic germs nor makes arbitrary singularities smooth. The task-granted strict real-PL multiplier theorem could be applied **after** these hypotheses and recognition were verified. We have not obtained them for arbitrary $W$ and do not claim a new special case through this chain.

For current regularization, smoothing on one coarse tropical chart would have to preserve positive middle cones and both closedness equations after every additional analytic-unit moment refinement. A chartwise convolution does not come with such compatibility, a globally defined current, or local weak convergence on all ordinary analytic tests. We have constructed none of these. Similarly, a strong-current restriction map gives no lift with the required properties; even a lift would need a proof that the restricted strong product agrees with the Borel ordinary product (2). Neither theorem in this draft assumes that bridge.

There is a further obstruction to using the positivity theorem to prove recursive symmetry without another analytic argument. In an induction, bounded predecessors can be discontinuous. Weak convergence of their positive differentiated currents only controls integrals against continuous scalars; it does not justify multiplying moving currents by an arbitrary discontinuous predecessor. Theorem 1 avoids this issue because its coefficient measures belong to one **fixed** $T$. It proves the ordered recursion, not a general interchange of its factors. A capacity or compatible regularization proof would be needed before making that additional assertion.

No actual analytic negative construction was completed. In particular the known weighted cross has unequal upstairs curvatures and cannot certify the target premise, while nonsmooth real-PL harmonic functions in the granted scope already obey the ordinary multiplier equality. Theorem 2 shows why adding more indices to ordinary harmonic approximants on a fixed affinoid does not evade their smoothness restriction; it does not manufacture admissible potentials or a defective current.

# Concrete unresolved analytic statement and comparison

The genuinely established step is Theorem 1's full bounded-generated-Psh positivity, with every sheafified ordinal and all original ambient fields and current degrees. It removes the need to assume reverse CPsh recognition for **separate-product positivity**. Theorem 2 is a further rigorous diagnostic of the fixed-domain harmonic approximation route, based on an actual published analytic theorem. Neither is a new all-current equality result in a previously residual range.

One concrete sufficient analytic statement still lacking a proof is:

> For every original $K,X$, every ordinary $W$, and every finite continuous generated-Psh pair $U,V$ with $D[U]=D[V]$, each point has a fixed ordinary neighbourhood $N\subset W$ on which **both** $U-V$ and $V-U$ belong to the original generated Psh sheaf, by a valid ordinary-local sheafified generating construction.

Theorem 1 and pointedness would prove (1) from this statement, with no current approximation. This is a sufficient route, not a claimed equivalence or a new proved recognition theorem. A different proof preserving all signed curvature relations could settle P21.3 without this stronger two-sign membership statement. The missing current-regularization alternative would require local weak approximation of every allowed $T$ by actual middle-positive doubly closed ordinary currents for which relation preservation has already been proved, with compatibility on all analytic refinements. No such density theorem is supplied or proved.

The catalogue's normal-curve, componentwise, fixed-polycylinder, ordinary-basis, specified-tower, invariant split-toric, strict real-PL and explicitly weak-approximable cases are not counted as resolutions. In particular a cover of one specified open does not produce an ordinary-open basis for all opens. This draft does not identify arbitrary ambient zero Hessians with curvewise harmonicity or with weak form Hessians, and retains the trivial-valuation, singular and nonreduced parts of the original residual gap. Its status is partial and `candidate=false`.
