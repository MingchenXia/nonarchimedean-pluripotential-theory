  Recall that the elements of A0,0 are called weakly smooth functions. It is easy to see
that they are continuous real functions.
Proposition 10.17. Let f : X → R be a function. The following are equivalent:
   (1) f is weakly smooth and d′ d′′ f = 0.
   (2) f is harmonic (and hence R-PL).
Proof. Assume first that f is harmonic (hence R-PL). By Proposition 7.5, we may shrink
X to assume that f is an R-linear combination of Z-harmonic functions. By linearity, we
may assume that f is itself Z-harmonic. Then we can use f as a harmonic tropicalization.
Using f = IdR ◦ f , we deduce that f ∈ A0,0 (X ) and d′ d′′ f = f ∗ (d′ d′′ IdR ) = 0.
   Conversely, let f be a weakly smooth function with d′ d′′ f = 0. We have to show that
f is a harmonic function. This can be checked locally at x ∈ X , so we may assume that X
is a strictly affinoid domain and that f is the composition of a harmonic tropicalization
h = (h1 , . . . , hn ): X → Rn with a smooth function g : Rn → R. We use the Taylor expansion
in ν := h(x) to write

for ω = (ω1 , . . . , ωn ) in a neighbourhood of ν. Using injectivity of h∗ from Proposi-
tion 10.9, we deduce from d′ d′′ h = 0 that d′ d′′ (g|h(X ) ) = 0, and hence that g is affine on
every face of the tropical variety h(X ). Thus the restrictions of g and of g(ν)+a1 ω1 +· · ·+
