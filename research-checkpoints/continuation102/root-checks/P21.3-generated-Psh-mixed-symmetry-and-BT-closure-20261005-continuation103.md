## 1. A continuous multiplier and weak positive currents

If S_j are positive ordinary currents converging weakly to a positive S,
and v is continuous, then v S_j converges weakly to v S.

Indeed discard the antisymmetric part of a compact smooth test, since
positive currents are symmetric. Write its symmetric part alpha as a difference of
two compact middle-positive tests eta_1-eta_2. Weak convergence bounds
the positive masses <S_j,eta_1+eta_2>. On a neighbourhood of their compact
supports, approximate v uniformly by an ordinary smooth function g.
Ordinary smooth cutoffs permit compact support for this approximation.
Then the error in pairing (v-g)S_j with alpha is at most

    ||v-g||_C <S_j,eta_1+eta_2>.

The same estimate holds for S. The middle term g(S_j-S) tends weakly to
zero since g alpha is a compact smooth test. Send j to infinity first
and the uniform approximation error to zero last. This proves the claim.
It is exactly the fixed-multiplier case of CLD Lemma8.5.1; v need not be
psh or smooth. Positive masses, ordinary compact supports, and ordinary
smooth density are all used. No strong-current lift is assumed.

## 2. Symmetry with one continuous factor

Let 0<=p<=n-2 and let T have bidegree (p,p). If v is continuous generated
Psh and u is any locally bounded real generated-Psh function, then

    D(u D(vT)) = D(v D(uT)).                         (S)

Both inner currents are positive and doubly closed by the independently
reconstructed continuation102 induction. Thus the locally bounded Borel
products in (S) are actual ordinary currents, including discontinuous u.

Fix v and T and induct on the ordinal stage of u, simultaneously on all
ordinary opens. At rank zero u is smooth psh. The smooth multiplier
identity and closedness give

    D(u D(vT)) = Du wedge D(vT)
              = D(v (Du wedge T))
              = D(v D(uT)).

The middle equality follows by commuting D with the smooth doubly closed
form Du in differential duality; no differentiation of v as a classical
function is required.

At a successor, locally take the original decreasing sequence u_j->u
from the preceding Psh stage. Local boundedness of the FINAL u supplies
a finite common lower bound on each compact. Upper semicontinuity of
u_1 supplies a finite upper bound. Consequently every u_j is locally
bounded, and the induction hypothesis legitimately gives

    D(u_j D(vT)) = D(v D(u_jT)).

For the left side, fix the positive current D(vT) and use bounded
dominated convergence separately for its positive coefficient measures
against a compact middle-positive decomposition of D alpha. This gives

    D(u_j D(vT)) -> D(u D(vT))

weakly, even when u is discontinuous. For the right side the same fixed-T
argument gives D(u_jT)->D(uT), all of these currents being positive by
continuation102. Section1, using CONTINUITY OF v, now gives

    v D(u_jT) -> v D(uT).

Differential duality passes D to this limit. Hence (S) holds at the
successor. No continuity, uniform approximation or CPsh recognition of
the predecessors u_j has been used.

At a limit ordinal each germ is already in one earlier stage.
Restrict to that ordinary neighbourhood and use its earlier-rank
assertion. Ordinary current locality concludes the limit step.
Stabilization of the generated sheaf proves (S) as stated.

In particular, two continuous generated-Psh factors commute for every
original T. By linearity this also gives

    D(h D(vT)) = D(v D(hT))                           (S±)

whenever h=U-V with U,V continuous generated Psh. Neither h nor -h
needs to belong to Psh for this signed identity.

## 3. Closure of the class satisfying the original pair relation

Call a positive doubly closed current S pair-compatible when, on every
ordinary open, U,V continuous generated Psh with DU=DV imply
D(US)=D(VS). This is the exact original pair condition, not a claim that
every positive current has it.

The original fundamental current delta_X is pair-compatible by the
definition of DU. Pair-compatible currents are closed under addition,
nonnegative scalar multiplication and ordinary locality. They are also
closed under weak limits of positive currents: multiply by the fixed
continuous U and V using Section1, then pass D to the weak limit.

Wedge multiplication by an ordinary smooth positive doubly closed form
alpha preserves this class, by the smooth closed-form identity

    D(h (S wedge alpha)) = D(hS) wedge alpha.

Most significantly, if S is pair-compatible and v is CONTINUOUS
generated Psh, then D(vS) is pair-compatible. Positivity and closedness
follow from continuation102. For an arbitrary original admissible
h=U-V with Dh=0, the pair condition gives D(hS)=0, while (S±) gives

    D(h D(vS)) = D(v D(hS)) = 0.

The permitted degree condition is imposed at each multiplication; a
current of top degree needs no further nonzero D product. Thus every
current constructed from delta_X by these operations is pair-compatible,
without importing the printed reverse CPsh-recognition assertion.
