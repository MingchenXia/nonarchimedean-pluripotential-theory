Result and unchanged question
Original P21.3 is not solved here. This paper reconstructs the bounded-generated-potential
argument and the proposed mixed symmetry from primary ordinary CLD calculus. It proves a
further consequence: differentiation by a locally bounded, possibly discontinuous generatedPsh potential preserves the continuous-pair Bedford–Taylor property. This consequence does not
require continuity of that differentiating potential. Continuity of the original test pair supplies
the continuous factor in the symmetry theorem. We also prove a limited obstruction to a fixed
convolution regularizer on actual split-torus coordinates. We obtain neither an exhaustion of
arbitrary currents nor an analytic counterexample.
Throughout, K is any complete non-Archimedean field, including a trivially valued field. The space
X is good, separated, strict, boundaryless, and pure of analytic dimension n ≥ 2. All opens are
ordinary opens. Put D = d′ d′′ . A current T of bidegree (p, p) is ordinary CLD, middle-positive,
and satisfies both d′ T = d′′ T = 0. Except where stated otherwise, 0 ≤ p ≤ n − 1. We retain
the canonical calibration, singularities, nilpotents, and actual generic component lengths. Write
[f ] = f δX , where δX is the canonical integration current. The original assertion is

for every W ⊂ X, T, U, V ∈ Psh(W ) ∩ C(W ),

D[U ] = D[V ] =⇒ D(U T ) = D(V T ).

Here U, V are finite real continuous sections of the generated Psh sheaf, as defined below. No
equality Psh ∩C = CPsh is assumed. A negative answer to (Q) would require an actual analytic
space in this scope, a separately admissible pair, its upstairs canonical curvature equality, an
allowed current, and a compact ordinary smooth test detecting a nonzero difference. Nothing in the
diagnostic example below supplies such data.

P0: analytic and ordinary-local scope. Analytic spaces have compact-neighbourhood bases;
good analytic spaces have affinoid-neighbourhood bases; ordinary opens are analytic domains [11.1.7].
A topologically separated pure n-dimensional analytic space has its canonical calibrated level-n
tropical structure, compatible with analytic-domain restriction [15.7.1–2]. A good Hausdorff analytic
space is locally holomorphically separated and hence locally differentially separated [15.2.2–4]. On
such a space compact supports admit smooth cutoffs, finite nonnegative smooth localization, and
uniform approximation of compact continuous scalars by compact smooth scalars [15.2.5–9]. Global
paracompactness is not required for these compact-support assertions. The hypotheses apply to
each original ordinary open, in either valuation regime. Boundarylessness is retained when invoking
Stokes.
P1: generated functions. Definition 8.1.1 defines Psh as the smallest ordinary subsheaf of
functions valued in R ∪ {−∞} containing smooth functions of positive smooth Hessian and closed
under decreasing sequential limits. Lemma 8.1.4 describes the sheafified ordinal construction. We
reconstruct stabilization, upper semicontinuity, and the bounded-final-function steps rather than
relying on the printed proof’s extraneous integrability qualification. Definition 8.1.7 instead generates
CPsh inside continuous functions by uniform sequential closure. The printed reverse recognition in
8.1.11 is not used.
P2: middle positivity. Every compact symmetric smooth (q, q) test is a difference of two compact
middle-positive tests, provided its support is contained in a paracompact locally differentially
separated open [6.5.2]. The statement explicitly includes compact supports on almost-good analytic
spaces; good suffices here. Middle-positive currents are symmetric and nonnegative on middlepositive complementary tests; positivity is ordinary-local [7.2.1–4]. Positive smooth factors preserve
middle-positive currents [7.2.7(b)], since wedges of middle-positive forms are middle-positive [1.2.12,
1.10.3, 3.5.5]. This is not a replacement by strongly positive tests. A current and its negative cannot
both be middle-positive unless the current is zero [7.2.3; also proved below using the decomposition].

This last argument strengthens the continuous-differentiating-factor closure proposed in the supplied
unreviewed draft. It is obtained by orienting the one-continuous-factor identity with U, V as the
continuous factors. In particular the property persists through ordered differentiations by several
locally bounded discontinuous generated potentials; this does not interchange two discontinuous
factors or extend the original pair condition to discontinuous pairs.
One can now form local cones from δX by these operations and weak positive closure, with degree
fixed at each limit. All their members are actual ordinary positive doubly closed compatible currents.
The assertion about this cone is a mathematical closure theorem. It does not mean that an arbitrary
original current lies in it, locally or globally.

The concrete sufficient exhaustion statement left unproved is:
For every original K, X, p, every ordinary W , every allowed positive doubly closed current
T , and every x ∈ W , there is one ordinary neighbourhood N ⊂ W and a net of actual
positive doubly closed currents of degree (p, p) on N , each obtained locally from the
original canonical integration current by the operations of Theorem 3, converging weakly
to T |N against all ordinary compact smooth tests, including every analytic-unit moment
refinement.
Theorem 3 would imply (Q) from this statement. We have not proved it, and the hypothesis is not
a reformulation of positivity or a consequence of scalar density. It may be stronger than necessary.
A direct preservation theorem for arbitrary T , or a valid generating construction for both signs of
U − V on one fixed ordinary neighbourhood, could instead resolve the original problem. In that
latter route, Theorem 1 and pointedness would apply. Neither construction has been found here.
Mixed symmetry itself stops short of the target: for arbitrary T , equation (12) still contains D(hT )
on its right-hand side. Knowing D[h] = 0 identifies D(hδX ), not D(hT ). Using (12) to declare the
latter zero would assume the original assertion. Likewise equal canonical curvatures of separately
generated U, V do not give equal curvatures of their unrelated decreasing predecessors. Our induction
proves positivity and the correctly oriented symmetry, but does not preserve an arbitrary signed
relation at predecessor ranks.
The catalogue’s granted strict real-PL theorem restricts the function class and excludes trivial
valuation; the granted pure-curve recognition is intrinsically one-dimensional. Neither is used as
a premise of the above higher-dimensional proofs. An ordinary ambient curvature equation has
not been restricted to arbitrary curves or ascended under arbitrary analytic scalar extensions.
