## The proved partial theorem

**Theorem A.** In the retained domain there is a canonical positive symmetric mixed Radon operator
\[
\operatorname{MA}:(\mathcal E^1)^n\longrightarrow\{\text{positive Radon measures on }T\text{ of mass }V\}.
\]
It extends the genuine continuous operator, is affine under positive convex combination of the curvature in each slot, and is independent of all continuous psh decreasing approximations of the actual inputs. Every \(w\in\mathcal E^1\) is integrable against every mixed output. For arbitrary common directed indices, if
\[
u_{r,i}\downarrow u_r\in\mathcal E^1\ (1\leq r\leq n),\qquad
w_i\downarrow w\in\mathcal E^1,
\]
with all stages in \(\mathcal E^1\), then
\[
\operatorname{MA}(u_{1,i},\ldots,u_{n,i})\longrightarrow\operatorname{MA}(u_1,\ldots,u_n)
\quad\text{weakly},
\qquad
\int w_i\operatorname{MA}(u_{1,i},\ldots,u_{n,i})\longrightarrow\int w\operatorname{MA}(u_1,\ldots,u_n).
\tag{2}
\]
The majorant energy in (1) equals its genuine mixed-integral formula. Its affine-segment derivative is
\[
\frac d{dt}E((1-t)u+tv)=\int_T(v-u)\operatorname{MA}((1-t)u+tv),\quad 0\leq t\leq1,
\tag{3}
\]
with one-sided derivatives at endpoints. Strict Borel mixed locality and comparison hold: if \(R=(r_2,\ldots,r_n)\in(\mathcal E^1)^{n-1}\) and \(m=\max(u,v)\), then
\[
\begin{split}
\mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}\operatorname{MA}(m,R)&=\mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}\operatorname{MA}(u,R),\\
\mathbf1_{\{v>u\}}\operatorname{MA}(m,R)&=\mathbf1_{\{v>u\}}\operatorname{MA}(v,R),\\
\operatorname{MA}(v,R)(\{u<v\})&\leq\operatorname{MA}(u,R)(\{u<v\}).
\end{split}\tag{4}
\]
Pure comparison follows by replacing one slot at a time in (4). The absolute finite-energy class and its measures are reference independent. For every complete real-valued extension \(F/K\), pullback preserves extended energy and satisfies the mixed pushforward law, with the same geometric mass \(V\).

## Continuous calculus and an ordered estimate

For continuous \(u,v\), the cocycle law gives
\[
E(v)-E(u)=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^n\int_T(v-u)\,\theta_v^j\theta_u^{n-j}.
\tag{5}
\]
These are positive measures of mass \(V\). The continuous cone is convex over real coefficients: rational combinations of FS metrics are FS by clearing denominators and tensoring section systems; uniform FS closure gives the continuous rational case, and uniform approximation of real coefficients gives the real case. Finite maxima are psh. Constant shifts preserve curvature.

For a product \(Q\) of \(n-1\) continuous psh curvatures and continuous scalar differences of psh metrics, possibly on auxiliary bundles, put
\[
D_Q(f,g)=-\int_T f\,dd^cg\,Q.
\tag{6}
\]
The continuous dpsh calculus makes this representation independent. It is symmetric, nonnegative on the diagonal, and satisfies Cauchy--Schwarz. Here is the scope check rather than a singular assertion: uniformly regularize the finitely many continuous metrics by smooth semipositive metrics on their own bundles. For smooth data, compact boundaryless Stokes gives
\[
D_Q(f,g)=\int_Tdf\wedge d^cg\,Q,\qquad D_Q(f,f)\geq0.
\]
Uniform regularization passes the integral to continuous data. The signed curvature products have a fixed total-variation bound, the sum of the finitely many positive intersection masses. If \(f_a\to f\) uniformly and \(\lambda_a\to\lambda\) weakly with that bound, split the integral error into
\[
\left|\int(f_a-f)\,d\lambda_a\right|\leq\|f_a-f\|_\infty\|\lambda_a\|_{\mathrm{TV}},\qquad
\int f\,d(\lambda_a-\lambda).
\]
Both vanish. Symmetry and diagonal positivity therefore survive. The nonnegative quadratic \(D_Q(f+sg,f+sg)\) proves Cauchy--Schwarz. This uses only continuous measures.

Along a continuous psh segment \(a_t=(1-t)u+tv\), formula (5) and multilinearity give an energy polynomial of degree at most \(n+1\), with first derivative \(\int(v-u)\theta_{a_t}^n\) and second derivative
\[
-nD_{\theta_{a_t}^{n-1}}(v-u,v-u)\leq0.
\]
Thus continuous energy is concave.

**Lemma 1 (ordered PL estimate).** For every \(f\in\mathrm{PL}_{\mathbb R}(T)\), there is a finite constant \(C_f\geq0\), independent of \(u,v\in\mathcal C\), such that \(u\leq v\) implies
\[
\left|\int f(\theta_v^n-\theta_u^n)\right|
\leq\sqrt{n(n+1)C_f(E(v)-E(u))}.
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** Express \(f=h-k\) as FS metrics on a common actual ample rational bundle \(A\), by the continuous PL lemma. One can take
\[
C_f=2\|f\|_\infty(A\cdot L^{n-1}).
\]
For \(Q_j=\theta_u^j\theta_v^{n-1-j}\), \(0\leq j<n\), the two positive measures \(dd^ch\,Q_j\), \(dd^ck\,Q_j\) each have mass \(A\cdot L^{n-1}\). Hence \(0\leq D_{Q_j}(f,f)\leq C_f\). With \(\delta=v-u\geq0\), factor the difference of the pure curvatures:
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}D_{Q_j}(\delta,\delta)
=\int\delta(\theta_u^n-\theta_v^n)
\leq\int\delta\theta_u^n
\leq(n+1)(E(v)-E(u)).
\]
The last bound keeps the \(j=0\) term of (5); all other terms are nonnegative. Cauchy--Schwarz first for each (D_{Q_j}), then for the finite sum, bounds
\[
\left|\sum_jD_{Q_j}(f,\delta)\right|
\leq\sqrt{nC_f\sum_jD_{Q_j}(\delta,\delta)}.
\]
This is (7). The PL tests are uniformly dense in \(C(T)\) by the real-weight separation theorem, including supported seminorm points. \(\square\)

## Directed limits: energy and Radon integration

**Lemma 2.** If actual psh scalars \(u_i\downarrow u\) have an actual psh endpoint, then \(E(u_i)\downarrow E(u)\), allowing \(E(u)=-\infty\). Extended energy is concave, and every finite positive convex combination of finite-energy endpoints is an actual finite-energy endpoint. Also \(\max(u,v)\in\mathcal E^1\) for \(u,v\in\mathcal E^1\).

**Proof.** Extended energy is monotone by the majorant infimum. Fix \(a\in\mathcal C\), \(a\geq u\), and \(\epsilon>0\). The closed compact sets
\[
\{u_i-a\geq\epsilon\}
\]
decrease to an empty intersection. Compactness and directedness give \(u_i\leq a+\epsilon\) on a tail. Hence \(\inf_iE(u_i)\leq E(a)+V\epsilon\). Infimize over \(a\), then let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). The reverse inequality is monotonicity. If the endpoint is identically \(-\infty\), the same argument with the continuous constant majorant (-M) makes the energies tend to \(-\infty\).

For a finite convex combination, use the product-directed continuous approximation nets. Convexity of the continuous cone and decreasing closure of the fixed-bundle psh cone produce the all-point limit, initially allowing an identically \(-\infty\) endpoint. Concavity bounds its stage energies below by the finite weighted sum of endpoint energies, excluding that endpoint by the preceding constant-majorant argument. Passing continuous concavity through the proved energy limit gives extended concavity. Zero coefficients are omitted. For maxima, use the decreasing maxima of continuous approximants and monotonicity of energy. Their limit is actual psh and has energy at least each original energy. \(\square\)

**Lemma 3 (Radon directed integration).** If \(\lambda\) is a finite Radon measure on a compact Hausdorff space and \(g_i\) is an increasing directed net of nonnegative lsc functions, then
\[
\int\sup_i g_i\,d\lambda=\sup_i\int g_i\,d\lambda.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(\lambda_a\to\lambda\) weakly, then for a nonnegative lsc \(g\),
\[
\int g\,d\lambda\leq\liminf_a\int g\,d\lambda_a.
\tag{9}
\]

**Proof.** The strict superlevel of the supremum in (8) is the directed union of the open strict superlevels of its stages. Every compact subset admits a finite subcover, dominated by one index. Inner regularity therefore gives equality of its measure with the supremum of the stage superlevel measures. For a truncated layer integral, a finite lower step sum has finitely many levels; choose a single index approximating all those masses at once. Refine the step sum and let the truncation grow. This proves (8), including infinite values. For (9), compact Hausdorff normality and inner regularity give
\[
\int g\,d\lambda=\sup_{h\in C(T),\ 0\leq h\leq g}\int h\,d\lambda.
\]
To check this, choose inner compact subsets of finitely many strict open levels and continuous cutoffs supported in these levels and equal to one on the compacts. Their sum times the level spacing is bounded by \(g\) and approximates a truncated layer integral. Each fixed minorant passes through weak convergence; taking the supremum proves (9). Neither statement is measurable-net MCT. \(\square\)

## Pure measures and positive mixed polarization

For \(u\in\mathcal E^1\), take any defining continuous psh net \(u_a\downarrow u\). Lemma 2 gives a finite limiting energy. Estimate (7) makes the continuous pure measures Cauchy on every PL test. For incomparable late indices, compare each to a common later stage. Their common mass \(V\) gives a uniform bound \(V\|f\|_\infty\), so PL density yields a limit on every continuous test. Positivity and the value \(V\) at (1) give, by Riesz representation, a positive Radon measure of mass \(V\), denoted \(\operatorname{MA}(u)\).

Independence is an ordered argument, not a choice of target. If \(v_b\downarrow u\) is another approximation, \(\max(u_a,v_b)\downarrow u\) on the product index. Its energy tends to \(E(u)\). Apply (7) to each original stage and that maximum. Both errors tend to zero, identifying the two measures. The constant approximation identifies this operator with the genuine continuous one at continuous endpoints.

For singular ordered \(u\leq v\), the same product maximum of their continuous approximants decreases to \(v\). Pass (7) to the already constructed weak pure limits and their finite energies. Thus (7) holds for ordered finite-energy endpoints as well. Lemma 2 then makes \(u_i\downarrow u\in\mathcal E^1\) imply pure weak convergence, with arbitrary directed stages in \(\mathcal E^1\).

For a nonempty subset \(I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\), put \(u_I=|I|^{-1}\sum_{r\in I}u_r\in\mathcal E^1\), and define
\[
\operatorname{MA}(u_1,\ldots,u_n)
=\frac1{n!}\sum_{I\ne\varnothing}(-1)^{n-|I|}|I|^n\operatorname{MA}(u_I).
\tag{10}
\]
In continuous data this is the actual mixed measure: expanding \(\theta_{u_I}=|I|^{-1}\sum_{r\in I}\theta_{u_r}\), inclusion--exclusion cancels every monomial missing a label. The surviving product has coefficient (n!). In particular the signed scalar coefficients sum to one. In general, approximate all actual slots continuously on the product-directed index. At each stage (10) is a positive genuine mixed measure of mass \(V\), and each pure subset term converges. Thus its weak limit is positive of mass \(V\). This proves positivity rather than assuming it of a signed formula. It also proves approximation independence, symmetry, and the convex-affine slot laws by passage from the continuous identities. For singular decreasing slot nets, apply pure convergence to each subset average. This proves the weak part of (2).

## Integrability and a uniform singular-test bound

For any finite family of endpoints and chosen tails of their continuous majorants, a constant change \(\psi\mapsto\psi+C\) makes every scalar in those tails nonpositive. Curvatures do not change, and the energy in the new scalar coordinates is \(E(u)-VC\). Hence write \(B(u)=-E(u)\geq0\) while using this normalization. This is a temporary common scalar normalization, not an alteration of the absolute metrics.

**Lemma 4.** For nonpositive \(w,u_1,\ldots,u_n\in\mathcal E^1\),
\[
\int(-w)\operatorname{MA}(u_1,\ldots,u_n)
\leq\frac{(n+1)^{n+1}}{n!}\left(B(w)+\sum_{r=1}^nB(u_r)\right).
\tag{11}
\]
Every finite-energy test is therefore integrable against every mixed output, and each such output gives zero mass to that test's pole set.

**Proof.** For continuous data set \(z=(w+u_1+\cdots+u_n)/(n+1)\). Concavity bounds \(B(z)\) above by the sum of these costs divided by \(n+1\). In the nonnegative terms defining \(B(z)\), its top term
\[
\frac1{n+1}\int(-z)\theta_z^n
\]
contains the desired integral with coefficient (n!/(n+1)^{n+2}): one factor comes from (-z), \(n\) from the curvature average, and one from the energy denominator. Keeping only that term proves (11). Fix a continuous nonpositive test and decrease the curvature approximants. Their weak convergence and finite energy limits pass this inequality to the actual slots. Now decrease the continuous test majorants to \(w\). Their costs are at most \(B(w)\), and (8) on the one fixed mixed output passes their nonnegative integrals to the integral of (-w). Adding constants back gives unrestricted integrability. There is no uncountable intersection of full-measure sets: each finite family has its own finite-value set. \(\square\)

**Lemma 5.** If \(u,w\in\mathcal E^1\) and \(w_k\downarrow w\) are continuous psh, then
\[
\int(w_k-w)\operatorname{MA}(u)
\leq A_n\left[E\left(\frac{u+w_k}{2}\right)-E\left(\frac{u+w}{2}\right)\right],
\qquad A_n=2^{n+1}(n+1).
\tag{12}
\]
The difference in the integral is defined on its finite-value full-measure set.

**Proof.** First let \(u\) be continuous and \(j\geq k\). Apply (5) to \(h_k=(u+w_k)/2\geq h_j=(u+w_j)/2\), keeping the top \(j=n\) term. The positive curvature expansion gives \(\operatorname{MA}(h_k)\geq2^{-n}\operatorname{MA}(u)\). Since \(h_k-h_j=(w_k-w_j)/2\geq0\), (12) follows with \(w_j\) in place of \(w\). The nonnegative continuous differences increase on the \(j\)-tail; (8) passes their integrals to the endpoint, and Lemma 2 passes the energies. For singular \(u\), take continuous \(u_a\downarrow u\). The function \(w_k-w\) is nonnegative lsc, with possible infinity at a pole, and is integrable against all measures under consideration by Lemma 4. Apply (9) to the weak limit of \(\operatorname{MA}(u_a)\). The two right-side energies have finite decreasing limits, so their difference converges to the right side of (12). This proves the assertion. \(\square\)

To prove fixed-test weighted convergence, take \(u_i\downarrow u\) in \(\mathcal E^1\), and continuous \(w_k\downarrow w\). The product-directed potentials ((u_i+w_k)/2) decrease to \((u+w)/2\in\mathcal E^1\). Given \(\epsilon>0\), Lemma 2 supplies \(i_0,k_0\) such that
\[
0\leq E((u_i+w_k)/2)-E((u+w)/2)<\epsilon\quad(i\geq i_0,k\geq k_0).
\]
Also \(E((u_i+w)/2)\geq E((u+w)/2)\). Equation (12) therefore bounds the error of replacing \(w\) by the **fixed** continuous (w_{k_0}), uniformly for the late moving measures, by \(A_n\epsilon\). It gives the same bound for the endpoint measure. Weak convergence at this continuous test proves convergence at \(w\).

If instead \(w_i\downarrow w\) is singular, fix \(k\) and \(\eta>0\). The compact sets \(\{w_i\geq w_k+\eta\}\) decrease to the empty set, so \(w_i\leq w_k+\eta\) on a tail. Thus
\[
0\leq\int(w_i-w)\operatorname{MA}(u_i)
\leq\int(w_k-w)\operatorname{MA}(u_i)+\eta V.
\]
Use the preceding uniform error with \(k=k_0\), then let \(\epsilon,\eta\downarrow0\). Summing these finite, legitimate test integrals in (10) proves the full mixed weighted assertion (2). No measurable-net convergence against moving measures is used.

Apply (2) to each term of the continuous energy formula for \(u_a\downarrow u\): the moving weight is \(u_a\), the first \(j\) slots are \(u_a\), and the other slots are zero. Lemma 2 gives

\tag{13}
\]

\[
E(v)-E(u)=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^n\int_T(v-u)\operatorname{MA}(v^{[j]},u^{[n-j]}).
\tag{14}
\]
Each difference in (14) is taken where both tests are finite, which is a full-measure set for the displayed output by Lemma 4.

For \(u_t=(1-t)u+tv\), Lemma 2 gives actual finite-energy endpoints for all \(t\in[0,1]\). At each continuous approximation stage, the energy is a polynomial of degree at most \(n+1\). The coefficients of its derivative are fixed binomial factors times mixed integrals with weight one of the two endpoints. Formula (2) passes all these finitely many coefficients to their finite actual integrals. The constant coefficient tends to \(E(u)\). Integrating the limiting polynomial and using decreasing energy continuity at each fixed \(t\) proves that this polynomial is \(E(u_t)\) and that its derivative is (3). This is a finite coefficient argument, not an interchange of unrestricted limits and derivatives.

\tag{15}

The geometric intersection degree is unchanged by scalar extension. No field-degree multiplier, regular model over a DVR, or discrete valuation is inserted into (15).

## From continuous locality to strict Borel locality

**Lemma 6 (continuous local bridge).** For \(u,v,R\in\mathcal C\) and \(m=\max(u,v)\), the genuine continuous mixed measure \(\operatorname{MA}(m,R)\) restricts on the open set \(\{u>v\}\) to \(\operatorname{MA}(u,R)\), and symmetrically on \(\{v>u\}\).

**Proof.** Work on an open trivialization of a cleared power of \(L\). The absolute local weights of each metric are uniform limits of smooth psh weights by the global smooth regularization theorem; hence they are locally psh-approachable in the exact CLD sense. Their local top products are a sheaf construction by Corollaries 5.6.5--5.6.6. On \(\{u>v\}\), the weight of \(m\) is literally the weight of \(u\), so these local currents coincide. Proposition 5.4.6 identifies their top positive currents with actual local Radon measures. The local products equal restrictions of the global genuine continuous mixed operator: use the same globally uniformly smooth regularizations, restrict on a compactly supported local smooth test, and invoke the uniqueness of the local product and of the global uniform limit. Local curvature is independent of trivialization, since the logarithm of an invertible transition function has zero \(dd^c\). Gluing gives the asserted restrictions. This establishes continuous locality without an envelope hypothesis. \(\square\)

**Lemma 7 (bounded Lipschitz transport).** Suppose \(\rho_i=\operatorname{MA}(a_{1,i},\ldots,a_{n,i})\) with all slots decreasing in \(\mathcal E^1\) to finite-energy endpoints and \(\rho\) its weak limit. For any finite fixed family \(b_1,\ldots,b_q\in\mathcal E^1\), any bounded Lipschitz \(F:\mathbb R^q\to\mathbb R\), and \(f\in C(T)\),
\[
\int fF(b_1,\ldots,b_q)\,d\rho_i\longrightarrow\int fF(b_1,\ldots,b_q)\,d\rho.
\tag{16}
\]
The compositions have arbitrary assigned values at poles, which are null for each measure in this assertion.

**Proof.** Choose continuous \(b_{r,k}\downarrow b_r\), independently. For a fixed tuple of approximation indices, (2) at fixed weights gives
\[
\int(b_{r,k}-b_r)\,d\rho_i\longrightarrow\int(b_{r,k}-b_r)\,d\rho.
\]
By (8), these nonnegative endpoint errors tend to zero as \(k\) increases. For the finite family choose one tuple making their sum small for the endpoint and, by the displayed convergence, uniformly for a tail of the moving outputs. The Lipschitz bound makes the change in either integral at most \(\|f\|_\infty\operatorname{Lip}(F)\) times this sum. The fixed-stage composition is continuous and bounded on \(T\), so its integrals converge weakly. Remove the approximation tuple to prove (16). Only finitely many tests are involved, and all are integrable by Lemma 4. \(\square\)

Now choose a product-directed family of continuous approximants \(u_i\downarrow u\), \(v_i\downarrow v\), and \(R_i\downarrow R\). Put
\[
m_i=\max(u_i,v_i)\downarrow m=\max(u,v),\qquad

## Mixed and pure comparison

Fix \(\epsilon>0\) and \(m_\epsilon=\max(u,v-\epsilon)\). All measures below have raw mass \(V\), and constant shifts do not change curvature. Strict locality gives
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{MA}(v,R)(\{u<v-\epsilon\})
&=\operatorname{MA}(m_\epsilon,R)(\{u<v-\epsilon\})\\
&\leq V-\operatorname{MA}(m_\epsilon,R)(\{u>v-\epsilon\})\\
&=V-\operatorname{MA}(u,R)(\{u>v-\epsilon\})\\
&=\operatorname{MA}(u,R)(\{u\leq v-\epsilon\})\\
&\leq\operatorname{MA}(u,R)(\{u<v\}).
\end{split}\tag{18}
\]
Any exceptional pole cases have zero measure by Lemma 4, so the complement equality is valid without infinite subtraction. Sequentially let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\); the left winner sets exhaust \(\{u<v\}\) up to those null sets. This proves mixed comparison. With \(R\) chosen successively to contain \(j\) copies of \(v\) and \(n-1-j\) copies of \(u\), \(0\leq j<n\), it gives
\[
\operatorname{MA}(v)(\{u<v\})\leq\operatorname{MA}(u)(\{u<v\}).
\tag{19}
\]
One must use this finite chain: applying single-slot comparison just once does not directly prove the pure statement in higher dimension. No skeleton or inverse measure construction is involved.

## Targets and variational consequences actually proved

\tag{21}
\]

**Proof.** Take continuous \(u_a\downarrow u\in\mathcal E^1\) and a common constant \(C\) bounding the tail above. The bound in (20) gives \(\int u_a\,d\mu\geq E(u_a)-J_\psi(\mu)\geq E(u)-J_\psi(\mu)\). The increasing continuous nonnegative functions \(C-u_a\) therefore have bounded integrals. Equation (8) gives integrability of \(u\) and convergence of its integrals. Lemma 2 then passes its objective to the endpoint. Thus every endpoint objective is at most \(J_\psi(\mu)\). Continuous competitors are finite energy, proving equality of the two suprema. \(\square\)

**Proposition 9.** Every pure operator output has finite dual cost. More precisely, if \(\nu=\operatorname{MA}(u)\), then
\[
J_\psi(\nu)=E(u)-\int_Tu\,d\nu<\infty.
\tag{22}
\]
Every solution of \(\operatorname{MA}(u)=\mu\) maximizes \(E(v)-\int v\,d\mu\) on the actual \(\mathcal E^1\) cone.

**Proof.** Extended concavity and the genuine affine right derivative (3) give the whole supporting plane
\[
E(v)\leq E(u)+\int_T(v-u)\,d\operatorname{MA}(u)\quad(v\in\mathcal E^1).
\tag{23}
\]
These are finite integrals by Lemma 4. Hence all continuous-test objectives at \(\nu\) are bounded above by the right side of (22). For continuous approximants of \(u\), the fixed-measure directed integral law (8) and Lemma 2 show that their objectives tend to that value. This proves (22). With a prescribed solution measure, (23) is exactly global maximality. It applies to every actual solution, not just to one constructed variationally. \(\square\)

There is a useful rigorous constraint on putative different solutions, weaker than uniqueness. If \(\operatorname{MA}(u)=\operatorname{MA}(v)=\mu\), both maximize the same objective by Proposition 9. Concavity makes their entire actual affine segment maximize. Let \(d=v-u\), interpreted inside legitimate integrals. Then \(E(u_t)\) is affine and

\tag{24}
\]
where the last expression is defined by finite multilinear differences of the constructed products. Expanding it gives a Bernstein polynomial with nonnegative coefficients

D_j=-\int d\,dd^cd\,\theta_u^j\theta_v^{n-1-j},\quad 0\leq j<n.
\]
Each \(D_j\geq0\) by passing the continuous positive forms in (6) through the weighted law (2). Vanishing in (24) forces every \(D_j=0\), since all interior Bernstein factors are positive. No proof here identifies this collection of zero mixed Dirichlet values with equality of actual all-point potentials modulo a constant. Thus (24) is an every-solution necessary degeneracy statement, not every-solution uniqueness.

\tag{25}

This follows from the actual majorant cocycle and boundedness of \(b\), so finite dual cost and all-test integrability are independent of reference.
