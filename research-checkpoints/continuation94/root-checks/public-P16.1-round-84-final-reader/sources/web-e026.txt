L22: 【8†Lemma 33.48.3.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be a proper scheme over $k$. Let $\mathcal{L}$ be an ample invertible $\mathcal{O}_ X$-module. Let $s \in \Gamma (X, \mathcal{L})$. Assume
L23: 
L24:   1. $s$ is a regular section (Divisors, Definition 【14†31.15.6】),
L25: 
L26:   2. for every closed point $x \in X$ we have $\text{depth}(\mathcal{O}_{X, x}) \geq 2$, and
L27: 
L28:   3. $X$ is connected.
L29: 
L30: Then the zero scheme $Z(s)$ of $s$ is connected.
L31: Proof. Since $s$ is a regular section, so is $s^ n \in \Gamma (X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n})$ for all $n > 1$. Moreover, the inclusion morphism $Z(s) \to Z(s^ n)$ is a bijection on underlying topological spaces. Hence if $Z(s)$ is disconnected, so is $Z(s^ n)$. Now consider the canonical short exact sequence
L32: 
L33: \[ 0 \to \mathcal{L}^{\otimes -n} \xrightarrow {s^ n} \mathcal{O}_ X \to \mathcal{O}_{Z(s^ n)} \to 0 \]
L34: Consider the $k$-algebra $R_ n = \Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_{Z(s^ n)})$. If $Z(s)$ is disconnected, i.e., $Z(s^ n)$ is disconnected, then either $R_ n$ is zero in case $Z(s^ n) = \emptyset $ or $R_ n$ contains a nontrivial idempotent in case $Z(s^ n) = U \amalg V$ with $U, V \subset Z(s^ n)$ open and nonempty (the reader may wish to consult Lemma 【15†33.9.3】). Thus the map $\Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_ X) \to R_ n$ cannot be an isomorphism.
L35: It follows that either $H^0(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes -n})$ or $H^1(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes -n})$ is nonzero for infinitely many positive $n$. This contradicts Lemma 【16†33.48.1】 or 【10†33.48.2】 and the proof is complete. $\square$
