L1@P0: ON THE STRUCTURE OF NONARCHIMEDEAN ANALYTIC CURVES
L2@P0: MATTHEW BAKER, SAM PAYNE, AND JOSEPH RABINOFF
L3@P0: ABSTRACT. Let K be an algebraically closed, complete nonarchimedean field and let X be a smooth
L4@P0: K-curve. In this paper we elaborate on several aspects of the structure of the Berkovich analytic space
L5@P0: Xan. We define semistable vertex sets of Xan and their associated skeleta, which are essentially finite
L6@P0: metric graphs embedded in Xan. We prove a folklore theorem which states that semistable vertex sets
L7@P0: of X are in natural bijective correspondence with semistable models of X, thus showing that our notion
L8@P0: of skeleton coincides with the standard definition of Berkovich [Ber90]. We use the skeletal theory to
L9@P0: define a canonical metric on H(Xan) ≔ Xan r X(K), and we give a proof of Thuillier’s nonarchimedean
L10@P0: Poincaré-Lelong formula in this language using results of Bosch and Lütkebohmert.
L11@P0: 1. INTRODUCTION
L12@P0: Throughout this paper we let K denote an algebraically closed field which is complete with respect
L13@P0: to a nontrivial, nonarchimedean valuation val : K → R ∪ {∞}. Let R be the valuation ring of K, mR
L14@P0: its maximal ideal, and k its residue field. In this situation, R is not Noetherian and k is algebraically
L15@P0: closed. We let | · | = exp(− val(·)) be an associated absolute value and let G = val(K×) ⊂ R be the
L16@P0: value group.
L17@P0: 1.1. Let X be a smooth, proper, connected algebraic K-curve and let Xan be its analytification in the
L18@P0: sense of Berkovich [Ber90]. The purpose of this note is to elaborate on the following aspects of the
L19@P0: structure of Xan:
L20@P0: (1) We define semistable vertex sets of X and their associated skeleta, which are finite metric
L21@P0: graphs contained in Xan.
L22@P0: (2) We make explicit the bijective correspondence between the semistable vertex sets of X and
L23@P0: the semistable models of X.
L24@P0: (3) We show that ‘most’ of X can be covered by skeleta as above, and use this fact to define a
L25@P0: canonical metric on H(Xan) ≔ Xan r X(K) such that the resulting metric space is locally
L26@P0: modeled on an R-tree.
L27@P0: (4) We use results of Bosch and Lütkebohmert to prove Thuillier’s nonarchimedean analogue of
L28@P0: the Poincaré-Lelong formula. This includes the fact that the valuation of a nonzero rational
L29@P0: function on X is a piecewise affine function on H(Xan), which is moreover harmonic in the
L30@P0: sense that the sum of the outgoing slopes at any point is zero.
L31@P0: The above results, while very useful and in large part well-known to experts, are often difficult or
L32@P0: impossible to extract from the literature, although Ducros [Duc13] is preparing a book on the subject.
L33@P0: In [BPR11] we apply these ideas to study the relationship between the analytification of a curve X
L34@P0: and its tropicalization with respect to a rational map to a torus. In particular, we study the metric
L35@P0: aspects of the tropicalization map and the relationship between skeleta and tropicalizations of X.
L36@P0: 1.2. Skeleta and semistable models. The theory of skeleta goes back to Berkovich [Ber90, Ber99],
L37@P0: and is elaborated somewhat in the case of curves in Thuillier’s (unpublished) thesis [Thu05]. In
L38@P0: Berkovich’s approach, a skeleton is a subset of Xan which is associated to a semistable model of X.
L39@P0: In contrast, we define the skeleton in terms of a semistable vertex set of X (called a triangulation
L40@P0: in [Duc08]; see also Temkin’s lecture notes [Tem]), which is a finite set V of type-2 points of Xan such
L41@P0: that Xan r V is a disjoint union of open balls and finitely many open annuli. This approach has the
L42@P0: advantage that it only makes reference to the analytic space Xan and is not conceptually tied to the
L43@P0: semistable reduction theory of X, thus making certain constructions more natural.

L88@P1: 1.4. In §5 we turn our attention to the metric nature of the analytic curve Xan. This is worked
L89@P1: out ‘by hand’ in the case X = P1 by Baker and Rumely [BR10], but does not otherwise explicitly
L90@P1: appear in the literature. We will prove that the metric graph structures on each skeleton of Xan are
L91@P1: compatible, and that the resulting metric on their union extends by continuity to a unique metric on

L95@P1: analysis necessary to give the statement of Thuillier’s theorem in terms of the nonarchimedean ddc
L96@P1: operator; for this reason we call the theorem the Slope Formula.
