L23: 【8†Lemma 33.47.3.】 In Situation 【10†33.47.2】 assume
L24: 
L25:   1. $X$ is smooth over $k$,
L26:   2. the image of $\psi : V \to \Gamma (X, \mathcal{L})$ generates $\mathcal{L}$,
L27: 
L28:   3. the corresponding morphism $\varphi _{\mathcal{L}, \psi } : X \to \mathbf{P}(V)$ is an immersion.
L29: 
L30: Then for general $v \in V \otimes _ k k'$ the scheme $H_ v$ is smooth over $k'$.
L31: 
L32: Proof. (We observe that $X$ is separated and finite type as a locally closed subscheme of a projective space.) Let us use the notation introduced above the statement of the lemma. We consider the projections
L33: \[ \xymatrix{ \mathbf{A}^ r_ k \times _ k X \ar[d] & H_{univ} \ar[l] \ar[ld]^ p \ar[r] \ar[rd]_ q & \mathbf{A}^ r_ k \times _ k X \ar[d] \\ X & & \mathbf{A}^ r_ k } \]
L34: Let $\Sigma \subset H_{univ}$ be the singular locus of the morphsm $q : H_{univ} \to \mathbf{A}^ r_ k$, i.e., the set of points where $q$ is not smooth. Then $\Sigma $ is closed because the smooth locus of a morphism is open by definition. Since the fibre of a smooth morphism is smooth, it suffices to prove $q(\Sigma )$ is contained in a proper closed subset of $\mathbf{A}^ r_ k$.
L35: Since $\Sigma $ (with reduced induced scheme structure) is a finite type scheme over $k$ it suffices to prove $\dim (\Sigma ) < r$ This follows from Lemma 【14†33.20.4】. Since dimensions aren't changed by replacing $k$ by a bigger field (Morphisms, Lemma 【15†29.29.3】), we may and do assume $k$ is algebraically closed.
L36: By dimension theory (Lemma 【14†33.20.4】), it suffices to prove that for $x \in X \setminus Z$ closed we have $p^{-1}(\{ x\} ) \cap \Sigma $ has dimension $< r - \dim (X')$ where $X'$ is the unique irreducible component of $X$ containing $x$. As $X$ is smooth over $k$ and $x$ is a closed point we have $\dim (X') = \dim \mathfrak m_ x/\mathfrak m_ x^2$ (Morphisms, Lemma 【16†29.35.12】 and Algebra, Lemma 【17†10.140.1】). Thus we win if
L37: \[ \dim p^{-1}(x) \cap \Sigma < r - \dim \mathfrak m_ x/\mathfrak m_ x^2 \]
L38: 
L39: for all $x \in X$ closed.
L40: Since $V$ globally generated $\mathcal{L}$, for every irreducible component $X'$ of $X$ there is a nonempty Zariski open of $\mathbf{A}^ r$ such that the fibres of $q$ over this open do not contain $X'$. (For example, if $x' \in X'$ is a closed point, then we can take the open corresponding to those vectors $v \in V$ such that $\psi (v)$ does not vanish at $x'$.
L41: This open will be the complement of a hyperplane in $\mathbf{A}^ r_ k$.) Let $U \subset \mathbf{A}^ r$ be the (nonempty) intersection of these opens. Then the fibres of $q^{-1}(U) \to U$ are effective Cartier divisors on the fibres of $U \times _ k X \to U$ (because a nonvanishing section of an invertible module on an integral scheme is a regular section). Hence the morphism $q^{-1}(U) \to U$ is flat by Divisors, Lemma 【18†31.19.9】.
L42: Thus for $x \in X$ closed and $v \in V = \mathbf{A}^ r_ k(k)$, if $(x, v) \in H_{univ}$, i.e., if $x \in H_ v$ then $q$ is smooth at $(x, v)$ if and only if the fibre $H_ v$ is smooth at $x$, see Morphisms, Lemma 【19†29.35.14】.
L43: Consider the image $\psi (v)_ x$ in the stalk $\mathcal{L}_ x$ of the section corresponding to $v \in V$. We have
L44: 
L45: \[ x \in H_ v \Leftrightarrow \psi (v)_ x \in \mathfrak m_ x\mathcal{L}_ x \]
L46: 
L47: If this is true, then we have
L48: 
L49: \[ H_ v\text{ singular at }x \Leftrightarrow \psi (v)_ x \in \mathfrak m_ x^2\mathcal{L}_ x \]
L50: Namely, $\psi (v)_ x$ is not contained in $\mathfrak m_ x^2\mathcal{L}_ x$ $\Leftrightarrow $ the local equation for $H_ v \subset X$ at $x$ is not contained in $\mathfrak m_ x^2$ $\Leftrightarrow $ $\mathcal{O}_{H_ v, x}$ is regular (Algebra, Lemma 【20†10.106.3】) $\Leftrightarrow $ $H_ v$ is smooth at $x$ over $k$ (Algebra, Lemma 【21†10.140.5】). We conclude that the closed points of $p^{-1}(x) \cap \Sigma $ correspond to those $v \in V$ such that $\psi (v)_ x \in \mathfrak m_ x^2\mathcal{L}_ x$.
L51: However, as $\varphi _{\mathcal{L}, \psi }$ is an immersion the map
L52: \[ V \longrightarrow \mathcal{L}_ x/\mathfrak m_ x^2\mathcal{L}_ x \]
L53: 
L54: is surjective (small detail omitted). By the above, the closed points of the locus $p^{-1}(x) \cap \Sigma $ viewed as a subspace of $V$ is the kernel of this map and hence has dimension $r - \dim \mathfrak m_ x/\mathfrak m_ x^2 - 1$ as desired. $\square$
