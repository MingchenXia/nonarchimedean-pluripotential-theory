L22: 【8†Proposition 29.28.1 (Generic flatness).】 Let $f : X \to S$ be a morphism of schemes. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a quasi-coherent sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_ X$-modules. Assume
L23: 
L24:   1. $S$ is integral,
L25: 
L26:   2. $f$ is of finite type, and
L27: 
L28:   3. $\mathcal{F}$ is a finite type $\mathcal{O}_ X$-module.
L29: 
L30: Then there exists an open dense subscheme $U \subset S$ such that $X_ U \to U$ is flat and of finite presentation and such that $\mathcal{F}|_{X_ U}$ is flat over $U$ and of finite presentation over $\mathcal{O}_{X_ U}$.
L31: Proof. As $S$ is integral it is irreducible (see Properties, Lemma 【14†28.3.4】) and any nonempty open is dense. Hence we may replace $S$ by an affine open of $S$ and assume that $S = \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)$ is affine. As $S$ is integral we see that $A$ is a domain. As $f$ is of finite type, it is quasi-compact, so $X$ is quasi-compact. Hence we can find a finite affine open cover $X = \bigcup _{i = 1, \ldots , n} X_ i$. Write $X_ i = \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(B_ i)$.
L32: Then $B_ i$ is a finite type $A$-algebra, see Lemma 【15†29.15.2】. Moreover there are finite type $B_ i$-modules $M_ i$ such that $\mathcal{F}|_{X_ i}$ is the quasi-coherent sheaf associated to the $B_ i$-module $M_ i$, see Properties, Lemma 【16†28.17.1】. Next, for each pair of indices $i, j$ choose an ideal $I_{ij} \subset B_ i$ such that $X_ i \setminus X_ i \cap X_ j = V(I_{ij})$ inside $X_ i = \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(B_ i)$. Set $M_{ij} = B_ i/I_{ij}$ and think of it as a $B_ i$-module.
L33: Then $V(I_{ij}) = \text{Supp}(M_{ij})$ and $M_{ij}$ is a finite $B_ i$-module.
L34: At this point we apply Algebra, Lemma 【17†10.118.3】 the pairs $(A \to B_ i, M_{ij})$ and to the pairs $(A \to B_ i, M_ i)$. Thus we obtain nonzero $f_{ij}, f_ i \in A$ such that (a) $A_{f_{ij}} \to B_{i, f_{ij}}$ is flat and of finite presentation and $M_{ij, f_{ij}}$ is flat over $A_{f_{ij}}$ and of finite presentation over $B_{i, f_{ij}}$, and (b) $B_{i, f_ i}$ is flat and of finite presentation over $A_ f$ and $M_{i, f_ i}$ is flat and of finite presentation over $B_{i, f_ i}$.
L35: Set $f = (\prod f_ i) (\prod f_{ij})$. We claim that taking $U = D(f)$ works.
L36: To prove our claim we may replace $A$ by $A_ f$, i.e., perform the base change by $U = \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A_ f) \to S$. After this base change we see that each of $A \to B_ i$ is flat and of finite presentation and that $M_ i$, $M_{ij}$ are flat over $A$ and of finite presentation over $B_ i$.
L37: This already proves that $X \to S$ is quasi-compact, locally of finite presentation, flat, and that $\mathcal{F}$ is flat over $S$ and of finite presentation over $\mathcal{O}_ X$, see Lemma 【18†29.22.2】 and Properties, Lemma 【19†28.17.2】. Since $M_{ij}$ is of finite presentation over $B_ i$ we see that $X_ i \cap X_ j = X_ i \setminus \text{Supp}(M_{ij})$ is a quasi-compact open of $X_ i$, see Algebra, Lemma 【20†10.40.8】. Hence we see that $X \to S$ is quasi-separated by Schemes, Lemma 【21†26.21.6】.
L38: This proves the proposition. $\square$
