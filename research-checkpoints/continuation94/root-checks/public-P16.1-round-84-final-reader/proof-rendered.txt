1. Exact disposition and hypotheses
The original P16.1 is not solved. The result below is a proof draft of fiber constancy and a conditional reduction of
the remaining equation theorem to a precisely specified countable original-class membership gate. Candidate=false.
All axes of the original family and the completion criterion are retained byte for byte in the preserved input and
task file. Nothing about the independent countable Runge work is assumed or inspected.
The equation domain considered here is the domain specified in the assignment: k is an arbitrary algebraically
closed, trivially valued, characteristic-zero field; X is a reduced irreducible geometrically unibranch projective
k-variety; and L is an actual ample rational line bundle. Clearing a positive tensor denominator reduces to honest
line bundles when necessary. All dimensions are allowed. For dimension zero, X = Spec k, the empty curvature
product has raw mass one, and the equation is immediate. The arguments involving curves and nonempty products
below concern positive dimension. We do not remove projectivity, ampleness, characteristic zero, algebraizability,
or the trivial valuation. In particular no arbitrary-field finite-energy theorem follows from the auxiliary nontrivially
valued fields used for fiber tests.

finite normalization X ν . Such a resolution is obtained in characteristic zero; when the task-granted contact bridge

First justify geometric connectedness. The finite normalization ν : X ν → X is a universal homeomorphism by
Stacks 0GIQ, since X is irreducible geometrically unibranch. The normal-target map ρ : Y → X ν satisfies all six
assumptions of Stacks 0AY8: it is proper; its target is integral and normal; its source is reduced; its single generic
point maps to the target generic point; and its generic fiber has H 0 = k(X). The normal-target Stein factor is
therefore the target, and every fiber is geometrically connected.
For any field point Spec F → X the normalization fiber is finite radicial. In characteristic zero its unique residue
field is F , so its reduction is Spec F , though it may have nilpotents. Reducing after an algebraic closure gives the
same underlying topological fiber of ρ. Hence every geometric fiber of π is connected. This reduction is used only
for connectivity and curves. It is never used to replace a fundamental cycle in a measure identity.
Fix s ∈ X an and one closed point z of the nonempty projective F -fiber Ys , where F = H (s). Its residue field
Fz /F is finite. The complete norm of F extends uniquely to Fz , giving a fixed analytic point z an over s. Now let

In a connected reduced projective fiber over algebraically closed Ky , the finite graph of irreducible components
joined by nonempty intersections is connected. Choose closed points in each intersection along a path between
components containing the two lifts. Within one component T , use a projective resolution Te → T in characteristic

8. Finite data and arbitrary cardinality
Assume henceforth countable EP just obtained conditionally. We reconstruct the full finite-data route. A finite
field of definition means a finitely generated subfield of k containing the finitely many coefficients in the chosen
presentations, followed by its algebraic closure inside k. This is countable in characteristic zero. It need not
contain the real constants occurring as weights; those are kept as the same finitely many real numbers, external to
the coefficient field.
For finite algebraic data, descent is literal. Projective equations, finite affine presentations, transition functions
of actual line bundles, finite section systems and their equalities, flag ideals, graph maps, the Gm -action and its
identities, and the specified generic-fiber isomorphism all involve finitely many coefficients and identities. Enlarge
the countable subfield to contain them. Generation by the descended sections is detected by the coherent cokernel,
which is zero after faithfully flat extension iff it was zero. An integral/reduced projective scheme downstairs is
detected in the same manner; since the field is algebraically closed and characteristic zero it is geometrically
integral/reduced. Actual ample bundles and relative ample model bundles descend by fpqc ampleness. A finite
birational map is descended together with a dense open on which it is an isomorphism; finiteness and that
isomorphism are fpqc properties. The dense open remains nonempty since its extension is nonempty.
Descend the normalization as a finite map with its actual source, not as an unsupported slogan. Include
its finite presentation, algebra inclusion and dense-open isomorphism. Its source is normal: normality descends
through the faithfully flat field extension. Over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field it is geometrically
normal (Stacks 0BXT, since the field is already perfect). The finite birational normal source is therefore the
normalization and remains so after any field enlargement. Its universal-homeomorphism property also descends:
after extending algebraically closed fields, a finite map’s geometric fibers and residue extensions detect universal
injectivity, while surjectivity is fpqc. Equivalently the finite diagonal’s surjectivity can be checked after this base
change. Hence the original downstairs X0 is geometrically unibranch; one has not silently replaced a nonnormal
generic fiber by its normalization.
For a finite model obstacle or Hdom competitor descend its actual generic-fiber model and bundle. Flatness
over the test line is detected faithfully flat, or directly by torsion freeness over k0 [t]. For relative integral closure
use BJ 1.13 and 1.15: it is equivalent to vertical R1 and to the special fiber having no embedded associated points
(vertical S2 ). Algebraically closed characteristic-zero field extensions are regular. In local noetherian rings the
flat depth formula and the corresponding dimension formula, with regular closed fibers, preserve and reflect S1 ;
regularity preserves and reflects the regular local rings at the generic points of the central fiber. Indeed all central

10.3 Full statement and detailed state of the art Problem 10.1. Extend the finite-energy existence and uniqueness
theory for the NA Monge Ampere equation beyond the currently proved combinations of smoothness, projectivity,
residue characteristic, valuation, positivity class, support, and algebraizability hypotheses.
This is not one yes-or-no assertion. Positive characteristic, singular or unibranch varieties,
nondiscrete valuations, big classes, arbitrary support, and nonalgebraizable analytic spaces require different inputs.
The known variational proofs use compactness, envelope regularity, differentiability of energy, and a comparison
principle; deleting a geometric hypothesis is valid only after replacing every place where it enters. The relevant
positive results and their distinct domains are surveyed in Book Chapter 28, Book Chapter 29. The projectivity
axis has one logical constraint. A proper algebraic K-scheme endowed with an ample line bundle is already
projective over K [Stacksb, Tag 0B5N]. Thus P16.2 cannot mean that one retains the BFJ line-bundle polarization
L while simply deleting the word projective. A nonprojective proper algebraizable extension must first replace that
positivity datumand then construct the corresponding psh, finite-energy, and comparison theoryrather than merely
rerun the original argument. No canonical replacement with the required equation theory is asserted here. One
exact source-stated axis is especially clean. PilleSchneider observes that the finite- energy space can be defined
over an arbitrary non-Archimedean field, but that the extension of the MongeAmpere operator is not known there

Proposition 10.1 (Arbitrary-field invariant toric skeletal endpoint). Let K be a complete nontrivially valued
non-Archimedean field, XSigma a proper toric K-variety, and L an ample toric line bundle. If mu is a positive
Radon measure on XSigmaan , is supported on the canonical skeleton NSigma , gives zero mass to NSigma \NR ,
and has total mass degL (XSigma ), then there is a toric continuous singular psh metric on L whose MongeAmpere
measure is mu. It is unique up to multiplication of the metric by a positive constant.
Thus nondiscreteness and the arbitrary complete ground field cause no obstruction in
this invariant toric, skeleton-supported range. Here continuous singular has the sources extended-valued meaning:
local weights are continuous into R {-infinity}, as in [BGGJK25, 5.3.1]. It does not imply finite real-valued weights
or finite energy. This is not an arbitrary- field finite-energy theorem in the BFJ sense: the result permits a singular
potential, requires torus invariance and skeletal support, and does not construct the general extended Monge
Ampere operator. In particular, P16.4 and P16.6 retain the respectively nonskeletal and noninvariant finite-energy
residual scopes. There is also a distinct, nonproper algebraic endpoint. Let n > d >= 1, let X be a smooth
projective Fano complex variety of dimension n, and let D be a reduced simple-normal-crossing anticanonical
divisor whose dual complex is the standard (d-1)-simplex. For the affine Calabi Yau complement X = X \ D,
Wangs Theorem A constructs a continuous non-Archimedean psh function on X an , over the trivially valued field
C, whose local MongeAmpere measure
