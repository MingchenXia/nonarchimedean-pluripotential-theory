## 2. Kähler classes up to the square-root threshold

For every actual Kähler form eta on M, let V=integral_M eta^2. Then

$$K_r=\pi^*[\eta]-r\{E\}\quad\hbox{is Kähler for }0<r<\sqrt V.$$

For sufficiently small r>0 this is the ordinary blowup Kähler construction: choose a curvature representative of O(-E) positive on the exceptional tangent directions, supported near E in a local blowup chart; pi*eta supplies transverse positivity and remains positive outside that neighborhood. For small r their sum is a Kähler representative. This local argument is also the blowup construction used in the reviewed round50 proof. Along the entire interval0<r<sqrt(V), the class has positive square V-r^2 and positive intersection r with E. These are all positive-dimensional irreducible analytic cycles on X. Hence the path lies in numerical positivity and is connected to its known small-r Kähler members. Demailly--Paun Main Theorem0.1 makes every member Kähler. In particular Omega is Kähler, and H_epsilon is Kähler when a epsilon<sqrt(V_epsilon), which holds for all sufficiently small epsilon.

A useful exact upper bound needs no divisorial Zariski decomposition. If a positive closed current S has class pi*[eta]-b{E} with b>=0, pair it with the actual Kähler representatives of K_r for0<r<sqrt(V). Positivity and cohomology give

$$0\leq\int_X S\wedge K_r=V-br.$$

Letting r increase to sqrt(V) yields b<=sqrt(V). The pairing uses a smooth actual Kähler form at each r, not the formal divisor E as a positive test form.

## 3. Compute the genuine body

Set s_epsilon=sqrt(V_epsilon)-a epsilon>0. I propose the exact formula

$$\Delta_{E,q}(H_\epsilon)
=\{(t,y):0\leq t\leq s_\epsilon,\quad0\leq y\leq a\epsilon+t\}.
\tag{1}$$

For the upper inclusion, take any actual current T whose rational analytic valuation is defined near q. Let t=nu(T,E). Divisor subtraction gives S=T-t[E]>=0, with class pi*[eta_epsilon]-(a epsilon+t){E}. The upper bound in Section2 gives a epsilon+t<=sqrt(V_epsilon), so t<=s_epsilon. The analytic residual restricts to a positive current on E because its generic Lelong number along E is0. Its mass is its actual intersection with E, namely a epsilon+t. Thus its point mass y at q lies between0 and a epsilon+t. This holds for all the generating valuations; it passes to their Euclidean closure. The displayed region is closed and convex.

For the lower inclusion, first choose rational t with0<=t<s_epsilon and rational y with0<=y<a epsilon+t. The class

$$F_t=\pi^*[\eta_\epsilon]-(a\epsilon+t)\{E\}$$

is Kähler by Section2. Its restriction to E has degree a epsilon+t. On E=P1 choose an actual probability Kähler form omega_E and the current

$$R=y[q]+(a\epsilon+t-y)\omega_E.$$

It is a positive Kähler current on E with rational analytic singularity coefficient y at q, and has exactly the restriction class of F_t. Deng Proposition4.16 gives an actual analytic Kähler current S in F_t with S|E=R. The proof chooses a rational auxiliary pole coefficient delta>0 with F_t-delta{E} still Kähler. Near q, the two rational coefficients y and delta have a common positive denominator; the local maximum is equivalent up to O(1) to the logarithm of a holomorphic ideal. Therefore S has the required rational analytic singularity near q and no generic pole on E. Adding the actual effective current t[E] gives T=S+t[E] in H_epsilon with valuation(t,y), and preserves the rational analytic local type. DRWNXZ Lemma3.1 realizes the same vector in the closure of gentle valuations. Rational points with these strict inequalities are dense in the displayed region. Closure supplies its boundary, including t=s_epsilon and y=a epsilon+t. This proves(1), subject to independent checking of the two explicit imported analytic applications.

The region's actual vertices are(0,0),(s_epsilon,0),(s_epsilon,sqrt(V_epsilon)),(0,a epsilon). Its Euclidean area is

$$\int_0^{s_\epsilon}(a\epsilon+t)\,dt
=\tfrac12(V_\epsilon-a^2\epsilon^2)

The area computation checks the actual mass convention; it is not used to define the body or to certify its geometric proof.

## 4. Scalar and coordinate rescaling

Without rescaling, both coordinates vanish and the limit is the origin. With the actual scalar rescaling epsilon^(-1/2), formula(1) gives the Hausdorff limit

$$\epsilon^{-1/2}\Delta_{E,q}(H_\epsilon)
\longrightarrow \{(x,z):0\leq x\leq\sqrt{2c},\quad0\leq z\leq x\}.
\tag{2}$$

Its affine dimension is2. The retained zero-volume nef class pi*[beta] has numerical dimension1, since its square is0 and it has positive intersection with Omega. Thus the natural scalar rescaling suggested by the first nonzero ambient-volume coefficient produces a two-dimensional triangle in this example. The first-order decay rate of area records the numerical information, while the rescaled body's ordinary affine dimension does not equal it.

More generally multiply the first and second coordinates by epsilon^(-p) and epsilon^(-q), respectively, for fixed real exponents. Each coordinate's maximum before rescaling is asymptotic to sqrt(2c epsilon). A compact finite limit requires p,q<=1/2. If both are strictly smaller, the limit is a point. If both equal1/2, the limit is the triangle(2). If p=1/2 and q<1/2, the limit is the horizontal interval[0,sqrt(2c)] times{0}. If q=1/2 and p<1/2, the limit is the vertical interval{0} times[0,sqrt(2c)]. Exponents greater than1/2 make the corresponding maximum diverge. A one-dimensional interval therefore requires a separate choice of which valuation coordinate survives; it does not arise from a scalar rescaling.

The length/size also remembers the retained first-order perturbation scale through c=integral beta gamma. Replacing Omega by b Omega changes the limiting triangle's side length by sqrt(b). If the first-order reference class is fixed, perturbations of gamma and a tending to those same data have the same limit by(1); no independence across different unretained first-order scales is asserted. These facts specify what additional reference and carrier choices a positive construction must address. They do not disprove every possible richer P-sensitive construction.
