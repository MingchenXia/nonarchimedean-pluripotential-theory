# 3. A topology on actual potentials and actual root homeomorphisms

Write $\mathcal P_\theta$ for normalized **actual** PSH restrictions on $K$. For each actual $\psi\in\mathrm{CPSH}(\theta)$ define
\[
S_\psi(u)=\sup_K(u-\psi)\in\mathbb R.
\]

**Lemma 3.1.** $\tau_\theta$ is Hausdorff, $E_\theta$ is upper semicontinuous in it, and every normalized raw-root bijection is a homeomorphism
\[
\mathcal R_{\eta\xi}:(\mathcal P_\eta,\tau_\eta)\longrightarrow
                     (\mathcal P_\xi,\tau_\xi).                         \tag{3.1}
\]
This lemma holds on every original cone; it asserts no compactness in general.

For separation, if $u(x)>v(x)$ at some $x\in K$, take a genuine decreasing CPSH defining net $\psi_j\downarrow v$. Some $\psi_j(x)<u(x)$, including when $v(x)=-\infty$. Then $S_{\psi_j}(u)>0$ and $S_{\psi_j}(v)\le0$. If distinct restrictions have the opposite inequality, exchange them. Equivariance and torification determine the full potential from the restriction, so this also separates actual normalized potentials.

For upper semicontinuity, if $E_\theta(u)<c$, choose a genuine continuous majorant $\psi\ge u$ with $E_\theta(\psi)<c-\epsilon$. The neighborhood $S_\psi(v)<\epsilon$ gives $v\le\psi+\epsilon$ and hence $E_\theta(v)\le E_\theta(\psi)+\epsilon<c$. This works also at energy $-\infty$. Thus $J_\theta$ is lower semicontinuous on the normalized class.

We prove (3.1) using strict global order, rather than pointwise convergence on a selected valuation set. For $\psi\in\mathrm{CPSH}(\xi)$ and $t\in\mathbb R$, put
\[
g_t=\mathcal R_{\xi\eta}(\psi+t)\in\mathrm{CPSH}(\eta).
\]
Raw order equivalence, including unbounded values, gives
\[
S_\psi(\mathcal R_{\eta\xi}u)\le t\ \Longleftrightarrow\ S_{g_t}(u)\le0.   \tag{3.2}
\]
There is also equivalence with strict $<$ on the left and strict $<0$ on the right. Indeed a uniform negative gap on one side remains a uniform negative gap after applying the inverse root: by (2.3), a constant shift $c>0$ changes every root between $c/b$ and $c/a$. The reverse map has degree ratios $1/b,1/a$. Therefore strictly negative suprema cannot become zero. Strict $>t$ corresponds to strict $>0$ by the non-strict equivalence. Both strict sublevel and strict superlevel preimages in (3.2) are open in $\tau_\eta$, proving continuity. Apply the same argument to the inverse. No strong $I$ continuity is inferred.

# 5. A finite-grid and tail proof of nonlinear-root $L^1$ continuity

**Lemma 5.1.** Let $u_i,u\in\mathcal E^1(\eta)$ be normalized actual potentials and let $\mu$ have finite energy. Suppose
\[
u_i\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1(\mu),\qquad
u_i\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1(\mu_r)\quad\text{for every rational }r\le0.  \tag{5.1}
\]
Then the actual normalized roots satisfy
\[
\mathcal R_{\eta\xi}u_i\longrightarrow\mathcal R_{\eta\xi}u
                       \text{ in }L^1(\mu).                           \tag{5.2}
\]
This holds for arbitrary nets, every original cone, and fixed rational or irrational fields. The hypotheses of (5.1) will be proved, rather than assumed, on the smooth-quotient subdomain in Section 6.

Put $R_i=\mathcal R_{\eta\xi}u_i$, $R=\mathcal R_{\eta\xi}u$. On $\mu$-almost every point relevant to any fixed comparison, the orbit functions
\[
G_i(r,x)=u_i(x+r\xi)=u_i(T_r x)+\rho_\eta(x+r\xi),\qquad G(r,x)=u(x+r\xi)
\]
are finite. Their slopes lie between $-b$ and $-a$. The null sets may depend on $i$; each estimate below concerns only finitely many functions for that $i$, so no common null set for an uncountable net is needed. Since $G_i(0,x)=u_i(x)\le0$, the roots are nonpositive and
\[
0\le -R_i(x)\le -u_i(x)/a,
\qquad 0\le -R(x)\le -u(x)/a.                              \tag{5.3}
\]

Fix a positive rational $M$ and a rational mesh $h=M/N$. On $[-M,0]$ use the finite grid $r_j=-M+jh$, $0\le j\le N$. The orbit Lipschitz bound gives
\[
\sup_{-M\le r\le0}|G_i(r,x)-G(r,x)|
 \le\max_j|G_i(r_j,x)-G(r_j,x)|+2bh.
\]
For two decreasing functions with downward slope at least $a$, inverse zero parameters, clipped to $[-M,0]$, differ by at most their sup-norm difference divided by $a$. To check boundary cases, if a clipped root is $-M$, the value at $-M$ is nonpositive; if the other clipped root is interior, its slope inequality bounds their distance by the discrepancy at $-M$. The same argument at zero and at two interior roots proves the assertion. Thus, writing $R_i^M=\max(R_i,-M)$,
\[
\int|R_i^M-R^M|\,d\mu
 \le a^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^N\|u_i-u\|_{L^1(\mu_{r_j})}+2bh/a.                \tag{5.4}
\]
The finite reference terms cancel. First let the net index tend to its limit with this finite grid fixed; then let $N\to\infty$. This proves clipped $L^1$ convergence for each fixed $M$.

The actual tail estimate, stronger than a bound on norms, is
\[
\int|R_i-R_i^M|\,d\mu\le a^{-1}\int(-u_i-aM)_+\,d\mu.                  \tag{5.5}
\]
It follows pointwise from (5.3). For fixed $M$, the right-hand integral converges to the one with $u$, since $z\mapsto(-z-aM)_+$ is 1-Lipschitz and (5.1) holds at $r=0$. Combining (5.4)--(5.5),
\[
\limsup_i\|R_i-R\|_{L^1(\mu)}
 \le \frac2a\int(-u-aM)_+\,d\mu.                               \tag{5.6}
\]
The last quantity tends to zero by integrability of the fixed actual $u$. This proves (5.2), with tail control and the order of limits explicit. It does not exchange an unbounded varying test with a weakly convergent measure and does not claim an unbounded moving-Reeb mixed-current theorem.
