L16: ## 53.2 Curves and function fields
L17: 
L18: In this section we elaborate on the results of Varieties, Section 【11†33.4】 in the case of curves.
L19: 
L20: 【12†Lemma 53.2.1.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be a curve and $Y$ a proper variety. Let $U \subset X$ be a nonempty open and let $f : U \to Y$ be a morphism. If $x \in X$ is a closed point such that $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is a discrete valuation ring, then there exist an open $U \subset U' \subset X$ containing $x$ and a morphism of varieties $f' : U' \to Y$ extending $f$.
L21: Proof. This is a special case of Morphisms, Lemma 【13†29.43.5】. $\square$
L22: 
L23: 【14†Lemma 53.2.2.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be a normal curve and $Y$ a proper variety. The set of rational maps from $X$ to $Y$ is the same as the set of morphisms $X \to Y$.
L24: Proof. A rational map from $X$ to $Y$ can be extended to a morphism $X \to Y$ by Lemma 【12†53.2.1】 as every local ring is a discrete valuation ring (for example by Varieties, Lemma 【15†33.43.8】). Conversely, if two morphisms $f,g: X \to Y$ are equivalent as rational maps, then $f = g$ by Morphisms, Lemma 【16†29.7.10】. $\square$
L25: 
L26: 【17†Lemma 53.2.3.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $f : X \to Y$ be a nonconstant morphism of curves over $k$. If $Y$ is normal, then $f$ is flat.
L27: Proof. Pick $x \in X$ mapping to $y \in Y$. Then $\mathcal{O}_{Y, y}$ is either a field or a discrete valuation ring (Varieties, Lemma 【15†33.43.8】). Since $f$ is nonconstant it is dominant (as it must map the generic point of $X$ to the generic point of $Y$). This implies that $\mathcal{O}_{Y, y} \to \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is injective (Morphisms, Lemma 【18†29.8.8】).
L28: Hence $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is torsion free as a $\mathcal{O}_{Y, y}$-module and therefore $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is flat as a $\mathcal{O}_{Y, y}$-module by More on Algebra, Lemma 【19†15.22.10】. $\square$
L29: 【20†Lemma 53.2.4.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $f : X \to Y$ be a morphism of schemes over $k$. Assume
L30: 
L31:   1. $Y$ is separated over $k$,
L32: 
L33:   2. $X$ is proper of dimension $\leq 1$ over $k$,
L34: 
L35:   3. $f(Z)$ has at least two points for every irreducible component $Z \subset X$ of dimension $1$.
L36: 
L37: Then $f$ is finite.
L38: Proof. The morphism $f$ is proper by Morphisms, Lemma 【21†29.42.7】. Thus $f(X)$ is closed and images of closed points are closed. Let $y \in Y$ be the image of a closed point in $X$. Then $f^{-1}(\{ y\} )$ is a closed subset of $X$ not containing any of the generic points of irreducible components of dimension $1$ by condition (3). It follows that $f^{-1}(\{ y\} )$ is finite.
L39: Hence $f$ is finite over an open neighbourhood of $y$ by More on Morphisms, Lemma 【22†37.44.2】 (if $Y$ is Noetherian, then you can use the easier Cohomology of Schemes, Lemma 【23†30.21.2】). Since we've seen above that there are enough of these points $y$, the proof is complete. $\square$
L40: 【24†Lemma 53.2.5.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X \to Y$ be a morphism of varieties with $Y$ proper and $X$ a curve. There exists a factorization $X \to \overline{X} \to Y$ where $X \to \overline{X}$ is an open immersion and $\overline{X}$ is a projective curve.
L41: 
L42: Proof. This is clear from Lemma 【12†53.2.1】 and Varieties, Lemma 【25†33.43.6】. $\square$
L43: Here is the main theorem of this section. We will say a morphism $f : X \to Y$ of varieties is constant if the image $f(X)$ consists of a single point $y$ of $Y$. If this happens then $y$ is a closed point of $Y$ (since the image of a closed point of $X$ will be a closed point of $Y$).
L44: 
L45: 【26†Theorem 53.2.6.】 Let $k$ be a field. The following categories are canonically equivalent
L46: 
L47:   1. The category of finitely generated field extensions $K/k$ of transcendence degree $1$.
L48:   2. The category of curves and dominant rational maps.
L49: 
L50:   3. The category of normal projective curves and nonconstant morphisms.
L51: 
L52:   4. The category of nonsingular projective curves and nonconstant morphisms.
L53: 
L54:   5. The category of regular projective curves and nonconstant morphisms.
L55: 
L56:   6. The category of normal proper curves and nonconstant morphisms.
L57: Proof. The equivalence between categories (1) and (2) is the restriction of the equivalence of Varieties, Theorem 【27†33.4.1】. Namely, a variety is a curve if and only if its function field has transcendence degree $1$, see for example Varieties, Lemma 【28†33.20.3】.
L58: The categories in (3), (4), (5), and (6) are the same. First of all, the terms “regular” and “nonsingular” are synonyms, see Properties, Definition 【29†28.9.1】. Being normal and regular are the same thing for Noetherian $1$-dimensional schemes (Properties, Lemmas 【30†28.9.4】 and 【31†28.12.6】). See Varieties, Lemma 【15†33.43.8】 for the case of curves. Thus (3) is the same as (5). Finally, (6) is the same as (3) by Varieties, Lemma 【32†33.43.4】.
L59: If $f : X \to Y$ is a nonconstant morphism of nonsingular projective curves, then $f$ sends the generic point $\eta $ of $X$ to the generic point $\xi $ of $Y$. Hence we obtain a morphism $k(Y) = \mathcal{O}_{Y, \xi } \to \mathcal{O}_{X, \eta } = k(X)$ in the category (1). If two morphisms $f,g: X \to Y$ gives the same morphism $k(Y) \to k(X)$, then by the equivalence between (1) and (2), $f$ and $g$ are equivalent as rational maps, so $f=g$ by Lemma 【14†53.2.2】.
L60: Conversely, suppose that we have a map $k(Y) \to k(X)$ in the category (1). Then we obtain a morphism $U \to Y$ for some nonempty open $U \subset X$. By Lemma 【12†53.2.1】 this extends to all of $X$ and we obtain a morphism in the category (5). Thus we see that there is a fully faithful functor (5)$\to $(1).
L61: To finish the proof we have to show that every $K/k$ in (1) is the function field of a normal projective curve. We already know that $K = k(X)$ for some curve $X$. After replacing $X$ by its normalization (which is a variety birational to $X$) we may assume $X$ is normal (Varieties, Lemma 【33†33.27.1】). Then we choose $X \to \overline{X}$ with $\overline{X} \setminus X = \{ x_1, \ldots , x_ n\} $ as in Varieties, Lemma 【25†33.43.6】.
L62: Since $X$ is normal and since each of the local rings $\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}, x_ i}$ is normal we conclude that $\overline{X}$ is a normal projective curve as desired. (Remark: We can also first compactify using Varieties, Lemma 【34†33.43.5】 and then normalize using Varieties, Lemma 【33†33.27.1】. Doing it this way we avoid using the somewhat tricky Morphisms, Lemma 【35†29.54.16】.) $\square$
L63: 【36†Definition 53.2.7.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be a curve. A nonsingular projective model of $X$ is a pair $(Y, \varphi )$ where $Y$ is a nonsingular projective curve and $\varphi : k(X) \to k(Y)$ is an isomorphism of function fields.
L64: A nonsingular projective model is determined up to unique isomorphism by Theorem 【26†53.2.6】. Thus we often say “the nonsingular projective model”. We usually drop $\varphi $ from the notation. Warning: it needn't be the case that $Y$ is smooth over $k$ but Lemma 【37†53.2.8】 shows this can only happen in positive characteristic.
L65: 
L66: 【37†Lemma 53.2.8.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be a curve and let $Y$ be the nonsingular projective model of $X$. If $k$ is perfect, then $Y$ is a smooth projective curve.
L67: Proof. See Varieties, Lemma 【15†33.43.8】 for example. $\square$
L68: 
L69: 【38†Lemma 53.2.9.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be a geometrically irreducible curve over $k$. For a field extension $K/k$ denote $Y_ K$ a nonsingular projective model of $(X_ K)_{red}$.
L70: 
L71:   1. If $X$ is proper, then $Y_ K$ is the normalization of $X_ K$.
L72: 
L73:   2. There exists $K/k$ finite purely inseparable such that $Y_ K$ is smooth.
L74: 
L75:   3. Whenever $Y_ K$ is smooth【39†^{1}】 we have $H^0(Y_ K, \mathcal{O}_{Y_ K}) = K$.
L76:   4. Given a commutative diagram
L77: 
L78: \[ \xymatrix{ \Omega & K' \ar[l] \\ K \ar[u] & k \ar[l] \ar[u] } \]
L79: 
L80: of fields such that $Y_ K$ and $Y_{K'}$ are smooth, then $Y_\Omega = (Y_ K)_\Omega = (Y_{K'})_\Omega $.
L81: 
L82: Proof. Let $X'$ be a nonsingular projective model of $X$. Then $X'$ and $X$ have isomorphic nonempty open subschemes. In particular $X'$ is geometrically irreducible as $X$ is (some details omitted). Thus we may assume that $X$ is projective.
L83: Assume $X$ is proper. Then $X_ K$ is proper and hence the normalization $(X_ K)^\nu $ is proper as a scheme finite over a proper scheme (Varieties, Lemma 【33†33.27.1】 and Morphisms, Lemmas 【40†29.45.11】 and 【41†29.42.4】). On the other hand, $X_ K$ is irreducible as $X$ is geometrically irreducible. Hence $X_ K^\nu $ is proper, normal, irreducible, and birational to $(X_ K)_{red}$. This proves (1) because a proper curve is projective (Varieties, Lemma 【32†33.43.4】).
