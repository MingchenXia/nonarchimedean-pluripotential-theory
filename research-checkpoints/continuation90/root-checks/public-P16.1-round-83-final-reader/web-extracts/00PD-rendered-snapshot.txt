L24: 【8†Lemma 10.119.7.】 Let $A$ be a ring. The following are equivalent.
L25:   1. The ring $A$ is a discrete valuation ring.
L26: 
L27:   2. The ring $A$ is a valuation ring and Noetherian but not a field.
L28: 
L29:   3. The ring $A$ is a regular local ring of dimension $1$.
L30: 
L31:   4. The ring $A$ is a Noetherian local domain with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ generated by a single nonzero element.
L32: 
L33:   5. The ring $A$ is a Noetherian local normal domain of dimension $1$.
L34: In this case if $\pi $ is a generator of the maximal ideal of $A$, then every nonzero element of $A$ can be uniquely written as $u\pi ^ n$, where $u \in A$ is a unit.
L35: Proof. The equivalence of (1) and (2) is Lemma 【15†10.50.18】. Moreover, in the proof of Lemma 【15†10.50.18】 we saw that if $A$ is a discrete valuation ring, then $A$ is a PID, hence (3). Note that a regular local ring is a domain (see Lemma 【16†10.106.2】). Using this the equivalence of (3) and (4) follows from dimension theory, see Section 【17†10.60】.
L36: Assume (3) and let $\pi $ be a generator of the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. For all $n \geq 0$ we have $\dim _{A/\mathfrak m} \mathfrak m^ n/\mathfrak m^{n + 1} = 1$ because it is generated by $\pi ^ n$ (and it cannot be zero). In particular $\mathfrak m^ n = (\pi ^ n)$ and the graded ring $\bigoplus \mathfrak m^ n/\mathfrak m^{n + 1}$ is isomorphic to the polynomial ring $A/\mathfrak m[T]$. For $x \in A \setminus \{ 0\} $ define $v(x) = \max \{ n \mid x \in \mathfrak m^ n\} $.
L37: In other words $x = u \pi ^{v(x)}$ with $u \in A^*$. By the remarks above we have $v(xy) = v(x) + v(y)$ for all $x, y \in A \setminus \{ 0\} $. We extend this to the field of fractions $K$ of $A$ by setting $v(a/b) = v(a) - v(b)$ (well defined by multiplicativity shown above). Then it is clear that $A$ is the set of elements of $K$ which have valuation $\geq 0$. Hence we see that $A$ is a valuation ring by Lemma 【18†10.50.16】.
L38: A valuation ring is a normal domain by Lemma 【19†10.50.3】. Hence we see that the equivalent conditions (1) – (3) imply (5). Assume (5). Suppose that $\mathfrak m$ cannot be generated by $1$ element to get a contradiction. Then Lemma 【20†10.119.3】 implies there is a finite ring map $A \to A'$ which is an isomorphism after inverting any nonzero element of $\mathfrak m$ but not an isomorphism. In particular we may identify $A'$ with a subset of the fraction field of $A$.
L39: Since $A \to A'$ is finite it is integral (see Lemma 【21†10.36.3】). Since $A$ is normal we get $A = A'$ a contradiction. $\square$
