L16: ## 33.27 Normalization
L17: 
L18: Some issues associated to normalization.
L19: 
L20: 【11†Lemma 33.27.1.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be a locally algebraic scheme over $k$. Let $\nu : X^\nu \to X$ be the normalization morphism, see Morphisms, Definition 【12†29.55.1】. Then
L21: 
L22:   1. $\nu $ is finite, dominant, and $X^\nu $ is a disjoint union of normal irreducible locally algebraic schemes over $k$,
L23: 
L24:   2. $\nu $ factors as $X^\nu \to X_{red} \to X$ and the first morphism is the normalization morphism of $X_{red}$,
L25:   3. if $X$ is a reduced algebraic scheme, then $\nu $ is birational,
L26: 
L27:   4. if $X$ is a variety, then $X^\nu $ is a variety and $\nu $ is a finite birational morphism of varieties.
L28: Proof. Since $X$ is locally of finite type over a field, we see that $X$ is locally Noetherian (Morphisms, Lemma 【13†29.15.6】) hence every quasi-compact open has finitely many irreducible components (Properties, Lemma 【14†28.5.7】). Thus Morphisms, Definition 【12†29.55.1】 applies. The normalization $X^\nu $ is always a disjoint union of normal integral schemes and the normalization morphism $\nu $ is always dominant, see Morphisms, Lemma 【15†29.55.5】.
L29: Since $X$ is universally Nagata (Morphisms, Lemma 【16†29.18.2】) we see that $\nu $ is finite (Morphisms, Lemma 【17†29.55.11】). Hence $X^\nu $ is locally algebraic too. At this point we have proved (1).
L30: Part (2) is Morphisms, Lemma 【18†29.55.2】.
L31: 
L32: Part (3) is Morphisms, Lemma 【19†29.55.7】.
L33: 
L34: Part (4) follows from (1), (2), (3), and the fact that $X^\nu $ is separated as a scheme finite over a separated scheme. $\square$
L35: 
L36: 【20†Lemma 33.27.2.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be a proper scheme over $k$. Let $\nu : X^\nu \to X$ be the normalization morphism, see Morphisms, Definition 【12†29.55.1】. Then $X^\nu $ is proper over $k$. If $X$ is projective over $k$, then $X^\nu $ is projective over $k$.
L37: Proof. By Lemma 【11†33.27.1】 the morphism $\nu $ is finite. Hence $X^\nu $ is proper over $k$ by Morphisms, Lemmas 【21†29.45.11】 and 【22†29.42.4】. The morphism $\nu $ is projective by Morphisms, Lemma 【23†29.45.16】 and hence if $X$ is projective over $k$, then $X^\nu $ is projective over $k$ by Morphisms, Lemma 【24†29.44.14】. $\square$
L38: 【25†Lemma 33.27.3.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $f : Y \to X$ be a quasi-compact morphism of locally algebraic schemes over $k$. Let $X'$ be the normalization of $X$ in $Y$. If $Y$ is reduced, then $X' \to X$ is finite.
L39: Proof. Since $Y$ is quasi-separated (by Properties, Lemma 【26†28.5.4】 and Morphisms, Lemma 【13†29.15.6】) the morphism $f$ is quasi-separated (Schemes, Lemma 【27†26.21.13】). Hence Morphisms, Definition 【28†29.54.3】 applies. The result follows from Morphisms, Lemma 【29†29.54.14】. This uses that locally algebraic schemes are locally Noetherian (hence have locally finitely many irreducible components) and that locally algebraic schemes are Nagata (Morphisms, Lemma 【16†29.18.2】). Some small details omitted.
L40: $\square$
L41: 【30†Lemma 33.27.4.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be an algebraic $k$-scheme. Then there exists a finite purely inseparable extension $k'/k$ such that the normalization $Y$ of $X_{k'}$ is geometrically normal over $k'$.
L42: Proof. Let $K = k^{perf}$ be the perfect closure. Let $Y_ K$ be the normalization of $X_ K$, see Lemma 【11†33.27.1】. By Limits, Lemma 【31†32.10.1】 there exists a finite sub extension $K/k'/k$ and a morphism $\nu : Y \to X_{k'}$ of finite presentation whose base change to $K$ is the normalization morphism $\nu _ K : Y_ K \to X_ K$. Observe that $Y$ is geometrically normal over $k'$ (Lemma 【32†33.10.3】). After increasing $k'$ we may assume $Y \to X_{k'}$ is finite (Limits, Lemma 【33†32.8.3】).
L43: Since $\nu _ K : Y_ K \to X_ K$ is the normalization morphism, it induces a birational morphism $Y_ K \to (X_ K)_{red}$. Hence there is a dense open $V_ K \subset X_ K$ such that $\nu _ K^{-1}(V_ K) \to V_ K$ is a closed immersion (inducing an isomorphism of $\nu _ K^{-1}(V_ K)$ with $V_{K, red}$, see for example Morphisms, Lemma 【34†29.52.6】).
L44: After increasing $k'$ we find $V_ K$ is the base change of a dense open $V \subset Y$ and the morphism $\nu ^{-1}(V) \to V$ is a closed immersion (Limits, Lemmas 【35†32.4.11】 and 【36†32.8.5】). It follows readily from this that $\nu $ is the normalization morphism and the proof is complete. $\square$
L45: 【37†Lemma 33.27.5.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be a locally algebraic $k$-scheme. Let $K/k$ be an extension of fields. Let $\nu : X^\nu \to X$ be the normalization of $X$ and let $Y^\nu \to X_ K$ be the normalization of the base change. Then the canonical morphism
L46: 
L47: \[ Y^\nu \longrightarrow X^\nu \times _{\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(k)} \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(K) \]
L48: 
L49: is an isomorphism if $K/k$ is separable and a universal homeomorphism in general.
L50: Proof. Set $Y = X_ K$. Let $X^{(0)}$, resp. $Y^{(0)}$ be the set of generic points of irreducible components of $X$, resp. $Y$. Then the projection morphism $\pi : Y \to X$ satisfies $\pi (Y^{(0)}) = X^{(0)}$. This is true because $\pi $ is surjective, open, and generizing, see Morphisms, Lemmas 【38†29.24.4】 and 【39†29.24.5】. If we view $X^{(0)}$, resp. $Y^{(0)}$ as (reduced) schemes, then $X^\nu $, resp. $Y^\nu $ is the normalization of $X$, resp. $Y$ in $X^{(0)}$, resp. $Y^{(0})$.
L51: Thus Morphisms, Lemma 【40†29.54.5】 gives a canonical morphism $Y^\nu \to X^\nu $ over $Y \to X$ which in turn gives the canonical morphism of the lemma by the universal property of the fibre product.
L52: To prove this morphism has the properties stated in the lemma we may assume $X = \mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)$ is affine. Let $Q(A_{red})$ be the total ring of fractions of $A_{red}$. Then $X^\nu $ is the spectrum of the integral closure $A'$ of $A$ in $Q(A_{red})$, see Morphisms, Lemmas 【18†29.55.2】 and 【41†29.55.3】. Similarly, $Y^\nu $ is the spectrum of the integral closure $B'$ of $A \otimes _ k K$ in $Q((A \otimes _ k K)_{red})$.
L53: There is a canonical map $Q(A_{red}) \to Q((A \otimes _ k K)_{red})$, a canonical map $A' \to B'$, and the morphism of the lemma corresponds to the induced map
L54: \[ A' \otimes _ k K \longrightarrow B' \]
L55: 
L56: of $K$-algebras. The kernel consists of nilpotent elements as the kernel of $Q(A_{red}) \otimes _ k K \to Q((A \otimes _ k K)_{red})$ is the set of nilpotent elements.
L57: 
L58: If $K/k$ is separable, then $A' \otimes _ k K$ is normal by Lemma 【42†33.10.6】. In particular it is reduced, whence $Q((A \otimes _ k K)_{red}) = Q(A' \otimes _ k K)$ and $B' = A' \otimes _ k K$ by Algebra, Lemma 【43†10.37.16】.
L59: Assume $K/k$ is not separable. Then the characteristic of $k$ is $p > 0$. We will show that for every $b \in B'$ there is a power $q$ of $p$ such that $b^ q$ is in the image of $A' \otimes _ k K$. This will prove that the displayed map is a universal homeomorphism by Algebra, Lemma 【44†10.46.7】. For a given $b$ there is a subfield $F \subset K$ with $F/k$ finitely generated such that $b$ is contained in $Q((A \otimes _ k F)_{red})$ and is integral over $A \otimes _ k F$.
L60: Choose a monic polynomial $P = T^ d + \alpha _1 T^{d - 1} + \ldots + \alpha _ d$ with $P(b) = 0$ and $\alpha _ i \in A \otimes _ k F$. Choose a transcendence basis $t_1, \ldots , t_ r$ for $F$ over $k$. Let $F/F'/k(t_1, \ldots , t_ r)$ be the maximal separable subextension (Fields, Lemma 【45†9.14.6】). Since $F/F'$ is finite purely inseparable, there is a $q$ such that $\lambda ^ q \in F'$ for all $\lambda \in F$.
L61: Then $b^ q$ is in $Q((A \otimes _ k F')_{red})$ and satisfies the polynomial $T^ d + \alpha _1^ q T^{d - 1} + \ldots + \alpha _ d^ q$ with $\alpha _ i^ q \in A \otimes _ k F'$. By the separable case we see that $b^ q \in A' \otimes _ k F'$ and the proof is complete. $\square$
L62: 【46†Lemma 33.27.6.】 Let $k$ be a field. Let $X$ be a locally algebraic $k$-scheme. Let $\nu : X^\nu \to X$ be the normalization of $X$. Let $x \in X$ be a point such that (a) $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is reduced, (b) $\dim (\mathcal{O}_{X, x}) = 1$, and (c) for every $x' \in X^\nu $ with $\nu (x') = x$ the extension $\kappa (x')/k$ is separable. Then $X$ is geometrically reduced at $x$ and $X^\nu $ is geometrically regular at $x'$ with $\nu (x') = x$.
L63: Proof. We will use the results of Lemma 【11†33.27.1】 without further mention. Let $x' \in X^\nu $ be a point over $x$. By dimension theory (Section 【47†33.20】) we have $\dim (\mathcal{O}_{X^\nu , x'}) = 1$. Since $X^\nu $ is normal, we see that $\mathcal{O}_{X^\nu , x'}$ is a discrete valuation ring (Properties, Lemma 【48†28.12.5】). Thus $\mathcal{O}_{X^\nu , x'}$ is a regular local $k$-algebra whose residue field is separable over $k$.
L64: Hence $k \to \mathcal{O}_{X^\nu , x'}$ is formally smooth in the $\mathfrak m_{x'}$-adic topology, see More on Algebra, Lemma 【49†15.39.5】. Then $\mathcal{O}_{X^\nu , x'}$ is geometrically regular over $k$ by More on Algebra, Theorem 【50†15.41.1】. Thus $X^\nu $ is geometrically regular at $x'$ by Lemma 【51†33.12.2】.
L65: Since $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is reduced, the family of maps $\mathcal{O}_{X, x} \to \mathcal{O}_{X^\nu , x'}$ is injective. Since $\mathcal{O}_{X^\nu , x'}$ is a geometrically reduced $k$-algebra, it follows immediately that $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is a geometrically reduced $k$-algebra. Hence $X$ is geometrically reduced at $x$ by Lemma 【52†33.6.2】. $\square$
