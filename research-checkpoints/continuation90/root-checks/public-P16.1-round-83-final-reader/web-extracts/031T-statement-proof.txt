L23: 【8†Lemma 10.157.6.】 Let $R$ be a Noetherian normal domain with fraction field $K$. Then
L24:   1. for any nonzero $a \in R$ the quotient $R/aR$ has no embedded primes, and all its associated primes have height $1$
L25: 
L26:   2. \[ R = \bigcap \nolimits _{\text{height}(\mathfrak p) = 1} R_{\mathfrak p} \]
L27: 
L28:   3. For any nonzero $x \in K$ the quotient $R/(R \cap xR)$ has no embedded primes, and all its associates primes have height $1$.
L29: Proof. By Lemma 【14†10.157.4】 we see that $R$ has $(S_2)$. Hence for any nonzero element $a \in R$ we see that $R/aR$ has $(S_1)$ (use Lemma 【15†10.72.6】 for example) Hence $R/aR$ has no embedded primes (Lemma 【16†10.157.2】). We conclude the associated primes of $R/aR$ are exactly the minimal primes $\mathfrak p$ over $(a)$, which have height $1$ as $a$ is not zero (Lemma 【17†10.60.11】). This proves (1).
L30: Thus, given $b \in R$ we have $b \in aR$ if and only if $b \in aR_{\mathfrak p}$ for every minimal prime $\mathfrak p$ over $(a)$ (see Lemma 【18†10.63.19】). These primes all have height $1$ as seen above so $b/a \in R$ if and only if $b/a \in R_{\mathfrak p}$ for all height 1 primes. Hence (2) holds.
L31: For (3) write $x = a/b$. Let $\mathfrak p_1, \ldots , \mathfrak p_ r$ be the minimal primes over $(ab)$. These all have height 1 by the above. Then we see that $R \cap xR = \bigcap _{i = 1, \ldots , r} (R \cap xR_{\mathfrak p_ i})$ by part (2) of the lemma. Hence $R/(R \cap xR)$ is a submodule of $\bigoplus R/(R \cap xR_{\mathfrak p_ i})$.
L32: As $R_{\mathfrak p_ i}$ is a discrete valuation ring (by property $(R_1)$ for the Noetherian normal domain $R$, see Lemma 【14†10.157.4】) we have $xR_{\mathfrak p_ i} = \mathfrak p_ i^{e_ i}R_{\mathfrak p_ i}$ for some $e_ i \in \mathbf{Z}$. Hence the direct sum is equal to $\bigoplus _{e_ i > 0} R/\mathfrak p_ i^{(e_ i)}$, see Definition 【19†10.64.1】. By Lemma 【20†10.64.2】 the only associated prime of the module $R/\mathfrak p^{(n)}$ is $\mathfrak p$.
L33: Hence the set of associate primes of $R/(R \cap xR)$ is a subset of $\{ \mathfrak p_ i\} $ and there are no inclusion relations among them. This proves (3). $\square$
