# Direct curve Gauss limits for the whole-base trace

This is an unreviewed proof draft, not a solved original problem or an accepted dependency. It combines the actual norm-law draft in round77, Section8, with that round's unreviewed smooth upper-growth and principal-open removal draft, Section7. The independent reviews80/81 are frozen and receive no feedback from this draft.

## 1. Exact domain and binding antecedents

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed, trivially valued and of characteristic zero, with no cardinality restriction. Let \(S=D(a)\subset\mathbb A_k^n\) be a nonempty principal affine open, \(Y\) an irreducible reduced affine \(k\)-variety, and \(\pi:Y\to S\) finite surjective of generic degree \(d\). Let \(u:Y^{an}\to\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}\) be usc, locally bounded above and finite at the generic trivial point. Retain ONE proper birational witness \(\mu:P\to Y\) on which \(u\circ\mu^{an}\) is locally a decreasing limit of the precise finite rational contact weights of round77. Its continuous supports have the same constant in the supporting inequality and equality.

Assume an actual upper growth bound \(u\le Cg\circ\pi+B\), for an actual AFS exhaustion \(g\) of the original \(S\), and positive \(C\). This upper-bound hypothesis is retained literally. The printed lower-growth convention is not substituted for it.

The following earlier assertions are explicit unreviewed antecedents: (A) round77's smooth affine upper-growth regularization and exact principal-open removal, with its geometric compactification and projective-domination/resolution hypotheses; (B) its generic norm polynomial law over the normal base ring, strictly positive integer residue characters, completed-field compatibility and exact finite AFS trace formulas; (C) the actual ordinary/local contact class and curve restriction mechanism. No preprint envelope theorem, singular EP, general affine theorem over arbitrary valued completed residue fields, or inverse equation is granted.

Conditional on A–C, the claim here is that the actual positive-weight trace
\[
N_\pi u(s)=\sum_{y\in\pi^{-1}(s)}\Gamma_y u(y),\qquad
\Gamma_y\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\quad\sum_y\Gamma_y=d,
\]
is the actual AFS-regularizable extension furnished by A at EVERY base point, including nonflat and removed points. All values on the original variety and the same original witness are retained.

## 2. The extension on the base is constructed first

Shrink to one principal open \(W=D(f)\subset S\) where \(\pi\) is finite étale and \(\mu\) is an isomorphism over the source. Such a nonempty open exists by characteristic zero, generic flatness/separability and the proper closed image of the complement of the witness's isomorphism locus. \(U=\pi^{-1}(W)\) is smooth affine. Apply A to the restriction of that SAME witness and input on \(U\). The finite-map upper bound is an AFS upper bound there: for original source generators, their monic equations over the original base bound their norms by a constant multiple of the base exhaustion; dominated low branches may be appended with explicit positive rational slopes. Retain these original-generator conversions, rather than changing the algebra.

The actual regularizing AFS sequence on \(U\), followed by B's finite-flat polynomial Gauss formula, gives an actual decreasing AFS sequence for \(N_\pi u\) on \(W\). Its limit has the same upper bound \(Cd\,g+B'\), since the positive weights sum to \(d\). Apply A's principal-open removal on the SMOOTH original \(S\) to obtain an actual AFS-regularizable \(v\) on \(S\) with
\[
v=N_\pi u\quad\hbox{on }W^{an}.
\]
No value of \(N_\pi u\) on \(S\setminus W\) is inferred at this stage. It is exactly those values that the next steps identify. Existence and exact geometry in A remain antecedents, not conclusions of this note.

## 3. One actual line through a prescribed analytic point

Fix arbitrary \(s\in S^{an}\), and put \(F=\mathcal H(s)\). The original coordinates give \(\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)\in F^n\), a rational \(F\)-point over \(s\). Choose \(z_0\in W(k)\) outside the finitely many additional closed bad images needed above. Set
\[
c(t)=\alpha+t(z_0-\alpha).
\]
This is an actual affine line over \(F\), restricted to the open parameter locus mapping into \(S_F\); it contains \(t=0\) and \(t=1\). If \(z_0=\alpha\), the prescribed point already belongs to \(W\) and needs no extension argument. Otherwise the line is nonconstant. Because the value at1 is good, the line is generically in \(W_F\). Each inverted defining polynomial has a nonzero restriction to this line. Consequently every positive-radius Gauss point \(\zeta_r\) on the parameter line, sufficiently near0, maps to \(W^{an}\). Its image \(s_r\) tends to \(s\).

The scheme-theoretic inverse image of the line can be nonreduced and nonflat. Reduce it and normalize it, writing \(q:C^\nu\to c(\mathbb A_F^1)\) and \(p:C^\nu\to Y_F\). There are no isolated reduced vertical components: the line is cut by \(n-1\) affine-linear equations in the integral \(n\)-dimensional finite-type \(Y_F\). Krull's height bound and the finite-type dimension formula give dimension at least1 for every minimal component, while finiteness over the line gives dimension at most1. Thus every component is a horizontal curve. The normalization is finite over the line. Each normalized component is a regular curve and hence smooth over the perfect characteristic-zero \(F\); its finite nonconstant map to the regular line is flat. The sum of its generic ranks is \(d\), since over the good open it is the original finite étale base change.

These algebraic inputs have their original hypotheses checked. Primary statements and pertinent proof bodies were read live in [Stacks normalization](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BXQ), [normal curves and flatness](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BXX), [normal curve local smoothness](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B8Y), [height bound](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BBZ), and [dimension formula](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02JT). The live reads are not pinned whole-page copies or proofs of all recursively cited background results.

## 4. The curve lifts to the ORIGINAL witness

Every component of \(C^\nu\) maps generically into the original witness's isomorphism locus. It therefore has a rational lift to \(P_F\) over \(Y_F\). At a closed curve point its local ring is a DVR. Properness of \(\mu_F\) supplies the unique local extension by the valuative criterion. Finite presentation extends that local map to a neighborhood; separatedness identifies overlapping extensions. This produces the lift of the entire curve to the SAME \(P_F\), and then after any complete extension of \(F\). A new witness for each limit or a dense-open/usc value comparison is not used.

The precise relative extension statement and its full pertinent proof are [Stacks29.43.5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BX4). Properness is relative over \(Y_F\); \(P_F\) need not be proper over \(F\).

Now restrict EACH original continuous contact approximant on a neighborhood of the lifted point before taking its decreasing limit. A support \(\sum_j a_j\log|h_j|+b\), with analytic units \(h_j\), pulls back to a harmonic function on the smooth analytic curve. If the pulled continuous weight minus an arbitrary harmonic test has a local maximum, its supporting harmonic function minus that test has the same maximum and is constant locally by the harmonic maximum principle. The weight minus the test is then constant there. Thuillier3.1.12 gives subharmonicity. Decreasing closure3.1.8(ii) gives subharmonicity or the identically-\(-\infty\) alternative on each local connected component. At a point where the limit is finite that alternative is excluded. When the center value is \(-\infty\), usc alone supplies the limit used below.

No current-positivity converse, pullback of an arbitrary current, or AFS regularization theorem over \(F\) occurs. The restriction concerns the actual contact expressions on the original witness.

## 5. Direct radial continuity at the closed centers

If \(F\) has trivial valuation, first adjoin an independent Laurent variable with nontrivial Gauss norm and complete. In either case pass isometrically to a completed algebraic closure \(L\). Its residue characteristic is zero because it contains the trivially valued characteristic-zero \(k\). The finitely many closed points over0 become \(L\)-rational points on the smooth normalized curve. Values pulled from the original \(u\) and \(v\) are unchanged.

The disc coordinate can be obtained directly from a smooth algebraic presentation. At the rational point choose equations \(F_i(z,w)=0\) with the square \(w\)-Jacobian invertible, and translate the point to0. On a sufficiently small polydisc, multiply by the inverse constant Jacobian and make the derivative of the nonlinear remainder in \(w\) have operator norm less than1. For each small \(z\), the map \(w\mapsto w-J^{-1}F(z,w)\) preserves a chosen small closed \(w\)-disc and is a strict contraction. Its iterates converge uniformly, giving analytic \(w(z)\); uniqueness holds also in every complete residue field. Projection to \(z\) and this analytic graph are inverse on the resulting curve neighborhood. Thus the coordinate describes the actual Berkovich disc, not just the completed algebraic local ring. The same contraction argument applies to any analytic \(z'=z+O(z^2)\) after shrinking.

For a rational center \(x\), use a smooth analytic coordinate \(z\) on a sufficiently small disc around it. On a strict closed subdisc \(D_R\), the boundary is the single Gauss point \(\xi_R\). For the pulled limit \(h\), finite at \(x\), the subharmonic comparison definition with a constant harmonic function gives
\[
h(x)\le h(\xi_R).
\]
The boundary value is finite by the strict-domain boundary lemma. Since \(\xi_R\to x\), usc gives the reverse limsup. Hence \(h(\xi_R)\to h(x)\). If \(h(x)=-\infty\), usc gives this directly; locally bounded upper values exclude any \(+\infty\) ambiguity.

This is an actual one-parameter limit on strict discs. Thuillier's pertinent full bodies were read at UTF-8 one-based LF3783–3945, including Definition3.1.5, Proposition3.1.8, Lemma3.1.9 and Corollary3.1.12. The harmonic maximum principle was read at LF3730–3741. The unit harmonic input and its application remain the explicit contact-curve antecedent C. The argument does not use the printed general-\(F\) affine regularization step in Wu6.12.

Apply this both to \(u\circ p\) at every curve center and to \(v\circ c\) at0. The latter is an actual decreasing AFS limit: its finite logarithmic approximants restrict to subharmonic curve functions, and the same comparison and decreasing-limit argument applies.

## 6. Exact Gauss fibers and all weights

At each \(L\)-rational curve center \(x\) over0, the actual finite map has local form
\[
t=b_x z^{e_x}U_x(z),\qquad b_x\ne0,\quad U_x(0)=1.
\]
After shrinking, \(U_x\) has an analytic \(e_x\)-th root: the binomial series converges when \(|U_x-1|<1\), and all integer denominators have norm1 in residue characteristic zero. Replacing \(z\) by \(zU_x^{1/e_x}\) gives the EXACT map \(t=b_x z^{e_x}\), not a leading-order approximation.

For \(r\in|L^\times|\) sufficiently small, its disc map has a unique point over \(\zeta_r\), namely the Gauss point of radius \((r/|b_x|)^{1/e_x}\), with degree \(e_x\). Scale both radii to1. The residue relation is \(Z^{e_x}-T\), irreducible over \(\widetilde L(T)\) by Eisenstein at \(T\); thus the Gauss extension has degree \(e_x\). Properness of the finite curve map ensures all preimages of sufficiently small base discs lie in the union of the finitely many center discs. No branch, center or residue extension is omitted.

For the last neighborhood assertion, fix one small strict closed base disc. Its finite inverse image is compact: finite integral equations bound each algebra generator on the base disc, and the inverse image is the Berkovich spectrum of its finite affinoid algebra. Denominators of the chosen base chart are bounded away from zero after shrinking about0. In that fixed compact inverse image, intersect the preimages of successively smaller closed discs with the closed complement of the union of center neighborhoods. These compact sets decrease to the empty set, because the intersection of the base discs is0 and its entire finite fiber is covered. The finite-intersection property makes one of the sets empty. This proves the whole-fiber neighborhood assertion without discarding points outside the displayed local coordinates.

Before splitting over \(L\), the weights are \(e_x[\kappa(x):F]\). They total \(d\). On the good open their trace is the original finite-flat trace. At0 they coincide with B's canonical norm-law weights, not the possibly larger nonflat fiber dimension: the generic polynomial norm on the finite étale restriction agrees with the product of norms of the normalized horizontal curves. Both coefficient laws are regular at0 and agree generically, hence agree after specialization. The positive residue characters identify their exponents at every original fiber factor. Completed-field compatibility groups the split centers back to each original analytic \(y\). This also proves independence from the chosen good line.

## 7. The actual equality at the formerly omitted point

For the small Gauss radii,
\[
v(s_r)=N_\pi u(s_r)
=\sum_{x\in q^{-1}(0)(L)}e_x\,u\bigl(p(\xi_{x,r})\bigr).
\]
The first equality uses \(s_r\in W^{an}\). The second is the exact finite-flat formula on the normalized line over the good open, with all residue degrees incorporated by the split centers.

Let \(r\downarrow0\) through strict radii in \(|L^\times|\). Section5 identifies each term's limit with \(u(p(x))\), and identifies the left limit with \(v(s)\). The sums are finite and the weights positive. If one center has value \(-\infty\), its term tends to \(-\infty\) while the other finitely many terms remain bounded above locally; otherwise all limits are finite. There is no cancellation of infinities. Section6 groups the center weights into the canonical original \(\Gamma_y\), giving
\[
v(s)=\sum_{y\in\pi^{-1}(s)}\Gamma_yu(y)=N_\pi u(s).
\]
As \(s\) was arbitrary, this identifies the actual extension on the whole base. The argument retains the original witness, the original algebra, the generic degree, every completed residue field, all positive weights and every finite or \(-\infty\) value.
