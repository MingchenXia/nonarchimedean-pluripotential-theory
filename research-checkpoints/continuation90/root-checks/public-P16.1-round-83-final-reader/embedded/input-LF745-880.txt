# Countable-field Runge reconstruction: barriers, uniform pole clearing and split traces

This is a new unreviewed proof draft. It does not close original P16.1. The exact round77 partial proof now has an actual independent round80 PASS, with every conditional geometric antecedent retained. Its complete body and report, rather than an assertion that singular EP is known, are the available background. The direct whole-base curve/Gauss trace in continuation86 remains new mathematics that must be reconstructed independently.

## 1. Domain and dependency ledger

Let \(k\) be a **countable**, algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero field. Let \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}R\) be reduced irreducible affine. Keep its original ring and generators. A weak input \(u\), finite at the generic trivial seminorm, is given on ONE proper birational witness by local decreasing finite contact weights and has an actual upper bound \(u\le Cg+B\), for an original affine FS exhaustion \(g\ge0\).

Three applications still require complete verification: (T) the continuation86 whole-base positive-weight weak trace, including all original and exceptional values; (G) controlled affine FS approximation in the prescribed original reference on a possibly singular affine variety, starting with an already genuine affine FS limit; and (H) the Runge Hartogs criterion in that same reference. The compact rational PL representation and continuous nef/FS bridge needed for G are in the accepted round77/80 scope. Neither singular EP nor an onto map from a resolution is an antecedent.

Below, the pole-clearing estimate is an independent algebraic repair. The split-fiber contradiction is conditional on T and G. The explicit strict barrier removes reliance on an unread strictly-convex extension proposition. H and the singular-stratum projection/local-to-global applications remain tasks for the next native reconstruction.

## 2. A failure in the printed denominator argument

In the diagnostic primary text, Wu 2609.27610v1, LF1846–1872, the implication \(p\notin(f)\Rightarrow p\) nonzero somewhere on \(V(f)\) is false for nonradical principal ideals. Already take \(R=k[x]\), \(f=x^2\), \(p=x\). A branch \(\tfrac32\log|p/f|\) has pole growth at most \(\log|f^{-1}|\), although its displayed denominator exponent times slope is \(3/2>1\). Thus the claimed inequality \(q r\le c\) cannot be used.

This is a defect in that proof step, not a counterexample to the desired hull estimate. The repair retains normalization as an algebraic auxiliary only and evaluates every final inequality on the original variety.

## 3. The uniform annihilator repair

Let \(f\ne0\) in \(R\), \(b\in R_f\setminus\{0\}\), and \(q,c\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\). Suppose
\[
q\log|b|\le\max(G,c\log|f^{-1}|)+B\quad\text{on }Y_f^{an},
\]
where \(G\) is a finite continuous original affine FS function. Choose \(N\ge1\) with \(m=Nc/q\in\mathbb Z\), and put \(h=b^Nf^m\in R_f\).

The finite normalization \(\bar R\) is a Noetherian normal domain. For any height-one prime of \(\bar R\) containing \(f\), let \(\operatorname{ord}_E\) be its DVR valuation. Its original affine functions have nonnegative order, so \(G\) is bounded along \(e^{-t\operatorname{ord}_E}\), \(t\to+\infty\). The growth inequality gives \(q\operatorname{ord}_E(b)+c\operatorname{ord}_E(f)\ge0\), hence \(\operatorname{ord}_E(h)\ge0\). At height-one primes not containing \(f\), \(h\in R_f\) already has nonnegative order. The intersection-of-height-one-localizations criterion gives \(h\in\bar R\), so \(h\) is integral over \(R\).

The finite algebra \(R[h]\) is contained in \(R_f\). A finite \(R\)-module generating list therefore has denominators killed by one power \(f^M\). Consequently
\[
f^M h^j\in R\quad\text{for EVERY }j\ge0
\]
with one \(M\), independent of \(j\). No claim that a power of every integral element belongs to \(R\) is made.

Let \(L\subset Y_f^{an}\) be compact and \(x\in\widehat L_Y\cap Y_f^{an}\). Every original polynomial \(f^Mh^j\) satisfies its hull bound. Taking \(j\)-th roots and letting \(j\to\infty\) gives
\[
|h(x)|\le\sup_L|h|.
\]
If the right side is zero, the same assertion follows directly from any positive \(j\). Since \(|\log|f||\le M_1\) on \(L\cup\{x\}\), it follows that
\[
q\log|b(x)|\le\sup_L q\log|b|+2cM_1.
\]
Additive branch constants are harmless. Applied to each branch of an actual affine FS maximum with the displayed upper bound, this proves the approximate local hull inequality without the false denominator-order assertion.

The primary algebraic inputs are finite normalization (Stacks 33.27.1, tag 0BXQ), the height-one intersection criterion (10.157.6, tag 031T), and the DVR criterion (10.119.7, tag 00PD). The finite-module denominator argument is given in full above. G is still needed to pass this estimate from controlled FS approximants to an already genuine local input.

## 4. Explicit soft maxima on extended tropical space

For \(t=(t_1,\ldots,t_s)\in(\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\})^s\), choose rational \(\beta\ge\log(s+1)\), and define
\[
\chi(t)=\beta^{-1}\log\left(1+\sum_{\ell=1}^s e^{-\beta t_\ell}\right).
\]
Use \(e^{-\beta(+\infty)}=0\). Then \(0\le\chi-\max(0,-t_1,\ldots,-t_s)\le1\). At a point \(t\), use derivative \(c_\ell=-e^{-\beta t_\ell}/(1+\sum e^{-\beta t_j})\) for finite coordinates and \(c_\ell=0\) for infinite coordinates. The affine support \(\ell(t')=\sum c_jt'_j+C\), with zero coefficients omitted, has equality at \(t\), lies below \(\chi\), and has a strict gap away from every neighborhood of \(t\).

For the last assertion, on the finite coordinates the log-sum-exp Bregman difference is strictly positive away from \(t\) and tends to infinity at infinity: the constant exponential has strictly positive weight, so the sum of the other support weights is strictly less than one. On coordinates infinite at \(t\), the omitted exponentials increase \(\chi\) strictly whenever such a coordinate is finite. Once the finite coordinates lie in a compact neighborhood of their target, imposing \(t'_j\le A\) on any omitted coordinate gives a uniform positive increase. These two facts prove the uniform gap on the complement of a product neighborhood, and hence on the complement of any larger prescribed neighborhood.

For rational \(\beta\), the tropical pullback of \(\chi\) admits global uniform rational convex-PL approximants from above. Use its convex conjugate, the continuous convex entropy function \(e(p)=\beta^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^s p_i\log p_i\) on the compact probability simplex. Take a rational grid triangulating every coordinate face with mesh tending to zero, and rational vertex heights lying slightly above \(e\). Their convex roof is at least \(e\) by convexity, and is at most the barycentric interpolation on each small simplex; uniform continuity bounds its excess by a rational \(\delta\downarrow0\). Subtract \(\delta\) and take conjugates. Each conjugate is the maximum of the affine functions associated to lower-hull grid vertices, with rational nonnegative slopes and rational heights, and lies between \(\chi\) and \(\chi+\delta\) globally. The same argument on every coordinate face includes infinite tropical coordinates. The summable constant-shift construction below gives decreasing approximants; no minimum of convex primal functions is used.

Pullback affine branches are positive rational combinations of original \(\log|f_j|\). Clearing the common slope denominator makes them logarithms of original monomials. A low constant branch and sufficiently small positive branches of all original generators can be retained. Thus this is an actual affine FS approximation, not an abstract convex completion.

## 5. A countable strict barrier and its local modification

Enumerate all \(f_i\in R\), retain generators \(z_1,\ldots,z_a\), and pull back the preceding \(\chi\) by their logarithmic norms. Write the resulting nonnegative functions as \(\rho_j\). Let \(g=\max(0,\log|z_1|,\ldots,\log|z_a|)\). Polynomial degree bounds over the trivially valued field give rational \(\gamma_j>0\) such that
\[
0\le\gamma_j\rho_j\le2^{-j}(g+1).
\]
Then \(\rho=\sum_j\gamma_j\rho_j\) converges locally uniformly, is finite continuous, and satisfies \(0\le\rho\le g+1\). The upper partial sums
\[
\rho^{(r)}=\sum_{j\le r}\gamma_j\rho_j+2^{-r}(g+1)
\]
decrease to \(\rho\). Each has global uniform affine FS approximation. If \(v_r\) approximates \(\rho^{(r)}\) with error \(\epsilon_r\), choose rational shifts \(B_r\downarrow0\) with \(B_r-B_{r+1}\ge\epsilon_r+\epsilon_{r+1}\). Then \(v_r+B_r\) decreases. This proves actual FS regularizability directly.

Given \(x\) and an open neighborhood \(U\), choose finitely many original functions whose tropical inverse image of a product neighborhood is contained in \(U\). Choose a sufficiently large index \(j\) and replace \(\rho_j\) by its affine support \(\ell_j\) at \(x\). Then
\[
\rho'=\rho-\gamma_j\rho_j+\gamma_j\ell_j,\qquad
\rho'\le\rho,\quad\rho'(x)=\rho(x),\quad\rho'\le\rho-\delta\ \text{outside }U
\]
for some \(\delta>0\). The displayed subtraction specifies the replacement; positivity is proved from the expression as the sum of the OTHER \(\rho_i\) and a positive combination of unit logarithms near \(x\), not by subtracting arbitrary psh functions.

To regularize a real positive coefficient \(a\log|f|\), choose nested rational intervals \(a_r\uparrow a\), \(b_r\downarrow a\), \(a_r>0\), and use \(\max(a_r\log|f|,b_r\log|f|)\). These decrease at positive, negative and zero logarithmic norms, including \(-\infty\). Sum them, approximate the additive constant from above, and append \(\epsilon_rg-r\) with positive rational \(\epsilon_r\downarrow0\). The last branch retains every original generator and tends to \(-\infty\) at every original point. Apply the same finite-sum/tail and summable-error construction to the other \(\rho_i\). This gives actual decreasing original affine FS approximants for \(\rho'\), including its \(-\infty\) locus.

This construction genuinely uses countability. It does not claim a single such barrier over an arbitrary-cardinality field.

## 6. The maximum principle away from the bad set

Assume \(u\) itself is already genuinely affine-regularizable on an affine cover of \(Y\setminus Z\). This stronger, intended assumption replaces the printed assertion that only \(u+c\rho\) is locally regularizable; the latter does not justify subtracting \(c\rho\).

Set \(u_c=u+c\rho\), \(c>0\). If \(x\in\widehat L_Y\setminus Z^{an}\) violates \(u_c(x)\le\sup_Lu_c\), choose a small \(\epsilon>0\) and the genuine function \(a=\max_i\log|f_i|\), where the \(f_i\) generate the ideal of \(Z\), so that the violation survives for \(u_c+\epsilon a\). Its maximum on the compact hull is attained at a point \(y\notin Z^{an}\cup L\). Choose a relatively compact open neighborhood \(V\) of \(y\), with closure in one principal good affine chart and disjoint from \(L\). Let \(K=\partial V\cap\widehat L_Y\).

Replace \(\rho\) by the local modification \(\rho'\) at \(y\). The weak function \(w=u+c\rho'+\epsilon a\) has \(w(y)\ge\sup_Kw+c\delta\). On the chosen good chart it is already a genuine affine FS limit and has a global original-ring FS upper bound. Rossi's local hull principle gives \(y\in\widehat K_Y\). G followed by the uniform-annihilator estimate gives \(w(y)\le\sup_Kw\), a contradiction.

For Rossi's principle itself, the standard argument is valid once the continuous compact PL bridge is used: if an original polynomial exceeds its boundary hull bound, glue \(\max(Cg_0,\log|p|-t)\) on \(V\) to \(Cg_0\) outside, where \(g_0\ge0\) vanishes on a thick polynomial polytope containing \(\widehat L_Y\). On a whole boundary collar the first branch wins. The result is continuous rational PL with harmonic supports and the same original reference. The accepted algebraic-first nef/FS bridge supplies a uniform global FS approximation. It separates \(y\) from \(L\), contradicting its hull membership. No singular envelope is invoked.

## 7. Corrected split-fiber trace contradiction

Suppose \(\pi:Y\to S\subset\mathbb A_k^n\) is finite, \(S\) affine open, \(\pi|_Z\) is a closed immersion onto its image, and \(Z\) and \(\pi^{-1}(\pi Z)\setminus Z\) are disjoint closed subsets. If \(\{u_c<0\}\) is not Runge, choose compact \(L\) and normalize \(\sup_{\widehat L_Y}u_c=0>\sup_Lu_c=-\delta\). A maximizing \(x\) belongs to \(Z^{an}\) by the preceding principle.

The hull \(C=\widehat{\{\pi(x)\}}_S\) lies in \((\pi Z)^{an}\). Its finite preimage splits into two disjoint compact pieces, one in \(Z\), the other in the complementary closed subset. Disjoint neighborhoods \(A_1,A_2\) can be chosen, and properness gives a base neighborhood \(A\) of \(C\) whose whole preimage lies in \(A_1\cup A_2\).

Choose an original nonnegative affine FS \(g_S\) and a strict polynomial presentation \(U\) containing \(C\), such that \(g_S=0\) on \(U\), \(\{g_S\le1\}\) is compact in \(A\), and \(W=\{g_S<1\}\). This uses finite polynomial separation and thickened rational inequalities. It does not identify topological interiors with strict inequalities for arbitrary degenerate presentations. The sets \(W_i=\pi^{-1}(W)\cap A_i\) are disjoint OPEN sets with controlled compact closures; the printed claim that these interiors are compact is discarded.

Extend their indicator \(h=1\) on \(W_1\), \(0\) on \(W_2\) by
\[
H=\max(3g_S\circ\pi-1,h)\ \text{on }\pi^{-1}(W),\qquad
H=3g_S\circ\pi-1\ \text{outside}.
\]
The reference wins on a whole collar. The trace of this finite continuous input is continuous by the accepted canonical norm formula. On \(U\) it equals the positive integer
\[
\Gamma_1(s)=\sum_{y\in\pi^{-1}(s)\cap W_1}\Gamma_y.
\]
It is LOCALLY constant. Shrink to one neighborhood of \(\pi(x)\) on which it equals \(\gamma\); no connectedness of all of \(U\) is assumed.

If \(\gamma>1\), usc and properness give a further base neighborhood whose whole \(W_1\)-fiber lies in \(\{u_c<\delta/[2(\gamma-1)]\}\). At the central fiber its \(W_1\)-part is just \(x\), because \(\pi|_Z\) is a closed immersion and the other closed piece avoids \(A_1\). If \(\gamma=1\), omit the divided bound: positivity and integer weights imply that every \(W_1\)-fiber has exactly one point of weight one.

Choose a relatively compact open \(V\) around \(x\) whose closure lies in the controlled source neighborhood. Put \(K=\partial V\cap\widehat L_Y\), and use \(\rho'\) with gap \(\delta'>0\) there. Then \(u'_c=u+c\rho'\) equals zero at \(x\), lies below \(u_c\), and satisfies \(u'_c\le-c\delta'\) on \(K\). The factor \(c\) is retained.

Set \(v=u'_c\) on \(W_1\), \(v=0\) on \(W_2\). It is weak on the restriction of the SAME witness. Let \(M\ge0\) bound it above on the controlled compact preimage, and choose \(C>M+1+\delta\). Extend
\[
\widetilde v=\max(Cg_S\circ\pi-\delta,v)\ \text{on }\pi^{-1}(W),\qquad
\widetilde v=Cg_S\circ\pi-\delta\ \text{outside}.
\]
On \(U_1\) it is \(\max(u'_c,-\delta)\), and on \(U_2\) it is zero. The reference wins on an entire outer collar, and the original witness still gives its weak upper-growth property. T gives an ACTUAL regularizable \(w=N_\pi\widetilde v\) on \(S\), and \(w\circ\pi\) is genuinely regularizable on \(Y\). Finite integral generator bounds and the prescribed outside affine FS branch retain every original source generator in this pullback.

Rossi gives \(x\in\widehat K_Y\), hence \(w(\pi x)\le\sup_Kw\circ\pi\). At \(x\) the left side is zero. At \(z\in K\cap Z^{an}\), the only \(U_1\)-branch over \(\pi z\) is \(z\), with total weight \(\gamma\), so
\[
w(\pi z)\le-\gamma\min(\delta,c\delta')<0.
\]
At \(y\in K\setminus Z^{an}\), the off-bad-set maximum principle gives \(\widetilde v(y)=-\delta\). If \(\gamma>1\), all other \(U_1\)-branches are bounded by \(\delta/[2(\gamma-1)]\), and their total weight is at most \(\gamma-1\); thus \(w(\pi y)\le-\delta/2\). If \(\gamma=1\), there are no other \(U_1\)-branches and \(w(\pi y)=-\delta\). The \(U_2\)-branches contribute zero. This contradicts the hull inequality and proves the split-case Runge conclusion conditional on T and G.

## 8. Remaining applications

The descending bad-set chain is \(Z_{r+1}=Z_r\cap\overline{\pi^{-1}(\pi Z_r)\setminus Z_r}\). Its dimension drop, localization outside \(\pi^{-1}(\pi Z_{r+1})\), application to the Hartogs function \(\log|w|+u\) on \(Y\times\mathbb A^1\), and final local-to-global conclusion must all be proved with one product witness and the original FS generators. The Hartogs Gauss coefficient radius formula is applied only AFTER genuine Runge exhaustions exist; radius \(+\infty\) corresponds to \(u=-\infty\). The smooth-stratum finite projection must also be constructed with the correct inverse image of the base affine open.

Even after these countable-field affine gates, resolved-nef fiber constancy, actual singular global descent, finite-data descent to arbitrary-cardinality fields, normalization/E1/product compatibility, and the full raw-mass finite-energy equation remain to be checked. This draft makes no assertion that those antecedents follow from the two new partial PASS reports.
