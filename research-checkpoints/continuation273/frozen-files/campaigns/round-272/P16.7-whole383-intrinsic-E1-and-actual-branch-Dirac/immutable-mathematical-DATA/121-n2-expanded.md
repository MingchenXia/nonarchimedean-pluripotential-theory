# n2. Base-field nonalgebraization via compatible frames and all pole divisors

Retain K=C((s)), |s|=exp(-1/8), t=s^8. Let T=(G_m^2)^an, lambda_1=(t,2), lambda_2=(3,t), Lambda=<lambda_1,lambda_2>, and X=T/Lambda. With the finite Picard bridge n1, the following argument no longer assumes F_K. It excludes EVERY separated finite-type K-algebraic space algebraization of X, not just projective schemes or algebraic group uniformizations. This is an unverified mathematical draft using the explicitly named analytic foundations; it does not solve P16.7.

Write x=(-log|z_1|,-log|z_2|). Multiplication by lambda(n)=lambda_1^(n_1)lambda_2^(n_2) sends x to x+n. Inverse images of open rectangles of widths less than one have disjoint nonidentity deck translates. Their quotient maps are ordinary open analytic charts, glued by locally constant deck transformations satisfying the cocycle. This constructs X. The applied open-quotient gluing statement and its entire immediate proof are Conrad--Temkin, *Non-archimedean analytification of algebraic spaces*, author version dated 24 February 2009, Lemma 4.2.3. The action relation in T times_K T is a locally finite union of closed translation graphs: on any pair of relatively compact subsets of T only finitely many n can occur, since n lies in a bounded difference of tropical-coordinate ranges. Thus its map to T times_K T is a closed immersion. Closed diagonals descend through the local isomorphism atlas; Conrad--Temkin 4.2.4 and its adjacent closed-immersion descent proof justify this in the analytic category, beyond mere Hausdorffness. X is separated.

The compact strict polyannulus x^(-1)([0,1]^2) surjects onto X. Goodness and strictness can also be seen pointwise: choose a center in (1/8 Z)^2 within 1/16 in each coordinate of a lift; the closed rectangle of width 1/2 about it lies in the open rectangle of width 3/4, which is a quotient chart. Its inverse image is a strict affinoid neighborhood of the point. Local charts are ordinary opens of the smooth boundaryless torus. Consequently X is good, strict, smooth, of pure dimension two, boundaryless and compact; with separatedness these are precisely analytic properness. T is connected: its nested centered closed polyannuli have integral Laurent algebras and nonempty intersections. Hence X is connected. Only this base-K geometry is asserted here.

We first prove Pic(T)=0, including compatibility along the noncompact exhaustion. Put a=1/8 and P_m={|s|^m<=|z_i|<=|s|^(-m)}. By n1 choose a frame e_m of a given line bundle on P_m. Every unit f on P_m has an inner estimate as follows. Its convergent Laurent expansion is sum b_beta z^beta. At the Gauss seminorm of log-radii y in [-ma,ma]^2, log|f| is the maximum of log|b_beta|+beta dot y. It and the corresponding function for f^(-1) are convex and sum to zero, so both are affine. A term achieving the maximum at an interior point has an integral slope alpha; since an affine function nonnegative on a neighborhood and zero at an interior point has zero linear part, that term agrees with the maximum affine function. The vertex inequalities yield

|b_beta/b_alpha| exp(ma |beta-alpha|_1)<=1.

It follows that f=b_alpha z^alpha(1+g), with zero constant coefficient in g and ||g||_(P_j)<=|s|^(m-j) for j<m. On P_m itself 1+g is a unit; no strict boundary estimate is claimed. For a unit on all of T the same affine Gauss-norm argument on all of R^2 makes it exactly b z^alpha, since any distinct exponent would dominate on some ray.

Choose frames successively and divide the (m+1)-st by a global scalar monomial to arrange e_(m+1)|_(P_m)=(1+g_m)e_m. For fixed j, the products p_j=product_(m=j)^infinity(1+g_m)|_(P_j) converge to units: the first factor is a unit, while all later errors have norm at most |s|^(m-j) tending to zero. Their inverse products also converge by the geometric series for the tail factors. The frames E_j=e_j p_j satisfy E_(j+1)|_(P_j)=E_j. Because P_j lies in the interior of P_(j+1), this exhaustion is admissible, and the frames glue to a frame on T. This proves Pic(T)=0 rather than simply choosing unrelated finite frames.

Pull any line bundle on X to T and use this global frame. A global section becomes an analytic Laurent function f satisfying

f(lambda z)=c_lambda z^(h_lambda) f(z),

where each multiplier is a global unit, hence a scalar monomial. Commuting the two deck transformations gives lambda_1^(h_2)=lambda_2^(h_1). Write h_1=(A,B), h_2=(C,D); the equality is t^C 2^D=3^A t^B. Valuations give C=B; then prime valuations in Q* give A=D=0. Thus h_1=(0,k), h_2=(k,0). The exponent map is additive on Lambda.

If k!=0 take n=(1,sign(k)), lambda=lambda(n), h=(k n_2,k n_1). Then h!=0 and Q=v(lambda^h)=2|k|>0. Comparing Laurent coefficients in f(lambda z)=c z^h f(z), starting with a_m!=0, gives for integers j>=0

a_(m+jh)=a_m c^j lambda^(-jm-hj(j+1)/2).

Its valuation has quadratic leading term -Q j(j+1)/2, and therefore tends to -infinity. On the fixed unit polyannulus the Laurent coefficients must tend to zero as their exponents escape to infinity, a contradiction. If k=0, two nonzero coefficients at exponents m,m' would give lambda_i^(m-m')=1 for both i. The valuations are respectively m_1-m'_1 and m_2-m'_2, so m=m'. Thus every nonzero section in this situation is a scalar monomial and is nowhere zero.

For an arbitrary nonzero meromorphic F on X, pull back to T. Smooth affinoid algebras are noetherian regular rings. The exact commutative algebra inputs are: every regular local ring is a UFD (current Stacks 0AG0, full statement and immediate inductive proof freshly read), and every noetherian normal domain R equals the intersection of R_p over its height-one primes inside its fraction field (031T, full statement and immediate proof freshly read). Define the pole module on a fraction chart as J={a in O:aF in O}. It is coherent as the kernel of the multiplication map to the denominator quotient. Localization at each prime of its regular affinoid algebra makes this ideal principal by unique factorization, with precisely the nonnegative pole orders. Thus it is a finite locally free rank-one module. These modules agree on overlaps by their intrinsic definition, giving the invertible subsheaf O(-P) of O. This constructs the effective Cartier pole divisor for all local meromorphic fractions, rather than just selected characters. Analytic sheaf restriction and affinoid coherent descent are the named Tate--Kiehl foundations. In particular, this step does not assert that an arbitrary analytic meromorphic sheaf follows from a fraction equalizer.

Trivialize O(-P)=J on T using Pic(T)=0, obtaining an analytic function f_0 with divisor P. Since f_0 is a section of the intrinsic pole ideal J, f_1=F f_0 is analytic by the DEFINITION of J, without assuming every meromorphic sheaf section is already one global affinoid fraction. Equivalently, on each local fraction chart the noetherian normal-domain intersection theorem removes all height-one poles. At a pole the numerator f_1 has order zero, and elsewhere f_0 has order zero, so they have no common height-one divisor. Lambda-invariance of F identifies the translated pole ideal with J. Its two global frames therefore differ by an analytic unit: f_0(lambda z)/f_0(z) and its inverse are analytic. The normal height-one criterion checks the same fact on fraction charts. Both f_0 and f_1 have the same scalar-monomial multipliers. The preceding recurrence forces k=0 and both functions to be scalar multiples of the same monomial. Therefore Gamma(X,M)=K. This argument includes all meromorphic pole divisors.

Now suppose Y is a separated finite-type K-algebraic space and Y^an is isomorphic to X. Conrad--Temkin 2.3.1, with its full immediate proof, reflects smoothness; Example 2.3.2 compares closed-point residue fields and completed local rings. The Berkovich/rigid dictionary applies because X is strict, good, separated and compact. Thus Y is smooth of dimension two. A nontrivial clopen decomposition would analytify to one, and every nonempty finite-type piece has a closed point with nonempty analytification, so Y is connected. Its finitely many irreducible components are disjoint and open by regularity. Hence Y is integral.

Here are the exact algebraic-space hypotheses. Regular noetherian etale charts have no embedded associated primes, so their weakly associated points are minimal. Finite-type charts satisfy all four equivalent finite-component conditions of Stacks 0BB1. Separatedness implies decency and field monomorphisms representing generic points. Stacks 086U gives a dense open scheme locus; for integral Y it contains the unique generic point. Stacks 0ENA, whose three hypotheses are just checked, identifies its global algebraic meromorphic functions with rational functions; 0END identifies this ring with the generic field K(Y). Stacks 0EN6 supplies the total-fraction description on affine etale charts. The current complete statements and immediate proofs were freshly read. A dense affine schematic open and Noether normalization give trdeg_K K(Y)=2.

We must separately construct the analytic meromorphic image. On regular Y a rational function has a Cartier pole divisor, an associated invertible sheaf, and numerator and denominator regular sections, all descending algebraically through etale charts. Analytify the sheaf and the sections using the coherent analytification functor of Conrad--Temkin 2.3. On scheme charts the local map from an algebraic local ring to the analytic local ring is flat: Ducros, *Families of Berkovich spaces*, 2.1.4 states regularity for schemes locally of finite type over an affinoid algebra, citing Berkovich 1993 Proposition 2.6.2 for flatness and Ducros 2009 Theorem 3.3 for regularity. Those deeper theorems are cited foundations, not immediate proofs supplied in this memoir. A local flat map is faithfully flat and preserves nonzerodivisors. Hence the ratio of the analytified sections is analytic meromorphic; that property descends across the analytic etale charts. To see injectivity choose a closed point of a dense affine scheme open outside the zeros of a nonzero numerator and denominator. Its finite residue extension of K is complete, and its analytic point has nonzero fraction value. We obtain a K-embedding K(Y)->Gamma(X,M)=K, contradicting transcendence degree two.

This excludes all separated finite-type algebraic spaces over the retained K. No arbitrary-extension nonalgebraization is claimed. The explicit real analytic positivity datum for future equation work is theta=d'd''((x_1^2+x_2^2)/2); the change under x->x+n is n dot x+|n|^2/2 with zero Hessian. No ample algebraic line bundle is introduced. Nonalgebraization supplies a foundation for P16.7, but the unbounded all-direction finite-energy operator and every-admissible-measure existence and uniqueness still remain unproved.
