Use X, τ, i and the bounded genuine CLD operator from n1–n2. Let C=R/Z with Lebesgue probability dy, and H={h∈C⁰(C):ρ_h=dy+h'' is positive distributionally}. Testing against one shows that ρ_h has mass one. The periodic Green polynomial G(y)=y²/2−y/2+1/12 has mean zero, equal endpoint values, interior second derivative one and derivative jump −1 at zero. Hence G''=dy−δ_0.

Complete circle solution. For every Radon probability ν on C, h_ν(y)=−∫G(y−a)dν(a) is continuous, mean zero, and satisfies h_ν''=ν−dy. The difference of two solutions has zero distributional second derivative, so is affine on the line; periodicity makes it constant. Thus all members of H are given this way modulo constants. Since G ranges from −1/24 to 1/12 and is 1/2-Lipschitz, h_ν lies in [−1/12,1/24], is 1/2-Lipschitz and has oscillation at most 1/8. After sup h=0, the class lies in [−1/8,0]; Arzelà–Ascoli gives compactness, and positivity of dy+h'' is preserved under uniform limits.

Separable analytic equation. Put S_θ={h_1∘τ_1+h_2∘τ_2:h_j∈H}. Convex convolution of local weights gives S_θ⊂C_θ. For u=h_1+h_2 and v=k_1+k_2, the genuine mixed measure is α_u∧α_v=i_*(ρ_{h_1}⊗ρ_{k_2}+ρ_{k_1}⊗ρ_{h_2}). For smooth factors this follows from the diagonal Hessian and degree-one coordinate calibration. Periodic positive convolution converges uniformly in the functions and weakly in curvature probabilities, so the bounded CLD continuity from n2 proves the formula for every factor. In particular MA_θ(u)=2i_*(ρ_{h_1}⊗ρ_{h_2}). Every product measure 2i_*(ν_1⊗ν_2), for arbitrary circle Radon probabilities, is solved by h_{ν_1}+h_{ν_2}. Probability marginals recover each curvature measure, so uniqueness holds modulo constants within S_θ. No uniqueness among all analytic psh functions is asserted.

Energy and first variation. Define e(h)=∫h dy−(1/2)∫|h'|²dy and E_sep(u)=2(e(h_1)+e(h_2)). All derivatives are square integrable by the Lipschitz bound. This is independent of the choice of separable decomposition, and E_sep(u+c)=E_sep(u)+2c. It agrees with the genuine mixed CLD expression E(u)=(1/3)∫u(θ²+θ∧α_u+α_u²): inserting the mixed formula reduces this to Σ_j(∫h_j dy+∫h_j dρ_{h_j}), and periodic integration by parts gives ∫h_j dρ_{h_j}=∫h_j dy−∫|h_j'|²dy. Convolution justifies integration by parts for these Lipschitz functions, with derivatives converging in L² and curvature measures weakly.

The exact difference identity is e(k)−e(h)=∫(k−h)dρ_h−(1/2)∫|k'−h'|²dy. It proves strict concavity modulo constants and, along every separable segment, dE_sep(u+a(v−u))/da=∫(v−u)MA_θ(u+a(v−u)). These are separable directions only.

Restricted dual energy and an actual failure. For a positive analytic Radon measure μ of mass two, define E*_sep(μ)=sup_{u∈S_θ}(E_sep(u)−∫u dμ). Constants cancel. Normalize sup h_1=sup h_2=0; then −1/4≤u≤0 and E_sep(u)≤0. Thus 0≤E*_sep(μ)≤1/2 for every such μ. Let ν_j=(1/2)(τ_j)_*μ. The exact difference identity shows that the maximizer is h_{ν_1}+h_{ν_2}, unique modulo constants. Its MA is 2i_*(ν_1⊗ν_2), which generally differs from μ.

Take the actual rigid analytic point y represented by z_1=z_2=1 and μ=2δ_y. Both marginals are δ_0, so the maximizer is u_0=−G(x_1)−G(x_2). Its energy is −1/6 and u_0(y)=−1/6. The maximal objective, hence restricted dual energy, is exactly −1/6−2(−1/6)=1/6. But MA_θ(u_0)=2δ_{i(0)}≠2δ_y: at the rigid point |z_j−1|=0, whereas at i(0) it is one. This is a rigorous counterexample to all-support existence using S_θ and its restricted dual energy. It is not a counterexample to the original target: finite energy of this rigid-point measure in a correctly constructed full analytic cone is not established.

Complete circle-envelope argument. For continuous a:C→R, define P(a)=sup{h∈H:h≤a}. Constants below min a make the set nonempty. The common Lipschitz bound makes the supremum continuous; on the lifted line, y²/2+P(a) is the supremum of convex functions and is convex. Thus P(a)∈H and is the largest permitted function. Order and constant translation give ||P(a)−P(b)||∞≤||a−b||∞.

On an interval in {P(a)<a}, write F=y²/2+P(a). If F has curvature there, choose an arbitrarily short closed interval with positive obstacle clearance on which F is not affine. Replacing F by its endpoint chord raises it, preserves convexity because the chord slope lies between the outside one-sided slopes, and stays below the obstacle when the interval is sufficiently short. Repeat the replacement in every integer translate, preserving F(y+1)=F(y)+y+1/2. This contradicts maximality. Hence ρ_{P(a)} is supported on {P(a)=a}, proving ∫(a−P(a))dρ_{P(a)}=0.

For p=P(a), p_b=P(a+bv), the difference identity and contact identities yield b∫v dρ_{p_b}≤e(p_b)−e(p)≤b∫v dρ_p. Uniform Lipschitz continuity of P gives p_b→p uniformly, hence curvature probabilities converge weakly. Divide by b, reverse inequalities for b<0, and pass to the limit to obtain d e(P(a+bv))/db at zero=∫v dρ_p. These are circle envelopes; no envelope over all analytic θ-psh functions is identified with them.
