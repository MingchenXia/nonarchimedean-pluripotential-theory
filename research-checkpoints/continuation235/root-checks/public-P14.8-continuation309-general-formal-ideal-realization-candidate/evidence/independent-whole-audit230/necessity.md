On D=B_a^3 set v=u+rho_a, psh and nontrivial. For each positive integer m use the Hilbert space H_m(D,v) of holomorphic F with norm² integral_D |F|² exp(-mv)dλ. Completeness follows because weighted L² convergence implies ordinary L² convergence on compact subsets (v has a finite upper bound there), and its distributional limit is holomorphic. The weighted measure has finite density a.e. and is sigma finite by exhausting where its density is bounded; its L² space and the closed Hilbert subspace are separable. O at one finite-valued point supplies a nonzero member. Its germs are nonzero throughout connected D. Thus every ideal stalk is nonzero, even at zeros of that member.

Exact generation. Fix x. N makes I_x=I(mv)_x a finite module in the Noetherian holomorphic local ring. Choose finite local generators g of I_x, representatives on a small weighted-integrability neighborhood, and a smooth cutoff chi supported there, equal1 near x. On D solve

The form is smooth, closed and supported away from x. Its W-norm is finite: pole and cutoff factors are bounded on its support, and |g|² exp(-mv) is integrable there. H gives a solution. The extra function W-mv is bounded above on bounded D, so integral |h|² exp(-mv)<infinity. Near x, dbar h=0, so h is holomorphic by local distributional regularity. Since v is locally bounded above, W-integrability implies integral |h|² |z-x|^(-2(n+q))<infinity. A first nonzero homogeneous Taylor term of degree k has nonzero spherical squared integral; its radial exponent is 2k-2q-1, integrable only when k>q. Hence h_x is in I_x intersect m_x^(q+1), including the identically zero case. F=chi g-h is globally holomorphic in D, belongs to H_m, and F_x equals g modulo m_x I_x. For all finite local generators these F's generate I_x by Nakayama applied to the quotient. Their generated subideal and I are coherent, so the coherent quotient vanishes near x. A finite cover of any smaller closed ball gives a finite family of global H_m members generating exactly I on its neighborhood. This proves exact generation, not merely the same zero locus.

Kernel and normal convergence. Cauchy estimates give uniform first-derivative bounds for norm1 functions on smaller compacts. Therefore the evaluation functionals ell_z in H_m* vary in norm continuously. Choose a countable orthonormal basis f_l. Parseval gives K_m(z)=sum_l |f_l(z)|²=norm(ell_z)², finite continuous. The partial diagonal sums increase continuously to K_m; Dini on compact sets gives uniform diagonal-tail convergence. Cauchy–Schwarz gives normally convergent holomorphic two-variable kernels sum_l f_l(z) f_l*(w) on compact products, where the second domain is the conjugate complex ball and f_l*(w)=overline(f_l(overline w)). These arguments justify bases, diagonal and product convergence; no infinite series is treated as a finite witness.
