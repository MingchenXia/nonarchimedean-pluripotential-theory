# Full reconstructed sufficiency proof

This is a mathematical proof under the ordinary local psh foundations checked in source-ledger.md, not a finite experiment. X is any connected compact Kähler manifold, omega Kähler, theta smooth closed real (1,1), dd^c=i partialbarpartial/(2pi). The input consists of arbitrary nonzero coherent ideals A_c at every real c>0. No relation among them is presumed. The permitted witness is exactly the fixed finite coordinate-ball/norm class in the question, with prescribed ideals A_(2^j), known theta-potentials and fixed radial quadratic barriers, explicit boundary gluing, numerical curvature/supremum bounds, and no arbitrary correction or psh target.

Each Phi_j is the canonical quasi-psh representative of its displayed finite maximum, with theta+dd^c Phi_j >= -epsilon_j omega, epsilon_j decreasing to0, and -B<=sup_X Phi_j<=B. Its generating ideal is nonzero, so its logarithmic norm is locally integrable and not identically -infinity. Fixed known smooth terms and finite maximum gluing preserve this. The comparison of two tuples generating the same ideal is local: each is a holomorphic matrix times the other; on a smaller compact neighborhood the matrices are bounded. Thus their squared norms are comparable in both directions, including common zeros. The resulting bounded correction need not be continuous at zeros and is never used to infer curvature; curvature is checked on the actual branches.

For fixed j put H_(j,N)=max_(j<=k<=N) Phi_k. Since epsilon_k<=epsilon_j, adding a smooth local potential rho_j of theta+epsilon_j omega makes every branch psh, and hence the finite maximum psh. The H_(j,N) increase, are <=B, and are >=Phi_j. These bounds imply local L¹ domination: their absolute values are bounded by a constant plus |Phi_j|. DCT gives H_(j,N) -> S_j=sup_(k>=j) Phi_k locally in L¹ and a.e. Distributional positivity passes to S_j+rho_j. Let v_j be its canonical quasi-psh representative, supplied by the positive-Hessian distribution theorem.

There are also uniform global upper/lower L¹ bounds, if bounds independent of both j and N are wanted. All Phi_j have the common lower curvature eta=theta+epsilon_1 omega and a point x_j with Phi_j(x_j)>=-B. Choose a finite small-cell subdivision of a fixed inner chart cover, each cell of diameter less than a quarter of a radius whose larger ball stays in that chart. A fixed anchor ball for the cell containing x_j lies in the ball centered at x_j of that radius. After adding eta's smooth potential, sub-mean at x_j bounds its negative-part integral, hence the anchor ball's integral. There are only finitely many possible anchor balls. Choose fixed overlap chains from each anchor to every ball of a finite target cover, using the propagation construction detailed below. Finitely many chains bound all Phi_j in L¹ by one constant. Since Phi_j<=H_(j,N)<=B, this also bounds all H_(j,N), independently of j,N. This uniform strengthening is not needed for the fixed-j DCT but verifies the finite-tail L¹ bounds explicitly.

For every N, H_(j,N)<=v_j a.e., hence everywhere. To see this last implication, add rho_j on a ball and average the two psh functions on shrinking balls; their canonical values equal the limits of these averages. It follows S_j<=v_j, and usc of v_j gives S_j*<=v_j. Conversely, averages of S_j+rho_j equal averages of v_j+rho_j. Their limits are v_j(x)+rho_j(x), and every average is bounded by the corresponding local supremum of S_j+rho_j. Letting the radius shrink gives v_j<=S_j*. Therefore
Psi_j=(sup_(k>=j) Phi_k)*=v_j=S_j a.e.,
theta+dd^c Psi_j>=-epsilon_j omega.
The equality is for canonical representatives; an arbitrary usc modification of an L¹ distribution would not suffice. Smooth potential differences reconcile these representatives on overlaps. Countability permits one volume-null set for all j.

The Psi_j decrease, satisfy Psi_j<=B, and sup_X Psi_j>=sup_X Phi_j>=-B. Their sets K_j={Psi_j>=-B-1} are closed in compact X, nonempty (usc attains the finite supremum), and nested. Compactness gives x0 in every K_j. The pointwise decreasing limit p thus satisfies p(x0)>=-B-1.

Here are the required integral propagation details, without assuming noncollapse as a theorem. Use the common lower curvature form eta=theta+epsilon_1 omega. In each relatively compact large chart add its fixed smooth potential sigma, so v_j=Psi_j+sigma are decreasing psh with an upper bound M independent of j. At x0 their values have a uniform lower bound. On any fixed ball centered at x0 inside the chart,
integral_ball (M-v_j) <= Vol(ball)(M-v_j(x0))
by the sub-mean inequality. This bounds the negative part uniformly. For a fixed positive-volume smaller ball E in an overlap where such an integral bound is already known, changing local potentials alters the bound only by a fixed constant. Since integral_E(M'-v'_j)<=K, for each j there is y_j in E with v'_j(y_j)>=M'-K/Vol(E) (or with an arbitrarily enlarged K). Choose E small enough that a fixed-radius ball about every y in E stays in the next large chart and contains a fixed next small ball T. Sub-mean at y_j bounds integral_(ball(y_j,r))(M'-v'_j), hence integral_T(M'-v'_j), uniformly. All radii, volumes, upper bounds and potential differences are fixed.

Such choices can be arranged along any path with successive small centers separated by less than a quarter-radius while the larger coordinate balls remain available; at a chart transition choose E compactly inside both charts and reduce radii once. A connected manifold is path connected. Cover compact X by finitely many target small balls and choose finitely many such chains from x0. This yields uniform local, then global, L¹ negative-part bounds. It uses neither a varying cover nor a point y fixed for every j. Dependence of y_j on j is harmless because containment and radii are uniform.

Now M-v_j increase and have bounded integrals. MCT makes their limit integrable and gives L¹ convergence of v_j, hence Psi_j, to p. A decreasing limit of usc functions is usc. To identify its exact canonical values, sub-mean for v_j gives p+sigma <= its ball averages after taking j->infinity. Usc gives the reverse shrinking-average inequality. Thus p is the canonical representative of its L¹ distribution; equivalently monotone restrictions to complex lines give psh after adding sigma. Its value at x0 is finite, so it is nontrivial. Finally distributional continuity of dd^c and epsilon_j->0 yield theta+dd^c p>=0. Hence u=p belongs to PSH(X,theta), and convergence is pointwise and global L¹. Connectedness was used precisely to propagate noncollapse; compactness alone would leave disconnected components unchecked.

For every x, real c>0 and germ f choose a coordinate ball x in U relatively compact in a domain of a holomorphic representative, with that representative holomorphic near closure U. For fixed j and integer cap R>=1, the functions
|f|² min{R,exp(-c H_(j,N))}
decrease with N and are dominated by R|f|², which is integrable because f is bounded on closure U and dV is smooth. Their pointwise limit uses S_j. DCT and the a.e. equality S_j=Psi_j give
lim_N integral_U |f|² min{R,exp(-c H_(j,N))}
= integral_U |f|² min{R,exp(-c Psi_j)}.
All N are taken with N>=j; no uniformity in N,j,R is asserted by DCT.

For fixed j, MCT as the integer R tends to infinity gives the uncapped integral, possibly infinite. Since Psi_j decrease to u, exp(-c Psi_j) increase to exp(-cu). Another MCT therefore gives the exact extended identity
sup_(j>=1,R integer>=1) lim_N integral_U |f|² min{R,exp(-c H_(j,N))}
= integral_U |f|² exp(-cu).
This sequential argument does not exchange any ideal intersection with a decreasing limit. At -infinity the capped exponential is R; a simultaneous zero of f contributes0 for every cap. The uncapped product is defined by the increasing caps, so no 0 times infinity occurs. Nontrivial psh functions are L¹ and their pole sets have volume0. Smooth changes of coordinate/positive volume change integrability by fixed two-sided constants locally, so null-set conventions have no effect.

Both germ directions, with their quantifiers, now follow. If f is in A_(c,x), the certificate supplies one U and one finite C, independent of every j and R; the identity bounds the full integral by C, giving f in I(cu)_x. Conversely if f is in I(cu)_x, shrink its local integrability neighborhood to a ball U whose closure lies there. Its full integral is finite; take this as C. Each capped tail integral is <=C because Psi_j>=u. The certificate's iff then puts f in A_(c,x). This also handles the zero germ. U,C can depend on x,c,f; neither a common U across germs nor a common C across exponents is required. Tests at all real c give exactly all-real ideal equality, not merely equality at dyadic scales.

Sufficiency is proved. It uses no strong openness, positive top mass, subadditivity, or prior R. A witness can reconstruct a raw u but its construction supplies no independence from the chosen norms.
