H. VIII6.5 gives the inverse-curvature-eigenvalue estimate on a weakly pseudoconvex Kähler manifold for (n,q)-forms. Its proof applies the weighted metric and exhaustion, then weak limits. The conversion to (0,q) adds Ricci curvature, zero for Euclidean balls. For q=1 and W with complex Hessian at least that of |z|², a smooth-weight estimate with numeric1 follows with matching Euclidean norms. Singular weights are justified by the whole6.9 regularization/exhaustion argument: on smaller balls convolve radially, obtaining W_t decreasing to W and preserving the strict lower Hessian; data norms against exp(-W_t) are at most their original norm. Fixed compact upper bounds on W_t give local unweighted L² bounds. Extract diagonal weak limits solving dbar h=a. First pass estimates with any fixed regularized weight, then let that weight decrease to W and exhaust the domain. Weak lower semicontinuity and monotone convergence give the final weighted bound. This applies to smooth closed cutoff data; smoothness of data does not imply smoothness of W.

Numeric1 is unnecessary. Directly6.9 with epsilon=1, q=1 on a bounded D gives
integral (1+|z|²)^(-1)|h|² exp(-W) <= 4 integral (1+|z|²)|a|² exp(-W).
Thus C_D=4(1+sup_D|z|²)^2 bounds the unmodified solution norm by the data norm. This is explicit geometry, independent of m,u. It can be absorbed in every overlap constant and does not change the original theorem. In both applications W=m(u+rho)+(n+q)log|z-x|²+|z|², q>=0. All terms are psh; cutoff derivatives avoid x; integrability of the chosen local germ or source Hilbert section makes the data norm finite. The logarithmic/quadratic extra factor is bounded above on bounded D, so the solution also has the original Hilbert norm.

O. In OT4.11 take a flat rank-n bundle, s(z)=(z-x)/(e d), d=diam D, Y={x}. This section is transverse, |s|<=e^(-1), the bundle curvature is zero, and the scalar weight is W. The q=0 theorem4.1 applies with alpha=1: i partialbarpartial W+n i partialbarpartial log|s|² is nonnegative. The determinant |Lambda^n ds|² is a fixed metric factor times (e d)^(-2n). Let M_n=max_{0<t<=e^(-1)} t^(2n)(-log t)^2<infinity. The4.11 estimate therefore implies
integral_D |F|² exp(-W) <= C_n M_n (e d)^(2n) exp(-W(x)), F(x)=1,
up to fixed Euclidean volume/norm factors. This proves precisely a fixed-domain geometric constant independent of W and x; hence of m,u. A fixed small quadratic, if needed for a strict application, changes the constant only geometrically. Do not infer a rescaling-uniform constant from the isolated C_n formula in4.12: its displayed diameter power does not warrant that interpretation. The derived bound has the required geometric dependence.

For singular W, either use4.11's explicit all-psh statement or reconstruct its extension by radial regularizations on increasing smaller domains. At a finite-valued x, W_t(x) decreases to W(x), so the right side is uniformly bounded by C_D exp(-W(x)). Local unweighted bounds give a normal subsequence of extensions, still F(x)=1. Fixed regularized weights, Fatou, and monotone convergence recover the singular-weight integral. For W(x)=-infinity only 0<=K_W(x) is required. The proof's approximate-equation case and4.9c correct q=0 regularity by a minimal solution and ellipticity before weak limits. No positive-degree Manivel regularity conjecture is applied. OT6.1 independently gives this kernel lower estimate and the mean-value upper estimate with half-weight convention; its 'uniformly on Omega' phrase is used only on compact subsets, as its local evaluation argument warrants. The Fréchet open mapping theorem makes the inverse continuous. The two topologies agree; this step is not an assumption of uniform analytic division.
