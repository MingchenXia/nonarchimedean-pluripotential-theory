This note proves the premises it uses rather than importing an external research theorem. The global raw-representative issue is treated separately in n2.

The convention is dd^c = (i/(2*pi))*partial*barpartial. With dA = dx dy it gives dd^c h = (Delta h)/(4*pi) dA for a smooth real function h. Thus log|z|^2, not log|z|, has curvature delta_0. Here is a distributional verification: log|z|^2 is locally integrable because integral_0^R r*|log r| dr is finite. For a compactly supported smooth test function eta, apply Green's identity outside |z|=epsilon. The inner boundary has outward normal -partial_r, so its contribution to integral log|z|^2 Delta eta is integral_0^{2*pi}[-2*epsilon*log epsilon*partial_r eta + 2*eta] dtheta. The first term tends to zero and the second to 4*pi*eta(0). There is no outer-boundary term after taking the outer circle beyond the test support. Hence Delta log|z|^2 = 4*pi delta_0, with precisely the claimed sign and normalization.

The harmonic-distribution premise has an elementary local proof. If Delta T = 0 on a planar open set, convolve T with a smooth radial unit-integral mollifier rho_epsilon. On interior disks H_epsilon = T*rho_epsilon is smooth and harmonic. A smooth harmonic function has its circle mean equal to its central value: differentiating its circular mean and applying the divergence theorem gives r*M'(r) = (1/(2*pi))*integral_{D_r} Delta H dA = 0. Integrating circular means against a radial mollifier therefore gives H*rho_delta = H wherever the convolution stays inside the domain. For epsilon,delta small compared with the distance of a fixed smaller disk from the boundary, associativity and commutativity of the local convolutions give H_epsilon*rho_delta = H_delta*rho_epsilon. The mean identities make the two sides H_epsilon and H_delta respectively. Consequently the smooth harmonic convolutions coincide on every sufficiently interior disk once both radii are small. Since T*rho_epsilon tends to T in distributions, this common smooth harmonic function represents T there. The representatives agree on overlaps. This proves the Weyl lemma used here without a sourced theorem.

A positive real distribution is a locally finite positive measure. Indeed, on a compact test support K choose a smooth nonnegative cutoff chi equal to one near K. Positivity gives |T(eta)| <= ||eta||_infinity*T(chi) for every real test eta supported in K, by applying it to ||eta||_infinity*chi +/- eta. Thus T has order zero locally, extends as a positive functional on continuous compactly supported functions, and the usual positive-functional measure representation gives a Radon measure. In the paper the curvature measures are already part of the hypotheses, so this observation is more than is needed.

The precise positive-curvature representative statement is an existence and almost-everywhere uniqueness statement. Let t be a real L1_loc distribution and dd^c t = mu >= 0 on a disk. Restrict mu to a smaller disk D_R with finite mass and form L(z) = integral_{D_R} log|z-zeta|^2 dmu(zeta). On each compact z-disk, integral |log|z-zeta|^2| dA(z) is bounded uniformly for zeta in D_R: the negative part is bounded by the radial integral on the unit disk, and the positive part is bounded on the bounded union of translated domains. Fubini therefore makes L locally L1 and finite almost everywhere. It also justifies pairing L with dd^c of a smooth compactly supported test function; the logarithmic fundamental solution gives dd^c L = mu|_{D_R}. On a smaller interior disk, dd^c(t-L)=0. The Weyl proof above supplies a smooth harmonic h, and t=L+h almost everywhere there.

For completeness, the integral L has its pointwise subharmonic representative directly. The kernel has a uniform finite upper bound when z,zeta stay in fixed bounded sets. Reverse Fatou applied after subtracting this bound proves upper semicontinuity of L, allowing the value -infinity. The circle average of log|z+rho*exp(i theta)-zeta|^2 is 2*log(max(rho,|z-zeta|)). For unequal radii this follows by factoring out the larger radius and averaging the convergent power series for log(1-a*exp(i theta)), |a|<1. At equality the same result follows by passage to the limit using the integrable logarithmic singularity in theta (equivalently the elementary integral of log|1-exp(i theta)|). This circle formula, integrated in rho, proves the disk-area submean inequality for the kernel. Integrating that inequality in zeta is justified by the local absolute-integrability bound; at a central value -infinity it is automatic. Thus L is subharmonic and is not identically -infinity since it is locally L1. Adding h preserves subharmonicity. Upper semicontinuity and the finite kernel bound also show that this representative is bounded above on smaller disks. For a subharmonic s its disk-area averages tend to s(x) as the radius decreases to zero: submean gives the lower bound and upper semicontinuity gives the upper bound, including at s(x)=-infinity. Two such representatives equal almost everywhere therefore equal everywhere. This proves the precise local Riesz and positive-curvature representative premises. It does not assert that an arbitrary USC L1 representative is already subharmonic.

Now let w be subharmonic near zero, not identically -infinity. Its positive curvature has finite atom a = (dd^c w)({0}) >= 0. Subtract the locally integrable atom potential distributionally: t = w - a*log|z|^2. Its curvature is the positive residual measure lambda = dd^c w - a*delta_0 and lambda({0})=0. The preceding argument gives a subharmonic representative v of t on a smaller disk, locally bounded above. In particular w=a*log|z|^2+v almost everywhere, which is all that integration requires. This subtraction never assumes a finite pole set or regularity of the residual measure. Other atoms and an arbitrary accumulation of residual poles are allowed.

Fix an actual real c>0 and a nonzero holomorphic germ f=z^k*g, where k is a nonnegative integer and g is a holomorphic unit. Shrink the disk so constants 0<g_- <= |g| <= g_+ hold. If k+1 <= c*a, an upper bound v<=M implies |f|^2*exp(-c*w) >= g_-^2*exp(-c*M)*|z|^{2k-2ca} almost everywhere. Its area integral has radial part integral_0^epsilon r^{2k-2ca+1} dr, which diverges exactly when k+1 <= ca. In the equality case the exponent is -1 and divergence is logarithmic. The argument uses the unit factor and covers every critical integer value ca, including cases where the residual potential has additional singularities.

For sufficiency suppose k+1>ca and set q=max(ca-k,0), so 0<=q<1. On a disk of radius at most one, |z|^{2k-2ca} <= |z|^{-2q}. Since lambda is locally finite with lambda({0})=0, continuity of measures from above gives lambda(D_r) -> 0 as r -> 0. Choose r<1/4 so m=lambda(D_r) satisfies c*m<1-q and take mu=lambda|_{D_r}. The locally L1 logarithmic potential has curvature mu, so on D_{r/2} the Riesz reconstruction gives v(z)=integral_{D_r} log|z-zeta|^2 dmu(zeta)+h(z) almost everywhere. Weyl's lemma makes h harmonic on D_r and bounded on D_{r/2}.

If m=0, v=h on the smaller disk and the integral reduces to that of |z|^{-2q}, finite because q<1. This includes q=0 and a=0. If m>0, mu/m is a probability measure. Put ell_T=max(log|z-zeta|^2,-T). Since all relevant distances are less than one, ell_T is bounded and nonpositive. Jensen for the convex exponential gives exp(-c*m*integral ell_T d(mu/m)) <= integral exp(-c*m*ell_T) d(mu/m). As T increases, ell_T decreases to the logarithm and both nonnegative sides increase. Monotone convergence yields

exp(-c*integral log|z-zeta|^2 dmu(zeta)) <= (1/m)*integral |z-zeta|^{-2cm} dmu(zeta),

with extended values allowed. No smoothness, discreteness or absolute continuity of mu is used.

Write s=cm. Since q+s<1, choose a number t strictly between q and 1-s, also strictly between zero and one, and set alpha=1/t, beta=1/(1-t). These are finite conjugate exponents greater than one, with q*alpha<1 and s*beta<1. This also handles q=0; alternatively take 1<beta<1/s first. For zeta in D_r, Holder gives

integral_{D_{r/2}} |z|^{-2q}|z-zeta|^{-2s} dA <= (integral_{D_{r/2}} |z|^{-2q alpha} dA)^{1/alpha} * (integral_{D_{2r}} |y|^{-2s beta} dA)^{1/beta}.

The translated domain D_{r/2}-zeta lies inside D_{2r}. Each radial integral is finite: integral_{D_R}|y|^{-2gamma}dA = 2*pi*R^{2-2gamma}/(2-2gamma) when gamma<1. Thus the right side is finite uniformly in zeta. Tonelli integrates this uniform bound against the probability mu/m. The bounded harmonic factor exp(-c*h) and the bounded unit |g|^2 preserve integrability. This proves sufficiency for m>0, q=0, a=0, arbitrary residual pole accumulation and arbitrary finite atoms in the residual measure.

We have proved, for every real c>0 and every nonzero germ of order k,

|f|^2 exp(-c*w) is locally integrable at zero if and only if k+1>ca.

The least nonnegative integer k satisfying this strict inequality is floor(ca), including when ca is an integer. The zero germ is included automatically, and multiplication by a holomorphic germ preserves the order condition. Hence the ideal is exactly (z^{floor(ca)}). In particular a=0 gives the full local ring for every finite c even if the subharmonic function is -infinity at the center or has poles accumulating there.

A smooth local addition changes the integrand by a bounded positive factor on a smaller disk, and changes curvature only by a smooth measure with no atom. An almost-everywhere replacement changes neither curvature distributions nor local integrability. These are the exact invariances needed to use the lemma for the paper's potentials. Jensen, Holder, Tonelli, Fubini, continuity of finite measures from above and the radial integrals have been used only under the explicit hypotheses given above.
