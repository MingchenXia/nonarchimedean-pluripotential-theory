ID: n2

SUMMARY:
Unverified partial proof: for algebraically closed complete nontrivially valued K of residue characteristic zero, X=P^1 and L=O(d), the genuine finite-energy operator solves every positive Radon target of raw mass d and finite dual energy. The criterion is J(μ)=I(μ)/2. No support or invariance restriction is imposed; general P16.4 remains unresolved.

DETAILED SUMMARY:
Constructs actual psh potentials u=c−p_μ using the full rooted Berkovich-tree Green kernel. Identifies the operator with genuine continuous Monge–Ampère measures and proves independence of decreasing approximations. Proves E(u)=dc−I(μ)/2, the exact dual measure condition, existence and uniqueness modulo constants, decreasing continuity, reference independence, finite-energy differentiation, comparison, and normalized compactness. An explicit measure off the standard toric skeleton has finite energy and a solution equal to −∞ at the rigid point 1. The result retains smooth projective algebraic geometry, ample O(d), nontrivial valuation and residue characteristic zero, but adds algebraic closedness and restricts geometry to the projective line.

TEXT:
This is a mathematical partial-proof draft, not an exhaustive finite computation or a solution of the original arbitrary-field atom. The complete argument is retained in `P16.4-argument.md` and the standalone `P16.4-paper.tex`. The permitted published background and exact application hypotheses are recorded in n1.

Retained domain: K is algebraically closed and complete for a nontrivial real-valued non-Archimedean absolute value, with residue characteristic zero; X=P^1_K; L=O(d), where d≥1 is an integer. Thus X is smooth, geometrically connected, algebraic, proper and projective, and L is ample. No discreteness, spherical completeness, countability or function-field algebraizability is imposed. Algebraic closedness and X=P^1 are additional restrictions. No descent to general K is asserted.

Let T=X^an, o its Gauss point, and h₀ the standard semipositive metric on O(d). Its genuine raw curvature is θ=dδ_o. A relative additive weight u gives metric h₀+u, or norm multiplied by exp(−u). Use raw energy, so E(u+c)=E(u)+dc. Exclude the identically −∞ weight.

The partial theorem is: the genuine operator on continuous semipositive metrics extends to E¹, continuously along decreasing psh nets. For every positive Radon μ on all of T with μ(T)=d and finite dual energy
J(μ)=sup{E(v)−∫v dμ : v continuous psh relative to h₀},
there exists u∈E¹ with MA(u)=μ, unique modulo constants. There is no support restriction. Here continuous energy is E(v)=(∫v MA(v)+d v(o))/2; extended energy is the infimum over continuous psh majorants. The theorem also includes comparison, differentiation along E¹ segments, reference independence and a strong-topology homeomorphism E¹/R onto these admissible measures.

Proof of the kernel and approximation assertions. The projective-line tree has unique paths with logarithmic length. Put k(x,y)=length([o,x]∩[o,y]). It is nonnegative, zero when either argument is o, and may be infinite at a rigid diagonal. Every nonrigid point has finite depth ℓ(x)=k(x,x). Type IV endpoints have positive limiting disk radius because K is complete. Let F run over finite rooted subtrees with type II vertices, and let r_F be rooted-path retraction. Algebraic closedness makes the nonzero value group divisible and hence dense in logarithmic lengths, so these trees exhaust every finite path. The functions k_F(x,y)=k(r_Fx,r_Fy) are bounded continuous kernels increasing to k. Retraction continuity follows from the disk weak topology: each attached branch is collapsed to its attachment point.

Functions factoring through these retractions form an algebra separating points. Stone–Weierstrass gives uniform density in C(T). Since g∘r_G=g whenever g factors through F⊂G, uniform approximation also gives f∘r_F→f uniformly for every continuous f. Therefore μ_F=(r_F)_*μ→μ weakly. Finite-tree piecewise affine functions with type II knots are uniformly dense as well.

For completeness, increasing directed suprema of nonnegative continuous functions commute with integration against a Radon measure on a compact Hausdorff space. To prove this without a false dominated-convergence assertion for nets, approximate superlevel sets from inside by compact subsets. The open inequalities defining a superlevel cover each compact subset; a finite subcover and directedness provide one index meeting all finitely many inequalities. Level partitions and truncation prove the integral equality. Apply the same argument to the Radon product on T×T. Fubini for the resulting nonnegative kernels follows by continuous approximation, since finite sums of products of continuous functions are uniformly dense on the compact product.

Define p_μ(x)=∫k(x,y)dμ(y), I(μ)=∬k(x,y)dμ(x)dμ(y), and p_{μ,F}(x)=∫k_F(x,y)dμ(y). Then p_{μ,F}=p_μ∘r_F, p_{μ,F} increases to p_μ, and I_F(μ)=∬k_F dμdμ increases to I(μ). The potential is lower semicontinuous, zero at o, and bounded at a finite-depth x by μ(T)ℓ(x).

Agreement with the genuine continuous operator. Use the graph Laplacian convention ∫f dd^c g=−D(f,g), where D(f,g) is the sum of edge integrals of f′g′. For a type II z, k_z=k(·,z) satisfies dd^c k_z=δ_o−δ_z. The metric h₀−dk_z is genuinely semipositive, not a separately declared inverse object: write z=ζ_{a,r}, r=|b|, and transform the standard Fubini–Study metric by the coordinate automorphism. Its relative weight is d[log max(r,|Z−a|)−log max(1,|Z|)] minus its value at o. Nested-disk computation gives exactly −dk_z, with slope −d on [o,z] and zero off that path. Its raw curvature is dδ_z.

For a finite atomic positive ν of mass d at type II points, h₀−p_ν is a convex combination of these genuine metrics, and dimension-one curvature is linear. Rational coefficients follow by tensor powers; real coefficients follow by uniform limits. For μ_F on a compact finite tree, approximate by positive atomic measures of the same mass at type II subdivision points. Moving points by graph distance ε changes the potential uniformly by at most dε. The genuine continuous-operator theorem checked in n1 therefore yields MA(−p_{μ,F})=μ_F.

Conversely, for any continuous psh weight v, published integration by parts, passed through uniform approximation, gives ∫k_z MA(v)=v(o)−v(z). Thus if ν=MA(v), v(z)=v(o)−p_ν(z) at every type II point. Along each rooted path, increasing type II cutoffs have kernels increasing to the endpoint kernel. Sequential monotone convergence and continuity of v give v(x)=v(o)−p_ν(x) everywhere. In particular normalized continuous weights are nonpositive. Constants and the k_z span finite-tree piecewise affine functions: their graph Laplacians and root values determine them. Their span is consequently dense in C(T).

Unbounded operator and uniqueness of representation. For every mass-d positive Radon μ, the genuine continuous psh weights c−p_{μ,F} decrease to c−p_μ. The limit is not identically −∞ since its value at o is c. The decreasing-limit closure checked in n1 makes it a psh metric, and its genuine approximating measures converge to μ.

Conversely take any decreasing net of continuous psh weights v_i with nontrivial limit u. Set c_i=v_i(o) and ν_i=MA(v_i). Since v_i≤c_i, the limit c=lim c_i is finite. At type II z, c_i−dℓ(z)≤v_i(z)≤c_i, so the limit there is finite. The integrals of k_z against ν_i converge. Weak compactness of mass-d Radon measures and density of the tests imply convergence to a unique μ. Hence u(z)=c−p_μ(z). At any other point x, each continuous representation satisfies v_i(x)=inf_z v_i(z), over type II cutoffs on [o,x). The two infima commute, proving u(x)=c−p_μ(x). Thus every psh weight has a unique representation c−p_μ.

Define MA(c−p_μ)=μ. The preceding proof establishes agreement with the genuine continuous operator and independence of every decreasing continuous approximation. The same dense-test argument gives weak continuity along decreasing nets of already singular psh weights with a nontrivial limit. In this linear one-dimensional sector the operator actually exists on the entire psh cone. This does not construct a higher-dimensional wedge product.

Energy identification. Complete the root-zero finite-tree piecewise affine functions for the Dirichlet norm D to obtain a Hilbert space H. Evaluation at a finite-depth point is bounded by |f(x)|≤sqrt(ℓ(x))‖f‖_H. Absolutely continuous finite-tree functions with square-integrable derivatives belong to H by linear interpolation approximation. On each oriented edge, the derivative of p_{μ,F} is the descendant mass beyond that edge. Integrating derivatives gives
D(p_{μ,F},p_{ν,F})=I_F(μ,ν)=∬k_F dμdν.
For F⊂G, restrictions agree and new derivatives lie on added edges. Thus p_{μ,F} is the orthogonal projection of p_{μ,G}, and ‖p_{μ,G}−p_{μ,F}‖²=I_G(μ)−I_F(μ). If I(μ)<∞, these potentials converge in H to their actual kernel potential. Conversely bounded restriction norms imply finite I. Taking limits gives I(μ,ν)=D(p_μ,p_ν)≤sqrt(I(μ)I(ν)) for finite-energy positive measures. Thus their actual mutual potential integrals are finite.

For continuous u=c−p_μ, the raw energy formula gives E(u)=dc−I(μ)/2. For any psh u the finite-tree majorants give the upper bound for the infimum-majorant energy. For the reverse bound, if v≥u is continuous psh and ε>0, the increasing open sets {c−p_{μ,F}<v+ε} cover compact T. A finite subcover makes c−p_{μ,F}≤v+ε eventually. Monotonicity of continuous energy yields the reverse bound after passage to the limit. Consequently
E(c−p_μ)=dc−I(μ)/2, and E¹={c−p_μ:I(μ)<∞}.
If I is infinite, the majorant energies tend to −∞. The same compact-open-cover argument proves energy continuity along arbitrary decreasing psh nets.

Exact measure condition. For finite I(μ) and continuous v=a−p_ν, constants cancel because both measures have raw mass d, and
E(v)−∫v dμ=I(μ,ν)−I(ν)/2≤I(μ)/2
by completing the Hilbert square. The continuous test v=−p_{μ,F} attains the value I_F(μ)/2 because I(μ,μ_F)=I_F(μ). These tests also show that J is infinite when I is infinite. Therefore J(μ)=I(μ)/2 exactly. Supremizing over Fubini–Study tests gives the same quantity by uniform density; over E¹ it gives the same quantity by mutual integrability. If energy and measure are normalized by d, the probability-measure dual energy is I(μ)/(2d). The actual target remains the original raw mass-d measure.

Existence and uniqueness. Finite J gives the actual solution u=−p_μ∈E¹ with MA(u)=μ. The unique kernel representation gives uniqueness modulo constants. More explicitly, for v=a−p_ν∈E¹,
E(v)−∫v dμ=I(μ)/2−‖p_ν−p_μ‖²/2.
Thus the actual solution, modulo constants, is the unique maximizer. Existence was proved by an actual constructed potential, not assumed in a conditional variational template.

Unbounded differentiation. For u,v∈E¹, let b=v−u along their affine psh segment. Mutual integrability makes b integrable against their actual measures, omitting null sets where potentials are infinite. The quadratic identity E(u)=du(o)−D(u,u)/2 gives the right derivative
(d/dt) E(u+tb)|_{t=0+}=d b(o)−D(u,b)=∫b MA(u).
This is an energy derivative for the actual unbounded operator, rather than continuous metric volume differentiation.

Comparison. Put w=v−u and η_ε(w)=min(1,max(0,w/ε)). On finite trees w_F=w∘r_F. The contractions agree on restrictions and have Dirichlet norm at most ε^−1‖w‖, so their increasing added-edge energies are bounded and their nets converge strongly, with fixed root value. On each finite tree D(η_ε(w_F),w_F)=ε^−1∑∫_{0<w_F<ε}|w_F′|²≥0; the sign passes to the Hilbert limit.
To identify the pairing with the actual measures, select increasing trees whose energies approach those of p_{MA(u)} and p_{MA(v)}. For either potential p and either measure ρ, ∫(p−p∘r_F)dρ tends to zero by the mutual-energy identity and Cauchy–Schwarz. The increasing potential approximations therefore converge to the canonical potentials ρ-almost everywhere. Their contractions converge boundedly almost everywhere; ordinary sequential dominated convergence identifies the actual integrals. Finite-tree integration by parts then gives ∫η_ε(w)(MA(v)−MA(u))=−D(η_ε(w),w)≤0. Letting ε decrease to zero proves MA(v)({u<v})≤MA(u)({u<v}). The exceptional infinite-potential sets are null by mutual integrability. The reference measure cancels without rescaling any restriction.

Compactness and strong topology. The tests k_z identify normalized psh weights u(o)=0 with the weakly compact space of positive mass-d Radon measures. The identification is a homeomorphism for pointwise convergence on type II points. Since I=sup_F I_F and each continuous-kernel quadratic integral is weakly continuous, I is lower semicontinuous; its sublevels are weakly compact. Bounded Dirichlet norms and convergence on the dense evaluation tests give weak Hilbert convergence. Convergence of energy gives convergence of squared norms, hence strong Hilbert convergence. Conversely weak convergence of measures and convergence of J=I/2 give these same conditions. This proves the asserted strong-topology homeomorphism.

Envelope and regularization inputs. Actual decreasing regularization was supplied by finite-tree potentials. The smooth-curve envelope-continuity result recorded in n1 applies to this domain, but the equation proof does not use an envelope or an unproved derivative of E composed with an envelope. Explicit Green inversion and the Hilbert-square identity replace that variational step. Approximation, compactness, the unbounded derivative and comparison used here have been proved above. This replacement is confined to the linear curve equation.

Reference dependence. For another continuous semipositive reference h₁=h₀+a on the same L, the continuous cocycle identity extends through the infimum-majorant definition, giving E_{h₁}(u−a)=E_{h₀}(u)−E_{h₀}(a). Consequently J_{h₁}(μ)=J_{h₀}(μ)−E_{h₀}(a)+∫a dμ. The correction is finite since a is continuous and μ has mass d. Thus the absolute finite-energy space and measure condition are reference-independent. The operator is intrinsic by agreement on continuous absolute metrics and independence of their decreasing approximations. Neither polarization nor raw mass is changed.

An explicit unbounded nonskeletal example. Choose 0<|π|<1, λ=−log|π|, z_j=ζ_{1,|π|^j}, and μ=∑_{j≥1} d/[j(j+1)] δ_{z_j}. The measure has raw mass d. Its nodes lie off the standard toric skeleton, approaching the rigid point 1, which has zero atom. Descendant mass on the jth length-λ edge is d/j. Hence I(μ)=λd²∑j^−2<∞, while p_μ(1)=λd∑(j+1)^−1=∞. The genuine finite-energy solution u=−p_μ therefore satisfies u(1)=−∞. No finite subtree contains this support, and its measure is not toric-skeletal, so the solution is noninvariant in this coordinate. Retraction to [o,z_N] gives μ_N=∑_{j<N}d/[j(j+1)]δ_{z_j}+(d/N)δ_{z_N}, I(μ_N)=λd²∑_{j≤N}j^−2, and u_N(z_N)=−λd∑_{j≤N}j^−1.

This proves the stated partial theorem under its exact retained and additional hypotheses. It neither removes algebraic closedness nor constructs higher-dimensional unbounded wedge products. The original P16.4 remains unresolved.
