Result and scope
The original P16.4 is not solved here. A definition of E 1 , a completion of a space of measures, and
differentiation of continuous metric volumes do not identify a genuine Monge–Ampere operator on
unbounded potentials. The verified source statements below do not supply that identification over
arbitrary complete valued fields in general dimension.
We prove a partial theorem on the entire Berkovich projective line, with arbitrary measure support.
Its potentials can be unbounded, its operator agrees with the usual continuous Monge–Ampere
measure, and its measure condition is exactly the dual finite-energy condition. This is a reconstruction
of classical curve potential theory in a particularly explicit sector, not a claimed new higher-
dimensional theorem. All arguments needed to identify its energy and operator are given below.
Retained hypotheses for the partial theorem. Let K be algebraically closed and complete
for a nontrivial real-valued non-Archimedean absolute value, with residue characteristic
zero. Let X = P1K , let d ≥ 1 be an integer, and let L = O(d). Thus X is smooth, geometrically
connected, algebraic, proper and projective, and L is ample. The additional restriction X = P1 and
the additional algebraic-closedness assumption are genuine restrictions. No discreteness, spherical
completeness, countability, or function-field algebraizability is assumed. Residue characteristic zero
is retained to keep this result on the assigned valuation axis, although the tree proof does not use
that characteristic restriction. No descent to a non-algebraically-closed ground field is claimed.

The rooted kernel and finite trees
The tree T has unique paths. On its nonrigid part these have their usual logarithmic length; paths
ending at a rigid point have infinite length. For x, y ∈ T let x ∧o y be the last point common to
their paths from o, and put

                             k(x, y) = length([o, x] ∩ [o, y]) ∈ [0, +∞].

In particular k(o, y) = 0. All nonrigid points have finite depth ℓ(x) = k(x, x); type IV endpoints
have positive limiting disk radius, since K is complete. No spherical completeness is needed.
Let F range over finite rooted subtrees whose vertices are type II points and which contain o. The
order is inclusion. Type II points are dense along every finite nondegenerate path because the
nonzero divisible group log |K × | is dense in R. Let rF : T → F be nearest rooted-path retraction
and set
                                        kF (x, y) = k(rF x, rF y).
These kernels are bounded and continuous on T × T , increase with F , and have supremum k. Indeed
kF counts the common path inside F ; along every common path its finite segments are exhausted by
type II cutoffs. Continuity follows from the disk weak topology: a finite-path retraction is constant
on each attached open branch and continuous along its finitely many retained edges.
Continuous functions factoring through finite-tree retractions form an algebra, contain constants,
and separate points. For distinct points a type II cutoff along a distinguishing path separates their
projections. Stone–Weierstrass therefore gives their uniform density in C(T ). If g = g ◦ rF and
G ⊃ F , then g ◦ rG = g. Approximation by such g proves f ◦ rG → f uniformly for every f ∈ C(T ),
and hence (rF )∗ µ → µ weakly for any Radon measure. Piecewise affine functions with type II knots
are likewise uniformly dense: approximate a continuous function on a finite compact graph by linear
interpolation and move knots to nearby type II points.

the strong-topology homeomorphism in Theorem 1, including for nets.

regularization. Continuity of psh envelopes for smooth projective curves over complete nontrivially

approximation, the unbounded energy derivative and comparison used in the proof have all been
proved above. This bypass is confined to a linear one-dimensional equation.
