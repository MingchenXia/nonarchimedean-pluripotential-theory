1. The algebraic curve and its actual polarization
Take K = C((t)) with 0 < |t| < 1. Let D be an integral rational projective nodal curve, with
normalization ν : P1K → D identifying the rational points 0 and ∞ to its sole singular point q. Such
a curve is obtained from a rational plane nodal cubic by a projective change of the normalization
parameter. It is geometrically integral and proper; it is not geometrically unibranch at q.
Glue the fibers of OP1 (2) at 0 and ∞ using the identity in the standard local frames. This gives a
line bundle L on D with ν ∗ L = O(2). Locally at the node its sections are pairs of regular functions
with the same value at the two branches. This module is free of rank one over the nodal local
ring after choosing the matching frames, so the gluing gives an invertible sheaf, rather than only
a torsion-free rank-one sheaf. Its degree is two; on an integral projective curve positive degree is
ample.

Bounded endpoint obstruction. There is no continuous global semipositive metric on L whose
genuine Chambert–Loir measure is µr .
Proof. For a continuous semipositive metric ϕ on L, normalization pullback is a continuous

For model metrics this is the degree-one intersection projection formula on a common model. For

4. Precisely stated finite-energy consequence
Consider a proposed equation theory on this (D, L) with the following interfaces:
   1. Its admissible potentials are actual psh metrics on L, so normalization pulls them back to the
      usual psh metric class on O(2), with the values of their reference differences equal at the two
      normalization preimages of q.
   2. Its finite-energy domain is the usual monotone extension of the continuous model energy, with
      the pullback/energy compatibility described in Section 3. In particular the target µr meets its
      raw mass and finite dual-energy conditions.
   3. Its measure operator agrees on the nonsingular locus with the usual one-dimensional
      curvature/Monge–Ampere operator and is positive of raw mass two.
Consequence. Such a theory cannot have an existence theorem for every measure of mass two and
finite dual energy.
Proof. A solution for µr would pull back to a psh metric ϕν on O(2) of finite energy. On P1 \ {0, ∞},
locality and interface 3 force its curvature to be 2δζr . Its usual positive global curvature has total
mass two. This is the degree formula on the smooth normalization; for finite energy its full-mass
measure also has total mass two. There is therefore no remaining positive mass at 0 or ∞. The
normalization equation is exactly the one solved by ϕr . Curve uniqueness gives ϕν = ϕr + c, again
contradicting interface 1. This proof only needs locality on the normalization-isomorphic locus
and the known smooth-curve mass; it does not assume an unproved global finite-energy projection
formula on the singular curve.
The example explains why merely normalizing a multibranch curve cannot prove the requested
equation theorem. It satisfies projectivity, ampleness, discrete valuation, characteristic-zero residue
and divisorial support; those axes cause no failure here. The conflict is the conductor’s equality
condition on metric values. A geometrically unibranch curve has no pair of preimages with this
condition, so this example is not an obstruction in that range. Higher-dimensional singular and the
genuinely unibranch extension of original P16.1 remain separate obligations.
