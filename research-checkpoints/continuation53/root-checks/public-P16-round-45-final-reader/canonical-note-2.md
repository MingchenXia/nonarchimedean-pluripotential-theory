Actual-X finite-energy MA is therefore the root-plus-Stieltjes measure, with inverse obtained by integrating measure tails. Theorem, a partial result. Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic zero with the trivial valuation, X an integral projective unibranch k-curve, and L an ample line bundle of degree V>0 on actual X. Integrality retains reducedness and connectedness; the field is complete. Use the canonical trivial metric and normalized intersection energy E(0)=0, E(φ+c)=E(φ)+c. No smoothness or normality of X, reducedness of its conductor, atomicity or skeletal support is assumed. The genuine raw finite-energy MA has mass V and induces a bijection E¹(X,L)/R onto positive Radon μ satisfying μ(X^an)=V and E*(μ/V)=sup_{φ∈E¹}(E(φ)−V⁻¹∫φ dμ)<∞. The published background is precisely scoped in n1.

Normalization and PL descent. The normalization ν:Y→X is finite; Y is smooth because k is perfect and Y is a normal curve. Properness centers every valuation of the common function field. At a closed point q of Y the local DVR makes every nontrivial valuation t ord_q, t>0. The other analytic points are the common trivial root o and closed-support endpoints q∞. Unibranchness gives one q over each p∈X(k), with residue field k. Thus ν^an bijects both generic-support valuations and endpoints. Compactness of the source and Hausdorffness of the target make it a homeomorphism.

Let m_p O_{Y,q}=m_q^{e_p}, where 1≤e_p<∞. Away from q the extended point ideal is the unit ideal. For rational a>0, the actual-X flag-ideal function h_{p,a}=e_p⁻¹ max(log|m_p|,−e_p a) pulls back to −min(t,a) on the q-ray and zero on every other ray. It belongs to PL(X) by BJ Example 2.5. These functions and rational constants span PL(Y) by Example 2.6. Hence ν* identifies PL(X) with PL(Y), also over R. The conductor Ann(ν_*O_Y/O_X) has finite support and may be nonreduced. No arbitrary section or power descent is used; e_p is the point-ideal order, not a conductor exponent. The single branch is essential to this isolated-ray identity.

Polarization and positivity. The quotient in 0→O_X→ν_*O_Y→T→0 has finite length. Tensoring by L^m and comparing linear coefficients of χ(Y,(ν*L)^m) and χ(X,L^m) proves deg_Y ν*L=V. Positive degree on a projective integral curve is ample, so the upstairs polarization is genuinely ample, with no mass multiplier. Canonical reference metrics pull back through local trivializations at centers.

For a descended PL function, the graph construction gives a projective surjective morphism of test configurations. Pullback preserves nefness. Conversely each vertical curve downstairs is dominated by a curve upstairs, obtained by projective hyperplane cuts; its pullback degree is a positive degree multiple of the downstairs degree. Thus nefness reflects. Common dominating models preserve the model-psh property. This applies BJ Proposition 3.6(vi) after PL descent has been proved. Approximate any psh function on Y decreasingly by H_dom(Y,ν*L). Its PL approximants descend and are nef-psh on actual X; decreasing closure gives psh descent. Ordinary pullback gives the reverse inclusion. Therefore ν* is a psh-cone isomorphism and a weak homeomorphism, since divisorial valuations of the common function field are identified. No envelope property on X has been assumed.

Energy and operator. Write a=φ(o) and b_q(t)=−φ'_q(t) almost everywhere. The smooth-curve criterion, transported through the proved cone isomorphism, says b_q≥0 is nonincreasing, Σ_q b_q(0+)≤V, and φ_q(t)=a−∫₀ᵗb_q. Endpoints have limiting values, possibly −∞. Only countably many rays are nonconstant. For PL potentials the genuine raw intersection measure is Vδ_o+Δφ. Integration by parts gives E(φ)=a−D(φ)/(2V), where D(φ)=Σ_q∫₀∞b_q². Model energy projection follows from the normalization fundamental-cycle projection formula, so no conductor boundary term is omitted.

Approximate b_q increasingly from below by nonincreasing rational step functions on finitely many rays and bounded intervals, using refining dyadic right-endpoint samples and rounding down. Initial budgets stay ≤V. Their integrals give decreasing PL psh approximants, including at endpoints. Monotone energy extension and monotone convergence prove the same formula for every psh φ. Thus E¹ is exactly D(φ)<∞. Actual ample-model approximation is also available: if f_n↓φ and ||h_n−f_n||∞≤ε_n=2⁻ⁿ, the potentials h_n+3ε_n decrease with the same limit. Proposition 3.10 supplies h_n∈H_dom(X,L) in the fixed ample class.

Finite D forces b_q(∞)=0. Define MA_raw,X(φ)=(V−Σ b_q(0+))δ_o+Σ_q(−db_q)|_(0,∞), transported to actual X. Each positive Stieltjes ray measure has mass b_q(0+); total mass is exactly V and endpoints have no mass. The countable sum is Radon, with total-variation tails tending to zero. It agrees with model intersection MA and therefore with the unique published decreasing finite-energy extension, by density of actual-X PL test functions. Energy projection extends decreasingly, identifying E¹ spaces and yielding MA_raw,X(φ)=ν_*MA_raw,Y(ν*φ). This is genuine MA, not another inverse measure construction.

Measure energy and inverse. Rays minus o, including endpoints, are disjoint open subsets. A compact subset of their union meets finitely many rays; Radon inner regularity therefore concentrates any finite μ away from o on countably many rays. For raw mass V, let μ_q be its ray restriction and τ_q(t)=μ_q((t,∞]), including possible endpoint mass. Put D(μ)=Σ∫τ_q². Endpoint mass forces D(μ)=∞. Tonelli gives −∫(φ−a)dμ=Σ∫b_qτ_q. If D(μ)<∞, Cauchy–Schwarz proves integrability of every finite-energy potential. Completing the square gives E(φ)−V⁻¹∫φdμ≤D(μ)/(2V).

For the reverse inequality, use continuous psh tests with slopes τ_q 1_[0,T) on finitely many rays F and zero elsewhere. Their initial budget is ≤V, and their objective is (2V)⁻¹Σ_{q∈F}∫₀ᵀτ_q². Increasing F,T proves E*(μ/V)=D(μ)/(2V), including the infinite case. Uniform H_dom approximation of these tests, and decreasing approximation with monotone integration for E¹ tests, proves equivalence of the two suprema.

When D(μ)<∞, define φ(o)=0 and φ_q(t)=−∫₀ᵗτ_q(s)ds=−∫min(t,u)dμ_q(u), with endpoint limits. The finite F,T tests decrease to it, so it is psh on actual X. Its energy is −D(μ)/(2V). Its Stieltjes measures are μ_q and its root coefficient is μ({o}), giving MA_raw(φ)=μ. Conversely any finite-energy solution has −db_q=μ_q and b_q(∞)=0, hence b_q=τ_q. These slopes determine all values, including endpoint limits, up to the root constant. This proves necessity and uniqueness without assuming an upstairs solution descends.

Required analytic replacements. The weak normalized psh cone on actual X is homeomorphic to the smooth Y cone, compact by BJ Theorems 5.20/5.11. Analytic homeomorphism commutes with usc regularization, and the proved cone bijection identifies every envelope competitor: ν*P_X(f)=P_Y(ν*f). Envelopes and their continuity therefore descend without a circular target-envelope assumption.

The quadratic energy differentiates along E¹ segments to V⁻¹∫(ψ−φ)MA_raw(φ). BJ Theorem 8.5 gives downward-energy differentiation for actual-X PL variations without smoothness. The proved envelope property identifies downward energy with E(P(φ+tf)). Uniform PL approximation with |f−f_m|≤ε_m bounds perturbed energy errors by |t|ε_m, proving d/dt|₀ E(P(φ+tf))=V⁻¹∫f MA_raw(φ) for every continuous f. For u=P(f), P(u+t(f−u))=u when 0≤t≤1, so differentiation also proves ∫(f−u)MA_raw(u)=0.

BJ locality for finite-energy maxima applies to the identified genuine operator. Equal raw mass and locality for max(φ,ψ−ε), followed by ε↓0, give ∫_{φ<ψ}MA_raw(ψ)≤∫_{φ<ψ}MA_raw(φ). Possible negative-infinite loci here are endpoints with zero MA mass. Published decreasing continuity holds for every finite-energy approximating net, not only the selected steps; Proposition 12.7 gives strong continuity. The cone and energy identifications preserve weak-plus-energy strong potential topology and weak-plus-dual-energy strong measure topology. The completed-square bound is attained by the explicit inverse, establishing actual variational attainment.

For a continuous semipositive reference ρ, relative energy is E_ρ(φ−ρ)=E(φ)−E(ρ), by the energy cocycle and decreasing extension. Dual energy becomes E*(μ/V)−E(ρ)+V⁻¹∫ρdμ, a finite change. Thus finite energy is independent of these bounded reference changes. Integrality makes one additive real constant the correct uniqueness ambiguity.
