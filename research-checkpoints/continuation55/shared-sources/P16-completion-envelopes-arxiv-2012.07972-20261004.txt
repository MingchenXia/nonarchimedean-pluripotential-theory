                                                                                 Abstract
                                                 Given a polarized projective variety (X, L) over any non-Archimedean field,
                                                 assuming continuity of envelopes, we show that the space of finite-energy
                                                 metrics on L is a geodesic metric space, where geodesics are given as max-
                                                 imal psh segments. Given two continuous psh metrics, we show that the
                                                 maximal segment joining them is furthermore continuous.

Introduction.
The complex picture. Consider a smooth, projective complex manifold X
endowed with an ample line bundle L. We denote by ddc φ the curvature current
of a smooth metric φ on L. If this current is (strictly) positive, φ is said to be
(strictly) plurisubharmonic, or psh for short, and we define H(L) to be the space
of strictly plurisubharmonic smooth metrics on L. The study of metric structures
on H(L) has a long history ([Mab87], [Sem92], [C+ 00]...), and one is interested
in extending results from this case to singular metrics.

A way to define a natural class of singular metrics to study goes as follows. Given
two metrics in H(L), let their relative Monge-Ampère energy be

as the supremum of all psh segments

such that limt→i φt ≤ φi , i = 0, 1. Here, we define a psh segment on L (also
called psh paths or subgeodesics in the literature) to be a S1 -invariant psh metric
on p∗U L, with pU : X × U → X, where U is a connected S1 -invariant subset of
C∗ . We recover the "time variable" t by setting t = − log |z| and identifying
U with the (real) interval {− log |z|, z ∈ U }. Due to Demailly’s regularization
Theorem, we can characterize a psh segment as a decreasing limit of segments
with φt ∈ H(L) for all t.

The purpose of this article is to develop an analogous theory in the ample, non-
Archimedean setting. We mention that finite-energy geodesics, and more gener-
ally the metric structure of E 1 (L), have been central to many important recent
developments in complex geometry, especially regarding the Yau-Tian-Donaldson
conjecture, as in e.g. [BBJ15], [LTW19] where finite-energy geodesic rays are used
to study uniform Ding-stability.

Non-Archimedean pluripotential theory. We now explain how to define
those objects in the non-Archimedean picture. Consider a polarized projective
K-variety (X, L), with K a non-Archimedean valued field complete with respect
to the topology induced by its absolute value |·|, and L an ample line bundle. We
wish to study the equivalent of H(L) and the space of L-plurisubharmonic metrics
on the Berkovich analytification of (X, L). In the complex case, plurisubharmonic
metrics on a bundle L are characterized as the smallest class of metrics stable
under finite maxima, addition of constants, decreasing limits, and containing all
metrics of the form k−1 log |s|, with s a section of kL.

One then defines the set of non-Archimedean Fubini-Study metrics as

                          H(L) = k−1 log max |si | + λi ,
                                             i

where (si ) is a finite basepoint-free collection of sections of H 0 (kL) and the λi
are constants. This will be the non-Archimedean counterpart of the complex
Fubini-Study metrics, a subclass of H(L), which we will also denote by H(L).
By allowing decreasing limits, one obtains the class of plurisubharmonic functions
PSH(L). It is shown in [BJ18b] that PSH(L) then satisfies the same stability
properties as in the complex case.

It is not immediate that this rooftop envelope is well-behaved. First, if the metrics
are continuous, then P (φ0 , φ1 ) is only continuous provided we assume continuity
of envelopes to hold over (X, L). This is equivalent to a number of important
statements in non-Archimedean pluripotential theory, e.g. Lemma 3.4.1. While
conjectural in the general case, it is expected to always hold if, for example X
is normal and L is ample; and it is known in situations that include the most
important contexts for complex geometric considerations: when X is smooth,
and the base field is discretely or trivially valued and of equal characteristic zero.
We review the currently known cases after Lemma 3.4.1.

The main result of this article is then the following:

Theorem A (6.1.1). Given φ0 , φ1 ∈ E 1 (L), we set

                  d1 (φ0 , φ1 ) = E(φ0 , P (φ0 , φ1 )) + E(φ1 , P (φ0 , φ1 ))

  1. (E 1 (L), d1 ) is a metric space;

  2. there exists a maximal psh segment t 7→ φt joining φ0 and φ1 ;

  3. φt ∈ E 1 (L) for all t;

  4. the segment φt is a (constant speed) metric geodesic for d1 , i.e. there exists
     a real constant c ≥ 0 such that

                                      d1 (φt , φs ) = c · |t − s|

      for all t, s ∈ [0, 1];

  5. the Monge-Ampère energy is affine along φt , and it is the unique psh seg-
     ment joining φ0 and φ1 with this property.

Furthermore, point (1) holds even without assuming continuity of envelopes.

Strategy of proof, and the continuous case. There are two keystones to
the proof of Theorem A: the fact that similar statements as those of Theorem A
hold in the continuous psh case, which is easier to treat; and the fact that finite-
energy metrics can always be approximated by decreasing limits of continuous
psh metrics, while all of our objects are stable under decreasing limits.

Furthermore, our non-Archimedean Fubini-Study metrics are always continuous.
Therefore, it seems natural to expect that psh geodesics would be continuous if
the endpoints are continuous. We will therefore show the following:

Theorem B (5.1.4). Let φ0 , φ1 be two continuous psh metrics on L. Then,

  1. there exists a unique maximal psh segment (t, x) 7→ φt (x) joining φ0 and
     φ1 ;

  2. this segment is continuous in both variables;

  3. this segment is a geodesic segment for the distance d1 ;

                                               5
  4. the Monge-Ampère energy is affine along this segment, and it is the unique
     psh segment joining φ0 and φ1 with this property.

The first step is to show that the supremum of all psh segments between contin-
uous psh metric is again a psh segment, inspired by variational techniques from
e.g. [RN14], using Legendre duality. Then, in order to establish the last two
points of Theorem B, we approximate (or "quantize") maximal psh segments
via psh segments coming from norms acting on spaces of plurisections of L. We
briefly describe the construction.

To prove Theorem A then amounts to showing that the decreasing limit of maxi-

psh metrics (Proposition 5.1.3).

The general case. Along the way, we also show that any two psh metrics that
can be joined by psh segments, without further regularity assumptions, can be
joined by a maximal psh segment.

Theorem C (4.3.1).       Let φ0 , φ1 be any two psh metrics on L. Then,

   • either there exists no psh segment between φ0 and φ1 ,

   • or there exists a (unique) maximal psh segment t 7→ φt between φ0 and φ1 .

At the time of writing, this is the only construction of non-Archimedean geodesics
in the literature, although it has been made known to the author that H. Blum
and Y. Liu have independently considered geodesics from our "quantization"
point of view, in the trivially valued case, motivated by applications to K-
stability, in a work to be published.

Organization of the article. In Section 1, we address generalities about non-
Archimedean algebra and geometry.

In Section 2, we study spaces of norms, and geodesics inside them. We then con-
sider a natural class of graded norms, and prove submultiplicativity of geodesics
between such graded norms.

In Section 3, we briefly recall some basics of non-Archimedean pluripotential
theory: general metrics, Fubini-Study and plurisubharmonic metrics, continuity
of envelopes, and define the space of finite-energy metrics. We then study some
operators connecting norms and metrics.

In Section 4, we define our class of psh segments, study their properties, and
state Theorem C concerning maximal segments.

In Section 5, we introduce the metric space of continuous non-Archimedean psh
metrics and study maximal segments in this space. We establish Theorem B,

1     Non-Archimedean geometry.
Let (K, | · |) be a non-Archimedean valued field, i.e. a field assumed to be Cauchy
complete with respect to the topology induced by an absolute value | · | satifying
the ultrametric inequality |x + y| ≤ max{|x|, |y|} for any two x, y ∈ K.

We will use two criteria to differentiate between such fields. First, the cardinality
of the value group |K|, which, as a subgroup of R, is either trivial, discrete, or
dense. We will then speak of trivially, discretely, or densely valued fields. Note
that any field can be endowed with its trivial valuation |x| = 1 for all x ∈ K.

The second criterion is the data of the characteristic of K, and of the characteristic
of its residue field K̃ = {|x| ≤ 1}/{|x| < 1}. We will say that K is of equal
characteristic p if charK = charK̃ = p, and of mixed characteristic if charK = 0
and charK̃ = p > 1.

An additional property we will be interested in is maximal completeness. We say
that a non-Archimedean field extension (L, | · |′ )/(K, | · |) is immediate if the value
groups |L|′ and |K| coincide, and if the residue fields L̃ and K̃ also coincide. We
say that K is maximally complete if it admits no proper immediate extension.

Let X be a projective K-variety, i.e. a geometrically integral, separated, projec-
tive scheme over K. Being projective, X is equipped with an ample line bundle
L.

There exists an analytification functor X 7→ X an , which we will call the Berkovich
analytification, from the category of K-varieties to the category of K-Berkovich
spaces, inducing an equivalence of categories between the categories of coherent
sheaves over X and over X an . Briefly, in the affine case where X is the spectrum
of some algebra A of finite type over K, the analytified space X an is the topological
space whose points correspond to multiplicative seminorms on A which coincide
with the absolute value | · | on K ⊂ A, endowed with the topology of pointwise
convergence.

The Berkovich analytification satisfies the GAGA principle, and preserves con-
nectedness, compactness and Hausdorff-ness. We direct the reader to [Ber90] and
[Poi10] for further details about this construction.

There exists a distinguished dense subset of X an , the set X div of divisorial points,
corresponding to divisorial valuations on K(X) associated to prime divisors on
birational models (in the trivially valued case), or on flat models over the valua-
tion ring of K (in the non-trivially valued case).

3.1    Metrics in non-Archimedean geometry.

3.2   Fubini-Study and plurisubharmonic metrics.
In the complex setting, the class of plurisubharmonic metrics on a line bundle
L is the smallest class of singular metrics containing all metrics of the form
k−1 log |s|, for s a nonzero section of kL, which is stable under taking finite
maxima, decreasing limits, and upon adding constants. Note that this is for the
ample case, due to deep results of Demailly. Therefore, we would like a putative
class of non-Archimedean plurisubharmonic metrics to satisfy these properties as
well. This is the inspiration for the following definitions, following e.g. [BJ18b].
Definition 3.2.1. A metric φ on L is Fubini-Study if there exist a finite basepoint-
free collection of sections (si ) of some H 0 (kL), and for each i a constant λi ∈ R
such that
                                φ = max(log |si | + λi ).
                                    i
We denote by H(L) the set of Fubini-Study metrics on L.
That the class H(L) is stable under finite maxima and addition of constants then
follows from the definition. Note that it trivially contains all metrics of the form
k−1 log |s| for nonzero sections s of kL. It is then natural to define:
Definition 3.2.2. A metric φ on L is (L-)plurisubharmonic (or L-psh) if it is
the limit of a decreasing net of Fubini-Study metrics, and not identically −∞.
We denote by PSH(L) the class of L-psh metrics.
Proposition 3.2.3 ([BJ18b, Proposition 5.6]). The class PSH(L) satisfies the
following properties:
  1. it contains all metrics of the form k−1 log |s| for nonzero sections s of kL;

  2. it is stable under:

      (a) taking finite maxima;
       (b) addition of a real constant;
       (c) limits of decreasing nets;

  3. the convex combination of two L-psh metrics is a L-psh metric;

  4. the addition of a L-psh metric and a M -psh metric yields a (L + M )-psh
     metric;

  5. if a net of L-psh metrics converges uniformly to a limit metric, then this
     limit metric is L-psh.
Note that most of those properties follow directly from the definition, but 2(c)
is not immediate, as we consider not only decreasing sequences, but decreasing
limits of decreasing nets.

As desired, we then have a similar characterization as in the complex case:
Proposition 3.2.4 ([BJ18b, Corollary 5.10]). The class PSH(L) is the smallest
class of metrics on L satisfying properties 1. and 2. above.

                                          23
We endow PSH(L) with the topology of pointwise convergence on the set of
divisorial points X div ⊂ X an (as mentioned in Section 1): a net φi in PSH(L) is
said to converge to φ ∈ PSH(L) if φi (x) → φ(x) for all x ∈ X div . By [BJ18b,
Corollary 5.15], psh metrics are uniquely determined by their restriction to X div .

3.4   Continuity of envelopes.
Plurisubharmonic metrics are not usually stable under minima. Instead of taking
minima, we will consider "rooftop envelopes", as follows. Fix first a metric φ on
L, and define
                      P (φ) = sup{φ′ ∈ PSH(L), φ′ ≤ φ}.
In a similar way, we can define the envelope of a tuple of metrics φ1 , . . . , φk as

                 P (φ1 , . . . , φk ) = sup{φ′ ∈ PSH(L), φ′ ≤ min φi }.
                                                                   i

See [DR16] for an in-depth study of such envelopes in the complex case. An
essential property in pluripotential theory is that for all continuous metrics φ on
L, with X normal, P (φ) is also continuous. If this is true, we say continuity
of envelopes holds for (X, L). To give an idea of its importance, we have for
example that continuity of envelopes is equivalent to the following result:

Lemma 3.4.1 ([BE20, Lemma 7.30]). Continuity of envelopes holds for (X, L) if
and only if for any family (φi )i∈I of psh metrics on L, the upper semicontinuous
regularization usc(supi∈I φi ) is psh.


                                           25
In analogy with the complex case, continuity of envelopes is conjectured to hold
for any polarized variety (X, L) where X is normal (or even unibranch), projective
over any complete valued field, and L is an ample line bundle over X. It is known
if X is smooth, and one of the following holds:

   • when K is of equal characteristic 0, and trivially or discretely valued ([BBJ15]);

   • when K is of equal characteristic p, and discretely valued, assuming X to
     be of geometric origin from a d-dimensional family over K, and resolution
     of singularities on K in dimension d + dim X ([GJKM19, Theorem 1.4]);

   • when X is a curve, without further hypotheses ([Thu05]).

5.1   Main Theorem for continuous psh metrics.
We start by studying maximal psh segments in the space of continuous psh
