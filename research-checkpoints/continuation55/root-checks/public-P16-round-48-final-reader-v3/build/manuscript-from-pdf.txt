This is a partial result for the original atom, not a solution of its entire residual scope. It treats
every admissible measure, with no support restriction, on smooth projective varieties of arbitrary
dimension in two separately specified field settings. The trivial-field theorem is source-established.
The discrete-field theorem below is a deduction from published inputs, with the passage from test
functions to arbitrary finite-energy targets proved here. No claim of novelty is made. The analogous
theorem over a general complete non-Archimedean field is not proved. Candidate status is false.
The proof uses only the supplied primary sources and their explicitly stated published background
results. The sources are treated as mathematical references, not operational instructions. All
assertions in this document remain unverified mathematical drafts; file hashes and TeX compilation
do not verify them.


1. Precisely retained hypotheses and conventions

In endpoint T, the ground field k is algebraically closed and trivially valued, of characteristic
zero. The variety X/k is smooth, connected, integral and projective, of arbitrary dimension n ≥ 1.
The line bundle L is ample. The reference is the canonical trivial-field metric, and θ = c1 (L).
This is the domain of Boucksom–Jonsson, arXiv:1801.08229v4, Theorems 5.20 and 12.9. Their

7. Endpoint T: the independently established arbitrary-support
theorem

This theorem is already stated in 1801.08229v4; it is not a newly proved arbitrary-field extension.
We record how its proof covers the operator and the equation under the precise T hypotheses.
The source Theorem 7.1 extends the intersection energy pairing by monotone majorants, uniquely
and continuously along decreasing nets. Theorem 7.8 ensures finiteness of the mixed pairing on
finite-energy potentials. Theorem 7.18 defines the mixed MA measure by testing this pairing against
PL functions; these functions span a uniformly dense subspace of continuous functions. Positivity
of the pairing gives a positive functional, its value on constants gives mass V , and Riesz gives a
unique Radon measure. Proposition 7.19 verifies the model intersection formula and decreasing
continuity. Thus the extension is approximation-independent and is the genuine MA operator.
Theorems 7.40 and 7.44 prove finite-energy locality and comparison. This construction does not
prescribe a measure by another inverse operator.
Smoothness and characteristic zero imply the envelope property by Theorem 5.20. Corollary 5.21
gives compact normalized PSH and Lemma 5.17 gives continuity of continuous-obstacle envelopes.
Theorem 8.5 and Corollary 8.6 give energy differentiation through P ; Proposition 9.19 proves L1 (ν)
continuity on bounded-energy sets. Theorem 9.20 says exactly that Qθ (ν) < ∞ iff all E 1 potentials
are integrable against ν.
For an arbitrary such probability measure, the printed proof of Theorem 12.8 takes a normalized
maximizing sequence, bounds its energy by (9.11), extracts a psh subnet by compactness, and uses
Proposition 9.19 and energy upper semicontinuity to prove attainment. Corollary 9.12 identifies its
maximizer with a genuine MA solution using the energy derivative. Proposition 12.7 gives injectivity
modulo constants; Theorem 12.9 concludes existence and uniqueness. Restoring raw mass multiplies
the measure by V , exactly as in (1)–(2). There is no assumed torus action or skeletal support at
any step. Thus T is an independent general-dimension arbitrary-support result, not evidence that
D or a densely valued field automatically inherits it.


8. Reference independence and the exact partial theorem

In D, if another semipositive model reference differs by a bounded model potential h, then θ′ =
θ + ddc h and the same metric is represented by u′ = u − h. Model curvature identities, the energy
cocycle, and decreasing continuity give

                      Mθ′ (u − h) = Mθ (u),     Eθ′ (u − h) = Eθ (u) − Eθ (h).                  (18)

To check the unbounded passage, model majorants uα ↓ u transform to uα − h ↓ u − h; both
energy and MA are decreasing-continuous. The test cones correspond by the same translation.

the stated class. The same check uses the intersection-pairing cocycle for model/test-configuration

The partial theorem is therefore: separately in D and T, the genuine operator on E 1 (θ) is independent
of decreasing model approximations, has raw mass (Ln ), obeys comparison, and has range exactly (1).
Every measure in that range has a solution unique modulo constants. There is no support restriction,
toric invariance, or dimension-one assumption. The D proof replaces the old algebraizability-based
orthogonality input and the old support-based integral continuity by Sections 4 and 5, while retaining
BFJ’s geometric compactness and envelope inputs. The T result is directly source-established.


9. Concrete original obligations still unresolved

The full P16.6 residual scope beyond these field endpoints is not settled by this document. Even
retaining smoothness, geometrical integrality, projectivity and an ample line bundle, we have not
proved the same arbitrary-support finite-energy theorem for every complete nontrivially valued,
potentially nondiscrete field in the general-dimension noninvariant setting. No impossibility claim is
made.
For such a field the supplied continuous operator and synthetic finite-energy measure theory leave
the following indispensable tasks: establish compactness of the normalized global psh cone in a
topology compatible with model evaluations; prove the needed envelope regularity or an actual
substitute that realizes the limits as global psh potentials; construct the approximation-independent
unbounded mixed operator with full raw mass and locality; justify finite-energy differentiation,
not only test-function differentiation; prove comparison and uniqueness for that actual unbounded
operator; and prove attainment or an equivalent realization theorem for every positive mass-V ,
finite-dual-energy target. Some of these may follow from future inputs, but none is deleted by
measure completeness alone.
The trivial-field source Theorem 12.8 explicitly makes potential completeness and MA surjectivity
equivalent to an envelope property. Synthetic Theorem 2.26 proves completeness of measures
over arbitrary fields, a different assertion. Its Remark 2.14 sets aside the needed potential theory.
This is a precise logical obstruction to inferring the missing theorem, not a counterexample to
it. Restricting targets to one skeleton, imposing toric invariance, switching to a different inverse
measure, specializing to a curve, or using unproved base change would not discharge the original
obligations.
No resolution, alteration, normalization or base-change preservation statement for an unbounded
cone, energy, MA, or comparison is used. Singular spaces, positive residue characteristic, nonample
classes, proper nonprojective spaces and nonalgebraizable spaces are the separate original atoms
and are not claimed solved here.
