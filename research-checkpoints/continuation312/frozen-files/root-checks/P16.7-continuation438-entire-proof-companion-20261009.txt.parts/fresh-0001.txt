\begin{abstract}
For two distinct residue angles, the mixed current of the quadratic
Dirac pencils is the sum of the original Gauss atom and a deeper isotropic
atom. This gives the entire uniform closed convex hull of a countable
angular family: its parameters are subprobability weights, its measure
depends on their squares, and its energy on their cubes. Equal averages
have exact energy error $2a/(3N^2)$. Every member of this closed hull
has full-dual objective gap at least $a/3$ against the limiting isotropic
whole Dirac. Consequently even uniform closure and arbitrary convex
mixing of these solved equations cannot provide a maximizing sequence.
The original existence problem and the disclosed geometric and analytic
foundation qualifications remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{The exact mixed angular current}
\begin{lemma}\label{mixed}
For distinct $j,k$,
\[
 B(Q_j,Q_k)=\nu_*=\delta_g+\delta_{x_{2a}}.
\]
Also $B(Q_j,q_*)=\nu_*$ for every $j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We identify two unit atoms of the actual positive mixed measure, whose
global mass is two. On the ordinary neighborhood of $x_{2a}$ where
$\max(|u|,|v|)<\sqrt R$, the product satisfies $|uv|<R$.
Both local potentials therefore differ smoothly harmonically from
$\log\max(R,|L_j|)$ and $\log\max(R,|L_k|)$.
The linear map $(u,v)\mapsto(L_j,L_k)$ has determinant
$\alpha_k-\alpha_j$, a norm-one unit, and its inverse has norm-one
coefficients. The dimension-one Gauss-fibre formula for the first
coordinate, proper restriction to its fixed completed-residue-field
curve, and the same formula for the second give the joint radius-$(R,R)$
Gauss point, coefficient one. This point is exactly $x_{2a}$: the unit
linear map preserves the equal-radius Gauss norm in both directions.
It belongs to the neighborhood since $R<\sqrt R$. Thus the mixed
measure has a unit atom there, on all analytic continuous tests.

Near $g$, take the ordinary open conditions
$|u|>\sqrt R$, $|v|>\sqrt R$; the product slot strictly exceeds the cap.
The translated coordinates are nonzero and, up to the smooth harmonic
logarithm of $uv$, the two potentials are
\[
 \log\max(1,|s_j|),\quad\log\max(1,|s_k|),
 \qquad s_j=1/v+\alpha_j/u.
\]
In the actual inverse coordinates $(1/u,1/v)$ the pair $(s_j,s_k)$
is another norm-one linear isomorphism. Its whole joint unit Gauss fibre
is the unit Gauss point in those inverse coordinates. Inversion and unit
translation give exactly the original $g$. The same two fixed coordinate
curve formulas give coefficient one, degree one. The entire joint fibre
lies in the chosen open neighborhood; no hidden additional fibre occurs.

These two atoms exhaust the positive mixed mass two, so there is no
remaining support. This argument uses actual products and fibres,
not a guess from disjoint negative defects. Finally average arbitrarily
many other angles, excluding $j$. The at-most-one-defect identity gives
uniform distance at most $a/N$ from $q_*$. Multilinearity gives
$B(Q_j,N^{-1}\sum_{k\ne j}Q_k)=\nu_*$. Local uniform product continuity
passes to the limit and proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{The complete uniform closed hull and its operator}
For a sequence $\beta=(\beta_j)$ with $\beta_j\ge0$ and
$\sum_j\beta_j\le1$, define
\[
 q_\beta=q_*+\sum_j\beta_j(Q_j-q_*),\qquad
 S_2=\sum_j\beta_j^2,\quad S_3=\sum_j\beta_j^3.
\]
The series converges uniformly: its tail norm is at most
$a\sup_{j>N}\beta_j\to0$, since at most one defect is nonzero at any
point. All these weights are bounded actual members of $\C$.

\begin{theorem}\label{hull}
The entire uniform closed convex hull of $\{Q_j:j\ge1\}$ consists
precisely of the $q_\beta$ above. Its norm, actual measure, energy and
full attained dual are
\[
 \|q_\beta-q_\gamma\|_\infty=a\sup_j|\beta_j-\gamma_j|,
\]
\[
 \MA(q_\beta)=(1-S_2)(\delta_g+\delta_{x_{2a}})
                    +2\sum_j\beta_j^2\delta_{\xi_j},
\]
\[
 E(q_\beta)=-\tfrac13-\tfrac{2a}3(1+S_3),\quad
 D(\MA(q_\beta))=\tfrac16+\tfrac{4a}3(1+S_3).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Finite convex combinations have probability parameters. The disjoint
pointwise defects bound their norm difference by $a\sup|\beta-\gamma|$;
evaluation at each $\xi_j$ gives the reverse bound. Any uniform limit
therefore has coordinatewise nonnegative limiting parameters, with sum
at most one by every finite partial sum, and the stated pointwise series
identifies the limit. Conversely $q_*$ belongs to the closed hull by the
uniform averages from ROOT437. The finite weights
\[
 q^{(N)}=(1-\sum_{j\le N}\beta_j)q_*+\sum_{j\le N}\beta_jQ_j
\]
belong to that closed hull and converge uniformly to $q_\beta$. Closure
of the actual minimal class proves positivity, including when the sum
is strictly below one.

For finite parameters, Lemma \ref{mixed}, $\MA(q_*)=\nu_*$ and
multilinearity give
\[
 \MA(q^{(N)})=(1-\sum_{j\le N}\beta_j^2)\nu_*
                  +2\sum_{j\le N}\beta_j^2\delta_{\xi_j}.
\]
Uniform product continuity and summable tails give the full formula.
This is a positive Radon measure of mass two, since $S_2\le1$;
the countable sum converges in total variation. Also $B(q_0,q_\beta)=2g$.
Writing $w=q_\beta-q_0$, its values are
\[
 w(g)=0,\quad w(x_{2a})=-2a,\quad
 w(\xi_j)=-a(1+\beta_j),\quad w(x_a)=-a.
\]
Consequently $\int w\,\MA(q_\beta)=-2a(1+S_3)$.
The scalar ray $q_0+h w$ has actual measure
$2g+h^2(\MA(q_\beta)-2g)$, so first variation gives the energy formula.
All target points have baseline value $-1/4$. The moment is therefore
$-1/2-2a(1+S_3)$, and the entire-class tangent inequality gives the
full-dual formula, attained at this actual bounded weight.
\end{proof}

For the equal average $A_N=N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^NQ_j$ this specializes to
\[

\]
The already proved uniform error is exactly $a/N$, but the energy error

\[

 =\tfrac16+\tfrac{4a}3-\tfrac{2a}3S_3,
\]
\[
 D(\mu)-\left(E(q_\beta)-\int q_\beta\,d\mu\right)
 \ge\tfrac a3+\tfrac{2a}3S_3\ge\tfrac a3.
\]
Thus no sequence in this entire closed hull is a maximizing sequence,

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

Subtracting yields the gap. Adding a constant shifts energy and target
moment by the same twice-constant, leaving the objective unchanged.
\end{proof}
