\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct the continuous and decreasing-generated Monge--Ampere
calculus on a particular proper, genuinely nonalgebraizable analytic
surface, including point atoms, full pointwise generated uniqueness,
finite scalar extension, and fixed bounded curvature probes. We then
prove a quantitative ordered energy-gap estimate. It shows that every
energy-bounded decreasing net of actual generated weights has an actual
continuous decreasing sequence presentation, without a countable
cofinality assumption. Its energy, Monge--Ampere measures, mixed products
and moving moments are continuous in the directed order. For each
arbitrary-support finite-full-dual measure there is also actual ordered
net $L^1$ convergence. These results do not prove compactness of arbitrary
normalized energy-sublevel nets, obstacle orthogonality or arbitrary-measure
existence. The unchanged original P16.7 remains unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Carrier, positivity and conventional foundations}
Set $K=\mathbb C((s))$, $|s|=e^{-1/8}$, $t=s^8$, and
\[
Y=(\mathbb G_m^2)^{\rm an}_K,\quad
\Lambda=\langle(t,2),(3,t)\rangle,\quad X=Y/\Lambda,
\qquad \tau=(-\log|z_1|,-\log|z_2|).
\]
The valuation translations of the two generators are the standard basis
of $\mathbb Z^2$. Tropical rectangles of widths less than one have
disjoint nonidentity translates and give ordinary open injective charts.
The compact polyannulus $\tau^{-1}([0,1]^2)$ covers the quotient.
On bounded boxes only finitely many closed deck graphs meet. A finite
union of injective charts is a flat quasi-finite surjection with finite
fibres; descent of the relation's coherent ideal makes the diagonal
closed. This uses Conrad--Temkin Lemmas 4.2.3--4.2.4: local quotients of
an etale relation on an ordinary open cover glue to the analytic quotient,
and coherent G-sheaves descend along flat quasi-finite analytic
surjections. The latter is used on the finite chart cover, never on the
infinite-sheeted map $Y\to X$. Thus $X$ is compact, separated, good,
strict, smooth, connected, boundaryless and pure of dimension two.
Strict affinoid neighbourhoods follow from rational radii in the divisible
value group. Compactness and absence of boundary give properness.

Here is a direct nonalgebraization argument, with its algebraic and
analytic foundations stated. Gruson IV.5 Theorem 1 says that completed
products of disc algebras with radii in the divisible value group and
unit-circle algebras have $SK^0=0$. Taking determinants trivializes
rank-one projective modules on these products. For a wide annulus,
trivialize its line bundle on the inner circle times the other factors,
glue to a trivial bundle on the inner disc, and fill to the outer disc.
Tate--Kiehl coherent gluing, in Van der Put 2.14--2.16.3, applies to this
finite rational cover, whose intersection is the inner circle. Induction
on the wide annulus factors gives
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(P_m)=0,
\quad P_m=\{|s|^m\le |z_i|\le |s|^{-m},\ i=1,2\}.
\]
This does not apply Gruson's theorem directly to wide annuli.
For a unit $b=\sum b_\beta z^\beta$ on $P_m$, its Gauss log norm and
the opposite log norm of its inverse are convex, so its log norm is
affine. A maximizing monomial at an interior point agrees with that
affine norm throughout the box. For its exponent $\alpha$,
\[
|b_\beta/b_\alpha|\exp((m/8)|\beta-\alpha|_1)\le1.
\]
Hence $b=b_\alpha z^\alpha(1+g)$ and
$\|g\|_{P_j}\le |s|^{m-j}$ for $j<m$. Consecutive frames can be
normalized by scalar monomials so their transition is $1+g_m$.
On every fixed $P_j$ the products of these transitions and inverse tails
converge. Correcting the frames by these products gives a global frame,
and therefore $\operatorname{Pic}(Y)=0$. A global analytic unit on $Y$
is a scalar monomial: a second exponent would dominate along a tropical
ray and contradict global affinity of the Gauss log norm.

Deck multipliers of a line bundle pulled to $Y$ are $c_i z^{h_i}$.
Write $h_1=(A,B)$, $h_2=(C,D)$. The cocycle condition is
$t^C2^D=3^At^B$, so $C=B$, $A=D=0$ and
$h_1=(0,k)$, $h_2=(k,0)$. If $k\ne0$, take
$\lambda=\lambda_1\lambda_2^{\operatorname{sign}k}$; its exponent
$h=(k\operatorname{sign}k,k)$ satisfies $v(\lambda^h)=2|k|>0$.
For a section $f(\lambda z)=cz^hf(z)$ its nonzero coefficient orbit is
\[
a_{m+jh}=a_m c^j\lambda^{-jm-hj(j+1)/2}.
\]
Its valuations have negative quadratic leading term, impossible for a
Laurent series convergent on the unit polyannulus. If $k=0$, two nonzero
exponents would differ by $(p,q)$ with $t^p2^q=3^pt^q=1$;
valuation and then constants force $p=q=0$. Thus each nonzero section
is a nowhere-zero monomial and sections with the same multipliers are
proportional.

For a meromorphic function $F$ the pole ideal
$J=\{b:bF\text{ is analytic}\}$ is a coherent invertible ideal:
locally it is the kernel of multiplication by a numerator into the
quotient by a denominator; regular local factoriality makes this kernel
principal, and flat analytic restriction preserves it. The conventional
inputs are Stacks 0AG0 (a regular local ring is a UFD) and Ducros,
\emph{Families of Berkovich spaces} 2.1.3(2),(5) and 2.1.4 (the
relevant affinoid/analytic-local restrictions and algebraic-to-analytic
local maps are regular). Trivialize $J$ on $Y$. Its frame and its product
with $F$ are sections with the same deck multipliers. Hence
$\Gamma(X,\mathscr M_X)=K$.
If $X=Z^{\rm an}$ for a separated finite-type algebraic space,
Conrad--Temkin Theorem 2.3.1 reflects separatedness, smoothness and
properness. Smooth connected $Z$ is integral of dimension two.
Stacks 086U supplies a dense scheme locus for a decent space, 0BB1
supplies finiteness of the generic points on quasi-compact etale charts,
and 0EN6, 0ENA, 0END identify its rational and meromorphic function
field. Their hypotheses (weak associates of codimension zero and
generic points represented by field monomorphisms) hold for smooth
separated finite-type $Z$. Flat analytic local maps preserve denominators;
a closed point in a scheme open detects each nonzero rational function.
Consequently $K(Z)\hookrightarrow\Gamma(X,\mathscr M_X)=K$, contrary
to $\operatorname{trdeg}_K K(Z)=2$. This proves genuine
nonalgebraizability over the stated field, without asserting it over all
extensions.

Locally put $\rho=|\tau|^2/2$ and $\theta=\DD\rho$.
Deck changes of $\rho$ are smooth affine unit logarithms. Identity-torus
calibration has saturated lattice $\mathbb Z^2$, analytic multiplicity
one and the raw factor $2!$, so $\theta^2$ is twice Lebesgue measure
on the reference Gauss section and
\[
M=\int_X\theta^2=2.
\]
No probability normalization is used. In particular $\theta$ vanishes
on residue bidiscs; reference gradient energy alone cannot separate
weights.

Let $\mathscr P$ be the minimal sheaf of finite continuous CLD psh
functions containing ordinary smooth psh functions and closed under
local uniform limits (CLD v2 Definition 8.1.7 and Lemma 8.1.8). Set
\[
\C=\{a\in C(X,\RR):\rho+a\in\mathscr P\text{ locally}\},\quad
\theta_a=\theta+\DD a,
\quad B(a,b)=\theta_a\wedge\theta_b,\quad \MA(a)=B(a,a).
\]
All continuous closure stages are retained. The published continuous
product rules used are CLD 8.5.1 (continuous multiplication and
convergence for positive currents), 8.5.3 (differentiation through smooth
closed factors), 8.5.5--6 (positivity of $\DD(fT)$ for continuous psh
$f$ and positive closed $T$, and proper projection), 8.7.1--4
(recursive products, local uniform continuity, positivity, symmetry),
8.8.4--5 (continuous dpsh extension and smooth $\DD$-null changes).
The carrier is boundaryless; all local factors are continuous dpsh or
continuous psh as required. Positive top currents are actual Radon
measures by 7.2.8, and 7.2.9--12 gives coefficient measures and positive
order-zero extension. Smooth cutoffs, partitions and scalar approximation
are supplied by 6.2.4--8 on locally differentially separated charts.
Green and Stokes are 5.11.1; boundary terms vanish here. The compatibility
of separated equidimensional analytic spaces with this tropical calculus
is 15.7.1. We do not use arbitrary-multiplier closedness in 8.6.7.
These are conventional analytic foundations, with the whole applied
immediate units recorded in the local ledger. Their deeper Berkovich,
Temkin, Ducros, Kiehl, Thuillier, graded commutative algebra and relative
intersection foundations are disclosed, not claimed freshly recursively
audited.

\section{All-stage Green calculus and bounded energy}
Convex and finite-max closure of $\C$ follows at all stages: first use
smooth convex soft maxima, then fix one slot and use the uniform-closed
sheaf/minimality argument, and repeat in the other slot. Constants are
allowed. The bounded products glue across affine reference changes and
have mass $M$ by testing exact terms with 1.

For positive closed $(1,1)$ current $T$, complete compact smooth
$(1,0)$ forms in the positive semidefinite pairing
$\int \alpha\wedge J\beta\wedge T$. For a nonnegative compact smooth
cutoff $\chi$ use the corresponding local completion. Uniformly smooth
approximable functions with strongly convergent gradients form a sheaf
$H_T$: partitions and Leibniz patch local approximants. Continuous
bounded coefficients act by approximation and
$\|f\alpha\|\le\|f\|_\infty\|\alpha\|$.
A uniformly zero sequence with a strong gradient limit has zero
gradient: by closedness it pairs to zero with every smooth Hilbert test,
since its pairing is the integral of the function against the fixed
differentiated test. These tests are dense.
If $f\in H_T$ and $L_Tf=\DD(fT)$ is signed Radon, smooth
approximation first in $f$ and then in a compactly supported $g$ gives
\[
\int g L_Tf=-Q_T(f,g),\qquad
Q_{T,\chi}(f)=\tfrac12\int f^2\DD\chi\wedge T-
\int\chi fL_Tf.
\]
The sheaf $\{f\in H_T:L_Tf\ge0\}$ contains smooth psh functions.
If such $f_j$ converge uniformly, choose nested cutoffs $\chi,\eta$,
$\eta=1$ near $\operatorname{supp}\chi$, with $|f_j|\le B$ on
the entire support of $\eta$. For $\epsilon=\|f_j-f_k\|$ there,
positivity and the displayed identity give
\[
0\le Q_{T,\chi}(f_j-f_k)\le
\tfrac12\epsilon^2\|\DD\chi\wedge T\|_{\rm TV}
+2B\epsilon\|\chi\|_\infty\|\DD\eta\wedge T\|_{\rm TV}.
\]
Thus the gradients are strongly Cauchy. Choose local smooth approximants
with both uniform and gradient errors tending to zero. Positivity
survives distributional limits. Minimality includes every stage of
$\mathscr P$; differences and finite patching give, for continuous dpsh
$f,g$ on $X$,
\[
Q_T(f,g)=-\int f\DD(gT)=-\int g\DD(fT),\quad Q_T(f)\ge0.
\tag{G}
\]
This supplies strong gradients rather than an unsupported pullback of
an arbitrary current.

Define
\[
E(a)=\tfrac13\int a\,[\theta^2+B(a,0)+\MA(a)].
\]
Differentiating the cubic using (G), for $a_t=(1-t)a+tb$ gives
\[
E'(a_t)=\int(b-a)\MA(a_t),\qquad
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}E(a_t)=-2Q_{\theta_{a_t}}(b-a)\le0.
\]
Consequently $E$ is concave, monotone, $E(0)=0$,
$E(a+c)=E(a)+2c$, and tangent inequalities hold. Integrating the
quadratic Bernstein coefficients gives the cocycle
\[
E(b)-E(a)=\tfrac13\int(b-a)[\MA(a)+B(a,b)+\MA(b)].
\tag{C}
\]
If $a\le b$, every one of these positive integrals is at most
$3(E(b)-E(a))$.
For bounded comparison use $m=\max(a,b-\epsilon)$, locality on the
strict ordinary open comparison sets and equal mass. This gives
$\MA(b)\{a<b-\epsilon\}\le\MA(a)\{a\le b-\epsilon\}$;
both increasing unions as $\epsilon\downarrow0$ equal $\{a<b\}$.
Contact atoms are retained on the indicated sides.

\section{Canonical generated calculus and the full bounded dual}
Define $\G$ by actual pointwise presentations
$a_j\downarrow u$, $a_j\in\C$, $\inf_j E(a_j)>-\infty$.
All values, including poles, are actual USC representatives. The
following compact strict-domination fact will be used repeatedly:
if a decreasing net of USC functions $f_i$ has infimum $f$ and a
finite continuous $h$ satisfies $f<h$ everywhere on compact $X$,
the increasing ordinary opens $\{f_i<h\}$ cover $X$; a finite
subcover and directedness give one global late index. No psh assertion
is made about a minimum of finitely many bounds.

Compare presentations with a term of the other plus $\epsilon$ using
this fact and the constant rule. Reversing roles proves independence
of $E(u)=\lim E(a_j)$, and the same argument proves order preservation
and energy continuity whenever a decreasing generated family has an
already generated infimum. It applies to nets as well as sequences.
An identically $-\infty$ limit would eventually lie below every negative
constant, contradicting the energy bound. Finite convex combinations
are generated and concavity passes to them.

On every finite simplex of generated weights the bounded energies are
polynomials of total degree at most three. Values converge at a finite
unisolvent set, so all coefficients converge. Existence of such a set
follows since a polynomial vanishing on an open simplex is zero.
Zero-weight slots are omitted at boundary points. This is finite
polynomial interpolation, with no differentiation of an uncontrolled
infinite limit.
For $a_j\downarrow u$ and $v\in\C$, put
$P_j(t)=E((1-t)a_j+tv)$ and $R_j(t)=E(ta_j)$. First variation gives
\[
\int v\MA(a_j)=P'_j(0)+R'_j(1).
\]
Thus its limit is canonical. The span of $\C$ is a uniformly dense
vector lattice in $C(X)$. Different tropical classes are separated by
small smooth periodic perturbations. In a single fibre different
seminorms are separated by a Laurent polynomial $P$ and a small cap
$\max(\rho_a,\delta+\epsilon(\log|P|-b))-\rho_a$, zero on a
fixed tropical boundary collar and near polynomial zeros, extended by
zero. Finite maxima give the lattice identity
\[
\max(A(p-q),A(r-s))=
2A\max((p+s)/2,(r+q)/2)-A(q+s).
\]
Constants, separation and the finite-cover lattice approximation prove
density. Positive common mass extends convergence to every continuous
test. Compact Hausdorff Riesz representation gives an actual positive
Radon $\MA(u)$ of mass 2. Polarization gives actual positive canonical
mixed products $B(u,v)$, obtained from bounded analytic products.

For positive Radon $\mu$ define the FULL bounded dual
\[
E^*(\mu)=\sup_{a\in\C}(E(a)-\int a\,d\mu).
\]
Constants force $\mu(X)=2$ if this is finite. For $u\le0$,
monotone convergence from a common continuous upper bound gives
\[
\int(-u)\,d\mu\le E^*(\mu)-E(u).
\tag{D}
\]
This proves integrability and $L^1(\mu)$ convergence of presentations;
generated pole sets are $\mu$-null. Bounded tangent gives
$E^*(\MA(a))=E(a)-\int a\MA(a)$.
The coefficient $D_u=\lim\int a_j\MA(a_j)=R'(1)$ exists.
Passing bounded tangent with fixed bounded competitors first yields
$E^*(\MA(u))\le E(u)-D_u<\infty$, so integrability precedes
the use of any unbounded moving integral. For $k\ge j$, (C) gives
\[
0\le\int(a_j-a_k)\MA(a_k)\le3(E(a_j)-E(a_k)).
\]
Let $k\to\infty$ using the fixed continuous $a_j$, and then
$j\to\infty$ using the established $L^1$ convergence. It follows
that $D_u=\int u\MA(u)$ and
$E^*(\MA(u))=E(u)-\int u\MA(u)$.

The coefficient $\partial_s E(tu+sv)|_{(1,0)}$ equals
$\int v\MA(u)$. Prove this first for bounded $v$, then decrease
bounded presentations to $v$, using coefficient convergence and (D).
For ANY continuous decreasing presentations $a_j\downarrow u$,
$b_j\downarrow v$, interpolation of $E(ta_j+sb_j)$ therefore gives
all four moving moments
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\int a_j\MA(a_j)\to\int u\MA(u),&
\int b_j\MA(a_j)\to\int v\MA(u),\\
\int a_j\MA(b_j)\to\int u\MA(v),&
\int b_j\MA(b_j)\to\int v\MA(v).
\end{array}
\tag{4M}
\]
Polarization gives mixed moments and $B(p,q)\le2\MA((p+q)/2)$.
First variation and tangent inequalities consequently pass to generated
segments. Nonopen comparison and max locality follow by bounded
Lipschitz ramps: replace presentation differences by generated differences
using moving $L^1$, then by fixed continuous presentation terms using
fixed-slot $L^1$, pass weak limits, and decrease those terms. At every
atom-free level the ramps give indicator convergence; there are at most
countably many bad levels. Regular finite Borel restrictions are determined
by continuous tests. Thus
\[
\MA(v)\{u<v\}\le\MA(u)\{u<v\},\quad
1_{u>v}\MA(\max(u,v))=1_{u>v}\MA(u).
\]
For $u\le0$, $u^A=\max(u,-A)$,
$1_{u>-A}\MA(u^A)=1_{u>-A}\MA(u)$; the entire contact set
$u=-A$ is excluded. If $E(u)\ge-C$, (D) and bounded dual give discarded
mass at most $3C/A$. These generated clamps are not assumed to be
preexisting continuous CLD functions.

\section{All bounded tests and actual point atoms}
For $a,b,w\le0$ of energy at least $-C$, (C) at zero gives
$-\int q\MA(q)\le3C$ and $E^*(\MA(q))\le2C$.
Hence
\[
\int(-p)\MA(q)\le3C,\qquad
\int(-p)B(q,r)\le6C.
\tag{M}
\]
Set $f=a-b$, $g=(a+b)/2$, $h=w-g$ and
$I(a,b)=\int(a-b)(\MA(b)-\MA(a))$.
Green and symmetry give
\[
I=Q_{\theta_a}(f)+Q_{\theta_b}(f),\qquad
Q_{\theta_w}(f)-I/2=Q_{\theta_a}(f,h)-Q_{\theta_b}(f,h).
\]
The second identity has both sides $-\int f\DD f\wedge\DD h$.
Since $|h|\le-w+(-a-b)/2$, (M) bounds its MA moments by $6C$
and mixed moments by $12C$. Expanding
$Q_{\theta_a}(h)=-\int h[B(a,w)-(\MA(a)+B(a,b))/2]$
bounds this slot by $12C+3C+6C=21C$, and likewise for $b$.
Direct-sum Cauchy--Schwarz proves
\[
Q_{\theta_w}(a-b)\le I/2+\sqrt{42CI}.
\tag{T}
\]
Fixed additive shifts leave curvatures, $Q$ and $I$ unchanged.

\begin{proposition}\label{atoms}
At every point $x\in X$ of total graded dimension two there is
$w_x\in\C$ and $\alpha_x>0$ with
$\MA(w_x)\ge\alpha_x\delta_x$. Every generated weight is finite
at $x$. No ground-field extension is used to construct this atom.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In an injective torus chart a full-dimensional point has support kernel
zero by the graded Abhyankar inequality. Its completed field is the
completion of $K(z_1,z_2)$ with the point norm. Completion adds no
initial forms: approximate a nonzero element with error smaller than its
norm. Initial Laurent polynomials generate its graded residue field by
fractions. Choose two homogeneous algebraically independent such
initials, represented by Laurent polynomials $P_1,P_2$.
With $r_i=|P_i(x)|>0$, independence makes the image of $x$ under
$(P_1,P_2)$ exactly the weighted Gauss point of radii $r_i$, including
arbitrary positive real radii. Boundarylessness places $x$ in the
relative interior. CLD 11.6.5(c) applies: both dimensions are at most
two, the image has graded dimension two and the target is reduced.
It makes the map finite and flat at $x$. By 11.6.3 take compact local
representatives of rank $D>0$ with singleton Gauss fibre $\{x\}$.
Near the target Gauss point the target's pleasant moment has plane
calibration one; CLD 14.5.2--3 gives degree $D$ upstairs.

For $\psi=\max(0,\log(|P_1|/r_1),\log(|P_2|/r_2))$, its smoothed
log-sum-exp models have plane Hessian determinant $p_0p_1p_2$ after
the corresponding scale change. The gradient map fills the open
standard simplex, of area $1/2$, so the raw $2!$ top mass is one.
The smooth measures concentrate at its vertex as the smoothing tends
to zero. Their pushforwards on the local finite representative have
mass $D$. Finite properness and the singleton Gauss fibre show convergence
against every actual continuous upstairs test to $D\delta_x$:
given a neighbourhood of $x$, shrink the target so its full inverse
image lies there, and use bounded continuous oscillation. Uniform
continuity of bounded products identifies this with $(\DD\psi)^2$
near $x$. This is an actual atom, not just its tropical pushforward.

On a small tropical box choose $\rho_a=|\tau-a|^2/2$,
$0<\delta$ smaller than the minimum of $\rho_a$ on a boundary collar,
and $\epsilon>0$ so small that
$w_x=\max(\rho_a,\delta+\epsilon\psi)-\rho_a$ is zero on that
collar. Extend by zero. At polynomial zeros use capped smooth logs
which are constant near zero and affine on the relevant nonzero norm
ranges, then soft maxima. They are smooth psh and converge uniformly;
thus $w_x\in\C$. On an inner neighbourhood of $x$ its curvature is
$\epsilon\DD\psi$, giving $\alpha_x=\epsilon^2D>0$.
The WHOLE bounded measure $\MA(w_x)$ has finite full dual by tangent.
If $u\le0$, $E(u)\ge-C$, (D) implies
\[
-u(x)\le [E^*(\MA(w_x))+C]/\alpha_x<\infty.
\]
We do not infer finite full dual for a normalized Dirac component.
\end{proof}

\section{Independent audit of full generated uniqueness}
We include the curve argument to make clear which identity supplies
pointwise uniqueness; the fixed-probe identity in the next section alone
does not do so. If $\MA(u)=\MA(v)$, (4M) gives $I(a_j,b_j)\to0$
for ANY continuous decreasing presentations. Their upper bounds and
finite lower energies allow fixed shifts and one constant $C$ in (T).
Hence $Q_{\theta_w}(a_j-b_j)\to0$ for each fixed $w\in\C$.

\begin{lemma}\label{disc}
For a complete nontrivially valued $F\supset K$, a full graded-dimension
one point $q$ on $\mathbb G_{m,F}^{\rm an}$, $|z(q)|=r$, lifts after
a FINITE $E/F$ to $\eta_{a,R}$, $a\in E$, $0<R\le r$.
The path $\eta_{a,t}$, $R\le t\le r$, has constant $|z|=r$,
finite logarithmic length $\ell=\log(r/R)$, and ends at $\eta_{0,r}$.
For continuous ambient dpsh restrictions with strong gradients,
\[
|h(q)-h(\eta_{0,r})|^2\le
\ell\int_C\chi\,d'h\wedge d''h
\tag{P}
\]
whenever a nonnegative compact smooth $\chi$ is one on the projected
finite path.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lift to the completed algebraic closure $C_F$. By CLD 13.8.8(d),
applied only to this Abhyankar line-point presentation, its graded
residue field is finite over the compositum of the old one and
$\widetilde {C_F}$. The latter is algebraic over $\widetilde F$;
completion adds no initial forms. Thus the lift is still full-dimensional.
For its norm let $R=\inf_a|z-a|$, and choose centres with radii
decreasing to $R$. Their discs are nested by the ultrametric inequality.
If the intersection has a centre $a$, factoring polynomials into linear
factors gives the Gauss norm $\eta_{a,R}$. If $R=0$ it is rigid and
has graded dimension zero. If the nested discs have empty intersection,
each $b$ is eventually outside one; the initial form of $z-b$ equals
that of the constant centre minus $b$. All rational-function initials
then belong to the base graded field, as do those of its completion,
again giving dimension zero. Therefore $R>0$ and a Gauss centre exists.
Approximate it by an algebraic $a_0$ within distance $R$ and put
$E=F(a_0)$. The discs agree, so the point over $E$ projects to $q$.
Since $r=\max(|a_0|,R)$, the stated path has the asserted norms and
endpoint.

On the Gauss interval the moment $z-a_0$ has local analytic degree one,
primitive slope one and, by CLD 14.5.2--3, calibration one.
Refining a finite cover of smooth moment charts, unit log norms are
piecewise affine with finitely many breaks on this compact interval.
The positive curve energy restricts to $|H'(\log t)|^2d\log t$;
other branches have nonnegative energy. Cauchy--Schwarz on the finite
interval gives (P) for smooth restrictions. Finite varying-ground-field
integration has factor ONE, by CLD 15.3.1 and 16.1.1:
$\int_{C_E}p^*(\chi d'h\wedge d''h)=\int_C\chi d'h\wedge d''h$.
Uniform/strong approximants pass this inequality to continuous ambient
dpsh restrictions. Their strong convergence on the actual curve is
provided by the positive fibre-current inequality below. No arbitrary

\section{New quantitative ordered energy-gap estimate}
\begin{theorem}\label{gap}
Suppose $a,b,w\in\C$ are nonpositive, $a\le b$, and each energy
is at least $-C$, $C\ge0$. Put $\delta=E(b)-E(a)$. Then
\[
0\le\int(b-a)\MA(w)\le F_C(\delta),\qquad
F_C(d)=3d+\sqrt{12C\bigl(\tfrac92d+\sqrt{126Cd}\bigr)}.
\tag{EG}
\]
In particular $F_C(d)\to0$ as $d\downarrow0$, without a common
sup-norm lower bound. At a point atom of $\MA(w)$ of size $\alpha$,
$0\le b(x)-a(x)\le F_C(\delta)/\alpha$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $f=b-a\ge0$. By (C),
$\int f\MA(a)\le3\delta$ and
\[
0\le I(a,b)=\int f(\MA(a)-\MA(b))\le3\delta.
\]
With $T=\theta_w+\theta_a$, $k=w-a$, symmetry gives
$\MA(w)-\MA(a)=\DD(kT)$. Green and Cauchy--Schwarz yield
\[
\int f\MA(w)=\int f\MA(a)-Q_T(f,k)
\le3\delta+\sqrt{Q_T(f)Q_T(k)}.
\]
Estimate (T) and $Q_{\theta_a}(f)\le I$ give
$Q_T(f)\le\frac32I+\sqrt{42CI}$.
Also $Q_T(k)=I(a,w)\le\int |a-w|(\MA(a)+\MA(w))\le12C$
by the four bounds in (M). Substituting $I\le3\delta$ proves (EG).
The point estimate uses positivity and the actual atom. If $C=0$, the
same calculation is valid with its zero terms.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{ggap}
The estimate holds for ORDERED actual $u\le v$ in $\G$, with
$\delta=E(v)-E(u)$, after a common upper shift and a correspondingly
fixed energy constant. For each fixed bounded $w$ it bounds
$\int(v-u)\MA(w)$ and every finite full-dimensional point difference.
In particular $u\le v$ and $E(u)=E(v)$ force $u=v$ pointwise.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $u,v\le B$ and $E(u),E(v)\ge L$, choose any presentations
$A_j\downarrow u$, $B_j\downarrow v$. Replace $B_j$ by
$\max(A_j,B_j)$; it still decreases to $v$ and now dominates $A_j$.
Compact strict domination discards initial terms so both are at most
$B+1$. Subtract $B+1$ from both; energies are at least
$L-2(B+1)$. Independently subtract $\sup_Xw$ from the fixed test.
Choose
\[
C\ge\max\{0,\ 2(B+1)-L,\ -E(w-\sup_Xw)\}.
\]
This single constant works for both presentations. Their energy
differences tend to $E(v)-E(u)$, and their integrals converge by (D).
All full-dimensional endpoint values are finite by Proposition
\ref{atoms}; the point estimate therefore passes to actual limits.
For equal energy all these differences vanish, and (S) proves equality
on all points, including poles. Differences in integrals are assigned
arbitrarily on the common measure-null pole sets.
\end{proof}

\section{New actual closure for arbitrary decreasing nets}
First we give the sequence representation lemma, rather than citing a
supplied note. Suppose $u_k\in\G$ decrease with lower energy bound
$L$. Choose presentations $f_{k,j}$ and enumerate ALL pairs as
$h_1,h_2,\ldots$. Their infimum is the actual $u=\inf_k u_k$.
Set $\epsilon_n=2^{-n}$ and $r_n=\sum_{\ell>n}\epsilon_\ell=2^{-n}$.
Choose $k_n\ge\max(n,k_{n-1})$ so that
$u_{k_n}<h_m+\epsilon_n$ for $m\le n$, by compact strict domination.
For $n>1$, $u_{k_n}\le u_{k_{n-1}}\le v_{n-1}$.
Choose a sufficiently late presentation term $v_n=f_{k_n,j_n}$ so
that it is below all the first $n$ bounds and below
$v_{n-1}+\epsilon_n$. The finitely many strict continuous bounds are
only comparisons, never minima inserted into the psh class.
Then $a_n=v_n+r_n$ decreases, dominates $u$, and eventually lies
below $h_m+\epsilon_n+r_n$ for every $m$. Its exact pointwise limit
is $u$, including poles. Its energies are at least $L$, since
$v_n\ge u_{k_n}$. Thus $u\in\G$. Order gives $E(u)\le L$;
compact strict domination against a presentation of $u$ plus
$\epsilon$ gives the reverse inequality $L\le E(u)$. This proves
countable decreasing closure with $E(u_k)\downarrow E(u)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{net}
Let $(u_i)_{i\in I}$ be ANY directed decreasing net in $\G$,
with $L=\inf_iE(u_i)>-\infty$. Its ACTUAL pointwise infimum
$u=\inf_i u_i$ belongs to the original SEQUENCE-generated class
$\G$, and $E(u_i)\downarrow E(u)=L$. There is a decreasing
sequence of indices selected by energy whose weights have exact
pointwise infimum $u$, although it need not be cofinal in $I$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Restrict to a tail $i\ge i_0$; its infimum and energy infimum agree
with those of the original net by directedness. The USC weight
$u_{i_0}$ is bounded above, so all tail terms have a common finite
upper bound $B$. Recursively choose $i_n\ge i_{n-1}$ with
$E(u_{i_n})\le L+2^{-n}$: find one index with such an energy and
then a directed upper bound, using energy monotonicity.
The sequence lemma gives
$v=\inf_nu_{i_n}\in\G$ and $E(v)=L$.

Fix an arbitrary $i$ and a full-dimensional point $x$. For each $n$
choose $j_n\ge i,i_n$. Then
$u_{j_n}\le u_{i_n}$ and $u_{j_n}\le u_i$;
$0\le E(u_{i_n})-E(u_{j_n})\le2^{-n}$.
All terms are at most $B$ and have energy at least $L$. Corollary
\ref{ggap}, with the fixed atom $w_x$, uses one constant $C_x$
independent of $i,n,j_n$, and gives
\[
0\le u_{i_n}(x)-u_{j_n}(x)
\le F_{C_x}(2^{-n})/\alpha_x\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since $u_{j_n}(x)\le u_i(x)$, taking the limit proves
$v(x)\le u_i(x)$. Conversely $u\le v$, so $u=v$ at ALL
full-dimensional points. This is energy selection, not a hidden
countable cofinality assertion.

The infimum of USC functions is USC. GR Proposition 4.2(4) makes
the local weight $\rho+u$ classical psh by its decreasing-net rule.
The local weight $\rho+v$ is classical psh as generated. All
affinoid Shilov points are full-dimensional; applying (S) to the two
actual local weights shows $u=v$ pointwise, including poles.
Consequently $u\in\G$ and $E(u)=L$ as asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{netcontinuity}
Under Theorem \ref{net}, $\MA(u_i)\to\MA(u)$ weakly against
EVERY continuous analytic-space test. Two or finitely many decreasing
energy-bounded generated nets have canonical mixed-product and moving
moment convergence on a common directed product index. For each positive
Radon $\mu$ of raw mass 2 and $E^*(\mu)<\infty$,
\[
\int|u_i-u|\,d\mu\longrightarrow0.
\]
No support, invariance, metrizability or countable full-support measure
assumption is imposed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every fixed convex combination of the decreasing nets has a finite
lower energy bound by concavity; Theorem \ref{net} supplies its actual
generated limit and energy continuity. At finitely many interpolation
nodes all cubic energy coefficients converge. The first-variation
coefficient identities prove weak convergence against $\C$, all
four moving MA moments and their mixed polarizations. Density of the
span of $\C$ and common mass 2 give every continuous test.

For $L^1(\mu)$, do NOT invoke a general monotone convergence theorem
for arbitrary measurable nets. The actual $u$ has a sequence
presentation $a_m\downarrow u$, which converges in $L^1(\mu)$
by (D). For each $m$ and $\epsilon>0$, compact strict domination
against $a_m+\epsilon$ gives eventually
$u\le u_i<a_m+\epsilon$ everywhere. Hence, outside the common
$\mu$-null pole sets for the individual integrals,
\[
0\le\int(u_i-u)\,d\mu
\le\int(a_m-u)\,d\mu+2\epsilon.
\]
First choose $m$, then $\epsilon$, and take a sufficiently late
index. This proves net $L^1$ convergence. In fact $\{u=-\infty\}$
is a common $\mu$-null set by (D): outside it, $u_i\ge u$ makes every
$u_i$ finite. No assertion about uncountable unions of null sets is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem also applies to downward filtered families indexed by their
order, and shows that real energy selects an actual sequential infimum
even on this nonmetrizable carrier. It does not say that arbitrary
psh functions with a proposed intrinsic energy belong to $\G$.
Nor can one apply it to the minima or infima of a nonordered family:
those intermediate functions need not be psh or already generated.
The remaining rooftop/envelope step is precisely where such an argument
would need a new input.
Ordinary monotone convergence for arbitrary measurable nets would be
false: on $[0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure, index by finite subsets $F$
and take $1_{[0,1]\setminus F}$. The net decreases pointwise to zero
while every integral is one. This diagnostic is not an analytic psh
counterexample; the strict-continuous-majorant argument in Corollary
\ref{netcontinuity} supplies the missing condition in the actual theorem.
\end{remark}
