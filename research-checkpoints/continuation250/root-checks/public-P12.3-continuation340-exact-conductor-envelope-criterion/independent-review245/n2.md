Fix the exact G331 cone, without changing its models or supports. Let X=V_a(R) be a nonempty compact primary section, Q the finite nonnegative real sums of negative unit-or-primary ideal gauges, C its uniform closure in finite continuous functions, and D infima of actual nonempty decreasing C-nets, including bottom. C contains negative constants, is a cone and is closed under finite maxima. D is nonpositive and upper semicontinuous, since {inf_i g_i<t}=union_i{g_i<t}. Finite maxima in D are legitimate: on the product directed set, max(g_i,h_j) decreases to max(inf g_i,inf h_j), because simultaneous finite upper tests can be met. Subtracting a nonnegative constant preserves C,D. Empty boundaries are treated separately as the unique empty function.

For finite continuous phi define p=P_D phi as the supremum of all D-minorants. Negative constants bound it below, and min(phi,0) bounds it above. If u=inf_i g_i<=phi and epsilon>0, the open sets {g_i<phi+epsilon} cover X. Directedness and a finite subcover select one common term; g_i-epsilon is a C-minorant and is at least u-epsilon. Thus P_D phi=P_C phi. The common finite bounded p is lower semicontinuous.

If a finite continuous u belongs to D, the same compact-cover argument, now with tests g_i<u+epsilon, proves uniform convergence and u in C. If p is continuous, use the open cover {g>p-epsilon} over all C-minorants and take the finite maximum from a finite subcover. It lies between p-epsilon and p, so uniform closedness gives p in C. Consequently p in D iff p is continuous iff p in C, equivalently iff a greatest D-minorant exists. Monotonicity, positive homogeneity and clipping of the obstacle against zero follow from the families. Shifting competitors down proves ||P phi-P psi||<=||phi-psi|| for continuous finite obstacles. None of these assertions grants increasing closure.

Put p*(x)=inf_{open U containing x}sup_U p. A bounded function's p* is upper semicontinuous: if p*(x)<t, some U has sup_U p<t and the same bound works at every point of U. Since phi is continuous and p<=phi, p*<=phi. Every original D-minorant is below p*, and every D-minorant of p* is below phi. Thus P_D(p*)=p. If p* in D it is itself a competitor, forcing p*<=p, hence p*=p. As p is lsc this makes it continuous. Conversely continuous p equals p* and belongs to C. Therefore

  p* in D iff p continuous iff p in C iff p*=p.

This strengthens actual242 n1 by proving the regularization criterion; it verifies ROOT335 section6 rather than accepting it as a premise.

There is an exact ideal construction in every primary gauge. For k>=1 define J_k={f in R: v(f)>=-k phi(v) for every v in X}. It is an ideal by the semivaluation axioms and nonnegativity on R. If M>=max(0,-min phi) and L is an integer >=kM, then a^L subset J_k. If phi is negative somewhere, units are excluded and J_k is proper maximal-primary; otherwise J_k=R. Define a_k=-v(J_k)/k in Q, with the unit convention zero. Finite generation proves a_k<=phi. Every rational single profile -v(I)/k<=phi has I subset J_k, hence is <=a_k.

To pass from rational profiles to the full C-envelope, approximate each finite real Q sum rationally, synchronize denominators and take the product ideal. This gives uniform density of single rational profiles in C. Given g in C below phi and epsilon>0, approximate it with error less than epsilon/4 by q=-v(I)/k. Subtract a rational delta between epsilon/4 and epsilon/2. Since v(a)=1, common denominators and a boundary-ideal power turn q-delta into one rational primary profile, still <=phi and within epsilon of g. Therefore p=sup_k a_k. The inclusions J_k J_l subset J_{k+l} and J_k^l subset J_{kl} follow pointwise from their defining inequalities. Consequently a_{n!} increases and has supremum p, since every fixed k divides eventually n!. This is a raw formula; its increasing limit need not be in D.

For a second primary ideal b write d(v)=v(b), H_ab(v)=v/d(v), T_ab f(H_ab v)=f(v)/d(v). G331 gives homeomorphism H_ab and order bijections T_ab on C,D, with d and 1/d bounded positive continuous. Transform obstacles by the same rule. Bijection of competitors proves P_b(T_ab phi)=T_ab(P_a phi), for arbitrary extended obstacles. The defining inequalities for J_k multiply by d, so J_k is literally the same ideal in both gauges. Composition telescopes: the second denominator is v(c)/v(b), yielding H_ac and division by v(c). For bounded p, regularization also transports: near a fixed point, |p(y)/d(y)-p(y)/d(x)| tends uniformly to zero because p is bounded and 1/d is continuous; taking neighborhood upper limits gives (T_ab p)*=T_ab(p*). Thus the finite-continuous obstruction and criterion are gauge invariant. No infinite conductor value is divided out.
