                                                  Abstract
          This paper does not solve the unchanged original P13.2. It verifies the invariant carrier
      and probability normalization, repairs density and energy-sign statements in the supplied pub-
      lic sources, and proves that finite-energy maximizing sequences are Cauchy in the fixed-Reeb
      potential quasi-pseudometric. It also computes a genuine irrational toric model output sup-
      ported at a nondivisorial quasimonomial point. The potential realization, target identification,
      and general existence passages remain unproved. No sufficient measure criterion for all normal
      polarized affine cones is claimed.


1    Scope and source convention
The setting is an algebraically closed, trivially valued field of characteristic zero and a normal
affine cone X = Spec R with good effective torus action T and Reeb field ξ. No rationality of ξ or
smoothness of the cone is assumed in Sections 2–5. The explicit models in Section 6 are smooth
toric special cases.
    The only primary sources used are [1, 2, 3, 4], at the exact public versions listed in the bibliog-
raphy. The accompanying source-extracts/source-provenance.json embeds literal statements
and relevant proof bodies, source versions and hashes, and precise application checks. LF ranges
here mean one-based inclusive ranges of the supplied literal text split by the newline character;
form feeds remain in their lines. All eight supplied PDF/text hashes match. These execution and
provenance facts do not establish mathematical correctness.
    The task input states that uniqueness modulo constants is already established on the genuine
image. That uniqueness is retained as given; it is not promoted to surjectivity. The additional
mathematical arguments here are partial.

every character value zero and taking the minimum of coefficient values. This is multiplicative:
the initial Laurent polynomials over the graded residue domain are nonzero and their product is
nonzero. Pullback gives τ (v), including infinite evaluations. Homogeneous evaluations are un-

    The invariant-semivaluation criterion in [1, Lemma 2.8], LF 269–297, has a character-matrix
proof: for the finitely many characters in any f , choose torus points whose evaluation matrix is
invertible and express each fα as a linear combination of translates of f . It follows that a torus-
invariant semivaluation satisfies v(f ) = min v(fα ). Hence τ lands in the invariant locus and fixes
it. Each evaluation of τ (v) is a finite minimum of continuous extended-real evaluations, proving
continuity. Homogeneous generators of m show that τ preserves the link.
    If v(m) = 0 and v(g) < 0, choose h ∈ m of finite value. The values of g r h ∈ m tend to −∞, a
contradiction. Thus all evaluations are nonnegative. Bounded semivaluations form a closed subset
of [0, +∞]R : the semivaluation equations and inequalities are closed in these evaluation coordinates.
The link is the closed subset specified by the finite-generator equation minj v(fj ) = 0. It is compact
Hausdorff. The fixed locus of the continuous idempotent τ is closed, so K is compact.
    Homogeneous FS formulas depend only on homogeneous evaluations. Uniform limits and de-
creasing pointwise limits preserve this dependence. These are precisely the operations defining
continuous psh and general psh functions in [2].

Proposition 2 (Actual output carrier). Every genuine finite-energy mixed Monge–Ampère measure
is a probability supported on K.

                                                           2
Proof. The model result is [1, Corollary 5.7], LF 1049–1054. Its proof, LF 1092–1200, uses the
vanishing homogeneous top wedge, local tropical facet calculations, and compactness to obtain
finite support. The invariant current makes its support a finite T (k)-set. A divisible abelian
group has no nontrivial finite quotient: if the quotient has exponent e, multiplication by e is
both surjective and zero. Thus each support point is torus-invariant. This is a pointwise-support
assertion, stronger than setwise invariance of a measure. The sentence about a Hessian being zero
in the preceding proof must mean its determinant is zero: linearity in the Reeb direction makes
the Hessian singular.
    Theorem 2.12 of [2] already states the model carrier X0T . Model approximation proves the
continuous case; decreasing-net continuity of its Theorem 6.3 proves the finite-energy case. A
weak limit of probabilities supported on a closed subset K is again supported there. Indeed every
nonnegative continuous function vanishing on K has integral zero at the limit, and Radon regularity
gives zero mass to the complement. Normalized mass remains one.

    The auxiliary [1, Lemma 5.8], LF 1058–1091, has a further literal defect: its boundary-current
sum uses full hyperplanes rather than active facets. For ψ(x, y) = max{x, y}, a test form near (0, 1)
sees its x = 0 term but no part of {ψ = 0}. The boundary calculation must restrict to the oriented
facets
                                  {vi · x = 0, vj · x ≤ 0 for all j},
with redundancies removed. In the relative interior of a facet the active form is unique. Gradients of
uniformly converging convex smooth approximants converge to that form’s gradient, by the upper
and lower finite-difference bounds for convex derivatives. The same bounds give local boundedness.
Facet intersections have lower dimension and zero facet integration measure, so exhaustion of the
relative interiors gives the required restricted boundary trace. No full-hyperplane identity or general
mass coefficient is used here. The normalized model-carrier application also uses the independently
printed [2, Theorem 2.12] under its precise normal-cone, Reeb, and homogeneous-FS hypotheses.

Proposition 3. Dξ is a real vector lattice containing constants, and is uniformly dense in C(K).

Proof. The same-ray rules of [2, Proposition 3.3] say that addition at aξ, bξ lands at ab/(a + b)ξ
and that scalar multiplication rescales the Reeb ray. Thus sums and real multiples of differences
remain differences. For u = ϕ1 − ϕ2 and v = ψ1 − ψ2 use the identity

                         max{u, v} = max{ϕ1 + ψ2 , ψ1 + ϕ2 } − (ϕ2 + ψ2 ).

All potentials on the right have the same proportional polarization. Constants arise from ρ + c − ρ.

d = ⟨ξ, α⟩ > 0. The function

is infinite, separates the points. The real lattice Stone–Weierstrass theorem proves density.

7    The unresolved realization passages
The attempted transfers from the supplied sources leave the following concrete gaps. This is not a
theorem asserting that they can never be filled.

Potential compactness and strong completeness. Decreasing-net closure, quasimonomial
value bounds, and upper semicontinuity of energy in [2] do not prove compactness of normalized
cone psh functions. A product-space limit need not be a genuine psh function. The proof of [3,
Theorem 5.11] constructs upper envelopes of tails under the envelope property. Its Theorem 12.8
equates that projective property with strong completeness and surjectivity. No cone proof of that
envelope property or an adequate substitute was found or established here.

Orthogonality and synthetic hypotheses. Finite       R
                                                          max stability does not imply the directed
orthogonality limit in [4, Definition 2.15], namely (f − ψ) MA(ψ) → 0 for psh tests below each
obstacle. Proposition 2.19 derives uniform differentiation from that limit. A synthetic application
on K needs an actual ordered form space, its positivity and integration axioms, identification of its
psh class with genuine cone potentials, and orthogonality. Here its dimension would be n − 1, with
the radial factor retained; the paper’s stipulated positive dimension also needs separate treatment
when n = 1. That formalism and its orthogonality are not supplied by notation alone.

Target integration continuity. The decreasing-net result above does not give L1 (µ) continuity
for arbitrary weakly converging, energy-bounded potentials. The proof of [3, Proposition 9.19]
uses uniform target approximation by model-MA measures and an earlier estimate, whose target
approximation depends on uniform differentiation. Applying it without the cone analogues would
be circular.

Differentiation and Euler–Lagrange. Admissible psh segments at a putative maximizer give
only                      Z
                                 (ψ − ϕ)(MA(ϕ) − µ) ≤ 0      (ψ psh).

A constrained maximum can have a nonzero outward derivative; −x2 − x on x ≥ 0 is a simple
example. The two-sided dense-test variation in [3, Theorem 8.5] uses relative volumes of projective
section norms, added polarizations, and fixed mixed masses. Its Theorem 9.11 identifies limits of
maximizing outputs, and Corollary 9.12 proves the Euler–Lagrange equality from that differentia-
bility. A genuine cone replacement for the relative-volume comparison is not proved here.

Completing measures is not realizing potentials. Theorems 2.23 and 2.26 of [4] assume
orthogonality and prove density and completeness of measures. The introduction expressly does
not investigate finite-energy potentials. Even establishing all synthetic axioms would not by itself
identify completed objects with Wu’s E 1 potentials. The Cauchy estimate (7) makes that missing
step explicit.

Rational and varying-Reeb transfers. Lemmas 5.11–5.12 and Proposition 5.13 of [1] identify
homogeneous model functions and measures with quotient functions for a primitive rational Reeb
field. The quotient can be singular. Theorem 12.9 of [3] assumes a smooth projective variety. Even
for a smooth quotient and a larger torus, preservation of the actual invariant carrier and invariant

potential class requires proof; full quotient surjectivity alone does not give it, and nonlinear MA
cannot be averaged formally. No such quotient transfer is used here.
    Lemmas 2.11 and 3.2 of [2] approximate individual continuous potentials with varying Reeb
fields; Proposition 3.3 adds only proportional rays. They do not prove estimates uniform for target
solutions in two changing nonproportional polarizations or finite-energy realization through an
irrational limit. Restricting every intermediate output to divisorial support would also contradict
the explicit irrational atom above.

Final mathematical status. No intrinsic sufficient condition satisfying the assignment’s exclu-
sions is established. Neither variational attainment, an unknown inverse potential, nor a closure in
unknown outputs is presented as a characterization. General existence on normal polarized affine
cones, including irrational Reeb fields, remains unresolved in this deliverable. The given uniqueness
on the image is retained.
