# A quantitative comparison of raw bounded-obstacle FS envelopes

This is a fresh, unreviewed root deduction. It is not a solution of original
P13.1 or P13.2, and has not been supplied to the running P13.2 author. It makes
no energy differentiation, potential completeness, Monge–Ampère surjectivity
or envelope regularity claim for arbitrary normal cones.

The mathematical input is Wu, arXiv:2406.13684v1, Definition2.9 and Lemma2.10,
literal LF277–297 in the supplied public extract. Lemma3.2 (LF350–371) already
asserts a varying-Reeb approximation for continuous functions. The point here
is an explicit bound for raw obstacle envelopes uniform in the homogeneous
presentation and its degrees; no novelty claim is made.

Let R be the coordinate ring of a normal polarized affine cone with good torus
action over an algebraically closed trivially valued field of characteristic
zero. Let g_1,...,g_s be nonconstant homogeneous algebra generators with
weights beta_i; they generate the cone-point maximal ideal m. Work on the
fixed invariant carrier K=X_0^T. Its semivaluations satisfy v(m)=0, so
v(g_i)>=0 for every i, with at least one value zero. Consequently v(f)>=0
for every nonzero homogeneous f in m. The trivial semivaluation v_triv is in K.

For a Reeb field xi, an FS presentation on K is

    psi_xi(v)=max_j (-v(f_j)+lambda_j)/<xi,alpha_j>,

where f_j are nonzero homogeneous functions whose common zero set is the
cone point, alpha_j their nonzero weights, and lambda_j real numbers. This
common zero condition is the actual condition in Wu Definition2.9. The
functions are finite and continuous on K: the ideal they generate contains
m^N for some N, which prevents every v(f_j) from being positive or infinite
when v(m)=0. This also supplies a finite uniform lower bound from the fixed
coefficients lambda_j. Suprema are attained at v_triv, and

    sup_K psi_xi=psi_xi(v_triv)=max_j lambda_j/<xi,alpha_j>.

Choose Reeb fields xi,zeta and 0<delta<1 such that

    1-delta <= <zeta,beta_i>/<xi,beta_i> <= 1+delta

for every algebra-generator weight beta_i. Every nonzero weight alpha of R
is the weight of some nonzero monomial in the generators, so alpha is a
nonnegative integral combination of the beta_i. Thus the same ratio bounds
hold for every such alpha, uniformly in its degree.

First suppose -M<=psi_xi<=0 on K. Its value at v_triv implies lambda_j<=0
for all terms. Every numerator -v(f_j)+lambda_j is nonpositive. Keeping
the same functions and coefficients and replacing xi by zeta gives an actual
FS function psi_zeta, with the same common zero set. Term by term,

    psi_xi/(1-delta) <= psi_zeta <= psi_xi/(1+delta).

The inequalities remain valid for a term equal to minus infinity, and the
finite maximum makes their final values real. Hence

    ||psi_zeta-psi_xi||_infinity <= delta M/(1-delta).          (1)

Conversely, if -M<=chi_zeta<=0, replacing its denominators by xi multiplies
each nonpositive term by a number in [1-delta,1+delta]. Taking maxima gives

    (1+delta)chi_zeta <= chi_xi <= (1-delta)chi_zeta,
    ||chi_xi-chi_zeta||_infinity <= delta M.                   (2)

These are correspondences between chosen presentations; they do not define
a presentation-independent map between completed potential spaces.

For a continuous bounded function f on K define the *raw* envelope

    Q_xi(f)(v)=sup{psi(v):psi is the restriction of an xi-FS function,
                             psi<=f everywhere on K}.

Its values are finite. Indeed the generators give the reference FS function
max_i(-v(g_i)/<xi,beta_i>)=0 on K, so every constant is an FS restriction.
Maxima and addition of constants preserve the FS class by Lemma2.10.

Subtract sup_K f, which commutes with this envelope, and put
M=osc_K(f). We now have -M<=f<=0. Any admissible psi can be replaced by
max(psi,-M), which is still admissible and has larger values; thus the
supremum can be restricted to -M<=psi<=0. Transport it to zeta and subtract
epsilon=delta M/(1-delta). By (1), this shifted zeta-FS function is <=f and
is >=psi-2epsilon. Taking the supremum gives

    Q_zeta(f)>=Q_xi(f)-2delta M/(1-delta).

Apply the analogous argument to clipped zeta competitors and (2) to obtain

    Q_xi(f)>=Q_zeta(f)-2delta M.

Therefore, including arbitrary constant shifts of f,

    ||Q_zeta(f)-Q_xi(f)||_infinity
                         <= 2delta osc_K(f)/(1-delta).        (3)

If M=0 the envelopes are the same constant, so the estimate holds then too.
No compactness of a potential space, attainment, rational quotient or energy
theorem was used. The estimate also passes to upper semicontinuous
regularizations, since taking limsup in neighborhoods preserves inequalities
with a fixed additive constant. That observation alone does not make the
regularized envelope psh or continuous.

As a precise conditional consequence, if a sequence of Reeb fields zeta_l
converges to xi and each *raw* Q_zeta_l(f) is already known to be continuous,
then (3) shows uniform convergence to Q_xi(f), hence its continuity. The
finite-generator ratios provide delta_l->0. Establishing the premise for
all rational quotients of an arbitrary normal cone is an independent
geometric obligation. The supplied Boucksom–Jonsson arXiv:1801.08229v4
TheoremA only grants general surjectivity for smooth characteristic-zero
projective varieties (and varieties of dimension at most two); its normal
singular quotients cannot silently be treated as smooth.

The remaining original P13.1 obligations include the comparisons on genuine
unbounded PSH/finite-energy spaces and the uniform estimates actually needed
to differentiate energy. The remaining P13.2 obligation is actual
realization of the image condition on K with its probability normalization.
This bounded-obstacle calculation closes neither original problem.
