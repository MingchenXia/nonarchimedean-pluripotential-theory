## 1. Actual objects and probability normalization

Let $X=\operatorname{Spec}R$ be a normal affine cone of dimension $n$, with good effective torus action $T$, homogeneous decomposition $R=\bigoplus_\alpha R_\alpha$, cone vertex $o$, maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and Reeb field $\xi$ in the open Reeb cone. Put

$$X_0=\{v:v(\mathfrak m)=0\},\qquad K=X_0^T.$$

Write $\rho=\varphi_\xi$. Using homogeneous generators $f_j$ of $\mathfrak m$,

$$\rho(v)=\max_j\frac{-v(f_j)}{\langle\xi,\alpha_j\rangle},\qquad \rho^+=\max\{\rho,0\}.$$

In particular $\rho|_{X_0}=0$. The source volume convention is

$$V_\xi=\operatorname{vol}(\xi)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{n!}{m^n}\sum_{\langle\xi,\alpha\rangle\le m}\dim R_\alpha.$$

The genuine mixed operator and energy are, with the source's $d'd''$ convention,

$$\operatorname{MA}_\xi(\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_{n-1})=V_\xi^{-1}d'd''\varphi_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d'd''\varphi_{n-1}\wedge d'd''\rho^+,$$

$$E_\xi(\varphi)=\frac{1}{nV_\xi}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int(\varphi-\rho)(d'd''\varphi)^j\wedge(d'd''\rho)^{n-1-j}\wedge d'd''\rho^+.$$

Thus the transverse dimension is $n-1$, there are $n$ terms in the energy sum, and the energy denominator is $nV_\xi$, not $(n+1)V_\xi$. The radial factor is part of the measure and cannot be discarded. Wu21 Definition 2.4 (LF 205–211), Wu24 Theorem 2.12 (LF 321–332), and Definition 4.1/Proposition 4.2 (LF 522–554) fix these conventions. The mixed measures are probabilities; $E(\rho)=0$ and $E(\varphi+c)=E(\varphi)+c$.

For a finite positive Radon measure $\nu$ of mass $m$, translating any continuous psh potential gives

$$E(\varphi+c)-\int(\varphi+c)d\nu=E(\varphi)-\int\varphi d\nu+(1-m)c.$$

Consequently finiteness of this dual supremum forces $m=1$. This is a direct check on the actual affine-cone normalization, not an imported projective convention. For $a>0$, $V_{a\xi}=a^{-n}V_\xi$, $\rho_{a\xi}=a^{-1}\rho_\xi$, and

$$\operatorname{MA}_{a\xi}(a^{-1}\varphi)=\operatorname{MA}_\xi(\varphi),\qquad E_{a\xi}(a^{-1}\varphi)=a^{-1}E_\xi(\varphi).$$

These identities follow by scaling all $n$ current factors. They concern a single Reeb ray.

## 2. Torification, the model carrier, and corrected density

Define torification by

$$\tau(v)(f)=\min_{\alpha:f_\alpha\ne0}v(f_\alpha),\qquad f=\sum_\alpha f_\alpha.$$

Here is a construction that also verifies multiplicativity. The action homomorphism sends $f$ to $\sum f_\alpha\chi^\alpha$ in $R[M]$. Pass from $R$ to the fraction field of $R/\ker v$, equipped with its induced valuation, and extend that valuation to the Laurent polynomial ring by the Gauss rule, giving every character value zero. The minimum-of-coefficients rule is multiplicative: reducing terms of minimum valuation gives nonzero Laurent polynomials over the graded residue domain, whose product is nonzero. Pull back along the action homomorphism. This gives precisely the displayed formula, including the case where all coefficients vanish in $R/\ker v$. It proves that $\tau(v)$ is a semivaluation.

Homogeneous evaluations are unchanged. The criterion in Wu21 Lemma 2.8 (LF 269–297), proved there by an invertible character matrix, implies that $\tau(v)$ is $T$-invariant and fixes invariant semivaluations. Every evaluation $\tau(v)(f)$ is a finite minimum of continuous extended-real evaluations, so $\tau$ is continuous. The homogeneous generators of $\mathfrak m$ show $\tau(X_0)\subset K$ and $\tau^2=\tau$. Thus $K$ is a closed compact retract of $X_0$.

For completeness, $v(\mathfrak m)=0$ forces $v(g)\ge0$ for every $g\in R$: if $v(g)<0$, choose $h\in\mathfrak m$ with finite value and apply $v$ to $g^rh\in\mathfrak m$ as $r\to\infty$. This contradicts $\inf_{\mathfrak m}v=0$. The bounded-semivaluation space is a closed subset of the compact product $[0,+\infty]^R$, with one evaluation coordinate for every element of $R$. The link is its closed subset given by the equation $\min_jv(f_j)=0$ for finitely many generators of $\mathfrak m$. This also verifies compactness without identifying the entire punctured analytic cone with the link.

Every homogeneous FS test factors through $\tau$. Uniform limits and decreasing pointwise limits preserve this identity, so all genuine psh and finite-energy potentials factor through $\tau$ on $X_0$. The same holds for differences of continuous potentials.

**Model carrier.** Wu21 Corollary 5.7 states explicitly that the unnormalized mixed model measure has finite support on $T$-invariant points of $\{\rho=0\}$ (LF 1049–1054). Its proof uses vanishing of the top homogeneous wedge off the vertex, tropical facet calculations to obtain locally discrete support, compactness to make the support finite, and torus invariance of the current to make that finite support a $T(k)$-set. The final argument is valid: a divisible abelian group has no nontrivial finite quotient, since a finite abelian group of exponent $e$ has $eG=0$. Hence the action on the finite support is trivial, and every supporting point is invariant (LF 1092–1200). This proves support in $K$, rather than merely setwise torus invariance of a diffuse measure. The sentence in the preceding proof that a Hessian “has to be zero” should mean singular: linearity in the Reeb direction makes its determinant zero, not the whole Hessian zero.

There is an additional literal defect in the auxiliary Wu21 Lemma 5.8 (LF 1058–1091): it sums currents on the *full* hyperplanes $V_i=\{v_i\cdot x=0\}$, although the zero set of the maximum contains only active boundary facets. For $\psi(x,y)=\max\{x,y\}$ a test form near $(0,1)$ sees the $x=0$ term on the left and no point of $\{\psi=0\}$ on the right. Thus the lemma cannot be applied literally. In the boundary computation, one must use the oriented facets of the polyhedral region, restricted to $\{v_i\cdot x=0,\ v_j\cdot x\le0\ \forall j\}$, with redundant facets removed. On a compact subset of the relative interior of such a facet the active linear form is unique, so gradients of locally uniformly converging convex smooth approximants converge to its gradient; this follows by bounding derivatives between the two finite-difference quotients on a small line segment. Gradients are locally bounded by the same convexity estimate. Intersections of facets have lower dimension and give zero facet integration measure, allowing exhaustion of facet interiors. This supplies the needed boundary-trace restriction in the local discrete-support argument. No equality involving the full hyperplanes is used here, and no general mass coefficient is imported from that defective lemma. The normalized carrier application also uses Wu24's independently printed Theorem 2.12, under exactly its normal-cone, Reeb, and homogeneous-FS hypotheses.

Wu24 Theorem 2.12 already prints $X_0^T$ as the model carrier. For continuous potentials use model approximation at fixed $\xi$, and for finite-energy potentials use the decreasing-net continuity of the genuine mixed operator in Wu24 Theorem 6.3. Every approximating measure has mass one on closed $K$. A weak limit has the same property: for each nonnegative continuous function vanishing on $K$, its integral is zero throughout and at the limit; Radon regularity then gives zero mass to $X_0\setminus K$. Hence every genuine finite-energy output is a probability on $K$.

**Corrected density theorem.** Let $D_\xi$ denote restrictions to $K$ of differences $\phi-\psi$, where $\phi,\psi\in\operatorname{CPSH}(a\xi)$ for some $a>0$. Then $D_\xi$ is a real vector lattice containing constants and is uniformly dense in $C(K)$.

To prove the vector-lattice assertion, use the same-ray addition and scaling rules of Wu24 Proposition 3.3. If $u=\phi_1-\phi_2$ at $a\xi$ and $v=\psi_1-\psi_2$ at $b\xi$, their sum is a difference at $ab/(a+b)\xi$, and

$$\max\{u,v\}=\max\{\phi_1+\psi_2,\psi_1+\phi_2\}-(\phi_2+\psi_2).$$

Both terms on the right have that same polarization. Multiplication by a positive scalar follows from rescaling the polarization; negatives follow by interchanging the terms. Constants come from $\rho+c-\rho$.

For separation, two different invariant semivaluations differ on some nonzero homogeneous $f\in R_\alpha$. The weight is nonzero, since the zero-weight part is the ground field for a good cone. Put $d=\langle\xi,\alpha\rangle>0$. For sufficiently large finite $A$ the function

$$u_{f,A}=\max\{(-v(f)+A)/d,\rho(v)\}-\rho(v)$$

separates the two points. On $K$ it is $\max\{(A-v(f))/d,0\}$; this also separates a finite evaluation from $+\infty$. The first maximum is a genuine FS function because the reference generators are retained. The real lattice Stone–Weierstrass theorem now proves the density assertion.

Wu24 Theorem 3.5 literally prints density in $C(X_0)$ (LF 416–417), and its proof assumes two different points differ on a homogeneous function (LF 434–435). That assumption holds on $K$, not on the full link. The same correction applies to Theorem 3.6: its tests can approximate continuous equivariant functions that factor through torification. Also, the printed algebraic formula for the maximum at LF 424–432 is not the valid lattice identity above.

**Explicit full-link counterexample.** Take $X=\mathbb A^2$, $T=(\mathbb G_m)^2$, and $\xi=(1,1)$. Let $w$ be evaluation at $(1,1)$ as a semivaluation, and let $v_{\rm triv}$ be the generic trivial valuation. Both belong to $X_0$. Every nonzero homogeneous monomial has value zero at both, so $\tau(w)=v_{\rm triv}$. But $w(x-y)=+\infty$, whereas $v_{\rm triv}(x-y)=0$. Thus they are distinct and $w\notin K$. All psh tests agree at these points. Since each FS function has its maximum on $X_0$ at the trivial valuation, $E(\phi)\le\phi(v_{\rm triv})$; pass to decreasing approximants to obtain this for finite-energy functions. Therefore

$$E^\vee(\delta_w)=E^\vee(\delta_{v_{\rm triv}})=0.$$

Nevertheless $\delta_w$ cannot be an output, by the carrier result. This does not refute the target problem, whose target domain is $K$. It proves that unrestricted full-link dual energy cannot determine the genuine output measure. The continuous function $e^{-v(x-y)}$ also directly separates the two points while all homogeneous tests agree.

## 3. Energy signs and literal normalization defects

For $\phi,\psi\in\mathcal E^1(\xi)$ the correct inequalities are

$$\int(\phi-\psi)\operatorname{MA}(\phi)\ \le\ E(\phi)-E(\psi)\ \le\ \int(\phi-\psi)\operatorname{MA}(\psi).\tag{1}$$

For continuous potentials, the successive mixed terms in the energy difference decrease with the number of $\phi$ factors, by integration by parts and the Hodge inequality. Their average lies between the first and last terms. For finite-energy potentials approximate both decreasingly; Wu24 Theorem 6.3 gives continuity of all involved integrals and energies. This proves (1), with no arbitrary non-psh variation.

Equivalently, along an admissible segment the first derivative is $\int u\operatorname{MA}(\phi_t)$ and the second derivative is

$$\frac{n-1}{V_\xi}\int u\,d'd''u\wedge(d'd''\phi_t)^{n-2}\wedge d'd''\rho^+\le0.$$

Wu24 prints an incorrect intermediate second-derivative formula at LF 595–600, omitting the $d'd''u$ factor and the $n-1$ coefficient; the correct formula appears immediately afterwards at LF 605–610. Its Hodge proposition at LF 485 names the equivariant difference space, although its proof assumes the invariant difference space. Our use is only for $u=\phi-\psi$, which is invariant.

Wu24 Proposition 7.3's proof prints the first inequality in (1) with the opposite direction (LF 1247–1249). Repairing that direction gives, for every genuine output,

$$E^\vee(\operatorname{MA}(\phi))=E(\phi)-\int\phi\operatorname{MA}(\phi)<\infty.\tag{2}$$

Indeed (1) bounds $E(\psi)-\int\psi\operatorname{MA}(\phi)$ above by the right side of (2), and equality is obtained at $\psi=\phi$. Mixed finite-energy integrability makes the right side finite. The resulting energy duality in Proposition 7.3 is consistent; the printed supporting inequality is not.

There are also genuine normalization conflicts in Wu21. Its Lemma 3.16 gives $S=\frac n{n+1}\widetilde S$ for the truncated-degree filtration invariant, while Corollary 5.14 identifies the genuine energy with projective energy. Theorem 5.15(1) nevertheless prints $E^{\rm NA}=S$. For the homogeneous filtration weight $\chi(f_\alpha)=c\langle\xi,\alpha\rangle$ on a polynomial cone, the FS potential is $\rho+c$, so the probability-normalized energy is $c$. Counting monomials in the truncated simplex gives $S=cn/(n+1)$, whereas the equal-degree invariant is $\widetilde S=c$. This disproves a literal application of $E=S$ with those definitions. It agrees with the $(n+1)/n$ factor occurring in Wu24 Proposition 7.4. No general equality in that proposition's irrational approximation is inferred here; its printed conclusion is only an upper bound.

Likewise Wu21 Theorem 6.3 (LF 1442–1447) prints

$$E^{\rm NA}(\chi)=\frac1{(n+1)V_\xi}\int_{P_\xi}\widetilde\chi\,d\lambda,\qquad \lambda(P_\xi)=V_\xi.$$

For the same constant-on-the-slice weight $\widetilde\chi=c$ this is $c/(n+1)$, contradicting $E(\rho+c)=c$. The formula, with its literal stated measure convention, is not applied. This constant-shift check does not determine every correction to that section; it suffices to rule out using its displayed normalization unchanged.

## 4. Intrinsic necessary test and finite-energy approximations

On the actual carrier define

$$E^\vee_\xi(\mu)=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal E^1(\xi)}\left(E_\xi(\phi)-\int_K\phi\,d\mu\right).$$

The following intrinsic **necessary**, not sufficient, test uses only homogeneous FS functions:

$$\mu\text{ is a probability on }K,\qquad\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal H(\xi)}\left(E_\xi(\phi)-\int_K\phi\,d\mu\right)<\infty.\tag{3}$$

Its equivalence to finite dual energy is proved next; its sufficiency for solving the equation is not proved.

**Radon decreasing-net lemma.** If $f_i$ are continuous and decrease to an upper semicontinuous $f$ on compact $K$, then $\int f_i d\mu\downarrow\int f d\mu$, allowing $-\infty$. To verify the net issue, use the regularity formula $\int f d\mu=\inf_{g\in C(K),\,g\ge f}\int g d\mu$ for bounded-above usc functions, with truncation for $-\infty$. For any such $g$ and $\epsilon>0$, the open sets $\{f_i<g+\epsilon\}$ increase and cover $K$. Compactness supplies one index covering $K$. Consequently $\inf_i\int f_i\le\int g+\epsilon$. Taking infima and letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ proves the lemma. This is the compact-carrier argument behind BJ22 Lemma 7.17, without an unjustified countable monotone-convergence theorem for arbitrary nets.

Approximate any $\phi\in\mathcal E^1$ decreasingly by FS $\phi_i$. The energy converges to $E(\phi)$ by Wu24 Proposition 5.10, and the preceding lemma gives convergence of target integrals even when the limit is $-\infty$. Hence the supremum over FS equals the supremum over $\mathcal E^1$. If it is finite, every finite-energy potential has finite integral, because otherwise its own contribution to the supremum would be $+\infty$. Formula (2) proves that every output satisfies (3).

This argument gives target integration continuity for decreasing FS approximation of a *fixed* potential. It does not prove continuity for arbitrary weakly convergent energy-bounded potentials, as needed in an attainment argument.

**A repaired elementary mixed-integrability bound.** Let $\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{n-1}$ be continuous psh functions nonpositive on $K$, and put $B=\max_i\{-E(\phi_i)\}$. Then

$$\int_K\phi_0\operatorname{MA}(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_{n-1})\ge-\frac{n^{n+1}}{(n-1)!}B.\tag{4}$$

To prove it put $\bar\phi=n^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i$. Concavity gives $E(\bar\phi)\ge-B$. Every mixed term in its energy sum is nonpositive. Keeping just the last term therefore gives the valid inequality

$$E(\bar\phi)\le\frac1n\int\bar\phi\operatorname{MA}(\bar\phi).$$

Since $\bar\phi\le\phi_0/n$, positivity gives an upper bound $n^{-2}\int\phi_0\operatorname{MA}(\bar\phi)$. In the expansion of $\operatorname{MA}(\bar\phi)$, the term with one copy of each $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_{n-1}$ has coefficient $(n-1)!/n^{n-1}$. Multiplication by $\phi_0\le0$ permits dropping all other terms for an upper bound. Thus

$$-B\le E(\bar\phi)\le\frac{(n-1)!}{n^{n+1}}\int\phi_0\operatorname{MA}(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_{n-1}),$$

which is (4). Wu24 Lemma 6.4's proof has an incorrect $E(\bar\phi)\le\int\bar\phi\operatorname{MA}(\bar\phi)$; the extra $1/n$ in the repaired bound matters. The displayed definition of $J$ there also omits the $-E$ term and the energy sum has an incorrect upper index. Formula (4) is a repaired continuous estimate with an explicit, nonoptimal constant. Passing from it to arbitrary weak target integration is not justified by this bound alone.

## 5. What maximizing sequences actually yield

Let $\mu$ satisfy (3). The finite supremum has an FS maximizing sequence $\phi_j$ by the defining property of a supremum. Set

$$\Delta_j=E^\vee(\mu)-E(\phi_j)+\int\phi_jd\mu\longrightarrow0.$$

The fixed-Reeb estimates already suffice to prove that this sequence is Cauchy for $I$, modulo constants. Here are the details, avoiding a realization assumption. Write $Q_n$ for a quasi-triangle constant:

$$I(\phi,\psi)\le Q_n\bigl(I(\phi,\theta)+I(\theta,\psi)\bigr).$$

For $\theta=(\phi+\psi)/2$, the $J\ge I/n$ bounds give

$$E(\theta)-\tfrac12E(\phi)-\tfrac12E(\psi)=\tfrac12J_\theta(\phi)+\tfrac12J_\theta(\psi)\ge\frac{I(\phi,\psi)}{2nQ_n}.$$

The integral terms cancel because $\theta$ is the midpoint. The left side is also

$$\tfrac12\Delta_\mu(\phi)+\tfrac12\Delta_\mu(\psi)-\Delta_\mu(\theta)\le\tfrac12\bigl(\Delta_\mu(\phi)+\Delta_\mu(\psi)\bigr).$$

Therefore

$$I(\phi_j,\phi_k)\le nQ_n(\Delta_j+\Delta_k)\longrightarrow0.\tag{5}$$

This uses only Wu24's fixed-ray $I$–$J$ bounds and quasi-triangle inequality, whose proofs use the invariant differences and Hodge forms. Taking $\psi=\rho$, with $\Delta_\mu(\rho)=E^\vee(\mu)$, also gives

$$J_\rho(\phi_j)\le(n-1)Q_n\bigl(\Delta_j+E^\vee(\mu)\bigr).\tag{6}$$

Finally, Wu24 Lemma 7.7 gives that $\operatorname{MA}(\phi_j)$ is $I^\vee$-Cauchy. This statement concerns images of actual potentials only. It does **not** identify any weak limit with $\mu$, or prove that the Cauchy sequence has a limit in the genuine $\mathcal E^1/\mathbb R$. No closure of unknown outputs is proposed as an image characterization.

The source defines $I$ and $I^\vee$ as quasi-pseudometrics at this stage. Its literal Corollary 7.8 does not by itself establish global separation of measures. The problem input supplies the already established uniqueness modulo constants on the genuine image. With that separation on $\mathcal E^1/\mathbb R$, equal outputs force $I=0$ and hence potentials differing by a constant. This is the given uniqueness conclusion, not a new existence result.

## 6. Direct model computations, including a genuinely irrational atom

The following examples use the local tropical current rule in Wu21's proof of Corollary 5.7, with the mass computed explicitly rather than inferred from the defective toric energy formula.

For a soft maximum $L_\epsilon(z)=\epsilon\log(1+e^{z_1/\epsilon}+\cdots+e^{z_r/\epsilon})$, its gradient maps $\mathbb R^r$ diffeomorphically to the interior of the standard simplex. The change-of-variables formula gives

$$\int_{\mathbb R^r}\det(\nabla^2L_\epsilon)\,dz=1/r!.$$

The superform wedge power $(d'd''L_\epsilon)^r$ has density $r!\det(\nabla^2L_\epsilon)$, so its total tropical mass is one. These measures concentrate at zero as $\epsilon\downarrow0$ by scaling. Likewise the second derivative of $\epsilon\log(1+e^{t/\epsilon})$ has integral one and converges to the atom at zero. An invertible linear coordinate change with matrix $C$ multiplies the tropical top-degree integral by $|\det C|$: the superform has two copies of the coordinate determinant, and integration cancels one. This proves the local atom coefficients used below under the source's current convention.

On $\mathbb A^2$ with $\xi=(1,1)$, put $\rho=\max\{-a,-b\}$ in tropical coordinates and $\phi_s=\max\{-a,s-b\}$, $s>0$. The homogeneous curvature line of $\phi_s$ is $b-a=s$. It intersects the curvature of $\rho^+=\max\{0,-a,-b\}$ only at $(a,b)=(0,s)$. The determinant of the two gradient jumps is one. There is no boundary mass: near a zero coordinate the corresponding term can be discarded, leaving $\phi_s$ locally affine. Thus

$$\operatorname{MA}(\rho)=\delta_{(0,0)},\qquad\operatorname{MA}(\phi_s)=\delta_{(0,s)},\qquad V_\xi=1.$$

The two-term energy formula gives $E(\phi_s)=s/2$. In (1), with $\psi=\rho$, the two bounding integrals are respectively $0$ and $s$. Hence the printed Proposition 7.3 inequality would assert $s/2\le0$, an explicit counterexample to that displayed sign.

Now take $X=\mathbb A^3$, $T=(\mathbb G_m)^3$, and

$$\xi=(1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3),\quad \rho=\max\{-a_1,-a_2/\sqrt2,-a_3/\sqrt3\},$$
$$\phi=\max\{-a_1,1-a_2/\sqrt2,1-a_3/\sqrt3\}.$$

This is a genuine FS function with homogeneous generators $x_1,x_2,x_3$ and parameters $(0,\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$. The lattice-point simplex count gives

$$V_\xi=1/\sqrt6.$$

The square of the homogeneous curvature is supported on the line $a_2=\sqrt2(a_1+1)$, $a_3=\sqrt3(a_1+1)$. Away from this line at most two terms are active, making that square zero. On the line, $\rho=-a_1$, so the radial cut meets it only at

$$w=(0,\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\in K.$$

Near $w$ use coordinates

$$t=-a_1,\quad z_2=1+a_1-a_2/\sqrt2,\quad z_3=1+a_1-a_3/\sqrt3.$$

The reference cut is $\max\{0,t\}$, and $\phi=-a_1+\max\{0,z_2,z_3\}$. The affine common term has zero curvature. The coordinate matrix has determinant of absolute value $1/\sqrt6$, so the preceding smooth calculation proves

$$ (d'd''\phi)^2\wedge d'd''\rho^+=(1/\sqrt6)\delta_w,\qquad\operatorname{MA}_\xi(\phi)=\delta_w.$$

There is no extra coordinate-boundary mass, since at least one of the three terms disappears there and the remaining maximum has curvature rank at most one. The semivaluation $w$ is the monomial valuation giving $x_1$ value zero and $x_2,x_3$ values $\sqrt2,\sqrt3$. This gives a genuine irrational fixed-Reeb model output on the correct carrier, with mass exactly one. It is a restricted toric construction and provides no existence theorem for arbitrary measures or general geometry.

## 7. Search for the realization step: precise unresolved passages

The four papers do not furnish a justified transfer establishing the following cone statements. This is a finding about the attempted source applications, not a proof that no future argument or theorem can establish them.

1. **Potential-space compactness/completeness.** Wu24 gives decreasing-net closure, finite-value controls on quasimonomial points, and upper semicontinuity of energy. It does not prove compactness of normalized cone psh functions or strong completeness of the genuine $\mathcal E^1/\mathbb R$. Pointwise boundedness alone gives a limit in a product space; it does not show that the limit is a genuine psh function. BJ22 Theorem 5.11 proves projective compactness through the envelope property, explicitly using upper envelopes of tails. BJ22 Theorem 12.8 identifies that property with strong completeness and surjectivity for a projective ample class. Applying its conclusion to the cone would assume the missing realization.

2. **Envelopes/orthogonality.** Fixed-ray max stability supplies finite maxima, not closure of bounded arbitrary upper envelopes or the orthogonality limit. BJ23 Definition 2.15 requires the actual limit $\int(f-\psi)\operatorname{MA}(\psi)\to0$ for the directed psh tests below every test obstacle. Proposition 2.19 derives uniform differentiation from this assumption. A putative formalism on $K$ would need an ordered space of forms, all positivity axioms, identification of its test psh class with the genuine cone class, and orthogonality. The fixed-ray calculus does not prove that list.

3. **Target integration.** Decreasing FS approximation works by the lemma in Section 4. The general energy-bounded weak convergence needed for maximizing sequences does not follow from it. BJ22 Proposition 9.19 obtains that stronger continuity by uniform approximation of the target by model-MA measures. Its earlier approximation theorem uses uniform differentiation. Invoking Proposition 9.19 on the cone without proving that approximation is circular.

4. **Differentiation and Euler–Lagrange.** Wu24's derivatives along admissible psh segments are sufficient for (1) and (5). They do not give variations in both signs of every dense continuous test. At a hypothetical maximizer $\phi$, admissible segments only give $\int(\psi-\phi)(\operatorname{MA}(\phi)-\mu)\le0$ for psh $\psi$. A maximum on a constrained convex set can have a nonzero outward derivative; for example $-x^2-x$ is maximized at zero on $x\ge0$. BJ22 Theorem 8.5 differentiates an extended energy on arbitrary PL perturbations. Its proof uses projective section-norm relative volumes and mixed masses involving added polarizations. The cone version of that relative-volume comparison is not established here. BJ22 Theorem 9.11 and Corollary 9.12 derive target identification and the Euler–Lagrange equality from that two-sided differentiability, not from concavity alone.

5. **Completion of measures.** BJ23 Theorems 2.23 and 2.26 assume orthogonality, prove density of test outputs, and complete the measure space. The paper expressly does not investigate finite-energy potentials. Even a valid cone application would not identify every completed object with a Wu finite-energy potential. Equation (5) isolates the extra potential realization needed here.

6. **Rational quotient and varying Reeb.** Wu21 Lemmas 5.11–5.12 and Proposition 5.13 identify homogeneous model functions and their measures with quotient functions for primitive rational Reeb fields. A general quotient is normal and can be singular; the supplied BJ22 Theorem 12.9 assumes smoothness. Even for a smooth quotient and a torus larger than the Reeb circle, the actual carrier corresponds to an invariant subset of the quotient, and cone potentials correspond to invariant metrics. Full quotient surjectivity alone is not a proof of preservation of those classes. No quotient transfer or averaging of nonlinear MA is used here. Wu24 Lemmas 2.11/3.2 approximate individual continuous potentials by varying Reeb fields and Proposition 3.3 adds only proportional polarizations. They do not give uniform estimates for target solutions with two changing, nonproportional Reeb fields, nor justify commuting the approximation with finite-energy realization. The irrational toric atom in Section 6 shows why restricting every intermediate measure to divisorial support would also be wrong.

No intrinsic sufficient measure criterion meeting the assignment's exclusions has been established. In particular, variational attainment, existence of an inverse, or a closure defined by unknown finite-energy outputs is not presented as a characterization. The unchanged original P13.2 remains unresolved in this deliverable, including its genuinely general irrational geometry. Uniqueness modulo constants remains the established input on the image.
