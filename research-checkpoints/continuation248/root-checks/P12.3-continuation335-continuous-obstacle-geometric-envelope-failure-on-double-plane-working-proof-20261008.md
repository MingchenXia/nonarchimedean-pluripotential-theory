## 2. The whole boundary and a necessary inequality

The equation xy=0 forces v(x)=+infinity or v(y)=+infinity. Hence X is the union of two closed subsets

    X1 = {v(y)=+infinity},
    X2 = {v(x)=+infinity}.

They are precisely the m1=(x,z) and m2=(y,z) normalized boundaries of A1 and A2. Their intersection is one point c. Indeed, v(x)=v(y)=+infinity forces v(z)=1. On the quotient DVR k[z]_(z), every nonzero element is z^d times a unit, and units have value zero. Therefore this semivaluation is uniquely ord_z after quotienting by (x,y). This explicit description retains every possible support, not just fraction-field valuations.

Let o1 in X1 be ord_(x,z), normalized by o1(m1)=1. This is the usual total-order valuation of the two-dimensional regular local ring. For EVERY w in X1 and every f in A1,

    w(f) >= o1(f).

For a polynomial this follows because w(x),w(z)>=1 and the valuation of a sum is at least the minimum of the monomial values; a local unit denominator has value zero. The same argument handles cancellation and infinite values. Thus for every proper primary I in R,

    w(I A1) >= o1(I A1).

Extension to A1 is still proper primary: I is contained in m and contains a power of m. Consequently every q in Q_R satisfies q(w)<=q(o1) for w in X1. Uniform closure preserves that inequality, and taking the infimum of an ACTUAL decreasing net preserves it, including extended negative values. We conclude

    h(w) <= h(o1)       for every h in D_R and every w in X1.       (2.1)

This also holds for C_R. By G331 it is a necessary inequality for the actual geometric completed class D_geom, rather than merely for a selected primary subfamily. It is proved directly here; no normal-centre Izumi or zero-dichotomy theorem is needed.

## 3. A finite continuous obstacle

Define on the whole X

    phi(v) = -1/(1+v(x)),      with 1/(1+infinity)=0.

The coordinate evaluation v(x) is continuous with values in the extended interval. The displayed function is continuous there. Since v(x)>=1 whenever finite, phi lies in [-1/2,0]. In particular phi is a finite continuous obstacle on the COMPACT actual boundary.

On X2 it is zero; at c it is zero; at o1 it is -1/2. On X1 it can vary but is always at least -1/2. It agrees on the identified conductor points automatically because it is defined by a downstairs element.

For each integer N>=1 let

    I_N = (x^N, y, z^N) in R,
    h_N(v) = -v(I_N)/(2N).

The radical of I_N is m, so I_N is an actual proper primary ideal. Thus h_N belongs to Q_R and to the independently geometric core: on Y_(I_N), the negative exceptional Cartier divisor -(1/(2N))E_(I_N) is actual effective-negative and relatively ample, hence has the required nonnegative contracted-curve degrees. Its all-support evaluation is exactly h_N.

On X1, y has infinite value and min(v(x),v(z))=1. Therefore

    h_N = -1/2                       on ALL of X1.

On X2,

    h_N(w) = -(1/(2N)) min(w(y),N w(z)).

At c this is -1/2. At every w in X2 other than c, w(y) is finite, and h_N(w) tends increasingly to zero. The reason w(y) is finite off c is the DVR argument in section 2: if both x and y have infinite value then w=c. Infinite w(z), all cancellations and nonminimal supports are included in these formulas.

Each h_N is below phi: on X1 use phi>=-1/2, and on X2 use h_N<=0=phi. The entire sequence is increasing, since min(w(y)/N,w(z)) decreases with N on X2 and the values on X1 are fixed. Increasing is an observation, NOT a granted completion operation.

## 4. Exact raw envelope over the ENTIRE geometric completed class

Define the genuine full-family raw envelope

    p(v) = sup{h(v): h in D_geom(R), h<=phi on ALL X}.

Every h_N is in this family. Conversely any member h satisfies h(o1)<=phi(o1)=-1/2, so (2.1) gives h<=-1/2 on ALL X1. The constant -1/2 belongs to Q_R, because v(m)=1, and is below phi everywhere. Thus p=-1/2 on X1. On X2 outside c the sequence h_N supplies values increasing to zero, while the obstacle bounds every member by zero. Therefore the FULL raw envelope is exactly

    p(v) = -1/2  for v in X1,
    p(v) =  0    for v in X2 minus {c}.                         (4.1)

This computation uses all D_geom competitors in the upper bound, not just the displayed sequence. In particular p(c)=-1/2 despite phi(c)=0.

To see the actual discontinuity, let w_N on A2 be the monomial valuation with w_N(y)=N and w_N(z)=1. It is m2-normalized, differs from c, and converges to c on EVERY element of A2: a polynomial with nonzero residue modulo y eventually has the value of that residue under ord_z; a polynomial divisible by y has value tending to infinity; unit denominators remain zero. The same is true downstairs by restriction. Hence p(w_N)=0 while p(c)=-1/2.

Away from c, each branch image is disjoint from the other closed branch image, so it has ordinary open neighbourhoods avoiding that other branch. Thus p is locally constant away from c. Its upper-semicontinuous regularization is

    p*(v) = -1/2  for v in X1 minus {c},
    p*(v) =  0    for v in X2, including c.                    (4.2)

The increase at c follows from w_N->c and the global upper bound zero. Formula (4.2) is upper semicontinuous, remains below the continuous obstacle, and is discontinuous at c when approached through X1.

But p* is NOT in D_geom(R). If it were, the necessary inequality (2.1) at w=c would give

    0 = p*(c) <= p*(o1) = -1/2,

which is impossible. The raw envelope p is not in D_geom either: it is not upper semicontinuous, whereas an infimum of continuous functions is upper semicontinuous. Both failures concern the actual completed geometric boundary class already identified in G331. They are not failures of a different literal radial analytic class, and they do not require interpreting a measure on an undefined domain.

Thus blanket positivity, and blanket continuity, of the usc-regularized full geometric envelope for EVERY finite continuous obstacle fail on excellent reduced nonnormal two-dimensional germs. The obstruction persists even though the ideal/geometric comparison, all support evaluations and positive conductor descent are established.

## 5. Why normalization alone does not repair the envelope

Pull phi to the separated normalization boundary X1 disjoint_union X2. On X1 its full geometric raw envelope is the constant -1/2 by (2.1) and the same lower bound. On X2 its obstacle is identically zero and its envelope is identically zero. Both separated envelopes are finite continuous and geometric.

Their two values above c are -1/2 and zero. The pair is NOT fibre-constant and therefore does not descend through the conductor identification. This is a concrete obstruction to unrestricted envelope commutation with normalization, even for a pulled-back finite continuous obstacle. G331's descent theorem applies to already fibre-constant C/D functions; it never asserted that taking an envelope preserves this compatibility. This calculation does not refute G331.

The source functions h_N are individually compatible above c. Their increasing limit is compatible there but is not usc downstairs; performing usc regularization on the quotient raises the common value and breaks positivity on the first normal branch. Simply closing the cone under increasing limits would change its definition, and declaring the regularized result geometric would contradict (2.1).

## 6. A general compact-boundary lemma for continuous obstacles

The following statements are elementary consequences of the EXACT G331 C/D operations. Let X be any nonempty compact primary section, C=C_R, D=D_R, and phi a finite continuous obstacle. Then

    sup{h in D:h<=phi} = sup{g in C:g<=phi}                    (6.1)

pointwise, and this common raw function is finite and lower semicontinuous.

Proof. Negative constants supply a finite lower bound, and min(phi,0) supplies the upper bound. If h=inf_i g_i belongs to D and h<=phi, the increasing directed open sets {g_i<phi+epsilon} cover compact X. Finitely many indices and directedness give ONE g_i<phi+epsilon everywhere. Subtracting the negative constant epsilon preserves C; hence g_i-epsilon<=phi and g_i-epsilon>=h-epsilon. Let epsilon decrease. This proves the D-supremum is no greater than the C-supremum. The opposite inequality holds since a constant net makes C a subset of D. A supremum of continuous functions is lower semicontinuous.

Write p for (6.1). Its regularization p* is still <=phi. Every original D-minorant is <=p<=p*, and every D-minorant of p* is also a minorant of phi. Thus its full raw D-envelope is AGAIN p:

    P_D(p*) = p.                                                (6.2)

If p* belonged to D, it would itself be a competitor on the left, forcing p*<=p. Therefore p*=p. Since p is also lsc, it is continuous.

Conversely if p is continuous, compactness and C's finite-max stability approximate it UNIFORMLY from below: for each point select a C-minorant whose value is within epsilon of p; the strict comparison remains valid on an open neighbourhood; a finite cover and the maximum of those finitely many minorants give p-epsilon<g<=p everywhere. Uniform closure gives p in C. We have proved the exact equivalences

    p* in D   iff   p is continuous   iff   p in C
              iff   p*=p.                                     (6.3)

This is a TEST of the unresolved geometric envelope property, not a proof that every ring satisfies it. In the double-plane example (4.1), it detects the failure. It also shows that a claim of geometric positivity of the regularized envelope under these exact operations must actually prove continuity of its raw envelope for continuous obstacles.

The raw operator on finite continuous obstacles is order preserving and 1-Lipschitz in the uniform norm. If ||phi-psi||<=delta, subtract delta from each C-minorant of phi to obtain a C-minorant of psi, and reverse the argument. Neither this contraction nor raw algebraic idempotence proves p* belongs to D.

## 7. Exact ideal formula and gauge covariance

For a finite continuous phi define the actual ideal

    J_k(phi) = {f in R: v(f)>=-k phi(v) for EVERY v in X},
    a_k(v) = -v(J_k(phi))/k,             k=1,2,... .

The set J_k is an ideal: valuations of sums are at least the minimum, and multiplication by an R-element only increases the value. Choose M>=max(0,max_X(-phi)) and a nonnegative integer L>=kM. Then m^L is contained in J_k. When phi is negative somewhere, units are excluded, so J_k is proper primary; otherwise J_k=R. Its finite generators imply a_k<=phi at every point, including all supports.

Every single rational primary profile -v(I)/k below phi has I contained in J_k and is consequently <=a_k. Single rational primary profiles are uniformly dense in C by G331. Approximate a C-minorant uniformly and subtract a small rational negative constant, synchronizing denominators by ideal products and a primary-gauge power; this produces a single rational primary profile still below phi and arbitrarily close from below. Therefore the full raw envelope has the exact formula

    p = sup_k a_k.                                              (7.1)

Products satisfy J_k J_l subset J_(k+l) and J_k^l subset J_(kl). Thus a_(n!) increases and converges pointwise to (7.1), because every fixed k divides sufficiently large n!. This is explicit algebraic raw-envelope construction; no increasing-limit membership in D follows.

For another primary gauge b, let v_b=v/v(b) and phi_b(v_b)=phi(v)/v(b). Multiplying the defining J_k inequality by v(b)>0 shows J_k(phi_b) is EXACTLY the same downstairs ideal. Profiles and p transform by the same homogeneous cocycle. The gauge change is a homeomorphism with continuous positive bounded denominator. Since p is bounded on the compact section, usc regularization transforms by the same cocycle as well. Hence the obstruction and (6.3) are independent of primary gauge. ## 10. A general obstruction when a conductor fibre joins distinct normal centres

Suppose some normalization fibre contains a point w_A in one positive-dimensional normal local centre A and a point w_B in a DIFFERENT normal local centre B. Then the universal finite-continuous-obstacle geometric envelope property FAILS downstairs. No envelope-positivity assumption on the normal centres is required for this conclusion.

Choose a normalized centred divisorial valuation nu_A of A. It has finite conductor value. Thus x_A=rho(nu_A) belongs to no image of any other normal centre, since its full rho-fibre is a singleton. The finite union F of those other centre images is compact and closed in compact Hausdorff X. By continuous separation of a point and a closed set, choose a continuous phi:X->[-1,0] with phi(x_A)=-1 and phi=0 on F. This is an actual downstairs obstacle, so its two pulled-back values at every identified conductor pair agree. It is not an arbitrarily incompatible upstairs obstacle.

On centre B, rho^*phi is identically zero, so p_S is identically zero there. Indeed the tuple which is zero on B and a sufficiently negative constant on every other centre is a global semilocal C-minorant, by G331's proved product-class gluing. On centre A, every D_A-minorant h of rho^*phi satisfies h(nu_A)<=-1. G331's normal-centre inequality n6, with this fixed divisorial nu_A and any specified normalized w_A, gives a finite constant K>0 for which

    h(nu_A) >= K h(w_A).

It first holds for every real negative primary profile, then for uniform limits, then for ACTUAL decreasing-net infima; extended negative values are harmless. Therefore h(w_A)<=-1/K for EVERY minorant, and the raw normalized envelope satisfies p_S(w_A)<=-1/K<0. In contrast p_S(w_B)=0. The full p_S is not fibre-constant. Criterion (9.3) rules out p_R* in D_geom(R). Equivalently the raw p_R cannot be continuous.

All constants here are attached to a FIXED normalized divisorial point; no uniform Izumi bound over the whole compact boundary is asserted. If the primary gauge a contains m_A^r after local extension, G331 gives K=C_nu_A r. Proper-primary generators with infinite support values remain controlled because their primary ideal value is finite and attained by some nonzero generator. Units are treated separately. The elementary double-plane example of sections 1--5 is an explicit instance, with a complete formula for BOTH raw and regularized envelopes.

This obstruction excludes every excellent germ having an infinite-conductor boundary fibre which joins distinct normal centres. It does not exclude multibranch CURVES, whose boundary conductor values are finite and whose fibres are singletons. It is also not a classification of all nontrivial fibres inside ONE normal centre. The remaining normal-envelope regularity problem is exposed explicitly by (9.3), rather than implicitly assumed solved.
