## 1. The actual surface, ample bundles and intersections

Over any algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero
field k, let pi:X=Bl_p(P^2_k)->P^2_k, p=[0:0:1]. Write H=pi^*O(1)
and E for the effective exceptional Cartier divisor. With homogeneous
coordinates [X:Y:Z] and [U:V], the incidence equation

    X V = Y U

gives this blowup as a closed subscheme of P^2 x P^1. On Z!=0,
its two blowup charts have coordinates (x,v), y=xv, and (y,u), x=yu.
Away from p the first projection is an isomorphism. Thus X is a
smooth integral projective surface, over every such k. The second
projection r has r^*O(1)=H-E: the pencil of lines through p has
its exceptional factor removed.

Consequently every rational bundle A_epsilon=H-epsilon E, with
0<epsilon<1, is ample. Indeed

    A_epsilon=(1-epsilon)H+epsilon(H-E).

After clearing denominators it is the restriction of O(b,c) from
P^2 x P^1, with positive integers b,c. The Segre--Veronese embedding
makes O(b,c) very ample; its restriction remains very ample. This
argument applies to the ACTUAL bundle, with its stated isomorphism.

Two lines avoiding p compute H^2=1. The pullback H restricts trivially
to E, so H.E=0. In the blowup charts the exceptional normal bundle
is O_{P^1}(-1), hence E^2=-1. The last identification is also the
exact statement and printed proof of Stacks0AGQ, freshly read in
native renderer turn677588view0, inclusive lines22--42. That lemma
is applied to the two-dimensional regular local ring O_{P^2,p};
the exceptional normal bundle and its degree are determined there.

## 2. The same actual nonample bundle has volume one

Fix any rational a>0 and the ACTUAL rational line bundle L=H+aE.
It is nonnef since L.E=-a<0, and therefore nonample. For every
positive integer m clearing a, multiplication by the canonical
section of maE gives an isomorphism

    H^0(X,mH) -> H^0(X,mL).

Here is the reverse direction, without assuming a Zariski
decomposition theorem. A nonzero section of mL has an effective
divisor T=kE+R, where R has no E component. The canonical section
of O(R) restricts nontrivially to E, so R.E>=0. The intersection identity

    -ma = T.E = -k + R.E

implies k>=ma. The section is therefore divisible by the actual
Cartier section s_E^{ma}, leaving a section of mH. The division
is regular in the smooth local charts and hence globally.

Also H^0(X,mH)=H^0(P^2,O(m)). One can prove this directly: a section
descends over P^2 minus p using the isomorphism there, and it extends
over p. On the affine plane at p, an algebraic regular function on
the punctured plane lies in

    k[x,y]_x intersect k[x,y]_y = k[x,y]

inside k(x,y), by unique factorization. This proves the needed
extension in a local trivialization of O(m). Thus

    h^0(X,mL)=binomial(m+2,2),  vol(L)=1.

Equivalently, for any fixed denominator q of a, the equality for
mqL is binomial(mq+2,2), and division by (mq)^2 gives the same
rational-bundle volume. In particular L is big over every stated k.
The intersection L^2=1-a^2 may even be negative, showing why that
intersection is not automatically the equation's measure mass.
