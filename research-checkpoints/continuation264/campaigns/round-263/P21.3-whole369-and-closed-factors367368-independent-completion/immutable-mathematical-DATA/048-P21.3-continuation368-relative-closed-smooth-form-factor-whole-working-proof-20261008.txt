1. Category and definition.
Let X be a good separated boundaryless pure n-dimensional analytic
space over ANY complete non-Archimedean K, including trivial valuation.
Use ordinary smooth CLD test forms on ordinary opens, ordinary positive
currents, and their ordinary current differentials d',d''; put D=d'd''.
For any restriction-compatible class F(U) of finite continuous real
functions, call a positive doubly closed T BT_F if every pair u,v in F(U)
on EVERY ordinary open U with D[u]=D[v] has D((u-v)T|U)=0. This can use
all classical continuous individually psh functions in original P21.3;
no equality with the locally uniformly generated CPsh class is presumed.

Let T have bidegree (p,p) and BT_F, and let gamma be ANY smooth real form
of type (r,r), where p+r<=n. Define C=gamma wedge T by ordinary smooth
form-current multiplication. The claim is the following exact criterion:

  C has BT_F IFF C is positive and doubly closed.

Positivity of gamma itself and closedness of gamma itself are not
required. The actual product's positivity and closedness are required.
If gamma is strongly positive, positivity of C follows from ordinary
smooth factor positivity, but the theorem also permits other gamma for
which C is positive. The initial BT_F property of T is an explicit
hypothesis, not a consequence of positive double closedness alone.

2. Required ordinary multiplication facts, proved on compact tests.
For a positive current T and any compact smooth test theta of complementary
equal type, the construction in CLD7.2.11--12 gives a finite signed Radon
measure mu(T,theta) such that

  int phi dmu(T,theta) = <T,phi theta>

for every smooth scalar phi. To obtain it for arbitrary theta, split
theta into its symmetric and antisymmetric parts. The latter evaluates
to zero. On finitely many tropical charts covering the compact support,
the symmetric part is a difference of two positive compact smooth forms:
add and subtract a sufficiently large standard strongly positive form,
times a compact cutoff equal to one on the relevant support. Such finite
splitting and domination use CLD7.2.9 and the compact partition/cutoff
constructions CLD6.2.4--8. The positive measures for the split forms yield
the signed measure for theta. Uniqueness follows from density of compact
smooth scalars in compact continuous scalars (CLD6.2.6). The measure is
supported on supp(theta); in particular its total variation is finite.
The ordinary multiplier hT for continuous h acts by

  <hT,theta> = int h dmu(T,theta).

Let beta be any smooth form and omega a compact smooth test complementary
to beta wedge T. As T has even total degree, the left and right wedge
conventions agree for moving beta past T. Hence

  <beta wedge (hT),omega> = int h dmu(T,beta wedge omega).

If beta wedge T=0, the measure on the right is zero: test that zero current
against phi omega for every smooth scalar phi, then use the same measure
uniqueness. Thus

  beta wedge T=0 => beta wedge (hT)=0.                  (A)

This does not assert arbitrary continuous multiplication of every
order-zero current; it uses only coefficient measures of the positive T.

If C=gamma wedge T is positive, its continuous multiplication agrees
with the smooth-form product of hT. Indeed for each compact smooth omega
and smooth scalar phi,

  int phi dmu(C,omega)=<C,phi omega>
      =<T,phi gamma wedge omega>
      =int phi dmu(T,gamma wedge omega).

The two finite signed measures are equal by uniqueness. Consequently

  hC=gamma wedge (hT)                                  (B)

for every continuous h. This proves the compatibility actually needed
in the final BT_F assertion; it does not define a new nonsmooth wedge.

3. The graded distributional calculation.
For every current S and every smooth beta of total degree b, ordinary
current differentiation satisfies

  d'(beta wedge S)=d'beta wedge S+(-1)^b beta wedge d'S,
  d''(beta wedge S)=d''beta wedge S+(-1)^b beta wedge d''S.

These identities hold without S being closed. They follow by testing
against compact smooth omega and applying the smooth-form Leibniz rule
inside the defining differential transpose; equivalently use the entire
proof of CLD Lemma7.7.7 and its d'' analogue via J conjugation. One also
has d''d'=-d'd'' on all currents (CLD7.7.3).

Since gamma has even total degree 2r, applying both rules gives

  D(gamma wedge S)
   =Dgamma wedge S-d''gamma wedge d'S
       +d'gamma wedge d''S+gamma wedge DS.              (C)

Now T is doubly closed and C=gamma wedge T is doubly closed. Therefore

  d'gamma wedge T=0, d''gamma wedge T=0.

Differentiate the second equality by d'. Since d'T=0 and d''gamma has
odd total degree 2r+1, obtain Dgamma wedge T=0.
For arbitrary continuous h put S=hT. Apply (A) to these three smooth
forms to get

  d'gamma wedge S=d''gamma wedge S=Dgamma wedge S=0.

Differentiate the first identity by d'' and the second by d'. The forms
d'gamma,d''gamma have odd total degree, so the resulting identities are

  0=-Dgamma wedge S-d'gamma wedge d''S,
  0= Dgamma wedge S-d''gamma wedge d'S.

They give d'gamma wedge d''S=d''gamma wedge d'S=0. Insert into (C):

  D(gamma wedge (hT))=gamma wedge D(hT).                (D)

All factors differentiated on the form side are smooth. No nonsmooth
derivative of h has been multiplied by another nonsmooth coefficient,
and S=hT has not been assumed closed or positive.

4. Proof of the exact criterion on EVERY ordinary open.
Necessity is part of the BT_F definition. For sufficiency, assume C is
positive and doubly closed. Fix arbitrary ordinary U and u,v in F(U)
with D[u]=D[v]. For h=u-v the BT_F hypothesis on T gives D(hT|U)=0.
Identity (B) identifies hC with gamma wedge(hT) on U, and (D) then gives

  D(hC|U)=gamma|U wedge D(hT|U)=0.

This proves BT_F for C, with all positivity/closedness clauses present.
If desired, closedness can be stated exactly as

  d'gamma wedge T=d''gamma wedge T=0.

That equivalence follows from closedness of T and the first-order rules.
Thus smooth nonclosed form factors which annihilate the initial current's
differentials are covered; it is not necessary that the ambient form
itself be closed.

5. A precise fixed-chart local uniform limit consequence.
Let gamma_i be a NET of smooth (r,r) forms on an ordinary neighborhood,
each gamma_i wedge T positive and doubly closed. Suppose gamma_i converges
to a SMOOTH gamma, uniformly in coefficients on each compact test
support in a FIXED finite family of common tropical charts. This last
premise fixes the meaning of coefficient convergence and rules out an
unproved comparison across changing moments. Then gamma wedge T is
positive doubly closed and has BT_F.

For completeness this follows directly on tests, without a varying-T
multiplier continuity theorem. Express a compact smooth test omega by a
finite partition in the common charts. Expand gamma_i-gamma into the
finite exterior monomial basis there. For each monomial times omega the
positive T gives the finite signed measure of Section2. The uniformly
convergent smooth scalar coefficients therefore make

  <(gamma_i-gamma) wedge T,omega> ->0.

The same argument applies with omega replaced by d'omega,d''omega, so
weak differential continuity gives double closedness of gamma wedge T.
Positivity follows by passing to the limit on each compact positive test;
symmetry also passes on every test. For an admissible h=u-v and a compact
test omega, replace omega by Domega and multiply the scalar coefficients
by the bounded continuous h. Section2's measure estimate gives

  <D((gamma_i-gamma) wedge(hT)),omega> ->0.

Every D(gamma_i wedge(hT)) is zero by the exact criterion and (B), so
the limit is zero. That proves BT_F. The explicit smooth limit assumption
keeps C an ordinary smooth-factor current; no general nonsmooth form
wedge or unrestricted current-topology regularization is claimed.
