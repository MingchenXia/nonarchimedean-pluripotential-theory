## Retained field and exact finite Picard theorem

Let K=C((s)), |s|=exp(-1/8), t=s^8. Every finite product of closed discs, circles, and closed annuli with positive radii in |K*| has analytic Picard group zero. In particular Pic(P_m)=0 for every integer m>=1, where P_m has |s|^m<=|z_j|<=|s|^(-m), j=1,2. No arbitrary-extension assertion is needed here.

The exact external input is Gruson, *Fibres vectoriels sur un polydisque ultrametrique*, Ann. Sci. ENS (4) 1 (1968), 45--89, IV.5, Theorem 1, printed pp.79--81. For a complete real-valued K, put D=Q tensor v(K*). His disc algebra T_(1,r), r in D, consists of series sum a_i X^i with v(a_i)+ir tending to infinity; C consists of Laurent series with v(a_i) tending to infinity as |i| tends to infinity. If H is a completed tensor product of these disc and circle algebras, H is topologically finite type and filtered-noetherian, satisfies (R), and SK^0(H)=SK^1(H)=0. Here SK^0 is coker(Z->K^0(H)), 1->[H]; (R) is finite homological dimension for the appropriate finitely generated graded modules. Only SK^0 is applied. The full immediate proof passes to gr(H)=gr(K)[X_1,...,X_p,Y_1^+-1,...,Y_q^+-1]. Graded semisimplicity, polynomial graded K-theory and homogeneous localization give stable graded freeness; the filtered lifting results IV.2 give SK^0(H)=0. The separate SK^1/excellence argument is not used. Those deeper K-theory inputs remain the published theorem's dependencies, not newly proved assertions here. The authentic local full text/PDF and the complete LF1510--1690 source unit were checked. No wide annulus is inserted into H.

By coherent-affinoid correspondence a line bundle on a pure disc/circle product is a rank-one finite projective H-module P. SK^0=0 gives [P]=n[H]; rank gives n=1. Equality in the group completion of the direct-sum monoid means P+Q is isomorphic to H+Q for some finite projective Q. Its rank is locally constant; determinants on the rank components give P tensor det Q = det Q, hence P=H. Radii in |K*| are rescaled to unit factors. This proves the induction base.

The coherent foundation is Van der Put, *Rigid analytic spaces*, GAU 3 (1975--76), no.2, expose J7, 2.14--2.16.3: for a Tate algebra A, a finite A-module N gives the coherent sheaf U->O(U) tensor_A N; coherence can be checked on an affinoid G-cover; H^0 is N and H^i=0 for i>0. Its entire immediate argument is descent along A->B=product O(U_i), with B completed-tensor_A B=product O(U_i intersect U_j). Finite B-modules with compatible descent data descend, and the associated Hom equalizer is exact. The printed page abbreviates the descent condition; it must include its cocycle. Our transition map and its inverse satisfy it. The page explicitly cites Kiehl for the deeper descent theorem. The actual PDF one-based page 10, printed J7-09, was visually read in this turn; this is distinct from any historic native pixel claim.

Induct on the number k of wide factors. Let Z=A(r,R) times Y, 0<r<R in |K*|, with k wide factors. Both C(r) times Y and D(R) times Y have k-1. For an arbitrary line bundle L on Z, trivialize L on C(r) times Y. Glue it to the trivial line bundle on D(r) times Y using this frame. Indeed D(r) times Y and A(r,R) times Y are rational domains covering D(R) times Y and meet exactly in C(r) times Y. If |a|=r, the inequalities are |z/a|<=1 and |a/z|<=1. The denominator a is a unit, and (a,z) is the unit ideal, so both domains are legitimate rational localizations. Products with Y preserve this cover. Coherent G-descent produces an invertible sheaf on D(R) times Y. It is trivial by induction, hence L is trivial. This is sheaf descent on a finite G-cover, not an ordinary-open psh argument. It proves every finite product and every P_m over the retained K. Neither pro-Picard invariance nor topological contractibility is used.

We next prove Pic(T)=0, including compatibility. For a unit f=sum b_beta z^beta on P_m put a=1/8. Its Gauss log norm, on log radii y in [-ma,ma]^2, is max_beta(log|b_beta|+beta dot y). On all R^2 a distinct exponent would dominate on a ray, so a global unit of T is exactly a scalar monomial.

For a line bundle choose frames e_m on P_m by the finite theorem. Normalize successive frames by scalar monomials so e_(m+1)|P_m=(1+g_m)e_m. For each fixed j, p_j=product_(m=j)^infinity(1+g_m)|P_j converges to a unit: its first factor is a unit, later errors have norm at most |s|^(m-j), and inverse tails converge too. Then E_j=e_j p_j satisfies E_(j+1)|P_j=E_j. The P_j lie in the interiors of P_(j+1), so the admissible exhaustion glues these frames. This proves Pic(T)=0.
