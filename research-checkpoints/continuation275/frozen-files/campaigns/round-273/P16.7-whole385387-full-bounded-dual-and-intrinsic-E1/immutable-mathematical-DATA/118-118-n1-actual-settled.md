Retain K=C((s)), |s|=exp(-1/8). This is an unverified proof draft under the named published analytic foundations. We prove Pic(P_m,K)=0 for every integer m>=1, where P_m,K is the product of the annuli |s|^m<=|z_j|<=|s|^(-m). More generally, the same proof covers finite products of closed discs, circles and closed annuli with positive radii in |K*|. No arbitrary-extension quantifier is claimed.

The precise external input is Gruson, Fibres vectoriels sur un polydisque ultrametrique, Ann. Sci. ENS (4) 1 (1968), 45–89, IV.5 Theorem 1. For a complete real-valued field, its H is a completed tensor product of disc algebras T_(1,r), r in Q tensor v(K*), and unit-circle algebras C. Its conclusion includes SK^0(H)=0, where SK^0 is coker(Z->K^0(H)), with 1 mapping to [H]. The entire statement, immediate proof and applied K0 dependencies were freshly read. This theorem is applied only to pure disc/circle products, never to a wide-annulus factor.

First SK^0(H)=0 implies Pic(H)=0. By the coherent-affinoid correspondence, a line bundle corresponds to a rank-one finite projective module P. Its K0 class is n[H]; rank gives n=1. Equality in the Grothendieck group of finite projectives gives P direct-sum Q isomorphic to H direct-sum Q for some finite projective Q. Taking determinants and tensoring by det(Q)'s inverse gives P isomorphic to H. Thus no vector-bundle cancellation theorem is needed. Radii in |K*| can be rescaled to unit discs and circles.

The coherent-affinoid correspondence and finite rational-cover descent are the Tate–Kiehl foundations, in Van der Put, Rigid analytic spaces (1975–76), 2.14–2.16.3 and 3.4.5–6. The complete printed immediate descent proof was read; it cites Kiehl for the deeper affinoid theorem. These are analytic G-covers for sheaves, not ordinary open covers for psh approximation.

Induct on the number k of wide-annulus factors, allowing any number of disc/circle factors. Write Z=A(r,R) times Y, where 0<r<R belong to |K*|. Assume the assertion for fewer than k wide factors. Both D(R) times Y and C(r) times Y have k-1 wide factors. Given ANY line bundle L on Z, its restriction to C(r) times Y is trivial by induction. Choose a frame there and identify it with the trivial bundle on D(r) times Y. The rational domains A(r,R) times Y and D(r) times Y cover D(R) times Y and intersect in C(r) times Y. With |a|=r their inequalities are |a/z|<=1 and |z/a|<=1; a and z generate the unit ideal. They form a finite admissible affinoid cover. Coherent descent glues an invertible sheaf L' on D(R) times Y. By induction L' is trivial, hence so is L. This proves the induction.

Explicitly in two variables, first fill the second hole of C_1 times A_2, using C_1 times C_2 and C_1 times D_2, both pure products. Then extend L on A_1 times A_2 to D_1 times A_2 along C_1 times A_2. Finally fill the second hole using D_1 times C_2 and D_1 times D_2. The intermediate wide product is proved by filling, rather than by applying an arbitrary-base restricted-variable theorem.

Taking r=|s|^m and R=|s|^(-m) proves the needed F_K for every m. No formal-model extension, topological contractibility, pro-homotopy substitution or broader factoriality claim is used. This closes a finite base-field foundation; it does not solve P16.7.
