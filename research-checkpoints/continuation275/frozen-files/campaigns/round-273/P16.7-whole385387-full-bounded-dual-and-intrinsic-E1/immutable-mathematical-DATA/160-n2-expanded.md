# Identifying the second atom and solving finite nested branch measures

Use the base-field X, real theta, exact ordinary bounded class C_theta and genuine bounded products reconstructed in n1. The following is a new scoped bounded theorem, not an unbounded finite-energy equation theorem. For every N>0,

MA_theta(U_N)=delta_i(0)+delta_xi_N.

The original construction works also for 0<N<1. Its proof uses only r=exp(-N)<1: d_j<=0 and d_j=0 when |z_j|!=1, the support is inside the same fibre/sheet, logarithmic zero coordinates are omitted, and ordinary convex softmax provides the same approximants. No rational-radius or strictness claim is added.

We first identify the previously unspecified mass. The measure is supported on F and has restriction delta_xi_N on W by n1. On F, |z_j|=1 implies |w_j|<=1. If |w_1|<1 and |w_2|=1, choose an ordinary neighborhood on which ell_1<ell_2+b_1-b_2, b_1=0 and ell_2=log|w_2|. Then q+U_N=log|w_2|-(1/2)log|z_1z_2|-1/6 is affine in logarithms of units and has zero Hessian. The other case is symmetric. This includes w_j=0 by omission of its inactive logarithm.

The remaining locus has |z_1|=|z_2|=|w_1|=|w_2|=1. On an ordinary neighborhood all four functions are units and the truncations are inactive. Here q+U_N is a convex maximum of the four affine forms log|w_1|, log|w_1|+log|z_2|, log|w_2|, log|w_2|+log|z_1|, minus a smooth affine form and a constant. The applied support statement is CLD-v2 8.8.6, with its entire immediate proof: a bounded tropical difference-of-convex product in a common moment chart is supported on that moment's top-dimensional skeleton. Smooth convex approximants have this support by 5.8.4; local uniform product continuity preserves support, and subtraction gives the difference-of-convex case. The moment here is f=(z_1,w_1,z_2,w_2).

To check its skeleton, CLD-v2 12.2.7--10 states that tropical dimension at a point is at most the graded residual transcendence degree of the subfield generated by the moment coordinates. In the boundaryless case equality holds. The complete immediate argument in 12.2.8 cites the published local tropical dimension theorem [50, Theorem 3.4]; that deeper analytic input remains explicitly cited, not newly proved. We use only the upper bound. At the displayed unit-norm locus, graded reductions satisfy w_j=z_j-1 and all coordinates have degree one. If the two reductions z_1,z_2 are dependent over the residue field C, the generated graded field has transcendence degree at most one over graded K, so the point is outside the top skeleton and the measure vanishes near it. If they are independent, for every Laurent polynomial sum a_beta z^beta, divide by a coefficient of maximal norm. Its nonzero reduction cannot vanish, so its norm equals max|a_beta|. This characterizes exactly the original unit Gauss point i(0), as also checked directly in CLD 12.1.5. Thus the complement of W contributes only at i(0). Total mass two and branch mass one prove the asserted identity. This is an actual analytic support argument, not a polyhedral inverse convention.

There is a larger finite equation family. Choose 0<N_1<...<N_k, c_i>=0 and C=sum c_i<=1, and put u_c=u0+sum c_i D_(N_i). Convexity of C_theta gives membership. Set b_i=sum_(j>i)c_j and p_i=(b_i+c_i)^2-b_i^2. Then

MA_theta(u_c)=(2-C^2)delta_i(0)+sum p_i delta_xi_(N_i).

Proof: on W, the local weight, up to an affine term and constant, is sum c_i max(-N_i,log|w_1|,log|w_2|). Between successive depths it is affine plus a multiple of max(log|w_1|,log|w_2|), whose square vanishes. At xi_(N_i), set y_j=log|w_j|+N_i. The shallower truncations are locally constant and the deeper ones are locally untruncated. The remaining weight is

b_i max(y_1,y_2)+c_i max(0,y_1,y_2)
=max(b_i y_1,b_i y_2,(b_i+c_i)y_1,(b_i+c_i)y_2).

Its gradient polygon has vertices b_i e_1,b_i e_2,(b_i+c_i)e_1,(b_i+c_i)e_2 and area ((b_i+c_i)^2-b_i^2)/2. For c_i>0 its softmax gradient is a diffeomorphism onto the polygon interior: the Hessian is the positive covariance of the slope vectors, hence positive definite; strict convexity gives injectivity, and minimizing the softmax minus any interior linear functional gives surjectivity. Change of variables gives the determinant integral equal to polygon area, and rescaling concentrates at zero. For c_i=0 the polygon has no area and the square is zero. Actual degree-one translated Gauss sections and the exterior 2 give the claimed mass p_i. Points with a zero coordinate are handled by omission. Thus the only branch atoms are these xi_(N_i), and their total mass telescopes to C^2.

Outside W but on F, if exactly one |w_j|<1, the U_(N_i) weights are affine there while the u0 weight depends, apart from an affine term, on the other z coordinate alone; hence the top product vanishes. At all four unit norms the same common moment support argument just proved applies to their convex combination. The remaining mass 2-C^2 is therefore at i(0). This proves the formula for genuine bounded products on X.

Consequently every measure

mu=(2-P)delta_i(0)+sum p_i delta_xi_(N_i), p_i>=0, P=sum p_i<=1,

has an explicit bounded solution. Put T_i=sum_(j>=i)p_j, T_(k+1)=0, c_i=sqrt(T_i)-sqrt(T_(i+1)). Then b_i=sqrt(T_(i+1)), C=sqrt(P)<=1 and the preceding formula gives exactly mu. The coefficients are recovered from its branch masses, so uniqueness modulo constants holds within this fixed finite branch family. No uniqueness across C_theta or an unconstructed E1 class follows. This includes off-original-skeleton analytic support. It is a genuinely computed Monge--Ampere equation, not a surrogate measure assignment.

For every such u_c the three energy integrals are 0, -1/6, and -1/3-S, where

S=sum_i p_i sum_j c_j min(N_i,N_j).

Indeed D_(N_j) theta=D_(N_j) A=0, all MA support is in F, u0=-1/6 there, D_(N_j)(i(0))=0 and D_(N_j)(xi_(N_i))=-min(N_i,N_j). Thus E(u_c)=-1/6-S/3. This is finite bounded energy with the fixed raw mass two. It does not prove finite dual energy over all C_theta, and it is not offered as a definition of full admissibility. The coefficient restriction C<=1 gives a branch mass <=1 only for THIS family. No universal mass cap is asserted.
