# Conditional nonalgebraization and bounded analytic Monge–Ampère theory

Unverified research draft, 8 October 2026. Original target: P16.7.

This standalone paper proves a conditional constant-meromorphic-field/nonalgebraization theorem and unconditional bounded ordinary-local CLD results on an explicit compact analytic torus. Its new branch-energy computation separates restricted finite dual energy from analytic admissibility. The finite wide-polyannulus Picard foundation and the original intrinsic all-support E¹ theorem remain unresolved. All raw analytic masses are 2 unless explicitly stated otherwise.

The five numbered notes below are the full proof sections of this paper; their references to n1–n5 are internal. External sources and exact application boundaries are given after the proofs. No historical acceptance label is a premise.

## n1: Conditional constant meromorphic field and exclusion of all separated finite-type algebraic-space algebraizations; finite wide-polyannulus Picard vanishing remains unproved.

Fix K=C((s)), |s|=exp(-1/8), t=s^8, v=-log|.|. Put T=(G_m^2)^an, λ1=(t,2), λ2=(3,t), Λ=<λ1,λ2>, x=(-log|z1|,-log|z2|). The following geometric construction is unconditional in the standard Berkovich foundational setting. The subsequent nonalgebraization conclusion is explicitly conditional on F_L below; F_L is not established in this work.

The quotient X=T/Λ exists, is good, strict, smooth, separated, boundaryless, proper, geometrically connected and purely two-dimensional. Indeed x(λ(n)z)=x(z)+n. For any open rectangle B of widths less than one, x^-1(B) has disjoint deck translates. Its quotient image is an ordinary open analytic chart. On overlaps the deck element is locally constant and the transition is multiplication by λ(n); the group identity gives the cocycle. For compact C,D only finitely many integer n lie in x(D)-x(C), hence can make λ(n)C meet D. Relatively compact neighborhoods and separation of their finitely many relevant translates separate distinct orbits. For analytic separatedness the inverse image of the diagonal is the locally finite disjoint union of translation graphs in T×_K T, not merely a statement about a topological product. Each graph is a closed immersion, and on the disjoint-sheet atlas this gives the closed diagonal. The strict compact polyannulus x^-1([0,1]^2) surjects, so X is compact. For a point choose a center in (1/8 Z)^2 within 1/16 in each coordinate, a chart rectangle of width 3/4, and a closed rectangle of width 1/2 with that center. The closed rectangle's inverse image is a strict affinoid neighborhood lying inside the chart. This proves goodness and strictness. Charts are ordinary opens of the smooth boundaryless torus. Compactness, separatedness and empty relative boundary imply properness. The construction base changes to every complete isometric L/K with the same charts and periods. Increasing polyannuli |s|^m≤|zi|≤|s|^-m have integral Laurent-series algebras over L, hence connected spectra, and exhaust T_L; their quotient is connected. Regularity and local finiteness of components give integrality from connectedness. These arguments do not use F_L.

There are τ:X→R²/Z² and a closed Gauss section i. At x the seminorm on Laurent polynomials is max_m |a_m|exp(-m·x). The graded Laurent domain proves multiplicativity, and evaluations prove continuity. Translations send the Gauss point at x to that at x+n, so τ and i descend. Since τi=id and the real torus is compact while X is Hausdorff, i has closed image.

The explicit missing foundation F_L is: for every integer m≥1, the analytic Picard group of P_m,L={|s|^m≤|zi|≤|s|^-m, i=1,2} is zero. This is the minimal finite statement used below. No conclusion for an extension L is asserted without F_L for that extension. Van der Put, Rigid analytic spaces (1975–76), 3.4.8, prints a sufficient broader assertion: a regular factorial affinoid B remains factorial after adjoining a restricted disc variable or a unit-circle variable. His 3.4.9 extends a line bundle from a wide annulus by gluing a trivial inner disc along its inner circle and then uses factoriality of the larger disc. In dimension two its base B is already a wide annulus. Gruson, Fibrés vectoriels sur un polydisque ultramétrique (1968), IV.5 Theorem 1, has H a completed tensor product of disc algebras T_(1,r), r in the divisible hull of the field's value group, and unit-circle algebras C. It establishes filtered noetherianity, condition (R), and vanishing reduced K-groups for those H. Its graded algebra is a polynomial/Laurent polynomial algebra over gr(K). Its complete printed immediate proof does not extend the theorem to an arbitrary regular factorial wide-annulus base. A nonzero-width annulus is not a unit circle. This work does not promote that theorem's scope, or Papikian's deferred-proof discussion, into F_L. A direct proof or an applicable fully checked primary theorem for F_L remains missing.

Here is a complete proof of the remaining implication F_L⇒Γ(X_L,M)=L and exclusion of every separated finite-type algebraic-space algebraization. Write a=-log|s| and P_m=P_m,L. Laurent expansions on P_m converge in its polyannulus norm. For a unit f, its Gauss log norm N_f(y)=max_β(log|b_β|+β·y), y in [-ma,ma]², and that of its inverse are convex and sum to zero. Thus N_f is affine. At an interior maximum one Laurent term supplies its integral slope α, so N_f=log|b_α|+α·y. Vertex inequalities give |b_β/b_α|exp(ma|β-α|_1)≤1. Consequently f=bz^α(1+g), with zero constant coefficient in g and ||g||_(P_j)≤|s|^(m-j) for j<m. No smallness on P_m's boundary is claimed. On all of R² the same argument forces a global unit on T_L to be exactly bz^α: another exponent would exceed the affine norm on a ray.

Assuming F_L, choose a frame e_m for a line bundle on each P_m. Normalize a tentative frame on P_(m+1) by a global scalar monomial so e_(m+1)|P_m=(1+g_m)e_m with the preceding bounds. For fixed j the products ∏_(m=j)^∞(1+g_m)|P_j converge to a unit: the m=j term is already a unit, and later errors tend to zero with norm less than one; inverse products converge too. The frames E_j=e_j∏_(m=j)^∞(1+g_m)|P_j satisfy E_(j+1)|P_j=E_j. They glue along the admissible exhaustion, proving Pic(T_L)=0. Independent finite trivializations alone would not suffice.

Pull a line bundle on X_L to T_L and choose its global frame. A section becomes θ(λz)=c_λ z^(h_λ)θ(z). The multipliers are units, hence monomials. The commuting deck actions give h_(λλ')=h_λ+h_λ' and λ^(h_λ')=(λ')^(h_λ). If h_λ1=(A,B), h_λ2=(C,D), this symmetry reads t^C2^D=3^At^B. Valuation gives C=B, and unique prime factorization in Q gives A=D=0. Thus h_λ1=(0,k), h_λ2=(k,0). If k≠0 choose n=(1,sign k), λ=λ(n), h=(kn2,kn1). Then h≠0 and Q=v(λ^h)=2|k|>0. For a nonzero Laurent coefficient a_m of θ, coefficient comparison yields a_(m+jh)=a_m c_λ^j λ^(-jm-hj(j+1)/2). Its valuation tends to -∞ because its quadratic term is -Qj(j+1)/2. This contradicts coefficient decay on the fixed unit polyannulus as |m+jh|→∞. If k=0, any nonzero coefficient satisfies λ_i^m=c_i; two such exponents have λ_i^(m-m')=1. Their valuations force m=m'. The section is a scalar monomial and has no zeros. It trivializes its line bundle downstairs; in particular X_L has no nonzero effective analytic Cartier divisor.

For arbitrary nonzero meromorphic F on X_L, its pullback to the smooth torus has an effective Cartier pole divisor P. Smooth affinoid rings are regular; locally their height-one divisors are Cartier. These local pole ideals glue. Pic(T_L)=0 trivializes O(-P); its inclusion in O gives a global analytic θ0 with divisor P. Put θ1=Fθ0. There are no height-one poles, so normality, via the intersection of height-one valuation rings on each normal affinoid, makes θ1 analytic. The pair has no common height-one divisor: at a pole the adjusted numerator has order zero, and elsewhere θ0 has order zero. Invariance of F implies P is invariant, so θ0(λz)/θ0(z) has divisor zero; normality of it and its inverse makes it an analytic unit. Both θi have that common multiplier. The preceding recurrence forces them to be scalar multiples of the same monomial. Hence F∈L. This treats every meromorphic function, not selected characters.

Suppose Y is a separated finite-type L-algebraic space with Y^an≅X_L. The analytification preservation/reflection of smoothness is Conrad–Temkin, Non-archimedean analytification of algebraic spaces (author PDF dated 24 February 2009), Theorem 2.3.1; its full immediate proof was freshly read. Completed local rings at corresponding closed/rigid points agree by its Example 2.3.2, which also preserves residue fields. Thus Y is smooth of dimension two. Connectedness follows since a nontrivial clopen algebraic decomposition analytifies to one; every nonempty finite-type piece has a closed point and nonempty analytification. The finitely many irreducible components of regular Y are disjoint and open, hence Y is integral.

All Stacks 0ENA hypotheses hold here: regular noetherian charts have only minimal weakly associated primes; finite-type noetherian étale charts satisfy 0BB1's finite-component conditions; separated algebraic spaces are decent, so generic points are represented by monomorphisms from fields. By 086U the schematic locus is dense and contains the generic point. Tags 0ENA and 0END identify rational functions with global algebraic meromorphic functions and the generic field; on a dense affine schematic open, Noether normalization gives trdeg_L L(Y)=2.

The analytic passage requires an analytic input in addition to these algebraic tags. For a finite-type scheme the stalk map O_(Y,y_al)→O_(Y^an,y) is flat (indeed regular), as stated in Ducros, Families of Berkovich spaces, 2.1.4, citing Berkovich 1993, 2.6.2 and Ducros 2009, 3.3. It is a local flat map and hence faithfully flat. A local algebraic fraction with nonzerodivisor denominator therefore becomes an analytic meromorphic fraction. Here is the fraction-ring descent calculation wherever finite étale affinoid charts are used: for reduced noetherian affinoid A and finite faithfully flat étale B, localization at nonzerodivisors of A gives a product of fields, and B becomes a product of finite separable extensions. Hence Q(B)=Q(A)⊗_A B, and faithful-flat descent gives the equalizer Q(A)→Q(B)⇉Q(B⊗_A B). This calculation alone is not a general analytic meromorphic descent theorem. For the present algebraic space no such additional theorem is needed: on regular Y the Cartier pole divisor of a rational function is defined on algebraic étale charts, and the corresponding invertible sheaf and two global regular sections descend algebraically. Invertible sheaves and sections analytify (Conrad–Temkin, §2.3). The denominator remains a nonzerodivisor on scheme charts by flatness; this property descends through faithfully flat analytic étale charts. Their ratio is consequently a global analytic meromorphic function. For injectivity, take g/h≠0 on a dense affine schematic open and choose a closed point outside V(gh). Its finite residue extension is complete with the extended norm, and the corresponding analytic point has nonzero value g/h. The map L(Y)→Γ(X_L,M)=L fixes L, contradicting transcendence degree two. This argument uses algebraic divisor descent, not a GAGA assertion about the group law.

The conditional theorem excludes schemes and algebraic spaces, without assuming projectivity or algebraicity of the group law. Its condition F_L remains unresolved; no unconditional nonalgebraization certificate is issued. ## n2: Repaired middle-positive bounded CLD operator, raw mass 2, bounded comparison and actual off-canonical-skeleton mass-one atoms; no unbounded E¹ extension.

On the unconditional good strict proper smooth analytic surface X and the Gauss section i of n1, there is a bounded locally generated CLD theory. This note uses only the unconditional geometry in n1, not its unresolved Picard hypothesis. It proves an ordinary-local mixed operator and an off-canonical-skeleton atom. It does not identify its class with classical curve-test psh, model-semipositive PL, weak-smooth psh, or a full intrinsic psh class.

Write x_j=-log|z_j| and q=(x_1²+x_2²)/2 on a deck sheet. Since q(x+n)-q(x)=n·x+|n|²/2 is a smooth function with d'd''=0, θ=d'd''q descends to X. Let C_θ consist of continuous real u on X for which q+u is, on ordinary open neighborhoods, a locally uniform limit of ordinary CLD smooth functions with middle-positive Hessian. This is a specified generated class; it is not defined by asking that the operator being constructed be positive. Every function in this class is bounded because X is compact.

Here are the required order and measure arguments, including the repair of the ambient-cone error. In a real tropical coordinate space V, a symmetric (p,p) coefficient form is a symmetric bilinear form on Λ^p V, after the standard sign convention. Middle positivity means this coefficient matrix is positive semidefinite. Strong positivity is the cone generated by ww^t with w a decomposable p-vector. For dim V=4 and p=2, every w=a∧b obeys w_12 w_34-w_13 w_24+w_14 w_23=0. The associated linear functional on symmetric coefficient matrices therefore vanishes on the span of the strong cone. It does not vanish on (e_12+e_34)(e_12+e_34)^t. Thus strong positive squares do not span all symmetric forms. The intrinsic dimension of an analytic surface does not establish the contrary assertion for its arbitrary-dimensional moment charts. No such assertion is used here.

For the middle cone, a positive Euclidean form λ has λ^p represented by p! times a positive definite identity matrix. On a fixed compact tropical support every smooth symmetric coefficient matrix B satisfies -Cλ^p≤B≤Cλ^p for a sufficiently large finite C. Choose a smooth nonnegative cutoff equal to one on that support and supported in the SAME ordinary open. With this cutoff, adding Cλ^p writes the form as a difference of two smooth compactly supported middle-positive forms. A finite moment-chart cover, localization, and a smooth partition of unity give this decomposition for every symmetric compactly supported analytic test form. It is not necessary that the two positive supports equal the original support, only that they be compact in the same ordinary open. This is the correct content of CLD 6.5.1–3, based on 1.2.6–13. Those complete statements and immediate proofs were freshly read.

Let T be a middle-positive current; symmetry is included in this term, as in CLD 7.2.1. For a positive test form η the functional φ↦〈T,φη〉 on smooth scalar functions is positive. A cutoff plateau bounds it by the supremum norm on each compact set, so density and the Riesz theorem extend it to a Radon measure. For a symmetric β=η_+-η_-, the difference of these measures is a signed measure μ_(T,β). Equality on smooth scalars proves that this definition is independent of the decomposition and supported in supp β. Antisymmetric test forms pair to zero by symmetry. Therefore continuous multiplication bT is well-defined on all test forms. Moreover

|〈bT,β〉|≤sup_(supp η_+ ∪ supp η_-) |b| ·〈T,η_++η_-〉.

If positive T_j→T weakly and continuous b_j→b locally uniformly, then b_jT_j→bT. To prove this for a fixed β, choose smooth g uniformly ε-close to b on the compact union of the positive supports and split b_jT_j-bT=(b_j-g)T_j+g(T_j-T)+(g-b)T. The displayed bound handles the first and last terms, since 〈T_j,η_++η_-〉 is bounded; the middle term tends to zero. Let ε↓0. This proves the multiplication continuity, rather than assuming it from weak convergence. The scalar-measure and coefficient-measure arguments are CLD 7.2.8 and 7.2.11–12; the full immediate proofs were freshly read. The printed strong domination in 7.2.9 is not a premise.

For closed symmetric T and smooth g, the Leibniz rule gives d'd''(gT)=d'd''g∧T. Apply it using complementary test degrees; d'T=d''T=0 eliminates the cross terms. For continuous g, the left side is defined by the just-constructed order-zero product. It is not asserted that an arbitrary continuous multiplier preserves closedness. In particular, smooth changes of local potential with d'd''g=0 leave the recursively defined products unchanged.

On a sheet put f=q+u and g=q+v. Define

α_u=θ+d'd''[u],  B(u,v)=d'd''(f d'd''[g]).

Local smooth positive approximants f_j,g_j first give d'd''g_j→d'd''g. The multiplication continuity gives f_j d'd''g_j→f d'd''g, hence their differentiated products converge. The smooth products are symmetric and positive; consequently the limit is symmetric and positive and independent of both approximating sequences. It is closed and symmetric as a top-degree current. The smooth-null change rule glues B on ordinary overlaps. CLD 8.5.1, 8.5.3, 8.5.5, 8.7, 8.8.1 and 8.8.4–8 provide the same recursive calculus; their complete applied statements and immediate proofs were read. By CLD 15.7.1–2 the canonical calibrated tropical level equals the dimension, two, on this topologically separated pure analytic space. A positive top-degree current is therefore a genuine Radon measure on X, not merely a measure after tropical pushforward. This last assertion uses the freshly read scalar-measure proof and the freshly read immediate proof of 15.7.1, with deeper canonical integration foundations cited below.

The identity torus moment chart has saturated tangent lattice Z² and analytic degree one. There θ²=2 i_*dx, where dx is probability Lebesgue measure on R²/Z². Thus the RAW reference mass is 2. The factor 2 is the CLD exterior-product factor 2!, not a probability normalization. Canonical integration uses the full generic multiplicities defined by CLD 14.3; no blanket replacement of generic Artinian lengths by one is made. Only the smooth identity torus or a translated smooth identity torus is used for the explicit computations, where the degree is actually one. All algebraic sheaf and analytic integration foundations used here are standard CLD/Berkovich foundations; their full recursive construction has not been independently rebuilt.

Globally B(u,v)=θ∧α_v+d'd''(uα_v) and θ∧α_v=θ²+d'd''(vθ). Pairing against the constant 1 on compact boundaryless X makes both differentiated terms zero. Hence B(u,v)(X)=2. Put MA_θ(u)=B(u,u). The same multiplication argument proves weak continuity under uniform convergence inside C_θ. For decreasing sequences whose limit is still continuous, Dini's theorem on compact sets gives this continuity. No continuity for an unbounded decreasing limit is claimed.

Smooth convex nondecreasing regularizations of max, applied to f and g, preserve positive Hessians by the chain rule. Passing to limits proves max(u,v)∈C_θ. Ordinary locality of the current construction yields comparison: for w=max(u,v-ε), locality on the two strict regions and equal total mass give

MA_θ(v){u<v-ε}≤2-MA_θ(u){u>v-ε}=MA_θ(u){u≤v-ε}.

Let ε↓0; both relevant families increase to {u<v}. Thus MA_θ(v){u<v}≤MA_θ(u){u<v}. This is bounded comparison only; it is not a proof of full-E¹ uniqueness or comparison for singular potentials.

The following branch construction supplies actual analytic mass beyond the canonical Gauss section. Let G(y)=y²/2-y/2+1/12 on [0,1], extended periodically, and u_0=-G(x_1)-G(x_2). Its distributional second derivative is G''=dy-δ_0: the slope drops by one at the identified endpoint, and the ordinary second derivative elsewhere is one. Periodic nonnegative convolution of -G therefore gives smooth functions h_ε with 1+h_ε''≥0, tending uniformly to -G. The diagonal identity-chart Hessians of q+h_ε(x_1)+h_ε(x_2) have exterior square 2 i_*((dy+h_ε'')⊗(dy+h_ε'')). The Gauss section is continuous, and these factors tend weakly to δ_0. The bounded operator continuity just proved gives u_0∈C_θ and MA_θ(u_0)=2δ_(i(0)). Its range is [-1/6,1/12]. On a sheet V={|x_j|<1/4}, for any integer N≥1 put r_N=|t|^N=e^-N,

b_j=log max(1,|z_j|), ℓ_(j,N)=log max(r_N,|z_j-1|), d_(j,N)=ℓ_(j,N)-b_j,
D_N=max(d_(1,N),d_(2,N)).

Each d_j≤0, and d_j=0 whenever |z_j|≠1. Consequently D_N is nonzero only in the compact fiber F=τ^-1(0) contained in V. Extend D_N by zero outside V. Its negative locus is W={|z_j-1|<1 for both j}; this lies in F and is an ordinary open subset of X. The extension is continuous: the compact fiber is contained in V, and near every point with at least one |z_j|≠1 the extension is identically zero. Put U_N=u_0+D_N. On V its local weight is

q+U_N=max(ℓ_(1,N)+b_2, b_1+ℓ_(2,N))-(1/2)log|z_1z_2|-1/6.

The last logarithm is smooth pluriharmonic. Every truncated log is locally constant near a zero; elsewhere it is a convex maximum of logarithms of invertible analytic functions. Ordinary smooth convex approximations exist on these moment charts. Therefore the displayed weight is a locally uniform smooth-positive limit, so U_N∈C_θ. This proof uses ordinary locality, including the loci z_j-1=0; it does not presume those functions invertible everywhere.

Inside W write w_j=z_j-1. The weight becomes H_N=log max(r_N,|w_1|,|w_2|)-1/6. Let ξ_N be the Gauss seminorm of the closed w-polydisc with both radii r_N. It is an actual point of W. There |z_j|=1 and |z_j-1|=r_N<1, whereas at i(0) one has |z_j-1|=1. Hence ξ_N is off i(R²/Z²).

Near ξ_N the w_j are invertible and the identity translated torus has saturated lattice Z² and degree one. In logarithmic coordinates centered at radius r_N, a convex regularization of max(0,y_1,y_2) is ε log(1+exp(y_1/ε)+exp(y_2/ε)). Its gradient maps R² diffeomorphically onto the interior of the simplex with vertices 0,e_1,e_2. The change-of-variables formula therefore gives total Hessian-determinant integral equal to that simplex's area, 1/2. Rescaling y by ε shows concentration at y=0. The CLD exterior square multiplies this by 2!, giving mass one. The smooth measures are carried by the translated-torus Gauss section; continuity of that section converts this to convergence against every continuous analytic test function, and the bounded operator continuity identifies the limiting measure. At other points with w_1,w_2 invertible the convex weight is locally affine or depends on a single independent coordinate, so its exterior Hessian square vanishes. At w_j=0 omit that inactive truncated logarithm on a neighborhood; the remaining weight again has rank at most one. It follows that

MA_θ(U_N)|W=δ_(ξ_N),   MA_θ(U_N)(X)=2.

The residual mass one outside W is not identified here. In particular this is an exact analytic atom, not an inference from tropical pushforward alone. The formula uses CLD 5.8.1–6, 12.1.2–5, 14.3.1–5, 14.5.2–3, 8.2.1–3 and the bounded calculus above; their full immediately applied passages were freshly read. ## n3: Exact separable skeletal equations and circle envelopes/energy; all mass-two measures have finite restricted dual energy, which does not imply full admissibility.

There is a complete separable, skeletal, bounded equation and envelope theory on (X,θ) of n1–n2. This is a restricted theorem, not an intrinsic all-support E¹ theorem. Throughout, dy is probability Lebesgue measure on C=R/Z, while analytic Monge–Ampère measures have raw mass 2.

Let H={h∈C(C):ρ_h=dy+h''≥0 distributionally}. Every ρ_h has mass one. For G(y)=y²/2-y/2+1/12 extended periodically, G has mean zero and G''=dy-δ_0. Given any probability ν on C, h_ν=-G*ν satisfies ρ_(h_ν)=ν. Conversely h-h_(ρ_h) has zero distributional second derivative and is periodic, hence constant. Thus H/R is exactly the circle probability measures. Since G lies in [-1/24,1/12] and is 1/2-Lipschitz, its mean-zero solutions lie in [-1/12,1/24], are 1/2-Lipschitz, and have oscillation at most 1/8. The normalized set {h∈H:sup h=0} is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous, hence compact by Arzelà–Ascoli; positivity is closed under uniform convergence by distributional testing. These are explicit compactness replacements only in the circle directions.

Put S_θ={h_1∘τ_1+h_2∘τ_2:h_j∈H}. Convolution by nonnegative periodic smooth kernels gives uniform approximants with 1+h_j''≥0; diagonal local Hessians prove S_θ⊂C_θ. For u=h_1(x_1)+h_2(x_2), v=k_1(x_1)+k_2(x_2), the smooth identity-torus computation and n2's uniform continuity give

B(u,v)=i_*(ρ_(h_1)⊗ρ_(k_2)+ρ_(k_1)⊗ρ_(h_2)),
MA_θ(u)=2i_*(ρ_(h_1)⊗ρ_(h_2)).

Weak convergence of the product probabilities follows by density of finite sums of products of circle continuous functions on C². Continuity of the actual Gauss section i then gives convergence against analytic continuous test functions. Thus the equality is one of measures on X. It is not only a pushforward formula.

Every measure 2i_*(ν_1⊗ν_2), with ν_j arbitrary circle probabilities, is therefore solved by u=h_(ν_1)∘τ_1+h_(ν_2)∘τ_2. Marginal recovery and the preceding circle uniqueness prove uniqueness modulo constants within S_θ. This theorem makes no uniqueness assertion against an arbitrary member of C_θ or any proposed larger psh class.

All circle potentials are Lipschitz. Their energy

e(h)=∫_C h dy-(1/2)∫_C |h'|²dy

is finite. Distributional integration by parts is legitimate: convolution gives uniform convergence of h and L² convergence of h', while the positive measures ρ_h converge weakly. Hence ∫h dρ_h=∫hdy-∫|h'|²dy. For h,k∈H one obtains the exact identity

e(k)-e(h)=∫(k-h)dρ_h-(1/2)∫|k'-h'|²dy.

In particular e is concave, strictly concave modulo constants, and its derivative along every admissible circle segment is integration against ρ_h. Define E_sep(u)=2(e(h_1)+e(h_2)); moving a constant between the two factors does not change this expression. It has the required additive covariance E_sep(u+c)=E_sep(u)+2c. For the bounded CLD mixed energy

E_θ(u)=(1/3)∫_X u(θ²+θ∧α_u+MA_θ(u)),

the measure formulas above show E_θ=E_sep on S_θ. Indeed θ∧α_u=i_*(ρ_(h_1)⊗dy+dy⊗ρ_(h_2)); after summing the three terms and dividing by three, each factor contributes ∫hdy+∫h dρ_h=2e(h). This proves the normalization directly. It does not establish differentiability of E_θ in all analytic directions.

For ANY positive Radon μ on X of mass 2 define the restricted dual energy

E*_sep(μ)=sup_(u∈S_θ)(E_sep(u)-∫u dμ).

It is finite, between 0 and 1/2. To see this, normalize each h_j by sup h_j=0. Then -1/4≤u≤0 and E_sep(u)≤0, giving the upper bound; u=0 gives the lower bound. Let ν_j=(1/2)(τ_j)_*μ. The functional equals 2Σ_j(e(h_j)-∫h_j dν_j). The exact difference identity proves that its unique maximizing factors modulo constants satisfy ρ_(h_j)=ν_j. Their analytic Monge–Ampère measure is the product measure 2i_*(ν_1⊗ν_2), regardless of the original μ's analytic support or correlation. Consequently this finite dual functional does not certify full finite energy or solve arbitrary support.

For example let y be the K-rational analytic point (z_1,z_2)=(1,1) in the chosen quotient sheet, and μ=2δ_y. Its circle marginals are δ_0. The maximizer is u_0=-G(x_1)-G(x_2), whose derivative energy satisfies ∫_C|G'|²dy=1/12. Thus E_sep(u_0)=-1/6, u_0(y)=-1/6, and E*_sep(2δ_y)=1/6. But MA_θ(u_0)=2δ_(i(0)), and i(0)≠y since |z_j-1| is respectively 1 and 0. This proves failure of this restricted inverse problem for a measure of finite RESTRICTED dual energy. It is not a counterexample to the literal P16.7 finite-energy equation.

For completeness the circle envelope has the full obstacle properties in its exact scope. For a∈C(C), let P(a) be the pointwise supremum of all h∈H with h≤a. Constants give a nonempty family.
