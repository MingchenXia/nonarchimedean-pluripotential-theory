# A restricted intrinsic unbounded radial energy theory

Use the analytic X, theta and bounded calculus of n1 and the genuine finite-branch products of n2. This is a complete new scoped construction on the actual analytic space, with a deliberately restricted radial domain. It is not the full all-direction psh/E1 class, and its measure range is not the original admissibility criterion. Fix a discrete sequence 0<N_1<N_2<... tending to infinity. Let y=(1,1), o=i(0). All radii are real analytic Gauss radii; no strictness assertion at these radii is required.

Define the continuous extended depth R:X->[0,infinity] by R=-log max(|w_1|,|w_2|) on W and R=0 on its complement. At the boundary of W the maximum norm tends to one; near the boundary of the sheet W is absent. Its only infinite value is at y. Every D_N=-min(N,R). For c_i>=0 with C=sum c_i<=1, define

T(t)=sum_(N_i>=t)c_i, H(r)=integral_0^r T(t)dt,
u=u0-H(R)+b, b in R,

with the canonical limiting value at y, possibly minus infinity. Denote this domain by Gamma. H is increasing, concave, 1-Lipschitz on finite intervals, H(0)=0, and T decreases to zero. The partial sums u_m=u0+sum_(i<=m)c_iD_(N_i)+b decrease to u. On each compact subset of X\{y}, R is bounded, and the omitted terms are bounded by sup R times sum_(i>m)c_i. Thus convergence is locally uniform there. The common moment presentations from n1--n2, with inactive zero coordinates omitted, show q+u is locally uniformly approximable by ordinary smooth-positive weights there. Hence the local bounded operator is already defined on X\{y}; no operator is assigned merely to an unsupported global limit.

Put C_i=sum_(j>=i)c_j. By n2 and local uniform continuity, the actual local top measure has atoms p_i=C_i^2-C_(i+1)^2 at xi_(N_i) and mass 2-C^2 at o. Away from those points at finite depth the same local moments give zero top measure. Since C_i->0, sum p_i=C^2. Extending the local measure by zero at y gives a positive analytic Radon measure of FULL raw mass two:

MA(u)=(2-C^2)delta_o+sum_i(C_i^2-C_(i+1)^2)delta_xi_(N_i).

This agrees with the bounded operator whenever u is bounded: H(infinity)<infinity is equivalent to sum c_i N_i<infinity, and then the partial sums converge uniformly on all X. If u is unbounded, H(infinity)=infinity and its singular locus is exactly {y}. The canonical nonpluripolar recipe is directly verified. The continuous theta-positive truncation u^(M)=u0+b-min(H(R),M) is locally max(u,u0+b-M). If H(infinity)<=M it is already the bounded u. Otherwise it is constant relative to u0 near y. It belongs to C_theta: in the latter case clipped finite sums converge locally uniformly off y in the common moment charts, are eventually exactly constant relative to u0 on a neighborhood of y, and convex max regularization gives ordinary approximants. On {H(R)<M}, bounded locality gives exactly the constructed local product. The extra clamp atom is excluded by this strict set. As M increases the restricted products exhaust the local measure off y; omitted branch tail tends to zero because it is bounded by T(r)^2 at depth r and T(r)->0. Their mass therefore tends to two. The operator is obtained from genuine bounded local products by the full-mass nonpluripolar truncation recipe, not an inverse measure declaration. Ordinary truncations max(u,-j) yield the same recipe: for all sufficiently large j, only W is truncated, where u0=-1/6, and these are exactly the same clamps with M=j+b-1/6.

Mixed products are constructed locally as well. For u,v with tails T,S on this fixed grid, their local bounded mixed product on X\{y}, extended by zero at y, is

B(u,v)=(2-C_u C_v)delta_o
        +sum_i(C_i^u C_i^v-C_(i+1)^u C_(i+1)^v)delta_xi_(N_i).

Polarize n2's finite formula using genuine bounded products and then pass locally uniformly off y. The displayed branch masses are nonnegative because both tails decrease. They sum to C_u C_v; both tails tend to zero. Thus this mixed product is positive, symmetric, local and has mass two. Truncating each potential checks mixed full mass by the same tail argument. The theta-mixed measure remains theta wedge A and theta^2 retains its raw mass-two normalization. No full-class unbounded wedge theorem is invoked.

For b=0 the three energy integrals are 0, -1/6, and -1/3-integral_0^infinity T(t)^3dt. The first two measures are supported away from the singular point, and D_N annihilates theta and A. For the top measure Tonelli gives

sum_i p_i H(N_i)
=integral_0^infinity T(t) [sum_(N_i>=t)p_i]dt
=integral_0^infinity T(t)^3dt.

Negative parts are handled by this nonnegative identity; there is no infinity cancellation. Define Gamma^1 by finiteness of this tail integral. This is exactly finiteness of the genuine three-term energy in Gamma, and

E(u)=-1/6-(1/3)integral T^3+2b.

Canonical finite sums have tails increasing to T, so monotone convergence proves their energies converge to E(u); their actual MA measures converge weakly with all tail mass accounted for. A concrete unbounded example is N_i=i and c_i=1/(i(i+1)). Then C_i=1/i, C=1, H(infinity)=sum_i1/(i+1)=infinity, whereas integral T^3=sum_i1/i^3<infinity by the integral test. Thus u(y)=-infinity and u has finite energy in this intrinsic domain. Its MA is delta_o+sum_i(1/i^2-1/(i+1)^2)delta_xi_i, of mass two and with no rigid atom. No exact zeta value is needed.

Every segment first variation inside Gamma^1 holds. On a segment with tails T_t=(1-t)T+tS, differentiation is justified by (T+S)^3<=4(T^3+S^3) and Holder. The derivative is -integral(S-T)T_t^2, plus twice the derivative of b. The potential difference on X\{y} is -integral_0^R(S-T), plus the constant difference. Signed Fubini is justified by Holder in L3 and L(3/2), and integration against MA(u_t) gives exactly the derivative. The measure has no atom at y, so no undefined difference of two minus infinities is integrated. Hence dE(u_t)/dt=integral(v-u)MA(u_t) for every direction within Gamma^1. The second derivative is -2integral(S-T)^2T_t<=0. Thus E is concave. If v<=u pointwise off y, segment variation is nonpositive and E(v)<=E(u). These are domain-specific variational statements.

Comparison also holds within Gamma. Take canonical finite sums u_m,v_m on the same fixed grid. Their measures converge in total variation: each fixed atomic mass converges, the mass at o converges, and the branch tail after depth N_k is uniformly at most (C_(k+1))^2. Their potentials converge locally uniformly off y. Choose epsilon>0 avoiding the countably many values v(x)-u(x) at x=o and x=xi_(N_i). In the bounded comparison inequality for u_m,v_m, strict-set indicators therefore stabilize at each fixed atom. The total-variation convergence and the uniform branch-tail bound pass the inequality to MA(v){u<v-epsilon}<=MA(u){u<=v-epsilon}. Both sides give zero mass to y. Let epsilon decrease to zero along noncritical values; monotone convergence gives MA(v){u<v}<=MA(u){u<v}. This does not compare a Gamma potential with an arbitrary outside-class potential.

There is a useful separated compactness theorem within Gamma^1. All b=0 potentials have supremum 1/12, since H>=0 and a maximum of u0 lies outside F; normalize by subtracting 1/12. Fix integral T^3<=L. Monotonicity gives r T(r)^3<=L. Thus the coefficient tail at depth r is at most (L/r)^(1/3), and the MA tail is at most (L/r)^(2/3). Diagonal extraction gives convergence of every c_i. The uniform coefficient-tail estimate prevents loss of their total sum and gives uniform convergence of H on each finite interval. The limit still has sum c_i<=1 and, by Fatou, integral T^3<=L. Potentials converge uniformly on every compact subset of X\{y}. This topology is separated on Gamma: H determines every jump coefficient and the canonical value at y, while normalization determines b. It is compact, not just sequentially compact: the coefficient parameter set is a closed subset of the compact metrizable product [0,1]^N; the uniform tail estimate gives the claimed topology continuously on that set. The actual MA measures converge weakly on X by convergence of each atomic coefficient and the uniform mass-tail estimate. This is a specified separated weak replacement within Gamma, not on all analytic potentials.

Decreasing continuity holds under the energy bound. If normalized u_m in Gamma^1 decrease pointwise with integral T_m^3<=L, their H_m increase to a finite continuous 1-Lipschitz concave H on each finite interval. Slopes on each fixed grid interval converge, determining the limit coefficients. Fatou and the tail estimate show T(infinity)=0 and integral T^3<=L, so the limit is in Gamma^1. Compactness then gives local uniform convergence off y and weak convergence of the actual MA measures. Energy converges too: first variation gives E(u_m)>=E(u), so integral T_m^3<=integral T^3; Fatou supplies the opposite liminf inequality. This is decreasing continuity in the declared energy-bounded radial domain, not for arbitrary analytic psh sequences.

The equation range is explicit. Given nonnegative p_i with P=sum p_i<=1 and no rigid atom, put P_i=sum_(j>=i)p_j, c_i=sqrt(P_i)-sqrt(P_(i+1)). Then C=sqrt(P)<=1 and the genuine operator constructed above equals (2-P)delta_o+sum p_i delta_xi_(N_i). It has finite energy in Gamma exactly when

integral_0^infinity P_tail(t)^(3/2)dt<infinity,
P_tail(t)=sum_(N_i>=t)p_i.

The operator recovers C_i=sqrt(P_i), hence every c_i. Thus uniqueness modulo constants holds in Gamma^1. This is a complete equation computation for this chosen range. P<=1 describes this constructed domain's range and is NOT declared full intrinsic admissibility.

For measures in that range the dual over the entire Gamma^1 domain can also be computed. For a test tail T, nonnegative Tonelli first yields the extended-valued expression E(u)-integral u dmu=1/6+integral(P_tail T-T^3/3). When P_tail^(3/2) is integrable, Holder justifies the finite signed expression. Pointwise Young optimization at T=sqrt(P_tail), a permitted decreasing grid tail, gives the dual 1/6+(2/3)integral P_tail^(3/2). If this integral is infinite, finite grid cutoffs of the optimizing coefficients give objectives tending to infinity, so the restricted dual is infinite. Indeed their tails increase to sqrt(P_tail), and for those tails the objective is nonnegative and increases to (2/3)P_tail^(3/2) pointwise; monotone convergence applies. This restricted criterion exactly matches energies of solutions in this range. For P>1 the upper slope constraint is active: n3 gives a finite restricted dual for 2delta_xi_(N0) when the grid contains N0, although no Gamma solution exists. Full original admissibility is therefore not settled by this criterion.

This supplies an intrinsic UNBOUNDED special class, genuine mixed/full-mass products, finite energy, comparison, separated compactness and its own variations/equations. It supplies neither arbitrary analytic support nor arbitrary all-direction psh potentials, and no all-obstacle envelope theorem is claimed. The original target remains open.
