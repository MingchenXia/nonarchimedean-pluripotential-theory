# n1. Filling annular holes closes the finite base-K Picard gate

This is an unverified proof draft, not an execution certificate. Retain K=C((s)) with |s|=exp(-1/8). The result is Pic(P_m,K)=0 for every integer m>=1, where P_m,K is the product of the two closed annuli |s|^m<=|z_j|<=|s|^(-m). In fact the argument proves the following slightly larger finite statement over this same K: every finite product of closed discs, circles, and closed annuli whose positive radii belong to |K*| has zero analytic Picard group. No arbitrary-extension quantifier is needed or claimed.

The source input is precisely Gruson, *Fibres vectoriels sur un polydisque ultrametrique*, Ann. Sci. ENS (4) 1 (1968), 45--89, IV.5, Theorem 1 (printed pp. 79--81). Its H is a completed tensor product of disc algebras T_(1,r), r in Q tensor v(K*), and unit-circle algebras C. The conclusion includes SK^0(H)=0; SK^0 is the cokernel of Z->[K^0(H)] sending 1 to [H]. This theorem concerns discs and circles. It is never applied here with a wide-annulus factor. Its full immediate proof and the K^0 dependencies are recorded in the local source ledger. Only the K^0 conclusion is used, so its additional SK^1 calculation and its valued-excellence dependency are not needed for the inference below.

First, SK^0(H)=0 implies Pic(H)=0. The finite analytic line bundles on an affinoid correspond to finite projective modules of rank one. If P has rank one, its class in K^0(H) is n[H] for an integer n; evaluating rank at any prime gives n=1. Equality in the Grothendieck group of the direct-sum monoid of finite projectives means P direct-sum Q is isomorphic to H direct-sum Q for some finite projective Q. Taking top exterior powers gives P tensor det(Q) isomorphic to det(Q). Tensoring with the dual of det(Q) gives P isomorphic to H. Thus no cancellation assertion for arbitrary vector bundles is required. A circle of radius c in |K*| is carried to a unit circle by z->z/a with |a|=c. Discs are similarly rescaled to unit discs. This proves the zero-Picard assertion for products having no wide-annulus factors.

We use analytic G-sheaves for line bundles. Finite rational-domain covers support sheaf gluing; the affinoid correspondence and coherent descent are the Tate--Kiehl foundations, in the precise formulation of Van der Put, *Rigid analytic spaces* (1975--76), 2.14--2.16.3 and 3.4.5--6. The complete immediate proof of 2.16.3, including its explicit descent diagram, has been read; it cites Kiehl for the deeper affinoid descent theorem. These covers are not represented as ordinary open covers for a psh construction.

Here is the induction, which repairs the foundation without asserting arbitrary-base factoriality. Write D(R)={|z|<=R}, D(r)={|z|<=r}, A(r,R)={r<=|z|<=R}, and C(r)={|z|=r}, with 0<r<R in |K*|. Suppose all finite products with fewer than k wide-annulus factors, and any number of disc or circle factors, have zero Picard group. Let Z=A(r,R) times Y have k such factors. The product D(R) times Y has k-1; the overlap C(r) times Y has k-1 as well.

Take ANY analytic line bundle L on Z. Its restriction to C(r) times Y is trivial by induction. Choose one frame there. On D(r) times Y take the trivial bundle and identify its frame with that chosen frame on the overlap. The two analytic domains A(r,R) times Y and D(r) times Y cover D(R) times Y; they are rational domains, their intersection is exactly C(r) times Y, and they form a finite admissible affinoid cover. For example, the two inequalities |z/a|<=1 and |a/z|<=1, with |a|=r, give the disc and annular sides; the denominator z together with the nonzero constant a generates the unit ideal. Their products with Y remain rational domains. The chosen isomorphism and its inverse give descent data; the two-member cocycle condition is automatic. Ordinary sheaf gluing on this analytic G-cover produces an invertible sheaf L' on D(R) times Y. Local freeness and coherence descend by the stated affinoid theorem. By induction L' is trivial, so its restriction L is trivial. This proves the induction for every k.

For the exact two-variable case one may check the order without any induction shorthand. First prove Pic(C_1 times A_2)=0 by extending across the second hole: the overlap C_1 times C_2 and the filled space C_1 times D_2 are pure circle/disc products. Next extend an arbitrary L on A_1 times A_2 to D_1 times A_2 using that boundary result. Finally extend to D_1 times D_2 using the pure product D_1 times C_2 on the second overlap. The bidisc is covered by Gruson. In particular, the delicate intermediate D_1 times A_2 is proved by filling, not by applying his theorem to a wide-annulus base.

Taking r=|s|^m, R=|s|^(-m) establishes the exact base-field gate F_K for every m. No topological contractibility argument, formal-model extension assertion, pro-homotopy limit, or regular-factorial-base restricted-variable theorem is used. This is a complete finite Picard bridge under the stated standard affinoid foundations, not the original finite-energy equation. It makes no assertion about arbitrary radii outside |K*| or arbitrary complete extensions L/K.
