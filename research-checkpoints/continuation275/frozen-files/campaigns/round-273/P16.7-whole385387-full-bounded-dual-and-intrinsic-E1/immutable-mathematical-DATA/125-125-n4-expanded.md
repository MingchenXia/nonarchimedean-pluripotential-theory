The kernel G(y)=y^2/2-y/2+1/12 on [0,1], periodically extended, has mean zero and G''=dy-delta_0: its endpoint derivative drops by one. For every circle probability nu put h_nu=-G*nu. Then rho_h_nu=nu. Conversely h-h_rho_h has zero second derivative; as a periodic distribution it is constant. Since G has range [-1/24,1/12] and Lipschitz constant 1/2, the mean-zero potentials have range [-1/12,1/24], Lipschitz constant 1/2 and oscillation at most 1/8. Sup-zero normalization gives a uniformly bounded equicontinuous closed family, hence uniform compactness by Arzela-Ascoli. Distributional positivity passes under uniform limits. These are circle-direction statements only.

Let S_theta={h1(tau1)+h2(tau2): h_j in H}. Positive periodic convolution gives actual ordinary smooth-positive approximants; thus S_theta is contained in C_theta. For u=h1+h2 and v=k1+k2, diagonal smooth Hessians and n3's bounded continuity give the exact ANALYTIC measure equalities

B(u,v)=i_*(rho_h1 tensor rho_k2+rho_k1 tensor rho_h2),

All h are Lipschitz, so e(h)=integral h dy-(1/2) integral |h'|^2 dy is finite. Periodic convolution gives uniform convergence of h and L2 convergence of derivatives, while rho_h converges weakly. This proves integral h d rho_h=integral h dy-integral|h'|^2 and

For completeness, all circle obstacle and variation claims are also reconstructed. For continuous a:C->R let P(a)=sup{h in H:h<=a}. Constants below min a give a nonempty family. Its common Lipschitz bound, its upper bound a, and its constant lower bound give a finite continuous Lipschitz supremum. On R, y^2/2+P(a)(y) is the supremum of convex functions, so is convex. Thus P(a) belongs to H and is the largest admissible minorant. Monotonicity and addition of constants give ||P(a)-P(b)||_infinity<=||a-b||_infinity.

Put p=P(a), F(y)=y^2/2+p(y). If its curvature charged a noncontact arc, select a short interval [c,d] compactly in that arc with positive curvature in its interior. The clearance a-p has a positive minimum there. Replace F on that interval by its endpoint chord. The chord raises F somewhere; its slope lies between the outer one-sided slopes, so the result remains convex. On a fixed compact arc these slopes are bounded, and the increase is at most a constant times d-c. Choose the interval short enough to stay below the obstacle. Repeat the change on integer translates, using F(y+n)=F(y)+ny+n^2/2; the affine correction preserves the chord construction and the periodicity of F-y^2/2. This gives a strictly larger admissible minorant, contradiction. Any positive curvature on an arc can be localized on arbitrarily short such intervals. Hence rho_p is supported on {p=a} and integral(a-p)d rho_p=0.

For continuous v let p_b=P(a+bv). The energy difference identity, its reverse, and the two contact statements give

b integral v d rho_pb <= e(p_b)-e(p) <= b integral v d rho_p.

Nonexpansiveness implies p_b->p uniformly as b->0. Distributional testing and fixed mass imply rho_pb->rho_p weakly. Divide the inequalities by b, reversing them if b<0, to obtain d/db at zero e(P(a+bv))=integral v d rho_p. This supplies orthogonality and first variation in EVERY circle direction; it is not an envelope theorem for arbitrary analytic obstacles, even when the given obstacle happens to be separable.
