## 1. A genuine full-link separation obstruction

Its homogeneous nonconstant polynomials are scalar monomials. On the compact link X_0={v: v((x,y))=0}, consider the trivial semivaluation v_0 and the analytic point v_1 induced by evaluation at (1,1), followed by the trivial absolute value on k.

These are distinct Berkovich points: |x-1|(v_0)=1 whereas |x-1|(v_1)=0. Both lie in X_0 since |x|=|y|=1 there. Every nonzero homogeneous polynomial has absolute value one at both points. Therefore every homogeneous Fubini--Study function in Wu Definition 2.9 has the same value at v_0 and v_1. The same is true of all locally uniform limits, all differences of such limits, and all decreasing-limit psh functions.

Consequently the difference-of-CPSH class does not separate points of the full X_0 and cannot be dense in C^0(X_0). ## 2. Correct orientation of the tangent inequality

Concavity and the first derivative of energy on the positive cone give

\[
 E(psi)-E(phi)\leq\int(psi-phi)\,MA(phi),
\]

or, equivalently,

\[
 E(phi)-E(psi)\geq\int(phi-psi)\,MA(phi).
\]

The intermediate displayed inequality in the proof of Wu Proposition 7.3 has the opposite orientation. Its asserted dual-energy identity uses the orientation written above. Any application must reconstruct the tangent argument from the correct concavity statement and derivative, rather than treating the displayed sign as a valid estimate. This observation alone neither disproves that proposition's conclusion nor verifies the unbounded limiting passage.

## 3. Uniform comparison for bounded normalized FS data

Let R be generated by finitely many homogeneous nonconstant elements of weights beta_1,...,beta_N. Let xi and zeta be Reeb vectors such that

\[
 1-\delta\leq\frac{\langle zeta,beta_i\rangle}{\langle xi,beta_i\rangle}
 \leq1+\delta\qquad(1\leq i\leq N),\quad 0\leq\delta<1.
\]

Every nonzero weight alpha of R is a nonnegative integer combination of the beta_i: a homogeneous element is represented by a sum of homogeneous monomials of that weight. Therefore the same ratio bounds hold for every such alpha, by taking a weighted average of the generator ratios.

Write a normalized FS function as

\[
 phi(v)=\max_j\frac{-v(f_j)+\lambda_j}{\langle xi,alpha_j\rangle},
 \qquad phi(v_0)=0.
\]

The normalization implies lambda_j<=0 for every j. For v in X_0, v(f_j)>=0, so every numerator is nonpositive. Keep the same homogeneous generators and constants and replace xi by zeta in the denominators, obtaining the genuine FS function phi_zeta. For each nonpositive summand q, multiplication by a number in [1/(1+delta),1/(1-delta)] puts it between q/(1-delta) and q/(1+delta). Taking maxima yields

\[
 \frac{phi}{1-\delta}\leq phi_{zeta}\leq\frac{phi}{1+\delta}
 \quad\text{on }X_0.
\]

In particular, if -M<=phi<=0 on X_0, then

\[
 \|phi_{zeta}-phi\|_{C^0(X_0)}\leq\frac{\delta M}{1-\delta}.
\]

The bound is independent of the degrees, number and weights of the FS generators. The construction retains their common zero set, so its m-primary requirement is preserved.

A rational quotient may be singular; the smooth-projective envelope theorem cannot be invoked there without a separate geometric argument. These remain indispensable checks for any proposed use of the estimate.
