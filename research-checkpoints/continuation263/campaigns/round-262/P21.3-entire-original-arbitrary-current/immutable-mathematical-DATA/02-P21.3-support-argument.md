# Compact selection and a dual test-measure reduction for P21.3

Research draft, 3 October 2026. The universal P21.3 atom remains unresolved.
The results below are supporting lemmas, not a new Bedford–Taylor positive
range or an analytic counterexample. No priority, formal verification, or
independent-review claim is made.

## 1. Target, scope, and inputs

The target fixes a complete non-Archimedean field \(K\), possibly trivially
valued, a good separated strict boundaryless pure \(n\)-dimensional
\(K\)-analytic space \(X\), \(n\geq2\), an arbitrary **ordinary** open
\(W\subset X\), and a positive, \(d'\)- and \(d''\)-closed ordinary CLD
current \(T\) of bidegree \((p,p)\), \(0\leq p\leq n-1\). Its assertion is

\[
d'd''[U]=d'd''[V]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d'd''((U-V)T|_W)=0
\]

for every finite continuous CLD-psh pair \(U,V\) on \(W\). Here
\([f]=f\delta_X\), using the canonical integration current, including
generic component lengths. Positivity means the CLD middle cone.

This draft proves two support results.

1. Transfinite decreasing constructions **on one unchanged domain**, with
   no intermediate sheafification, have a compact upper-selection property.
   Their finite continuous outputs are locally decreasing uniform limits
   of smooth psh functions on a neighbourhood fixed before the sequence.
   Discontinuous intermediate functions cause no problem in this result.
2. For a fixed target current, annihilation of **all continuous
   zero-current functions** is equivalent to a precise weak closure
   condition on signed test measures. This is an exact dual reformulation
   of a stronger multiplier property, not a proof that the condition holds.

Neither result assumes nontrivial valuation. Neither recognizes arbitrary
continuous positive-Hessian functions as CPsh, constructs curvature-matched
approximants for a pair, or supplies a density theorem for currents.

The accepted strict real-PL theorem and its full proof and audit were read
in the supplied deliverables. Their scope is complete **nontrivially**
valued fields, actual GJR real-PL differences and their stated
fixed-neighbourhood locally uniform zero-current net closure. That theorem
is not a premise of either proof here. Its review does not certify these
new results or a changed recognition theorem. The prior elliptic-disc
surface, coarse fan, scalar-ascent, and strong-product attempts were read
and are not counted as new counterexamples.

The primary source is [CLD], arXiv:1204.6277v2, 25 July 2025. The exact
inputs needed here are:

* Definition 8.1.1: Psh is the smallest ordinary subsheaf of functions
  with values in \(\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}\) containing smooth functions
  with positive smooth Hessian and stable under decreasing sequential
  limits. The printed definition itself has no local-integrability
  clause; Lemma 8.1.12 separately treats locally integrable sections.
  The selection argument also works if local integrability is required
  at every limit, since it only uses an existing construction.
* Definition 8.1.7: CPsh is the smallest ordinary subsheaf of finite
  continuous functions containing the same smooth generators and stable
  under uniform sequential limits. Lemmas 8.1.4 and 8.1.8 describe the
  respective **sheaf** stages: local decreasing/uniform limits at
  successors and local earlier-stage membership at limits.
* Section 11.1.7: an analytic space has a basis of compact neighbourhoods;
  good spaces have affinoid neighbourhood bases. In the separated scope
  the underlying space is Hausdorff. Thus a point of an ordinary open
  has a compact neighbourhood contained in that open, whose ordinary
  interior still contains the point.
* Theorem 15.7.1 and Section 15.7.2: a topologically separated pure
  analytic space has the canonical calibrated tropical structure of its
  dimension, and restriction to an analytic domain agrees with its own
  structure. Boundaryless ordinary opens are locally differentially
  separated. No valuation or reducedness restriction is imposed.
* Proposition 6.5.2: a symmetric compactly supported smooth \((q,q)\)
  test is a difference of two middle-positive compactly supported smooth
  tests, provided its support has the stated paracompact and locally
  differentially separated neighbourhood. Its analytic support
  hypothesis holds for compact supports on an almost-good space, hence
  in the good boundaryless scope here.
* Sections 7.2.8, 7.2.11–12: a positive current paired with a complementary
  positive smooth test gives a positive Radon coefficient measure;
  locally bounded Borel functions have the ordinary order-zero product.
  We give the needed signed-measure construction below using middle
  positivity and the preceding positive-test decomposition. We do not
  use a claim that strongly positive tests span all symmetric tests.
* The ordinary current differential is dual to the test differential;
  for symmetric equal-bidegree tests the convention gives
  \(\langle d'd''(hT),\alpha\rangle
  =\langle hT,d'd''\alpha\rangle\). This is the convention used in
  Lemma 7.7.5 and Section 8.5.3. The calculus has
  \(d'^2=d''^2=0\), \(d'd''=-d''d'\), and \(Jd'd''=d'd''J\).

The compact-selection proof below is elementary and proves its own Dini
statement. The dual-closure proof uses a finite-dimensional separation
argument given in full. Proposition 8.1.11's disputed reverse inclusion
and its same-rank assertion are not imported.

## 2. Compact upper selection for upper semicontinuous sequences

**Lemma 1.** Let \(C\) be a compact topological space. Let
\(v_j:C\to\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}\) be upper semicontinuous and
decrease pointwise to \(v\). Let \(F:C\to\mathbb R\) be continuous and
assume \(v<F\) everywhere. Then some \(j\) satisfies \(v_j<F\)
everywhere on \(C\).

**Proof.** Each \(E_j=\{v_j<F\}\) is open in \(C\). They increase
with \(j\) and their union is \(C\), because of the strict pointwise
inequality and convergence. A finite subcover exists. Taking the largest
of its finitely many indices gives a single \(E_j=C\). This proof needs
neither metrizability nor continuity of the \(v_j\). \(\square\)

**Corollary 1 (upper semicontinuous Dini).** If the same sequence has a
finite continuous limit \(u\), it converges uniformly on \(C\).

**Proof.** Decrease gives \(v_j\geq u\). Apply Lemma 1 to
\(F=u+\varepsilon\) for each \(\varepsilon>0\). Eventually
\(0\leq v_j-u<\varepsilon\) on \(C\). This is precisely uniform
convergence. \(\square\)

Thus discontinuity of decreasing successor approximants does not itself
invalidate the Dini conclusion for a continuous finite output. However,
being a uniform approximant is not being a **CPsh** uniform approximant.
That distinction matters in the full sheaf construction.

## 3. A construction with one fixed domain

Fix a locally compact Hausdorff space \(O\) and a family
\(\mathcal S(O)\subset C(O,\mathbb R)\) of generators. Define classes
of functions with values in \(\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}\) by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_0(O)&=\mathcal S(O),\\
\mathcal M_{\alpha+1}(O)&=\mathcal M_\alpha(O)\cup
 \{\inf_j v_j: v_j\in\mathcal M_\alpha(O),\ v_{j+1}\leq v_j\},\\
\mathcal M_\lambda(O)&=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}\mathcal M_\beta(O)
 \quad (\lambda\text{ a nonzero limit ordinal}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Write \(\mathcal M(O)\) for their eventual union. This is a construction
in the set of functions on the fixed set \(O\); equivalently it is the
smallest class containing the generators and stable under decreasing
sequences. One may use the stages up to their stabilization: a strictly
increasing chain cannot add a new function at every stage beyond the
cardinality of this function set. In particular every member has an
ordinal rank. There is **no** gluing operation at successor stages and
**no** local earlier-rank membership at limit stages.

For the analytic generators, each \(\mathcal M_\alpha(O)\) is contained
in Psh\((O)\): the claim holds at rank zero, the Psh closure axiom
handles successors, and unions handle limit ranks. This inclusion does
not identify the fixed-domain class with the generated sheaf.

Every member is upper semicontinuous. This follows by transfinite induction:
the generators are continuous, a decreasing limit is upper
semicontinuous since \(\{\inf_jv_j<a\}=\bigcup_j\{v_j<a\}\), and
limit stages take unions of classes, not a new limit of functions.

**Theorem 1 (compact selection at arbitrary fixed-domain rank).** For
every \(v\in\mathcal M(O)\), every compact \(C\subset O\), and every
continuous \(F:C\to\mathbb R\) with \(v|_C<F\), there is a generator
\(s\in\mathcal S(O)\) such that

\[
v\leq s\text{ on all of }O,\qquad s|_C<F.
\]

**Proof.** Induct on the stage containing \(v\). At rank zero use
\(s=v\). At a successor, a member already in the previous class is
covered by induction. Otherwise it is the decreasing limit of
\(v_j\in\mathcal M_\alpha(O)\). Lemma 1 selects an index \(j\) with
\(v_j<F\) on the **same** compact \(C\). The induction hypothesis
selects \(s\in\mathcal S(O)\) satisfying \(v_j\leq s\) on all of
\(O\) and \(s<F\) on \(C\). Since \(v\leq v_j\) everywhere,
this \(s\) works. At a limit stage, \(v\) belongs on all of \(O\) to
one earlier class; apply that case. The induction never shrinks \(O\)
or \(C\). This proves the statement for every rank. \(\square\)

Notice that the limit at an intermediate node need not be continuous. The
continuous function is the strict upper obstacle \(F\) on the compact.
The selection of a single child does not assert uniform convergence to a
discontinuous intermediate limit.

**Theorem 2 (continuous output collapses to the first local stage).**
Let \(O\) be an ordinary open of the target analytic space. Take
\(\mathcal S(O)\) to be the ordinary CLD-smooth functions with positive
smooth Hessian on \(O\). Suppose \(u\in\mathcal M(O)\) is finite and
continuous. For every point \(x\in O\), choose **before** the
approximation index a compact neighbourhood \(C\subset O\), and put
\(B=\operatorname{Int}_O(C)\), with \(x\in B\). There is a sequence
of smooth psh functions on this one \(B\) which decreases to \(u|_B\)
and converges uniformly there. In particular \(u\in\mathrm{CPsh}(O)\)
and locally belongs to both first stages Psh\(_1\) and CPsh\(_1\).

**Proof.** Set \(\varepsilon_m=3^{-m}\). Theorem 1 with
\(v=u\) and \(F=u|_C+\varepsilon_m\) gives a smooth psh
\(s_m\) defined on **all of the original** \(O\) with

\[
u\leq s_m\text{ on }O,\qquad
0\leq s_m-u<\varepsilon_m\text{ on }C.
\]

Define \(q_m=s_m+\varepsilon_m\), and restrict to \(B\). Addition
of a constant does not change its smooth Hessian. On \(B\),

\[
u+\varepsilon_m\leq q_m<u+2\varepsilon_m,
\qquad
q_{m+1}<u+2\varepsilon_{m+1}
 <u+\varepsilon_m\leq q_m.
\]

Thus \(q_m\) decreases on this fixed \(B\) and
\(\sup_B|q_m-u|\leq2\varepsilon_m\to0\). The first-stage
conclusions follow from the definitions; minimal CPsh and its ordinary
sheaf property give the result on \(O\). No positivity was obtained by
a partition of unity. No sequence of approximation-dependent domains or
intersection of infinitely many neighbourhoods occurs. \(\square\)

For precision one can sheafify **once, after** the fixed-domain
construction: let \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{loc}}(W)\) consist of functions
which locally belong to \(\mathcal M(O)\) on an ordinary open
\(O\subset W\). Restriction of each rank construction commutes with
restriction of the domain, by transfinite induction. Therefore this local
membership rule is a sheaf.

**Corollary 2.** As ordinary sheaves of finite continuous functions,

\[
C\cap\mathcal M_{\mathrm{loc}}
 =\mathrm{CPsh}_1=C\cap\mathrm{Psh}_1.
\]

**Proof.** Theorem 2 gives the first inclusion into both first stages.
For the reverse inclusion from CPsh\(_1\), work on a presentation open
\(N\) where smooth psh functions converge uniformly to \(u\). Choose
members \(s_m\) with
\(\sup_N|s_m-u|<\delta_m\), \(\delta_m=4^{-m}\), and set
\(r_m=s_m+2\delta_m\). Then

\[
u+\delta_m<r_m<u+3\delta_m,
\qquad r_{m+1}<u+3\delta_{m+1}<u+\delta_m<r_m.
\]

They decrease to \(u\) on \(N\). Thus
\(u\in\mathcal M_1(N)\) and \(u\in\mathrm{Psh}_1(N)\).
Conversely a continuous Psh\(_1\) presentation is a decreasing sequence
of smooth functions on one open; Corollary 1, or Theorem 2, gives a
uniform sequence on a fixed compact-neighbourhood interior. Ordinary
locality yields the equality claimed. \(\square\)

This does **not** identify \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{loc}}\) with the full
Psh sheaf. In the full Psh construction a preceding-rank section can have
only local presentations, on different opens. A compact \(C\) selected
at the root may not lie in any one of them. Theorem 1 then cannot descend
to a single child defined on all of that \(C\). A finite cover gives
several local generators, not one positive global generator. We have
proved no positive gluing theorem for those generators. Nor have we shown
that the once-sheafified class is stable under all decreasing sequences.
No same-rank version of CLD 8.1.11 is asserted.

## 4. Signed test measures and the stronger continuous kernel property

We work now on an arbitrary ordinary \(W\) in the target, with the fixed
current \(T\). Let \(C(W)\) be all finite real continuous functions,
including those not globally bounded. Let \(\mathscr M_c(W)\) be the
real vector space of finite signed Radon measures with compact support.
Each such measure integrates every member of \(C(W)\). Equip this vector
space with the weak topology \(\sigma(\mathscr M_c(W),C(W))\): its
neighbourhoods are prescribed by finitely many continuous function
pairings. This is not convergence only against smooth functions.

If \(\beta\) is a symmetric compactly supported smooth
\((n-1,n-1)\)-form, define a signed measure \(\nu_\beta\) by

\[
\int_W f\,d\nu_\beta
 =\langle f\delta_X,d'd''\beta\rangle
 =\langle d'd''[f],\beta\rangle,\qquad f\in C(W).
\]

If \(\alpha\) is a symmetric compactly supported smooth
\((n-p-1,n-p-1)\)-form, similarly define \(\nu_{T,\alpha}\) by

\[
\int_W f\,d\nu_{T,\alpha}
 =\langle fT,d'd''\alpha\rangle
 =\langle d'd''(fT),\alpha\rangle.
\]

Here and below all current products are the ordinary products, and all
integration multiplicities belong to the original analytic calibration.

**Lemma 2 (existence of the signed test measures).** These formulas
define measures in \(\mathscr M_c(W)\), supported respectively in
\(\operatorname{supp}\beta\) and \(\operatorname{supp}\alpha\).

**Proof.** Let \(\eta\) stand for the complementary test
\(d'd''\alpha\); it is symmetric. Proposition 6.5.2 gives
\(\eta=\eta_1-\eta_2\), where \(\eta_i\) are positive compact
tests. Middle positivity of \(T\) makes
\(g\mapsto\langle T,g\eta_i\rangle\) a positive scalar
distribution. A smooth cutoff equal to one on the relevant compact gives
its sup-norm bound. Smooth scalar density (CLD 6.2.5–6) extends it
uniquely to a positive Radon measure, which is the coefficient measure of
CLD 7.2.11. Its mass is \(\langle T,\eta_i\rangle\).

Their difference is a finite signed compact measure; pairing it with any
continuous \(f\) is the ordinary product of CLD 7.2.12. This definition
is independent of the decomposition: the pairings agree for all smooth
scalars, and smooth scalar density makes the Radon measures identical.
Outside \(\operatorname{supp}\eta\), smooth scalar pairings vanish;
the same density there proves that the signed measure has zero
restriction. Therefore its support is contained in
\(\operatorname{supp}\eta\subset\operatorname{supp}\alpha\).
The case \(T=\delta_X\) is the same argument. The source support
hypotheses hold on the good boundaryless ordinary open, as stated in
Section 1. No spanning assertion for strongly positive forms is used.
\(\square\)

Write

\[
\mathscr L_W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\nu_\beta:\beta
 \text{ as above}\},\qquad
\mathscr H_W=\{h\in C(W):d'd''[h]=0\}.
\]

By symmetry of the function Hessian and the current convention,
\(\mathscr H_W\) is exactly the annihilator of \(\mathscr L_W\) in
\(C(W)\). Testing symmetric forms suffices; antisymmetric forms pair to
zero.

In fact the ambient test space is linear and
\(\nu_{\beta_1}+a\nu_{\beta_2}=\nu_{\beta_1+a\beta_2}\), so its
image already equals the displayed real span. The span notation emphasizes
that no positivity restriction is imposed on these approximating measures.

**Theorem 3 (dual reduction).** For the fixed \(T\), the following
are equivalent:

1. For every \(h\in\mathscr H_W\),
   \(d'd''(hT)=0\).
2. For every test \(\alpha\) of the displayed degree,

   \[
   \nu_{T,\alpha}\in
   \overline{\mathscr L_W}^{\,\sigma(\mathscr M_c(W),C(W))}.
   \]

**Proof.** If condition 2 holds, the continuous evaluation
\(\nu\mapsto\int h\,d\nu\) vanishes on \(\mathscr L_W\) for
\(h\in\mathscr H_W\), hence on its weak closure. It therefore
vanishes on every \(\nu_{T,\alpha}\), which is condition 1.

Conversely suppose a particular \(\nu=\nu_{T,\alpha}\) is outside
that closure. By the definition of the weak topology there are finitely
many \(f_1,\ldots,f_m\in C(W)\) and a positive tolerance for which
the corresponding basic neighbourhood of \(\nu\) misses
\(\mathscr L_W\). Define

\[
P:\mathscr M_c(W)\longrightarrow\mathbb R^m,
\qquad P(\mu)=(\int f_i\,d\mu)_{i=1}^m.
\]

Then \(P(\nu)\notin P(\mathscr L_W)\), for equality would produce
a measure of \(\mathscr L_W\) inside the forbidden neighbourhood.
The image \(P(\mathscr L_W)\) is a vector subspace of the
finite-dimensional space \(\mathbb R^m\), hence closed. There is a
vector \(c\) orthogonal to that subspace with
\(c\cdot P(\nu)\ne0\): take the nonzero orthogonal projection of
\(P(\nu)\) onto the complement. Put \(h=\sum_i c_i f_i\).
It is finite continuous, annihilates every member of
\(\mathscr L_W\), and does not annihilate \(\nu\). Thus
\(d'd''[h]=0\) but
\(\langle d'd''(hT),\alpha\rangle\ne0\), contradicting
condition 1. This proves the converse without importing a separation or
current-density theorem. \(\square\)

The closure may require a **net** of finite real linear combinations of
ambient Hessian test measures. It is not a positive-current approximation
and need not have common compact support or a uniform total-variation
bound. The convergence topology tests all of \(C(W)\); convergence only
against smooth scalars would not prove the stated implication for a
continuous \(h\). The theorem does not assert that any of these nets
exists for arbitrary \(T\).

Condition 1 is stronger than the target's property: the target uses only
\(h=U-V\) with finite continuous psh \(U,V\). Failure of condition 2
would supply a continuous analytic zero-current multiplier defect, but
the finite-dimensional separation proof does **not** make its \(h\)
a difference of finite continuous psh functions. Such failure alone would
therefore still not be an admissible P21.3 counterexample. No failure of
condition 2 is established in this draft.

## 5. What is still missing for a pair

The new selection argument concerns individual functions. Even if a full
Psh-to-CPsh conversion were later repaired, for \(h=U-V\) it would give
\(U,V\in\mathrm{CPsh}\), not
\(h,-h\in\mathrm{CPsh}\). CPsh is a cone. No operation in its
definition subtracts two positive approximations while preserving a
curvature identity. The equality \(d'd''[U]=d'd''[V]\) is upstairs
against \(\delta_X\); it supplies no equality of the finitely indexed
approximants or of their products with an arbitrary singular \(T\).

For a fixed \(T\) and fixed test \(\alpha\), Lemma 2 also gives the
legitimate estimate

\[
|\langle d'd''((f-g)T),\alpha\rangle|
 \leq \|f-g\|_{C^0(C)}\,
 \langle T,\eta_1+\eta_2\rangle,
\]

where \(C\) contains the positive-test decomposition supports for
\(d'd''\alpha=\eta_1-\eta_2\). The compact and coefficient are
fixed before the function approximation index. Consequently uniform
scalar approximation **does** control these differentiated pairings
against a fixed test. What it does not do is identify the limit as zero.
If \(h_j\to h\) locally uniformly, it shows
\(d'd''(h_jT)\to d'd''(hT)\). An additional argument is needed to
show that this limit vanishes. Distributional
\(d'd''[h_j]\to0\) against the ambient integration current supplies
no comparison with the signed measures \(\nu_{T,\alpha}\).

This distinction prevents a false diagnostic: when \(h_j\to0\)
locally uniformly, the displayed estimate already forces
\(d'd''(h_jT)\to0\). There cannot be a nonzero fixed-test defect in
that situation. The missing case has a potentially nonzero continuous
zero-current limit \(h\), not a zero scalar limit.

One possible independent route is now expressed exactly by Theorem 3:
prove its dual closure for the actual analytic test measures. No coarse
tropical pushforward, scalar extension of an arbitrary current, strong
current product, or weak-current density is a premise for that route.

## 6. Self-audit and comparison with the residual atom

Theorem 1 uses a continuous strict upper obstacle on one fixed compact
and preserves the lower inequality on the entire domain. It does not
use false uniform convergence to a discontinuous intermediate limit.
At limit ranks its earlier-rank membership is global by definition;
substituting local membership would invalidate that proof step.
Theorem 2 chooses its compact neighbourhood before \(m\), has smooth
positive Hessian at every finite step, and explicitly makes a decreasing
sequence. The one-time sheafification in Corollary 2 is distinguished
from sheafification between ordinal stages. These qualifications are
mathematical hypotheses, not cosmetic language.

Theorem 3 keeps the current arbitrary and treats every target degree,
including scalar tests at \(p=n-1\). Positive-test decomposition and
ordinary coefficient measures retain calibrations and generic lengths.
Its weak topology is spelled out, its necessity proof constructs a
continuous witness only, and no psh difference is inferred from that
witness. Neither theorem uses an unproved external recognition result.
They apply over trivial as well as nontrivial valuations, but provide no
new universal trivial-valuation Bedford–Taylor result.

The full Psh sheaf conversion still lacks a positive gluing argument on a
neighbourhood fixed before the tolerance. The universal kernel step
still lacks either two-sign recognition, a curvature-compatible
fixed-domain approximation/estimate, the dual closure of Theorem 3, or
another direct multiplier argument. No analytic counterexample satisfying
the equal-curvature and psh-pair premises was found. The already accepted
real-PL result is neither repeated as the new theorem nor counted as a
solution of the residual atom. The status is **partial supporting
progress; P21.3 unresolved**.
