\section{Convex combinations: completion is derived}
For $\ell=1,\ldots,r$, suppose independently indexed
actual genuine inputs satisfy
\[
 a_{\ell,i_\ell}\to a_\ell\ \hbox{weakly},\qquad
 E(a_{\ell,i_\ell})\to E(a_\ell).
\]
The generic values converge; first normalize all of them
to zero. Choose one $D\geq1$ bounding their costs and
the limiting costs on cofinal tails.
For the product index $i=(i_1,\ldots,i_r)$ set
\[
 A_i^t=\sum_\ell t_\ell a_{\ell,i_\ell},\qquad
 A^t=\sum_\ell t_\ell a_\ell,\qquad t\in\Delta_{r-1}.
\]
All these are actual normalized genuine finite-energy
potentials. Their normalized costs are at most $D$ by concavity.

\begin{lemma}
For every $t\in\Delta_{r-1}$,
\[
 |E(A_i^t)-E(A^t)|
 \leq C_nD^{1-\alpha}
          \sum_{\ell=1}^r t_\ell
                \delta(a_{\ell,i_\ell},a_\ell)^\alpha.  \tag{5}
\]
In particular the energy convergence is uniform on the
entire real simplex. No barycenter completion is assumed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The midpoints $(a_{\ell,i_\ell}+a_\ell)/2$ converge
weakly to $a_\ell$. Concavity gives the lower limiting
energy $E(a_\ell)$, while weak upper semicontinuity gives
that same upper limiting energy. Thus each displayed
midpoint defect tends to zero.

Apply the plane at $A^t$ to $A_i^t$ and the plane at
$A_i^t$ to $A^t$. The two signed energy differences
are bounded by
\[
 \sum_\ell t_\ell\int|a_{\ell,i_\ell}-a_\ell|\,d\MA(W),
                  \qquad W=A^t\ \hbox{or}\ A_i^t.
\]
Formula (2), with every cost at most $D$, bounds each
integral by
$C_nD^{1-\alpha}\delta(a_{\ell,i_\ell},a_\ell)^\alpha$.
All integrals are at actual genuine outputs, and are
finite by input~2. This proves (5).
The supremum over $t$ is bounded by the maximum of the
$r$ defect terms times $C_nD^{1-\alpha}$, which tends
to zero on the product index.
\end{proof}

\section{All mixed coefficients and changing weights}
\begin{theorem}
Under the exact conditional inputs~1--4, every multi-index
$\beta$ with $|\beta|=n$ satisfies
\[
 \MA(a_{1,i_1}^{\beta_1},\ldots,a_{r,i_r}^{\beta_r})
       \longrightarrow
 \MA(a_1^{\beta_1},\ldots,a_r^{\beta_r}).                \tag{6}
\]
Every energy-polynomial coefficient likewise converges.
If independently $v_k\to v$ weakly have bounded normalized
costs, then on the product index
\[
 (R-v_k)\MA(a_{1,i_1}^{\beta_1},\ldots,a_{r,i_r}^{\beta_r})
       \longrightarrow
 (R-v)\MA(a_1^{\beta_1},\ldots,a_r^{\beta_r})             \tag{7}
\]
as finite original weighted Radon measures.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix, before any limiting operation, finitely many
rational simplex points unisolvent for homogeneous
polynomials of degree $n$ restricted to the simplex.
Such points exist: a homogeneous polynomial vanishing
on the relative interior of the simplex vanishes by
homogeneity on the open positive cone and hence is zero.
Rational points are dense, so an invertible evaluation
matrix can be selected there. This is one fixed finite
matrix with every raw multinomial coefficient included.

At each selected $t$, the genuine $A_i^t$ converge
weakly to $A^t$ in coordinates and have converging
energies by (5). Apply (3) to their diagonal outputs.
Formula (4) and the inverse fixed matrix give (6).
The same argument for scalar energies, using a fixed
rational matrix for degree $n+1$, gives every coefficient
of the energy polynomial. A polynomial of lower degree
on the simplex is homogenized with powers of
$\sum t_\ell=1$, so this interpolation covers it too.

Now use the second part of (3) at those same fixed
degree-$n$ evaluation points, with the independent
changing test $v_k$. Multiplication by $R-v_k$ at each
finite measure's full-measure finite-cost set preserves
the finite identity (4). Inversion therefore gives (7).
No moving polynomial parameter, moving support indicator,
or uncountable intersection is passed through weak
convergence. Positivity and raw mass $V$ are properties
of the input~4 coefficients, not conclusions drawn from
an arbitrary signed interpolation.

For original nonnormalized potentials, normalize each
input first. Every base output is constant invariant.
Formula (4), at fixed parameters followed by finite
interpolation, gives the same invariance in each mixed slot.
The energy of a convex combination acquires exactly
$V\sum_\ell t_\ell a_{\ell,i_\ell}(o)$, converging
uniformly to its limiting linear term. Restore testing
constants in (7) by subtracting
$v_k(o)\MA(a_{1,i_1}^{\beta_1},\ldots,a_{r,i_r}^{\beta_r})$;
formula (6) and convergence of the generic values pass
that term with full mass $V$.
\end{proof}
