# Local CLD-v2 stage bound for semipositive real-PL competitors

This is a root working derivation from checked published foundations, preserved locally while the independent R39 author works. It is not an accepted paper, a priority claim, an envelope theorem, or closure of P03.2. It has not been submitted to a provider or supplied to a running author.

Let \(K\) be complete and nontrivially valued, and let \(X\) be good, separated and strictly \(K\)-analytic. Use the ordinary analytic topology. Write \(\mathcal P_0\) for the sheaf of ordinary CLD-smooth functions whose \(d'd''\) is a positive form, and \(\mathcal P_{i+1}\) for functions locally obtained as decreasing **sequential** limits of sections of \(\mathcal P_i\). These are the ordinal stages of CD-v2 Lemma 8.1.4; the version number v2 is distinct from the ordinal index. The stage construction uses forms, so its definition is not the boundaryless equivalence with positivity of a current in Proposition 8.3.1.

Two precise conclusions follow:

1. A locally Fubini–Study real-PL function belongs to \(\mathcal P_1\).
2. A semipositive real-PL function belongs to \(\mathcal P_2\) locally at every point of \(\operatorname{Int}(X)\).

## Exact foundations

Gubler–Jell–Rabinoff, arXiv:2111.05741, 7.9, gives on an **ordinary open** neighborhood the LFS presentation as a finite nonnegative real linear combination of
\[
\max_j\{-\log|f_j|\},
\]
where the \(f_j\) are invertible analytic functions. The signs and invertibility were checked against the original PDF47. Proposition 7.10, with its complete proof on PDF47–48, gives for a semipositive real-PL function at an **interior** point a fixed analytic neighborhood \(U\) and LFS real-PL functions \(h_i\) on \(U\) converging uniformly to the restriction of the original function. Its proof makes \(U\) an ordinary open neighborhood via the inverse image of the appropriate reduction subset. We use only that direction of the proposition.

Chambert–Loir–Ducros, arXiv:1204.6277v2, 8.1.4, defines the sequential stages. Its 12.4.1–12.4.6 and 15.1.1–15.1.2 identify the ordinary analytic affine charts and ordinary smooth functions with moment-coordinate presentations. Proposition 8.2.1 preserves stage zero under smooth convex coordinatewise nondecreasing composition. The logarithm of an analytic unit is an affine smooth function with zero \(d'd''\); arbitrary real affine linear combinations in the real coordinate space are smooth with zero second differential. These are form statements and do not require the current comparison across a relative boundary.

## Proof for an LFS function

Fix one ordinary neighborhood on which its presentation is
\[
h=\sum_{k=1}^r\lambda_k\max_{1\leq j\leq m_k}a_{kj},
\qquad a_{kj}=-\log|f_{kj}|,\quad\lambda_k\geq0.
\]
For each positive integer \(n\), put
\[
h_n=\sum_{k=1}^r\frac{\lambda_k}{n}
             \log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m_k}\exp(n a_{kj})\right).
\]
All entries \(f_{kj}\) are invertible on this same ordinary neighborhood. Thus \(h_n\) is an ordinary smooth moment-coordinate function. The log-sum-exponential function is smooth, convex, and coordinatewise nondecreasing: its gradient is the vector of nonnegative softmax weights, and its Hessian is \(n\) times their covariance matrix, which is positive semidefinite. Consequently the smooth convex-composition formula gives \(d'd''h_n\geq0\). This proves \(h_n\in\mathcal P_0\).

For real numbers \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\),
\[
\max_j a_j\leq \frac1n\log\sum_j e^{na_j}
\leq\max_j a_j+\frac{\log m}{n}.
\]
The expression decreases as \(n\) increases. Indeed, if \(n'>n\), set \(p=n'/n>1\) and \(b_j=e^{na_j}>0\). The elementary inequality \((\sum_j b_j^p)^{1/p}\leq\sum_j b_j\) gives this monotonicity after taking logarithms and dividing by \(n\). Nonnegative coefficients preserve the inequality. Therefore
\[
h_n\downarrow h,\qquad
0\leq h_n-h\leq\frac{\sum_k\lambda_k\log m_k}{n}
\]
on the whole fixed neighborhood. In particular this is a decreasing sequence of ordinary smooth psh functions. The sheaf property proves the first conclusion on all ordinary neighborhoods with an LFS presentation, including such presentations at a relative-boundary point. No assertion about a general semipositive real-PL function at a boundary point is inferred.

## Proof for a semipositive real-PL function at an interior point

Apply GJR Proposition 7.10 at \(x\in\operatorname{Int}(X)\). On a fixed ordinary neighborhood \(U\) it provides LFS functions \(h_i\) with
\[
\epsilon_i:=\|h_i-h\|_{\infty,U}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Each \(h_i\) is a section of \(\mathcal P_1\) on \(U\), by the preceding proof. This membership does not assert that all \(h_i\) use the same smooth moment chart: \(\mathcal P_1\) is a sheaf. It does give sections on one common \(U\), as required by the successor-stage definition.

If one \(\epsilon_i=0\), then \(h\in\mathcal P_1(U)\subseteq\mathcal P_2(U)\). Otherwise choose a subsequence, still denoted by \(h_i\), with \(\epsilon_{i+1}<\epsilon_i/3\). Define
\[
v_i=h_i+2\epsilon_i.
\]
Addition of a real constant preserves \(\mathcal P_1\): translate any local decreasing smooth approximation by that same constant. Moreover
\[
v_{i+1}\leq h+3\epsilon_{i+1}
             \leq h+\epsilon_i
             \leq h_i+2\epsilon_i=v_i,
\qquad
h+\epsilon_i\leq v_i\leq h+3\epsilon_i.
\]
Thus \(v_i\downarrow h\) on \(U\), with each \(v_i\in\mathcal P_1(U)\). This is exactly \(h\in\mathcal P_2(U)\).

This is also the elementary uniform-to-decreasing conversion used in CD-v2 Lemma 8.1.10, with the stage and common-neighborhood requirements made explicit.

## Remaining envelope obligation

The original obstacle envelope is a regularized **supremum** of these competitors. Neither of the preceding arguments proves that an arbitrary locally upper-bounded supremum, followed by usc regularization, remains in \(\mathcal P_2\). The successor stages are defined by decreasing sequences; this operation cannot be replaced by increasing suprema or arbitrary directed nets without a separate theorem.

The proof does not give approximants below an arbitrary obstacle, identify whole competitor families across scalar extension, prove boundary approximation for every semipositive real-PL function, or classify geometry of envelopes. No original atom is closed by this working derivation.
