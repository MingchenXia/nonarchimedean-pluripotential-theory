ID: n1

SUMMARY:
Draft sufficient theorem for the FULL exact union on every compact strict rig-smooth curve affinoid over any complete nontrivially valued field, including normal curves over perfect fields, genus vertices and cycles. Legal skeleton functions are realized as global semipositive real PL; all finite continuous obstacles and all analytic scalar extensions are controlled. This is not the original sharp classification.

DETAILED SUMMARY:
The raw full LFS/semipositive-real-PL envelope is a uniform limit of global semipositive real-PL minorants b_n with 0<=P-b_n<=2/n, hence finite continuous and equal to its usc regularization. Exact raw-envelope pullback holds for every analytic extension. The proof constructs actual vertical Cartier star metrics before checking proper reduction-component degrees, solves the finite graph obstacle, compares against every analytic competitor, obtains arbitrary-obstacle density on an ambient projective curve after finite separable extension, uses legal value-group cuts, and descends finite Galois averages. Over a perfect field, bijective normalization gives another ground-field continuity sector, without asserting its scalar full-family envelope identity when branches split. All assertions are unverified drafts; higher-dimensional sharpness and necessity remain open.

TEXT:
Let K be complete and nontrivially valued and X a good separated strictly affinoid rig-smooth curve of pure dimension one, possibly disconnected. For finite continuous f let P_X(f) be the supremum of ALL finite functions h<=f which are locally Fubini–Study or semipositive real PL. Use the published definitions in Gubler–Jell–Rabinoff (GJR), arXiv:2111.05741v3, 7 March 2025, Definitions 5.1 and 7.8. LFS is a subclass of semipositive R-PL, so the exact union is the entire semipositive R-PL family. Constants belong to it. The draft theorem is that P_X(f) is finite continuous, equals its usc regularization, admits GLOBAL semipositive R-PL minorants b_n with 0<=P_X(f)-b_n<=2/n, and satisfies P_{X_F}(f composed with pi)=P_X(f) composed with pi for EVERY analytic extension F/K, with the full upstairs family.

Actual realization precedes positivity. For a strictly semistable model, its finite weighted skeleton Sigma has edge lengths in Gamma=-log|K*| and relative-boundary vertices B. New formal subdivision vertices are chosen at Gamma coordinates. GJR 15.3–15.4 and Proposition 15.5 give admissible subdivisions and vertical Cartier metrics in this legal range. On a small legal star about v, let T_i be distance along arm i and zero on every other arm. In the formal node chart XY=gamma_i, with central endpoint -log|X|=0 and arm endpoint -log|X|=-log|gamma_i|, the vertical Cartier divisor has equations X on the node, 1 on the central side away from it, gamma_i on the arm side away from it, and 1 on other nodes. The central overlap has X a unit; the arm overlap has X/gamma_i=1/Y a unit. These are the canonical Cartier data of GJR 15.4, also on etale charts. The metric on O(D_i), canonically trivial on the generic fiber, has -log||1||=T_i composed with tau. Its residue restriction on the central component is O(p_i), with degree the edge weight. It need not be principal at a positive-genus vertex.

An arbitrary REAL affine star function is c+sum s_i T_i. Thus its pullback is actually R-PL by a finite real linear combination of these PL metrics. On an edge it has an annulus-coordinate expression c+s(-log|T|), and on omitted residue discs it is constant. These local expressions agree globally and prove GLOBAL R-PL membership by GJR Definition 5.1 and Remark 5.2. There is no assumption of globally commensurable integral slopes around cycles. Positivity is checked at ordinary germs: on each interior proper reduction component the residue real line bundle has degree sum w(e) partial_e A, as in the checked proof of GJR Proposition 15.7. Nefness on that curve is precisely nonnegative degree; degree zero gives harmonicity. At a relative-boundary vertex the reduction component is nonproper and imposes no sign, GJR Proposition 15.5(2). Off the finite vertices the formulas are harmonic. All omitted directions and type-4 points are included in the analytic construction.

For an obstacle G affine on a finite legal graph subdivision, consider vertex vectors a<=G with D a(v)=sum_{vw}w(vw)(a(w)-a(v))/length(vw)>=0 at vertices outside B. Constants at min G are feasible. Coordinatewise maxima preserve these inequalities: at v select the vector giving its maximum there, and all neighboring maximum values only increase its D value. A compact truncation between min G and max G therefore has a greatest feasible vector u, obtained by maximizing each coordinate and taking their finite coordinatewise maximum. It is greatest among all feasible vectors by clipping from below at min G. At B it saturates G; at an interior vertex below G it has D u=0, since otherwise that coordinate could be increased slightly. Its affine interpolant U is below G and its pullback H is a GLOBAL semipositive R-PL competitor. It is harmonic off the contact vertex set S={u=G}, which contains B.

Now bound every analytic competitor, not merely graph competitors. By Gubler–Rabinoff (GRa), arXiv:2506.13548v2, 17 September 2025, Theorem 4.3, each full competitor h is continuous and classically psh. On S it satisfies h<=G composed with tau=H. Off S, h-H is classically psh since H is harmonic. A positive global maximum of h-H has a closed nonempty maximum locus disjoint from S. All its points are interior because B is in S. GRa Theorem 6.1 makes the locus open. It would contain an entire connected component, impossible because each affinoid curve component has nonempty boundary (GRa Lemma 6.2). Therefore P(G composed with tau)=H on the WHOLE analytic curve.

The same comparison holds after EVERY analytic extension E: H pulls back semipositively and harmonically off pi^{-1}(S) by GJR Proposition 7.6, extended boundary is contained over B by GRa 2.11(5'), and each extended affinoid component has nonempty boundary. Hence the full upstairs envelope of the pulled graph obstacle is exactly H pulled back. No openness of scalar projection or commutation of usc regularization is assumed.

Arbitrary-obstacle density is obtained using actual published hypotheses. Thuillier's 2005 thesis, HAL tel-00010990v1, Theorem 2.3.8 and its proof embed a strict rig-smooth affinoid, after finite separable extension, in a smooth projective curve. Extend f continuously to that compact Hausdorff curve by Tietze. Apply Thu Corollary 3.2.9 and the proof of Proposition 3.2.7 there, on the boundaryless ambient curve. Proposition 3.2.4 gives finite skeleton factorization after another finite separable extension. This is not yet declared real PL. Move its finitely many interior breakpoints to legal nearby coordinates, keeping their values and interpolating. A piecewise affine increasing homeomorphism of each edge tends uniformly to the identity, so uniform continuity gives uniform convergence of these legal interpolants. Dense Gamma supplies the cuts directly. If Gamma=delta Z, adjoin an Nth root of an element of valuation delta, with N prime to char K when needed; a sufficiently fine delta/N grid is then legal in a finite separable extension. Finally take a finite Galois L/K containing all these fields. Semistable base-change retractions commute in the node charts and on component tubes, as in Thu Proposition 2.2.21. Uniform errors are preserved by surjectivity. Thus for every epsilon>0 a finite Galois L and a legal graph obstacle g on X_L satisfy ||g-f pulled back||<=epsilon. This controls arbitrary noninvariant obstacles, including their omitted-direction and type-4 values.

Let H=P_{X_L}(g), already realized and fully compared. Conjugating its formal charts and residue bundles shows every Galois translate H_sigma is globally semipositive R-PL. Put A=average_sigma H_sigma. Addition, not a pointwise maximum of real-PL functions, makes A invariant semipositive R-PL. GJR Corollary 5.13 and Proposition 7.7 descend A-epsilon to a global semipositive R-PL function b_epsilon on X. It is <=f. For EVERY ground competitor h, h pulled back minus epsilon is below every translated g. Comparison therefore gives h pulled back <=H_sigma+epsilon for each sigma. Averaging and surjectivity give b_epsilon<=P_X(f)<=b_epsilon+2epsilon. Taking epsilon=1/n proves continuity by uniform convergence on the entire X.

For arbitrary F/K choose a common analytic extension E of F and finite L, for instance a field factor of F tensor_K L. Pull any full F-competitor to E and repeat comparison against each translated graph obstacle. Surjectivity of X_E -> X_F gives b_epsilon pulled back <=P_{X_F}(f pulled back)<=b_epsilon pulled back+2epsilon. The same sequence therefore proves exact raw-envelope compatibility and continuity for every analytic extension. Every extended curve pullback of the limit is subharmonic: pass uniform limits in harmonic comparison on a strict affinoid test domain, or use GRa Theorem 6.5 and its checked proof. Positivity is a separate consequence and no CLD-v2 assertion is made.

For perfect K a normal curve is rig-smooth (GRa Lemma 3.9(1)), so this includes every requested normal affinoid over algebraically closed K with genus vertices and cyclic skeleta. Also, if a reduced strict affinoid over perfect K has bijective normalization, that finite map is a homeomorphism. A transferred normalization competitor is R-PL by GRa Lemma 3.9(6), then semipositive by proper-surjective descent, GJR Proposition 7.6(1'). The full envelopes and uniform approximants therefore descend, giving ground-field continuity. Its pulled-back ground envelope remains continuous and passes extended curve tests. Equality with the enlarged upstairs competitor envelope is not claimed where normalization splits.
