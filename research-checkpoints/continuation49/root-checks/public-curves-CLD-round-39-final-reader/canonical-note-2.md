ID: n2

SUMMARY:
Full-union envelopes have ordinary CLD stage 1 on boundaryless reduced good strict pure curves with bijective normalization over perfect countably dense fields whose residue is algebraic over a finite field. This includes positive-genus curves over Qp. Without the residue/genus hypothesis the checked route gives only stage 3.

DETAILED SUMMARY:
For every usc obstacle, finite maxima containing a baseline make the entire normalized competitor family locally bounded on both sides. Thuillier's supremum theorem gives a subharmonic regularized envelope. Countable local topology extracts a sequence from a decreasing continuous net on a fixed ordinary neighborhood. Under the residue hypothesis, GJR's approximants are ordinary smooth, yielding stage 1. They descend through homeomorphic normalization because ordinary smooth functions are locally constant at rigid points. All analytic extensions and curve pullbacks preserve the bound. Without ordinary/weak sheaf equality, the real-PL regularization and stage-2 approximation lemma yield stage 3, not stage 2.

TEXT:
Theorem. Let K be complete, nontrivially valued and perfect, with a countable dense subset. Let X be a nonempty reduced, separated, good, strictly K-analytic curve pure of dimension one, with ∂X=∅ and normalization ν:X̃→X bijective on Berkovich points. If the residue field K̃ is algebraic over a finite field, then for EVERY usc f:X→R∪{−∞}, the usc regularization u of the supremum over the FULL real-valued locally-Fubini–Study/semipositive-real-PL minorant union belongs to Psh_{X,1}⊂Psh_{X,2}. At every point it is a decreasing sequence of finite ordinary CLD-smooth functions with positive d'd'' on one ordinary neighborhood. The same conclusion holds under the alternative hypothesis that X̃ is potentially locally P1. Without either residue/genus hypothesis, the proof below establishes only Psh_{X,3}.

Full-family supremum step. If the family is empty, both envelopes are −∞, approximated by the smooth constants −j. Otherwise choose one competitor h_0. Every competitor is finite continuous and classically psh by n1. Therefore f is finite everywhere and locally upper bounded. Also h_0≤s≤u≤f, the last inequality following from usc of f. GRc Lemma 3.9 makes X̃ rig-smooth and boundaryless. Since ν is a bijective topological quotient, it is a homeomorphism. Pull back the entire family, direct finite subsets containing h_0 by inclusion, and take their maxima. These are subharmonic and locally bounded below by h_0∘ν and above by f∘ν. They need not be real PL; no real-PL maximum closure is assumed. Thuillier 3.1.8(iii) makes (s∘ν)* subharmonic. Homeomorphic regularization gives (s∘ν)*=u∘ν. This treats the entire family without a modulus or invariance restriction.

Sequence extraction. A strict K-affinoid algebra is separable as a Banach space because countably many Tate polynomials with coefficients in a countable dense subset of K have dense images. The inequality | |a(x)|−|b(x)| |≤||a−b|| shows that countably many evaluations generate the topology and separate points of its spectrum. The compact spectrum embeds in a countable product of compact real intervals, hence is second countable. A relatively compact ordinary open W in a good Hausdorff space is covered by finitely many interiors of affinoid neighborhoods of its compact closure, so is second countable. For a decreasing directed net a_i of continuous functions with infimum v on W, {v<t}=⋃_i{a_i<t} is open and Lindelof. Choose a countable subcover for each rational t, enumerate all chosen indices, and successively take dominating indices. This gives a decreasing sequence on the SAME W. If its limit exceeded v at a point, an intermediate rational threshold and its chosen covering index would contradict that inequality. Thus the sequence converges to v, including where v=−∞.

Stage 1. On a relatively compact ordinary neighborhood W̃ in X̃, GJR 15.11 gives a decreasing net of finite weakly smooth psh functions converging to u∘ν. The residue hypothesis, or the stated potentially-local-P1 hypothesis, makes these ordinary smooth by GJR 15.10. Their positive ordinary currents give positive ordinary forms by CD 8.3.1(b), so they are stage 0. Extract an actual decreasing sequence on W̃.

Ordinary smooth descent. Normalization is an analytic isomorphism off a discrete set of non-normal rigid points. An ordinary smooth function is locally constant at a rigid point, including one with a finite residue extension. To prove this, let g be an analytic unit at such a point z, α=g(z), ρ=|α|>0, and P its monic minimal polynomial of degree d. All conjugate roots have modulus ρ because K is complete. On the ordinary neighborhood |P(g)|<ρ^d, if |g(y)|≠ρ then, after algebraic extension of H(y), every root difference has modulus max(|g(y)|,ρ), giving |P(g(y))|≥ρ^d, a contradiction. Hence |g| is constant there. Apply this to the finitely many units in an ordinary smooth moment expression. Transfer each normalized approximant through the homeomorphism. Away from non-normal points it is smooth and positive by analytic isomorphism; at those points it is constant on an ordinary neighborhood, hence smooth with zero d'd''. Thus the transferred functions are stage 0 on W=ν(W̃), and decrease to u. This proves stage 1. Induction gives the same descent for the finite ordinal stages used below.

General residue fields. Thuillier 3.4.2 supplies a decreasing net of continuous subharmonic lisse functions on W̃. They are real PL by Proposition 3.2.5 and semipositive by the supplied GRs Proposition 5.1. The interior lemma in n1 places each in stage 2. Extracting a decreasing sequence gives stage 3; descent gives Psh_{X,3}. No collapse to stage 2 is justified.

Scalar and curve quantifiers. For EVERY complete analytic extension K'/K and analytic curve morphism g:C→X_{K'}, CD 12.4.5/8.1.5 preserves the proved ordinal bound for u∘π∘g. The ordinary smooth sequences and all their decreasing limits pull back. Smooth psh curve functions are subharmonic, and decreasing limits remain so, as checked in GRc §1.2 and Proposition 3.5. This also gives scalar-stable classical pshness, including −∞ in the extended convention. Neither openness of scalar projection nor equality of envelopes for enlarged scalar-extension competitor families is asserted or needed.

This is a noninvariant full-family sector covering boundaryless ordinary open domains in smooth elliptic and higher-genus curves over Qp. It is not a rational-tree example or selected invariant family. Continuity is not asserted. The sufficient field hypotheses are not shown necessary; relative-boundary points and arbitrary imperfect perfected-unibranch descent remain unresolved.
