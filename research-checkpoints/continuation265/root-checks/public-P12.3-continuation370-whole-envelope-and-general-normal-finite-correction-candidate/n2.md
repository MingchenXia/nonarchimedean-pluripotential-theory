# Regular excellent surface models are actual primary blowups in every characteristic.

Let R be an excellent normal local domain of dimension two, with arbitrary residue field and arbitrary, imperfect, positive or mixed characteristic. For finitely many primary ideals, including a, take the product blowup and its finite normalization. It is an integral projective surface, unchanged on the punctured spectrum. Its singular locus is finite: excellence makes the regular locus open and normality excludes singular codimension-one points. Its singular local rings have normal completions by current Stacks 0C23 (normal formal fibres; excellence supplies them). These are exactly condition (4) of CURRENT 54.14.5, 0BGP, in full Section 0BGJ. Its (4)->(3) construction produces a regular projective T by normalized blowups, still unchanged on the puncture and principalizing the chosen ideals. The immediate source proof has been read; its recursive Lipman, complete-ring, rational-double-point, descent and dualizing foundations remain ordinary published dependencies. No characteristic-free resolution in dimension >=3 is inferred.

Stacks 0AY8 applies with normal integral base, reduced integral T, dominating generic point and generic H0 equal to Frac(R). Thus Gamma(T,O)=R and the full closed fibre is geometrically connected. Regular local factoriality makes its exceptional integral curves E_i actual Cartier divisors. Since aO_T is invertible, write E_a=sum_i b_i E_i with b_i>0 integral. Its support is the entire fibre. A Cartier cut on a regular surface is pure codimension one, so no isolated fibre point is discarded.

Use degrees over the actual residue field k, and define M_ij=deg_k O_T(E_j)|E_i. For i!=j these are sums of local intersection lengths weighted by [kappa(p):k]. Symmetry follows from the regular local intersection pairing, and off-diagonal entries are nonnegative. The graph is connected by connectedness of the full fibre. The pullback of the ample tautological bundle on Bl_a gives Mb<=0. There is a strict coordinate: if Bl_a had only a zero-dimensional fibre its proper birational map would be finite and, over normal R, an isomorphism. Then a would be invertible/principal, contradicting the height theorem for a primary ideal in dimension two. Thus Bl_a has a fibre curve, and surjectivity of T->Bl_a gives a curve with positive pullback degree.

Put A=-M and t_i=b_i z_i. Expansion gives the exact identity
 t^T A t = sum_i (Ab)_i b_i z_i^2 + sum_{i<j} (-A_ij)b_i b_j (z_i-z_j)^2.
Equality implies z constant on the connected graph and zero at a strict coordinate. Hence A is SPD. Every principal A_J is SPD with nonpositive off-diagonal entries. Its inverse is entrywise nonnegative: if A_J x=y>=0, write x=x_+-x_- with disjoint nonnegative parts. A nonzero negative part would give x_-^T y=x_-^T A_J x_+-x_-^T A_J x_-<0, contradiction. The vector A^-1 1 is strictly positive; at a zero coordinate its row product would be nonpositive. It is rational since A is integral. Clear denominators to obtain integral d_i>0 with Md<0.

D0=sum d_i E_i is an actual effective Cartier divisor. L=O_T(-D0) has positive degree on every integral curve of the FULL, possibly nonreduced closed fibre. Current 0B5Y applies to that proper scheme of dimension <=1 over any field and makes L ample on the fibre; 0D2N makes it relatively ample near the closed point. An open neighbourhood of a local base's closed point is the entire base. Relative ampleness is absolute over the affine base. Full 0B5S provides coherence and generation/Serre results; a high power L^N has finitely many sections defining a closed immersion.

I=Gamma(T,L^N) is a finite ideal of Gamma(T,O)=R. It is proper because its sections vanish on the nonempty boundary, and unit off m by localization of coherent global sections, so it is m-primary. Generation gives IO_T=L^N. Choose ideal generators including the immersion sections. Adding sections preserves closed immersion: on an original section chart the new coordinates are regular ratios; properness closes the image. T and the ACTUAL Bl_I are integral closed subschemes of the same projective space with the identical punctured graph of those generators. Both are its scheme-theoretic closure: on each affine chart their prime ideals consist of the functions vanishing on this dense graph. Thus T=Bl_I, not just a normal modification with a numerically ample boundary. The same construction applies to every regular common refinement used below.
