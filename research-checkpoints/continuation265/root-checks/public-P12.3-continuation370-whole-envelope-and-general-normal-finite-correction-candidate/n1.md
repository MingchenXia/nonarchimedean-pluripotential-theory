# Raw geometric envelopes and actual decreasing-net closure on the full boundary.

Fix an excellent local ring R, replace it by its reduction, and fix a proper m-primary ideal a. All centred real semivaluations with v(a)=1 are retained, including nonminimal prime supports. Qualified G331 gives the compact Hausdorff space X, its independently defined geometric core, C and D. The core uses actual negative effective finite real Cartier presentations on actual primary blowups, tested by degree on every contracted proper integral curve. C is its uniform closure; D is the infimum of ONE actual decreasing C-net, with bottom included. G331 proves the comparison with unit-or-primary ideal profiles, finite MAX/addition/scaling, full-support divisorial density on normal domains, signed-profile density, and full positive normalization descent. None is an envelope grant. Its actual-primary-model, corrected Keeler, published-source, smooth CLOSED algebraically closed characteristic-zero, reduction and radial qualifications are retained in inputs/grants/G331.txt.

For finite continuous f define p(f)=sup{h in D:h<=f}. A negative constant gives a bounded lower competitor. If h=inf_alpha h_alpha<=f is its actual C representation, the increasing open sets {h_alpha<f+epsilon} cover X. Compactness and directedness select one index valid everywhere. Then h_alpha-epsilon is a C competitor and is >=h-epsilon. Thus the C and D suprema agree. In particular p is LSC, bounded between a negative constant and min(f,0).

If p is continuous, the open sets {g>p-epsilon}, for C competitors g, cover X. Finitely many suffice, and their maximum is in C, below p, with uniform error <epsilon. Closedness gives p in C. Conversely membership of p in D gives USC, hence continuity. Its upper regularization p*(x)=inf_{U open containing x} sup_U p satisfies p<=p*<=min(f,0). If p* is in D, it is an admissible competitor, so p*<=p and equality follows. Consequently a greatest D minorant exists iff p is continuous iff p* is in D; in this case it is in C. A maximal D minorant is necessarily greatest, since MAX with any competitor is again admissible. Negative shifts compare competitor sets and prove |p(f)-p(g)|<=||f-g|| uniformly.

For k>=1 define J_k={r in R: v(r)>=-k f(v) for every v in X}. Ultrametricity and v(R)>=0 prove ideal closure. If f>=-M, a^ceil(kM) is contained; J_k is the unit ideal if f>=0 everywhere and otherwise is proper primary. It is integrally closed: if r violates the inequality at v, v(r) is finite, and in a monic ideal-integrality equation r^d+sum_{i=1}^d c_i r^{d-i}=0, c_i in J_k^i, the first term has strictly smaller value than all others. Kill the support first to apply the least-term test in the valued quotient field. This is impossible. Products give J_k J_l subset J_{k+l} and J_k^l subset J_{kl}. Individual ideals are finitely generated; finite generation of the whole graded algebra is not asserted. The profiles -v(J_{n!})/n! therefore increase. To identify their supremum with p, approximate a C competitor uniformly by a rational negative single-ideal profile, then subtract a small rational primary constant so it is below that competitor. Such a profile -v(I)/k below f means I subset J_k. At all sufficiently large factorials, J_k^{n!/k} subset J_{n!}; their profiles dominate this approximation. Sending the error to zero proves p=sup_n -v(J_{n!})/n! on all supports. This is pointwise convergence, with no increasing-D closure inference.

Here is a separate proof that D is closed under decreasing D-nets. On a normal local domain let u=inf_i u_i for such a net. It is USC. If u(x)=0 anywhere, every nonpositive u_i is zero there and G331's normal zero dichotomy makes each identically zero. Otherwise some term is nonzero, so a tail satisfies u_i<=-c for a finite c>0 by compact USC boundedness, including an all-bottom term. For a finite continuous test F>u, compact increasing open sets {u_i<F} give one tail term below F everywhere. Its ONE C representation supplies g<F and g<-c/2 everywhere. Choose 0<b<1 with b||g||<min_X(F-g). Then q=(1-b)g is in C, q<F, and q>u: at finite u it lies above g by at least bc/2, while at bottom it is finite.

Apply this insertion to the continuous minimum of two strict C majorants. The set of all such majorants is downward directed. It has infimum u: for x and finite t>u(x), choose a finite ell between u(x) and t, use USC to find a neighbourhood where u<ell, and use a Urysohn cutoff to interpolate ell near x with a global strict upper bound outside. This constructs a continuous F>u with F(x)<t. Insertion then gives a C majorant below t at x, even at a pole. Reverse pointwise order supplies ONE actual decreasing C-net to u. Finite products treat all normal centres. For a general reduced germ pull the given net to finite normalization, apply the normal result centrewise, and use G331 D descent: its limit is constant on every full fibre because it is a downstairs function. This proves decreasing-D closure without flattening nested nets or arbitrary-infimum closure. Existing energy criteria can only be used after this membership proof.
