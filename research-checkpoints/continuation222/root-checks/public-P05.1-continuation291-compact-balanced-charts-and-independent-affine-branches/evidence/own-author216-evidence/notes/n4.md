We reconstruct the literal negative sector: every finite globally embedded noncompact rational polyhedral support with a literal PA_Z polyhedrally convex reference gamma positive at infinity has a bounded literal obstacle whose actual unrestricted envelope is not uniformly regularizable by literal PC PA functions. Balancing is unnecessary. On positive-dimensional finite globally embedded positive balanced supports, n1 proves noncompactness, so every admitted literal reference fails regularity. This does not eliminate compact chart spaces, which n1 treats positively.

Choose a noncompact connected component, rational p there and rational C>gamma(p). Put phi=max(gamma,C) and D=phi-gamma. Rational coefficients make every inserted wall rational and phi remains PC by the max Jensen inequality and n1's continuous PA test. D is literal, nonnegative, positive at p, and compactly supported. Indeed on each pointed carrying cell gamma has positive recession slope on its unit recession section; its vertex part is bounded. Finitely many cells give gamma(x)>=kappa||x||-A outside a bounded set. Take a finite pointed rational common refinement carrying gamma and D. On an unbounded cell, bounded affine D has zero recession slopes; translating along any nonzero recession direction eventually leaves its compact support. Thus D is identically zero there.

Finite polyhedral connected components are path connected. A path from p to a far zero of D meets a bounded positive-dimensional carrying cell with nonzero affine slope k for D. Choose a primitive lattice direction w there with k(w)!=0 and a rational segment a+[0,L]w in its relative interior. For integer N>|k(w)| let f=gamma+D/N=(1-1/N)gamma+(1/N)phi. It is continuous PC with the same growth and has nonintegral slope q_0=m+k(w)/N on that segment, where m is integral.

For each carrying vertex v, an ambient integral coordinate norm rho(x)=max_j|ell_j(x)| and sufficiently large common integer M give D(v)/N+M rho(x-v)>=D(x)/N on X. Near v this follows from its finitely many incident affine slopes; outside a neighborhood the bounded numerator divided by rho(x-v) is bounded. Define u=min{D, min_v(D(v)/N+M rho(x-v))}. Every slope is integral, every constant rational, and all min/max walls rational. It is bounded, D/N<=u<=D, has u(v)=D(v)/N, and vanishes on unbounded carrying cells. Therefore f is a competitor below gamma+u.

For any printed PC competitor q below this obstacle, a point of a bounded cell is a convex combination of rational vertices; their obstacle values are f and f is affine, so q<=f there. On an unbounded pointed cell write y=sum alpha_j v_j+sum beta_k r_k, with alpha,beta nonnegative and sum alpha=1. Pick v_0 with alpha_0>0 and large rational T satisfying sum beta_k/T<=alpha_0. Transfer those weights from v_0 to rational endpoints v_0+T r_k. They have weighted average y. On this entire cell D=u=0, so its obstacle equals affine f=gamma. PCC gives q(y)<=f(y), even for real y and real weights, since endpoints are rational. Thus the actual unrestricted envelope is exactly f on every cell.

Any literal PC PA approximant psi has nondecreasing integer slopes along the primitive segment. Let k' be its right slope at the midpoint. If k'<=floor(q_0), use the left half-segment; if k'>=ceil(q_0), use the right half-segment. In either case the difference of the endpoint errors has absolute size at least (L/2) dist(q_0,Z). Each error is bounded by ||psi-f||, so ||psi-f||>=(L/4) dist(q_0,Z)>0. Literal uniform or decreasing-uniform approximation is impossible. No literal Monge–Ampere measure is assigned to this endpoint.

The simplest exact example is gamma=|x|, u=min(1/2,(1-|x|)_+), envelope f=max(|x|,(|x|+1)/2). Its fractional segment slope is 1/2 on [0,1] and the proved uniform error floor is 1/8. Grid evaluation is only supporting evidence; the vertex/recession proof establishes the envelope and the slope argument establishes the floor.
