TEXT:
The results of n1 and n2 do not provide the original characterization. The following necessity statements and limitations distinguish proved facts from shortcut failures.

Sequence obstruction. If u=inf_n v_n on an ordinary neighbourhood V, with all v_n finite continuous, then for every real a,
{u<a}=⋃_{n,k≥1}{v_n≤a−1/k}.
If u(x)<a, some v_n(x)<a and then some k supplies the displayed closed-set inequality; the converse is immediate. Every set on the right is closed in V. Thus strict sublevel sets must be F_sigma. Strict real-PL functions are continuous, so this is necessary for the requested sequence. On a nonmetrizable space, upper semicontinuity alone does not supply it. No classical psh function violating this condition is constructed here; it is not claimed to be a sharp analytic failure criterion. The Lindelof extraction of n1 is sufficient, and does not prove countable dense fields necessary.

Retraction necessity. Suppose analytic maps s:B→X and p:X→B satisfy p∘s=id. If every h∘p has local decreasing semipositive strict real-PL regularization near s(b), pull its approximants back by s on the ordinary open s^{-1}(V). GJR 5.4 and 7.6 prove strict membership and semipositivity; pointwise evaluation proves the same decreasing limit h. Therefore the base curve remains a genuine necessary test for a projective bundle with sections. This is a functorial necessity statement, not a classification of all good strict spaces.

Strict-max membership counterexample. GJR Example 5.3, PDF page 31, uses additive value group Q and the rank-one torus. The two functions π log|T|+1 and 0 are strict real-PL, while their maximum has tropical potential max{−πt+1,0} and corner t=1/π∉Q. Strict affinoid tropical intervals have rational vertices, and the published example concludes that this maximum is not strict real-PL. It is classical psh by the classical max/cone property, since log|T| is pluriharmonic. This refutes closure under arbitrary real maxima and the all-domain-lisse identification. It does not show failure of decreasing strict real-PL regularization: the mesh repair in n1 deliberately avoids that inference.

Saturation and actual boundaries. On an affine coordinate chart in a projective fibre, log|z_1| is classical psh and varies along that fibre. The chart omits the hyperplane at infinity, so its fibre portion is not compact. Compact fibre constancy therefore cannot descend every arbitrary psh germ from such a chart. This is a counterexample to the descent shortcut, not to regularization. A compact closed disc also has nonconstant classical psh functions, for example max{log|T|,log|a|} with 0<|a|<1. Here the maximum is legal integral PL on the two strict domains cut at |T|=|a|. Its maximum occurs at the actual relative boundary. A boundaryless maximum principle cannot be applied there.

Normalization limitations. The smooth normal unibranch examples in n2 require no normalization. Their existence does not prove normality or unibranchness sufficient for arbitrary higher-dimensional germs. Singular-curve normalization is finite but need not be flat; GJR Proposition 5.11 cannot simply descend strict PL approximants through it. A topological homeomorphism of a normalization likewise does not establish strict membership downstairs. Identifying values of independently chosen branch approximants does not prove monotonicity on one ordinary neighbourhood. No general singular-curve or higher-dimensional normalization criterion is established.

Residue and genus range. The strict star construction of n1 uses integral model functions and real coefficients, not ordinary local logarithmic harmonicity. Positive-genus residue components and nontorsion residue Picard classes are allowed. GJR Remark 15.10 explains that harmonic functions need not be ordinary CLD smooth. Wanner’s cached arXiv:1801.04713v3, Corollary 5.4, page 31, assumes a smooth proper curve and finite continuous starting functions, with residue field algebraic over a finite field or the projective-line/Mumford alternatives for its decreasing ordinary CLD-smooth conclusion. These are comparison facts only. They are not used to add false restrictions to strict real-PL regularization, or to claim ordinary smoothness. No locally Fubini–Study, weakly smooth or CLD-smooth conclusion is made for the present atom.

Remaining original obligations. A sharp characterization for the full arbitrary classical psh germ class in dimension at least two remains unresolved. So do constructions for arbitrary germs in ordinary affine opens of smooth normal unibranch spaces; sharp necessity and genuine failure examples for the regularization property; general normalization descent; unrestricted extraction when second countability is unavailable; curve boundary cases outside the proved range; and general tropical-chart overlap gluing outside the globally pulled-back bundle sector. Neither the F_sigma test nor the shortcut counterexamples establish the sharp failure range. Original P02.1 therefore remains open in this draft, and candidate=false.
