ID: n1

SUMMARY:
Draft sufficient sector: arbitrary classical psh functions on saturated projective bundles and proper-factor products over arbitrary-genus curve domains admit decreasing LFS nets on fixed ordinary neighborhoods. Sequences are proved when the complete algebraically closed ground field is countably dense. Arbitrary nonsaturated germs remain untreated.

DETAILED SUMMARY:
For complete algebraically closed nontrivially valued K, a type-2-only harmonic interpolation net on a fixed strict affinoid curve neighborhood is realized as strict-domain real PL. One fixed nonnegative graph potential gives positive residue degree at every positive-genus residue point, making every perturbed interpolant genuinely LFS. Rational sublevel covers yield a sequence without assuming target continuity when K is countably dense. Proper connected fibres force arbitrary classical psh targets on saturated total spaces to descend to the curve. Actual sections, transition-chart agreement, and pullback after every analytic scalar extension are checked. This is a sufficient sector, not the original characterization.

TEXT:
Draft theorem. Let K be complete, algebraically closed and nontrivially valued. Let C be an ordinary open subset of the analytification of a smooth connected algebraic K-curve, of arbitrary genus. For every classical psh g:C→R∪{−∞} and c∈C, there is one ordinary open neighborhood U of c carrying a decreasing net of finite LFS functions with infimum g|U. If K has a countable dense subset, there is a decreasing sequence on that same U. No target continuity or residue-field torsion hypothesis is imposed.

The corresponding higher-dimensional sufficient sector consists of actual projective bundles f:P(E)→C of positive relative dimension and products C×P^an, where P is a nonempty geometrically connected proper algebraic K-variety of positive dimension. Every arbitrary classical psh function defined on the entire total space, or a saturated ordinary open subset f⁻¹(C₀), has this regularization locally on a fixed saturated neighborhood. This quantifier does not cover arbitrary germs defined only on nonsaturated small open sets.

Published foundations were checked in the supplied caches. GR arXiv:2506.13548v2 Definition 4.1 includes upper semicontinuous extended-real targets and all analytic scalar extensions in the curve test. Its 4.1.2 identifies the curve class with subharmonicity. GJR arXiv:2111.05741 Definition 5.1 uses strict affinoid domains; Definition 7.8 requires semiample real residue classes for LFS membership. GJR 15.4–15.7 provide strict skeleton realization and the outgoing-slope/residue-degree formula. Thuillier 2.2.22 permits finite separable refinement at type-2 vertices; algebraic closure makes that extension K itself. Thuillier 3.4.1–3.4.2 give harmonic interpolation and determination by type-2/type-3 values; 3.4.6 gives continuity on skeleton segments. The cached GR arXiv:2503.05513 calls the curve nef/subharmonic comparison Proposition 5.1. GR's later citation uses different numbering.

Construction and proof. Choose a connected compact strictly affinoid neighborhood Y⊂C with c∈U=Y\∂Y. The actual analytic boundary ∂Y is finite and type 2. Since C is boundaryless and separated, U is its ordinary topological interior in C. Each connected positive-dimensional affinoid component has nonempty boundary. Otherwise compactness and the classical maximum principle applied to every log|a| would make all seminorm evaluations constant and its spectrum a singleton.

If g is identically −∞ on the connected curve component, use constants −j. Otherwise g is finite at type-2 and type-3 points. For each finite type-2 set S⊂Y containing ∂Y, refine a semistable skeleton to include S as vertices. Assign g(s) at s∈S and solve the finite harmonic Dirichlet system at the other vertices. Pull the affine graph solution back to Y, obtaining H_S. Its uniqueness follows from the harmonic maximum principle and makes it independent of the chosen refinement. It is finite, continuous, harmonic on Y\S, and equals g on S.

Strict real-PL realization is explicit. At a type-2 star with value-group cuts, use one basis function per incident edge: value zero at the center, slope one on that edge and zero on the other edges. Each basis has integral slopes and value-group intercepts and is integral PL by GJR 15.4–15.5. An arbitrary real-affine star function is its center constant plus the real linear combination determined by its outgoing slopes. At type-3 edge points use an annulus coordinate; off the skeleton the function is locally constant. Ordinary sheafiness of strict real PL gives membership on Y. Arbitrary real breakpoints are not introduced.

Subharmonic comparison gives g≤H_S and H_S≥H_S′ when S⊂S′. To check H_S is subharmonic on U, let a harmonic h dominate H_S on the boundary of an affinoid V⊂U. It first dominates g on V. Hence it dominates H_S at S∩V. Comparing harmonic functions on V\S then gives h≥H_S on V. Thus H_S has nef residues by the strict curve comparison.

The decreasing infimum w=inf_S H_S is subharmonic and agrees with g at every type-2 point. At type-3 points, both restrictions to surrounding annulus segments are finite convex continuous functions and agree on dense type-2 points. They therefore agree there as well. Thuillier 3.4.1 recovers all remaining values, including type-1 poles, from type-2/type-3 values. Consequently w=g. This restricts the interpolation construction to type-2 sets instead of identifying all-domain Thuillier lisse functions with strict real PL.

Now fix one initial semistable skeleton Σ₀ of Y. Its finitely many interior positive-genus residue vertices form a set B. Every other type-2 point in U lies in an annulus or open disc and has residue curve P¹; refinements add only genus-zero vertices. Solve on Σ₀ the graph problem q=0 at ∂Y and
Σ_{e=vw}(q_w−q_v)/ℓ_e=1 for v∈B, and 0 at other interior vertices.
The negative coefficient matrix has quadratic form Σ_e(a_v−a_w)²/ℓ_e with zero boundary values, so it is positive definite and the solution exists. Extend q affinely and set Q=q∘τ₀+A, with A=max_Y|q∘τ₀|. Then Q≥0. It is strict real PL, subharmonic on U, and its residue degree is one at each point of B. Boundary slope sums need not be positive, but those boundary points are removed from U.

For ε>0 put V_{S,ε}=H_S+εQ on U. At positive-genus residue points its residue degree is strictly positive. Every positive-degree real Picard class on a smooth proper curve is ample in the real-class sense: represent it by finitely many integral classes, surround its coefficient vector by a rational simplex in the positive-degree half-space, use positive barycentric coefficients, and clear denominators. This expresses it as a nonnegative real combination of integral positive-degree line bundles, each having a globally generated positive power by curve Riemann–Roch. At genus-zero points a nonnegative-degree class is semiample because Pic(P¹)⊗R=R[O(1)]. At non-type-2 points the residue is zero. Thus every V_{S,ε} is genuinely LFS by GJR 7.8, on the same U. Enlarging S and decreasing ε gives a decreasing LFS net with infimum g.

For a sequence assume K is countably dense. Its strict affinoid algebras are separable Banach spaces. A countable dense set of algebra elements induces the spectrum topology because ||a(x)|−|b(x)||≤||a−b||. Hence Y and U are second countable. For each rational t, the open set {g<t} is the union of the open sets {H_S<t}. Choose a countable subcover, list all selected finite sets T_k, and let S_j be their cumulative unions with ∂Y. Then H_{S_j} decreases to g at every point, including −∞ values. The actual approximants are v_j=H_{S_j}+2⁻ʲQ. Nonnegativity of Q proves v_{j+1}≤v_j, and each term is LFS. This does not use Dini or assume a continuous target; the selected sequence need not be cofinal in the net.

For proper factors, compactness and boundarylessness follow from proper analytification, Berkovich 2.6.9. Connectedness over every complete extension F follows by applying Berkovich 7.1.1 to H_c⁰ of a constant finite sheaf on the proper geometrically connected algebraic P_F: analytic disconnection would supply a nonconstant locally constant section. A classical psh function restricted to any fibre over H(c) is constant by the maximum principle, GR 6.1. Local projective coordinate sections, or a rational point of P for products, give g=u∘s, prove that the descended target is classical psh, and show agreement on overlaps. Pull back v_j along f. GJR 5.4 gives strict real PL; GJR 7.9's ordinary-local maxima of logarithms of units pull back to prove LFS membership. Transition charts give exactly the same pulled functions.

After any analytic extension L/K, these already-defined functions remain LFS on the inverse-image ordinary open set, with unchanged inequalities and limit u∘π. All construction data were over K. This compatibility concerns pullbacks of the original target; fresh targets over arbitrary nonseparable L are not asserted to have sequences.
