Fix K=C((s)), |s|=exp(-1/8), t=s^8, v=-log|.|. Put T=(G_m²)^an, λ1=(t,2), λ2=(3,t), Λ=〈λ1,λ2〉 and x=(-log|z1|,-log|z2|). The geometric construction below is unconditional in the standard Berkovich foundational setting. The subsequent nonalgebraization conclusion is explicitly conditional on F_L, which is not established here.

The quotient X=T/Λ exists and is good, strict, smooth, separated, boundaryless, proper, geometrically connected and purely two-dimensional. Indeed x(λ(n)z)=x(z)+n. For an open rectangle B of widths less than one, x^-1(B) has disjoint deck translates and gives an ordinary open quotient chart. On overlaps the deck element is locally constant; multiplication by λ(n) gives analytic transitions satisfying the cocycle. For compact C,D only finitely many n can lie in x(D)-x(C), hence can make λ(n)C meet D. Relatively compact neighborhoods and separation of their finitely many relevant translates separate distinct orbits. For analytic separatedness, the inverse image of the diagonal is the locally finite disjoint union of closed translation graphs in T×_K T. Closed-immersion descent on the sheet atlas gives the closed diagonal. The strict compact polyannulus x^-1([0,1]²) surjects, so X is compact. For any point choose a center in (1/8 Z)² within 1/16 in each coordinate, an outer chart rectangle of width 3/4 and an inner closed rectangle of width 1/2. The inner inverse image is a strict affinoid neighborhood. This proves goodness and strictness. Ordinary-open torus charts prove smoothness, pure dimension two and empty relative boundary. Compactness, separatedness and empty boundary imply properness. The same charts and periods construct X_L over every complete isometric extension L/K. Increasing integral Laurent polyannuli exhaust T_L, so T_L and its quotient are connected. Regular components are locally finite and disjoint, giving integrality from connectedness. None of these arguments uses F_L.

There are τ:X→R²/Z² and a closed Gauss section i. At x the Laurent-polynomial seminorm is max_m |a_m|exp(-m·x). The graded Laurent domain proves multiplicativity, and evaluations prove continuity. Translation takes the Gauss point at x to that at x+n. Thus τ and i descend, τi=id, and i has closed image because its domain is compact and X is Hausdorff.

The missing foundation F_L is precisely: Pic(P_m,L)=0 for every integer m≥1, where P_m,L={|s|^m≤|z_j|≤|s|^-m, j=1,2}. No conclusion for an extension L is asserted without F_L for that extension. Van der Put, Rigid analytic spaces (1975–76), 3.4.8, prints the broader sufficient assertion that regular factorial affinoid B remains factorial after adjoining a restricted disc or unit-circle variable. His 3.4.9 extends a line bundle across an inner disc and applies this assertion; in the second-coordinate step its base is already a wide annulus. Gruson, Fibrés vectoriels sur un polydisque ultramétrique (1968), IV.5 Theorem 1, instead treats completed tensor products of discs T_(1,r), r in the divisible hull of the value group, and unit circles C. Its immediate proof uses a polynomial/Laurent polynomial graded algebra over gr(K). It does not establish arbitrary wide-annulus-base factoriality. A nonzero-width annulus is not a unit circle. Neither that narrower theorem nor Papikian's deferred-proof discussion discharges F_L.

Here is the complete remaining implication. Write a=-log|s|. For a unit f=Σ b_βz^β on P_m, its Gauss log norm N_f(y)=max_β(log|b_β|+β·y), y∈[-ma,ma]², and the norm of its inverse are convex and sum to zero. Hence N_f is affine. A Laurent term attaining the norm at an interior point supplies its integral slope α. Vertex inequalities give |b_β/b_α|exp(ma|β-α|_1)≤1. Therefore f=bz^α(1+g), with zero constant coefficient in g and ||g||_(P_j)≤|s|^(m-j) whenever j<m. Smallness on P_m's boundary is not claimed. On all R² the same argument forces every global torus unit to be exactly bz^α, since any different exponent would exceed the affine norm on a ray.

Assume F_L and choose frames e_m of a line bundle on P_m. Normalize each new frame by a scalar monomial so that e_(m+1)|P_m=(1+g_m)e_m with the preceding bounds. For fixed j, ∏_(m=j)^∞(1+g_m)|P_j converges to a unit: its first term is already a unit, and later errors have norm tending to zero, eventually less than one; inverse products also converge. The frames E_j=e_j∏_(m=j)^∞(1+g_m)|P_j satisfy E_(j+1)|P_j=E_j. They glue along the exhaustion, proving Pic(T_L)=0. Independent finite trivializations alone would not prove this.

Pull a line bundle on X_L to T_L and choose its global frame. A section satisfies θ(λz)=c_λz^(h_λ)θ(z). Its multipliers are torus units, hence monomials. Commuting deck actions give additive h and λ^(h_λ′)=(λ′)^(h_λ). If h_λ1=(A,B), h_λ2=(C,D), then t^C2^D=3^At^B. Valuation gives C=B; unique prime factorization in Q gives A=D=0. Thus h_λ1=(0,k), h_λ2=(k,0). If k≠0 choose n=(1,sign k), λ=λ(n) and h=(kn2,kn1). Then Q=v(λ^h)=2|k|>0. For a nonzero Laurent coefficient a_m, comparison gives a_(m+jh)=a_m c_λ^j λ^(-jm-hj(j+1)/2). Its valuation tends to -∞, contradicting Laurent coefficient decay on the fixed unit polyannulus. If k=0, every nonzero coefficient obeys λ_i^m=c_i. Two such exponents have λ_i^(m-m′)=1; their valuations force m=m′. The section is one scalar monomial, has no zeros and trivializes its descended bundle. In particular X_L has no nonzero effective analytic Cartier divisor.

For an arbitrary nonzero meromorphic F on X_L, its pullback to the smooth torus has an effective Cartier pole divisor P. Regular local rings make the local pole ideals invertible, and they glue. Pic(T_L)=0 trivializes O(-P); its inclusion into O gives a global analytic θ0 with divisor P. Then θ1=Fθ0 has no height-one poles, so normality, by intersection of height-one valuation rings on each normal affinoid, makes it analytic. The pair has no common height-one divisor. Deck invariance of P makes θ0(λz)/θ0(z) a meromorphic unit with zero divisor; normality of it and its inverse makes it an analytic torus unit. Both θi have the same multiplier. The recurrence therefore forces them to be scalar multiples of the same monomial. Consequently Γ(X_L,M)=L. This treats all meromorphic fractions, not selected characters.

Suppose a separated finite-type L-algebraic space Y has Y^an≅X_L. Conrad–Temkin, Non-archimedean analytification of algebraic spaces, Theorem 2.3.1, preserves and reflects smoothness; Example 2.3.2 identifies closed-point residue fields and completed local rings. The complete applied statements and immediate proofs were freshly read. Applying the associated rigid-space formulation, Y is smooth of dimension two. A nontrivial clopen decomposition would analytify to one; each nonempty finite-type piece has a closed point and nonempty analytification. Thus Y is connected. Its finitely many regular irreducible components are disjoint and open, so it is integral.

All Stacks 0ENA hypotheses hold: regular noetherian charts have only codimension-zero weakly associated points; finite-type noetherian étale charts satisfy 0BB1's finite-component conditions; separatedness implies decency and generic field monomorphisms. By 086U the schematic locus is dense and contains the generic point. Tags 0ENA and 0END identify global algebraic meromorphic functions with the generic field. Analytic passage needs an additional input. Ducros, Families of Berkovich spaces, 2.1.4, states that algebraic-to-analytic local-ring maps are regular, hence flat, citing Berkovich (1993), 2.6.2, and Ducros (2009), 3.3. These are honestly cited deeper published foundations. A local flat map is faithfully flat and preserves nonzerodivisors. For reduced noetherian affinoid A and finite faithfully flat étale B, localization gives products of fields, so Q(B)=Q(A)⊗_A B and faithful-flat descent gives the equalizer Q(A)→Q(B)⇉Q(B⊗_A B). This calculation alone is not a general analytic meromorphic-descent theorem. In the present case, the algebraic Cartier pole divisor, its invertible sheaf and its two regular sections descend algebraically and analytify by Conrad–Temkin §2.3. Nonzerodivisor preservation on scheme charts descends through faithfully flat analytic étale charts, making their ratio a global analytic meromorphic function.
