# An explicit replacement for the tropical barrier in Lyuhui Wu Lemma 6.6

This is a fresh, unreviewed root deduction. It has not been supplied to the
active P16.1 author. It does not establish the entire envelope theorem or
close original P16.1. Its source target is arXiv:2609.27610v1, literal
LF1964–1989. The source's supporting-affine expression must be read as
`chi(t') > l(t')`, rather than the printed `chi(t') > l(t)`.

The source uses two assertions imported from the unlocated APW26b preprint
to construct a strictly supporting convex function on tropical affine
space and obtain a uniform separation outside a neighborhood. Both of
these particular assertions have the following direct replacement.

Let T=R union {+infinity}, with its usual extended order topology, and let
k>=1. Choose a>0 with log(k+1)/a<=1. On T^k put

    chi(t) = (1/a) log(1 + sum_i exp(-a t_i)),
    exp(-a infinity)=0.

This is finite and continuous, and

    max(0,-t_1,...,-t_k) <= chi(t)
                         <= max(0,-t_1,...,-t_k)+1.

For the right inequality use the sum of k+1 nonnegative terms, bounded
above by k+1 times their maximum. The logarithm is well-defined because
the first term is one. On each finite coordinate stratum the function is
smooth and strictly convex in its finite coordinates.

Fix t in T^k and set

    Z = 1 + sum_i exp(-a t_i),
    p_0=1/Z,  p_i=exp(-a t_i)/Z.

Thus p_0>0, p_i>=0, sum_{i=0}^k p_i=1, and p_i=0 exactly when t_i=+infinity.
Let I={i:p_i>0}. Define

    C=chi(t)+sum_{i in I}p_i t_i,
    l_t(t')=C-sum_{i in I}p_i t'_i.

We ignore zero coefficients on infinite coordinates. If a coordinate
t'_i is +infinity for some i in I, l_t(t')=-infinity. All coefficients
of this affine function are nonpositive. The entropy identity gives

    C= -(1/a)sum_{i=0}^k p_i log(p_i) >= 0,
    0 log(0)=0.

The log-sum-exp variational formula, or the Gibbs inequality, gives

    chi(t') >= l_t(t'),
    chi(t')=l_t(t') if and only if t'=t.

Here is a proof including infinite coordinates. Define p'_i from t' by
the same rule as above. If t'_i=+infinity for some i in I, the assertion
is immediate. Otherwise

    a(chi(t')-l_t(t')) = sum_{i=0}^k p_i log(p_i/p'_i),

where terms with p_i=0 vanish. The right side is the relative entropy
of probability vectors and is nonnegative, with equality precisely when
p'=p. This equality criterion follows from log x<=x-1: apply it to
p'_i/p_i on the positive support of p; equality also forces no positive
p' mass outside that support. Since p_0,p'_0>0 and
p_i/p_0=exp(-a t_i), equality of the probability vectors is equivalent
to equality of all coordinates in T^k. In particular l_t(t)=chi(t).

It remains to prove the uniform gap outside every open neighborhood V
of t; pointwise strictness alone is insufficient on a noncompact space.
Write D(t')=chi(t')-l_t(t') in [0,+infinity]. For b>=0 let

    K_b={t':D(t')<=b}.

These sets are compact. To see this, let m=min_i t'_i when at least one
coordinate is finite. If m<0, then chi(t')>=-m and
l_t(t')<=C-(1-p_0)m. Consequently

    D(t') >= -p_0 m-C.

Thus K_b has all coordinates bounded below by -B, with
B=(b+C)/p_0. The case m>=0 and the all-infinite point cause no exception.
Moreover chi>=0 and D<=b give

    sum_{i in I}p_i t'_i <= b+C.

Hence for each i in I,

    t'_i <= (b+C+B sum_{j in I, j!=i}p_j)/p_i = A_i < infinity.

There is no upper bound needed for i outside I. Therefore K_b lies in
the compact product of intervals [-B,A_i] for i in I and [-B,+infinity]
for i outside I. On that product D is continuous with values in the
extended nonnegative real line: at an infinite coordinate with positive
p_i its value tends to +infinity, and coordinates with zero p_i do not
enter the affine term. Thus K_b is closed in that compact product.

The decreasing compact family K_b, b>0, has intersection K_0={t}.
For any neighborhood V of t, compactness of K_1 and the finite
intersection property imply K_b subset V for some 0<b<=1. Consequently

    l_t(t') < chi(t')-b  for every t' outside V.

This proves the exact separating-barrier property without either cited
APW26b assertion.

There is also a direct approximation observation relevant to the source's
subsequent Lemma 6.7. If h_1,...,h_k are regular functions on an affine
variety, set t_i=-log|h_i|. The variational formula expresses the pullback
of chi as

    sup_{p in the standard k-simplex}
       (sum_{i=1}^k p_i log|h_i| - (1/a)sum_{i=0}^k p_i log p_i).

Restrict p to rational probability vectors. The same supremum is
obtained, including where some h_i vanish: use rational approximations
inside the appropriate face, with p_i=0 on vanishing coordinates. For
such a vector, choosing a common denominator m expresses its affine
term as

    (1/m)log|product_i h_i^(m p_i)| + constant.

Finite maxima containing the p_0=1 term are finite continuous maxima of
logarithmic monomials. Increasing finite lists of all rational probability vectors
converge pointwise to the continuous chi pullback. Dini's lemma for an
increasing sequence on each compact subset gives local uniform
convergence. Each finite maximum has logarithmic growth controlled by
max(0,log|h_1|,...,log|h_k|) plus the entropy bound. This proves a local uniform approximation by finite logarithmic maxima.
It does not yet prove approximation in the source's actual FS class: its
Definition 3.15 requires rational coefficients and constants, a list that
generates the coordinate algebra, and its regular logarithmic psh class
requires decreasing FS approximants. The entropy constants above are
generally real, the monomials need not generate that algebra, and the
sequence just constructed is increasing. These are separate obligations,
not consequences of Dini's lemma.

The remaining source application must still be checked against its exact
FS/PSH_log_reg definitions, particularly closure under positive real
linear combinations of logarithmic functions and the infinite weighted
series in Lemma 6.7. This note does not claim that every other APW import,
every local/global psh step, the arbitrary-field descent, or the whole
finite-energy equation follows from this elementary barrier. These are
separate obligations for a fresh native reconstruction and review.

## Local decreasing rational polyhedral approximation of this particular chi

The following additional deduction concerns this explicit chi, not an
arbitrary tropical convex function. Fix a positive rational B and the
compact box Q_B=[-B,+infinity]^k. Enumerate all rational probability
vectors in the simplex, including p_0=1. For each rational vector p its
logarithmic affine term has rational slopes and a real entropy constant.
The maximum with the constant zero is finite continuous on Q_B, even
where some coordinates are infinite. Finite maxima over the first N
vectors increase to chi. The face-wise rational-density argument above
gives pointwise convergence, so Dini gives uniform convergence on Q_B.

For a positive rational epsilon, choose such a finite maximum within
epsilon/2 of chi, and replace each of its entropy constants by a rational
number below it and within epsilon/2. Keep the zero term. The resulting
finite maximum h_epsilon satisfies

    chi-epsilon <= h_epsilon <= chi on Q_B.

It is a finite maximum of affine expressions with nonpositive rational
slopes, with infinite-valued expressions omitted whenever a coefficient
is positive on an infinite coordinate. It is finite continuous, rational
PL, and convex on the open set {t_i>-B for all i}. Here is the PL check at
infinite coordinates: at a fixed point, terms whose support contains an
infinite coordinate tend to -infinity and can be discarded in a
neighborhood because the constant term remains available. Every
remaining term is a genuine rational affine function in a neighborhood.
There is also a finite cover: for each subset J of the finite list of
nonconstant terms, require all terms outside J to be strictly below zero
and every coordinate used by a term in J to be finite. These open sets
cover the domain; terms of value exactly zero can be included in J.

Choose epsilon_j=4^(-j), and set

    g_j=h_epsilon_j+2 epsilon_j.

On Q_B,

    chi+epsilon_j <= g_j <= chi+2 epsilon_j,
    g_(j+1) <= chi+epsilon_j/2 < g_j.

Thus these particular convex rational PL approximants decrease uniformly
to chi on the indicated open neighborhood, including its infinite
coordinate strata. Pulling back along a finite tropicalization proves
local decreasing tropical-PL approximation on each compact domain where
the regular functions have bounded absolute values. This discharges the
local polyhedral approximation obligation for this chi without invoking
the unlocated APW general approximation theorem.

This does not itself prove a decreasing sequence in the global FS class
of Definition 3.15. Nor does it verify the global regularization lemma,
real linear combinations, or infinite weighted sums. Those applications
are still separate. In particular the author's Definition 3.15(2)
literally imposes the lower growth bound `u <= v+O(1)` for an FS function
u, whereas the proof of Lemma 3.26 starts with the upper bound `v <= g`.
The lower growth definition also excludes a function taking -infinity
at an affine point, while Example 3.27 expressly allows such zero sets.
This is an identifiable convention inconsistency, not a proof that the
intended global envelope theorem is false. A reconstruction must fix and
state the convention and verify all dependent steps.
