# P13.1: direct Reeb-orbit comparison and an audited conditional envelope transfer

This is a partial result, not a solution of unchanged P13.1. The direct results below concern the actual FS, continuous psh and decreasing-limit psh spaces on a normal affine cone, for every pair of Reeb fields, including irrational fields. They do not establish preservation of finite energy or uniform energy differentiation. The envelope conclusions are expressly conditional on an indispensable, not independently established, projective envelope input from Lyuhui Wu's 2609.27610v1. No assertion of that preprint is treated as independently accepted.

The mathematical arguments are drafts. The exact finite diagnostic checks, source hashes and successful TeX builds do not verify them.

## 1. Setting and exact conventions

Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic zero with its trivial valuation, X=Spec R a normal affine cone with a good effective torus T action, o its vertex, and n=dim X. Write R=⊕ R_alpha, M=Hom(T,G_m), and q_eta(alpha)=<eta,alpha> for eta in the Reeb cone. All potentials in this note are the actual functions on the punctured analytification defined in Yueqiao Wu, 2406.13684v1, Definitions 2.9, 3.1 and 5.1: H(eta) consists of finite maxima

    phi(v)=max_j (-v(f_j)+lambda_j)/q_eta(alpha_j),

with nonzero homogeneous f_j whose ideal is m-primary; CPSH(eta) is their local uniform closure; PSH(eta) consists of nonidentically minus-infinite decreasing limits of CPSH(eta). No formal energy completion is used. Each function factors through the continuous torification r_T(v)(f)=min_alpha v(f_alpha). Comparisons are first made on the T-invariant punctured semivaluations and are extended to all points by r_T.

The fixed compact carrier is S=X_0^T, where X_0={v:v(m)=0}. These semivaluations are nonnegative on R: a negative value on a member of m would, on its powers, force v(m)=-infinity. Fix homogeneous algebra generators g_1,...,g_N of m, with weights beta_i. Put

    rho_eta=max_i -v(g_i)/q_eta(beta_i).

The ultrametric inequality applied to homogeneous polynomial expressions in the g_i shows that this also equals the supremum over all nonzero homogeneous f in m of -v(f)/q_eta(wt f). Indeed each monomial quotient is a degree-weighted average of the generator quotients and an ultrametric sum cannot exceed their maximum. Thus rho_eta is the source reference potential, and rho_eta=0 on S. The torification retraction takes X_0 onto S, and S is closed: its invariant locus is defined by the equalities v(f)=min_alpha v(f_alpha). Compactness follows from the fixed compact link. The cone dimension n, not n-1, occurs in the energy denominator.

The actual probability and energy conventions, used whenever an operator is mentioned, are

    MA_eta(phi_1,...,phi_{n-1})
      = vol(eta)^(-1) d'd''phi_1 ∧ ... ∧ d'd''phi_{n-1} ∧ d'd''rho_eta^+,
    rho_eta^+=max(rho_eta,0),
    E_eta(phi)=1/n sum_{j=0}^{n-1} ∫_S phi MA_eta(phi[j],rho_eta[n-1-j]).

Only the actual positive/model/mixed MA of the source is meant. The source asserts that these are probabilities supported on S. This is not a signed or formal inverse-potential construction. For the direct orbit and filtration lemmas no MA result is needed. For unbounded potentials the last displayed energy formula needs the actual fixed-Reeb finite-energy theorem; it is not taken as a definition of a new cross-Reeb operator.

## 2. Finite-generation constants, uniform in all weights

For xi,zeta set

    l=min_i q_zeta(beta_i)/q_xi(beta_i),
    u=max_i q_zeta(beta_i)/q_xi(beta_i),
    delta=max_i |log(q_zeta(beta_i)/q_xi(beta_i))|.

Then 0<l<=u<infinity and l q_xi(alpha)<=q_zeta(alpha)<=u q_xi(alpha) for every nonzero occurring weight alpha. To prove this, express a nonzero element of R_alpha as a homogeneous polynomial in the generators. Some nonzero monomial occurs, so alpha is a nonnegative integer sum of beta_i. Its ratio is a convex combination of the generator ratios, with coefficients the generator xi-degrees divided by q_xi(alpha). Relations do not affect this argument. In particular e^(-delta)<=l<=u<=e^delta.

These constants work for rational and irrational fields. On every compact subset K of the Reeb cone, a_K=min_{eta in K,i} q_eta(beta_i)>0, and

    delta(xi,zeta)<= max_i ||beta_i||_*/a_K · ||xi-zeta||,  xi,zeta in K.

This follows from |log a-log b|<=|a-b|/min(a,b). The constants depend on the cone generators and K, but not on the potential or on rational denominators. The same argument also gives, in the extended sense,

    u^(-1) T(v;xi)<=T(v;zeta)<=l^(-1) T(v;xi)

for the actual nonnegative homogeneous valuative supremum T on S. This is not a finite-energy estimate.

## 3. A presentation-independent orbit map on the actual PSH spaces

For a T-invariant semivaluation v and t in R, let a_eta(t)v be the torus translation with

    (a_eta(t)v)(f_alpha)=v(f_alpha)+t q_eta(alpha).

For arbitrary f it is the minimum over its homogeneous components. This is the Gauss torus action recorded in Wu24, Section 2.2, LF 230-242. It preserves the punctured invariant locus, is continuous in (eta,t,v), is invertible there, and different translations commute. Algebraically it is a multiplicative semivaluation: after selecting an additive total order on the character lattice, the extreme component among the components of least translated value in each factor gives an extreme product component of that value; terms of larger value cannot cancel it. The other valuation axioms follow from the minimum and the ultrametric inequality. The same construction with -t is its inverse on invariant points.

For each eta every invariant punctured v has a unique translate in S. In fact min_i(v(g_i)+t q_eta(beta_i)) is continuous and strictly increasing from -infinity to +infinity, since at least one generator is finite and all generator degrees are positive. Its unique zero is rho_eta(v). Hence a_eta(rho_eta(v))v belongs to S. This also proves that the uniform norm on S of a difference of eta-equivariant functions equals its supremum norm on the whole invariant punctured cone.

For any phi in H(xi) and t>=0, finite generation gives

    phi(v)-ut <= phi(a_zeta(t)v) <= phi(v)-lt.                   (3.1)

This follows term by term from the finite maximum: the slope of its j-th term is -q_zeta(alpha_j)/q_xi(alpha_j), in [-u,-l]. For t<0 the inequalities have the corresponding reversed bounds. The inequalities pass first through local uniform limits and then through decreasing pointwise limits. Consequently for any phi in PSH(xi), at any v either phi is minus-infinite on the entire zeta orbit, or t -> phi(a_zeta(t)v) is a finite Lipschitz, strictly decreasing function, with slopes between these two bounds and limits +infinity and -infinity at the two ends.

Define C_{xi,zeta}phi(v) to be the unique real t such that

    phi(a_zeta(t)v)=0,                                         (3.2)

when phi(v) is finite, and minus-infinity otherwise. This definition uses the function phi itself, not its FS presentation. For an FS presentation it gives exactly

    C_{xi,zeta}phi(v)=max_j (-v(f_j)+lambda_j)/q_zeta(alpha_j).   (3.3)

Indeed at t equal to the right side every numerator after translation is nonpositive and at least one is zero. All denominators in (3.2) are positive. In particular two equal FS functions have equal transported functions, even if their presentations differ. Their common zero set, equivalently their common nonpositive sublevel set with transverse orbit, determines the transport.

### Closure and decreasing limits

If phi is in CPSH(xi) and phi_j in H(xi) converge locally uniformly to phi, the transported FS functions converge locally uniformly to the root function (3.2). On a compact invariant set A, phi is bounded. By (3.1) all its roots and, eventually, the approximating roots lie in [-B,B], with B finite. The union {a_zeta(t)v:v in A, |t|<=B} is compact. An error epsilon in phi on this union gives a root error at most epsilon/l, by the lower slope bound. Thus the root function belongs to CPSH(zeta). Compact sets on the full punctured cone reduce to their compact torifications, so this is the required local uniform topology, not only a topology on S.

If phi_j decreases to phi, the root functions decrease. At a point with finite phi(v), set t=C_{xi,zeta}phi(v). Then phi_j(a_zeta(t)v) decreases to zero, and the lower slope bound gives

    0<=C_{xi,zeta}phi_j(v)-t<=phi_j(a_zeta(t)v)/l ->0.

If phi(v)=-infinity, the bound

    min(phi_j(v)/l,phi_j(v)/u)
      <= C_{xi,zeta}phi_j(v)
      <= max(phi_j(v)/l,phi_j(v)/u)

forces the transported limit to minus-infinity. Thus the definition of PSH itself shows that C_{xi,zeta}phi is in PSH(zeta). It is not identically minus-infinite because phi has a finite point. This proves the actual decreasing-limit passage, without invoking an energy completion.

### Inverse, cocycle, order and normalization

Transport preserves the zero set. Since phi(a_xi(t)v)=phi(v)-t for a finite phi(v), root transport back along xi recovers phi(v). At a minus-infinite point the whole xi orbit and transported function stay minus-infinite. The same reasoning on a theta orbit proves

    C_{zeta,xi} C_{xi,zeta}=id,
    C_{zeta,theta} C_{xi,zeta}=C_{xi,theta}.

Order of functions implies order of their roots. We obtain order bijections H(xi)<->H(zeta), CPSH(xi)<->CPSH(zeta), and PSH(xi)<->PSH(zeta), commuting with decreasing nets.

On S all v(f_j)>=0. An FS function therefore has its maximum at the trivial semivaluation v_triv, and the same fact passes to continuous and decreasing limits. A nontrivial psh function has finite value there: otherwise it would be minus-infinite on S and, by xi equivariance and the slice description, everywhere. Thus the normalization phi(v_triv)=sup_S phi=0 is legitimate, and is preserved by C because v_triv itself is the zero on its zeta orbit.

For any phi<=0 on S, including functions with supremum strictly below zero,

    phi/l <= C_{xi,zeta}phi <= phi/u,
    e^delta phi <= C_{xi,zeta}phi <= e^(-delta) phi.            (3.4)

The first bounds follow by taking the zero in (3.1) at negative time. For finite values they imply

    |C_{xi,zeta}phi-phi| <= (e^delta-1)(-phi).                 (3.5)

Both functions are minus-infinite at the same points. For any fixed Radon probability mu on S, if phi<=0 is in L^1(mu), the transported function is in L^1(mu), with

    |∫ C_{xi,zeta}phi dmu-∫phi dmu|
        <=(e^delta-1)∫(-phi)dmu.                              (3.6)

This is ordinary domination for one fixed measure; it makes no assertion about moving MA measures. On potentials in [-M,0], (3.5) gives a uniform error (e^delta-1)M, independent of presentation and rational denominator.

The map is nonlinear and does not commute with constants. On A^2 with xi=(1,1), zeta=(1,2), transport of rho_xi+c is max(log|x|+c,(log|y|+c)/2). For c>0 this takes c at v_triv and c/2 at v(x)>c, v(y)=0. It is not a constant shift of rho_zeta. In particular none of the assertions above identifies Monge–Ampère operators or their energies through an affine linear change of potential. Normalized representatives can be transported; an additive or energetic functoriality is not proved.

## 4. Carrier-only density, with the quantifier repaired

Wu24 Theorem 3.5 states full-link density, but its proof at LF 433-435 assumes that distinct full-link points differ on a homogeneous function. That assumption fails. For A^2 and its full diagonal torus, v=ord_{x-y} and w=v_triv both lie in X_0 and have value zero on every nonzero monomial, hence have the same torification. They differ on x-y. All homogeneous FS potentials and all their limits take equal values at v and w. Thus such tests do not separate arbitrary full-link points.

They do separate points of S. If distinct invariant points v,w differ on a homogeneous f, choose L>0 large enough that the function

    u_{f,L}=max(-v(f)/q_xi(wt f),rho_xi-L)-rho_xi

distinguishes them. Its restriction is max(-v(f)/q_xi(wt f),-L). This also works when one value is infinite. The numerator maximum is FS: include the fixed generators with constants -L q_xi(beta_i).

For completeness the usual lattice argument is valid on S. Same-ray sums of CPSH functions have the harmonic-sum polarization. For rational xi this follows by taking common homogeneous degrees and multiplying the two finite lists of sections. For arbitrary xi, approximate xi by rational fields and transport fixed approximating FS lists; (3.1)-(3.3) give the required local uniform convergence. Diagonal approximation gives the CPSH assertion. Therefore

    V_xi={a(phi-psi): a>0, phi,psi in CPSH(xi)}|_S

is a real vector space containing constants and stable under max: for u=a(phi-psi), v=b(theta-chi), use

    max(u,v)=max(a phi+b chi,b theta+a psi)-(a psi+b chi),

whose two terms have the same harmonic-sum polarization xi/(a+b); dividing each by a+b puts them in CPSH(xi). The real lattice Stone–Weierstrass theorem now proves density in C(S). This proof uses only homogeneous-point separation on S. Measures and convergence tests in this note are confined to this fixed carrier.

## 5. Quantitative cumulative-filtration comparison

This is an algebraic theorem for an actual nonnegative, multiplicative, T-invariant, linearly bounded filtration F as in Wu22, Definition 3.5. It applies to irrational fields. Define

    R_eta(m)=⊕_{q_eta(alpha)<=m} R_alpha,
    N_eta(m)=dim R_eta(m),
    A_eta(m)=sum of its filtration jumping numbers,
    V_eta=lim n! N_eta(m)/m^n,
    S_eta=lim A_eta(m)/(m N_eta(m)).

The last limit is the supplied Wu22 Theorem 3.12/Corollary 3.13, with nonnegative jumps and linear boundedness as hypotheses. A_eta is also ∫_0^infinity dim F^t R_eta(m) dt by elementary finite-dimensional filtered linear algebra, so it is monotone under inclusion of these graded subspaces. Existence along real m follows from integer m by floor/ceiling and monotonicity; the normalizing powers have ratio tending to one. The volume limit is the actual cone volume.

The weight bounds give

    R_xi(m/u) ⊂ R_zeta(m) ⊂ R_xi(m/l).

Hence

    u^(-n)V_xi <= V_zeta <= l^(-n)V_xi,
    u^(-n-1) S_xi V_xi <= S_zeta V_zeta
                             <= l^(-n-1) S_xi V_xi.

Dividing positive volumes yields the explicit uniform result

    (l^n/u^(n+1)) S_xi <= S_zeta <= (u^n/l^(n+1)) S_xi,
    e^(-(2n+1)delta) S_xi <= S_zeta
                                  <=e^((2n+1)delta) S_xi.   (5.1)

The constants are independent of F, of its number of generators, and of rational denominators. If S_xi=0, (5.1) implies S_zeta=0; no logarithm of zero is taken. Similarly linear-growth thresholds compare by l,u. This proves quantitative continuity of an actual filtration invariant, not of E on the completed potential spaces. Negative or signed filtrations are outside the statement. Shifting all FS constants to make jumps nonnegative at xi twists by q_xi and is not a constant shift after transport to zeta.

### A normalization obstruction in the supplied 2022 text

Take X=A^2, xi=(1,1), and F^t R spanned by monomials of total degree at least t. Its finite-type norm is chi(x)=chi(y)=1. The induced FS function is rho_xi+1, so the probability energy formula gives E_xi=1. For every positive integer m,

    N(m)=(m+1)(m+2)/2,
    A(m)=sum_{d=0}^m d(d+1)=m(m+1)(m+2)/3,
    S_m=A(m)/(mN(m))=2/3.

Thus S=2/3 and E=1. Wu22 Theorem 5.15 at LF 1357-1372, and Theorem 1.1, print E=S. This is incompatible with the actual definitions, also with Lemma 3.16(b), which prints S=n/(n+1) times the degreewise average. The factor (n+1)/n would repair this example and the rational degreewise comparison; no general corrected identity is silently assumed here. Wu24 Proposition 7.4 explicitly uses this factor for rational Dirac energies. Therefore (5.1) is not promoted to an analytic energy theorem by citing the printed E=S identity.

### Energy sign audit

For actual same-Reeb CPSH potentials, integration by parts and the supplied Hodge inequality give the concave-energy bounds

    ∫(phi-psi) MA(phi) <= E(phi)-E(psi)
                                  <= ∫(phi-psi) MA(psi).     (5.2)

Indeed the mixed integrals in the difference formula decrease from the psi endpoint to the phi endpoint, and E(phi)-E(psi) is their average. Wu24 Proposition 7.3 at LF 1249 prints the first inequality with the opposite direction. The dual tangent formula requires (5.2), not that printed sign. The formal first variation in Proposition 4.2 differentiates actual same-Reeb energy; it is not differentiation of an obstacle envelope.

The sign chain in Wu24 Lemma 6.4 at LF 1015-1017 is also not used. A direct replacement for normalized nonpositive FS potentials phi_0,...,phi_{n-1} is as follows. Set tau=(sum_i phi_i)/n. Positivity and the energy formula give

    ∫tau MA(tau) >= n E(tau) >= -n max_i(-E(phi_i)).

The first inequality follows because the other n-1 energy summands are nonpositive. Meanwhile tau<=phi_0/n, and positivity of the mixed expansion gives

    ∫tau MA(tau) <= (1/n)∫phi_0 MA(tau)
       <= ((n-1)!/n^n) ∫phi_0 MA(phi_1,...,phi_{n-1}).

Here the inequality reverses when multiplying the positive measure domination by phi_0<=0. Combining gives a finite-dimensional constant bound

    ∫phi_0 MA(phi_1,...,phi_{n-1})
       >= - n^(n+1)/(n-1)! · max_i(-E(phi_i)).

This repaired fixed-Reeb estimate uses actual positive mixed MA only. It does not prove a cross-Reeb limiting theorem.

## 6. Audit of Lyuhui Wu 2609.27610v1

The author is Lyuhui Wu, distinct from Yueqiao Wu of Wu22/Wu24. The cached preprint is v1, arXiv-stamped 23 September 2026. Its body Theorem 7.1, LF 2697-2699, states that a geometrically unibranch projective variety over a discretely or trivially valued equicharacteristic-zero non-Archimedean field has the envelope property. Completeness is the standing convention and is explicit in Theorem 6.5. Our trivially valued k is complete. A normal projective quotient over our algebraically closed characteristic-zero k is geometrically unibranch; this does not require the quotient to be smooth or to have quotient singularities.

The following proof route was inspected, not merely the abstract:

* On a countable-dense-subfield ground field, Section 7 resolves singularities, uses the smooth envelope theorem, connected geometric fibres and Proposition 2.24 to descend a usc upper envelope, then invokes Theorem 6.5 to turn weak psh into global psh.
* Theorem 6.5, LF 1938-1959, assumes an ample rational line/model datum and a countable dense subfield. Its proof uses chart potentials rho_i, Theorem 6.3, logarithmic regularization, and a c>1 approximation before c decreases to 1. The reduction at LF 1942 from an arbitrary ample semipositive model form to a trivial embedding form is not justified in that paragraph; an adequate reference-change or reduction argument would be needed for that generality.
* Theorem 6.3 is proved by a singular-locus induction and finite maps to smooth affine targets, using Propositions 6.12/6.14, Lemma 6.15, Runge criteria and the strict psh barrier from Lemmas 6.6/6.7. Lemma 6.6 imports APW26b Proposition 5.7 and Lemma 5.11. The construction also uses Lemma 3.4, whose first three assertions import APW26b Proposition 3.10, Proposition 3.15(3), and Theorem 5.14. The APW dependencies enter an actual proof step, not only bibliography context.
* Section 7, LF 2718-2747, separately removes the countability restriction. It descends the projective variety, model form and a PL obstacle to a countable field, then descends each psh PL competitor to a larger countable field. Orthogonality, MA pushforward under base extension, positivity and domination identify the pulled-back envelope. For trivial valuations, admitting a countable dense subfield forces the field itself to be countable, so Theorem 6.5 cannot simply be applied to arbitrary k.
* The competitor quantifier at LF 2731 must include psi in PSH∩PL and psi<=f; the literal unrestricted PL sentence cannot be valid. The support equality displayed at LF 2742-2743 needs only an inclusion, because the earlier premise gives Supp(MA)<=the contact locus. These corrections do not supply the missing local inputs or the MA base-change proof.

The supplied bibliography at LF 2830-2833 gives APW26a and APW26b only as “Preprint (2026)”, without arXiv IDs. Their proof texts are not among the permitted five sources. This note neither refutes nor independently establishes the envelope theorem. It retains an explicit premise (Env): the projective envelope theorem, with its imported local results, reference reductions, orthogonality, base-change compatibility and domination proof steps valid in the stated setting. All uses of (Env) below are conditional. ## 7. Rational quotient and conditional transfer to the fixed carrier

Let eta=s eta_0 be rational, eta_0 primitive integral and s>0 rational. The positive eta_0 grading defines V_eta=Proj R. This is projective: a sufficiently divisible Veronese of the finitely generated positive graded ring supplies a projective embedding. It is normal: on D_+(f), R_f is normal and its degree-zero subring is normal. An element integral over that degree-zero subring is integral over R_f, belongs to R_f by normality, and its degree-zero character makes it invariant, hence in the degree-zero subring. These affine charts cover V_eta. Since k is algebraically closed of characteristic zero this normal variety is geometrically normal and geometrically unibranch. The hypotheses concern normality and unibranchness, not a claim that its singularities are finite quotient singularities. It may have arbitrary normal singularities inherited from X.

Choose d sufficiently divisible that L_d=O_{V_eta}(d) is an actual ample line bundle and the Veronese section datum is valid. The actual rational polarization for eta is

    L_eta=(1/(s d)) L_d.

This can equally be remembered as the ample orbiline datum of the Seifert G_m bundle (X minus o)->V_eta, with its finite stabilizers and boundary multiplicities. Those multiplicities must not be discarded by identifying O(1) with an honest line bundle without passing to d. The torus T/G_m(eta_0) remains as a residual torus, and L_d has its induced linearization. For n>=2, normality gives the usual section-ring identification from the punctured cone by Hartogs; alternatively sufficiently divisible section degrees suffice for every FS list. The n=1 effective normal cone has no nonproportional Reeb pair to consider.

Wu22 Lemmas 5.11-5.12 and Proposition 5.13 identify V_eta^an with the G_m-invariant slice X_0^{G_m(eta_0)}, and identify homogeneous common-degree FS functions with global FS metrics on L_eta. The common degree is obtained by taking powers to a multiple of the finitely many eta_0-degrees; their common zero locus is empty on Proj. The 1/s scaling above agrees with q_eta=s q_eta0 and the cone proportional law. The projective dimension is n-1. The full V_eta^an corresponds to the larger G_m carrier, while residual torification gives the smaller, fixed S=X_0^T. Confusing these two carriers would change the assertion.

Assume (Env). For h in C(S), lift it to H=h∘r_T on the larger rational slice, hence on V_eta^an. This is a continuous residual-torus-invariant obstacle. The global envelope U=P_{L_eta}(H) is continuous psh by (Env). It is residual T(k)-invariant because translating any competitor preserves both the polarization/reference and H.

Here group invariance is converted to homogeneous approximation rather than assumed to be torification invariance. Approximate U uniformly by global FS metrics on sufficiently divisible L_d. Replace each such metric by the supremum of its residual T(k)-translates. If a section decomposes into residual weight components s_alpha, then

    sup_{t in T(k)} |sum_alpha t^alpha s_alpha|(v)
       = max_alpha |s_alpha|(v).

The upper bound is ultrametric. For the lower bound choose t avoiding cancellation in the leading residue Laurent polynomial; a nonzero Laurent polynomial over an extension of the infinite field k cannot vanish on all T(k). Thus the supremum reduces to a finite FS maximum of the components. Their common zero set stays empty. The uniform error to invariant U is unchanged. These invariant FS approximants correspond to homogeneous cone FS functions and factor through r_T; therefore U descends to a CPSH(eta) function on S.

Every cone PSH(eta) competitor below h gives, via its defining decreasing net, a global residual-invariant projective psh competitor below H. Conversely the descended U is a cone competitor and is below h. Thus, conditionally,

    P_eta h := sup{phi|_S:phi in PSH(eta), phi|_S<=h}

is exactly the restriction of U and lies in CPSH(eta)|_S. This proves the transfer at a fixed rational field under (Env), including the ample rational datum and residual invariance. It does not compare two varying quotient varieties by pretending that they are the same projective variety.

## 8. Conditional, quantified envelope comparison including irrational fields

The previous conditional rational conclusion and the direct orbit map imply a conditional irrational conclusion without qualitative approximation constants. Let h in C(S), subtract sup h and put M=osc_S h, so -M<=h<=0. Any competitor for P_xi h can be replaced by max(phi,rho_xi-M), still a competitor and now in [-M,0] on S. Maximum stability follows directly from finite maxima and decreasing defining nets.

Set kappa=e^delta-1. For each such bounded competitor, (3.5) shows that C_{xi,zeta}phi-kappa M is a zeta competitor below h. Therefore

    P_xi h <= P_zeta h+2 kappa M.

If zeta is rational and (Env) is assumed, P_zeta h is itself a bounded continuous psh potential in [-M,0]. Transport back and subtract kappa M to obtain a xi competitor below h. Hence

    |P_xi h-P_zeta h| <=2(e^delta-1)M,
    ||C_{zeta,xi}P_zeta h-P_xi h||_infinity
                                      <=3(e^delta-1)M.       (8.1)

Choose rational zeta_j tending to an arbitrary xi. The finite-generation constants give delta_j->0. Each transported envelope is an actual CPSH(xi) function, and (8.1) gives uniform convergence on S to P_xi h. By xi equivariance, this is local uniform convergence on the punctured cone. The defining closedness of CPSH(xi) now gives P_xi h in CPSH(xi). Repeating the two-sided argument proves the first bound in (8.1) for every pair xi,zeta, rational or irrational. Restoring the obstacle constant is legitimate because P_eta(h+c)=P_eta(h)+c at each fixed eta; no constant-equivariance of C is used.

This supplies a conditional envelope calculus with constants uniform over all obstacles of bounded oscillation. At each fixed eta it also supplies the standard 1-Lipschitz obstacle comparison by monotonicity and constants. For a decreasing net of bounded usc obstacles h_j with limit h, provided their envelopes are psh and a nontrivial limiting competitor exists, the usual order proof gives P_eta h_j down to P_eta h: the decreasing psh limit is below h and every competitor below h lies below each envelope. Without those hypotheses no assertion about an unbounded obstacle envelope is made. This fixed-Reeb order argument does not identify energies or prove integrability for changing MA measures.

## 9. Exact unresolved analytic passages

Even granting (Env), the results above do not yield the required uniform differentiability. The following assertions are not proved here:

1. A cross-Reeb estimate for E_zeta(C_{xi,zeta}phi) for every phi in E^1(xi), with constants independent of bounded truncation and FS presentation. In particular C(E^1(xi))=E^1(zeta) is not established.
2. Control of actual positive mixed MA products through bounded cross-Reeb approximants, followed by uniformly integrable singular tails. Weak convergence alone cannot justify integrals of unbounded transported potentials; (3.6) is for a fixed measure only. Wu24 Theorem 6.3 assumes one fixed xi and decreasing nets whose limits have finite energy there. It cannot be applied before the unproved first assertion, or to nets with two changing Reeb fields.
3. An actual cone obstacle orthogonality theorem and a uniform derivative error such as

       |E_xi^down(phi+t f)-E_xi(phi)-t∫_S f MA_xi(phi)|
           <= C(K,f) t^2

   for xi in a compact Reeb slice K and phi in the required finite-energy domain, with a genuinely defined obstacle extension E_xi^down and constants uniform in phi and bounded approximants. No such extension or error is introduced by fiat here.
4. The imported proof steps in (Env), especially the APW26b local assertions used by Lemma 6.6/3.4 and the countability-removal base-change argument, are not independently established from the five sources.

Boucksom–Jonsson 1801.08229v4 Theorem 8.5 does give a uniform O(t^2) error for all E^1 potentials on one projective variety at a fixed ample class. Its proof, LF 4919-4943, constructs an actual positive mixed measure for omega+epsilon omega-prime and uses its mixed intersection masses.
