# Exact result and retained task

The original P16.1 target is unchanged. It asks for the genuine finite-energy
equation on every reduced irreducible geometrically unibranch projective
variety over every algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero
field, in every dimension, for every ample line bundle. The proposed
singular envelope proof has not been completed in this reconstruction.
Accordingly this paper is a **partial result**, and its candidate flag is
false. Ample real numerical Cartier classes are included in the genuine
global partial theorem below; the new local algebraic constructions use
rational polarization data.

The principal new analytic result proved here is a curve statement with
relative tropical contact supports. It does not require an ambient convex
extension. Together with a determinant calculation it proves slicing-weight
independence and the exact radial trace equality required in Wu 6.12, whenever
the preceding open-trace extension has been obtained. The latter antecedent
is kept visible. A second result supplies a bounded genuine model test with
positive Monge--Ampère mass at any prescribed divisorial point. This removes
the *Dirac equation* antecedent from a local domination argument, but cannot
replace its local mass, comparison and determination hypotheses.

We reconstruct the other explicit algebraic inputs and the global operator
separately. We also derive the calibrated facet sign in a finite harmonic
chart, and explain precisely what remains to apply it to the whole stated
local class. We do not claim that these inputs alone prove higher-dimensional
bounded local/global equality, Rossi's principle, or singular envelopes.

# The actual general-variety global operator

## Models, energy, and measures before envelopes

Fix an algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero field $k$,
a reduced irreducible projective $k$-variety $X$ of dimension $n$, and
$\omega\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)\subset N^1(X)_{\mathbb R}$. Thus $\omega$
may be $c_1(L)$ for any ample line bundle. Put $V=(\omega^n)>0$.
BJ's conventions, LF435--469, permit reduced nonnormal varieties. Section 7
returns to this scope after the smooth-only b-divisor subsection. Its
operator is already available on this singular domain, independently of
geometric unibranchness or an envelope property.

A test configuration is closed integrally in its *prescribed generic
fiber*: on a chart $A$ over $k[t]$, use the integral closure in
$A\otimes_{k[t]}k(t)$. Absolute normalization can change a nonnormal
generic fiber and is not substituted. Finiteness of this relative closure,
equivariance and preservation of the generic fiber are BJ 1.10--1.12.
The vertical Serre criterion 1.13 follows from the determinant trick for
the polar ideal: integral closedness forces every associated polar point
to have invertible maximal ideal, hence to be a vertical DVR; conversely
vertical depth two excludes poles at higher-codimension associated points.
Thus vertical components $E$ have well-defined
$b_E=\operatorname{ord}_E(t)>0$ and
$v_E=b_E^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_E|_{k(X)}$.

On a common relatively integrally closed configuration, nef model metrics
represented by vertical Cartier divisors $D_i$ have the genuine mixed raw
measure
$$
 M^{\mathrm{raw}}=\sum_E b_E
       \bigl((\omega_{1,\mathcal X}+D_1)|_E\cdots
             (\omega_{n,\mathcal X}+D_n)|_E\bigr)\delta_{v_E}.             \tag{1}
$$
Intersection uses fundamental cycles, including multiplicities. The
weights are nonnegative because the restricted classes are nef. The
central fiber cycle is rationally equivalent to a generic fiber, so its
total mass is $(\omega_1\cdots\omega_n)_X$. BJ 4.3 is the genuine model
nef/PSH criterion; it is not a smooth BFJ criterion applied to a quotient.

Compactification over $\mathbb P^1$ defines the symmetric pairing
$$
 \langle(\theta_0,\phi_0),\ldots,(\theta_n,\phi_n)\rangle
       =\prod_i(\theta_{i,\overline{\mathcal X}}+D_i).
$$
Projection formula gives independence of the dominating configuration.
Translation of entry $i$ by $c$ adds $c(\prod_{j\ne i}\theta_j)_X$.
BJ 7.1 extends the pairing to ample psh tuples by the infimum over
dominating model majorants. Monotonicity is model intersection positivity.
For upper semicontinuity, choose one majorant tuple with pairing below a
specified threshold, and use continuity of $\sup(\phi_i-\psi_i)$ to bound
nearby tuples by those majorants plus small constants. The translation
rule bounds the change. This proves the extension and its decreasing-net
continuity without envelopes.

Define the actual trivial-reference energy and its finite-energy space by
$$
 E_\omega(\phi)=\frac{\langle(\omega,\phi)^{n+1}\rangle}{(n+1)V},\qquad
 \mathcal E^1(X,\omega)=\{\phi\in\PSH(X,\omega):E_\omega(\phi)>-\infty\}.
                                                               \tag{2}
$$
Then $E_\omega(0)=0$ and $E_\omega(\phi+c)=E_\omega(\phi)+c$ exactly.
It is increasing, concave, usc and decreasing-continuous. BJ 7.8 proves
finite mixed pairings on $\mathcal E^1$ tuples: after subtracting constants,
compare the finitely many ample classes by positive multiples. Its Lemmas
7.9--7.10 bound the energy of a sum in terms of individual energies, and
expansion of the sum bounds each mixed pairing. The cone spans the space of
class/potential differences, so BJ 7.14 extends the pairing multilinearly.

For $h\in\operatorname{PL}(X)_{\mathbb R}$ prescribe
$$
 \int h\,dM^{\mathrm{raw}}=
 \langle(0,h),(\theta_1,\phi_1),\ldots,(\theta_n,\phi_n)\rangle.          \tag{3}
$$
In the nef-psh case this is a positive linear functional, bounded by its
mass times $\|h\|_\infty$. Genuine model-PL density in $C^0(X^{\rm an})$
and Radon representation give its unique positive Radon extension.
Multilinearity gives the signed case. These are BJ 7.18--7.19, including
agreement with (1), exact mass, mixed integrability of finite-energy tests,
and decreasing continuity. In particular no operator is defined by
assigning a desired inverse measure.

Weighted decreasing continuity is BJ 7.21. The integral with test $\phi_0$
is the pairing **containing $\phi_0$ minus the pairing containing zero**.
Both pairings converge. Applying the increasing-lsc weighted-measure lemma
to a translated $-\phi_0$ proves weak convergence of the weighted measures.
This also corrects the reversed signs in LF4119--4123. Quantitative mixed
pairing estimates in Section 7 give strong continuity. Positive finite-energy
mixed measures charge no pluripolar sets. The diagonal probability measure
is
$$\MA_\omega(\phi)=V^{-1}(\omega+dd^c\phi)^n;$$
its raw version has mass $V$, and $\MA_\omega(0)=\delta_{v_{\rm triv}}$.

## Comparison, uniqueness, and the exact target condition

At a strict winning model component, the maximum model agrees with the
winning model near that component. Their intersection weights agree.
Weighted decreasing continuity with test
$\max(\phi,\psi)-\psi$ extends this to BJ 7.40:
$$
 \mathbf1_{\{\phi>\psi\}}\MA(\max(\phi,\psi))
    =\mathbf1_{\{\phi>\psi\}}\MA(\phi).                                \tag{4}
$$
Its limiting citation is weighted 7.21, rather than mere unweighted
continuity. Equal masses and (4), applied with $\psi-\epsilon$, give
$$
 \MA(\psi)(\{\phi<\psi-\epsilon\})
    \le\MA(\phi)(\{\phi\le\psi-\epsilon\}).
$$
Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives the comparison inequality
$$\int_{\{\phi<\psi\}}\MA(\psi)\le\int_{\{\phi<\psi\}}\MA(\phi).       \tag{5}$$
The strict winning locus carries the measure of the winning potential.

Use
$$I(\phi,\psi)=\int(\phi-\psi)(\MA(\psi)-\MA(\phi)),\qquad
 J_\psi(\phi)=E(\psi)-E(\phi)+\int(\phi-\psi)\MA(\psi).$$
Mixed intersection negativity gives $I\ge0$ and
$I/(n+1)\le J_\psi\le nI/(n+1)$. The energy difference formula is
$$
 E(\phi)-E(\psi)=\frac1{(n+1)V}\sum_{j=0}^n
 \int(\phi-\psi)(\omega+dd^c\phi)^j\wedge(\omega+dd^c\psi)^{n-j}.       \tag{6}
$$
BJ 10.3--10.4 prove that $I=0$ implies a locally constant difference.
In our irreducible scope, normalize equal suprema and test the separation
estimate with $\delta_v$ and $\delta_{v_{\rm triv}}$. Divisorial Diracs have
finite dual energy by BJ 11.1. Equality at divisorial points then gives
global equality by BJ 4.22. Thus equal measures give **one** additive real
constant, before EP. Global domination 10.6 also precedes EP: replace
$\phi$ by $\rho=\max(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal E^1$. Under the a.e.
inequality its $I$ with $\psi$ is
$-\int(\rho-\psi)\MA(\rho)\le0$. Separation and the a.e. equality remove
the resulting constant. This only applies to genuine global potentials.

For a positive Radon raw target $\nu$ the exact required mass is $V$.
For $\mu=\nu/V$, define
$$
 E^\vee_\omega(\mu)=\sup_{\psi\in\mathcal E^1(X,\omega)}
       \left(E_\omega(\psi)-\int\psi\,d\mu\right).                     \tag{7}
$$
Finiteness of (7) is equivalent to integrability of every $\mathcal E^1$
potential, by BJ 9.20 in its irreducible scope. Here is the operative proof
with the signs fixed. Normalize $\sup\psi=0$ and put $J=\sup\psi-E$.
If integration were unbounded below on $J\le C$, choose $\psi_j$ with
$\int\psi_j\,d\mu\le-2^j$. The decreasing convex partial sums
$\sum_{j\le m}2^{-j}\psi_j+2^{-m}0$ have energy at least $-C$; their
limit has finite energy and integral $-\infty$, a contradiction. The mixed
positive expansion gives
$$I(t\psi,0)\le t[1-(1-t)^n]I(\psi,0)\le nt^2 I(\psi,0),\quad0\le t\le1.$$
Together with $J\asymp I(\cdot,0)$, scaling by $t=J(\psi)^{-1/2}$ when
$J\ge1$ proves $-\int\psi\,d\mu\le A\sqrt{J(\psi)}+B$. Subtracting $J$
proves (7). Conversely a finite (7) excludes an infinite negative integral
of any finite-energy potential. A solution necessarily has finite (7):
mixed integrability and the tangent inequality from (6) show that it
computes the supremum. Arbitrary support is allowed throughout.

## The conditional existence theorem, with every antecedent retained

Assume the genuine envelope property (EP) for $\omega$: the usc supremum
of each nonempty bounded-above family of global $\omega$-psh functions
is global $\omega$-psh. Then every target (7) has an $\mathcal E^1$ solution,
unique modulo one constant. Conversely surjectivity onto all such targets
implies EP. This is the actual general-variety BJ 12.8, not the smooth
sufficient theorem 12.9.

EP supplies compactness by BJ 5.11. Bounded divisorial evaluations give a
Tychonoff subnet. Its usc tail suprema are psh by EP, and BJ's
nonnegligibility at divisorial points identifies their decreasing limit
with the limiting divisorial values. It is nontrivial and sup-normalized.
This proves compactness downstairs, without assuming smoothness.

The replacement for smooth differentiation is BJ 8.5:
$$E^\downarrow(\phi+\epsilon f)=E(\phi)+\epsilon\int f\MA(\phi)
                           +O_f(\epsilon^2),\quad f\in\operatorname{PL}_{\mathbb R}, \tag{8}$$
uniformly over $\phi\in\mathcal E^1$. Its proof decomposes $f$ into two
auxiliary ample model potentials, uses model-volume estimates, and expands
mixed positive measures of fixed intersection mass. The volume ratio
changes by $O(\epsilon)$ and its prefactor supplies another $\epsilon$.
Decreasing approximation passes to finite energy. Apply it to both signs.
For continuous $f$, uniform PL approximation with error $\eta$ changes
energy by at most $|t|\eta$. The derivative squeeze has endpoints
$\int f_m\MA(\phi)\pm\eta$, not two minus signs. EP is required to
identify $E^\downarrow(\phi+tf)$ with $E(P_\omega(\phi+tf))$ for unbounded
$\phi$. Thus the two-sided envelope-energy derivative follows under its
actual antecedent.

Take a sup-zero model maximizing sequence for (7). BJ 9.15 bounds $J$
by its dual deficit plus $E^\vee(\mu)$. Compactness gives a weak subnet;
$J$ lsc puts the limit in $\mathcal E^1$. BJ 9.19 gives $L^1(\mu)$
convergence on bounded-$J$ sets. Its proof approximates the target first by
MA of maximizing model potentials, using (8) and the Legendre inequality
before attainment. The integration error bound 9.17 is uniform on those
sets, while model measures are finite sums of divisorial evaluations.
The correct identity for absolute differences is
$|\phi_i-\phi|=2\max(\phi_i,\phi)-\phi_i-\phi$. Target integration
therefore converges and energy usc gives attainment. For a maximizing net
of deficit $\delta_i$, (8) yields
$$\epsilon\int f(\MA(\phi_i)-\mu)\le\delta_i+C_f\epsilon^2.$$
Divide by $\epsilon>0$, pass to the net limit, let $\epsilon\downarrow0$,
and use $-f$. A constant maximizing net at the attained potential gives
the genuine equation. The raw objective is $VE-\int\phi\,d\nu$; its
translation invariance uses precisely raw mass $V$.

BJ 10.14 gives target completeness before EP and 12.7 gives the
bi-Lipschitz MA embedding. Surjectivity makes it onto and gives strong
$\mathcal E^1/\mathbb R$ completeness; adding supremum differences gives
full completeness. Conversely, bounded increasing finite maxima of model
competitors are Cauchy because (6) gives
$J_{\phi_i}(\phi_j)\le n(E(\phi_j)-E(\phi_i))$ for $i\le j$.
Completeness and divisorial determination identify the limit with the
envelope; the continuous-obstacle characterization gives EP. Hence this
paper's conditional theorem cannot satisfy the original existence task.
For $n=0$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}k$, $V=1$, potentials are constants and
$E(c)=c$; the equation is immediate.

# Corrected auxiliary local conventions

For an integral affine algebra $R$ over a complete field, write
$$\operatorname{AFS}(R)=\left\{\max\{\lambda,c_i\log|f_i|+C_i\}_i\right\},$$
where the listed $f_i$ generate $R$ as a ground-field algebra, $c_i>0$
are rational, and $\lambda,C_i$ are rational. This is precisely W 3.15(1).
It has a finite floor, so it is finite and continuous. All regularizable
auxiliary statements here use **upper growth** $u\le h+O(1)$, not W
3.15(2)'s lower bound $h\le u+O(1)$. On $\mathbb A^1$,
$\max(\log|z|,-m)\downarrow\log|z|$ is an actual AFS sequence. No finite
floor can be below $\log|z|(0)=-\infty$. This disproves the literal
lower-growth membership, not a corrected upper-growth theorem.

For a finite PL function on a relative tropical image, its finite affine
selections obey W 3.1(1): every summand is defined and finite on its stated
neighborhood. At an infinite coordinate its coefficient must be zero.
The corrected contact condition is
$$f(t')\ge\ell(t')+C\quad\hbox{near }t,\qquad f(t)=\ell(t)+C.              \tag{9}$$
The constant is the same in both expressions. The literal omission of $C$
in W 3.1(3) makes the condition vacuous for a continuous function with a
local lower bound. We use (9), explicitly as an auxiliary correction;
BJ's genuine global PSH and finite-energy problem are not redefined.

Finite maxima and positive rational sums of AFS functions remain AFS.
Distribute finite maxima and clear logarithmic coefficients into regular
monomials. Keep each single-generator branch obtained by choosing floors
in all other factors in its original form. Its coefficient stays positive
rational and its intercept is rational, so the original generator list is
retained. The sum of floors is rational. Their differences form a rational
vector lattice since
$\max(a-b,c-d)=\max(a+d,c+b)-(b+d)$. Constants belong to this difference
lattice: if $h$ is AFS, $h+c-h=c$ for rational $c$.

# Relative tropical supports on smooth curves

## Lisse membership and the outgoing-slope proof

**Curve theorem.** Let $\Omega$ be complete, algebraically closed and
nontrivially valued, of residue characteristic zero, and let $C$ be a smooth
algebraic $\Omega$-curve. A finite continuous function $b$ on an analytic
open is locally subharmonic if it is a finite PL selection in regular
log-norm coordinates and satisfies (9) on the relative tropical image.
Consequently a decreasing sequence of these functions on a common open
has a subharmonic or identically $-\infty$ limit on each connected component.

Here is a proof without ambient convex smoothing. At a point where the
coordinates are finite, shrink so the finitely many relevant regular
functions are analytic units. At an infinite coordinate, restrict to a
finite affine chart containing the image point. Every selected affine
form on that chart has coefficient zero in that coordinate. This removes
that coordinate on a smaller neighborhood. Identically zero pulled
coordinates are removed in the same way, rather than assigned a finite
logarithm. All remaining coordinates are units.

For a strictly affinoid neighborhood inside this unit locus, choose one
strictly semistable model. Existence and its finite skeleton are Wa
2.2--2.3. Th 2.2.24 shows that the logarithm of a unit factors through the
retraction to this skeleton: the Shilov maximum inequality on each
retraction fiber, applied both to the unit and its inverse, is equality.
Its edge restriction is piecewise affine and its interior weighted
Laplacian is zero. Equivalently Th 2.3.20 makes each unit log harmonic.
All the finitely many units use this *same* model; refinements preserve
their pullbacks. Compactness allows a finite common subdivision.

The function $b$ also factors through the skeleton. On a subdivided edge,
its coordinates are affine. A continuous selection of finitely many
affine functions on an interval can change selection only at intersections
of two distinct affine functions. There are finitely many such intersections;
coincident affine functions give the same value. Subdivide there. This
proves piecewise affineness, including real breakpoints which need not be
formal vertices. It is precisely Wa 2.7 lisse membership. Local lisse
membership glues; finite compact covers give the same property on smaller
strict affinoids. At a type I point after discarding zero coordinates,
unit norms are constant on a small disk. At a type IV point finitely many
unit-log germs are constant by Th 2.3.21's proof, LF3604--3607. A continuous
finite selection of constant values is locally constant on a connected
smaller neighborhood. These cases do not create a skeleton coefficient.

At any interior skeleton point $x$, pull back the relative support in
(9) to a harmonic function $H=\ell(-\log|g_i|)+C$. Then $b-H$ has local
minimum zero. Every outgoing one-sided edge slope satisfies
$\partial_e(b-H)\ge0$. The unit-log balancing identity gives
$\sum_e w_e\partial_e H=0$, with positive edge weights. Therefore
$$\Delta b(\{x\})=\sum_e w_e\partial_e b
                   =\sum_e w_e\partial_e(b-H)\ge0.                   \tag{10}$$
At a type III breakpoint there are two outgoing directions and the same
argument applies. At any other interior point the Laplacian is zero.
Affinoid boundary coefficients are not tested for subharmonicity on the
open set. Wa 2.11, or Th 3.4.4 with its maximum-principle proof, now gives
subharmonicity. The sheaf property gives the conclusion on the open.
Th 3.1.8(ii) gives the decreasing-limit statement, including the separate
identically $-\infty$ alternative.

An algebraic map and a complete field extension pull back the regular
functions and preserve their norm values at the restricted analytic point.
They preserve the finite selections and the relative support inequality
on a smaller image. Thus this theorem applies to the curve restrictions
of the corrected local decreasing tropical-PL class. It does not require
that the relative support extend convexly to an ambient Euclidean open.

For clarity about that distinction, let $S$ be the three positive rays
generated by $(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)$. Let $f$ be zero on the first two and
$f(t,t)=t$ on the third. It has relative local supports, including zero
at the origin. A convex ambient extension would satisfy
$f(t/2,t/2)\le(f(t,0)+f(0,t))/2=0$, a contradiction. This unbalanced
image is not asserted to be a tropicalization of a boundaryless variety;
it only prevents an invalid general ambient-extension inference.

Wa Section 4 additionally assumes a smooth **proper** algebraic curve.
When using Wa 4.3 to identify a lisse subharmonic function with a positive
CLD current, or Wa 4.7 in the converse direction, first compactify the
smooth curve by the normalization of its projective function-field model.
Over the perfect $\Omega$ this regular curve is smooth, and the original
curve is an open subcurve. Functions are only considered on the original
open, without extension across its new boundary. Thus Wa's exact scope
is met. The slope proof itself uses Section 2 and Th and does not require
4.7 as an extra antecedent.

## The radial finite-trace boundary equality

Let $F$ be any complete residue-characteristic-zero field, $a\in C(F)$
on a smooth curve, and $q:D\to C$ a finite map from a disjoint union of
smooth curves, each dominating $C$. Suppose $v$ and $w$ near the center
and its finite preimage are usc decreasing limits of corrected relative
support PL functions on common neighborhoods. Identically $-\infty$
restrictions are permitted and treated separately. Assume that on a
punctured algebraic neighborhood
$$v(t)=\sum_{x\mid t}\dim_{H(t)}(\mathcal O(D)\otimes H(t))_x\,w(x).     \tag{11}$$
Then (11) holds at $a$.

For nontrivial $F$, take its completed algebraic closure $\Omega$. For
trivial $F$, first extend to $F((T))$ with $|T|=e^{-1}$ and then take the
completed algebraic closure. These are complete algebraically closed
nontrivially valued extensions; residue characteristic remains zero.
The curve theorem applies after extension and turns the pulled sequences
into subharmonic functions or identically $-\infty$ restrictions. Artinian
trace base change is proved independently below, so (11) pulls back
without an AFS-regularizability antecedent. The geometric fiber now consists
of rational points.

For a subharmonic function $h$ at a rational center, let $\xi_R$ be the
single boundary Gauss point of a sufficiently small closed coordinate
disk, $R\in|\Omega^\times|$. Disk comparison with the constant harmonic
function gives $h(0)\le h(\xi_R)$; Th 3.1.9 guarantees finite boundary
values for a nontrivial subharmonic function. Since $\xi_R\to0$, usc gives
the reverse upper limiting inequality. Hence
$h(\xi_R)\to h(0)$, also if $h(0)=-\infty$. This is a boundary Gauss
limit, not an ordinary punctured-neighborhood limit. The identically
$-\infty$ case is immediate.

Choose target coordinate $t$ and source coordinates $z_i$ at the finitely
many centers. On small disks
$t=c_i z_i^{e_i}U_i(z_i)$, $U_i(0)=1$, where $e_i$ is the source DVR
order of $q^*t$. Shrink until $|U_i-1|<1$. Residue characteristic zero
implies that every nonzero rational number has norm one. Thus the binomial
series for $U_i^{1/e_i}$ converges; its coefficients are bounded. The
change $y_i=z_iU_i^{1/e_i}$ has linear coefficient one and is invertible
on a smaller disk by a contraction for the higher terms. We obtain
$t=c_i y_i^{e_i}$.

A target Gauss point of small radius $r\in|\Omega^\times|$ has one
source Gauss point in each disk, of radius
$R_i=(r/|c_i|)^{1/e_i}$. Rescale to unit radii. The residue equation
$Y^{e_i}=\alpha T$ is Eisenstein at $T$ over $\widetilde\Omega(T)$, so
its residue degree is $e_i$. The polynomial field extension already has
degree $e_i$; consequently this is a single analytic fiber point of
actual fiber-algebra dimension $e_i$. It is not an unweighted count.
At the center the Artinian dimension is likewise $e_i$.

Finite properness excludes other preimages for small target disks: the
closed complement of chosen open source disks has closed image avoiding
the center. Shrink the target into its complement. Its Gauss points also
avoid the finite algebraic exceptional set in (11). Therefore
$$v(\xi_r)=\sum_i e_iw(\xi_{R_i}).$$
The radial limits prove the center equality. If one limit is $-\infty$,
usc bounds the finitely many other terms above on small neighborhoods,
so the sum tends to $-\infty$. No subtraction of infinities occurs.
Aggregate geometric Artinian dimensions and descend the equality to $F$.
This proves the required boundary identity without arbitrary-$H(s)$
affine regularization.

# Determinants, base change, and fixed slicing weights

## Finite-flat traces are actual affine FS functions

Let $A$ be an integral affine algebra over a complete characteristic-zero
field, and $B$ a reduced finite locally free $A$-algebra of rank $d$.
At an analytic base point $s$, put $F=H(s)$. The actual fiber algebra is
$$B\otimes_A F=\prod_{y\mid s} B_{s,y},\qquad
 m_y=\dim_F B_{s,y},\qquad\sum_y m_y=d.$$
Nilpotents and residue degrees are retained. Its residue fields are finite
complete valued extensions of $F$, with their unique extended norms. Define
$N(u)(s)=\sum_y m_yu(y)$.

For $u=\max(\lambda,c_i\log|f_i|+C_i)\in\operatorname{AFS}(B)$, choose
integer $D>0$ clearing the $c_i$. Let $b_0=1$, $b_i=f_i^{Dc_i}$,
$w_0=D\lambda$, $w_i=DC_i$. Multiplication by $\sum_iT_i b_i$ on the
locally free module $B$ induces a scalar endomorphism on $\det_A B$.
Consequently its determinant is a globally defined polynomial
$$P(T)=\det(\operatorname{mult}_{\sum T_i b_i})
      =\sum_{|\beta|=d}a_\beta T^\beta\in A[T],\qquad a_{(d,0,\ldots,0)}=1. \tag{12}$$
A globally free basis is unnecessary.

After a complete algebraically closed scalar extension of $F$, each local
Artinian factor has a residue-field composition series. Multiplication by
$\sum T_i b_i$ acts on each quotient by its residue scalar. Therefore
$$P_s(T)=\prod_x\left(\sum_iT_i b_i(x)\right)^{\ell_x}.$$
Above an original $y$ the total geometric lengths are $m_y$: its separable
residue field gives $[H(y):F]$ embeddings, each repeated by the original
Artinian length. This is exactly the required analytic fiber dimension.

For every positive real polyradius $r$, the weighted Gauss norm is
multiplicative. To prove this without a value-group restriction, choose
the lexicographically largest exponent attaining each polynomial's
maximum weighted norm. In the coefficient at their sum, there is exactly
one product of maximal terms: any other maximal exponent in either factor
is smaller, and their sum is then smaller. All remaining contributions
have strictly smaller weighted norm. They cannot cancel that contribution.
The reverse bound is the ultrametric triangle inequality. Isometric scalar
extension preserves coefficient norms term by term. Applying this to (12)
with $r_i=e^{w_i}$ gives
$$
 N(u)=\max_\beta\left\{D^{-1}\log|a_\beta|
                         +D^{-1}\sum_i\beta_iw_i\right\}.              \tag{13}
$$
Zero coefficients are discarded. The $T_0^d$ coefficient gives the rational
floor $d\lambda$. All logarithmic coefficients are positive rational.

The $a_\beta$ need not generate $A$, so (13) alone is not AFS membership.
Choose algebra generators $g_j$ of $A$. Their images in $B$ are polynomials
in the source generators $f_i$. Let $Q=\max(0,\log|f_i|)$ and
$c_* =\min_i c_i>0$. Floors and intercepts give a rational $M_0$ with
$u\ge c_*Q-M_0$. A polynomial expression of degree $e_j$ gives
$\log|g_j\circ\pi|\le e_jQ+L_j$, where $L_j$ bounds coefficient norms.
Choose rational $\eta_j>0$ with $\eta_je_j\le c_*$ and rational $M_j$
large enough. Then $u\ge\eta_j\log|g_j\circ\pi|-M_j$ everywhere. Summing
on the fiber gives
$$N(u)\ge d\eta_j\log|g_j|-dM_j.$$
Adding these dominated branches leaves (13) unchanged and supplies the
original base generators. This proves **actual** AFS membership.

Fixed finite fiber sums preserve supplied decreasing AFS sequences, also
when a summand tends to $-\infty$. They have local upper bounds from their
first continuous term, so $+\infty-\infty$ never occurs. Upper growth
descends along finite maps: for an integral source coordinate a monic
equation gives the root bound
$\log|f|\le\max_{j<m}(\log|a_j|/(m-j))$; polynomial degree bounds on its
base coefficients give upper AFS growth. Generic finiteness of a trace
limit is a separate obligation: a fiber all of whose values are finite
is sufficient, while finiteness at just one source point is insufficient.
If the limit is finite continuous, Dini applies on compact inverse images
and the trace error is at most $d$ times the source error. Traces are then
continuous. Dini is not applied to unbounded singular limits.

For example, $A=k[a]$, $B=k[z]$, $a=z^2$, and
$u=\max(\log|z|,-1)$ give $P=T_0^2-aT_1^2$ and
$N(u)=\max(-2,\log|a|)$. At zero the one-point nilpotent fiber has dimension
two and contributes $2u(0)=-2$. Split points or a degree-two residue field
give the same value away from zero. An unweighted point count fails.

## Artinian trace base change without any regularization antecedent

For a scalar-extended base point $s'$ above $s$, the fiber is
$(B\otimes_A H(s))\otimes_{H(s)}H(s')$. Original idempotents first split
this algebra by original $y$; each factor then splits into refined local
Artinian factors. Their dimensions over $H(s')$ sum to the dimension of
the original factor over $H(s)$. Every refined norm restricts to the
original norm since finite residue-field norm extension is unique. Hence
$$\sum_{x\mapsto y}\dim_{H(s')}B'_{s',x}=m_y,\qquad
 N_{\pi'}(a\circ r)(s')=N_\pi(a)(s)                                    \tag{14}$$
for every function $a$ on the original fiber, including $-\infty$ values
with local upper bounds. This also holds for nonflat finite fibers when
their actual dimensions are used. It proves the base change used in (11)
without assuming any FS approximation of $w$.

## Fixed nonflat weights from global FS tests

Now retain the original algebraically closed trivial $k$, take integral
affine $Y$ of dimension $n\ge1$, and a finite surjection $\pi:Y\to S$,
where $S$ is an affine open of $\mathbb A_k^n$. Its ring $A$ is normal;
put $B=k[Y]$, $K=\operatorname{Frac}A$, $L=\operatorname{Frac}B$ and
$d=[L:K]$. Choose a dense principal open $W$ on which the map is étale.

For $s\in S^{\rm an}$ take its $F=H(s)$-rational lift $s'$ and a line
through $s'$ generically in $W_F$. It exists since $F$ is infinite and a
nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on every line direction through a point.
Intersect the line with $S_F$. Its preimage is cut in the geometrically
integral $Y_F$ by $n-1$ equations. The height bound and the affine
dimension formula give every minimal component dimension at least one;
finiteness gives at most one. Thus no point is an isolated minimal
zero-dimensional component. Reduce and normalize this curve preimage.
The resulting $D\to C$ is finite. Each component dominates the smooth
line, and its module over a target DVR is torsion-free; hence it is flat.
The generic étale fiber is unchanged and the rank is $d$. Normal curves
over the perfect $F$ are smooth. Normalization covers every underlying
point over $s'$, giving positive integers $m_{y,C}$ at the original finite
analytic fiber points, totaling $d$.

For a global AFS test $h$ on $Y$, replace determinant (12) by the generic
field norm $N_{L/K}(\sum T_i b_i)$. Its coefficients extend across the
nonflat locus. Indeed all conjugates of the $b_i$ are integral over
$A[T]$, so their product is integral; it lies in $K[T]$. Normality of
$A[T]$ puts the norm polynomial in $A[T]$. The generator argument for
(13) gives a global AFS function $v_h$ on $S$, agreeing with the actual
trace on $W$. Pull $v_h,h$ to the normalized slicing curve. Their relative
support PL sequences and (14) meet the curve theorem, so (11) gives
$$v_h(s)=\sum_{y\mid s}m_{y,C}h(y).                                    \tag{15}$$
The norm polynomial does not depend on $C$ or $W$.

Global AFS tests determine individual weights on this finite fiber. Their
difference lattice contains rational constants and separates points:
different analytic points have different regular-function seminorms.
Truncate the distinguishing log norm at a sufficiently low rational floor,
and add very low generator branches dominated at the two points. This is
an actual AFS test separating them. The closure of the rational lattice
on a finite set contains real constants and real affine rescalings. For
each pair it interpolates two values; finite minima and maxima then
interpolate every prescribed finite-set function. Thus it is all real
functions on the fiber. Equality of test integrals in (15) gives equality
of each individual weight. Write the resulting fixed weights $m_y(\pi)$.
They are independent of the line, the étale open, and the input tested.
At a nonflat point they are **normalized slicing degrees**, not necessarily
dimensions of the original nonflat fiber algebra.

It follows unconditionally that the fixed trace of a supplied decreasing
global AFS sequence is decreasing global AFS-regularizable, provided the
trace limit is generically finite. Global FS tests themselves have a
continuous trace through the whole nonflat locus by the same norm formula.

For a weak local input $u$ with proper birational witness
$\mu:\widetilde Y\to Y$, first shrink $W$ so $\mu$ is an isomorphism above
$\pi^{-1}(W)$. The exceptional image is a proper closed subset of $Y$;
its finite image in $S$ is closed of smaller dimension. Choose the line
*after* this shrink. Its generic lift through $\mu$ extends at every
closed point of its normalized smooth curve by properness and the DVR
valuative criterion; separatedness glues the extensions. The curve theorem
applies to the pullback of $u\circ\mu$, also allowing identically
$-\infty$ restrictions. If a locally subharmonic, for example globally
regularizable, trace extension $v$ on the smooth base has already been
constructed agreeing on $W$, (11) proves its value at $s$ equals the fixed
weighted trace of $u$. The weights cannot depend on $u$ because (15)
proved their independence first. A line chosen before the witness need
not lift. This repair removes the arbitrary-$H(s)$ regularization
antecedent specifically from the boundary equality, **not** from the
preceding open-trace extension or its smooth weak regularization input.

The retained domain really can produce such nondiscrete internal fields.
Consider the map
$$Y=\operatorname{Spec}k[a,b,y,z]/(b^2-a^3)\longrightarrow
S=\operatorname{Spec}k[a,y,z].$$
It is finite flat with basis $1,b$.
At the target point with kernel $(a)$ and monomial weights
$v(y)=1,v(z)=\sqrt2$, its residue completion contains the nondiscrete
value group $\mathbb Z+\sqrt2\mathbb Z$; the pigeonhole principle gives
nonzero elements arbitrarily close to zero. The line $a=\tau$, with
$y,z$ fixed at the rational lift, has normalized preimage
$\tau=t^2,b=t^3$ over that completion. This is an affine chart of the
projective cusp curve times two projective lines, a geometrically
unibranch projective threefold with ample product polarization. The
radial theorem applies to the needed equality over this field, but no
arbitrary-field AFS theorem is inferred.

# Global entropy supports and localization

## Uniform simplex approximation and the real logarithmic support

For nonzero regular functions $f_i$ including affine generators, let
$x_i=\log|f_i|\in\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}$ and choose rational $a>0$
with $\log(r+1)/a\le1$. Set
$$\chi(x)=a^{-1}\log(1+\sum_i e^{ax_i}).$$
It lies between $\max(0,x_i)$ and that maximum plus one. On the probability
simplex $\Delta$, with $x_0=0$ and $F(p)=a^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^r p_i\log p_i$,
Gibbs' inequality gives
$$\chi(x)=\sup_{p\in\Delta}\{\sum_{i\ge1}p_ix_i-F(p)\}.                 \tag{16}$$
The elementary proof is $\log z\le z-1$ applied to the probability ratios.
At $x_i=-\infty$ restrict to the face $p_i=0$.

Take rational triangulations of $\Delta$ with arbitrarily small mesh,
respecting every face and retaining its vertices. The lower convex envelope
of their lifted vertex values lies above $F$ by convexity and at most
$\eta$ above it by uniform continuity and barycentric interpolation in
one small simplex. Its dual is the finite vertex maximum. Therefore
the corresponding maximum $h$ obeys $\chi-\eta\le h\le\chi$ on **all**
finite $x$, without a bounded-coordinate restriction. Round each intercept
downward to a rational value with error at most $\eta$, retaining the
zero vertex intercepts. The error is at most $2\eta$ globally. Faces give
the same inequalities at negative infinite coordinates. Clear rational
slopes into regular monomials; the vertex branches retain zero and each
original generator. These are actual AFS functions. With errors
$e_m=4^{-m}$, $h_m+2e_m$ decreases to $\chi$ globally, since it is at least
$\chi+e_m$ while the next term is at most $\chi+2e_{m+1}<\chi+e_m$.

At contact $x$, let $p_0=1/Z$, $p_i=e^{ax_i}/Z$, and omit zero coefficients.
Its support is
$$\ell(x')=-a^{-1}\sum p_i\log p_i+\sum_{p_i>0}p_ix_i'.$$
Its gap times $a$ is relative entropy $\sum p_i\log(p_i/p'_i)$, with
unique zero at $x$, including faces. For uniform separation use
$t_i=-x_i$, $C=-a^{-1}\sum p_i\log p_i$, and
$D(t')=\chi(-t')-C+\sum p_i t_i'$. If $D\le b$, then
$\min t_i'\ge-(b+C)/p_0$ and $\sum p_i t_i'\le b+C$.
Thus all coordinates have a finite lower bound; positive-support
coordinates have finite upper bounds, and the others lie in compact
extended intervals $[-B,+\infty]$. The gap is lsc there, with value
$+\infty$ at a positive-support pole. Its sublevels are compact, and their
intersection as $b\downarrow0$ is the single contact point. They eventually
lie in any contact neighborhood, proving the strict uniform gap outside.

On countable $k$, enumerate nonzero regular functions and let $\rho_j$
be these finite barriers for the generators and first $j$ functions.
For $v=\max(0,\log|z_i|)$, polynomial degree bounds give
$\rho_j\le C_j(v+1)$. Choose positive rational $\gamma_j$ with
$\gamma_j\rho_j\le2^{-j}(v+1)$. The series $\rho=\sum\gamma_j\rho_j$
converges locally uniformly and has upper growth. The upper partial sums
$$U_N=\sum_{j\le N}\gamma_j\rho_j+2^{-N}(v+1)$$
decrease to it. Finite sums admit uniform global AFS approximants
$U_N-e_N\le W_N\le U_N$, $e_N=4^{-N}$. Then $W_N+2e_N$ decreases
globally to $\rho$. Omitting a fixed term has the same proof.

For the real support $s=C+\sum_i\alpha_i\log|f_i|$, $\alpha_i>0$,
choose $d_i>0$ with $\log|f_i|\le d_iv$ globally. Let rational
$q_{i,m}\uparrow\alpha_i$, rational $B_m\downarrow A=\sum d_i\alpha_i$
strictly from above, and rational $C_m\downarrow C$. Define
$b_m=B_m-\sum d_iq_{i,m}>0$ and
$$s_m=C_m+\sum_i q_{i,m}\log|f_i|+b_mv.$$
Away from poles,
$$s_m-s=(C_m-C)+\sum_i(\alpha_i-q_{i,m})(d_iv-\log|f_i|)+(B_m-A)v\ge0,$$
$$s_m-s_{m+1}=(C_m-C_{m+1})+(B_m-B_{m+1})v
              +\sum_i(q_{i,m+1}-q_{i,m})(d_iv-\log|f_i|)\ge0.$$
At poles both untruncated values are $-\infty$, so compare values directly.
For rational $\epsilon_m\downarrow0$,
$H_m=\max(s_m,\epsilon_mv-m)$ is actual AFS and decreases to $s$.
Expand $v$ and clear powers; the second branch supplies the rational floor
and original single-generator branches. Combining this sequence with the
remaining barrier series gives a global AFS sequence for
$\rho'=\gamma_j\ell+\sum_{i\ne j}\gamma_i\rho_i$.
The initial seminorm topology supplies a finite-list tropical neighborhood
inside any prescribed open. Hence $\rho'$ is finite at contact, agrees
there with $\rho$, and is below $\rho-\delta$ outside that open. This proves
the exact supporting construction without singular analytic-PL gluing,
an APW premise, or singular EP.

## Actual poles repair the localization estimate

Let $R$ be integral affine over trivial $k$, $f\ne0$, $L$ nonempty compact
in $Y_f^{\rm an}$, and $x$ an $R$-polynomial-hull point in $Y_f^{\rm an}$.
Choose $M$ with $|\log|f||\le M$ on $L\cup\{x\}$. Suppose
$u\in\operatorname{AFS}(R)$, $h\in R_f$ and
$$a\log|h|+b\le\max(u,-c\log|f|)+O(1),\qquad a,c>0.$$
Then
$$a\log|h(x)|+b\le\sup_L(a\log|h|+b)+2cM.                              \tag{17}$$

On the finite normalization $\overline R$ in the common fraction field,
evaluate on every prime divisor ray $\tau\operatorname{ord}_D$.
Regular functions from $R$ have nonnegative orders, so their AFS maximum
is uniformly bounded above and below on the ray. Division by $\tau$
forces $a\max(-\operatorname{ord}_D h,0)\le c\operatorname{ord}_D f$
whenever $h$ has a pole; no pole is allowed if $\operatorname{ord}_D f=0$.
These function-field valuations are points of $Y_f^{\rm an}$ even when
their centers lie on $f=0$.

For positive integers $p,q$ with $q/p>c/a$, $z=h^pf^q$ has nonnegative
order at every divisor of $\operatorname{Spec}\overline R$. The normal
codimension-one intersection criterion gives $z\in\overline R$, hence
integrality over $R$. As $z\in R_f$, the finite algebra $B=R[z]$ satisfies
$B_f=R_f$. One $f^N$ kills $B/R$. Thus $z^\ell f^N\in R$ for every
$\ell\ge1$. Apply the *original* $R$-polynomial hull inequality to this
regular function, multiply logarithms by $a/(p\ell)$, and use the two
bounds by $M$. The error is
$2a(q/p+N/(p\ell))M$. Let $\ell\to\infty$ and $q/p\downarrow c/a$.
If $h(x)=0$ the result is immediate; if $h$ vanishes on $L$, the same
hull test forces $h(x)=0$. This proves (17), including all $-\infty$
endpoints and nonnormal algebras. Finite maxima give its AFS version.

The printed denominator bound fails for $f=z^2$, numerator $g=z$,
$h=g/f=z^{-1}$, $u=0$, $a=2,c=1$: the growth maxima agree although
the claimed $ar\le c$ fails. Here $g\notin(f)$ yet vanishes everywhere
on $Z(f)$. Actual pole orders repair this intermediate claim. To deduce
the full regularizable maximum principle one still needs controlled
approximants on $Y_f$ with growth at most $2\max(u,-c\log|f|)+O(1)$.
For a supplied such sequence, (17) gives $4cM$. Decreasing usc suprema on
compact $L$ converge to the limiting supremum by nested closed superlevel
sets; ordinary Dini for a singular limit is unnecessary. Letting
$c\downarrow0$ proves the maximum principle under that approximation
antecedent. That antecedent is not proved by (17) itself.

# Algebraic collars and their exact limitation

For the projective closure of an affine embedding, choose very ample $L$,
a complete rational FS reference $\theta$, and distinguished section
$s_0$ with $Y=X\setminus Z(s_0)$. Arrange the proper AFS exhaustion
$g=-\log\|s_0\|_\theta\ge0$. An actual AFS expression
$a=\max(\lambda,q_j\log|f_j|+A_j)$ has
$f_j=F_j/s_0^{d_j}$ with global homogeneous sections $F_j$; this follows
by homogenizing its polynomial expressions in the affine embedding.
Fix rational $c,C,R$, $c,R>0$, with $a-cg\le C$ on the entire shell
$R\le g\le R+1$. Set $\psi=\max(a-cg,C)$ for $g\le R$ and $C$ outside.

Here is an unconditional representation in BJ's genuine *rational* model-PL
lattice. Let $G=\min(g,R+1)$ and
$H_j=\max(\log\|F_j\|_{d_j\theta},-M_j)$. Clipped section norms are
differences of actual basepoint-free FS metrics: relative to the trivial
metric, the numerator maximum is
$\max(\log\|F_j\|_{\rm triv},d_jp_\theta-M_j)$, and its second branch
contains the complete reference system. Subtract $d_jp_\theta$. Tensor
powers clear rational data. The same applies to $G$ using $s_0$.
Choose rational $M_j$ so that
$-q_jM_j+(q_jd_j-c)G+A_j\le C$ at both endpoints of $[0,R+1]$.
Then clipping cannot alter a branch above the floor. Let
$$A_*=\max\{C,\lambda-cG,\ q_jH_j+(q_jd_j-c)G+A_j\},$$
choose rational $K_*>0$ with $C+K_*\ge\sup_X A_*$, and set
$$F_*=\min\{A_*,C+K_*(R+1-G)\}.                                      \tag{18}$$
All signed coefficients are rational, as required by BJ 2.1's vector
lattice. On $g\le R$ the cap is inactive; on the shell $A_*=C$; for
$g\ge R+1$, including the whole boundary divisor, $G=R+1$ and the cap is
$C$. Thus $F_*=\psi$ everywhere. No arbitrary analytic PL is declared to
be equivariant algebraic model PL.

For these particular collars, the local metric weights are finite convex
maxima of algebraic unit logarithms wherever nonconstant, and a
semipositive-reference constant near the outer boundary. Convex smoothing
on finite coordinates gives positive CLD curvature there. At a zero
coordinate the floor removes its branch on a smaller neighborhood.
This proves current positivity for this explicit class, without the
general relative-image positivity gate below.

Represent the total metric by its equivariant configuration and extend
to $k((t))$, $|t|=e^{-1}$. BJ flag-ideal generators $t^{-\ell}f$ have
norm $e^\ell|f(p(x))|$ at **every** extended analytic point. Thus the
formal-model metric is the actual pulled-back metric, not only its value
on the Gauss section. Integral closure of an ideal does not change these
norms: a monic integral equation and the ultrametric root bound control
each added integral element. Relative closure keeps the original nonnormal
generic fiber. Clearing rational coefficients gives an actual model bundle.
CLD 18.8.6 preserves positive curvature under this field extension.

To obtain a central curve degree inequality, take an analytic point
reducing to the generic point of the integral curve on the formal special
fiber. Formal reduction is surjective on this flat admissible model.
CLD 17.7.10 identifies the curve and the restricted bundle as a residual
pre-model. CLD 18.8.4 with one bundle gives nonnegative degree there.
It is applicable to integer PL metrics; no use of 18.8.8's strictness
ambiguity is needed in this direction. Field/component extension degrees
in projection formula are positive, so the same inequality descends if
a complete algebraic closure is used. Every vertical integral curve has
nonnegative degree; hence the represented bundle is relatively nef and
BJ 4.3 makes this particular collar genuinely global psh. Nilpotent
special-fiber structure is retained in cycles and does not change the
nef test on integral curves.

If a global AFS sequence $a_i\downarrow u$ is already supplied and
$u\le g+B$, then for $c>1$ choose one outer shell with
$u-cg<C-1$. The compact sets $\{a_i-cg\ge C\}$ on the shell are nested
with empty intersection, so an entire tail satisfies the same collar
inequality. Formula (18) yields genuine global model psh truncations
decreasing to the desired bounded floor. Decrease floors to $-\infty$;
finiteness of $u$ somewhere ensures nontriviality. Global decreasing
closure and BJ 12.18 apply. In a rational class, uniform approximation
of nef models by ample FS metrics with summable constant tails preserves
decrease. Add a complete very ample system with sufficiently low rational
weights, leaving the metric unchanged. Ratios by powers of $s_0$ generate
$k[Y]$ and its own section gives a rational floor. This proves controlled
AFS regularization in the legitimate **already-regularizable** case. It
does not establish the same antecedent for arbitrary local or weak inputs.

For an already-Runge Hartogs domain containing the zero section, strict
polynomial polytope exhaustion gives
$G_j(y,w)=\sum_i f_{j,i}(y)w^i$ and, on the zero-section domain,
$$-\log d_{\Omega_r}(y)=\max_{j,i\ge1}
                         (\log|f_{j,i}(y)|-c_j)/i.                    \tag{19}$$
The Gauss supremum on the fiber disk proves both bounds for its radius.
Use strict inequality opens rather than the false general equality
$\operatorname{int}\{|f|\le a\}=\{|f|<a\}$, which fails for a constant
constraint. Finite coefficients in (19) are global algebraic functions.
Adding $\max(H_r,\epsilon_rg-r)$ supplies a rational floor and the original
generators. On each compact coordinate sublevel an entire tail decreases;
with upper growth, the common shell and (18) give decreasing global
truncations and actual AFS regularization. The nested exhaustions and
compact smaller closed fiber disks give convergence even for infinite
radius. This verifies the algebraic coefficient step when the domain is
already Runge. It does not prove the weak-input Hartogs domain is Runge.

# Calibrated facets and the bounded-local gate

## What the harmonic-chart computation actually proves

CLD 7.8.4 proves vanishing of a boundary integral for a harmonic PL chart;
it is not itself a statement that all relative-support functions are psh.
Its proof, LF9594--9653, derives the facet balancing relation from Green's
formula 5.11.1. We can reconstruct the required sign and positivity on a
*finite calibrated polyhedral chart*. This useful restricted statement
has the following precise assumptions. On a compact domain inside the
analytic open, a strongly supporting skeleton has a finite cell
decomposition adapted to the canonical calibration, to the test, to all
unit-log coordinates, and to the affine selections of $b$. The harmonic
coordinates defining the test have been added to the chart. On each
top-dimensional cell $\sigma$, $b$ and the coordinates are affine; on
every interior facet $\tau$ the chart and test restrictions agree from
all adjacent cells. Collapsed cells are assessed in this refined chart.

Choose a positive facet-volume normalization and the transverse vector
$e_{\sigma,\tau}$ pointing from $\tau$ into $\sigma$, scaled by its
positive canonical calibration. More explicitly, the calibration weight
can be absorbed into this transverse vector, as in CLD LF9621--9638;
if it is written separately it is a positive $w_\sigma$. For an affine
harmonic $H$, that calculation is
$$\sum_{\sigma\supset\tau}w_\sigma\partial_{e_{\sigma,\tau}}H=0.        \tag{20}$$
The vector is inward relative to its adjacent cell but is an outgoing
direction from the facet; in dimension one it is exactly the outgoing
direction used in (10). This convention is necessary for the sign.

Apply Green's formula cell by cell. The interior $dd^cb$ is zero on
affine cells. The terms containing $b\,d''\alpha$ cancel across a facet
by continuity and the harmonic-chart boundary balancing in CLD 7.8.4.
The remaining derivative terms give the corner coefficient
$$\kappa_\tau(b)=\sum_{\sigma\supset\tau}
                      w_\sigma\partial_{e_{\sigma,\tau}}b_\sigma.     \tag{21}$$
The sign is verified by $b(x)=|x|$ on a line: the two directions contribute
$1+1$, giving positive Laplacian, not its negative. A tangential component
of a transverse vector changes both $b$ and a tangential contact support
by the same amount; subtraction eliminates it. Choose a relative support
$H$ at an interior point of $\tau$. Since $b-H$ is affine on $\tau$ and
has a local minimum zero, its tangential derivative is zero. Its derivative
into every adjacent cell is nonnegative by (9). Subtracting (20) gives
$$\kappa_\tau(b)=\sum_{\sigma\supset\tau}
                w_\sigma\partial_{e_{\sigma,\tau}}(b-H)_\sigma\ge0.    \tag{22}$$
The restriction of a strongly positive $(n-1,n-1)$ test to the facet is
a nonnegative calibrated density. Thus the sum of (21) times those
densities is nonnegative. Tests supported away from the analytic boundary
have no outer boundary terms. If the refined chart collapses a facet to
dimension below $n-1$, its pulled-back facet-volume test vanishes. If its
rank is sufficient, use (20)--(22). One cannot discard a cell just because
the *original* tropicalization had too small a rank: refinement by the
test coordinates is essential. This proves positive curvature under the
stated finite-chart assumptions.

The remaining application is substantive. W's corrected local convention
is a finite affine selection on relative images of arbitrary analytic
neighborhoods. To use the preceding calculation and model theorems for
every such selection, one must obtain finite rational analytic domains
where, after tensoring, it is a unit logarithm plus a constant, with a
compatible strongly supporting refined chart for every test. Max/min
formulas for globally algebraic inputs provide this explicitly; (18) is
one such class. A word such as "PL" does not supply the required analytic
domain covering for an arbitrary relative selection. This general passage
has not been fully proved in this paper. Thus (22) is not promoted into
an unconditional higher-dimensional local/global theorem.

## Strictness and the narrower smooth route

The actual source definitions are different. CLD 12.5.2 allows
$\log|g|+c$, $c\in\mathbb R$, for integer PL, but strict integer PL is
G-locally $\log|g|$ on strict analytic domains. Proposition 17.8.4
explicitly assumes **strict integer PL**. Corollary 18.8.8 is printed
for integer PL and its proof cites 17.8.4 for (iii) to (i). The displayed
proof does not explain that change of hypothesis. This is an application
gap here, not a counterexample to the corollary. The positive-current to
residual-nef direction 18.8.4 and its field-extension consequence 18.8.6
are stated for integer PL and can be used without it. The separate
18.8.9 does impose nontrivial valuation and is not substituted.

If a continuous rational analytic metric on a smooth connected projective
$X/k$ already has a finite rational-domain representation and positive
curvature, there is a valid narrower route. Extend to $K=k((t))$,
$|t|=e^{-1}$. A common tensor power clears rational slopes and rational
constants; the latter are realized by powers of $t$. If the domains are
strict, this is a strict PL metric in GM's sense. GM 1.1 is now applicable:
the field is nontrivially valued and complete and the scheme is proper.
It supplies an algebraic/formal model metric. CLD 18.8.6 pulls positivity
to $K$, and the residual central-curve argument above makes the model

# Ample model atoms and the conditional smooth globalization repair

## A genuine positive atom without a Dirac equation

**Model-atom proposition.** On every reduced irreducible projective $X/k$
with rational ample class $c_1(L)$, every source-convention divisorial
point $x\in X^{\rm div}$ has positive actual MA mass for some bounded
genuine ample model potential $b$.

BJ 2.12 realizes $x$ as $v_E$ on the relative integral closure of a
flag-ideal blowup dominating $X\times\mathbb A^1$. Its proof uses 1.20
and passage to a higher configuration, preserving that component's
valuation. BJ 2.33 supplies on this same model an ample test bundle:
choose a relatively ample vertical rational divisor $D$ over the trivial
configuration and take $\mathcal L=L_{\mathcal X}+\epsilon D$ for small
positive rational $\epsilon$. The class is ample relative to $\mathbb A^1$,
so its restriction to each central integral component is ample. Hence
$(\mathcal L|_E)^n>0$, and the weight $b_E(\mathcal L|_E)^n$ in (1) is
strictly positive. Relative integral closure retained the original
generic fiber even when nonnormal. Normalization by $V$ preserves strict
positivity. Multiple components with the same valuation add positive
weights. This proves the proposition without EP or equation surjectivity.
The source divisorial convention uses rationally scaled valuations; no
model atom at every arbitrary real scaled point is asserted. The trivial
valuation is supplied by the trivial ample model. Dimension zero is the
one-point case already treated.

## What model atoms prove once local mass and comparison are supplied

Suppose a bounded local $\omega$-psh cone on the projective space has
compatible mixed positive measures, exact raw mass $V$, strict-max locality,
comparison (5), divisorial determination, and contains the bounded genuine
model potentials with their actual measures. These are explicit
antecedents. If bounded $\phi\le\psi$ a.e. for the measure of $\psi$,
fix divisorial $x$, choose the model atom $b$, and translate it so
$b<\phi$ with a uniform strict margin. For $0<t<1$ put
$\phi_t=(1-t)\phi+tb$. Then $\phi_t<\psi$ a.e. for the measure of $\psi$.
Comparison and equal masses give
$$V=\MA^{\rm raw}(\psi)(\{\phi_t<\psi\})
   \le\MA^{\rm raw}(\phi_t)(\{\phi_t<\psi\})\le V.$$
Mixed positivity gives $\MA^{\rm raw}(\phi_t)\ge t^n\MA^{\rm raw}(b)$,
so its atom at $x$ is positive. The strict set has full measure, so
$\phi_t(x)<\psi(x)$. Let $t\downarrow0$ and use divisorial determination
to get $\phi\le\psi$ everywhere. This rigorously replaces W 4.16's
continuous Dirac solution antecedent, but not its preceding local theory.

On a smooth connected projective $X$ in characteristic zero, known smooth
EP would then give bounded local/global equality. For any bounded usc
obstacle $\phi$ on compact Hausdorff $X^{\rm an}$ use the directed net of
all continuous majorants, ordered downward by finite minima, not a
sequence. Such majorants separate a point from closed superlevel sets by
normality: if $c>\phi(x)$, choose an open neighborhood on which $\phi<c$
and a continuous cutoff equal to zero at $x$ and one off that neighborhood;
interpolate between $c$ and a common upper bound. Finite minima give a
decreasing net with infimum $\phi$. Common lower and upper truncations
preserve these properties.

Smooth EP makes $p_i=P_\omega(f_i)$ continuous genuine global psh.
Contact orthogonality follows from the actual derivative: the obstacle
$p_i+t(f_i-p_i)$ has the same envelope $p_i$ for $0\le t\le1$, so
$\int(f_i-p_i)\MA(p_i)=0$. Thus its measure is on the closed contact set.
On that set $\phi\le f_i=p_i$. The conditional local domination just
proved gives $\phi\le p_i\le f_i$. These decreasing genuine envelopes
limit to $\phi$ with a common lower bound; genuine closure gives global
pshness. Bounded floors give the unbounded nontrivial case. Nef references
can be approached from above by ample ones using the actual global class
closure, provided the corresponding local hypotheses hold. No sequence
of majorants on an uncountable-field analytification is asserted.

Likewise the smooth affine regularization has a noncircular **conditional**
route. On a smooth projective compactification with effective ample
boundary choose actual proper AFS $g$. Boundary blowups preserve an
effective ample boundary divisor by taking a sufficiently large multiple
of its pullback minus the exceptional divisor, whose negative is relatively
ample. For a local input $u\le Ag+B$, choose rational $A'>A$ and $c>1$.
The floors $\max(u-cA'g,C)$ extend constantly by $C$ on one whole open
boundary collar since $B-(cA'-A)g<C$ there. On the affine chart their
local reference weights are $\max(u,C+cA'g)$, and near the boundary they
are semipositive-reference constants. The conditional bounded local/global

restriction give the sought affine sequence, with complete-system
generators and a floor. No already-regularizable extension lemma is used
to prove this antecedent.

For weak inputs one would resolve and compactify the witness to a smooth

# Rossi, the integer trace, and the remaining singular induction

W 5.6 constructs on an arbitrary relatively compact analytic neighborhood
$V$ a function equal to $Cu$ outside and
$\max(Cu,\log|f|-t)$ inside. Its boundary margin is correct, but its next
step invokes W 3.24 to approximate this analytic-piece PL function globally
by affine FS. Such a piece need not be a single global algebraic maximum.
Formula (18) supplies no extension on this arbitrary analytic $V$. We
have neither an independent polynomial-hull proof of Rossi's assertion
nor a complete general analytic-piece model representation here. The
source's conclusion is not disproved by that missing reconstruction.

There is a separate small logical error which can be repaired locally.
In W 6.15 LF2456--2458, continuity of a positive integer trace is used
to infer one integer $\gamma$ on the entire chosen open. Continuity gives
*local* constancy, not constancy on a possibly disconnected open. An example
occurs within the original field domain: take $z\mapsto a=z^2$ on
$\mathbb A^1_k$, with $k$ algebraically closed trivial characteristic zero.
Let the base open be the disjoint union of small disks about $a=0$ and
$a=1$, with radius $\rho<1$. Over the first choose $W_1$ to contain its
whole preimage disk; over the second choose $W_1$ to contain the disk about
$z=1$ and $W_2$ the disk about $z=-1$. The locally constant test is one
on $W_1$ and zero on $W_2$. Its actual finite-flat trace is two on the
first base disk and one on the second. At the branch center its Artinian
length is two; elsewhere all factors over the first disk are chosen,
with dimensions totaling two whether or not they split. Over the second
the unit-root expansion gives the two disjoint
disks and trace one. Thus it is a continuous integer trace on a
disconnected open with no single $\gamma$. One may take smooth closed
$Z=\{0,1\}$ on the source; its target images are distinct, and its other
fiber point is $-1$, so this also fits the local split geometry.

The correct repair is to shrink around the tested base point to a
neighborhood on which the *continuous* fixed trace is constant. Divisorial
determination, if already proved for that trace extension, then identifies
that integer at any remaining points of this smaller neighborhood. A
neighborhood crossing two different locally constant values cannot be
used. If $\gamma=1$, the fiber subset in $W_1$ has total positive integer
weight one, so it consists of the tested point with weight one. The other
weighted sum there is zero. In the negative estimate of W Step 5, its
trace is then at most $-\delta$; no bound
$\delta/(2(\gamma-1))$ is imposed and no division by zero is made. For
$\gamma>1$, the original finite weighted bound works after this local
shrink. These repairs do not construct the arbitrary analytic-piece
function $\widetilde h$ or $\widetilde v$ that the norm is applied to.

The dependency chain is therefore still broken before the singular EP

# Normalization, conditional cardinality removal, and a genuine obstruction

## One-way identities and the separate onto obligation

For reduced irreducible geometrically unibranch $X$, its finite normalization
$q:X^\nu\to X$ is birational of degree one. The local geometric branch
condition says that each geometric fiber is a singleton with purely
inseparable residue extension; characteristic zero makes that extension
an equality. Thus it is a universal homeomorphism. Its analytification is
a homeomorphism as well: an analytic point is a norm on the residue field
at its support, and the unique support point upstairs has that same residue
field. Properness turns the resulting continuous bijection on compact
Hausdorff analytifications into a homeomorphism. This is a topological
fact, not PSH descent.

Pullback of genuine global PSH is unconditional. Projection formula and
BJ 7.2 give equal volume and energy,
$$E_{q^*\omega}(q^*\phi)=E_\omega(\phi).$$
BJ 7.19 gives the actual mixed operator identity
$$q_*\bigwedge_i(q^*\omega_i+dd^cq^*\phi_i)
             =\bigwedge_i(\omega_i+dd^c\phi_i).                        \tag{25}$$
It follows by model projection formula and decreasing pairing continuity,
or by testing (3). Therefore a downstairs $\mathcal E^1$ potential pulls
back to one upstairs. For a downstairs psh potential, membership in
$\mathcal E^1$ is equivalent to that of its pullback by the equal energy.

Actual onto PSH is a separate theorem with an actual antecedent: BJ 5.13
requires a sequence of larger classes $\omega_m>\omega$, tending to
$\omega$, each with EP. Its proof inserts small pulled-back logarithmic
ideal corrections, obtains downstairs potentials in those larger classes,
uses their increasing-net closure, then uses divisorial determination.
Under this hypothesis it is onto and a homeomorphism. The equal energies
then make it onto $\mathcal E^1$, and (25) gives the operator correspondence.
In the unibranch homeomorphic case, probability measures correspond
bijectively; onto PSH gives equal suprema in (7), so finite-dual-energy
targets correspond as well. These onto statements remain conditional
here. Topological homeomorphism and the one-way energy identities do
not remove the EP hypothesis.

## What the countable-to-arbitrary-field passage would prove

Suppose the rational ample singular envelope theorem on countable
algebraically closed trivial subfields has actually been proved. Finite
geometry, line bundle, rational model obstacle, and model competitor data
descend to such a $k_1\subset k$, since the algebraic closure of the
finitely generated coefficient field inside $k$ is countable. These trivial
fields are complete. Reducedness, irreducibility and ampleness descend
faithfully. Over characteristic-zero perfect fields finite-type normal
algebras are geometrically normal. Their finite normalization therefore
commutes with these field extensions: its extension is finite normal
birational and hence the normalization. A geometric second branch would
persist after extension; thus geometric unibranchness descends.

Let $u_1=P_{\omega_1}(f_1)$ be the assumed continuous envelope for a
rational model-PL obstacle. Pullback $u$ is a genuine global psh minorant.
The envelope of $u_1+t(f_1-u_1)$ remains $u_1$; continuous genuine energy
differentiation gives
$\int(f_1-u_1)\MA(u_1)=0$. The measure is on contact.
A genuine global model competitor below $f$ descends to a larger countable
algebraically closed $k_2$. Relative nefness descends by the curve-degree
test and faithful extension, and ampleness by faithful flatness. Analytic
restriction is surjective; BJ LF755--758 supplies a continuous section
for complete field extension of the original trivial algebraically closed
field. Thus the descended competitor is below the descended obstacle.

The needed genuine mixed-measure field pushforward has an independent
model check. Regular characteristic-zero extension preserves vertical
$R_1$ and $S_2$: regular local fibers preserve regularity at vertical
generic points and the depth inequality at vertical points. Hence BJ 1.13
preserves relative integral closure in the given generic fiber. Central
integral components over algebraically closed $k_1$ are geometrically
integral. They do not split, their DVR orders of $t$ are unchanged, and
their intersection degrees remain equal by flat extension of cycles.
Their divisorial restrictions are the original $v_E$. Thus (1) has the
same pushed-forward weights. Decreasing model approximation and testing
continuous functions give
$$r_{21*}M_{\omega_2}^{\rm raw}(r_{21}^*u_1)
                           =M_{\omega_1}^{\rm raw}(u_1).               \tag{26}$$
This does not absolutely normalize the nonnormal generic fiber away.
Positivity and (26) put the upstairs measure on pulled-back contact, so
the global competitor is below the pulled-back envelope a.e. BJ global
domination 10.6 is valid on this singular irreducible $k_2$-variety and
gives the inequality everywhere. Model suprema, the envelope contraction
in sup norm, and PL density give continuous obstacles over arbitrary $k$.
No singular BFJ Dirac equation is used.

For ample real classes, choose rational ample $\omega_j\to\omega$ with
$\omega_j-\omega$ ample. The genuine class closure and inclusions
$\PSH(\omega)\subset\PSH(\omega_j)$ give EP in $\omega$ once the rational
antecedent is proved. These are conditional passages; neither produces
the missing countable rational singular base. Applying BJ 12.8 and 5.13
after that base would settle the raw equation, all normalization levels
and arbitrary-support targets, but this paper has not reached it.

## A nonsolvable finite-energy target on a nodal curve

The obstruction outside unibranchness is genuine and unconditional. Let
$C$ be an irreducible rational projective nodal curve, $q:\mathbb P^1\to C$
its normalization, and $p_1\ne p_2$ the node preimages. Put $d=\deg L>0$
and $v=\operatorname{ord}_{p_1}$ on the common function field. It is a
downstairs divisorial point and $\delta_v$ has finite dual energy by BJ 11.1.

Blow up $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1$ at $(p_1,0)$ with exceptional
$E$. The model class
$p^*q^*L+d\mathcal X_0-dE$ has degree zero on the strict central component
and degree $d$ on $E$, both multiplicity one. These are all vertical
curves, so it is relatively nef; (1) gives raw measure $d\delta_v$.
Its bounded model potential is
$g(s\operatorname{ord}_{p_1})=d\max(1-s,0)$ on the chosen ray and $d$
on the other rays. In particular its endpoint values at $p_1,p_2$ are
$0,d$.

If a downstairs $\mathcal E^1$ solution to $d\delta_v$ existed, its
pullback would have finite energy. Identity (25), positivity and total
mass force its upstairs measure to be $d\delta_v$, since a valuation on
the common function field has a unique analytic lift. Upstairs uniqueness
on $\mathbb P^1$ forces its potential to be $g+c$. But a pullback from
$C$ has equal values at the two node-preimage endpoints, whereas $g+c$
differs by $d>0$. This is a contradiction, agreeing with BJ 12.11.
It is not a counterexample on a unibranch variety and does not change the
requested domain.

    (mathcalL|F)^n > 0,

## Unreviewed root proposal: P16.1-Gauss-descent-of-genuine-FS-and-model-psh-20261005-continuation71.md

# Direct Gauss descent for genuine FS limits

This is an unreviewed root proposal. It targets the continuous model-to-genuine-PSH gate in P16.1. No singular envelope theorem, local/global equality for arbitrary bounded local psh functions, or original-problem closure is asserted. All source applications and the Gauss-section construction require independent checking.

## 1. Proposed descent lemma

Let k be algebraically closed, trivially valued, of characteristic zero, X a projective geometrically reduced k-variety, and L an ample Q-line bundle. Put K=k((t)), with |t|=e^(-1), and let pi:X_K^an -> X^an be the base-extension projection. Define genuine PSH on both sides by decreasing FS nets, as in the actual BE1805.01016v3 Definition7.2. Use the canonical trivial reference metric h_0 on L and its pullback.

Proposed statement: for any generically finite function u:X^an -> R union {-infinity},

    u belongs to genuine PSH_k(L) iff pi^*u belongs to genuine PSH_K(L_K).

In particular the analogous equivalence holds for CPSH, with its uniform FS approximation. No global smoothness of X is used. This statement concerns BE's actual FS-defined cone; it does not identify arbitrary positive-current or locally generated classes with that cone.

The forward implication follows by pulling back the defining FS net; actual BE7.20 supplies FS/base-extension compatibility. The proposed converse uses a continuous Gauss section sigma of pi and exact finite FS descent along sigma.

## 2. The Gauss section

For v in X^an, complete its residue field H(v), introduce an independent indeterminate T of Gauss radius e^(-1), and complete H(v)(T) with its Gauss absolute value. The convergent map k((t))->this field sending t to T defines an extension of H(v) and hence a point sigma(v) of X_K^an above v. On an affine chart and a finite tensor expression with Laurent expansions, its evaluation is

    |sum_(r>=r0) t^r f_r|_(sigma(v)) = max_(r>=r0) e^(-r)|f_r(v)|.

The f_r here lie in the finite-dimensional k-span of the finitely many chart functions occurring in the tensor expression. On each compact chart set their absolute values are uniformly bounded. Consequently the tails decay uniformly, and finite maximum evaluations prove continuity. Multiplicativity is the ordinary multiplicativity of a Gauss norm on the independent indeterminate, including when the radius lies in the value group of H(v). Transition maps agree because the construction takes place in H(v), so these local descriptions glue; pi composed with sigma is the identity. A completed Gauss field is used, rather than identifying t with an element of H(v), which could introduce cancellation.

The construction and the asserted evaluation for completed analytic functions must be checked on the actual algebraic analytification charts. For the global-section application below, only finite sums of Laurent coefficients times algebraic global sections are needed. No claim about arbitrary analytic coefficients or arbitrary valued extensions is required.

## 3. Exact finite descent of a global FS metric

Take any FS metric psi on L_K. After tensor clearing it has the form

    psi = (1/m) max_j {log|s_j|_(m h_0,K)+lambda_j},

relative to the reference, where the finite sections s_j of mL_K have no common zero and lambda_j are real. Proper base change of H^0 gives H^0(X_K,mL_K)=H^0(X,mL) tensor_k K. Expand each s_j as

    s_j=sum_(r>=r0) t^r f_(j,r), with f_(j,r) in H^0(X,mL).

Choose one k-basis of this finite-dimensional space; each Laurent coefficient section is a k-linear combination of that basis. Every k-section has canonical reference norm at most one everywhere on X^an. By the Gauss formula,

    psi(sigma(v)) = (1/m) max_(j,r) {log|f_(j,r)(v)|_(m h_0)+lambda_j-r}.

The left side is a continuous finite-valued function on the compact X^an, so has a finite lower bound b. The terms with r>R are bounded above by (max_j lambda_j-R)/m. Choose R so this is strictly smaller than b. Those terms never contribute, giving the exact finite formula

    sigma^*psi = (1/m) max_(j,r<=R) {log|f_(j,r)|_(m h_0)+lambda_j-r}.

The retained coefficient sections are globally generating. If all vanished at a closed k-point, their maximum there would be minus infinity, contradicting the finite left side. Algebraic closedness ensures every nonempty base locus has such a closed point. Thus sigma^*psi is an actual FS metric over k, with the same L and real constants. This is an exact metric identity, not only an approximation on a toric subset.

Now let pi^*u be the decreasing limit of an actual FS_K net psi_i. Each sigma^*psi_i is actual FS_k, by the preceding argument (with a cutoff depending on i), and the net is still decreasing. Since pi composed with sigma is the identity, its pointwise limit is u. This proves the proposed PSH descent. For continuous u, a uniform FS_K approximation pulls back uniformly and gives uniform FS_k approximation. The proof accommodates nonmetrizable analytifications by retaining nets.

If a finite Laurent extension k((t^(1/q))) is needed to clear rational PL constants, the same proof uses its parameter of radius e^(-1/q); coefficient weights become -r/q and remain allowed rational FS constants. Changing this mathematical auxiliary field does not require any change to the execution environment.

## 4. Potential application to globally glued rational analytic PL metrics

Suppose a continuous metric u on an ample L has been constructed globally on X^an by rational local PL formulas and a common finite analytic collar, with actual positive CLD curvature. To apply the lemma, separately establish all of the following:

* its pullback to a suitable Laurent field is strict rational analytic PL, after clearing constants/slopes and checking its actual admissible G-domain cover;

* the descended FS limits identify with BJ's genuine PSH cone on the trivial field through its actual FS conventions, regularization and uniform closure.

Then u is genuine CPSH_k(L) by direct Gauss descent, even on singular X. This would replace the use of the smooth-only wording of BJ Remark4.19 for this particular continuous model route. It does not assert the converse for arbitrary positive continuous current metrics, which need not be analytic PL, or a global regularization theorem for bounded local limits.

The same route could validate rational PL collar gluing on a singular projective compactification, if the calibrated relative-support curvature and strict PL conditions have already been proved. It could also remove one antecedent in the narrow affine collar and Rossi argument. Arbitrary analytic-piece trace, gamma localization, genuine Runge induction, singular envelope regularity, local/global equality and finite-energy variational attainment still require independent arguments; no circular use of those desired theorems is permitted.

## Unreviewed root proposal: P16.1-localized-rational-neighborhoods-and-base-extension-corners-20261005-continuation72.md

# Localized rational neighborhoods and repeating the corner argument after extension

This is an unreviewed root proposal, written after checkpoint71. It addresses two precise gates in the bounded-local collar and continuous-model route. It is not an accepted lemma, a singular envelope theorem, or a solution of P16.1. It will not be supplied to the running round61 worker.

## 1. Rational sublevel neighborhoods after algebraic localization

Let X be projective over an algebraically closed trivially valued field k, x in X^an, and U an analytic open neighborhood of x. Choose an algebraic affine open Y containing x. The Berkovich topology of Y^an is generated by finitely many evaluations of regular functions. A basic neighborhood of x contained in U intersect Y^an can therefore impose finitely many strict upper and lower inequalities on log absolute values of regular functions.

If a required lower absolute-value inequality is |f|>a>0, then |f(x)|>0. Algebraically localize at f. The point x remains in the principal affine open Y_f because f is not in its analytic kernel. On that open the inequality is |f^(-1)|<a^(-1), an upper inequality for a regular function. At a point with |f(x)|=0 no positive lower inequality containing x is imposed, so this localization is never attempted for a zero evaluation. The inverse formulation is the reason arbitrary neighborhoods cannot simply be assumed to be polynomial sublevels on one previously fixed affine chart.

After finitely many localizations, all inequalities are upper inequalities |g_j|<b_j for g_j regular on one affine open Y'. Choose rational logarithmic bounds C_j strictly between log|g_j(x)| and log b_j, taking any finite rational bound if the left side is minus infinity. Include a finite set of algebra generators of k[Y'] and give them large rational upper bounds that still contain x strictly. Set

    v = max(lambda, log|g_1|-C_1, ..., log|g_r|-C_r),
    Q = {v<=0},

where lambda is a rational negative floor. The common generator bounds make Q compact: it is a closed bounded multiplicative-seminorm subset of a finite polydisc via the closed affine embedding Y' -> A^r. The original constraints show Q is contained in U. Because every displayed inequality is strict at x, x is in the analytic interior of Q. Select rational epsilon>0 below every finite margin at x and take lambda<-epsilon. Then

    P={v<=-epsilon}, x in interior(P), P contained in interior(Q).

Both P and Q are actual rational polydomain sublevels in Y'^an, with finitely many rational logarithmic thresholds. They are nonstrict domains over the trivial field in general. After extension to k((t^(1/q))) for a common denominator q, the thresholds are in the value group, so their pullbacks are compact strictly affinoid Laurent/Weierstrass domains. This statement is about these constructed domains, not arbitrary analytic domains or arbitrary real radii.

To realize v as -log||s_0|| for an ample FS reference on a projective compactification, a common line-bundle power must clear the poles of all g_j and of the chosen inverse functions along the affine boundary. It must also retain a complete generating system and a distinguished section s_0 whose nonvanishing locus contains Y'. The negative floor lambda is the weight of the distinguished section; replacing it by zero would invalidate P. The exact affine compactification and pole-clearing construction remains a source/application check, although it is independent of psh regularization.

## 2. Recompute curvature rather than assume pullback of distributions

Suppose a continuous local support PL weight has a finite expression

    u = f(-log|g_1|, ..., -log|g_r|)

on a neighborhood, with the corrected contact-support condition on its actual tropical image. Every affine support satisfies both equality at the contact point and the lower inequality with the same additive constant. At infinite-coordinate points use the finite affine convention: a nonzero coefficient cannot multiply an infinite coordinate, so the branch must be described by coordinates that are finite on a smaller neighborhood. The functions for those coordinates are analytic units there.

After Laurent base extension, the evaluation of every pulled algebraic function or analytic unit is unchanged by the projection pi: |g_K(y)|=|g(pi(y))|. The continuous Gauss section of pi ensures that the tropical image of the pulled neighborhood is exactly the original tropical image. Consequently its relative contact supports remain contact supports on the base-extended image. This reasoning requires the actual pulled neighborhood and the actual source functions; it is not a statement about unrelated analytic charts on the extension.

One can now repeat the calibrated harmonic-chart Green formula on the extended space. On a refined supported tropical complex, subtract the contact affine support along an interior facet. Its tangential derivative agrees with u, while its inward directional derivatives are no larger than those of u. Harmonic balancing cancels the affine part, leaving a nonnegative sum of the calibrated inward derivatives. Compactly supported strongly positive tests have nonnegative densities on the supported facets. The test-coordinate refinements, orientation, positive calibrations, possible collapsed cells, and absence of boundary terms are exactly the same obligations as in the round70 proposed corner argument and remain to be reconstructed completely.

If those obligations are verified on the extended charts, they prove positive curvature there directly. They avoid using a positive-current pullback statement before verifying membership in a psh cone. This removes that logical circularity from the proposed strict residual-nef application; it does not remove the actual calibrated-corner obligation.

## 3. Branch domains still need a genuine polyhedral argument

A finite continuous selection of rational affine formulas is not, merely by its name, an admissible analytic branch cover. On a compact rational domain one should obtain an actual finite rational polyhedral tropical image, refine it by the equality hyperplanes of all branch formulas, and check that the selected formula is fixed on each connected relative cell. Distinct affine values cannot change along a connected cell without meeting an equality hyperplane. The preimages of the resulting rational polyhedra are specified by finitely many inequalities between monomials in analytic units, with value-group constants after the same finite Laurent extension. These are strict Laurent domains; the finite weak-inequality covers must be checked to be admissible G-covers.

The missing assertion here is the exact finite rational-polyhedral image/refinement theorem for these compact domains and the harmonic-chart data, including its hypotheses. It must be supplied from an actual allowed primary passage or proved. A general relative subset of Euclidean space need not have such a decomposition. No ambient convex extension, rational semipositive approximation for arbitrary real coefficients, or unsupported global model representation is inferred.

Once the cover and direct positive-curvature checks are complete, the GM formal-metric construction, CLD strict residual-nef criterion, BE ample nef-to-FS theorem, and exact Gauss FS descent from the checkpoint71 proposals form a concrete chain to genuine global CPSH on singular X. The later bounded-local weighted products, total mass/comparison, arbitrary local/global equality, Rossi/Runge, normalization descent and singular envelope regularity still require their own proofs.

## 2. Independence uses one shared collar before comparing limits

translation invariance uses precisely mass $V$.

The MA embedding and target completeness yield strong completeness after

# Corrected rational local class

Use the following auxiliary class; it does not redefine BJ's genuine global
PSH. A continuous finite local weight is in $\mathcal C_{\mathbb Q}$ if,
locally, it is a finite continuous selection of rational affine expressions
in finitely many algebraic log norms (analytic unit log norms may also be
retained after pullback). At an infinite coordinate the finite affine
convention requires coefficient zero on the chart containing that point.
Contact positivity means that at each point of the relative tropical image
there is a finite affine support $\ell+C$, defined near that point, with
$$b(t')\ge\ell(t')+C,\qquad b(t)=\ell(t)+C. \tag{9}$$
The same constant occurs in both expressions. Supports can have real
coefficients; the selected branches have rational coefficients and constants.
This distinction is used in the curve test. The usc, generically finite local
decreasing limits of sequences in this class, on common neighborhoods, form
$\mathcal P_{\mathbb Q}$. Logarithmic poles are allowed in those limits.

For affine $Y=\operatorname{Spec}R$ write
$$\operatorname{AFS}(R)=\{\max(\lambda,c_i\log|f_i|+C_i)_i\},$$
where $c_i>0$, $\lambda,C_i\in\mathbb Q$, and the listed regular $f_i$
include algebra generators of $R$. Growth here always means $u\le h+O(1)$.
The literal lower-growth convention in W 3.15(2) fails for
$\max(\log|z|,-m)\downarrow\log|z|$ at zero. The omitted contact constant
in W 3.1(3) also cannot be used: taking a sufficiently negative affine
constant would otherwise make a purported support condition vacuous.
These are corrections of auxiliary formulas, not alterations of P16.1.

Finite maxima and positive rational sums preserve AFS. Distribute maxima,
clear positive rational exponents into monomials and retain each original
generator branch by choosing the floors in all other factors. Floors and
intercepts remain rational. Differences form a rational vector lattice since
$\max(a-b,c-d)=\max(a+d,c+b)-(b+d)$. AFS functions have compact coordinate
sublevels because the generator branches bound a closed affine embedding.

# Actual strict branch covers on the Laurent field

**Rational selection lemma.** On a compact strict Laurent-domain neighborhood
over a discretely valued Laurent field, a finite continuous rational affine
selection in finitely many analytic unit log norms is actual strict
$\mathbb Q$-PL in the G-topology.

To prove this, apply the published D 3.2, LF2077--2150, to the compact strict
space and the units. Its log image is a finite rational polytope with constants
in $\log|K^\times|\otimes\mathbb Q$ and dimension **at most** the analytic
dimension. The proof reduces to algebraic rational domains and uses actual
valued-field image Theorem 1.2, including closedness and the transcendence-degree
upper bound. Neither CLD's printed dimension equality nor an immersion of the
moment map is needed; a constant moment map has zero-dimensional image.

Subdivide that finite polytope by every equality hyperplane of the candidate
affine expressions. Each relative open cell is convex and connected. On it
two expressions either agree identically or have different values everywhere.
Continuity of the selection makes the group of the selected equal expressions
locally constant: near a point their value is separated from the finitely
many other values. Connectedness makes it constant on the cell. Continuity
extends the selected value to its closure.

The preimage of a closed refined cell is cut out by finitely many weak
rational monomial inequalities in units. Clear denominators in slopes and
constants. An inequality becomes $|t^a\prod u_j^{m_j}|\le1$, so its
preimage is a strict Laurent domain, including equality cuts as two inequalities.
These domains form a finite admissible G-cover. Indeed they are compact and
closed, and for any point the union of those containing it is a neighborhood:
discard the finitely many closed domains not containing it. The domain
intersection construction is the ordinary finite Laurent-domain covering
construction. This also deals with lower-dimensional cells. On each domain
an integer multiple of the selected function is the log norm of an analytic
unit, with its rational constant realized by a power of the Laurent parameter.

The neighborhood and unit assumptions are constructed before applying D.
At infinite coordinates shrink the finite affine chart and discard precisely
the coordinates with zero coefficients; do not take logarithms of
identically zero pullbacks. Remaining coordinates are nonvanishing and are
units on a smaller neighborhood. A compact cover gives finitely many such
charts and one common tensor denominator. An arbitrary continuous relative
selection without these rational presentations is outside this lemma.

# Smooth analytic curves detect the sign

**Curve lemma.** Over a complete algebraically closed nontrivially valued
field $\Omega$ of residue characteristic zero, a continuous finite
$\mathcal C_{\mathbb Q}$ pullback on an arbitrary smooth analytic curve is
subharmonic. The map into the ambient space need not be algebraic.

Local algebraicity of the smooth strict analytic curve, as used in actual
GR 5.1, permits the semistable curve neighborhoods of Wa Section 2 and Th.
The coordinate pullbacks remain **analytic units**; local algebraicity does
not convert them into regular algebraic functions. On a small strict affinoid
neighborhood choose one strictly semistable model. Th 2.2.24(ii), applied to
each unit and its inverse, factors all their log norms through that same
finite skeleton. Its part (i) gives finite piecewise affineness; part (iii)
and 2.3.20 give harmonic balancing. Refining one model for finitely many
units retains these facts.

On every subdivided edge the coordinates are affine. A finite continuous
selection among affine expressions changes value only at the finitely many
intersections of distinct expressions. Coincident expressions have equal
values. Subdivide at those intersections; real breakpoints are permitted in
the definition of lisse. At an interior skeleton point subtract the pulled
contact support $H=\ell(\log|u_j|)+C$. It is harmonic and $b-H$ has local
minimum zero. Each outgoing slope of $b-H$ is nonnegative, while the positive
weighted outgoing slopes of $H$ sum to zero. Thus
$$\Delta b(\{x\})=\sum_e w_e\partial_e(b-H)\ge0. \tag{10}$$
At type III breakpoints there are two outgoing directions. At type I and IV
points the unit-log germs are locally constant, by the proof of Th 2.3.21,
LF3604--3607. A continuous finite selection of constant values is constant
on a connected smaller neighborhood. Boundary coefficients of the affinoid
are not tested on the curve open. Lisse membership and Th 3.4.4/Wa 2.11
therefore give subharmonicity, and the sheaf property glues it. Th 3.1.8(ii)
passes decreasing limits, allowing an identically $-\infty$ component.

If Wa Section 4 is used to identify this with a CLD positive current, its
smooth **proper** curve hypothesis must also be met. Compactify the locally
algebraic smooth curve using its normalized projective function-field model.
Over perfect $\Omega$ it is smooth; apply Wa only on the original open,
without extending the function across the added boundary. The slope proof
above uses Th and Wa Section 2 directly and does not need Section 4 as an
extra unproved antecedent.

not just a numerically related line bundle.

For nefness use $\Omega=\widehat{\overline K}$. The pullback weights under

The already established strict PL membership permits GR 5.1 and 5.4:

# Exact Gauss FS descent and exact model-function descent

## The section and coefficient cutoff

BJ Remark 1.9 supplies a continuous Gauss section
$\sigma:X^{\rm an}\to X_K^{\rm an}$ of $\pi$. Here is the coefficient
construction needed beyond its finite-polynomial formula. For $x$ complete
$H(x)(T)$ with independent Gauss variable of radius $e^{-1/q}$; map
$\varpi$ to $T$. For a Laurent series with coefficients in a finite
$k$-span of regular functions, the norm is
$$\left|\sum_{r\ge r_0}\varpi^r f_r\right|_{\sigma(x)}
                =\max_{r\ge r_0}e^{-r/q}|f_r(x)|. \tag{11}$$
On a compact chart the coefficient functions are uniformly bounded, so the
tails tend uniformly to zero. Finite maxima prove continuity. The Gauss norm
is multiplicative on the independent variable, even if its radius belongs
to the value group of $H(x)$; it is not evaluation at a chosen element of
$H(x)$ that could cause cancellation. These constructions agree through
localizations and line-bundle frames. Thus (11) applies to global sections
with Laurent coefficients. No Laurent expansion over $\Omega$ is used.

Take an FS metric on $B_K$, clear tensors, and write its potential relative
to the pulled canonical reference as
$$\psi=\frac1m\max_j\{\log\|s_j\|_{mh_{0,K}}+\lambda_j\},$$
with a finite globally generating system in $H^0(X_K,mB_K)$. Flat extension
of coherent $H^0$ gives $H^0(X_K,mB_K)=H^0(X,mB)\otimes_kK$.
Expanding the coefficients of a fixed finite $k$-basis gives
$s_j=\sum_{r\ge r_0}\varpi^r f_{jr}$, $f_{jr}\in H^0(X,mB)$.
Every $k$-section has canonical reference norm at most one on $X^{\rm an}$:
its evaluation at the proper center belongs to the valuation ring.
Consequently
$$\sigma^*\psi=\frac1m\max_{j,r}
           \{\log\|f_{jr}\|_{mh_0}+\lambda_j-r/q\}. \tag{12}$$
This continuous finite function on compact $X^{\rm an}$ has a lower bound
$b$. For large $R$, all $r>R$ terms are strictly below $b$, uniformly,
because their coefficient norms are at most one. They never contribute.
Formula (12) is therefore an **exact finite** FS formula. Its retained
sections generate: a nonempty common zero locus would have a closed
$k$-point, where the finite formula is $-\infty$, a contradiction.
Rational weights remain rational. Pulling decreasing nets through $\sigma$
preserves decrease and the pointwise limit; pulling uniform approximations
preserves uniform error. The forward implication of FS-defined PSH descent
is direct pullback. Its converse uses (12) and $\pi\sigma=\operatorname{id}$.

For a rational ample line bundle, BE's FS-defined cone over $k$ is BJ's
genuine cone: actual globally generating rational FS potentials are BJ
$H(L)$; a single-section potential is the limit of its maximum with a
complete-system floor; and BJ 4.18 characterizes the generated decreasing
cone. This checks the class rather than invoking the smooth BFJ remark.

## The BE same-class difference

**Exact model-function lemma.** Any rational model function $F_K$ on
$X_K^{\rm an}$ has $F_K\circ\sigma\in\operatorname{PL}(X)$ exactly.

It is a rational model metric on $\mathcal O_{X_K}$. Represent it on a
projective algebraic model as above and clear its denominator. Choose any
ample model bundle $\mathcal A$. For large $r$ both $r\mathcal A+\mathcal B$
and $r\mathcal A$ are ample, where $\mathcal B$ models the trivial generic
bundle. Suitable integral multiples are relatively globally generated over
the noetherian Laurent DVR. Their finite module of sections generates;
restriction to the generic fiber gives finite global section systems.
BE 5.14 and the whole proof of 5.16(vi), LF1989--2033, give exact pure FS
metrics $\psi,\psi'$ with $F_K=\psi-\psi'$. Their common generic rational
bundle $M$ need not descend to $k$.

Choose a fixed ample $H$ on $X/k$ and a large integer $a$ so that
$aH_K-M$ is ample. A generating system of a suitable multiple gives a pure
FS metric $\tau$ on this bundle. BE 5.4's product-section calculation and
Lemma 5.17, LF2036--2043, put $\psi+\tau$ and $\psi'+\tau$ on the
same descending bundle $aH_K$; $\tau$ cancels exactly. Tensor denominators
are common in both expressions. Apply (12) only to their **final** section
systems in multiples of $aH_K$. Their potentials relative to the same
canonical reference are actual rational BJ FS potentials; their difference
is $F_K\circ\sigma$. This proves the lemma.

There is no requirement that $\mathcal A$ have generic restriction a power
of $H$, or that it be equivariant. No use of the avoidable GM 4.12
generic-class construction is retained. Exact PL membership of the
difference supplies no nefness assertion.

## Compact local extension

**Compact extension theorem.** If $f$ is a finite rational local affine
selection on an analytic open $U\subset X^{\rm an}$ and $C\Subset U$ is
compact, there is $F\in\operatorname{PL}(X)$ agreeing with $f$ on an
analytic neighborhood of $C$.

The collars below give a finite union $W$ of compact rational domain
neighborhoods with $C\subset\operatorname{int}W\subset W\subset U$.
After finite Laurent extension, $W_K$ is compact and strict. The selection
lemma, without contact positivity, makes $Nf_K$ a formal metric on the
trivial bundle on $W_K$ for one integer $N$. Apply GM 2.7 to this actual
metric and the paracompact strict space $X_K^{\rm an}$: it extends to a
global formal metric on that same trivial bundle. GM's proof extends a
coherent model sheaf and makes it invertible by an admissible blowup.
Divide by $N$, use proper algebraic/formal representation, and apply the
exact model-function lemma. Since $\sigma(W)\subset W_K$ and
$\pi\sigma=\operatorname{id}$, the resulting $F$ agrees with $f$ on
$W$, hence near $C$. Agreement on the entire original open is neither
claimed nor needed.

# Ample principal-open collars on the original nonnormal variety

**Collar lemma.** For $x\in X^{\rm an}$ and an analytic open $U$ containing
$x$, one can choose a standard open $D(a)$ for a section of an ample
power of $L$, a rational FS local potential $v$ in the class $c_1(L)$,
and rational $\epsilon>0$, such that
$$x\in\operatorname{int}\{v\le-\epsilon\},\quad
 \{v\le-\epsilon\}\subset\operatorname{int}\{v\le0\},\quad
 \{v\le0\}\Subset U\cap D(a)^{\rm an}. \tag{13}$$

First take a very ample power of $L$ and a section $s$ nonzero at the
algebraic support of $x$. The affine standard open $D(s)$ contains $x$.
The initial seminorm topology gives finitely many regular evaluation
inequalities inside $U$. A positive lower bound $|f|>c$ containing $x$
has $f(x)\ne0$; on $D(f)$ replace it with $|f^{-1}|<c^{-1}$. Never
invert a function zero at $x$.

Here is section localization without normality. For an ample invertible
sheaf $A$ and a section $s$, the sheaf localization along $s$ is the
filtered colimit of tensor powers with transition multiplication by $s$.
On a finite affine trivializing cover the identity is ordinary localization
$M_s=\varinjlim(M\xrightarrow{s}M\xrightarrow{s}\cdots)$. Finite
intersections allow one common exponent for sections and their matching
relations; equivalently the finite equalizer computing global sections
commutes with this filtered colimit. Thus
$\Gamma(D(s),\mathcal O_X)=\varinjlim_d H^0(X,A^d)/s^d$.
No integral closure or normalization enters. Write the finitely many
localized functions $f_i=\tau_i/s^{d_i}$. The product
$a=s\prod_i\tau_i$ is a section of one positive ample power $L^d$, and
$$D(a)=D(s)\cap\bigcap_iD(\tau_i)$$
exactly. The factor $s$ retains the old boundary.

On $Y'=D(a)$ write every remaining regular constraint with denominator a
power of $a$, then multiply numerators to one common power $a^m$. Choose
$m$ large enough that $L^{dm}$ is very ample. Add a complete very ample
section system, together with the constraint numerators and $a^m$ itself.
Ratios to $a^m$ include generators of $k[Y']$: the very ample embedding
on its affine standard chart is a closed affine embedding. Give extra
generator ratios sufficiently large rational bounds at $x$.

Choose rational $C_j$ inside the strict upper margins and $\lambda<0$.
In the class of $L$ the local FS potential is
$$v=\frac1{dm}\max\{dm\lambda,\ \log|\sigma_j/a^m|-C_j\}. \tag{14}$$
In particular the bound on $\log|\sigma_j/a^m|$ is $C_j$, not
$dmC_j$. The finite floor branch is the weight of $a^m$. All sections
globally generate, so (14) is the local weight of an actual global rational
FS metric $\theta$. Generator bounds make $Q=\{v\le0\}$ compact as a
closed bounded seminorm set in a finite polydisc. The constraints put it
inside $U$. Choose rational $\epsilon>0$ smaller than all divided margins
and with $\lambda<-\epsilon$. On $P=\{v\le-\epsilon\}$ every constraint
has a uniform strict margin, proving $P\subset\operatorname{int}Q$.
After clearing rational thresholds, these domains pull back to strict
affinoid domains over a finite Laurent field. Finite unions give the
neighborhoods needed in the compact extension theorem.

# Global rational PL partitions and compact integration by parts

For a finite analytic open cover $(U_i)$, compact Hausdorff shrinking gives
compact $K_i\subset U_i$ covering $X^{\rm an}$. Choose continuous bumps
$0\le a_i\le1$, equal to one on $K_i$, with compact support in $U_i$.
By BJ 2.2 choose rational algebraic PL $p_i$ with error less than $1/8$.
Set
$$\chi_i=\min(1,\max(0,2p_i-1/2)).$$
The rational vector lattice BJ 2.1--2.3 retains PL membership. These are
one on $K_i$; their supports lie in $\{p_i\ge1/4\}\Subset U_i$,
since $p_i<1/8$ off the compact support of $a_i$. Define
$$\rho_1=\chi_1,\qquad
 \rho_i=\min\left(\chi_i,1-\sum_{j<i}\rho_j\right). \tag{15}$$
Induction gives nonnegative entries, subordinate supports, and partial sum
$\min(1,\sum_{j\le i}\chi_j)$. As some $\chi_i$ is one at every point,
$\sum\rho_i=1$. No variable denominator is introduced. The same
construction supplies a rational PL cutoff equal to one near any compact
set and compactly supported in its chosen open.

For finite rational local PL functions $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ on $U$ and a
rational local PL test $g$ with compact support there, its zero extension
is continuous and locally rational PL on $X^{\rm an}$, with a whole zero
collar. The global formal representation and exact Gauss descent therefore
make that extension an actual global BJ PL function. Extend the $f_i$
exactly near its support by the compact extension theorem. Actual model
locality and the symmetric global intersection pairing give
$$\int_U g\,dd^cf_1\cdots dd^cf_n
       =\int_U f_1\,dd^cg\,dd^cf_2\cdots dd^cf_n. \tag{16}$$
Here signed products use the multilinear genuine model measure. Its locality
for these continuous PL metrics is checked through the CLD model measure,
not an unnamed local operator: flag ideals on an equivariant model give
the pullback metric at every point over the Laurent field; integral ideal
closure preserves those norms by the monic root bound; BE 8.18 and
CLD 18.4.7 identify actual intersection weights. CLD's residual formula
retains graded degrees, multiplicities and defects. Consequently their
locality pushes down to the stated model/intersection locality. Alternatively
BJ 7.23 and 7.42 state continuous compatibility. Since $dd^cg$ vanishes
on the whole zero open, the second integral is confined to the same compact
support. Global IBP from (3) and pairing symmetry proves (16), with no
singular equation antecedent.

# Common bounded-local representation and raw local mass

The following partial theorem concerns $\mathcal P_{\mathbb Q}$ with the
precise common-neighborhood approximation just defined, and rational
semipositive reference metrics. It is not an identification with every weak
psh function.

**Bounded local theorem.** Bounded $\mathcal P_{\mathbb Q}$ inputs have
mixed local positive measures agreeing with the genuine continuous model
products, independent of charts and approximations, and weighted decreasing
continuity for bounded tests in that class. For a bounded global locally
$L$-psh potential the diagonal raw mass is $V$, strict-max locality uses the
winning measure, and comparison and divisorial determination hold.

## One genuine representation for the entire tail

Choose (13) inside a common approximation neighborhood for a finite tuple
of bounded inputs and bounded tests, with $|u_i|\le M$ there. Enlarge $M$
rationally. Write the actual FS reference as $\theta=\omega+dd^cp$,
with $p$ the bounded rational global model potential difference. Choose
rational $A$ with $A\epsilon>2M+1$ and set $h=Av+M+1$.
On $P$ it is below $-M$. On the compact boundary strip
$Q\cap\{v\ge-1/(2A)\}$ it is at least $M+1/2$.
For decreasing continuous approximants $u_{i,j}$, the nested closed sets
$\{u_{i,j}\ge h\}$ in this strip have empty intersection. Compactness
makes one entire tail satisfy $u_{i,j}<h$. This is not Dini to a
discontinuous limit. There is a whole winning-branch collar for that tail.

Define globally continuous potentials relative to $A\theta$ by
$$\Psi_{i,j}=\max(u_{i,j}-h,0)\text{ on }Q,
       \qquad \Psi_{i,j}=0\text{ outside }Q.$$
On the nonconstant region their metric weight is
$Av+\Psi_{i,j}=\max(u_{i,j}-M-1,Av)$, a corrected contact rational
selection. Near the entire boundary it is the reference metric. The
continuous rational metric theorem makes these **genuine global** psh
metrics. Their bounded decreasing limits are genuine by decreasing closure,
and finite energy by (2). With
$$\Phi_i=\Psi_i/A+p,\qquad G=v-p+(M+1)/A, \tag{17}$$
on $P$ one has exactly
$$u_i/A=\Phi_i+G,\quad dd^cG=\omega,
       \quad dd^cu_i=A(\omega+dd^c\Phi_i). \tag{18}$$
The same data work for the whole mixed tuple, its tests and every tail term.
The reference and scalar in (17) cannot be omitted.

## Independence, weights and compact IBP

Define the local mixed measure on the interior of $P$ by
$$A^n\bigwedge_{i=1}^n(\omega+dd^c\Phi_i). \tag{19}$$
For continuous inputs this is their intrinsic continuous product, by the
model/CLD compatibility just checked, locally uniform continuous convergence
and uniform FS approximation. For a fixed representation the limiting global
truncations are the explicit functions $\max(u_i-h,0)$; hence any valid
approximation has the same limit and BJ decreasing continuity gives the same
measure. To compare two representations at a point, shrink inside both
inner domains and construct a third collar there. Restrict the first
approximation sequence to it; continuous locality and decreasing continuity
identify its two measures. Do the same with the second. Both restricted
sequences have the same explicit truncation limit for the third data, so
the measures agree. This proves overlap and approximation independence
without assuming that one sequence extends across the other's outer domain.

For a bounded decreasing local test $u_{0,j}$ use
$u_{0,j}=A\Phi_{0,j}+AG$. BJ 7.21 applies to the first term; the second
is fixed continuous and bounded on a compact test neighborhood. A cutoff
supported in that neighborhood permits extension by zero of the continuous
test factor multiplying $G$. This proves weak convergence of weighted local
measures. Bounded tests are integrable. Signed PL factors are handled by
expressing global PL as differences of genuine ample FS metrics with a
common class, using BE 5.17/BJ's ample generating cone; local frames give
the corresponding two continuous psh weights. Each term meets the same
weighted-continuity hypothesis.

Apply (16) to continuous tail inputs and pass to limits by this weighted
continuity, with a PL cutoff equal to one on the support of $g$. This proves
(16) when $f_1$ and the other local psh inputs are bounded decreasing limits;
on the right the signed $dd^cg$ product is supported in $\operatorname{supp}g$
by continuous locality and then the weighted limits. No arbitrary bounded
decreasing theorem is attributed to CLD 8.7.2: that proposition requires
locally uniform convergence to continuous functions. The bounded limit
passage here uses the actual BJ pairing and (17).

## The sum to $dd^c1$ calculation

Let $\phi$ be bounded and globally locally $L$-psh in the specified class.
Let $T=\omega+dd^c\phi$ and $T_0=\omega$ for the canonical reference.
On a local frame a finite rational FS potential $g_i$ represents $T_0$;
the local bounded psh weight for $T$ is $\phi+g_i$. Write
$S=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T^jT_0^{n-1-j}$. These mixed products exist by (19),
are positive and patch intrinsically. With the global subordinate PL
partition (15), compact IBP gives
$$
 \int\rho_i(T^n-T_0^n)
 =\int\rho_i\,dd^c\phi\,S
 =\int\phi\,dd^c\rho_i\,S. \tag{20}
$$
To justify the second equality apply (16) separately to $\phi+g_i$ and
$g_i$ on the same compact support and subtract. Choose a further PL cutoff
one there before taking weighted limits. Every signed measure
$dd^c\rho_i\,S$ is supported inside $U_i$ by locality; every weighted
integral is finite since $\phi$ is bounded. Local multilinearity and
overlap compatibility then give globally
$\sum_i dd^c\rho_i\,S=dd^c(\sum_i\rho_i)\,S=dd^c1\,S=0$.
Summing (20) proves $\int T^n=\int T_0^n=V$. The mixed version changes
one factor at a time and has raw mass $(L_1\cdots L_n)$ for the retained
rational reference data. There is no division by $V$ during this local
calculation and no sign reversal.

## Locality, comparison and determination

On a common inner collar the floor is inactive for both bounded inputs.

# Compact perturbations, Rossi and controlled affine approximation

## The actual compactification and affine generators

Let $b=\max(\lambda,c_i\log|f_i|+C_i)\in\operatorname{AFS}(R)$.
Choose $D$ so that $p_i=Dc_i$ are positive integers. The finite coordinates
$f_i^{p_i}$ together with the original $f_i$ give a closed affine embedding
of $Y$. Add the original coordinates at low rational weights: the branches
$\log|f_i|-M_i$ are dominated globally by
$\max(D\lambda,p_i\log|f_i|+DC_i)$, for large $M_i$, since $p_i\ge1$.
Thus the finite projective section maximum on the closure $\overline Y$
in this embedding, with hyperplane section $a$, has local weight exactly
$b$ on the rational ample bundle $B=\mathcal O(1)/D$. The floor is the
branch of $a$. Its nonvanishing chart is exactly the original $Y$.

If a continuous $\mathcal C_{\mathbb Q}$ function $v$ on $Y^{\rm an}$
equals $b$ off a compact set, its metric difference $v-b$ extends by zero
near the boundary of $\overline Y^{\rm an}$. Compactness and disjointness
from that boundary give an actual open zero neighborhood. It is therefore
an actual continuous globally glued metric, equal to the reference there.
The continuous rational metric theorem gives genuine CPSH, and BE 7.6 and
Gauss descent give uniform rational global FS approximation.

To convert a global FS approximant to actual AFS, tensor to a sufficiently
high multiple of $B$. Append a complete very ample section system at
sufficiently low rational weights; boundedness of its section norms for
the continuous metric ensures these branches do not change the maximum.
Include $a^l$ and $g_j a^{l-1}$ for original affine coordinate generators
$g_j$, on the same $\mathcal O(l)$, with low weights. Their ratios give
a constant branch and generators of $R$. The other section ratios are
regular on $Y$. All coefficients are the same positive rational tensor
factor, and all weights are rational. A merely basepoint-free system is
not asserted to embed $Y$. Subtracting the same reference on the affine
chart leaves the global uniform error unchanged even on this noncompact
chart. Errors $\epsilon_j$ with $3\epsilon_{j+1}\le\epsilon_j$ and shifts
$2\epsilon_j$ turn uniform approximants into a decreasing AFS sequence.

## Rossi's arbitrary analytic neighborhood

Let $L_0$ be nonempty compact in $Y^{\rm an}$, $z\in\widehat{L_0}\setminus L_0$
for the regular-polynomial hull, and $V$ an open relatively compact
neighborhood of $z$ disjoint from $L_0$. Suppose
$\log|f(z)|>t>\sup_{\widehat{L_0}\cap\partial V}\log|f|$ for rational $t$.
The bad compact boundary set $A=\partial V\cap\{|f|\ge e^t\}$ is
disjoint from the hull. For each point of $A$, a regular polynomial
separates it from $L_0$; choose a rational logarithmic bound strictly
between its norm there and its supremum on $L_0$. A finite subcover gives
finitely many inequalities satisfied by the entire hull and excluding $A$.
Add large rational coordinate bounds for affine generators to make their
intersection $P$ compact. Then
$u=\max(0,\log|p_i|-c_i,\log|g_j|-B_j)$ is actual AFS, vanishes on $P$,
and is strictly positive on $A$. The hull is compact by the generator
bounds, so it lies in $P$.

Choose a positive integer $C$ with $Cu>\log|f|-t$ on the whole boundary:
on $A$ use its positive minimum; off $A$ the right side is negative.
Continuity and compactness give a whole winning-branch boundary collar,
including zeros of $f$, where the finite branch wins. The function equal
to $Cu$ outside $V$ and $\max(Cu,\log|f|-t)$ inside is therefore a
continuous rational contact PL compact perturbation of $Cu$; arbitrary
geometry of $V$ creates no new seam. The compact-perturbation theorem
gives an AFS approximant with error less than one third of
$v(z)>0$. It separates $z$ from $L_0$, contradicting hull membership.
Indeed every AFS branch is bounded at a hull point by its supremum on
$L_0$, and the same holds for their finite maximum. This proves the Rossi
inequality in this original trivial-field domain without arbitrary affine
regularization as an antecedent. It does not prove the later weak trace
or Runge assertions.

## Moving compact truncations

Suppose an actual AFS sequence $u_j\downarrow u$ is already supplied,
$u\le g$, and $g\ge1$ is an actual rational AFS exhaustion. Use its
fixed compactification and reference. Choose negative integers
$C_r\downarrow-\infty$ and rational $R_r\uparrow\infty$ with
$R_r>2-C_r$. On $Q_r=\{g\le R_r\}$ the closed sets
$\{u_j\ge g+1\}$ decrease to the empty set; choose increasing $j_r$
so $u_{j_r}<g+1$ there. Put
$$\phi_r=\max(u_{j_r}-2g,C_r)\text{ on }Q_r,
            \qquad\phi_r=C_r\text{ off }Q_r. \tag{21}$$
The reference is twice the class of $g$. At its boundary the first term
is below $1-R_r<C_r$, giving a whole constant collar. Its interior
metric weight is $\max(u_{j_r},C_r+2g)$, so the continuous metric bridge
gives genuine CPSH for (21).

On $Q_{r-1}$ monotonicity follows from both decreasing inputs. On the
new annulus the old potential is $C_{r-1}$ and the first new branch is
below $1-g\le1-R_{r-1}<C_{r-1}$; the floor is smaller too. Outside
$Q_r$ both are their floors. Thus $\phi_r$ decreases globally, to
$u-2g$ on $Y$ and $-\infty$ on the boundary. Generic finiteness of $u$
prevents a trivial limit. Choose uniform rational FS approximants $F_r$
with errors $\epsilon_r=4^{-r}$ and
$B_r=2\sum_{i\ge r}\epsilon_i$. Then $H_r=F_r+B_r$ decreases since
$H_r-H_{r-1}\le\epsilon_r-\epsilon_{r-1}\le0$, and has the same limit.
After the exact generator/floor conversion, $w_r=H_r+2g$ is an actual
decreasing AFS sequence tending to $u$, with
$w_r\le2g+\sup H_1$. The uniform errors are metric differences.
There is no diagonal-monotonicity or metrizability assumption.

The same construction with a single continuous contact rational PL $b\le h$
uses $b$ in every collar, without assuming it is already regularizable.
It proves genuine global PSH of $b-2h$ on the compactification, with
boundary value $-\infty$.

## Principal-open damping, with its exact antecedent

For an **already AFS-regularizable** $v$ on $Y_f$ with $v\le g$ on $Y_f$,
take rational $c>0$ and $g_c=\max(g,-(c/3)\log|f|)$. This is an actual
AFS exhaustion of $Y_f$: include $f^{-1}$ together with the old generators.
The controlled construction gives $w_i\downarrow v$ and
$w_i\le2g_c+B$, with one common $B$. Therefore
$$w_i+c\log|f|\le\max(2g+c\log|f|,(c/3)\log|f|)+B. \tag{22}$$
On bounded neighborhoods this tends to $-\infty$ along $Z(f)$.
With rational floors $D_i\downarrow-\infty$, define
$b_i=\max(w_i+c\log|f|,D_i)$ on $Y_f$, and $D_i$ on $Z(f)$.
Each is continuous contact rational PL, constant on a genuine neighborhood
of every point of $Z(f)$; they decrease. Polynomial degree estimates
bound all $b_i$ above by one positive rational AFS exhaustion $h$ on $Y$,
including their floors. The single-function construction makes
$\psi_i=b_i-2h$, extended by $-\infty$, genuine global PSH on its fixed
compactification. They decrease; $\psi_i\le-h$. Thus
$\eta_i=\max(\psi_i,-i)$ is continuous even at the boundary, with a
whole constant collar, and genuine CPSH by maximum stability. Uniform
rational FS approximation and the summable shifts just used give an
explicit decreasing AFS sequence for the damped function on $Y$.
This repairs the already-regularizable damping implication with upper
growth and all scalar factors. It does not identify every weak input as
already regularizable on $Y_f$.

# Curve trace and localization checks retained separately

For a finite locally free reduced $B/A$ of rank $d$ and
$u=\max(\lambda,c_i\log|f_i|+C_i)$, choose $D$ clearing the coefficients,
$b_0=1$, $b_i=f_i^{Dc_i}$, $w_0=D\lambda$, $w_i=DC_i$. The determinant
on the invertible module $\det_A B$ gives
$$P(T)=\det(\operatorname{mult}_{\sum T_i b_i})
             =\sum_{|\beta|=d}a_\beta T^\beta\in A[T].$$
On a geometric fiber it is the product of the linear factors, repeated
with their Artinian lengths. Multiplicativity of a weighted Gauss norm
gives the actual weighted trace
$$N(u)=\max_\beta\left(D^{-1}\log|a_\beta|
                         +D^{-1}\sum\beta_iw_i\right). \tag{23}$$
One proves multiplicativity at arbitrary positive radii by choosing the
lexicographically largest maximal exponent in each factor; their product
is the unique maximal contribution to the coefficient at their sum.
The $T_0^d$ coefficient gives floor $d\lambda$. Base generators are
supplied by polynomial degree bounds in the source generators: for small
rational $\eta_j>0$ one has
$u\ge\eta_j\log|g_j\circ\pi|-M_j$; the summed dominated branches
leave (23) unchanged. Thus it is actual AFS. Under complete field
extension, refined Artinian factor dimensions sum to the original ones
and finite residue-field norms restrict uniquely; trace base change
therefore holds without any regularizability antecedent.

For the radial boundary identity of a finite smooth-curve map, extend a
complete residue-characteristic-zero field to a completed algebraic
closure, adjoining a Laurent parameter first if its valuation is trivial.
For a nontrivial subharmonic function near a rational center, disk
comparison gives $h(0)\le h(\xi_R)$; usc gives the reverse limiting bound
as boundary Gauss points tend to zero, including a value $-\infty$.
This is a Gauss radial limit, not an arbitrary punctured limit. Locally
$t=c z^eU(z)$, $U(0)=1$. Residue characteristic zero makes the binomial
root $U^{1/e}$ converge after shrinking; its coordinate change is
invertible. Thus $t=cy^e$. A small target Gauss point has one source
Gauss point in that disk, of radius $(r/|c|)^{1/e}$ and actual fiber
dimension $e$: its scaled residue equation is Eisenstein at the target
variable. The center has the same Artinian length. Finite properness
excludes extra preimages near the center. Hence an already constructed
open weighted trace equality passes to the center by radial limits.
If a summand tends to $-\infty$, all others have local upper bounds, so
the sum has that limit without subtracting infinities.

For nonflat finite maps to a smooth affine base, norm-polynomial coefficients
extend across the nonflat locus by integrality over the normal base ring.
Normalize a line preimage through the $H(s)$-rational lift, chosen generically
in an étale open. Its components have dimension one by the height bound
and finiteness, and normalize to smooth finite-flat curves of total rank
$d$. Formula (23) and the radial test give the same weighted sums of all
global AFS tests for every such line. The difference lattice of AFS tests
separates the finite analytic fiber (use truncated distinguishing regular
norms and low generator branches), and finite lattice interpolation
determines every weight. These are fixed positive normalized slicing
weights, not necessarily the original nonflat fiber-algebra dimensions.
For a weak input a line must be chosen after shrinking the isomorphism
locus of its proper witness; properness extends the lifted curve over
its DVRs. This verifies boundary equality **if the open weak trace
extension has been obtained**. It does not construct that extension.

The localization estimate also has a nonnormal algebraic proof. For
$h\in R_f$, $u\in\operatorname{AFS}(R)$ and
$a\log|h|+b\le\max(u,-c\log|f|)+O(1)$, divisorial rays on the finite
normalization force $a\max(-\operatorname{ord}_D h,0)\le c\operatorname{ord}_D f$.
For integers $p,q>0$ with $q/p>c/a$, $z=h^pf^q$ has no divisorial
poles and is integral over $R$. Since $R[z]_f=R_f$, one $f^N$ kills
$R[z]/R$; thus $z^\ell f^N\in R$ for every $\ell$. For a hull point
$x$ and compact $L_0\subset Y_f^{\rm an}$ with
$|\log|f||\le M$ on their union, the original $R$-hull inequality gives,
after $\ell\to\infty$ and $q/p\downarrow c/a$,
$$a\log|h(x)|+b\le\sup_{L_0}(a\log|h|+b)+2cM. \tag{24}$$
Zero and $-\infty$ endpoints follow by the same regular hull tests.
Actual pole orders, rather than denominator-power counting, are essential:
$f=z^2$, $h=z^{-1}$, $a=2$, $c=1$ refutes the latter counting rule.

# The remaining singular-envelope chain is not completed

The preceding rational local mass, continuous compact perturbation and
already-regularizable controlled damping arguments remove specific
antecedents. They do not establish the complete weak/global identification
asserted in W 6.5. The following are concrete unresolved gates in this
deliverable, not conclusions that their source statements are false.

1. A complete smooth upper-growth affine regularization theorem for all
   weak inputs, with resolved proper witnesses, nef pullback class and
   actual section/generator conversion, followed by the controlled
   principal-open removable extension for those inputs. Known smooth
   nef EP is available, but each compactification and descent application
   still has to be supplied. The parameter family $c_i\log|f|$ is not
   globally monotone when $\log|f|$ changes sign; it requires the exact
   upper-envelope/usc-closure theorem with a common upper bound. Neither
   (22) nor a continuous perturbation proves this weak-input gate.
2. Construct the open weak trace extension in W 6.12 Step 2 before using
   the repaired radial Step 3. Fixed slicing weights and the determinant
   calculation do not by themselves regularize the witness's input on
   the smooth étale open or extend its trace across the complement.
3. Reconstruct the full split-piece, Hartogs/Runge and singular-stratum
   induction in W 6.14--6.16 and 6.3--6.5. The continuous split-piece
   indicator in 6.15 can be treated by the compact-perturbation theorem;
   the weak split-piece function still needs a resolved witness, controlled
   trace and extension. Continuity of its integer trace implies local
   constancy, not one integer on a disconnected open. Shrink about the
   tested point. For $\gamma=1$ no other positive weight lies in that
   piece, so the other sum is zero; do not divide by $\gamma-1$.
   The polynomial Hartogs radius formula proves a decreasing distance
   function for an already Runge domain; it does not prove the required
   weak Hartogs domain is Runge. The finite local-projection geometry and
   all induction uses have not been proved here.
4. Consequently the countable rational singular EP base is unresolved.
   W 7.1, LF2697--2717, descends a smooth-resolution envelope and then
   explicitly invokes weak/global 6.5. The new preceding partial theorems
   cannot be substituted for that missing conclusion. The subsequent
   arbitrary-cardinality/real-class envelope passages, onto normalization
   and unconditional variational attainment remain conditional.

For arbitrary-cardinality descent, finite geometry, model obstacles and
competitors do descend to countable algebraically closed subfields of $k$.
But contact orthogonality must belong to the genuine countable singular
envelope before it can be used to compare competitors. Relative integral
closure must be retained under regular field extension via vertical DVR,
depth, component and multiplicity checks; mixed pushforward then follows
from (1), projection formula and decreasing pairing continuity. Merely
asserting base change of an absolutely normalized model would change the
generic fiber. No such base theorem is obtained here. Real ample-class EP
would follow from approaching rational ample classes from above and genuine
class closure, only after that rational base is established. The already
known real-class global operator in (1)--(8) does not supply real-class EP.

For geometrically unibranch $X$, finite normalization $q:X^\nu\to X$ is
birational degree one and a universal homeomorphism in characteristic zero:
geometric fibers are singletons with purely inseparable residue extensions,
which here are equalities. Norms on those residue fields give a bijection
of analytifications; properness makes it a homeomorphism. Unconditionally
pullback preserves genuine PSH, equal volume and energy, and
$$q_*\bigwedge_i(q^*\omega_i+dd^cq^*\phi_i)
                 =\bigwedge_i(\omega_i+dd^c\phi_i). \tag{25}$$
These are the model projection formula and BJ 7.2/7.19. Onto PSH is
separate: actual BJ **Lemma** 5.13 assumes approaching larger classes with
EP. Only with that antecedent does one get onto $\mathcal E^1$, equal
dual-energy target sets and full equation descent. Topological
homeomorphism does not prove analytic cone surjectivity.

There is a real obstruction outside unibranchness, already in BJ 12.11:
on an irreducible rational nodal curve with normalization $\mathbb P^1$
and node preimages $p_1\ne p_2$, $\delta_{\operatorname{ord}_{p_1}}$ has
finite dual energy but no downstairs solution. For degree $d>0$, blow up
$(p_1,0)$ in $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1$; the model
$p^*q^*L+d\mathcal X_0-dE$ has central degrees $0,d$ and multiplicities
one, hence raw measure $d\delta_{\operatorname{ord}_{p_1}}$. Its potential
has endpoint values $0,d$ at the node preimages. A downstairs solution
would pull back with that target by (25), positivity and fixed mass;
upstairs uniqueness forces this potential plus a constant, whereas a
downstairs pullback has equal values at the two preimages. This obstruction
does not apply to the requested unibranch domain and does not settle it.

nef local/global equality is obtained by adding a rational positive multiple of
