9. Exact algebraic reach and the remaining original gap
When n = 2, every rational effective quotient is an integral normal projective curve, by the accepted normal-
Veronese construction. At each closed point its one-dimensional normal local ring is a DVR and therefore regular.
Over the perfect field k, the regular curve is smooth: equivalently, in an affine presentation the cotangent space
has dimension one, the Jacobian has rank equal to the codimension at every closed point, and the smooth locus
contains all closed points and hence is the whole finite-type curve. The dimension-zero quotient for n = 1 is a
point. This proves the claimed all-cone result for n ≤ 2 without adding smoothness to the original cone.
For higher dimension the smooth-quotient hypothesis can fail. Take the specified torus T = Gm acting by ordinary
positive degree on
                                      R = k[x0 , x1 , x2 , x3 ]/(x0 x1 − x22 ).
This ring is the invariant subring k[a, b, c]{±1} , where the involution sends (a, b, c) to (−a, −b, c), with x0 = a2 , x1 =
b2 , x2 = ab, x3 = c. It is a normal domain: a finite group invariant subring of a normal domain is normal, by the
integrality and invariant-fraction-field argument already granted in P13.1. It has dimension three, degree-zero part
k, and is a normal good affine cone for this scalar torus. Its projective quotient is the quadric cone

                                                     Y = {x0 x1 = x22 } ⊂ P3 .

At [0 : 0 : 0 : 1], its local affine equation is uv = w2 ; the cotangent space has dimension three whereas the local
dimension is two. Thus Y is singular. Every rational Reeb field in this specified one-dimensional torus yields
the same Proj, since scaling and Veronese do not alter it. This example only demonstrates that the theorem’s
additional algebraic hypothesis is absent on part of the original domain. It is not a nonexistence counterexample.
To finish original P13.2 one still needs genuine normalized compactness, or another actual realization theorem,
for normal cones without such a smooth rational quotient. The present topology and root-tail lemma reduce
