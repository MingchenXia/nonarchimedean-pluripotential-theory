Theorem: on a complete rational affine d-plane V with actual finite rational-carrier convex gamma in PA_R positive at infinity, every continuous convex f with ||f-gamma|| finite has, for every eta>0 and finite rational S, a finite rational-carrier convex PA_R majorant F satisfying f<=F<=f+eta globally, F(s)=f(s) for s in S, and F^infinity=gamma^infinity. No reference heights or slopes are assumed rational. This is a global theorem and does not extend by itself to a nonconvex singular support. The coefficient/category and divisor facts used are proved in n1.

We supply the finite convex foundations. A cone {Ax>=0} splits off ker A. On its complement ell=sum_j(Ax)_j is strictly positive on the cone except zero; its ell=1 section is bounded, since an unbounded sequence normalized to unit length would give ell=0 there. A bounded polyhedron is the convex hull of finitely many vertices: from a nonvertex move in both directions annihilating all active equalities until a new independent inequality becomes active; express the point as their convex combination and induct on active rank. There are only finitely many independent active systems. This generates the cone; add both signs of lineality generators. Homogenizing {Ax>=b} with t>=0, Ax-tb>=0 gives vertices from generators t>0 and recession rays from t=0. Rational systems give rational choices. Conversely, expressing a point as a convex combination of point generators plus a nonnegative combination of ray generators and eliminating variables by pairing lower and upper bounds (Fourier–Motzkin) gives finitely many weak inequalities. The generated set is a closed polyhedron. Equality in a supporting functional retains exactly its minimal point generators and zero ray generators.

Finite convex functions on the whole plane are continuous. Enclose a ball of radius 2r in a cube and let B be the maximum at its vertices. Convexity bounds values above by B; reflection in its center x bounds them below on the radius-r ball by 2f(x)-B. Extend each short secant to length r to bound its slope by 2(B-f(x))/r on the radius-r/2 ball, in both directions. For an affine support at x project (x,f(x)-t) to the closed epigraph, normalize the separating projection normals and let t decrease to zero. Contact points approach (x,f(x)). The limiting vertical normal is nonpositive and cannot be zero, since a nonzero horizontal functional cannot be bounded on the full spatial projection. Division gives an affine support. Taking supports proves f=f**.

Write gamma=max_j(a_j.x+b_j), h=gamma^infinity=max_j a_j.x, Delta=conv{a_j}. Each full-dimensional affine piece of a convex PA function is a global support, proving this expression. Gamma-h is bounded and h(v)>0 for v!=0, so 0 is interior to Delta. Recession cones of the finite rational carrier cover V: some cell meets unboundedly many points of any spatial ray, and its inequalities put that direction in its recession cone. Subdivide them into pointed rational cones and choose rational ray generators r_k. As h is linear on each recession cone, epi h is cone{(r_k,h(r_k)),(0,1)}. Heights may be irrational; spatial rays remain rational.

The Legendre transform f*(p)=sup_x(p.x-f(x)) has domain exactly Delta and is uniformly bounded there, since f-h is bounded. Outside Delta a separating direction v gives p.v>h(v) and an infinite supremum along that ray. It is convex and lower semicontinuous. A finite lower-semicontinuous convex function g on a compact polytope is continuous even at the boundary: bound it above by B=max g at the vertices; at p choose r smaller than every inactive inequality's slack divided by normal norm. For nearby y in the polytope, z=p+r(y-p)/||y-p|| stays in it. Convexity gives g(y)<=g(p)+(||y-p||/r)(B-g(p)), while lower semicontinuity gives the reverse limiting bound. Thus f* is continuous on Delta.

Continuity of f lets its transform be computed using only rational x. For each p choose a rational near optimizer with error less than eta/2. Continuity of f* makes its error less than eta on a neighborhood of p. A finite subcover, with S added, gives a finite rational set A and g_A(p)=max_{x in A}(p.x-f(x)) with f*(p)-eta<=g_A(p)<=f*(p) for all p in Delta.

Set E_A=conv{(x,f(x)):x in A}+epi h. It is a closed polyhedron, projects onto all V, and is upward closed. Dual support yields f(x+v)<=f(x)+h(v), so convexity puts E_A inside epi f. Its vertical fibers have finite lower endpoints and define a finite convex PA function F_A. Direct maximization over the point and ray generators gives F_A*=g_A on Delta and infinity outside Delta. Double duality gives f<=F_A<=f+eta on the entire unbounded plane. Each prescribed lifted point belongs to E_A, so F_A(s)=f(s).

Each graph face of E_A is generated by a subset of its lifted points and zero-support rays, never the vertical ray. Its projection is the convex hull of rational spatial points plus a cone of rational spatial rays. The polyhedral facts just proved make it rational. Projection is injective on the graph, so common graph faces project to common faces. These finitely many projections provide an actual rational carrying complex. The rational coefficient-space argument of n1 puts the real function in PA_R; ordinary convex supports give genuine PA positivity. Thus no irrational maximum wall is treated as rational geometry. Exact recession follows from rec(E_A)=epi h: the convex hull of lifted points is bounded; divide a ray sequence by its length and use closedness of epi h for the reverse inclusion. Also the global error gives bounded difference from the actual gamma.

Choose eta_n=2^(-n) and majorants F_n. Then G_n=F_n+4*2^(-n) has 0<=G_n-f<=5*2^(-n), while G_n-G_(n+1)>=4*2^(-n)-5*2^(-n-1)>0. Rational constant shifts preserve carrier and exact recession. This gives decreasing global uniform approximation. The unshifted majorants interpolate S exactly; the shifted sequence does not retain exact interpolation, but uses a common rational shift.

The result reconstructs the needed affine route from unreviewed 282/283/284 data; none of their conclusions is imported as a grant. It proves no all-conical or arbitrary singular-union statement.
