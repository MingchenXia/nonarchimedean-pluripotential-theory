## 2. Actual algebraic splitting and bundle identification

**Lemma 2.** There is a finite Galois extension $E/K$ and an algebraic isomorphism $X_E\simeq\mathbb P^1_E$. Over every extension of $E$, the actual pulled-back rational bundle is $\mathcal O(a)$, with its unchanged geometric degree $a$.

**Proof.** Coherent cohomology of $\mathcal O_X$ commutes with any field extension here. A finite affine cover of the separated projective scheme has affine intersections, and its Čech complex computes this cohomology. Tensoring that complex with the extension field is exact, so its cohomology tensors with that field. In particular $H^1(X_{\overline K},\mathcal O)=0$.

Global regular functions on a geometrically integral proper curve over an algebraically closed field are constants: a nonconstant global regular function would give a nonconstant proper morphism to the affine line, whose image would be the affine line; the affine line is not proper. The proper image of a proper variety is proper, whereas a proper closed subset of the affine line is finite. Integrality then forces a constant. Together with the same cohomology base change, this also gives $H^0(X_F,\mathcal O)=F$ for every field extension $F/K$.

Choose a point $P\in X(\overline K)$, defined over a finite extension $E_0/K$. On the smooth curve $X_{E_0}$ it is an effective Cartier divisor of degree one. The exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O\longrightarrow\mathcal O(P)
\longrightarrow\mathcal O(P)|_P\longrightarrow0
\]
and the vanishing of $H^1(\mathcal O)$ show that $H^0(\mathcal O(P))$ has dimension two and surjects onto the one-dimensional fiber at $P$. Its canonical section is nonzero away from $P$, and a section mapping to a nonzero fiber value generates at $P$. Thus these two sections have no common zero. They define a nonconstant morphism to $\mathbb P^1_{E_0}$ whose pullback of $\mathcal O(1)$ is $\mathcal O(P)$.

Here is a direct proof that this morphism is an isomorphism, avoiding a hidden finite-map or Riemann--Roch input. Write its affine coordinate as $f$. The chosen nonzero fiber value says that $f$ has exactly one pole, at $P$, of order one. For each $n\geq1$, the exact sequence for $(n-1)P$ and $nP$ gives, by induction, $H^1(\mathcal O(nP))=0$ and $\dim H^0(\mathcal O(nP))=n+1$. The functions $1,f,\ldots,f^n$ have distinct pole orders, so form a basis. For any rational function $q$ on the curve, its poles form a finite set. At each pole $R\ne P$, $f$ is regular and its residue is algebraic over $E_0$. A polynomial in $f$ vanishing at that residue has positive vanishing order at $R$. A product $h(f)$ of sufficiently high powers of these polynomials cancels all poles of $q$ away from $P$. Therefore $h(f)q$ belongs to some $H^0(\mathcal O(nP))$ and is a polynomial in $f$. Since $f$ is nonconstant, $h(f)\ne0$. Thus $E_0(X)=E_0(f)$, giving a rational inverse to the displayed morphism.

This inverse extends across every closed point of $\mathbb P^1$: its local ring is a discrete valuation ring, and the valuative criterion of properness of $X_{E_0}$ extends the generic inverse uniquely to its spectrum. Finite presentation extends each such local morphism to a neighborhood (choose an affine chart around its closed image and clear the finitely many denominators); separatedness makes the extensions agree. The two compositions agree with the identity generically and hence everywhere on the integral curves. This proves splitting over $E_0$. Enlarge $E_0$ to its finite Galois closure; characteristic zero ensures separability.

For the bundle, $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1_E)=\mathbb Z$ by degree. A generic trivialization writes any line bundle as a divisor; a finite closed point defined by an irreducible polynomial of degree $d$ is equivalent to $d[\infty]$ by that polynomial's divisor. Principal divisors have degree zero. After clearing the rational denominator, the pulled-back bundle is therefore $\mathcal O(ma)$ for an integer $ma$, and the rational bundle is $\mathcal O(a)$. Intersection degree is unchanged by field extension. There is no factor $[E:K]$ in $a$. This is an identification of the actual bundle, not only its numerical class. $\square$

The argument supplies the precise splitting antecedent needed to apply Q. It agrees with the projective-line characterization in the Stacks Project, Section 53.10, especially Lemma 53.10.2 and Proposition 53.10.4, but reconstructs the needed degree-one implication above without importing its other cases or its Riemann--Roch chain. Ordinary Čech cohomology, elementary rational-function/divisor calculus, and the valuative criterion of properness are algebraic foundations, not unproved arbitrary-field pluripotential inputs.

## 3. A fixed finite-disk point on the actual upstairs carrier

Now suppose the valuation of $K$ is nontrivial. Write
\[
C=\widehat{\overline K},\quad Y=X_C^{\mathrm{an}},\quad
\pi:Y\longrightarrow T,\quad G=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K/K).
\]
Use the isomorphism of Lemma 2 to identify $Y$ with $(\mathbb P^1_C)^{\mathrm{an}}$. The completed algebraic closure and its disk/tree structure are those in D and the nontrivial-field part of the complete Q body. The absolute value on $C$ has divisible logarithmic value group. Its nonzero values are unchanged by completion: if algebraic elements converge to a nonzero element, their absolute values are eventually equal to its absolute value. Thus every needed half-length in this group is again the logarithm of an element's absolute value.

In the chosen coordinates, a point $\zeta(b,r)$ with $b\in C$ and $r\in|C^*|$ is a type-II positive-radius finite disk point. Let $y_0$ be the coordinate Gauss point. The group action is semilinear, not a fixed-coordinate trivial action. Since the algebraic isomorphism is defined over a finite Galois $E/K$, the subgroup $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K/E)$ fixes $y_0$: Gauss evaluation is a coefficient maximum, and this subgroup fixes the coordinate sections and preserves coefficient norms. Consequently $S=G y_0$ is a finite set of type-II points.

**Lemma 3.** The action of $G$ preserves lengths and type II on the finite disk tree. The convex hull $H$ of $S$ is a finite compact metric tree and has a $G$-fixed type-II point $y$.

**Proof.** Under the chosen algebraic splitting, each group action is a valued coefficient automorphism followed by an algebraic automorphism of the split line. Such an automorphism pulls back $\mathcal O(1)$ to $\mathcal O(1)$ and therefore acts through an invertible matrix on its two-dimensional section space; it is a projective linear coordinate change. The coefficient automorphism sends disk centers to their images, preserves radii and preserves least containing disks. It is a tree isometry. A projective linear change is generated by translations, nonzero scalings and inversion. Translations preserve radii and least containing disks. Scalings multiply all radii and all center differences by the same norm, leaving logarithmic path lengths unchanged. For inversion, a disk with $r<|b|$ is sent to the disk of center $b^{-1}$ and radius $r/|b|^2$: expand $1/T$ on that disk or evaluate its geometric series. On the disk chains meeting zero, inversion interchanges the zero and infinity chains and replaces log radius by its negative. Subdivide an arc at its meeting disk if necessary; these formulas preserve each subarc length and hence the whole length. They preserve radii in $|C^*|$. Projective linear changes preserve the unique arcs constructed by the disk classification in Q, so the action has the asserted properties.

The convex hull of finitely many nonterminal disk points consists of finitely many finite arcs, and has no classical or type-IV terminal ends. Its leaves belong to the given finite set. The meet of two type-II disk points has radius $\max(r,s,|b-c|)\in|C^*|$. Therefore the finite hull's branch vertices are type II and its edge lengths lie in the logarithmic value group.

Here is the finite-tree fixed-point proof. Choose leaves $A,B\in S$ realizing the diameter $D$ of $H$, and let $y$ be the midpoint of $[A,B]$. If $x\in H$ projects to $p\in[A,B]$, put $t=d(A,p)$ and $h=d(p,x)$. Diameter maximality gives $t+h\leq D$ and $D-t+h\leq D$, so $h\leq\min(t,D-t)$. Hence
\[
d(y,x)=|t-D/2|+h\leq D/2.
\]
Any point $z$ has $\max(d(z,A),d(z,B))\geq D/2$. Equality is possible only at their midpoint: if its projection has coordinate $t$ and its off-segment distance is $h$, these two distances are $t+h$ and $D-t+h$, forcing $t=D/2$ and $h=0$. Thus $y$ is the unique center of minimum radius. Every isometry of $H$ fixes it. This includes every element of $G$. If $D=0$, use the sole point.

The endpoints and branch vertices of $H$ are type II. On the edge containing $y$, its distance from a type-II endpoint is obtained from $D/2$ by subtracting finitely many edge lengths. It lies in the divisible logarithmic value group. Moving that distance along the nested disk chain gives a radius in $|C^*|$, so $y$ is type II. This also covers a center that is already a branch vertex. $\square$

No fixed-point theorem for an abstract completion is used. The point is on the actual analytic carrier. Nor is an arbitrary analytic-topology compact orbit assumed bounded in its path metric: the orbit is **finite**, by algebraic coefficient descent.

## 4. Actual FS realization and descent of a norming reference

**Lemma 4.** On the actual rational bundle $L_C$ there is a continuous FS metric $\phi_y$ with genuine measure $a\delta_y$. After adding one finite constant it is $G$-invariant. Its descended continuous metric on $L$ satisfies (4).

\[

\]
are independent and basepoint free. The associated FS metric on $\mathcal O(1)$ is the standard Gauss metric in the coordinate $w=(T_1-bT_0)/(cT_0)$. It is continuous and has finite energy relative to any continuous reference. No formal frame, graph distribution, or model-recognition conjecture enters this construction.

Choose any continuous psh metric $\psi_0$ on $L$ (a generated positive multiple yields one). Its pullback is invariant. For $g\in G$, naturality sends the above continuous curvature to $a\delta_{gy}=a\delta_y$. Apply Q's every-solution uniqueness on the actual split $\mathbb P^1_C$ bundle to $\phi_y$ and $g^*\phi_y$: they differ by a finite constant as actual metrics at every point. Normalize $\phi_y$ by

\sup_Y(\phi_y-\psi_{0,C})=0.
\]
The supremum is finite since both metrics are continuous on the compact carrier. Semilinear pullback preserves the supremum relative to the invariant $\psi_{0,C}$. Thus each of the constants just obtained is zero and $\phi_y$ is genuinely invariant. This argument invokes Q only after both actual upstairs continuous solutions and their genuine measures have been exhibited.

D's continuous invariant metric descent gives a continuous actual psh reference $\psi$ on $L$ with $\psi_C=\phi_y$. O's pushforward gives

It remains to prove the norming part, which is not automatic from one atomic measure. In Gauss coordinate $w$, every section of $\mathcal O(d)$ is a polynomial of degree at most $d$ on the affine chart. Its norm for the degree-$d$ standard Gauss reference is
\[
\frac{|\sum_{j=0}^d b_jw^j|_x}{\max(1,|w|_x)^d}
\leq\max_j|b_j|.
\]
The second chart supplies the same bound at infinity. Gauss evaluation attains the coefficient maximum. Multiplying the reference by a constant only scales these section norms by a fixed factor. Hence **each** global section attains its global norm at $y$ for $m\phi_y$.

The scalar of an FS metric relative to $\phi_y$ is a finite maximum of these logarithmic section norms plus real weights, divided by $m$. All terms attain their supremum at the same point $y$, so the maximum does too. Pullback of a downstairs FS system is such an actual upstairs system. Surjectivity of $\pi$ in D gives
\[
\sup_T v=\sup_Y\pi^*v=(\pi^*v)(y)=v(\xi)
\]
for every downstairs FS scalar $v$.

Finally take an actual decreasing FS net $v_i\downarrow u$. The finite numbers $c_i=v_i(\xi)=\sup_Tv_i$ decrease. If their infimum were negative infinity, the inequalities $v_i\leq c_i$ would force the endpoint to be identically negative infinity. Otherwise the infimum $c$ is finite, $u\leq c$ everywhere, and $u(\xi)=c$. This proves (4) at all points, including poles. It also proves every actual psh scalar is finite at $\xi$. $\square$

This lemma verifies the full extra reference hypothesis of N on every genus-zero curve, rather than promoting N's projective-space example to arbitrary $X$. The descended reference need only be continuous actual psh; it is not claimed to be a single FS system over $K$.

## 5. Intrinsic targets and exact curve coercivity

Use the reference of Lemma 4 and normalize by $u(\xi)=0$. Then $u\leq0$, and put
\[

U_\mu=\sup_{u\in\mathcal E^1,\ u(\xi)=0,\ B(u)\leq1}A_\mu(u).

\[
J(\mu)<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad U_\mu<\infty

\mathcal E^1\subset L^1(\mu)
\tag{6}
\]
for every positive Radon mass-$a$ target. This verifies the intrinsic converse on the original carrier, not only integrability of invariant upstairs tests.

\[
\operatorname{MA}(tu)=(1-t)a\delta_\xi+t\operatorname{MA}(u).
\]

\[
E(tu)=\frac t2\int_Tu\,[ (2-t)a\delta_\xi+t\operatorname{MA}(u)]
=t^2E(u),\qquad B(tu)=t^2B(u).
\tag{7}
\]
Every integral is finite by O. At $t=0$ the metric is the reference, so no zero multiple of a pole is evaluated.

If (6) holds, choose $t=1$ for $B(u)\leq1$, and $t=B(u)^{-1/2}$ otherwise. The unit-cost bound gives the sharper curve estimates
\[
A_\mu(u)\leq U_\mu\max\{1,\sqrt{B(u)}\}
\leq U_\mu+U_\mu\sqrt{B(u)},
\tag{8}

\[
E(u)-\int_Tu\,d\mu\leq-B(u)+U_\mu+U_\mu\sqrt{B(u)},

The functional tends to negative infinity with the normalized cost. Coercivity is not used as a substitute for actual attainment; attainment is proved next from the actual upstairs equation.

## 7. An explicit nonsplit nondiscrete example

Let $K=\mathbb R((t^{\mathbb Q}))$ be the Hahn field of series with well-ordered rational exponent support and real coefficients. Give it $|f|=\exp(-\min\operatorname{supp}f)$. This is a real-valued nontrivial nondiscrete absolute value, its residue field is $\mathbb R$, and both characteristics are zero.

Completeness can be checked directly. From a Cauchy sequence choose a subsequence whose successive differences have valuation greater than the positive integer index. The sum of the first term and these differences defines a Hahn series: below any fixed real bound only finitely many difference supports occur, and a finite union of well-ordered subsets is well ordered. Any nonempty subset of the full support has a least element by restricting below one of its elements. Coefficients at each exponent are locally finite. The resulting series is the limit; the original Cauchy sequence has the same limit. This absolute value gives a metric, so sequential completeness is completeness. The leading coefficient order makes $K$ an ordered field.

Consider
\[
X:\ X_0^2+X_1^2+X_2^2=0\ \subset\mathbb P^2_K,
\qquad L=\mathcal O_X(1),\qquad a=2.
\tag{14}
\]
It is smooth and geometrically integral. The sum of three squares is positive for any nonzero triple in an ordered field, so $X(K)$ is empty and $X$ is nonsplit. Over $E=K(i)$ the map
\[
[s:r]\longmapsto[s^2-r^2:i(s^2+r^2):2sr]
\tag{15}
\]
is an isomorphism onto the conic. Its inverse is $[X_0-iX_1:X_2]$ on one chart and $[X_2:-X_0-iX_1]$ on the other; the displayed pairs agree on overlap. The two charts cover the conic. Formula (15) shows the actual bundle pulls back to $\mathcal O(2)$. The nontrivial Galois automorphism acts on the parameter by coefficient conjugation followed by $z\mapsto-1/z$, which fixes the Gauss point. The subgroup fixing $E$ fixes that point as well.

The embedding FS reference downstairs pulls back to twice the standard reference. Indeed $|2|=|i|=1$, and
\[

\]
as analytic section norms: the upper bound is ultrametricity, and the lower bound follows by recovering $2s^2$ and $2r^2$ from the first two terms. Thus (4) holds for this explicit reference, with raw reference measure $2\delta_\xi$.

There is also an explicit nonreference target. On the split upstairs line put $y_-=\zeta(0,|t|)$ and $y_+=\zeta(0,|t|^{-1})$. Their orbit is exactly this pair. Their common image is $\eta\ne\xi$: in D each fiber is precisely one orbit, whereas $\xi$ has its fixed Gauss-point fiber. \tag{16}
