## Exact source gap

Lyuhui Wu2609.27610v1, Lemma5.11 proof, LF1851--1872, asserts that if
g is not in (f), h=g/f^r is not regular, and a log|h|+b is bounded above by
max(u,-c log|f|)+O(1), then ar<=c. It justifies this by choosing a point of
Z(f) where g does not vanish. This implication fails when (f) is nonradical.

Take Y=A^1_k, f=z^2, g=z, r=1, u=0, c=1, a=2, b=0. Then g is not in (f),
h=1/z, and

    w=max(2 log|h|,0)=max(-log|f|,0)=u_c.

All are admissible rational-coefficient FS expressions on Y_f. Yet ar=2>1=c,
and g vanishes at every point of Z(f), so the chosen source point cannot exist.
This refutes the asserted intermediate denominator bound, **not** the hull
inequality of Lemma5.11 or the whole envelope theorem. Counting denominator
powers is not the same as counting actual pole orders.

## Proposed replacement lemma, valid without normality of Y

Let R be a finitely generated integral k-algebra, k trivially valued, f a
nonzero element, and Y=Spec R. Let L be a nonempty compact subset of Y_f^an,
x a point of its R-polynomial hull in Y_f^an, and assume

    |log|f||<=M on L union {x}.

Let u be an affine FS function on Y (a finite max of positive rational
coefficients times logarithms of regular functions and a constant floor).
Let h in R_f and a>0. Suppose

    a log|h|+b <= max(u,-c log|f|)+O(1) on Y_f^an,

with c>0. The proposed conclusion is

    a log|h(x)|+b <= sup_L(a log|h|+b)+2cM.

If h=0 or h(x)=0, the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise use the finite
normalization Rbar of R in its fraction field. At every prime divisor D of
Spec Rbar, evaluate the growth inequality on the analytic divisorial ray
tau ord_D, tau>0. These valuations restrict to points of Y_f^an because
f is nonzero as a rational function. Every element of R has nonnegative
order at D, so u(tau ord_D) is bounded above and below independently of tau.
For e=ord_D(f)>=0 the inequality forces

    a max(-ord_D(h),0)<=c e

whenever h has a pole. In particular no pole is allowed when e=0.

Choose positive integers p,q with q/p>c/a and put z=h^p f^q. Its order is
nonnegative at every prime divisor of Spec Rbar. Normality's codimension-one
intersection criterion gives z in Rbar, hence z is integral over R.
Also z is in R_f. The finite algebra B=R[z] therefore satisfies B_f=R_f;
the finite R-module B/R is killed by some f^N. For every ell>=1,

    H_ell=z^ell f^N=h^(p ell) f^(q ell+N) in R.

The polynomial-hull property applies to this actual regular function:

    log|H_ell(x)| <= sup_L log|H_ell|.

After multiplying by a/(p ell), subtracting the f term at x, and using
the two bounds by M, this becomes

    a log|h(x)|+b
       <=sup_L(a log|h|+b)+2a(q/p+N/(p ell))M.

First let ell->infinity, then choose rationals q/p decreasing to c/a. This
gives the stated 2cM bound. The O(1) in the growth assumption disappears in
the divisorial-ray limit. The finite normalization and the auxiliary finite
algebra are algebraic steps; no envelope regularization has been used.
If sup_L log|h|=-infinity, the same hull inequality for H_ell forces h(x)=0,
so the extended-real endpoint is also covered.

For an FS maximum w=max(a_i log|h_i|+b_i,lambda) bounded by u_c+O(1), apply
this argument to each branch; the constant branch is immediate. Taking their
maximum gives precisely the 2cM conclusion of the original Lemma5.11. It
handles actual poles even on nonnormal affine varieties and does not require
an artificial assumption that f defines a reduced hypersurface.

## Application to the localization maximum principle

If v is already in the corrected upper-growth regularizable affine class,
and v<=u+O(1), its existing regularization with controlled growth (source
Lemma3.25(2)) can produce FS approximants with growth at most 2u_c+O(1).
The repaired lemma then gives the 4cM bound used by Proposition5.10. On the
compact L, decreasing upper-semicontinuous approximants have decreasing
suprema converging to sup_L v; ordinary uniform Dini convergence of an
unbounded singular v is not required. Pointwise convergence handles x.
Letting c decrease to zero yields v(x)<=sup_L v.

This application assumes the regularizable membership and its controlled
approximation have actually been established; it must not be used to supply
that same membership circularly. The separate smooth local-truncation repair
and the subsequent singular induction remain to be checked in their own
dependency order. Other Runge, convex-approximation, measure, descent and
cardinality inputs of the envelope theorem are not verified merely by this
pole-order argument. Original solved count remains 3/42.
