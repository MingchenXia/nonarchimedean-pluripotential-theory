## F1. Finite polyhedral foundations

A cone with finitely many homogeneous rows has lineality equal to their common kernel. In a rational complementary space the pointed cone has a positive linear functional, obtained by summing its nonnegative defining rows. The section of value one is bounded: an unbounded sequence normalized to a unit limit would give a nonzero cone vector of value zero. A bounded polyhedron is generated by its finite vertices. At a nonvertex, move along the kernel of active rows in both signs until another independent row becomes active; boundedness supplies both endpoints, and induction on active rank gives a convex combination of vertices. Rational systems give rational vertices. Homogenizing Ax<=b as At<=lambda b, lambda>=0 gives point and ray generation; include both signs of lineality.

Generator representations are polyhedra because their nonnegative coefficient systems project to polyhedra. Eliminate a variable by pairing all its lower bounds with all its upper bounds, retaining rows independent of it. This is precisely nonemptiness of its allowable interval, including missing-sided and equality cases. Rational coefficients remain rational under elimination.

For E_i=conv A_i+cone R_i, the closed union hull is conv(union A_i)+cone(union R_i). The right side is a closed polyhedron containing the union. To obtain a source ray sr at a hull point u, use (1-delta)u+delta(a+sr/delta) with a in that ray's source polyhedron and let delta tend to zero. Approximate closed-hull u by ordinary-hull points, and add finitely many rays. Closure is essential.

For an upward stable lifted polyhedron with a common affine lower bound, every nonempty vertical fiber attains a finite minimum. In its inequality description all height coefficients oriented as lower bounds are nonnegative; positive ones divide to give finitely many affine lower rows, while zero ones describe the spatial domain. Thus the lower function is their maximum on its possibly proper domain and has an ambient support at every real point, including its boundary. This is a proof for a proper domain, not an unjustified full-space subgradient import.

If its point/ray generators have rational spatial coordinates and real heights, a lower graph face is exposed by t-ell(x). It uses only zero-slack points and rays, has no vertical ray, and projects injectively to their rational spatial hull/cone. Graph subfaces are also lower: sum active inequalities at a relative interior point; at least one positive height row is active and all others have nonnegative height coefficient. Alternatively perturb a lower exposure by a small subface exposure preserving finite positive slacks. Unique lower heights make projected intersections common faces. They form a finite rational carrier. On any fixed rational carrier, matching affine tuples satisfy rational homogeneous equations; a rational kernel basis exists by Gaussian elimination. Clear denominators to get integral tangent slopes and rational constants on each basis tuple. Any matching real tuple is in their finite real span. This proves genuine REAL-PA, without rounding real heights or assuming a generic irrational-wall maximum has rational spatial walls.

## F2. From fixed original supports to all structures

Suppose q is continuous and has an ambient touching affine lower support at every point on all original cells incident to its minimal original face. Its restriction to each convex original cell is a supremum of touching affine lower bounds, hence convex. A segment lying in X partitions into finitely many original-cell intervals. At a transition the common support puts the left directional slope below its slope and the right slope above it. Consequently there is no downward jump, and the whole restriction is convex. This also follows by concatenating one-variable secant inequalities.

For a PCC witnessed in any rational carrying structure, each segment from center to endpoint lies in X. A short initial piece is in an original closed cell containing the center. The support there bounds its initial derivative below, and whole-segment convexity extends the support inequality to the distant endpoint. Summing cancels the ambient covector by the barycenter equation. This proves PC for all structures and real endpoints, not just a single fixed structure.
