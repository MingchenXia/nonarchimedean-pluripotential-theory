\begin{abstract}
Single-face primal epigraph correction can match an entire unbounded face and provide common supports on entire incident cells. We reconstruct that statement, including its rational spatial carriers and real heights. Our new obstruction to iteration occurs on a positive balanced complex in every dimension at least two, with several different positive-dimensional shared faces, an actual admitted reference, and a fixed non-piecewise-affine continuous convex target. Starting from arbitrarily accurate cellwise real piecewise-affine majorants, every finite sequence of the prescribed face corrections fails simultaneous polyhedral convexity; alternating corrections converge geometrically on a fixed rational carrier. We then give a finite linear feasibility system exactly characterizing simultaneous continuous polyhedral convexity in specified polyhedral bands on a prescribed rational carrier. The system controls whole traces and ambient supports at all real face points and has no independence assumption on transverse directions. Its general feasibility is not proved. We independently reconstruct the supplied balanced three-line obstruction to ambient convex restrictions and extend it to positive-dimensional overlaps. These are limitations of construction methods and a finite certificate theorem, not counterexamples to balanced real-PA density. Original P05.1 remains OPEN; candidate=false.
\end{abstract}

\section{Elementary finite polyhedral facts}
\begin{lemma}[Generators, projections and finite-union hulls]\label{lem:hull}
A nonempty polyhedron has a finite representation $\conv A+\cone R$. Lineality is included by both signs of a basis. Rational polyhedra permit rational generators. Linear projections of polyhedra are polyhedra. If $E_i=\conv A_i+\cone R_i$ are finitely many nonempty polyhedra, then
\[
 \clconv\bigcup_i E_i=\conv\bigcup_i A_i+\cone\bigcup_i R_i.                 \tag{2.1}
\]
The hull on the left need not be closed before taking its closure.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a cone given by finitely many homogeneous rows, remove its lineality, the joint row kernel. In the pointed quotient the sum of nonnegative defining rows is positive away from zero. Its section of value one is bounded: normalization of an unbounded sequence would give a nonzero cone vector of value zero. A bounded polyhedron is generated by its vertices. Indeed, at a nonvertex move in both signs of a direction annihilating all active rows until additional independent rows become active. Boundedness supplies endpoints; express the original point as their convex combination and induct on active rank. There are finitely many full-rank row systems. Rational systems have rational solutions. This proves finite cone generation. Homogenize $Ax\leq b$ using $At\leq \lambda b$, $\lambda\geq0$, and separate positive and zero $\lambda$ generators to obtain points and rays. Add signed lineality generators.

For the converse and projection statement, introduce nonnegative generator coefficients and hull coefficients of sum one. Eliminate variables: pair every lower bound on a variable with every upper bound, retaining rows not involving it. These weak inequalities are exactly nonemptiness of the allowed interval; a missing side imposes no bound. This finite process proves polyhedrality and preserves rational coefficients. Equalities are paired inequalities. It also proves the statements in relative affine hulls.

The right side of (2.1) is a closed polyhedron and contains every $E_i$. Conversely, for $u$ in the ordinary hull, $a$ a point in the source of a ray $r$, and $s\geq0$, the source combinations
$(1-\delta)u+\delta(a+sr/\delta)$ converge to $u+sr$ as $\delta\downarrow0$. Pass first to limits of $u$, and then add finitely many rays. The closed hull contains the right side. This proves (2.1).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rational lower graph carriers with real heights]\label{lem:graph}
Let $K\subset E\times\R$ be upward stable, generated by finitely many lifted points and rays with rational spatial coordinates and arbitrary real vertical coordinates. Suppose it has an affine lower bound and a nonempty spatial domain $D$. Its attained fiber minima define a continuous finite convex polyhedral function $G$ on $D$. The domain and a finite carrier of its lower graph are rational spatial polyhedra. The function is genuine real PA on that carrier, even for a proper or lower-dimensional domain.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Spatial projection in the generator description is rational, hence $D$ is a rational polyhedron. A nonempty closed fiber bounded below attains its minimum. In a finite inequality description upward stability forces every nonspatial lower inequality to have a positive coefficient of height. Divide such rows by that coefficient. The resulting finitely many affine lower bounds give $G$ as their maximum on $D$. Rows of zero height coefficient describe $D$; affine-hull equations are spatial and can first be removed. Thus $G$ is continuous relative to $D$, and an active affine row gives an ambient support at every boundary or interior point.

A face exposed by $t-\ell(x)\geq0$ has point generators where equality holds and ray generators of zero slack. Its graph projects injectively; the vertical ray is excluded. The projection is the hull and cone of the corresponding rational spatial generators. Every graph subface also has an exposing functional with positive height coefficient: perturb its parent's lower functional by a sufficiently small face-exposing functional, preserving the finitely many positive generator slacks and the height sign. Equivalently use sums of active lower rows plus sufficiently small spatial active rows. The projected lower faces therefore form a finite rational complex: unique lower heights identify intersections and projection on any affine graph preserves its faces. They cover $D$.

Matching affine coefficient tuples on a fixed rational carrier satisfy rational homogeneous linear equations, tested on rational affine bases of intersections. Gaussian elimination gives a rational basis of the solution space. Clear denominators of each basis tuple so its intrinsic slopes are integral on all saturated tangent lattices and its constants are rational in rational affine coordinates. Each basis tuple is literal PA; the actual matching tuple is a finite real combination of them. This establishes precisely the scalar extension, without rounding heights or asserting closure under a generic irrational-wall maximum.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local supports imply every printed PCC]\label{lem:local}
Suppose a continuous function $q$ on a finite embedded complex admits, at each $y$, one ambient affine support on all original closed cells containing its minimal original face. Then $q$ satisfies the PC inequalities on every rational carrying structure, even with real endpoints.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A segment lying in $X$ meets finitely many original cells. Partition it at the endpoints of these intersections. Supports at points inside and at transitions imply local convexity of its one-variable restriction: the left slope at a transition is at most the supporting slope, which is at most the right slope. On an individual interval, supports at all points imply ordinary convexity by taking the supremum of the touching affine lower bounds. These inequalities concatenate, so the restriction to the whole segment is convex.

Given a PCC witnessed in any other structure, the segments from its center $y$ to all endpoints lie in that structure's incident convex cells, hence in $X$. Near $y$ a segment lies in an original closed cell containing $y$; this follows from the finite interval partition and closedness. The chosen support $p_y$ bounds its initial right derivative below. Whole-segment convexity then gives $q(x_i)-q(y)\geq p_y(x_i-y)$. Multiply by the coefficients and sum. The barycenter equation cancels $p_y$. This proves the claimed Jensen inequalities with no implicit coarsening assumption.
\end{proof}

\section{Reconstruction of ROOT310: one original face}
\begin{theorem}[Single-face correction]\label{thm:single}
Let nonempty closed rational cells $\sigma_i$ have a common nonempty actual face $\tau$. Let finite continuous convex $f_i$ agree on $\tau$. At every $y\in\tau$ assume one ambient covector supports all $f_i$ on their entire cells. Suppose convex rational-spatial real-PA majorants satisfy $f_i\leq P_i\leq f_i+\eta$. Then there are convex continuous rational-spatial real-PA functions $Q_i$ with
\[
 f_i\leq Q_i\leq P_i\leq f_i+\eta,
 \qquad Q_i|_\tau=g_\tau,
 \qquad f_\tau\leq g_\tau\leq f_\tau+\eta,
\]
and one common ambient support on all entire $\sigma_i$ at every real point of $\tau$. Bounded growth, exact recession, and contacts where some $P_i(y)=f_\tau(y)$ are preserved. The theorem has no assertion about other shared faces.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $E_i=\epi(P_i+I_{\sigma_i})$. Each rational affinity region gives graph point/ray generators with actual real heights and the vertical ray; include signed spatial lineality where appropriate. Convexity and Lemma~\ref{lem:hull} give such a finite representation of $E_i$ itself. Set
\[
 K=\clconv\bigcup_i E_i,\qquad G(x)=\min\{t:(x,t)\in K\},\qquad g_\tau=G|_\tau.
\]
A common original support at one fixed face point is an affine lower bound for every $E_i$ and hence $K$. Its domain is the rational spatial hull/cone of all generators, not necessarily all of $E$. All fibers in that domain have finite attained bottoms. Lemma~\ref{lem:graph} applies. At any $y\in\tau$, the original common support at $y$ bounds $G(y)$ below by $f_\tau(y)$; inclusion of each $E_i$ bounds it above by $\min_i P_i(y)$. Thus the asserted whole-face bound holds. Restrict the rational graph carrier to $\tau$ to obtain a rational carrier for $g_\tau$ and for $T=\epi(g_\tau+I_\tau)$.

Set $K_i'=\clconv(E_i\cup T)$ and define its lower function $Q_i$. Both generating sets lie in the closed convex epigraph of $f_i+I_{\sigma_i}$. The spatial domain is exactly $\sigma_i$, since $E_i$ already projects onto it and $\tau\subset\sigma_i$. Thus the fibers are attained and $f_i\leq Q_i\leq P_i$. The graph lemma supplies continuity and the finite rational carrier.

To check the exact restriction, expose $\tau$ \emph{inside} $\sigma_i$ by a rational affine form $a_i\geq0$ with zero set $\tau$. In the finite generator representation of $K_i'$, point values and linear ray values of $a_i$ are nonnegative. Its zero face uses only zero point generators and zero rays. Zero points from $E_i$ lie in $\epi(P_i|_\tau)$, a subset of $T$. If $(v,h)$ is a zero ray of $E_i$, then $y+tv\in\tau$ for $y\in\tau$, and
\[
 g_\tau(y+tv)\leq P_i(y+tv)\leq P_i(y)+th.
\]
For each affine branch of the finite maximum $g_\tau$, divide this inequality by $t$ and pass to infinity to obtain slope at most $h$ on $v$. Thus $(v,h)\in\rec T$ from every base point of $T$. This explicitly checks the needed recession inclusion; mere set inclusion is not used as a substitute. Signed lineality is included as separate rays. The zero face is exactly $T$, since $T$ itself is among its generators. If $\tau=\sigma_i$, the same argument with $a_i=0$ says directly $E_i\subset T$ and $K_i'=T$. Hence $Q_i|_\tau=g_\tau$.

Also $E_i,T\subset K$, so $K_i'\subset K$ and $Q_i\geq G$ on $\sigma_i$. At $y\in\tau$ equality holds. An active ambient affine lower row of $G$ at $y$ therefore supports all $Q_i$ on their entire domains. This covers irrational points, boundary points and unbounded parts of the face. At an initial exact contact, the sandwich forces equality throughout the face trace. Along any feasible recession ray, bounded differences from $f_i$ give the same limiting convex secant slope; the slopes are finite because $P_i$ is finite polyhedral. This proves exact recession and bounded-relative growth, including any actual admitted reference growth. No operator is assigned to $G$ or to other non-PA intermediates.
\end{proof}

If every pairwise intersection in a complex is exactly this one face, these supports and ordinary cell supports imply PC by Lemma~\ref{lem:local}; matching on a common finite rational refinement proves genuine global real PA. Dyadic shifts give decreasing uniform approximants in that restricted geometry: from $0\leq q_n-f\leq\eta_n=2^{-n}$, take $q_n+4\eta_n$, whose successive decrease is at least $3\eta_n/2$. The shifts lose unshifted contacts. This reconstruction is not our new general result. A smaller face of the incident union need not be exposed in $K$, and no iteration argument follows from the exposure inside one $\sigma_i$.

\section{New result: nontermination with several unbounded shared faces}
Fix $k\geq1$ and coordinates $(t,z)\in\R\times\R^k$ with lattice $\Z^{k+1}$. Let the five top cells be
\[
 (-\infty,0]\times\R^k,\quad [0,1]\times\R^k,\quad [1,2]\times\R^k,
 \quad [2,3]\times\R^k,\quad [3,\infty)\times\R^k.
\]
Include all four actual shared faces $H_j=\{j\}\times\R^k$, $j=0,1,2,3$. These affine faces have no nonempty proper faces. Give all top cells the same arbitrary actual real weight $w>0$. Their primitive normals at $H_j$ are $\pm e_t$, so this is positive and balanced everywhere. Its support is the complete $\R^{k+1}$, with several distinct unbounded positive-dimensional overlaps and noncompact proper original cells.

Keep the actual reference and target
\[
 \gamma(t,z)=\max(-t,0,t-3)+\sum_{r=1}^k|z_r|,
 \qquad f(t,z)=\max(-t,0,t-3)+\sum_{r=1}^k\sqrt{1+z_r^2}.                 \tag{4.1}
\]
The reference is literal PA and globally convex, hence PC. A fixed orthant refinement in $z$ carries it affinely and preserves balancing (opposite coordinate normals have equal inherited weights). On bounded $t$-slabs its nonzero recession slopes are $\sum|v_{z,r}|$; on outer slabs they are $-v_t+\sum|v_z|$ or $v_t+\sum|v_z|$ with the corresponding sign on $v_t$. All are strictly positive. Thus the actual reference is admitted without substitution. For $\phi(s)=\sqrt{1+s^2}$, $\phi''(s)=(1+s^2)^{-3/2}>0$ and $0<\phi(s)-|s|=1/(\phi(s)+|s|)\leq1$. These identities prove convexity, $0<f-\gamma\leq k$, and non-PA: restriction to any varying $z$ coordinate has strictly positive second derivative, whereas finite PA is affine on some nonempty interval.

\begin{lemma}[Arbitrarily accurate rational-carrier transverse roofs]\label{lem:roof}
For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a convex genuine real-PA $p(z)$ on $\R^k$, on a finite rational carrier, with
\[
 \sum\sqrt{1+z_r^2}\leq p(z)\leq\sum\sqrt{1+z_r^2}+\varepsilon,
 \qquad p^\infty(v)=\sum|v_r|.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\phi(s)=\sqrt{1+s^2}$ choose a positive rational $R$ with $\phi(R)-R<\varepsilon/(2k)$. Interpolate its actual real values on a rational mesh of $[-R,R]$ of maximal length less than $\varepsilon/(2k)$, including $0$. Extend outside using slopes $-1$ and $1$ through the endpoint values. Convex chord slopes increase and lie in $[-1,1]$, so the extension is convex. Chords majorize $\phi$; its Lipschitz constant one bounds their excess by the mesh length. On a tail, Lipschitzness gives majorization and the excess increases to $\phi(R)-R$. Sum these one-variable functions. Their product grid is rational; the real-scalar membership follows by the matching-kernel proof in Lemma~\ref{lem:graph}, or directly by real multiples of rational hinge functions and a real constant. Their exact tail slopes prove the recession formula. No target or sample height was rounded.
\end{proof}

On the five cells choose initial majorants $P_i(t,z)=\max(-t,0,t-3)+p(z)+h(t)$, restricted cellwise, where $h=0$ outside $[0,3]$ and is the affine interpolation of
\[
 (h(0),h(1),h(2),h(3))=(0,\eta,\eta,0),\qquad \eta>0.                \tag{4.2}
\]
Every individual majorant is convex on its entire original cell, continuous and real PA there. They match all whole-face traces initially, have the exact actual recession, and $0\leq P_i-f\leq\varepsilon+\eta$. Both parameters may be made arbitrarily small for the fixed target (4.1). They need not be PC simultaneously.

\begin{lemma}[Exact action of a face correction]\label{lem:update}
With current positive heights $(a,b)$ at $H_1,H_2$ and fixed zero heights at $H_0,H_3$, the correction of Theorem~\ref{thm:single} on $H_1$ replaces
\[
 a\longmapsto\min(a,b/2);
\]
on $H_2$ it replaces $b\longmapsto\min(b,a/2)$. It changes the neighboring slab interpolants to the corresponding affine chords and preserves all other face traces. Corrections on $H_0,H_3$ do nothing.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For two bounded adjacent slabs the hull lower function factors as the ordinary one-variable convex hull of their three graph vertices plus $p(z)$. The lower bound follows from convex Jensen for $p$ for every hull generator combination and from its exact recession inequality. Conversely use the same $z$ at all vertices, which realizes each one-dimensional hull height; upward rays give the rest. At the middle vertex the lower hull of heights $(u,v,w)$ at consecutive integer points has height $\min(v,(u+w)/2)$. This follows by either retaining the middle point or interpolating the two outer points; those are the only possible lower hull segments. Correcting one bounded slab by the new end-face epigraph gives precisely its endpoint chord plus $p$, as its other endpoint is an exposed face and the new chord lies below its previous affine formula. Thus $u=0,w=b$ gives the first update and $u=a,w=0$ gives the second.

At $H_0$ and $H_3$, a support of $p$ together with horizontal slope zero lies below both incident roofs since their excess heights are nonnegative. One roof already has face height $p$. Hence the common hull trace is exactly $p$, the face epigraph is already in both original epigraphs, and the correction changes neither. This also treats their unbounded recession directions without ignoring hull closure.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No finite correction schedule; geometric alternating limit]\label{thm:nonterm}
Starting with (4.2), no finite sequence of corrections at the four actual shared faces yields a PC function, although every correction supplies common ambient supports at all points of its processed whole face. Alternately process $H_1,H_2$. After $n\geq1$ full cycles,
\[
 a_n=\eta\,2^{1-2n},\qquad b_n=\eta\,2^{-2n}.                        \tag{4.3}
\]
The roofs converge uniformly, geometrically, on a fixed finite rational carrier to the globally convex real-PA function $\max(-t,0,t-3)+p(z)$. The process does not terminate in finitely many steps.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exact update map sends positive heights to positive heights. Hence all finite schedules leave $a,b>0$. PC at $H_1$ would require the one-variable slopes $a$ and $b-a$ to be nondecreasing, namely $b\geq2a$. PC at $H_2$ requires $a\geq2b$. These two inequalities imply $a=b=0$ when $a,b\geq0$. Therefore every finite schedule violates a legal printed PCC on at least one of these faces. At a violating face use the rational endpoints $(j-r,0),(j+r,0)$ for rational $0<r<1$, assigned to its two original slabs, and coefficients $1/2$. Their average is the center $(j,0)$ and the downward slope jump makes their value average strictly less than the center value. The failure is incident and legal, not a distant Jensen comparison.

The first two updates give $(a,b)=(\eta/2,\eta/4)$; every subsequent cycle multiplies both by $1/4$. This proves (4.3). After processing $H_1$ its two adjacent slopes agree. Processing $H_2$ then makes the right slope at $H_1$ equal to $-a/2$, while its left slope remains $a$. Thus its previous common support is destroyed, even though its whole trace has not changed. The endpoints just specified witness a deficit $3ra/4$ there. At every $z$, the same incompatible horizontal slopes preclude a common ambient support. The trace-matching property alone is insufficient.

The uniform excess over the limiting roof is $\max(a_n,b_n)=a_n$. The transverse carrier of $p$ and the original integer slab cuts remain fixed at every step. All intermediate functions have the same recession and are bounded relative to the actual $\gamma$. The limit is PC, real PA and within $\varepsilon$ above $f$. Thus this theorem concerns failure of finite iterative synchronization, even with a fixed finite carrier and arbitrarily accurate original majorants. It does not refute original regularization: this complete-affine pair already has regularity, and these limiting roofs themselves approximate the fixed target as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

\section{A different construction: simultaneous finite linear certificates}
\subsection{Finite inputs and explicit rows}
Let $\Pi$ satisfy the positive balanced hypotheses of Section 1. Fix the actual admitted real-PA reference $\gamma$. For each original top cell $\sigma$, let $A_\sigma,B_\sigma$ be continuous real-PA bounds on that cell, both at bounded distance from $\gamma$. They need not match across faces. Choose a finite rational refinement $\mathcal C$ of the original complex and all bound carriers. Unknown $q$ is affine on each carrier cell. No PA convexity condition is included in the input as an unexplained oracle.

For each nonempty $c\in\mathcal C$ choose rational generators
\[
 c=\conv V_c+\cone R_c,
\]
including both signs for spatial lineality. Use unknown ambient affine coefficients $(a_c,b_c)$ for its formula. Different ambient extensions of an intrinsic formula are harmless. Add the following finite linear rows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Matching: on every inclusion $\rho\preceq c$, impose $a_c v+b_c=a_\rho v+b_\rho$ for $v\in V_\rho$ and $a_c r=a_\rho r$ for $r\in R_\rho$.
\item Bands: for each $c\subset\sigma$, if the bounds on $c$ have formulas $A_c,B_c$, impose $A_c(v)\leq a_cv+b_c\leq B_c(v)$ for $v\in V_c$ and $a_A r\leq a_c r\leq a_B r$ for $r\in R_c$. At overlapping bounds include all relevant original-cell rows.
\item Supports: for every original face $\tau$ and every carrier cell $\rho\subset\tau$, introduce one ambient affine function $\ell_{\tau\rho}(x)=p_{\tau\rho}x+d_{\tau\rho}$. Impose equality with $q|_\rho$ on $V_\rho$ and $R_\rho$. For every original top cell $\sigma\succeq\tau$ and every $c\subset\sigma$, impose
\[
 a_cv+b_c\geq\ell_{\tau\rho}(v)\quad(v\in V_c),\qquad
 a_cr\geq p_{\tau\rho}r\quad(r\in R_c).                            \tag{5.1}
\]
\end{enumerate}
Every spatial coefficient in these rows is rational. The bound coefficients and solutions may be actual irrational reals. Affine matching equations are rational homogeneous equations; the remaining known band entries need not be rational. This is a finite real linear feasibility problem. Rational data also permit exact rational linear elimination. Prescribed rational contacts may be added as finite evaluation equalities without changing the theorem below, provided the same equalities are included in its stated existence condition.

\begin{theorem}[Exact simultaneous certificate on a fixed carrier]\label{thm:certificate}
The displayed system is feasible if and only if there exists a continuous printed-PC genuine real-PA $q$, affine on $\mathcal C$, with $A_\sigma\leq q|_\sigma\leq B_\sigma$ on every entire original cell. A feasible solution provides one common ambient support at every real point of every original face, on its entire incident original cells. The statement permits several distinct overlaps, dependent transverse directions, unbounded faces, proper cells and actual real weights.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For sufficiency, matching on points and rays means matching on their hull/cone, so the affine formulas define a continuous global $q$. On a generator representation, the finite band rows imply the bounds at every point, including rays and both signs of lineality. The bounds imply $q-\gamma$ bounded. The rational matching-kernel argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:graph} proves genuine real-scalar PA membership.

Similarly (5.1) implies $q\geq\ell_{\tau\rho}$ on every entire incident original top cell, while its equality rows give $q=\ell_{\tau\rho}$ throughout $\rho$. Every point $y\in\tau$ belongs to some such $\rho$, so this one affine function is a common support at $y$, including every irrational or boundary point. Taking $\tau$ to be a top cell also ensures supports at its interior affinity regions. Lower original cells lie in incident top cells. Lemma~\ref{lem:local} now gives every printed PCC in every structure. This proves the continuous PC conclusion without finite-point interpolation being substituted for whole-face compatibility.

For necessity, the assumed $q$ is continuous, printed-PC, finite, and bounded relative to the actual cellwise-affine $\gamma$ on its rational carrying refinement. All positive actual real weights are balanced there as required. Apply qualified S306's entire-incident-cell support theorem on a common refinement to obtain convexity on the original cells, or first use its real-endpoint PCC assertion on the original cells. Apply its support construction to the original face at a point of $\ri(\rho)$; if that point lies in a smaller original face, its incident top-cell collection contains every top cell incident to $\tau$. The same S306 result applies to a rational common refinement carrying $\gamma$; its support initially holds on the incident refined pieces. Convexity on each whole original cell extends that support along all segments from the point, so it supports the entire original incident cells. This last extension is essential when $\gamma$ is not affine on coarse original cells.

Choose one such support for $y\in\ri(\rho)$. Since $q$ is affine on $\rho$, testing its inequality in both small tangent directions at $y$ forces its restriction to agree with $q$ on the entire affine hull of $\rho$. In dimension zero equality at the point suffices. Consequently it is one support for the whole $\rho$, not an independently chosen family at sampled points. Its values and slopes satisfy all equality and inequality generator rows. The original affine formulas and band assumptions supply the other rows. Finitely many choices give a feasible solution. No rationality of the covectors or real heights is assumed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Relation to uniform approximation and the actual envelopes}
Suppose qualified S306 has supplied independent roofs $P_\sigma$ with $f\leq P_\sigma\leq f+\delta$ for a continuous PC target in the actual growth class. Use the above system with $A_\sigma=P_\sigma-\delta$, $B_\sigma=P_\sigma$ on a common rational carrier. If it is feasible, then
\[
 f-\delta\leq q\leq f+\delta,
 \qquad f\leq q+\delta\leq f+2\delta.                              \tag{5.2}
\]
Feasibility on some carrier for each vanishing $\delta$ would therefore yield uniform global real-PA regularization, with exact actual recession by bounded differences. \section{Reconstruction of ROOT304 and a higher-dimensional extension}

\begin{proposition}[Infinite ambient-convex restriction error]\label{prop:ambient}
Every finite convex function $F$ on all $\R^2$ has infinite uniform restriction error from (6.1). No finite globally convex minorant of (6.1) is exact at $(1/2,1/2)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every integer $T\geq2$, choose $a_T=(T,T)\in L_1$, $b_T=(2-2T,0)\in L_0$, and their midpoint $c_T=(1-T/2,T/2)\in L_2$. Their reference values are $T,2T-2,3T-5/2$. If restriction error is $e<\infty$, global convex Jensen gives
\[
 3T-5/2-e\leq F(c_T)\leq\tfrac12(F(a_T)+F(b_T))\leq3T/2-1+e,
\]
so $e\geq3(T-1)/4$ for every $T$, a contradiction. These distant endpoints are not an incident printed PCC at $c_T$ and cannot be used against the reference's PC property.

For the minorant assertion, a finite convex function on full $\R^2$ has an ambient subgradient at every point. An elementary justification is to bound it on a cube by its vertex values, bound it below on a smaller reflected cube, and use convex secants to obtain local Lipschitzness. Its epigraph is therefore closed. Nearest-point separation of $(x,F(x)-\delta)$ from that epigraph, followed by a normalized unit-normal limit as $\delta\downarrow0$, gives a nonzero support. Its height coefficient is negative: a zero height coefficient would be a nonzero spatial functional supporting a full spatial domain, which is impossible. Dividing gives the subgradient.

If a global minorant $G$ equals $\gamma$ at $(1/2,1/2)$, the two signed tangent secants along $L_1,L_2$ force every ambient support to have tangent slopes $1,6$. Their independent directions force $p=(-5/2,7/2)$ and the touching form $\ell(x,y)=-5x/2+7y/2$. But $\ell(-1,0)=5/2>\gamma_0(-1)=1$, contrary to $\ell\leq G\leq\gamma$. This proves the second assertion, without presuming a support of the original PC reference on the nonincident line.
\end{proof}

For every $k\geq1$ take the product of this actual complex with $\R^k$ as the chosen original complex, use a coordinate-orthant refinement to carry the reference, and add $\sum|z_r|$ to (6.1).
