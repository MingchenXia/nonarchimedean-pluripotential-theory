## 2. Converting an already justified positive-volume normalization

Suppose a normalized functional I has already been established on actual metrics in one retained positive-volume datum, with constant-shift law I(u+c)=I(u)+c. Let V>0 be that datum's proven actual total mass, and put E=VI. Then

$$E(u+c)=E(u)+Vc.$$

If its actual outer directional derivative is known to be a probability-measure pairing, multiplying it by V gives the raw derivative. ## 3. A general necessary and sufficient zero-mass uniqueness criterion

Let S be a nonempty class of actual metrics on one compact carrier Z, closed under addition of real constants. Assume an operator T assigns each u in S a positive finite Radon measure of total mass0. Positivity implies

$$T(u)=0\qquad(u\in S).$$

Consequently the equation T(u)=mu has a positive-measure solution exactly when mu=0; in that case every metric in S solves it. The following are equivalent:

1. Every two solutions of the zero-target equation differ by a real constant.
2. S modulo real constants has one element.

For1 implies2 apply uniqueness to any pair in S with the common zero target. For2 implies1 use the fact that zero is the sole solvable positive target. This criterion uses no probability normalization, topology, approximation theorem, or inverse-measure definition. It applies to any proposed positive full-mass zero-volume operator, independently of how that operator is reconstructed.

Thus a claimed extension with raw top-degree mass0 and uniqueness modulo constants must prove that its entire retained finite-energy reference cone is one additive-constant orbit. Restricting to the full effective-divisor reference in appendix118 can satisfy this requirement because that cone is explicitly proved to be sigma_D plus constants. A general pseudoeffective reference cannot be assumed to satisfy it.

## 4. Dual-energy target tests and loss of information under a map

Here is a separate exact algebraic obstruction to a proposed target characterization. Let p:Z -> W be a continuous map of compact Hausdorff spaces. Let Q consist of actual continuous functions on W and suppose a retained original test cone contains precisely their pullbacks p*Q. Retain any functional F:Q -> R. For a positive finite Radon measure mu on Z define

$$D(mu)=\sup_{q\in Q}\left(F(q)-\int_Zq\circ p\,d\mu\right).$$

The pushforward identity gives

$$D(mu)=\sup_{q\in Q}\left(F(q)-\int_Wq\,d(p_*\mu)\right).$$

Hence two measures with equal pushforward have exactly the same dual value, including infinity. The same conclusion holds for extended-valued tests whenever the relevant integrals are defined, by the pushforward identity for measurable integrals. Any mass/dual-finiteness condition built only from these pullback tests is unable to distinguish two different lifts of one target.

If a genuine geometric metric operator produces only one of those lifts, then mass and this dual value alone cannot characterize its range. A further condition on the actual datum's measure law is required and must be proved intrinsic. This is a test-space statement, not an assertion that every nonample cone descends under some map, nor a construction of a lift by definition.
