\subsection{Abstract and status}\label{abstract-and-status}

We do not solve the entire original P19.2. We prove a sufficient
analytic comparison criterion for recovery of every closed Legendre
level of actual weak rays with the same prescribed finite-energy start.
Finite additive errors disappear by finite-interval Perron comparison.
The criterion treats arbitrary actual rays, without maximality or an
entropy condition on their start. Relative ceilings converge at genuine
psh levels, and an identically minus-infinite level is recovered by
uniform divergence from above. Raw mixed-mass continuity is not
inferred. With a finite-entropy start the same criterion yields every
joint entropy/time divergence when the target has infinite entropy
slope, including the nonmaximal branch. This is a conditional analytic
result: no geometric sequence satisfying the criterion for arbitrary
targets has been constructed.

A second result gives two different closed relative P profiles on
projective space with identical complete scalar raw-mass profiles,
energy and entropy. Both terminal potentials are genuine zero-mass
potentials. The example demonstrates loss of information, not failure of
existence of an approximation. The paper states exact outstanding
geometric, singularity and entropy requirements. All new arguments are
unverified drafts relative to the supplied grants.
\textbf{candidate=false}.

\subsection{1. The unchanged problem and formulation
boundaries}\label{the-unchanged-problem-and-formulation-boundaries}

The exact original P19.2 asks to construct model approximations in the
residual hybrid programme that preserve all required energy, entropy and
singularity data. Energy convergence alone is insufficient, and
published/source-stated projective Fujita regimes are established
subcases. The family is posed on compact Kähler manifolds. The supplied
assignment requires arbitrary actual finite-energy weak rays and their
actual prescribed singular starts, closed finer relative P data and all
raw mixed masses; it does not permit silently imposing projectivity,
maximality or finite initial entropy. P19.1 is granted. P19.3 and P19.4
are separate targets, and no conclusion about them is asserted here.

The excerpt does not specify a unique norm for the closed profile, a
uniform-in-level quantifier, or one exclusive definition of allowable
model/flag approximation for a nonmaximal ray. The assignment requires
these matters to be made precise; it does not supply a density theorem
for them. Accordingly, we distinguish a necessary list of retained
objects from the additional sufficient comparison hypothesis below.
Failure of that stronger hypothesis would not answer the existence
question in the original scope.

The actual retained objects are the ray and start; the usual datum on
the original carrier; normalized energy and probability entropy; each
closed Legendre level and its relative ceiling against the actual start;
all positive-degree raw mixed masses; and the distinction between a
genuine zero-mass potential and the identically minus-infinite
placeholder. Testing only mass vectors forgets P data. Testing only
multiplier ideals uses I data and likewise does not establish the
requested finer P data.

A minimal precise reading of closed-profile recovery tests every fixed
level in its specified original parameter, with convergence of the
actual relative ceilings at genuine levels and separately of the raw
mass vector. At a placeholder boundary it tests extended convergence to
minus infinity, not a singularity metric. A uniform-in-level
singularity-metric statement is an additional possible signature,
exhibited in T and S on their exact domains; it is not automatically a
consequence of that per-level reading. No topology is asserted here to
be the unique missing topology prescribed by the catalogue.

\subsection{2. Normalization and actual geometric
signatures}\label{normalization-and-actual-geometric-signatures}

Let \(X\) be connected, smooth and compact Kähler, of dimension
\(n\ge1\). Fix a Kähler form \(\omega\), \(\alpha=[\omega]\),
\(V=\int_X\omega^n\), and \(\mu_0=\omega^n/V\). We use \[
dd^c=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial,
\qquad t=-\log|\tau|^2,
\qquad dd^c\log|f|^2=[\operatorname{div}(f)].                 \tag{1}
\] On \(\mathcal E^1(X,\omega)\), take the complete finite-energy
extensions of \[
E(u)=\frac1{(n+1)V}\sum_{k=0}^n\int_Xu\,
 \langle\omega_u^k\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-k},\quad
\operatorname{MA}(u)=V^{-1}\langle\omega_u^n\rangle,
\quad H(u)=\operatorname{Ent}(\operatorname{MA}(u)\mid\mu_0).             \tag{2}
\] Here \(E(0)=0\), \(E(u+c)=E(u)+c\); entropy is full probability
entropy and is infinite without absolute continuity. An actual weak ray
\(u_t\) is strongly continuous at zero and is the canonical
finite-energy weak geodesic on every finite interval. Its prescribed
start is \(w=u_0\), retained without alteration.

Its usual datum is obtained from the actual generic divisorial
coefficients of \(U(x,\tau)=u_{-\log|\tau|^2}(x)\), with the affine time
normalization of R when required. The full original \(X^\beth\) carrier
is retained, including supported semivaluations. No infinite point value
of a singular start is divided by time.

An actual relatively Kähler cohomological model is a smooth equivariant
proper modification \(\rho:\mathcal Y\to
X\times\mathbf P^1\), trivial off the central fiber, with vertical real
divisor \(D\), such that \(\mathcal A=\rho_X^*\alpha+[D]\) is relatively
Kähler. The central multiplicity of \(F\) is
\(b_F=\operatorname{ord}_F(\tau)\), and \[
f_D(v_F)=D_F/b_F,\qquad
\operatorname{MA}_\alpha(f_D)=V^{-1}\sum_F b_F(\mathcal A^n\cdot F)\delta_{v_F},
\quad E_\alpha(f_D)=\frac{\mathcal A^{n+1}}{(n+1)V}.        \tag{3}
\] The compatibility sign is \(U+\log|s_D|^2=O(1)\) for bounded
compatible weights. In transcendental classes this is cohomological
geometry; no rational polarization is inferred. The smooth
compactification and trivialization near infinity are those in R/E.
Probability entropy is
\(H_\alpha(\phi)=\int_{X^\beth}A_X\operatorname{MA}_\alpha(\phi)\). On
an SNC model, the factor on \(F\) is \((\kappa_F+1-b_F)/b_F\), with
\(\kappa_F\) its relative canonical coefficient. A log-discrepancy
modification need not be semiample.

For a ray define \[
\chi_u(\lambda)=\inf_{t\ge0}(u_t-t\lambda),\qquad
\mathscr P_w[h]=\left(\lim_{C\to\infty}P_\omega(h+C,w)\right)^*.
                                                               \tag{4}
\] For genuine \(h\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega)\), the raw mixed
masses are \[
M_k(h)=V^{-1}\int_X\omega^{n-k}\wedge
                \langle\omega_h^k\rangle,\qquad1\le k\le n.   \tag{5}
\] If explicitly needed at \(h\equiv-\infty\), only these
positive-degree numbers are extended by zero. That convention supplies
no current, probability measure, singularity type or singularity metric.
For genuine \(h\), \(M_0(h)=1\), and it is not extended to the
placeholder. The absolute ceiling is \(\mathscr P_0[h]\), distinct from
the ceiling against \(w\).

\subsection{4. Uniform affine comparisons of actual
rays}\label{uniform-affine-comparisons-of-actual-rays}

\begin{lemma}[Removal of bounded time errors]\label{remove}
Let $u,v$ be actual finite-energy weak rays from the identical
$w\in\mathcal E^1$. If $a\in\mathbb R$, $C\ge0$, and
$v_t\le u_t+at+C$ for every $t\ge0$, then
$v_t\le u_t+at$ for every $t\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

\emph{Proof.} Fix \(0<t<T\). The canonical segment from \(w\) to
\(u_T+aT+C\) is \(u_s+as+Cs/T\), \(0\le s\le T\): it is the given
canonical geodesic plus an affine constant in time. The ray \(v\)
restricted to this interval has smaller terminal boundary and the same
initial boundary. Finite-energy Perron comparison from R gives
\(v_t\le u_t+at+Ct/T\). Let \(T\to\infty\). Inequalities between psh
representatives persist, including their pole sets. The assertion at
zero is already the identical prescribed start. \(\square\)

Applying this result in both directions proves that two actual rays from
the same start with a bounded difference over the whole half-line are
identical. Thus uniform additive approximation over all times is a rigid
additional formulation, not a reasonable hypothesis to silently
substitute for the original approximation problem. With different
starts, stationary rays at two distinct constant potentials already have
bounded pointwise difference without being identical.

\begin{theorem}[Closed-level recovery under an ordered affine bound]
\label{levels}
Let $u,v_j$ be actual finite-energy weak rays from the identical
$w\in\mathcal E^1$. Suppose $\epsilon_j\ge0$,
$\epsilon_j\to0$, and finite $C_j\ge0$ satisfy, for every
$x\in X$ and $t\ge0$,
\[
u_t-C_j\le v_{j,t}\le u_t+\epsilon_jt+C_j.                \tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
u_t\le v_{j,t}\le u_t+\epsilon_jt,
\qquad d_1(v_{j,t},u_t)\le\epsilon_jt,                   \tag{7}
\]
and every original closed parameter satisfies
\[
\chi_u(\lambda)\le\chi_{v_j}(\lambda)
                  \le\chi_u(\lambda-\epsilon_j),
\qquad\chi_{v_j}(\lambda)(x)\to\chi_u(\lambda)(x).       \tag{8}
\]
At a genuine psh target level and genuine psh approximating levels,
their relative ceilings against the unchanged $w$ converge
pointwise and in $L^1$. If $\chi_u(\lambda)\equiv-\infty$,
then $\sup_X\chi_{v_j}(\lambda)\to-\infty$.
The energy slopes $c_{v_j}$ satisfy
$0\le c_{v_j}-c_u\le\epsilon_j$.
Their actual usual NA data $\phi,\psi_j$ also satisfy
$\phi\le\psi_j\le\phi+\epsilon_j$ on the full original carrier, in
the extended order sense, and their NA energies converge.
No mixed-mass, singularity-metric or fixed-time entropy continuity is
part of the conclusion.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof.} Lemma \ref{remove} applied twice eliminates \(C_j\).
Energy monotonicity, translation, and the ordered distance identity give
(7). Taking the infimum of the two pointwise bounds in (7) over
\emph{the entire} \(t\ge0\) gives (8)'s inequalities; no exchange of an
index limit with that infimum occurs.

For fixed \(x\), \(f_x(\lambda)=\inf_{t\ge0}
(u_t(x)-t\lambda)\) is decreasing and upper semicontinuous as an infimum
of continuous affine functions of \(\lambda\). A term with value minus
infinity simply makes the infimum identically minus infinity. There are
no positive infinite values since the term at zero is \(w(x)\).
Therefore \(f_x(\lambda-\delta)\downarrow
f_x(\lambda)\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\), in the extended sense.
Squeezing proves the pointwise assertion at every parameter, including a
genuine zero-mass terminal potential or a placeholder.

The infimum of usc spatial functions is usc. Put
\(\delta_j=\sup_{m\ge j}\epsilon_m\downarrow0\); this is finite after
discarding finitely many indices. If the target level is identically
minus infinite, the usc functions \(\chi_u(\lambda-\delta_j)\) decrease
to minus infinity on compact \(X\). For each \(A>0\), their open
sublevel sets below \(-A\) increase to cover \(X\). A finite subcover
gives one index with the entire function below \(-A\). The upper bound
in (8) proves uniform divergence from above, including if some
approximating levels already are placeholders.

Where all levels are genuine psh, S's recognition lemma makes them their
actual relative ceilings. Also
\(\chi_u(\lambda)\le\chi_{v_j}(\lambda)\le w\le\sup_X w\). Genuine psh
potentials are locally integrable and have poles only on a measure-zero
set. This integrable lower bound and a finite upper bound give \(L^1\)
convergence by dominated convergence. This applies even when the
target's top mass is zero. Finally ray energy is affine with the common
initial value \(E(w)\); divide its bounded difference from (7) by
\(t>0\) to get the slope bound.

For the usual data, pull the inequalities in (7) to a generic central
Gauss component \(F\). The coefficient of \(t\) there is
\(-b_F\log|s_F|^2\) in (1). The negative normalized divisorial
coefficients defining the actual usual data are therefore between those
of \(u\) and \(u+\epsilon_jt\), whose data are \(\phi\) and
\(\phi+\epsilon_j\). This uses the actual generic coefficients, not
pointwise division of a pole by time. R's divisorial order determination
gives the same inequalities on the original \(X^\beth\), including
supported semivaluations. Monotonicity and translation of NA energy give
\(0\le E_\alpha(\psi_j)-E_\alpha(\phi)\le\epsilon_j\). \(\square\)

This sufficient hypothesis addresses actual relative ceilings; applying
an absolute ceiling would change the obstacle. It is stronger than
bounded-time \(d_1\) recovery or strong NA recovery and has not been
shown available for arbitrary geometric approximants. In particular, it
is not a completed construction for the nonmaximal branch.

\subsection{5. A conditional entropy theorem including nonmaximal
targets}\label{a-conditional-entropy-theorem-including-nonmaximal-targets}

Set \(\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)=-dd^c\log\omega^n\),
\(\overline S=nV^{-1}\int\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)\wedge\omega^{n-1}\), and
\[
T_\gamma(h)=V^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_Xh\,\gamma\wedge
 \langle\omega_h^k\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-1-k},\qquad
M(h)=H(h)+G(h),\quad G=T_{-\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)}+\overline S E.
                                                               \tag{9}
\] The probability entropy coefficient is one. R/E supply the finite
contracted-energy extensions and the lower semicontinuous convex
extended Mabuchi functional in exactly (1)--(2).

For a smooth positive closed \(\gamma\), define
\(K_\gamma=nV^{-1}\int_X\gamma\wedge\omega^{n-1}\). We need the
elementary comparison \[
u\le v\le u+c\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
0\le T_\gamma(v)-T_\gamma(u)\le K_\gamma c.             \tag{10}
\] For bounded potentials, differentiate along the affine psh segment
\(u+s(v-u)\). Stokes and differentiation of the finite sum give
\(\frac{d}{ds}T_\gamma=nV^{-1}\int(v-u)\gamma\wedge
\omega_{u+s(v-u)}^{n-1}\ge0\). This follows for bounded psh potentials
by the Bedford--Taylor energy cocycle granted in R; it does not require
a differentiable geodesic. Translation adds exactly \(K_\gamma c\),
since these bounded mixed products have their fixed cohomological
masses. For finite-energy \(u\le v\), their bounded truncations
\(\max(u,-m)\le\max(v,-m)\) converge decreasingly and in \(d_1\). The
contracted-energy continuity in E passes the monotonicity inequality to
the limit. Finite-energy cohomological mixed masses also pass the
translation identity. Applying monotonicity to \(v\le u+c\) proves the
upper bound in (10).

Write the fixed signed form \(-\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)=
\gamma_+-\gamma_-\) with smooth positive closed forms, using a
sufficiently large fixed multiple of \(\omega\). From (7), (10) and
energy comparison, \[
|G(v_{j,t})-G(u_t)|\le K_G\epsilon_jt,
\qquad K_G=K_{\gamma_+}+K_{\gamma_-}+|\overline S|.       \tag{11}
\] R's arbitrary-ray contracted-energy slopes give a finite limit
\(g_u=\lim_{t\to\infty}G(u_t)/t\). Thus (11) gives
\(G(v_{j,t_j})/t_j\to g_u\) on every joint sequence.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional joint infinite entropy recovery]
\label{entropy}
Under Theorem \ref{levels}' hypotheses, assume additionally
$H(w)<\infty$ and $H(u_t)/t\to+\infty$. Then every joint
$j\to\infty$, $t_j\to\infty$ satisfies
\[
H(v_{j,t_j})/t_j\longrightarrow+\infty.                 \tag{12}
\]
The approximating rays need not be maximal; their finite-time entropy
may be infinite. In particular the conclusion applies to every
nonmaximal target in R's actual-ray domain if the ordered affine
comparison sequence exists. No such geometric existence is claimed.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof.} Put \(a=M(w)<\infty\), \(f_j(t)=M(v_{j,t})\),
\(f(t)=M(u_t)\). These are extended convex functions with initial value
\(a\). For \(t\ge T>0\), convexity gives \[
\frac{f_j(t)}t\ge\frac a t+\frac{f_j(T)-a}T.             \tag{13}
\] If \(f_j(T)=+\infty\), convexity with finite value at zero implies
that all \(f_j(t)\), \(t\ge T\), are infinite; thus (13) is also valid
in that case. Fixed-time convergence in (7), and the granted lower
semicontinuity, give \(\liminf_j f_j(T)\ge f(T)\). Consequently every
joint liminf is at least \((f(T)-a)/T\). The target has infinite entropy
recession and finite \(g_u\), so \(f(t)/t\to+\infty\). If some \(f(T)\)
is infinite, the fixed-\(T\) bound already proves joint divergence.
Otherwise let \(T\to\infty\) after taking the joint liminf. Subtract the
finite joint correction in (11) to obtain (12). This extends the
elementary infinite-recession chord argument to possibly infinite
approximating functions; it does not use fixed-time entropy continuity.
\(\square\)

This theorem is weaker than an existence result and stronger than an
iterated entropy limit within its assumed comparison signature. For
finite-entropy maximal targets, E already supplies general joint model
recovery in its exact domain and is not reproved as a new result. For
infinite-initial-entropy starts (13) has no finite initial value; that
domain remains outside this conditional entropy theorem.

\subsection{6. Two different genuine zero-mass profiles with the same
scalar
data}\label{two-different-genuine-zero-mass-profiles-with-the-same-scalar-data}

We now give a concrete information-loss example within actual geometry.
It is not an obstruction to existence of an approximation.

\begin{proposition}\label{two}
On $(\mathbf P^n,\omega_{\rm FS})$, $n\ge2$, normalized to
volume one, there are two actual smooth zero-start maximal rays with
the same energy, the same entropy at every time, and the same complete
raw mixed-mass profile at every closed Legendre level. Their relative

\subsection{7. Boundary and model

Even terminal-parameter alignment matters. On any fixed compact Kähler
