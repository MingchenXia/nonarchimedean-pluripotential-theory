# n2. Canonical obstacle ideals and a finite-generation envelope criterion

Support: entire qualified G331 and earlier own n1 (the quotient-centre and signed-model evaluation argument). No historical test-ideal or isolated-envelope theorem is used. The finite-generation condition below is obstacle dependent and explicit; it is not assumed for arbitrary excellent germs or arbitrary obstacles.

Let R be excellent reduced local of positive dimension, a a proper primary gauge, X=V_a(R), and f a finite continuous real obstacle. Keep every prime support. Define, for m>=1,

\[
A_m(f)=\{g\in R: v(g)+m f(v)\ge0\text{ for every }v\in X\},\quad A_0=R,
\quad h_m=-v(A_m)/m.
\]

Each A_m is an ideal: addition uses the ultrametric inequality and multiplication by R uses nonnegative semivaluation values. Put C_0=max(0,-min_X f), and choose any positive integer N_m>=m C_0. Then a^{N_m} subset A_m. If f has a negative value, no unit can belong to A_m, so it is a proper primary ideal for EVERY m. If f>=0 everywhere, A_m=R and the envelope is zero. Thus every h_m is a finite continuous actual geometric primary profile, admissible below f. This already proves all-support primaryness without a Jacobian correction, colon saturation or division of infinite values.

The ideals are graded: A_m A_n subset A_{m+n}. They are integrally closed as ideals: in a monic equation for g integral over A_m, a finite v(g)<v(A_m) would make the monic term uniquely least. Therefore v(g)>=v(A_m)>=-m f(v); infinite values are automatic. This uses only finite ideal values, since A_m contains a primary power.

Define the raw function p(f)=sup\{u in D:u<=f\}. It equals sup_m h_m, and also the supremum with C in place of D. To prove this without claiming positivity of a supremum, a rational competitor -v(I)/r<=f has I subset A_r, and hence is <=h_r. For g in C below f, approximate uniformly by rational profiles and subtract an arbitrarily small negative rational a-gauge constant to keep the approximant below f. Let the error tend to zero. If u=inf_i g_i in an ACTUAL decreasing C-net and u<=f, the open sets {g_i<f+epsilon} cover X. They are directed increasing; compactness gives one index valid everywhere. Thus g_i-epsilon is a C competitor and u<=sup_m h_m+epsilon. Let epsilon tend to zero. Continuity of f is essential to this open-cover step. Every h_m is a D competitor, proving equality in both directions. Poles of u and the bottom element cause no problem.

Pointwise convergence of the FULL degree sequence follows directly. Write m=kr+s, 0<=s<r. Since A_1^s A_r^k subset A_m, with M_1=||v(A_1)||_infinity,

\[
h_m(v)\ge h_r(v)-rM_1/m,\qquad h_m(v)\le p(f)(v).
\]

Here h_r<=0 and all ideal values are finite. This proves lim_m h_m=p(f) pointwise, with no subsequence or positivity assertion. In particular for H_N=max_{1<=r<=N} h_r,

\[
H_N-NM_1/m\le h_m\le p(f).
\]

H_N is an actual geometric continuous competitor, using G331's closure under finite maxima. The raw p(f) is finite, nonpositive and lower semicontinuous, bounded below by h_1. No upper semicontinuous regularization is declared positive.

THE SUFFICIENT CONDITION. Suppose the canonical graded algebra A(f)=direct-sum_{m>=0} A_m(f)t^m is finitely generated over R. Choose finite homogeneous generators g_i t^{d_i}, d_i>=1, and discard zero generators. Degree-zero coefficients are in R; positive-degree generators can be chosen homogeneous by decomposing a finite generating set. Let L be a common multiple of all d_i, D_0=max_i d_i, and

\[
J=(g_i^{L/d_i})_i,\qquad H=-v(J)/L.
\]

Every element of A_m is an R-linear combination of degree-m monomials in these generators. Hence, on every support,

\[
v(A_m)\ge m\min_i v(g_i)/d_i.
\]

There is always a finite minimum: the algebra contains the primary ideal A_1 t, and if all generator values were infinite, all values of A_1 would be infinite. More algebraically, the radical of the generator coefficient ideal contains A_1; therefore J contains a primary power and is proper primary when f has a negative value. Also J subset A_L, so H<=f and is an admissible actual geometric primary profile. The preceding bound gives every h_m<=H; since the generators' pure powers belong to A_L, H<=h_L. Thus h_L=H=p(f). At a point where i realizes min v(g_i)/d_i, h_{d_i}>=-v(g_i)/d_i=H, whereas h_{d_i}<=H. Consequently H=max_{1<=r<=D_0} h_r as functions. The unconditional residue estimate now gives

\[
0\le P(f)-h_m\le D_0 M_1/m\qquad\text{on ALL }X.
\]

This is a proved obstacle-dependent UNIFORM vanishing error. Its correcting ideal A_1 is primary and fixed for this obstacle; the constant D_0 reflects this particular graded algebra. The estimate never invokes a finite value of a nonprimary Jacobian or conductor. It includes singular-supported valuations, mixed characteristic, imperfect residue fields, nonnormal rings and all quotient centres. H has actual negative effective nef Cartier presentation on Bl_J(R). G331's comparison and the raw equality prove it is the greatest D competitor, not just the supremum of an ideal family. Its continuity and geometric positivity are actual conclusions.

An exact algebraic characterization clarifies the condition. For finite continuous f on an excellent reduced germ, the following are equivalent: (i) A(f) is finitely generated; (ii) its raw geometric envelope is a rational unit-or-primary profile -v(J)/q; (iii) p(f) equals a finite maximum of its canonical profiles h_m. We proved (i) implies (ii) and (iii). A finite maximum is a rational primary profile by taking a common denominator and the sum of the corresponding powers of the A_m, so (iii) implies (ii).

For (ii), because h_m<=H and H<=f, the defining ideal is exactly

\[
A_m=\{g:v(g)\ge (m/q)v(J)\text{ on all }X\}
    =\{g:g^q\in\overline{J^m}\}.
\]

The first equality follows by bounding each g in A_m below by v(A_m); its converse uses H<=f. The second uses the Noetherian domain centred-DVR integral-closure criterion on every minimal-component quotient and then the all-minimal-component criterion. Every such quotient has positive dimension: a zero-dimensional minimal component of a local reduced ring would make its maximal ideal minimal and force dimension zero. The images of a,J are primary; every centred DVR normalizes into X. Integral dependence implies the opposite inequality on ALL supports by the monic least-term argument. These are the exact Swanson--Huneke 6.8.3--4 applications, with no missing zero-dimensional component.

Let B=R[Jt^q]. It is a reduced excellent finite-type algebra. Its total quotient ring is the product of K_j(t^q), where K_j are the fraction fields of the minimal-component domains; J contains a regular element by prime avoidance. The product of K_j(t) is a finite field-product extension. Excellence/Nagata finiteness makes the integral closure of B in this extension a finite B-module, including inseparable extensions. For g in A_m, a monic ideal-integrality equation for g^q over J^m, multiplied by the appropriate powers of t, makes g^q t^{mq} integral over B. Then g t^m is integral by its qth-power equation. Thus A(f) lies in this finite normalization and contains B. It is a B-submodule, hence a finite B-module by Noetherianity. This proves finite generation over R. In fact (ii) also gives h_q=H directly from J subset A_q.

The converse uses the standard excellent/Nagata normalization property in finite field extensions, not normalization of an arbitrary nonexcellent algebra. The graded algebra is constructed from the fixed obstacle, not a Cartier algebra of Frobenius operators. No general graded finite-generation premise has been introduced.

For a signed integral Cartier model obstacle f_D on an actual primary blowup of a normal R, one can compute these ideals on its finite normalization Y. Precisely, A_m=H^0(Y,O_Y(mD)) intersect R, using the same canonical rational trivialization of the exceptional divisor. The all-boundary inequality implies the height-one section tests; nonexceptional primes impose only regularity of g. Normal sections give one direction. Conversely pull a section to the valuation ring of Frac(R/p) as in n1: its coefficient in the local invertible lattice has nonnegative value, so v(g)+m f_D(v)>=0, with infinite g values automatic. This proves the equality on every support. For a rational divisor use multiples of its denominator. Thus finite generation of this PARTICULAR section-intersection algebra supplies the uniform construction for a signed primary/model obstacle; its finite generation is a hypothesis to prove in any application, not a theorem for arbitrary model obstacles.
