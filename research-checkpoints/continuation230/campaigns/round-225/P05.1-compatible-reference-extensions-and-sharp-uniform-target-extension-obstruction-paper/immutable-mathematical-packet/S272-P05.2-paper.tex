\begin{abstract}
For a finite positive balanced rational space globally embedded in one
lattice vector space, we prove raw mixed completion for every actual real
piecewise-affine positive-growth reference on rational carrying geometry.
A rational component of the reference supplies legitimate compact-test
auxiliaries and rational cutoffs. The common height constant on a fixed
relative-bound family is $(2d/\epsilon)CM+J_0$. No global weak or
bounded-Lipschitz uniformity is asserted. A different, finite convex
decomposition realizes any rational polyhedral convex local jet relative
to the actual reference, preserving its flat relations and raw atom.
Consequently the existing geometric finite-PA contact criterion extends
to arbitrary real references, with genuine PA roof witnesses. The
higher-dimensional all-obstacle envelope-domain and non-PA contact
classification remains open. These are unverified drafts;
candidate status is false.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact target, categories, and granted dependencies}
The original problem asks for necessary and sufficient structural
conditions on the actual pair $(X,\gamma)$, including genuine
regularization and the contact identity for \emph{all} admissible
obstacles. Neither raw mass nor the construction of an operator on a
proper subdomain satisfies that target. This paper proves two missing
actual-reference antecedents and a resulting necessary geometric
condition. It does not claim the entire classification.

Throughout $X\subset N_\R$ is the support of a finite rational complex,
pure of dimension $d\geq1$, with specified positive real top weights
$w_\sigma$ satisfying lattice balancing. The actual reference
$\gamma\in\PA_\R(X)\cap\PC_q(X)$ is positive at infinity. All geometry
is rational; affine coefficients may be irrational. The coefficient
class $\PA_\Z$ has integral tangent linear parts and rational constants;
$\PA_\R$ has arbitrary real affine coefficients on a \emph{finite
rational} carrying complex. Rational-affine functions need not belong
to $\PA_\Z$. Sums on common rational refinements stay in $\PA_\R$;
maxima with irrational affine data need not do so.

The printed class $\PC_q$ uses rational PCC endpoints: a PCC has
$x=\sum_i\alpha_i x_i$, $\alpha_i\geq0$, $\sum_i\alpha_i=1$,
$x_i\in X\cap N_\Q$, and faces $\tau\preceq\sigma_i$ on some
rational structure with $x\in\tau$, $x_i\in\sigma_i$. Centers and
coefficients may be real. Put $\PAPC_\R=\PA_\R\cap\PC_q$ and
\begin{equation}\label{envelope}
 P_\gamma(u)=\sup\{q\in\PC_q:q\leq\gamma+u,\ q\leq\gamma+O(1)\}.
\end{equation}
Competitors are not restricted to PA functions or assumed continuous.
For $u\in C_b(X)$, caller grant R230 identifies this unrestricted
envelope with its continuous completion, gives
$|P_\gamma(u)-\gamma|\leq\|u\|_\infty$ and the uniform envelope
inequality. It supplies no PA approximants in higher dimension.

The new real approximation domain is exactly
\[
 \mathcal D_\R(\gamma)=\overline{\{f\in\PAPC_\R(X):f-\gamma
                   \text{ bounded}\}}^{\,\|\cdot\|_\infty},
\]
inside continuous functions with bounded difference from this same
actual reference. We prove that this equals the decreasing uniform
approximation domain. No other continuous PC function is added.

For a cell, use the saturated lattice
$N_\sigma=N\cap\operatorname{span}_\R(\sigma-\sigma)$. An inward lift
$n_{\sigma/\tau}$ projects to the primitive generator of
$N_\sigma/N_\tau$ into $\sigma$. The positive convex raw divisor is
\begin{equation}\label{divisor}
 D_f c(\tau)=\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}c(\sigma)f_\sigma(n_{\sigma/\tau})
 -f_\tau\left(\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}c(\sigma)n_{\sigma/\tau}\right).
\end{equation}
Linear parts occur here. The raw finite mixed measure is $D_{f_1}
\cdots D_{f_d}[X]$ as an atomic measure. There is no additional factorial.

\section{Finite raw calibration and bounded integration by parts}
We inspected the complete pertinent proof units in the frozen original
BMPS v1 and BBGS publisher files. Here are the normalization and exact
finite identities used in each new application. They also follow from
the granted S262 finite recursion.

Choose an auxiliary Euclidean inner product and let $a_\sigma$ be the
covolume of $N_\sigma$ in its tangent space, with $a_v=1$. Saturation
makes a facet basis followed by a primitive inward lift a basis of
$N_\sigma$. Its perpendicular height is therefore $a_\sigma/a_\tau$.
If $\nu_{\sigma/\tau}$ is the inward unit conormal, then
\[
 \operatorname{pr}_{N_{\tau,\R}^{\perp}}n_{\sigma/\tau}
             =(a_\sigma/a_\tau)\nu_{\sigma/\tau}.
\]
Thus the Euclidean weights $a_\sigma c(\sigma)$ are balanced. Embed
$x$ as $(x,1)$ to meet BBGS's hyperplane quasi-embedding convention;
all tangent directions retain their original metric. Subtract an
ambient extension of $f_\tau$ in \eqref{divisor}. Continuity cancels
tangent components and balancing cancels the extension. BBGS's negative
unit-normal product $B$, applied to $-f$, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{conversion}
 B_{-f}(ac)(\tau)=a_\tau D_f c(\tau).
\end{equation}
This identity applies successively in dimensions $d,d-1,\ldots,1$,
including signed or zero intermediate weights, ending with $a_v=1$.

The full BBGS chain used here consists of Proposition 3.12, Proposition
3.15, Lemma 3.18 and Proposition 3.19. Subdivision has three cases:
inherited facets keep their conormals and weights; a new facet in an
old weighted cell has two opposite equal contributions; a face in an
old cell of larger dimension inherits zero weight. At an actual
codimension-two face in a top cell, the quotient wedge has exactly two
boundary rays, even when the top cell is nonsimplicial. Reflection
interchanges the two unit rays and the two oriented perpendiculars,
so their expansions have coefficients $a,b$ with the roles of the
rays exchanged. Expanding the double product groups a diagonal sum
$a f_\tau(v)g_\tau(v)$ and a paired cross sum
$b f_\tau(v)g_{\tau'}(v')$. Interchanging $\tau,\tau'$ in the cross
sum proves commutation. Ambient linear forms annihilate exactly
balanced weights by pairing with their conormal sum. Commutation then
preserves balancing under PA intersection; subdivision preserves it
by the first identity. The printed commuting reference in 3.19 is to
3.12; the needed commuting equality is in 3.15. Applying
\eqref{conversion} at each step proves refinement independence,
symmetry and balancing of our raw recursion. Tangent-lift changes
cancel directly in \eqref{divisor}. Real coefficients and actual real
weights are permitted by this complete chain.

Local affine supports imply positivity for PA intersection against
each nonnegative balanced intermediate cycle: after subtracting the
support agreeing on the center face, every inward residual slope is
nonnegative. The next section reconstructs these supports. Hence all
finite relative PC mixed measures used here are nonnegative finite
atomic measures.

\begin{lemma}\label{ibp}
Let $Z$ be a finite balanced one-cycle and $b,c$ bounded PA functions
on a common rational structure. Then $\deg D_b Z=0$ and
$\int bD_cZ=\int cD_bZ$. Every relative finite PA PC mixed tuple has
mass $M=\deg(\gamma)=\int D_\gamma^d[X]$. For finite relative $a_i,q$,
\begin{align}\label{relative}
 \int(\gamma-q)\MA(a_1,\ldots,a_d)
 &=\int(\gamma-a_1)\MA(q,a_2,\ldots,a_d)\notag\\
 &\quad+\int(a_1-q)\MA(\gamma,a_2,\ldots,a_d).
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Refine the finite cycle so all functions are affine on each edge;
insert rational vertices on lines. On an unbounded edge a bounded
affine function has zero slope. A bounded edge $[v,w]$ of primitive
length $L_e$ contributes
$Z(e)(b(v)-b(w))(c(w)-c(v))/L_e$ to the integral. This is symmetric.
Outgoing degree contributions cancel on bounded edges and vanish on
unbounded ones. Replace relative mixed factors by $\gamma$ one at a
time; the difference is $D_{a_i-\gamma}$ on the balanced one-cycle
formed by the remaining factors, so has zero degree. Applying bounded
IBP to $b=\gamma-q$, $c=a_1-\gamma$ and expanding proves
\eqref{relative}. All products at this stage are finite raw products.
\end{proof}

\section{The whole fixed-carrier coefficient cone}
Fix a finite rational carrier $\Pi$ for the actual $\gamma$ and cycle.
An ambient rational hyperplane arrangement may refine its cells into
pointed polyhedra; restrictions keep $\gamma$ affine and inherited
cycle weights. Use rational affine coordinates on each cell, without
introducing redundant ambient coefficients. Let $z$ list its affine
coefficients. Equality of restrictions on every shared face is a
finite rational system $Ez=0$: test a rational affine basis of that
face. This includes constant terms.

For a face $\tau$, at $p\in\relint(\tau)$ the tangent cones
$C_{\sigma/\tau}$ are generated by finitely many rational vectors.
Include both signs of a rational basis of common lineality and tangent
space, and keep assignments to incident cones. Put $U=(d_1\ \cdots\ d_m)$,
$s_i=f_\sigma(d_i)$ on the assigned cone, and
$K(U)=\{\lambda\geq0:U\lambda=0\}$. The germ is a continuous
homogeneous PA function on the union of these cones.

\begin{lemma}\label{cone}
The coefficient cone $\mathcal C_\Pi$ of continuous PA PC functions
on $\Pi$ is a closed rational polyhedral cone, independent of the
specified top weights. Its defining data are $Ez=0$ and the
nonnegative costs of finitely many rational circuits at \emph{every}
face. Tests at rational centers also cover irrational centers,
nonsimplicial cells and all lineality conditions. Positive growth
is given, after pointed refinement, by finitely many strict rational
linear inequalities on the nonzero recession-ray generators.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The active inequalities defining $\sigma$ at an interior point of
$\tau$ are exactly those active on $\tau$. The other inequalities
have positive slack at that point. Therefore its tangent cone and its
linear part are unchanged as the center ranges over $\relint(\tau)$.
This also proves that sufficiently short assigned generator segments
lie in their respective incident cells.

At a rational $p$, choose rational $t>0$ small enough for these
segments. A relation $\lambda\in K(U)$, normalized by
$\sum_i\lambda_i$, gives a PCC of rational endpoints $p+td_i$ with
center $p$. The PC inequality is $s\cdot\lambda\geq0$. Neither the
relation coefficients nor the top weights need be rational.

These inequalities are equivalent to a supporting ambient linear
form $\ell$ with $\ell(d_i)\leq s_i$. Here is finite duality. The
cone $T=\R_{\geq0}^m+\operatorname{image}(U^{\mathsf T})$ is finitely
generated using coordinate vectors and both signs of an image basis.
In any nonnegative representation eliminate dependent active columns:
subtract a signed dependency up to the first vanishing coefficient.
After finitely many steps the active columns are independent. Their
inverse maps bound the coefficients by a constant times the vector's
norm. Only finitely many independent subsets occur, so a converging
sequence has a converging representation on one subset. Thus $T$ is
closed. If $s\notin T$, the vector from $s$ to its nearest point in
$T$ pairs nonnegatively with $T$ and negatively with $s$. Pairing with
the coordinate generators and both image signs gives
$\lambda\geq0$, $U\lambda=0$, $s\cdot\lambda<0$, a contradiction.
Hence $s=U^{\mathsf T}\ell+b$ with $b\geq0$. Conversely this identity
pairs nonnegatively with $K(U)$.

Generator inequalities extend to each tangent cone by nonnegative
linearity. Including both tangent signs forces agreement along the
face. The same linear form works at every real center in that relative
face, with its affine value shifted appropriately. Thus there is an
ambient affine local support at every point. Such supports imply PC
on \emph{every} rational structure, not only $\Pi$: on any contained
segment, subtract the chord. A positive maximum would have a closed
nonempty maximum set in the segment interior. Both segment directions
force the local support there to have zero slope; its support then
makes the maximum set open, contradicting connectedness and the zero
endpoint values. Thus the segment restriction is convex. At a PCC
center, propagate its supporting slope along the contained segment
to each endpoint. One obtains
$f(x_i)\geq f(x)+\ell(x_i-x)$; summing cancels the barycenter.
This proves sufficiency, also for real endpoints.

For finiteness, an extreme ray of $K(U)$ has one-dimensional kernel
on its support. Otherwise a kernel vector not proportional to its
strictly positive coordinates, adjusted by a multiple of that ray,
has both signs; small perturbations in both directions contradict
extremality. Its support has at most $\operatorname{rank}U+1$
coordinates, and rational Gaussian elimination gives a rational
generator. Every relation is a sum of these minimal relations:
when its supported kernel has larger dimension, perturb in both
directions up to a vanishing coordinate and express it as a convex
combination of the two smaller-support relations; repeat. There are
only finitely many subsets. The slope costs of their generators
are rational linear functions of $z$. Taking all faces and $Ez=0$
gives the claimed cone. Actual weights never entered this test.

Recession cones have finite rational generators after removing
lineality. Pointed refinement permits positivity to be tested on
their nonzero rays; positivity there is equivalent to positivity
on the entire nonzero cone. Inactive bounded cells impose no growth
condition. A polyhedron is a bounded vertex hull plus its recession
cone: homogenize its rational inequalities, generate that rational
cone, and separate its positive-height and zero-height generators.
These elementary generating cones follow by taking a bounded
rational section after removing lineality. Their section vertices
solve full-rank rational active systems. Consequently positive
growth gives $f(x)\geq a\rho(x)-b$ on the finite support for some
$a>0,b$, where $\rho$ is any fixed rational lattice norm. This proves
properness and a finite lower bound as well.
\end{proof}

Write the finite PC costs as $B_jz\geq0$ within $Ez=0$, and the
growth costs as $R_kz>0$; discard vacuous zero rows. Let
$I=\{j:B_j\gamma=0\}$ and
\[
 F=\{z\in\mathcal C_\Pi:B_jz=0\ (j\in I)\},\quad
 L=\{z:Ez=0,\ B_jz=0\ (j\in I)\}.
\]
The inactive costs are strict at $\gamma$. Thus $F$ contains a relative
neighborhood of $\gamma$ in $L$, so $L=\operatorname{span}F$ and
$\gamma\in\relint F$. This is its minimal face. The subspace $L$
is rational, even though deciding which costs vanish uses the actual
reference, without a numerical tolerance convention.

\begin{theorem}[One rational positive component]\label{component}
There exist a rational-affine positive-growth
$\gamma_0\in\relint F$ and rational $\epsilon>0$ such that
\[
 \gamma'=\gamma-\epsilon\gamma_0\in\PAPC_\R(X)
 \quad\hbox{is positive at infinity},\qquad
 \gamma=\gamma'+\epsilon\gamma_0
\]
exactly on the same rational carrier. Every active PC cost remains
zero. The original reference is never replaced by an approximation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a rational basis of $L$ and approximate the actual coordinates
of $\gamma$ within that subspace. Close enough rational vectors retain
every inactive positive PC cost and every strict growth cost. Choose
one as $\gamma_0$. Active costs vanish exactly. For all inactive
costs choose $\epsilon$ smaller than
$B_j\gamma/B_j\gamma_0$, and for every growth row smaller than
$R_k\gamma/R_k\gamma_0$. These are finitely many positive ratios;
a strictly smaller positive rational number exists. The difference
then has all PC inequalities and strictly positive growth costs,
with shared-face equations intact. This argument also covers the
case with no inactive PC costs. No maximum is taken with irrational
data, so there are no new equality walls.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite positive convex decomposition]\label{simplex}
The actual reference is a finite convex combination
\[
 \gamma=\sum_{i=1}^m t_i\gamma_i,\qquad t_i>0,\quad\sum_i t_i=1,
\]
where all $\gamma_i$ are rational-affine positive-growth PC functions
on the same carrier and lie in $\relint F$. The $t_i$ may be
irrational. One may take $m\leq\dim L+1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In rational coordinates on $L$, the inactive PC and growth
inequalities define an open neighborhood of $\gamma$.
For $n=\dim L>0$, first choose a small rational $r>0$ and then
a rational point $b$ sufficiently close to $\gamma$. The rational vertices
$b-r\sum_{j=1}^n e_j$ and $b+r e_j$, $1\leq j\leq n$, have
barycenter $b$ and form a full-dimensional simplex. Choose $r$ small
enough and $b$ close enough that the simplex lies in that neighborhood
and $\gamma$ lies in its interior.
Its unique barycentric coordinates are strictly positive and sum
to one. The resulting coefficients give the stated exact identity
in the entire coefficient space. If $n=0$, the actual vector is
rational and a one-point combination suffices. Lineality, including
constants and affine functions, is retained in $L$ rather than
quotiented away.
\end{proof}

\section{Rational-component auxiliaries and height bounds}
\begin{lemma}\label{dc}
For each compactly supported ambient continuous rational-affine PA
test $\psi$, there are actual relative real PA PC functions
$\theta_0,\theta_1$ on finite rational structures with
$\theta_1-\theta_0=\epsilon\eta\psi|_X$ for some rational $\eta>0$.
They agree with the original $\gamma$ outside a compact set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the rational-reference compact-test construction to $\gamma_0$.
For completeness, choose a finite rational hyperplane arrangement
containing the walls of $\psi$, with rational affine forms $L_j$.
A sufficiently large rational $K$ makes $H=K\sum_j|L_j|$ and
$H+\psi$ ambient convex: each generic segment slope increases at
every wall after its negative bend is dominated by $2K$; limiting
generic segments covers all segments. Choose rational $\eta>0$
with recession growth of $\eta H$ strictly below that of $\gamma_0$,
using its proved coercive lower bound, and rational $A$ such that
$A+\eta H>\gamma_0$ on a neighborhood of the compact test support.
Set
$h_0=\max(\gamma_0,A+\eta H)$, $h_1=h_0+\eta\psi$.
These are rational-affine PA on rational equality walls.
The first function has supports of its active entries; near the
test support the second is $A+\eta(H+\psi)$, and outside it equals
$h_0$. The open descriptions cover support boundaries and ties.
Both are PC by local supports, equal $\gamma_0$ outside a compact
set, and $h_1-h_0=\eta\psi$.
Now define $\theta_l=\gamma'+\epsilon h_l$.
Adding supports preserves PC. A common rational refinement carries
the sums, whose difference from the actual $\gamma$ is
$\epsilon(h_l-\gamma_0)$. No new equality walls occur in this step.
\end{proof}

Compact rational PA test restrictions are uniformly dense in $C_c(X)$,
with support control. Here is the finite construction used below.
For nonnegative $g\in C_c(X)$ choose a uniform-continuity scale
$\delta$ and an integer $A>2\|g\|_\infty/\delta$.
Take a fine rational net $v_j\in X$ of its compact support, with
nonnegative rational sampled heights $b_j$ close to $g(v_j)$, and put
$G(x)=\max_j(b_j-A\rho(x-v_j))_+$ ambiently. At distances at least
$\delta$ each bump is zero; at smaller distances it exceeds $g(x)$
by at most the continuity and sampling errors. A net fine compared
with error divided by $A$ gives the reverse bound where $g$ is
positive. All data and equality walls are rational; the finitely
many bumps have compact support. Signed functions use the difference
of positive and negative constructions. When $\operatorname{supp}g$
lies in an open $O\subset X$, choose the bump radii smaller than
its distance to $X\setminus O$. Thus support may remain in $O$.

\begin{proposition}\label{estimate}
For a fixed test $\psi$ and any two actual relative finite PA PC
tuples $\mathbf a,\mathbf b$,
\[
 |\langle\psi,\MA(\mathbf a)-\MA(\mathbf b)\rangle|
 \leq \frac{2M}{\epsilon\eta_\psi}
                       \sum_i\|a_i-b_i\|_\infty.
\]
The constant is independent of their relative bounds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Telescope one factor at a time. The remaining $d-1$ factors form
positive balanced finite one-cycles $Z_i$. Bounded primitive-edge
IBP moves $D_{a_i-b_i}$ onto the compact test. By Lemma~\ref{dc},
$D_\psi Z_i=(D_{\theta_1}Z_i-D_{\theta_0}Z_i)/(\epsilon\eta_\psi)$.
Both positive mixed measures have raw mass $M$, since these
auxiliaries are relative to the \emph{same original} $\gamma$.
Their total variation difference is at most $2M$.
\end{proof}

Choose fixed rational $c\geq0$ such that $H_0=\gamma_0+c\geq0$;
$H_0$ is a proper continuous height. Put
\[
 q_j=\max(\gamma_0-j,-c),\quad
 \Theta_j=\gamma'+\epsilon q_j,\quad
 J_0=\int H_0\MA(\gamma),\qquad j=1,2,\ldots.
\]
Every $q_j$ is rational-affine PA PC, and every $\Theta_j$ is actual
relative real PA PC on rational geometry. One has
\begin{equation}\label{heightid}
 \gamma-\Theta_j=\epsilon\min(H_0,j).
\end{equation}
The finite reference measure has finite support, so $J_0<\infty$.
The irrational-wall function $\max(\gamma-j,-c)$ is not used.

\begin{proposition}\label{moment}
For every relative finite PA PC tuple with
$\max_i\|a_i-\gamma\|_\infty\leq C$,
\[
 \int H_0\MA(\mathbf a)\leq K_{C,0}
       :=\frac{2d}{\epsilon}CM+J_0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use \eqref{relative} with $q=\Theta_j$ and split
$a_1-\Theta_j=(a_1-\gamma)+(\gamma-\Theta_j)$. This gives
\begin{align*}
 \int(\gamma-\Theta_j)\MA(a_1,\ldots,a_d)
 &=\int(a_1-\gamma)
       [\MA(\gamma,a_2,\ldots,a_d)-\MA(\Theta_j,a_2,\ldots,a_d)]\\
 &\quad+\int(\gamma-\Theta_j)\MA(\gamma,a_2,\ldots,a_d).
\end{align*}
The error is at most $2CM$ since the two positive measures have
mass $M$. Repeat through all $d$ factors. By \eqref{heightid} the
remaining reference integral is at most $\epsilon J_0$.
Divide by $\epsilon$ and increase $j$; monotone convergence gives
the claimed height bound. Every product before this limit is a
genuine finite PA raw product, including the cutoff products.
\end{proof}

\section{Completion for the actual arbitrary real reference}
Fix a Euclidean metric on $X$. The vague uniformity uses finite lists
of $C_c(X)$ evaluations; the weak uniformity uses finite lists of
$C_b(X)$ evaluations. Define
\[
 d_{\rm BL}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\|g\|_\infty\leq1,\ \operatorname{Lip}g\leq1}
                         |\langle g,\mu-\nu\rangle|,
 \quad d_\infty(\mathbf f,\mathbf h)=\sum_i\|f_i-h_i\|_\infty.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{completion}
The finite raw real mixed operator extends uniquely to
$\mathcal D_\R(\gamma)^d$. The outputs are positive finite Radon
measures of mass $M$, symmetric, local, and equal to raw recursion
at finite PA tuples. Arbitrary uniform PA approximants converge to
the same measure against each $C_b$ test. Uniform closure equals
decreasing uniform regularization.

The extension is globally uniformly continuous for the vague
uniformity and continuous for the weak $C_b$ topology, including
nets. For \emph{each fixed} finite $C$, on
\[
 \mathcal F_C=\{\mathbf f\in\mathcal D_\R(\gamma)^d:
                       \max_i\|f_i-\gamma\|_\infty\leq C\}
\]
it is uniformly continuous for the weak $C_b$ uniformity and in
$d_{\rm BL}$. \emph{Every} completed tuple in this family satisfies
\[
 \int H_0\MA(\mathbf f)\leq K_{C,0},\qquad
 \MA(\mathbf f)\{H_0>T\}\leq K_{C,0}/T.
\]
There is no entire-domain weak/BL uniformity assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $M=0$, positivity makes all finite measures zero, and all conclusions
follow with the zero output; decreasing extraction below still applies.
Assume $M>0$. For any uniform-Cauchy PA tuple, Proposition~\ref{estimate}
makes its rational compact-test evaluations Cauchy. Density and common
mass $M$ give Cauchy evaluations on all $C_c(X)$; a sup-error $\eta$
in a test costs at most $2M\eta$ in a difference. The resulting
positive linear functional is bounded by $M\|g\|_\infty$.
Apply granted S262 Lemma 12 on the closed finite polyhedral metric
space $X$: precisely such a functional has a unique finite positive
Radon representation of mass at most $M$. This use needs no new
source representation theorem.

A particular convergent approximating family has one finite relative
bound $D$. Proposition~\ref{moment} gives tails at most $K_{D,0}/T$.
Choose continuous compact cutoffs $\chi_T$ equal to one on
$\{H_0\leq T\}$, with values in $[0,1]$; properness ensures compact
support. Vague convergence gives
$\int\chi_Td\mu\geq M-K_{D,0}/T$, hence $\mu(X)=M$.
For nonnegative compact tests $H_0\chi_T$, convergence passes the
moment bound; choose increasing cutoffs and increase $T$ to obtain
$\int H_0d\mu\leq K_{D,0}$. Truncating a fixed $g\in C_b$ by
$\chi_T$ costs at most $\|g\|_\infty K_{D,0}/T$ for either side.
Vague convergence of the truncated test then proves all-$C_b$
convergence. This argument uses the common bound for this family,
not for the whole domain.

Comparison of any two approximant families in Proposition~\ref{estimate}
proves independence. Passing that estimate along approximants for
any two completed tuples retains the same constant. For a tuple in
$\mathcal F_C$, choose approximation errors tending to zero, so its
approximants have relative bounds $C+o(1)$. Pass each nonnegative
compact height test and then increase its support. The exact
constant $K_{C,0}$ follows for every member of this entire family.
Thus no weak convergence premise was used to manufacture tightness.

For $g\in C_c$ approximate it within $\eta$ by one rational compact
PA test $\psi$. Uniformly over all completed tuples,
\[
 |\langle g,\MA(\mathbf f)-\MA(\mathbf h)\rangle|
 \leq2M\eta+L_\psi d_\infty(\mathbf f,\mathbf h),\quad
 L_\psi=2M/(\epsilon\eta_\psi).
\]
Choose $\eta$, then input distance. Finite evaluation lists prove
global vague uniform continuity. For fixed $C$ and $g\in C_b$,
cut off at height $T$ and approximate $\chi_Tg$ within $\eta$.
For all tuples in $\mathcal F_C$ the evaluation difference is at most
\[
 2\|g\|_\infty K_{C,0}/T+2M\eta+L_\psi d_\infty(\mathbf f,\mathbf h).
\]
Choosing $T$, $\eta$, then distance, and handling finite lists,
proves the stated fixed-family weak uniformity. A radius-one
neighborhood of any fixed tuple lies in
$\mathcal F_{1+\max_i\|f_i-\gamma\|_\infty}$, proving ordinary weak
continuity, also for nets.

For BL tests, restrictions to a compact cutoff support $K$ have a
finite uniform $\eta$-net: cover $K$ by finitely many radius-$r$
balls, quantize values at their centers with mesh $s$, and select
one actual global test from each occupied quantization class.
Two tests in that class differ on $K$ by at most $2r+2s$.
Choose $r,s$ for the desired $\eta$, approximate the finitely many
cutoff representatives by rational PA tests, and take their largest
$L_\psi$. The supremum evaluation difference is bounded by
$2K_{C,0}/T+4M\eta+L_{\max}d_\infty$. This proves BL uniformity
only on the fixed-$C$ family.

Symmetry passes from finite recursion. For locality, suppose two
tuples agree on open $O$ and choose a compact rational PA test with
support inside $O$. In finite telescoping, $D_\psi Z_i$ is supported
in this compact support since $\psi$ is zero on its open complement.
The same variation bound therefore multiplies only the local
argument difference there. Along approximants that difference
tends to zero. Controlled-support density proves equality of
output restrictions on $O$. Constant approximant sequences give
raw agreement.

Finally choose $a_n$ with error at most $2^{-n}$ and add the rational
constant $4\cdot2^{-n}$. The consecutive drop is at least
$2\cdot2^{-n}-(2^{-n}+2^{-n-1})=2^{-n-1}>0$ pointwise.
These are still real PA PC and relative to the actual reference.
They decrease uniformly to the endpoint. This proves equality
with the declared decreasing domain and its genuine antecedent.
Uniqueness follows from density of finite approximants and the
Hausdorff vague topology.
\end{proof}

The fixed-$C$ restriction is material and retained from S262.
For context, the granted line shifts $|x-2n|$ and
$|x-2n-1/(8n)|$ have distance $1/(8n)$ but raw measures
$2\delta_{2n}$ and $2\delta_{2n+1/(8n)}$. The single bounded
continuous test $\sin(\pi x^2)$ separates their evaluations by a
quantity tending to two. This precludes any global weak uniformity
claim even for a rational reference. The accepted real alternating
average-shift example also precludes global BL uniformity. Neither
example has a common relative bound. We do not claim these old
counterexamples as new findings.

\section{A separate decomposition preserves arbitrary-reference flat jets}
The component construction alone does \emph{not} preserve a bad
jet: adding $\gamma'$ can turn a zero relation cost positive.
The convex decomposition in Theorem~\ref{simplex} avoids this by
replacing every reference summand near the chosen point.

\begin{theorem}[Actual-reference local jet realization]\label{jets}
Let $v\in X\cap N_\Q$. Let $h$ be a continuous homogeneous
rational-affine PA PC function, with $h(0)=0$, on a finite rational
refinement of the tangent star of $X$ at $v$. There is an actual
global $F\in\PAPC_\R(X)$, $F-\gamma\in\PA_{\R,b}(X)$, with
germ $C+t+\delta h$ at $v$ for positive rational $t,\delta$ and
rational $C$. Thus all flat relations are preserved and its raw
atom is $\delta^d m_0(h)$.

For a real-coefficient jet having a flat relation and positive raw
atom, a rational-coefficient jet on the same specified rational
fan has both properties and is realized in this manner. This last
statement does not promise exact realization of the original
irrational jet coefficients.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Insert the rational point $v$ into a common rational carrier and
use Theorem~\ref{simplex} there. Its rational reference summands
$\gamma_i$ are all PC and positive-growth. Choose one rational
norm $\rho$, a common rational $\alpha>0$ slower than all their
recession growth, and a common rational $C>\max_i\gamma_i(v)$.
The functions
\[
 \Psi_i=\max(\gamma_i,C+\alpha\rho(x-v))
\]
are rational-affine PA PC, equal $C+\alpha\rho$ near $v$ and
equal $\gamma_i$ outside a compact set. The coercive lower bounds
ensure the latter property. Choose a common rational star radius
$r>0$ inside the norm-active neighborhoods and the actual local
tangent model; all finitely many summands permit one choice.
There is $A<\infty$ with $|h(x)|\leq A\rho(x)$ on this tangent
support. Choose rational $\delta>0$ so $\delta A\leq\alpha/2$
and rational $0<t<\alpha r/4$. Inside $\rho(x-v)<r$ define
\[
 G_i(x)=\max(\Psi_i(x),C+t+\delta h(x-v)),
\]
and outside set $G_i=\Psi_i$.
On $r/2\leq\rho<r$, the norm entry strictly dominates the inserted
jet, so there is an unchanged collar. Near $v$, since
$\alpha\rho-\delta h\leq(\alpha+\delta A)\rho$, the inserted jet
dominates for sufficiently small $\rho$ and the germ is exactly
$C+t+\delta h$ for \emph{every} $G_i$.

The fixed-carrier support criterion supplies supports for $h$.
Supports of active entries support a maximum, also at ties; the
collar gives supports at the gluing boundary. Thus the resulting
continuous functions satisfy all PCCs. The norm ball, local fan
and equality walls all have rational data, yielding finite
rational carrying structures. Extend local rational walls through
$v$ to an ambient finite hyperplane arrangement to obtain a common
global refinement if necessary. Every $G_i-\gamma_i$ is bounded
and compactly supported. This reconstructs precisely the
rational-reference jet construction granted in S262, with common
parameters; it uses no irrational maximum closure.

Now set $F=\sum_i t_i G_i$, with the actual positive coefficients
from Theorem~\ref{simplex}. A common finite rational refinement
carries this sum. PC is preserved by positive addition, and
$F-\gamma=\sum_i t_i(G_i-\gamma_i)$ is bounded real PA on that
refinement. Since $\sum_i t_i=1$, its germ is exactly
$C+t+\delta h$. Adding constants has zero divisor and linear
scaling in each of the $d$ primitive recursion steps gives the
atom $\delta^d m_0(h)$. Flat relation costs scale by $\delta$.
The unrelated original germ of $\gamma'$ has not been added at
the selected point.

For a real bad jet, impose on its fixed fan all shared-face
equations and all-face rational circuit inequalities from
Lemma~\ref{cone}. Decompose its zero-cost relation into extreme
relations; nonnegative costs imply some rational extreme cost
is zero. Impose that exact rational equality as well. At the
given jet, impose all currently active inequalities as equalities;
this is a rational linear solution space. Rational vectors are
dense in it, and inactive strict inequalities persist near the
given point. Thus feasible rational jets approach the actual
jet with this flat relation retained. The raw mass is a continuous
degree-$d$ polynomial in the slopes, with the actual top weights
unchanged, so strict positive mass persists. Apply the first
part to one such rational jet. None of these statements asserts
literal gluing or a PA operator at an unregularizable endpoint.
\end{proof}

\section{New necessary geometry for every actual real reference}
A \emph{flat relation} for a homogeneous PA germ is a nonzero
$\lambda\geq0$ with $U\lambda=0$ and $s\cdot\lambda=0$.
Use the generators of the whole tangent support, including both
lineality signs and every incident-cone assignment. Its meaning is
independent of generating lists: resolve arbitrary directions
inside their assigned cones into generators. The raw local atom
$m_0(h)$ always uses the actual cycle weights and primitive saturated
normals, never an ambient subgradient volume.

\begin{proposition}\label{floor}
For a PC PA germ on a positive balanced star, absence of a flat
relation supplies an ambient $\ell$ and $a>0$ with
$h(x)-\ell(x)\geq a\|x\|$ on its tangent support. Consequently
a finite PA envelope is contact at every positive raw atom
whose germ has no flat relation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
PC costs are nonnegative by Lemma~\ref{cone}. B225 positive spanning
makes $K(U)\cap\{\sum\lambda_i=1\}$ nonempty and compact.
If no cost is zero, its minimum $b$ is positive. Choose
$0<e<b$ and apply the finite separation proof to $s-e\mathbf1$.
It gives $s_i-\ell(d_i)\geq e$. A nonnegative representation of
$x$ then yields $h(x)-\ell(x)\geq e\|x\|/\max_i\|d_i\|$.
The PC hypothesis is essential; it has not been omitted.

An actual finite envelope $f$ is PC by taking the supremum of
the printed Jensen inequalities. At a noncontact carrying vertex
$v$, its continuous obstacle has positive gap throughout a small
star. If this germ had the strict floor just proved, raise the
affine function $f(v)+\ell(x-v)$ by a small $b'>0$ and take its
maximum with $f$ only within that star. Choose $b'$ below the
gap and the strict collar margin. The modification agrees with
$f$ on an outer collar, lies below the obstacle and differs from
$f$ boundedly. Supports persist for each active entry and at
ties; thus it is an unrestricted PC competitor preserving growth
and raising $f(v)$, a contradiction. The auxiliary maximum
need not be rational-geometry PA and receives no operator.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[An actual PA witness]\label{roof}
Suppose $F$ is an actual relative real PA PC function with flat
germ and positive raw atom $m_v$ at a rational vertex $v$.
There is a bounded real PA obstacle with actual envelope $F$
and contact integral exactly $-Rm_v<0$, for rational $R>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose rational $R$ so the closed norm ball meets only incident
affine cells of $F$ and contains no other vertex of a common
carrier for $F,\gamma,[X]$. Set
$B_R=(R-\rho(x-v))_+$ and $u=F-\gamma+B_R$.
This is bounded real PA on a finite rational structure. The
function $F$ itself is a relative PC competitor below $F+B_R$.
For a flat relation choose rational directions $d_i$ with real
nonnegative coefficients $\lambda_i$. The rational boundary
points $y_i=v+R d_i/\rho(d_i)$, weighted by
$\beta_i=\lambda_i\rho(d_i)/R$, have barycenter $v$ and average
$F$ value $F(v)$ after normalization. At these boundary points
the obstacle equals $F$, so every printed-PC competitor has
$q(v)\leq F(v)$. A rational point inside the ball lies on a
chord from $v$ to the rational boundary point on its ray in
the same affine cell. Jensen and affinity give $q\leq F$
there. Outside the ball the obstacle already equals $F$.
For arbitrary real centers, enclosing shrinking rational
simplices in the relative rational face give the same bound
by Jensen and continuity of $F$. No continuity of $q$ is used.
Thus the unrestricted envelope is exactly $F$, already real
PA and decreasing-regularizable by a constant sequence. The
usc regularization is the same. Refinement independence gives
zero artificial atoms. Only $v$ has raw mass in the roof, where
the contact gap is $-R$, proving the exact integral.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Reference-independent finite-PA obstruction sector]\label{criterion}
For \emph{every actual} real PA PC positive-growth reference on the
fixed weighted rational $X$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item All bounded real PA obstacles whose actual envelopes are
finite relative real PA have zero raw contact integral.
\item At every rational point of $X$ and every finite rational
refinement of its tangent star, each homogeneous PC PA jet
with a flat relation has zero raw top atom.
\end{enumerate}
The second condition may be tested on rational-coefficient jets.
If it fails, \emph{each actual real positive-growth reference}
has a genuine finite-PA obstacle witness with negative contact.
The geometry and weights, rather than irrational reference
coefficients, determine this finite-PA obstruction sector.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If (2) holds, every positive atom of a finite PA envelope has
a nonflat germ. Proposition~\ref{floor} forces contact there;
the finite atomic integral is zero. Conversely, if (2) fails,
Theorem~\ref{jets} realizes a rational bad jet with the actual
reference, positive raw atom and flat relation retained.
Proposition~\ref{roof} then gives the stated genuine obstacle
and negative integral. Rational bad jets suffice by the
last part of Theorem~\ref{jets}.
\end{proof}

This extends S262's rational-reference finite-PA geometric
corollary to arbitrary real references and removes its global
irrational-reference jet compatibility gap. It does not remove
the quantifier over rational refinements. On each specified fan
the cone is finite and the atom is a polynomial, but no bound
on needed refinements or reduction to a simpler geometric list
is proved here. A pair satisfying the entire original real
orthogonality property must satisfy (2). Failure excludes the
pair even at an actual PA endpoint; no operator-domain dilemma
is hidden in that exclusion.

The accepted $U_{3,4}$ and plane-sum mechanisms must still be
confronted by any proposed sufficient condition. Our theorem
retains their raw bad jets and shows that a change to an actual
irrational positive-growth reference cannot eliminate those
finite-PA geometric obstructions. We assert no weight-ray
criterion in dimension greater than one and no stronger
smoothness theorem.
