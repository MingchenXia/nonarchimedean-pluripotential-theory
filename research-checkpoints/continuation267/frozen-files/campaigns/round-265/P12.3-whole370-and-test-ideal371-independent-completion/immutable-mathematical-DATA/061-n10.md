# Energy and capacity diagnostics with correct signs and universal monomial counts.

All energy uses below retain the entire E350 qualifications: actual primary models, reduced boundary, p=n-1, specified D limits, one gauge factor, canonical truncation TV only, precise finite-reference-moment polar range, and no general Choquet or equation theorem. B341 is used for bounded mixed multiplicity moments at its exact arity. Write F>=0 for the bounded symmetric form, B=F, and E(u)=-F(u,u)/2 on surfaces. In particular B is NOT -F.

On the regular surface k[x,y]_{(x,y)} in maximal gauge, let I_j=(x,y^j), j>=1, I_0=R. These form a graded family since products lie in I_{j+l}. The profiles u_j=-min(v(x)/j,v(y)) increase to zero except at the unique point q with support (x), where their limit is -1. Monomial valuations v_L(x)=L,v_L(y)=1 tend to q, so that increasing limit is not USC and cannot be D. Its regularization and the envelope of obstacle zero are both zero, so this is not a normal CE counterexample.

For ALL nonnegative integers r,s the monomial staircase gives
 length R/m^r I_j^s = r(r+1)/2 + rs + j s(s+1)/2.
Indeed at x-exponent a<r+s the column threshold is r+s-a+(j-1)max(0,s-a); sum these thresholds. This proves e(m,I_j)=1 and e(I_j)=j universally, not from a guessed finite stabilization window. Consequently F(u_j,u_j)=1/j, E(u_j)=-1/(2j), and mu(u_j) has mass 1/j, so it converges to zero in total variation. For EVERY capacity input -1<=phi<=0 in D, F(phi,phi)<=F(-1,-1)=1 by order, and conditional Cauchy gives F(u_j,phi)<=1/sqrt(j). The test integral and Markov inequality show Cap{-u_j>epsilon}<=1/(epsilon sqrt(j)). Thus capacity and energy convergence need not put the exact increasing limit in D.

For the normal surface R=k[X,Y,Z]_{(X,Y,Z)}/(XY-Z^2) in ANY characteristic, normality is proved by hypersurface S2 and regular height-one charts. Put a=v(X), b=v(Y), min(a,b)=1 and v(Z)=(a+b)/2 where finite, with the multiplicative extended relation elsewhere. Let q have support (X,Z), b=1, a=infinity. For I_n=(X^n,XY^{n-1},Y^{2n}), u_n=-v(I_n)/n is -1 if a<=b, and -min(1+(a-1)/n,2) if a>b, including value -2 at q. It increases to h=-1 off q and h(q)=-2. The valuations a=L,b=1 tend to q, so h is non-USC, outside D, and h*=-1 is nevertheless a good continuous envelope.

The blowup of m is regular: X and Y charts are polynomial charts, and the Z chart has the relation x'y'=1. Its exceptional conic has degree two in every characteristic. Qualified geometric reference comparison gives mu(-1)=2 delta_{ord_m}. Set z_j=-min(v(X),j)=-v((X)+m^j), an actual decreasing C-sequence with limit z=-v(X) in D. Do NOT assume z in finite energy. For EVERY capacity input phi in [-1,0] in D, E350 coordinate monotonicity gives
 F(z_j,phi)<=F(z_j,-1)=2,
since ord_m(X)=1. The bounded test pairing identifies the left side with integral min(v(X),j) dmu(phi). Ordinary SEQUENTIAL monotone convergence of nonnegative Borel functions then gives integral v(X) dmu(phi)<=2. Markov and the supremum over EVERY phi prove Cap{a>T}<=2/T and Cap{q}=0. For 0<epsilon<1 the error set {-1-u_n>epsilon} is {a>1+n epsilon}; for epsilon>=1 it is empty. Capacity convergence follows, and h is continuous off open sets of arbitrarily small capacity. Quasicontinuity still does not prove h in D.

The parity semigroup identifies R with k[s^2,st,t^2]. Lattice exponents have even total degree. There are k^2 such monomials of degree <2k, proving e(m)=2. The Newton boundary of I_n has vertices (2n,0),(2,2n-2),(0,4n); the complementary polygon has area 2n^2+2n. Parity lattice density is one half. Its ideal powers have this leading count: any convex combination of the three generators with coefficients summing k can be rounded down, leaving at most two missing generators. Filling them changes each coordinate by a bound depending only on n. A fixed positive even-coordinate shift of the convex region therefore lies in the actual exponent set of I_n^k, and the reverse inclusion holds without a shift. Their difference is a boundary band with O(k) lattice points. Hence length R/I_n^k=(n^2+n)k^2+O(k), e(I_n)=2n^2+2n. Thus F(u_n,u_n)=2+2/n and E(u_n)=-1-1/n. This is a universal leading-coefficient argument; the retained finite enumerations only check stated n,k boxes.

Neither example refutes CE for normal germs. They refute increasing-limit membership inferred from energy/capacity convergence or from dense generic values. Full proofs: n10.md. The parity generator enumeration also admits an exact universal formula:
 length R/I_n^k=(n^2+n)k^2+(n^2-n+2)k, for all integers n,k>=1.
To prove it, pair the semigroup columns s-exponent 2p and 2p+1, p=0,...,nk-1. Their common minimum even t threshold is obtained by maximizing 4n i+(2n+2)j with i,j>=0, i+j<=k, ni+j<=p. For p<k the maximum uses i=0,j=p, so threshold=4nk-(2n+2)p. For n>1 and k<=p<nk set i0=floor((p-k)/(n-1)), q=p-k-(n-1)i0 in [0,n-2]. For i<=i0 the slot constraint binds and the objective increases with i; for i>i0 the exponent constraint binds and it decreases with i. The candidate i0+1 differs from i0 by 4n-(2n+2)(n-q)<=-4, so the maximum is i0,j=k-i0. The threshold is 2(n-1)(k-i0). Each even/odd column pair contributes exactly that even threshold, since allowed t exponents in the two columns have opposite parity. Sum p<k to get (3n-1)k^2+(n+1)k, and the remaining k blocks of length n-1 to get (n-1)^2 k(k+1). Their sum is the formula. For n=1 the second range is empty and the first sum is 2k^2+2k. This is an exact algebraic count for every n,k, stronger than the finite 48-case observation and consistent with the supported multiplicity sign.
