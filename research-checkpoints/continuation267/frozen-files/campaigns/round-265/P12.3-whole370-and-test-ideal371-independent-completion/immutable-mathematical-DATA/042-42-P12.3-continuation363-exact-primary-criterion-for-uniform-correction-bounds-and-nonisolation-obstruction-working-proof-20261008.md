## 1. Setting and full statement

Let `(R,m)` be an excellent normal local domain of positive dimension, and let `a` be an m-primary ideal. Use the ENTIRE intrinsic normalized semivaluation boundary

`X_a(R) = {v : R -> [0,+infinity] : v is centered at m, v(a)=1}`.

All support primes are retained. In particular, support primes strictly between `(0)` and `m` are not removed. For a finitely generated nonzero ideal `I=(f_1,...,f_r)`, put

`v(I)=min_j v(f_j)`.

This is independent of the generators and continuous into the extended interval `[0,+infinity]` with its order topology. For each divisorial point with support `(0)`, its value is finite. Write `X_a^div(R)` for the normalized divisorial points centered at `m`; their density in the ENTIRE `X_a(R)`, including nonminimal support points, is used at the exact qualified scope of G331.

**Theorem 1 (exact primary criterion).** For a nonzero ideal `I` of `R`, the following are equivalent:

1. There is a finite constant `M` such that `v(I) <= M` for every point of `X_a(R)`.
2. There is a finite constant `M` such that `v(I) <= M` for every point of `X_a^div(R)`.
3. `I=R`, or `I` is m-primary.

If `I` is proper and not m-primary, there is a full-support boundary point `v_0` with `v_0(I)=+infinity`. For every finite `M`, the nonempty OPEN set `{v:v(I)>M}` contains a divisorial point. In particular, even a restriction to divisors centered at the closed point does not repair the missing uniform bound.

**Corollary 2 (b-divisor sign).** On all divisors centered at `m`, the existence of a finite `c>=0` with

`Z(I) >= c Z(a)`

is equivalent to condition 3, with `Z(I)(v)=-v(I)` and `Z(a)(v)=-v(a)`. The direction of the inequality matters: both geometric profiles are nonpositive. For a proper nonprimary `I`, no such finite `c` exists.

**Corollary 3 (finite traces do not give the full bound).** Any fixed finite list of normalized divisorial points has a finite maximum of `v(I)`, even when `I` is nonprimary. Thus a finite determining-model trace bound can coexist with the failure of every global bound in Theorem 1. A proof that needs a global one-sided b-divisor inequality must supply it before using a finite trace-error argument.

## 2. Proof of Theorem 1

First suppose `I=R`. Then `v(I)=0` for every semivaluation, so `M=0` suffices.

Next suppose `I` is m-primary. Because `a` is finitely generated and each of its generators belongs to `sqrt(I)=m`, there is an integer `N>=1` such that `a^N` is contained in `I`. For example, choose an exponent making each generator's power belong to `I`, and use the pigeonhole argument on monomials of a sufficiently high total degree. Ideal inclusion reverses the valuation minimum, and finite ideal generation gives

`v(I) <= v(a^N) = N v(a) = N`

on EVERY semivaluation in the section, including points with nonminimal support. This proves condition 3 implies condition 1. Condition 1 implies condition 2 immediately.

For the converse, suppose `I` is proper and not m-primary. There is a prime `p` containing `I` with `p != m`. This follows from the description of `sqrt(I)` as the intersection of the primes containing `I`: if `m` were the only such prime, `sqrt(I)=m` and the ideal would be m-primary in the Noetherian local ring. The quotient `R/p` is an excellent local domain of positive dimension. The image `a_bar` of `a` is `(m/p)`-primary, since a power of `m` is contained in `a`, and its radical is `m/p`.

The intrinsic boundary of this positive-dimensional excellent quotient is nonempty, by the full support-quotient boundary construction used in G331. Choose a centered semivaluation `w` of `R/p` normalized by `w(a_bar)=1`. Its pullback `v_0` to `R` is centered at `m`, has `v_0(a)=1`, and has support containing `p`. Every generator of `I` vanishes in the quotient, so `v_0(I)=+infinity`.

For any finite real `M`, the set

`U_M={v in X_a(R): v(I)>M}`

is open: with finitely many generators it is the intersection of the open sets `{v:v(f_j)>M}`. It contains `v_0` and hence is nonempty. The qualified density of normalized divisorial points in the WHOLE section gives a point `v_M` in `U_M intersect X_a^div(R)`. Thus `v_M(I)>M`. This contradicts condition 2 for every proposed finite upper bound, and proves the converse and the additional assertions. This proof uses density to find an element of an open set; it does not extend an inequality between merely upper-semicontinuous functions from a dense set.

## 3. Proof of both corollaries and the gauge version

At an unnormalized divisor centered at `m`, divide by the strictly positive finite value `v(a)`. The proposed inequality

`-v(I) >= -c v(a)`

is precisely `v(I)/v(a) <= c`. Theorem 1 proves Corollary 2 with the correct sign. If `I=R`, take `c=0`; if `I` is m-primary, the integer bound in Section 2 works. In the nonprimary case the normalized divisors constructed there have unbounded ratios.

For Corollary 3, a divisorial valuation of the fraction field is finite on every nonzero element. A nonzero finitely generated ideal consequently has finite minimum at each such divisor. A finite list has a finite maximum. This elementary assertion says nothing about a uniform bound on the infinitely many other divisors; Theorem 1 supplies the exact failure.

Changing the primary gauge does not change the criterion. If `b` is another m-primary ideal, there are integers `r,s>=1` with `a^r` contained in `b` and `b^s` contained in `a`. Consequently

`1/s <= v(b)/v(a) <= r`

on all centered semivaluations after normalization by `a`. This factor is positive, finite, and continuous even on nonminimal support. The same function divided by this one gauge factor is unbounded exactly when the original is unbounded. The proof also follows directly by applying Theorem 1 with `b` in place of `a`.

### A concrete nonisolated normal threefold

The failure can occur for the actual Jacobian correction, rather than merely for an arbitrary nonprimary ideal. Let `k` be any field, and set

`R=(k[x,y,z,t]/(xy-z^2))_(x,y,z,t)`.

This is a three-dimensional normal local domain. Indeed, the polynomial quotient is a domain: the map into `k[u,v,t]` given by `x=u^2,y=v^2,z=uv` has image of transcendence degree3 and kernel the irreducible height-one relation `xy-z^2`. The hypersurface is Cohen--Macaulay and hence satisfies Serre's condition S2. The opens where `x` or `y` is invertible are localizations of the polynomial rings `k[x,x^-1,z,t]` or `k[y,y^-1,z,t]`, respectively, and are regular. The complement is contained in `V(x,y,z)`, of codimension2. Thus every height-one localization is regular, and the R1--S2 normality criterion applies. These elementary hypersurface and normality criteria are ordinary algebraic foundations; no resolution is used.

The prime `p=(x,y,z)` is strictly smaller than the maximal ideal, and `R/p=k[t]_(t)`. The Jacobian ideal from this hypersurface presentation is

`J=(x,y,2z)`.

For every characteristic its radical is `p`: in characteristic2 the relation makes `z^2` belong to `(x,y)`, and otherwise `z` is already among the generators. At the prime `p`, the local ring has dimension2 and embedding dimension3, since its defining quadratic relation gives no linear relation. In particular the singular locus contains this nonclosed prime; the germ is nonisolated.

Pull back the t-adic order on `R/p`, normalized by the maximal ideal of `R`. This full-support semivaluation has `v(t)=1` and `v(x)=v(y)=v(z)=+infinity`, so `v(J)=+infinity`. Theorem 1 gives normalized DIVISORIAL points with `v(J)` arbitrarily large. The source correction `Z(J)` therefore has no global lower bound by any finite multiple of `Z(m)`. The same is true of `Z(JL)` for every nonzero coherent ideal `L`, since `JL` is still contained in `p`. Over an algebraically closed field of characteristicZERO this is an explicit normal algebraic threefold exhibiting the failure of the correction comparison used in the isolated source proof. It does not show that its actual geometric envelopes are discontinuous.

For the simplest direct illustration of unbounded valuations, in the regular ring `k[x,y]_(x,y)` the ideal `(x)` has the normalized monomial divisorial valuations `v_N(x)=N,v_N(y)=1`, for every integer `N>=1`. Their limiting support is `(x)`. Here `(x)` is only an illustrative chosen ideal; the regular germ's Jacobian ideal is the unit ideal, so this example is not a failure of the regular-envelope approximation theorem.

## 4. Application boundary: the published upper-approximation proof

The published source discussed here is the THREE-author Boucksom--de Fernex--Favre paper *The volume of an isolated singularity*, Duke Mathematical Journal 161(8) (2012), from the author's official PDF. It is different from the FOUR-author singular-multiplier-ideal paper. In its isolated, normal, algebraically closed characteristic-zero setting, Theorem 4.11 proves upper approximation of `W=Env_X(C)` by rational multiples of primary ideal b-divisors. Its proof uses the inequality

`Z(b_k) >= kW + Q`, where `Q=Z(d(K_X))+Z(Jac_X)`.

It then bounds `Q` below by a constant multiple of `Z(m)` and uses `W <= epsilon Z(m)` for nonzero `W` to obtain

`Z(b_k) >= (k+c) W`.

The reciprocal `1/(k+c)` gives the desired GLOBAL upper bound on `W`, before the coefficientwise limit is used. Unit correction factors and the zero envelope require the explicit qualifications already recorded for ROOT360: cosupport contained in the closed point is the correct formulation, and rational constants are handled with the sign-appropriate upward rounding. Those qualifications are not new accepted envelope premises here.

For nonzero coherent correction ideals `J_1,J_2` in a normal local domain, `Q=Z(J_1J_2)`. If a prime `p != m` contains either factor, their product is proper and nonprimary. Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 prove that **no finite constant can make `Q >= c Z(m)` on all centered divisors**. More generally, this same obstruction applies to any proposed correction ideal with positive-dimensional cosupport. The proof does not need resolution, an SNC model, or a conjectural comparison with a psh order completion.

An m-primary truncation `J + a^N` has `v(J+a^N)=min(v(J),N)` on `X_a`. It restores a finite bound for that FIXED `N`, but it is not an unchanged version of `J`: at the point `v_0` above its value is exactly `N`, rather than infinity. For increasing `N`, the bound itself must increase without bound, as already seen at `v_0`. Thus simply truncating the correction and then citing the same uniform constant is invalid. This does not rule out a genuinely new two-parameter construction with a proved estimate and a controlled choice of parameters.

The finite-trace-to-uniform argument of ROOT360 begins with globally upper approximating nef primary b-divisors and compares their finite determining traces with the obstacle. Corollary 3 explains why finite trace errors alone cannot replace that missing global upper approximation in the nonprimary-correction setting. Neither a finite number of tests nor exact byte bindings of source statements supplies the missing inequality.
