# A boundary obstruction to sequential classical-psh regularization

Research draft, 3 October 2026. Partial progress for P02.1--P02.4.
This is **not** a characterization of the spaces in any atomic target.
If arbitrary decreasing nets are permitted, this example has explicit
approximating nets and does not refute that formulation.

## Precise statement

Let k be an uncountable algebraically closed field, equipped with the
trivial absolute value. Let K = k((pi)), with |pi| = exp(-1). For every
integer n >= 2 put

    D = M(K<T>),   X = M(K<T_1,...,T_n>).

Let eta be the Gauss point of D, p:X -> D the first-coordinate projection,
and i:D -> X the closed analytic embedding T_1=T, T_2=...=T_n=0. Define

    w(y) = 1 if y=eta, and 0 otherwise;
    u = w o p;       x = i(eta).

Then X is a good strict separated pure n-dimensional K-analytic space.
The function u is finite, bounded, upper semicontinuous, and classically
psh with the full scalar-extension curve-test meaning. The point x lies
in the relative boundary of X. On no ordinary open neighborhood W of x
is u the pointwise limit of a sequence of continuous real functions.
In particular there is no local decreasing sequence of semipositive real
PL, finite locally Fubini--Study, weakly smooth psh, or ordinary CLD-smooth
psh functions converging to u.

The last assertion for locally Fubini--Study uses the finite, basepoint-free
local logarithmic-max convention of the target. If one additionally allows
logarithmic maxima with a base locus, a decreasing approximating sequence
still cannot work: each term must be finite at x (it is at least u(x)=1),
and therefore is finite and continuous on a neighborhood of x; the same
proof only uses continuity there.

There are explicit decreasing **nets** of semipositive real PL / locally
Fubini--Study functions, and a separate decreasing net of ordinary
CLD-smooth psh functions, converging pointwise to u on X. For the same
construction over a *countable* algebraically closed coefficient field k,
these nets have decreasing cofinal sequences. Thus the cardinality
threshold is exact for this particular example, not for arbitrary spaces
or arbitrary classical psh functions.

The following are the exact relevant statements; they are cited, not
claimed independently proved by the present draft.

1. Section 2.4: M(A) has the topology generated by all evaluations |f|,
   f in A; it is compact. Its Shilov boundary is the unique minimal
   subset on which the maximum of every analytic function is attained.
   The spectrum is Hausdorff since distinct seminorms are separated by
   an evaluation. We apply this to the strict Tate algebras displayed above.
2. Section 3.1(3): the relative boundary of an affinoid analytic curve
   coincides with its Shilov boundary. Here an analytic curve means a
   separated analytic space of pure dimension one. D satisfies this.
3. Proposition 3.5(3): if an upper semicontinuous function
   v:C -> R union {-infinity} on an analytic curve C is subharmonic on
   Int(C), then it is subharmonic on C. Subharmonicity here is Definition
   3.4: after every nontrivially valued analytic field extension and every
   map from a smooth connected strict curve, the pullback is Thuillier
   subharmonic or identically -infinity. This is a functorial theorem,
   including scalar extensions, not merely an interior Laplacian test.
4. Section 4.1.2: on an analytic curve, a function is classically psh if
   and only if it is subharmonic. Definition 4.1 of classical psh tests
   every analytic extension K'/K, every K'-analytic curve C, and every
   analytic map C -> X_{K'} using the pullback through X_{K'} -> X.
5. Proposition 4.2(2): log|f| is classically psh for analytic f.
   Proposition 4.2(3): nonnegative linear combinations and finite maxima
   of classically psh functions are classically psh. Proposition 4.2(5):
   v is classically psh iff its pullback to any analytic extension is.
   Proposition 4.2(6), forward direction: the pullback of a classically
   psh function through any analytic morphism is classically psh.
6. Theorem 4.3: if the valuation on K is nontrivial, X is good and
   strictly K-analytic, and h:X -> R is real PL, then h is semipositive
   if and only if h is classically psh. We use it only for the displayed
   finite PL functions on D and X.
7. Section 2.11(4), equations (2.11.3)--(2.11.4): for X -> Y -> S,
   boundary(X/S) is contained in boundary(X/Y) union the inverse image
   of boundary(Y/S), with equality if Y -> S is locally separated.
   The structural morphism of the affinoid D is separated; hence
   p^{-1}(boundary(D)) is contained in boundary(X).

The geometry of a strict closed polydisc is the standard Tate-algebra
geometry: it is good, strict, separated, and pure dimension equal to its
number of variables (Berkovich, *Spectral theory and analytic geometry
over non-Archimedean fields*, 1990, Chapter 2, Section 2.1). The example
uses exactly this polydisc, no singular or nonstrict quotient. No claim
of boundaryless smoothness is made: it is a rig-smooth polydisc with
relative boundary.

For the negative result, the only facts about the four approximant
classes are definitional continuity facts. Real PL functions are
continuous; a finite local logarithmic maximum is continuous; ordinary
smooth functions are locally smooth Euclidean functions of logarithms
of analytic units; weakly smooth functions are locally smooth functions
of continuous harmonic tropicalizations. No positivity comparison
between ordinary and weak smoothness enters the proof.

## Proof of the obstruction

### 1. The boundary jump is classically psh

The field K is complete and nontrivially discretely valued. The Gauss
seminorm of f = sum_j c_j T^j is max_j |c_j|; the maximum exists because
c_j -> 0. It is a multiplicative seminorm (normalize two nonzero series
and reduce their product to the product of two nonzero polynomials in
k[T]). All points of D have |f(y)| <= ||f||_Gauss, and equality holds at
eta. Thus {eta} is a Shilov boundary and, by minimality, is the Shilov
boundary. By the curve-boundary statement, boundary(D)={eta}.

The singleton {eta} is closed in the Hausdorff space D. Therefore w is
upper semicontinuous: for 0<a<=1, {w<a}=D minus {eta}, with the other
sublevel sets either empty or all of D. On Int(D), w=0. Constants are
subharmonic, and Proposition 3.5(3) makes w subharmonic on the entire D.
Section 4.1.2 makes it classically psh. Pullback by p using Proposition
4.2(6) makes u classically psh on X. Its upper semicontinuity and finite
values also follow directly from the formula. Equation (2.11.4), with
equality for the separated D, puts x in boundary(X).

This step treats all scalar extensions: Proposition 3.5(3) has exactly
the Definition 3.4 functorial meaning, and Proposition 4.2(6) has exactly
the Definition 4.1 meaning. It would be invalid to replace this step by
the assertion that an arbitrary current-zero equation pulls back.

### 2. Every neighborhood of eta contains cofinitely many coefficient points

For a in k write a also for the K-rational point T=a of D. For each
nonzero f in K<T>, choose c in K* with |c|=||f||_Gauss. The reduction
of c^{-1} f is a nonzero polynomial bar(f) in k[T]. It is a polynomial
because the coefficients tend to zero. For all a except its finitely
many roots, reduction of c^{-1}f(a) is nonzero, and hence

    |f(a)| = ||f||_Gauss = |f(eta)|.

For zero f the equality holds at every a. An ordinary basic neighborhood
of eta imposes open conditions on finitely many evaluations |f|. Outside
the union of the finitely many root sets, each condition has exactly its
value at eta, so it is satisfied. Since these basic sets generate the
topology, every ordinary neighborhood of eta contains all but finitely
many of the points a in k. The points a are distinct and none is eta:
|T-a|(a)=0 whereas |T-a|(eta)=1.

### 3. There is no continuous pointwise approximating sequence on a common open

Suppose W is an ordinary open neighborhood of x and h_j:W -> R are
continuous with h_j -> u pointwise. Set U=i^{-1}(W), an ordinary open
neighborhood of eta. For each j, continuity at x gives a neighborhood
N_j of eta inside U such that

    |h_j(i(y))-h_j(x)| < 1/4  for every y in N_j.

Each N_j contains every coefficient point a outside a finite set E_j.
Because k is uncountable, choose a outside the countable union of all
E_j. Then i(a) belongs to W, u(i(a))=0, and the displayed estimate holds
for every j. On the other hand h_j(x) -> u(x)=1. Thus h_j(i(a)) > 1/2
for all sufficiently large j, contradicting h_j(i(a)) -> 0.

No monotonicity was used: the obstruction is to any pointwise continuous
sequence. For functions that are merely continuous near x, use their
individual neighborhoods when choosing N_j; the same proof works.
Every requested finite approximant class has this continuity at x, so
all four sequential regularizations fail. The quantifiers are one common
ordinary W and all j; a different domain for each j would not meet the
target's local approximation requirement.

## Explicit decreasing nets and the sharp countability threshold for this w

### 4. Every non-Gauss point is detected by a coefficient linear factor

If y != eta, some f in K<T> satisfies |f(y)| < ||f||_Gauss. Normalize
to ||f||_Gauss=1. Its reduction bar(f) is a nonzero polynomial. Lift its
coefficients using the coefficient field k subset K to obtain a polynomial
q in k[T]. Then ||f-q||_Gauss <= |pi| < 1. Algebraic closedness of k
gives q=c product_a(T-a)^{e_a}, with c in k*, and a finite product.
The inequality |f(y)|<1 implies |q(y)|<1. Every |T-a|(y) is at most 1
and |c|=1, so at least one factor satisfies |T-a|(y)<1. If q is constant
this is impossible, so this reasoning also excludes that case.

This uses algebraic closedness of the coefficient field, not algebraic
closedness of the valued field K. K itself need not be algebraically closed.

### 5. Locally Fubini--Study and semipositive real-PL nets

Direct Fin(k) x N_{>=1} by inclusion in the first coordinate and the
usual order in the second. For finite F subset k put

    P_F(T)=product_{a in F}(T-a),   P_empty=1,
    v_{F,m}(y)=max(0, 1 + m log|P_F(y)|).

At a zero of P_F the value is 0. Equivalently,

    v_{F,m}=log max(1, |pi^{-1} P_F^m|).

This is a finite logarithmic maximum of two analytic sections 1 and
pi^{-1}P_F^m, with denominator 1 and no common zero. It is locally
Fubini--Study in the target's local logarithmic-max convention. It is
continuous and real PL: the strict Laurent domains |pi^{-1}P_F^m|<=1
and |pi^{-1}P_F^m|>=1 cover D; the expressions are 0 on the first
and log|pi^{-1}P_F^m| on the second, where the analytic function is a
unit, and they agree on the overlap. The same formula in T_1 works on X.

By Proposition 4.2(2),(3), this finite maximum is classically psh.
Theorem 4.3 makes it semipositive, on the good strict D and on X.
This is a direct check, including boundary points; it does not use a
model-to-Fubini--Study approximation theorem that assumes boundarylessness.

All factors have norm <=1 on D. Increasing F or m therefore decreases
the argument 1+m log|P_F| and decreases v_{F,m}. At eta its value is
1. At y!=eta, choose a with |T-a|(y)<1 from step 4. Once F contains
a, increasing m forces the argument to -infinity (or it is already
-infinity). The net is eventually 0 at y. Hence v_{F,m} decreases
pointwise to w, and v_{F,m} o p decreases pointwise to u on X.

### 6. A separate ordinary CLD-smooth psh net

Choose a nonnegative even C-infinity probability density rho supported
in [-1/4,1/4], and put

    lambda(t)=integral_R max(0,t+s) rho(s) ds.

Differentiation gives lambda'(t)=integral_{s>-t} rho(s)ds >=0 and
lambda''(t)=rho(-t)>=0. Thus lambda is smooth, nondecreasing, and
convex. It is 0 for t<=-1/4 and equals t for t>=1/4, because rho has
mean zero. Define

    q_{F,m}=lambda(1+m log|P_F|),

with the value 0 on P_F=0. Near a zero, the argument is on the constant
negative tail of lambda, so q is identically 0 there. On P_F!=0,
P_F is an analytic unit on a neighborhood, and q is the smooth function
t -> lambda(1-mt) pulled back under the analytic moment-map
t=-log|P_F|. Its Euclidean second derivative is m^2 lambda''(1-mt)>=0.
This proves ordinary CLD smoothness and smooth-form psh positivity on
all of D and X, including their relative-boundary points. The local
expressions agree because they define the same global function of P_F.

The net decreases because lambda is nondecreasing. Its value at eta is
lambda(1)=1. At other points it is eventually 0, by step 4 and the
negative constant tail. Consequently q_{F,m} decreases to w, and the
same formula in T_1 decreases to u. Under an arbitrary complete
extension L/K these formulas are identical analytic expressions on X_L;
their unit-locus and constant-tail argument still proves ordinary
smoothness and positivity. Pointwise convergence to u o pi_{L/K}
holds because norms of the K-defined P_F at a point upstairs equal
their norms at its image downstairs. No current is pulled back in this
argument. No weak-smooth net or positivity comparison is needed for the
negative weak-smooth result.

### 7. The distinction between a net and a sequence is exact here

If k is countable, enumerate k={a_1,a_2,...}, take F_j={a_1,...,a_j}
and m_j=j. The preceding directed set has this cofinal sequence. The
proofs give decreasing sequences v_{F_j,j} and q_{F_j,j}, converging
pointwise to w on D and to u on X, even though the limit is discontinuous.
If k is uncountable, a countable family of finite F's cannot exhaust k,
so there is no countable cofinal subset of Fin(k) x N. Step 3 proves
the stronger assertion that even sequences unrelated to these explicit
nets cannot approximate u by continuous functions near x.

There is no locally uniform convergence at x: w jumps between value 1
at eta and value 0 at coefficient points in every neighborhood. A
locally uniform limit of continuous functions would be continuous.

## Comparison and remaining scope

The supplied catalogue's invariant toric theorems start with finite
continuous invariant current-psh functions on ordinary open torus domains;
its curve theorem assumes a continuous function on an open of a smooth
proper algebraic curve. This example is discontinuous at a relative
boundary of an affinoid domain. It satisfies the good strict ambient
hypotheses of the broad targets but none of those additional starting-class
hypotheses. It is not an abstract tropical obstruction: the analytic space,
field, function, test theorem, and point of failure have all been specified.

This establishes a partial negative result for sequential formulations
when relative boundaries and uncountable residue fields are allowed.
It does not classify all spaces with the approximation property, does
not settle any boundaryless higher-dimensional case, does not treat
arbitrary continuous noninvariant functions, and does not disprove a
net formulation. All four full atoms remain open in this report.
The countability threshold is sharp only for the displayed Laurent-field
boundary jump. No novelty or priority claim is made relative to the
entire literature; the result is not one of the known catalogue cases.
