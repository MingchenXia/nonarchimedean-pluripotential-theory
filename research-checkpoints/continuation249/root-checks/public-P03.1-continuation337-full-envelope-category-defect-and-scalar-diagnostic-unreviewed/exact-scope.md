# Exact scope

Decision: original P03.1 is not solved; `candidate=false`.

The binding target is classical positivity of the usc regularization of the supremum of the full, globally defined, real-valued LFS/semipositive-real-PL minorant union for every usc obstacle on the asserted analytic domain. Classical positivity includes every complete analytic field extension and every curve map there. The empty-family value is identically minus infinity. Relative boundary is allowed in the input problem. Normality or geometric unibranchness in higher dimension, with sharp substantive hypotheses, is the unresolved range.

Proposition 1 concerns that entire family in any dimension: if it is nonempty, its raw envelope is finite lsc and its strict usc defect is exactly its discontinuity set, an F-sigma meagre set. The empty case is treated separately. It applies at boundary points. Proposition 2 is a topological comparison with the pulled-back ground-field raw family; it does not identify the new family over an extension. It bounds the scalar defect by the base defect height, and proves the compact-fiber maximum formula when the projection is closed and has compact fibers.

The hairy-interval example proves that these topological facts, connected compact fibers and a continuous section do not force compatibility. Its competitors are continuous Lipschitz functions on a real interval, not analytic psh or real PL. It is not a normal analytic counterexample.

The split-branch and pinched-bidisc reconstructions use finite usc obstacles and the entire union. The nonsplit-node reconstruction uses a finite continuous obstacle, an irreducible strict surface and a genuine finite-extension disc test. None lies in the everywhere geometrically unibranch residual range. They are recorded as necessity reconstructions of supplied data, not new solutions. In the pinching geometry we prove seminormality of the actual affine algebra by the square-and-cube criterion, and do not claim a fresh proof of every analytic stalk's seminormality. Rig-smoothness off the pinched point allows the bidisc's absolute boundary; we do not call that entire locus smooth and boundaryless.

No general normal higher-dimensional sufficiency, geometric-unibranch sufficiency, sharp classification, scalar compatibility, PL globalization, or removable-defect theorem is established. No theorem restricted to a toric invariant family, a common-modulus family, a projective-fiber sector, or ground-field curves is offered as the original solution. P03.2 and P03.3 remain separate and receive no asserted general result.
