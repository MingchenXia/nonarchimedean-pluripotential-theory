This is a partial mathematical draft, not an independent correctness verdict. Let K be any complete nontrivially valued non-Archimedean field, k its residue field, a in K with 0<|a|<1, and c=-log|a|. Put D=M(K<T>) and X_n=M(K<T_1,...,T_n>) for n>=2. Let eta be the Gauss point, p the first-coordinate projection, and i:D->X_n the analytic section with the other coordinates zero. Define w=1_{eta}, u=w composed with p, and x=i(eta). These are actual good strict affinoid spaces, separated and of dimensions 1 and n. Claim: u is finite bounded upper semicontinuous and classical psh, x lies in the relative boundary, and u has local pointwise continuous sequential approximation near x iff k is countable. In the countable case a decreasing finite semipositive real-PL sequence works on all of X_n. A decreasing semipositive real-PL net works for every k.

Primary inputs and scope. Classical psh means Definition 4.1 of Gubler–Rabinoff, Classically psh and pluriharmonic functions on Berkovich spaces, arXiv:2506.13548v2, 17 September 2025: upper semicontinuity and subharmonic pullback along every analytic curve after every analytic field extension. Definition 3.4 permits identically negative-infinite functions; Section 4.1.3 reduces testing to smooth connected strict scalar curves. Proposition 4.2(2),(3) supplies analytic logarithms, finite maxima and nonnegative real combinations. Theorem 4.3 states that, over a complete nontrivially valued field, an already real-valued real-PL function on a good strictly analytic space is semipositive iff it is classical psh. No separatedness, pure-dimensionality or boundaryless assumption is added. Semipositivity is geometric residue/model nefness, as in Gubler–Jell–Rabinoff, Forms on Berkovich spaces based on harmonic tropicalizations, arXiv:2111.05741v3, Proposition 7.1 and Definition 7.2: nonnegative degrees on integral proper residue curves.

The immediate applied statements and proofs were read in authenticated primary texts. The proof of the classical/real-PL comparison uses the distinct March paper On semipositive piecewise linear functions in non-archimedean analytic geometry, arXiv:2503.05513v1. Its actual curve comparison and smooth-curve recognition are Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 5.4; the June paper cites earlier labels 3.1 and 3.5. The March proof uses residue degrees, local lifting of residue curves and proper-surjective semipositivity descent. Deeper published foundations—including Thuillier harmonic comparison and Poincare–Lelong, Wanner's PL/current comparison inside that proof, formal models, Temkin reductions and degree projection formulas—are imported and disclosed, not freshly proved recursively. No book theorem or historical PASS receipt is a premise.

Legal truncation lemma. For an analytic f on a good strict space and b in K*, H=log max(|b|,|f|) is finite integral PL and semipositive. On each strict affinoid neighborhood the strict Laurent domains |f|<=|b| and |f|>=|b| form a finite G-cover. Their expressions are log|b| and log|f|. On the latter f is a unit, with inverse furnished by adjoining b/f. The expressions agree on the intersection. The cut is an actual field value, so the construction respects strictness. Analytic log/max laws make H classical psh; the already-real-PL comparison gives residue-nef semipositivity. Positive real multiples and finite positive sums remain semipositive real-PL by common refinement and the cone/comparison argument. This does not assume closure under maxima of arbitrary real-PL functions, which can fail in the strict topology.

Decreasing-limit justification. An infimum of decreasing finite continuous classical psh functions is upper semicontinuous because its strict sublevels are unions of open strict sublevels. After any scalar extension and smooth connected strict curve test, write the finite subharmonic pullbacks as g_i and their infimum as g. If g is identically negative infinity, it is allowed. Otherwise let A be a strictly affinoid test domain and H harmonic with g<=H on its finite boundary. For epsilon>0 choose an index at each boundary point with g_i<H+epsilon. Directedness supplies one upper bound for these finitely many indices. Harmonic comparison gives g_i<=H+epsilon throughout A. Taking the infimum and then epsilon to zero proves the required subharmonic test, including negative-infinite values. This agrees with GR Proposition 4.2(4).

Detection lemma. Let R be the set of monic irreducibles q in k[T], and choose monic lifts Q_q in K^circ[T]. Every y different from eta has |Q_q(y)|<1 for some q. A nonzero Tate series has attained Gauss norm in |K*| because its coefficients tend to zero. Dividing by an attaining coefficient gives norm one and a nonzero polynomial reduction. Reduction of products proves multiplicativity of the Gauss norm. Every bounded multiplicative seminorm is bounded by that norm: apply its boundedness inequality to f^N and take Nth roots. Distinctness from eta therefore gives f with |f(y)|<||f||. Normalize and factor its reduction as dbar times a product of irreducibles. Lift dbar to a unit d and form the corresponding product R_f of the Q_q. Then ||f-R_f||<1, also for nondiscrete valuations: the difference coefficients have norms less than one and tend to zero, so their attained maximum is less than one. Hence |R_f(y)|<1. Since each factor has norm at most one and d has norm one, some factor has norm less than one. A nonzero constant reduction cannot occur here. No coefficient subfield or algebraic closure is needed.

Net construction. Direct Fin(R) times the positive integers by inclusion and integer order. Put P_F=product of Q_q for q in F, with P_empty=1, and
V_(F,m)=c^(-1) log max(1,|a^(-1)P_F^m|)=max(0,1+(m/c)log|P_F|).
At zeros the first formula is finite. The truncation lemma and positive scaling prove semipositive strict real-PL membership. The second formula is only a numerical identity. Every factor has norm at most one, so increasing either index decreases V. At eta each monic product has norm one and V=1. Away from eta, a detecting factor of norm r<1 bounds every later term containing it by max(0,1+(m/c)log r), eventually zero. If r=0 the value is zero immediately. Thus V decreases to w. Substituting T_1 produces one globally defined net on X_n decreasing to u. The decreasing-limit argument proves classical admissibility with every scalar curve test.

Boundary. The closed Gauss singleton attains every analytic norm and is therefore the Shilov boundary. GR-C Section 3.1(3) identifies it with the relative boundary of the affinoid curve D. The boundary composition rule of Section 2.11(4') is equality for X_n->D->M(K), since D's structural map is separated. It gives p^(-1)(eta) contained in the actual relative boundary of X_n, including x. The indicator formula also proves upper semicontinuity directly.

Countable case. If k is countable, k[T] and R are countable. R is infinite by the product-plus-one argument. Enumerate q_1,q_2,... and take F_j={q_1,...,q_j}, m_j=j. For every finite F and every m, sufficiently large j contain F and satisfy j>=m. This proves cofinality and hence decreasing convergence of the displayed sequence. No second-countability or countable dense subset of K is assumed for this particular function.

Uncountable case. Choose one lift b_alpha in K^circ of each alpha in k and the rigid point z_alpha with T=b_alpha. Every neighborhood of eta contains all but finitely many z_alpha: a normalized Tate series has nonzero polynomial reduction, and outside its finitely many residue roots its evaluation equals its Gauss norm. A basic neighborhood imposes conditions on finitely many evaluations. Suppose continuous h_j on an ordinary neighborhood W of x converge pointwise to u. Continuity supplies neighborhoods N_j of eta inside i^(-1)(W) with |h_j(i(y))-h_j(x)|<1/4. Their exceptional residue classes form a countable union of finite sets. Choose alpha outside it. All inequalities then hold at z_alpha, contradicting h_j(x)->1 and h_j(i(z_alpha))->0. The points are distinct by evaluating T-b_alpha. Monotonicity was not used.

The obstruction also passes to higher-dimensional good strict spaces admitting an analytic retract D->Y->D, by restriction through the section. The polydiscs provide actual examples. All constructed expressions agree on intersections of their strict domains because they are restrictions of one formula. Over any analytic extension the original formulas converge to the pulled-back indicator; they need not converge to the native upstairs Gauss indicator.

This criterion is exact only for this function. It is a relative-boundary sequence obstruction and admits explicit decreasing nets. Countable residue is not asserted sufficient for every classical psh input, and the original P02.1 characterization remains unresolved.
