Let K be complete and nontrivially valued, and X any good strictly K-analytic space. Give analytic functions f_1,f_2,... on this one X, bounded in absolute value by one, and real lambda_i>0. Define u=sum_i lambda_i log|f_i| as the infimum of its nonpositive finite partial sums, with log 0=-infinity. This definition uses analytic input data, not an approximation class. No continuity, finiteness or summability is assumed. Claim: u is classical psh and the decreasing pointwise limit on all of X of
u_j=sum_(i=1)^j lambda_i log max(|a|^j,|f_i|),
where a is fixed with 0<|a|<1. Every u_j is finite semipositive real-PL. Identically negative-infinite targets are permitted. Zero coefficients can be discarded.

The complete strict truncation and classical decreasing-limit lemmas, with exact primary-source conditions, are proved in n1 and used here. Each truncation is integral PL, cut at an actual field value |a|^j; real coefficients are applied afterward. No arbitrary-real-max closure, all-domain lisse identification, current recognition or smooth/FS comparison is used. Semipositivity is geometric residue/model nefness.

Proof. Each u_j is finite, continuous, nonpositive and semipositive real-PL by common refinement of its finitely many truncation covers. Increasing j lowers every old truncation threshold and adds a nonpositive summand, so u_(j+1)<=u_j. Omitting a nonpositive tail and clipping terms upward gives u_j>=u. Write ell=inf_j u_j. For fixed N and j>=N,
u_j<=sum_(i<=N) lambda_i log max(|a|^j,|f_i|).
At each point the finite sum on the right decreases to sum_(i<=N) lambda_i log|f_i|, including negative infinity when a factor vanishes. Hence ell is at most every finite partial sum, and therefore ell<=u. The reverse inequality was already proved, so ell=u everywhere, including divergent and polar cases. The decreasing-limit proof in n1 verifies upper semicontinuity and every scalar curve test.

All local expressions are restrictions of the same global formula and agree term by term on overlaps. Under an analytic morphism from a good strict source or any analytic field extension, use the pulled-back analytic functions. Their evaluations and bounds are the downstairs ones. The same truncation proof gives finite semipositive real-PL functions, decreasing to the pulled-back series. This concerns the original analytic data, not arbitrary new functions over an extension. No net extraction occurs: the sequence is explicitly constructed from the given countable family.

If C is compact and u is finite continuous on C, the increasing open subsets defined by u_j-u<epsilon cover C. Compactness supplies one index covering C, proving uniform convergence without metrizability. Only pointwise convergence is claimed for discontinuous or extended-valued targets.

Concrete noninvariant example. Over Q_p take the strict bidisc, f_1=T_1-T_2, f_i=T_1-p^(i-1)T_2 for i>=2, and lambda_i=2^(-i). All factors have norm at most one on the whole domain. At the Gauss point u=0. At (1,1) the first factor vanishes, giving u=-infinity. At (1+p,1), the first factor has norm |p| and all later factors have norm one, giving u=(1/2)log|p|. Multiplication of the first coordinate by the unit 1+p changes these values, so u is not invariant under the maximal compact torus. The sequence covers all its polar lines and relative-boundary points on one actual bidisc.

This is an explicit sufficient starting-function sector on good strict spaces. It does not prove that arbitrary classical psh functions have such a series representation, and therefore does not solve the original all-functions characterization.
