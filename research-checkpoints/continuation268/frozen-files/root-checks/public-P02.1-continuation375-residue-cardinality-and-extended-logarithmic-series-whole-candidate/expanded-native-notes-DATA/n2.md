Let K be complete and nontrivially valued and let X be any good strictly K-analytic space. Give analytic functions f_1,f_2,... on this one X, with |f_i|<=1 everywhere, and real lambda_i>0. Define u(y)=sum_i lambda_i log|f_i(y)| as the infimum of the nonpositive finite partial sums, with log 0=-infinity. This input definition uses analytic functions, not an approximation class. No continuity, finiteness or coefficient summability is assumed. Claim: u is classical psh and is the decreasing pointwise limit on all of X of finite semipositive real-PL functions u_j=sum_(i=1)^j lambda_i log max(|a|^j,|f_i|), where a is any fixed field element with 0<|a|<1. The target may be identically -infinity. Zero coefficients may be discarded to avoid undefined zero times negative infinity.

The complete strict integral truncation and classical decreasing-limit lemmas, with exact primary-source conditions, are proved in n1 and used here. In particular each max is an integral PL construction cut at an actual field value |a|^j; the real lambda_i are applied afterward. The approximant does not rely on arbitrary real-PL-max closure, an all-domain lisse identification, current recognition, or a smooth/FS comparison. Its semipositivity is geometric residue/model nefness, supplied after strict membership by the authenticated real-PL/classical comparison.

Proof. Every u_j is finite, continuous, nonpositive, and semipositive real-PL by finite common refinement of the truncation covers. All these terms have the same domain X. Raising j lowers the truncation threshold in every old summand and adds a nonpositive new term. Thus u_(j+1)<=u_j. Omitting a nonpositive tail and clipping old terms upward proves u_j>=u. Let ell=inf_j u_j. For every fixed N and j>=N, negativity of the omitted terms gives u_j<=sum_(i<=N) lambda_i log max(|a|^j,|f_i|). At a fixed point this finite sum decreases to sum_(i<=N) lambda_i log|f_i|. If any factor vanishes, both this limit and its extended sum are -infinity; positive lambda_i create no ambiguity. Therefore ell<=sum_(i<=N) lambda_i log|f_i| for every N. Taking the infimum in N yields ell<=u. Together with ell>=u this proves ell=u at every point, including all divergent and polar cases. The decreasing-limit argument of n1 verifies usc and all scalar curve tests, so u is classical psh.

Every strict-domain local expression is a restriction of the displayed global formula; on intersections the values agree term by term. Under an analytic morphism from a good strict source, or under any analytic field extension, evaluate the same pulled-back analytic functions. Their values and bounds are the downstairs values. The formula therefore commutes with pullback, remains finite semipositive strict real-PL by the same truncation argument, and decreases to the pulled-back series. This concerns pullbacks of the given data, not arbitrary new psh functions over an extension. There is no net extraction: the countable sequence is explicitly constructed from the given countable analytic input family.

If C is a compact subset on which u is finite and continuous, the increasing open subsets of C defined by u_j-u<epsilon cover C. Compactness supplies one index covering C. Hence convergence is uniform on C, without a metrizability assumption. For discontinuous or extended-valued limits only pointwise convergence is asserted.

Concrete noninvariant polar example. Over K=Q_p take the actual strict bidisc, f_1=T_1-T_2, f_i=T_1-p^(i-1)T_2 for i>=2, and lambda_i=2^(-i). Each function has norm at most one on the entire bidisc. At its Gauss point all f_i have norm one, giving u=0. At (1,1), f_1 vanishes and u=-infinity. At (1+p,1), f_1 has norm |p| and all later f_i are units of norm one, giving u=(1/2)log|p|. Multiplication of the first coordinate by the unit 1+p sends the first point to the second, so the function is not invariant under the maximal compact torus. Every polar line f_i=0 is included in the domain. The displayed u_j approximates all of it, including its relative boundary and extended poles.

This proves a sufficient starting-function sector on a geometrically unrestricted good strict X, not a sufficient all-functions regime for arbitrary higher-dimensional germs. No representation of an arbitrary classical psh function by this analytic series has been proved. The extra hypothesis is explicit and noncircular. The full P02.1 characterization remains unresolved, and no FS, weak-smooth or ordinary-smooth conclusion is inferred.
