Scalar-extension claim. For any analytic extension L/K, the n1 net, and when available its sequence, formed with the original K-defined polynomials converges to w_K o pi on D_L, by the literal norm identity for analytic evaluation. Its support is pi^(-1)(eta_K), not necessarily the singleton eta_L. n2 similarly preserves the series sequence for the original analytic data. This is a pointwise pullback statement on a fixed domain with exact agreement of its formulas on all strict-domain overlaps. It is not a theorem about every new classical psh function over L.

An explicit field proof makes the distinction concrete. Take K=Q_p, an uncountable index set A, and F=Q_p(t_alpha:alpha in A). On a polynomial give the norm by the maximum norm of its finitely many coefficients, equivalently the p-adic valuation is the minimum coefficient valuation. After scalar normalization, nonzero polynomial reductions have nonzero product in the domain F_p[t_alpha]. Thus the Gauss norm is multiplicative and extends consistently to fractions. It gives a valued field extending Q_p. Only finitely many variables occur in each polynomial. Its residue field is F_p(t_alpha:alpha in A): a unit fraction has equal numerator and denominator valuations, so after cancelling their common scalar its residue is the quotient of their nonzero reductions; every such residue fraction lifts. Elements of positive valuation reduce to zero. Let L be the completion. It has the same residue field, because every integral element of L is within distance less than one of an element of F, which is then integral with the same residue. Thus L is complete and nontrivially valued, with uncountable residue field, while K has finite residue field.

By n1 the native jump on D_K has a decreasing semipositive real-PL sequence, and its literal scalar pullback has the same sequence on D_L. The native jump w_L=1_{eta_L} has no continuous pointwise approximating sequence near eta_L, again by n1. For a fixed alpha, the L-rational point T=t_alpha maps to eta_K. To prove this directly, normalize any nonzero K-Tate series to norm one; its nonzero polynomial reduction lies in F_p[T], and evaluation at the transcendental residue of t_alpha is nonzero. Hence its value at that point is its Gauss norm. Equality for all analytic evaluations identifies its image as eta_K. Therefore (w_K o pi)(t_alpha)=1, whereas w_L(t_alpha)=0. The example does not claim the full all-functions P02.1 property over Q_p; it only distinguishes these two functions and the exact single-function criterion of n1. Substitution of T_1 gives the same phenomenon on actual higher-dimensional strict polydiscs.
