This is a partial mathematical draft, not an independent correctness verdict. Let K be any complete nontrivially valued non-Archimedean field, k its residue field, a in K with 0<|a|<1, and c=-log|a|. Put D=M(K<T>), X_n=M(K<T_1,...,T_n>) for n>=2. Let eta be the Gauss point, p the first-coordinate projection, and i:D->X_n the analytic section with the other coordinates zero. Define w=1_{eta}, u=w o p and x=i(eta). These are actual good strict affinoid spaces, separated and of the standard Tate-algebra dimensions 1 and n. Claim: u is finite bounded usc and classical psh, x is in the relative boundary, and u has local pointwise continuous sequential approximation near x iff k is countable. In the countable case a decreasing finite semipositive real-PL sequence works on all of X_n. For every k there is a decreasing semipositive real-PL net on all of X_n.

Definitions and primary inputs. Classical psh is the upper semicontinuous scalar-extension curve-test definition in Gubler--Rabinoff, Classically psh and pluriharmonic functions on Berkovich spaces, arXiv:2506.13548v2 (17 September 2025), Definition 4.1. The curve class in Definition 3.4 permits the identically -infinity function and tests smooth connected strict curves over every nontrivially valued analytic extension; Section 4.1.3 allows these smooth tests for the classical class. Proposition 4.2(2),(3) supplies analytic logarithms, finite maxima, and nonnegative linear combinations; (4) supplies decreasing-net closure. Theorem 4.3 states exactly that, over a nontrivially valued complete field, an already real-valued real-PL function on a good strictly analytic space is semipositive iff it is classical psh. No separatedness, pure-dimensionality or boundaryless qualification is added. Semipositivity here is geometric: the residue line bundle on the reduction of the germ is nef, meaning nonnegative degrees on integral proper residue curves on a finite-type model. Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff, Forms on Berkovich spaces based on harmonic tropicalizations, arXiv:2111.05741v3, Definition 5.1, Proposition 7.1 and Definition 7.2 give strict PL and residue/formal-model nefness, not an approximation-based definition.

The proof of GR Theorem 4.3 invokes the distinct March paper On semipositive piecewise linear functions in non-archimedean analytic geometry, arXiv:2503.05513v1, whose actual curve comparison and smooth-curve recognition are Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 5.4. Deeper imported foundations include Thuillier harmonic comparison/Poincare--Lelong, Wanner's PL/current comparison inside the March proof, formal-model existence, Temkin reductions, degree projection formulas, and standard affinoid/Tate theory. Legal truncation lemma. For any analytic f on a good strict space and b in K*, H=log max(|b|,|f|) is finite integral PL and semipositive. On every strict affinoid neighborhood, the strict Laurent domains |f|<=|b| and |f|>=|b| form a finite G-cover. Their expressions are log|b| and log|f| respectively. On the second domain f is a unit, with inverse furnished by adjoining b/f. The expressions agree on the intersection, so they give one continuous integral PL function. The cut is an actual field value, preserving strictness. Analytic log/max laws make H classical psh, so the already-real-PL comparison proves residue-nef semipositivity. Positive real multiples and finite positive real sums remain real-PL by common refinement of their strict covers and are semipositive by the same cone/comparison argument. We do not assume closure under maxima of arbitrary real-PL functions, which can fail in the strict topology (GJR Example 5.3).

Decreasing-limit justification for these finite approximants. The infimum of finite continuous decreasing classical psh functions is usc: its strict sublevels are unions of the approximants' open strict sublevels. After any analytic extension and pullback to a smooth connected strict test curve, call the finite subharmonic functions g_i and their infimum g. If g is identically -infinity it is admissible. Otherwise take a strictly affinoid test domain A and a harmonic H on A with g<=H on its finite boundary. For each epsilon>0 choose an index at each boundary point with g_i<H+epsilon. A directed upper bound for those finitely many indices gives one function dominated by H+epsilon on the whole boundary. Harmonic comparison gives domination throughout A. Take the infimum and then epsilon to zero. This proves the required subharmonic test including negative-infinite values, and agrees with GR Proposition 4.2(4). Thus no current recognition is substituted for classical admissibility.

Detection lemma. Let R be the set of monic irreducibles q in k[T], and choose a monic coefficient lift Q_q in K^circ[T]. Every y!=eta has |Q_q(y)|<1 for some q. Indeed, a nonzero Tate series f has attained Gauss norm in |K*|, since its coefficients tend to zero. Dividing by an attaining coefficient gives norm one and nonzero polynomial reduction. Reduction of products proves multiplicativity of the Gauss norm. Every bounded multiplicative seminorm is bounded by this norm: apply its boundedness inequality to f^N and take Nth roots. Distinctness from eta gives f with |f(y)|<||f||. Normalize and factor its reduction as dbar times product q_r^{e_r}. Lift dbar to a unit d and put R_f=d product Q_(q_r)^{e_r}. Then ||f-R_f||<1. This strict inequality needs no discreteness: the difference coefficients all have norm less than one and tend to zero, so their attained maximum is less than one. Hence |R_f(y)|<1. All factors are at most one and d has norm one, so some factor is less than one. A constant nonzero reduction cannot occur in this situation. This proves detection without a coefficient subfield or algebraic closure.

Net. Direct Fin(R) x N_(>=1) by inclusion and integer order. Set P_F=product_(q in F)Q_q, P_empty=1, and V_(F,m)=c^(-1)log max(1,|a^(-1)P_F^m|)=max(0,1+(m/c)log|P_F|). At zeros use the first finite formula. The legal truncation lemma and positive scalar 1/c prove semipositive strict real-PL membership. The last identity is numerical, not an arbitrary-real-max membership assertion. Each factor has norm <=1, so increasing either index decreases V. At eta every monic product has norm one, giving value 1. Off eta a detecting factor of norm r<1 makes every index containing it bounded above by max(0,1+(m/c)log r), eventually zero. If r=0 it is zero immediately. Thus V decreases to w. Substituting T_1 gives one global net on X_n decreasing to u. The decreasing-limit argument proves w and u classical psh with every scalar curve test.

Boundary. The Gauss singleton is closed and attains every analytic norm, hence is the Shilov boundary. GR-C Section 3.1(3) identifies it with the relative boundary of the affinoid curve D. The boundary composition rule in Section 2.11(4') is equality for X_n->D->M(K), because D's structural map is separated. It implies p^(-1)(eta) is contained in the actual relative boundary of X_n. This proves x is a relative-boundary point, while the indicator formula proves usc directly as well.

Countable case. k[T] and R are countable when k is countable. R is infinite by the usual product-plus-one argument. Enumerate it q_1,q_2,... and set F_j={q_1,...,q_j}, m_j=j. For every finite F and every m, all sufficiently large j contain F and satisfy j>=m. This is a proved cofinal sequence, so it has the same decreasing pointwise limit on the whole domain. No second countability or countable dense subset of K is assumed for this particular function.

Uncountable case. Choose one lift b_alpha in K^circ of each alpha in k and the rigid point z_alpha:T=b_alpha. Every neighborhood of eta contains all but finitely many z_alpha. For a nonzero normalized Tate series its reduction is a nonzero polynomial; except at its finite root set in k, evaluation at b_alpha has the same norm as at eta. A basic neighborhood imposes conditions on only finitely many evaluations, so only finitely many residue classes are exceptional. Suppose h_j are continuous on an ordinary neighborhood W of x and converge pointwise to u. On U=i^(-1)(W), continuity at eta supplies neighborhoods N_j with |h_j(i(y))-h_j(x)|<1/4. Countably many such neighborhoods miss only a countable union of finite sets of z_alpha. Choose a residue class outside that union. At the chosen point all inequalities hold, whereas h_j(x)->1 and h_j(i(z_alpha))->0. Contradiction. These two points are distinct by evaluation of T-b_alpha. Monotonicity was never used.

The same negative argument passes to any good strict higher-dimensional space with an analytic retract D->Y->D, by restriction through the section. The polydiscs are actual examples. Every constructed formula is global on one fixed domain and agrees on its strict-domain intersections. Over any analytic extension, the same K-defined formulas converge to the pullback indicator, by equality of analytic evaluations downstairs/upstairs; they do not automatically give the native upstairs Gauss indicator.

Limitations: this is an exact countability criterion for one function, not the original all-functions space characterization. The failure requires the displayed relative boundary. It is not a net obstruction: the explicit net always works. Countable residue is not asserted sufficient for every classical psh input. No other P02 atom is counted.
