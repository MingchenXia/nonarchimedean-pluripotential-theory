## 2. Define the LOCAL supported intersection before using ideals

Let Y be a primary blowup, and E an actual effective exceptional integral Cartier divisor. E is proper over R and is supported over m. A common power of m kills O_E: this follows from coherence and its closed-fibre support on a finite affine cover. Thus its cohomology has finite R-length. For coherent F on E define

    chi_R(E,F)=sum_j (-1)^j length_R H^j(E,F).

For line bundles L1,...,Lr on Y, the function

    P_E(t1,...,tr)=chi_R(E,O_E tensor L1^t1 tensor ... tensor Lr^tr)

is a polynomial of total degree at most n-1. This does NOT assume E is a scheme over k: filter O_E by powers of m. The finitely many graded coherent quotients are sheaves on the proper k-scheme E0 defined by mO_E. Tensoring with a line bundle is exact, so Euler additivity expresses P_E as a finite sum of the polynomials in 0BEL Lemma33.45.1. For a module annihilated by m its R-length is its k-dimension. The dimension bound on each graded support is n-1. This proves the polynomial assertion also in mixed characteristic and without a coefficient field.

For integral exceptional Cartier divisors F1,...,Fp define

    Q_Y(F1,...,Fp,E)
       = coefficient of t1 ... tp in P_E(O(F1)^t1 ... O(Fp)^tp).

For p=0 this is length_R H0(E,O_E), since E is zero-dimensional. This definition keeps O_E with ALL of its nilpotent thickness. Replacing E by its reduction would lose the relevant multiplicities.

The leading-cycle formula is

    Q_Y(F1,...,Fp,E)
      = sum_Z length_(O_Y,eta_Z)(O_E,eta_Z)
                   ( O(F1)|Z ... O(Fp)|Z . Z ),                 (2.1)

where Z ranges over integral reduced dimension-p components of E, and the parenthesized intersection is the ordinary numerical intersection on the proper k-scheme Z from 0BEL. To verify the weight, apply complete Lemma33.45.2 to every m-filtration quotient. At a generic point of E, additivity of composition length gives that the sum of the quotient lengths is exactly length(O_E,eta_Z). The residue field at this point is the same in O_E and its m-quotient. Lower-dimensional graded supports have lower polynomial degree and contribute zero. This also proves that a fixed extra line-bundle twist changes no degree-p coefficient.

The first p slots are additive under tensor products and symmetric: substitute variables in the degree-bound polynomial, exactly as in 0BEL Lemma33.45.5. The last effective-divisor slot is additive as well. Indeed for effective E1,E2 the exact sequence

    0 -> O_E1(-E2) -> O_(E1+E2) -> O_E2 -> 0

and invariance of the leading coefficient under a fixed twist give

    Q(F1,...,Fp,E1+E2)=Q(F1,...,Fp,E1)+Q(F1,...,Fp,E2).          (2.2)

The Cartier order-unit result of G331 gives every integral exceptional F as a difference of effective exceptional Cartier divisors: if E_I is the model's primary exceptional divisor, F+N E_I and N E_I are effective for large N. Equation(2.2) extends Q additively to the entire last integral Cartier slot, independently of this decomposition. Extend to rational and REAL Cartier divisors by multilinearity in a finite presentation.

Crucial support warning: Q is NOT defined as a degree of a class in global CH0(Y). For R a DVR, Y=Spec R and E=(t), its local degree is one while the principal divisor is zero in CH0(Y). The definition here works on the proper supported divisor/thickening and therefore retains that local degree.

## 3. Full symmetry from an actual supported Koszul complex

It remains to exchange the last slot with a line-bundle slot. This is a local-support calculation, not an appeal to global rational equivalence on Y.

Let E,F be effective exceptional Cartier divisors and L an arbitrary line bundle on Y. The two-term complexes

    K_E=[O(-E) -> O],       K_F=[O(-F) -> O]

use the actual Cartier sections. Their tensor products are the same total Koszul complex up to the standard sign interchange. K_E is a locally free resolution of O_E, and K_F is a locally free resolution of O_F. All cohomology of the total tensor complex is supported on E intersect F, proper over the Artinian base, hence has finite R-length cohomology. Computing its Euler characteristic in the two orders yields the exact identity

    chi_R(E,L|E)-chi_R(E,L(-F)|E)
      = chi_R(F,L|F)-chi_R(F,L(-E)|F).                         (3.1)

This equality also follows from the hypercohomology spectral sequence of either bounded two-term complex on E or F; Euler additivity is valid because every term there has finite-length cohomology. The calculation does not require F to cut a regular divisor on E, or E on F; their intersection can be improper and nonreduced.

Set L=O(F)^t O(E)^s tensor product_j O(G_j)^r_j, with n-2 further integral exceptional G_j. Evaluate at t=s=0 and take the coefficient r1...r_(n-2). On the left the finite difference in the F variable lowers polynomial degree by one. Since the original total degree is at most n-1, the only possible contributing leading monomial is t*r1...r_(n-2). On the right it is s*r1...r_(n-2). Therefore

    Q_Y(F,G1,...,G_(n-2),E)=Q_Y(E,G1,...,G_(n-2),F).           (3.2)

Extend by effective differences and REAL multilinearity. Combined with symmetry in the first slots, (3.2) proves that Q is fully symmetric in all n exceptional Cartier slots. For n=1 no swap is needed. The entire argument remains inside supported Euler characteristics, preserving the DVR example.

## 5. Raw primary ideals have exactly their mixed multiplicities

    0 -> O_Y(qD_I) -> O_Y((q-1)D_I)

therefore gives

    length_R(I^(q-1)/I^q)=chi_R(E_I,O_EI((q-1)D_I)).           (5.2)

    Q(D_I[n-1],E_I)=e_R(I),       -Q(D_I[n])=e_R(I).           (5.3)

## 11. Bounded open-set locality by two compact-cover limits

Let u,v be bounded D functions with u=v on an open U in X, and fix all other bounded D inputs. Choose actual decreasing C representations u_alpha->u and v_beta->v. Clamp them from below by their common bound -M using finite maxima, so all terms are bounded. Constants and maxima preserve C by qualified G331.

Fix a continuous test h with compact support K contained in U, and epsilon>0. For a fixed alpha, every x in K satisfies

    v(x)-epsilon=u(x)-epsilon < u_alpha(x).

The continuous functions v_beta decrease to v. The directed open sets

    {x: v_beta(x)-epsilon < u_alpha(x)}

cover K. Compactness supplies one beta0 and an open V containing K on which the strict inequality holds for every beta>=beta0. This V is FIXED for this alpha and epsilon. Continuous strict maximum locality with the other D slots gives, for every such beta,

    integral h d mu(max(u_alpha,v_beta-epsilon),others)
          =integral h d mu(u_alpha,others).                   (11.1)

As beta increases, the left selected potential decreases to max(u_alpha,v-epsilon). This limit lies in D, either by finite-max stability or the actual representation just used. Bounded decreasing-net continuity passes(11.1) to that limit. Then alpha increases; max(u_alpha,v-epsilon) decreases to max(u,v-epsilon), itself in D. The same continuity gives

    integral h d mu(max(u,v-epsilon),others)
          =integral h d mu(u,others).                        (11.2)

These are two successive justified limits, not an assumed flattening of nested D representations. Interchanging u and v proves

    integral h d mu(max(v,u-epsilon),others)
          =integral h d mu(v,others).                        (11.3)

Both maximum functions converge UNIFORMLY to max(u,v), with error at most epsilon. Section10 applies entirely inside the bounded D domain. Let epsilon tend to zero in(11.2),(11.3). The common limiting integral proves

    mu(u,others)|U=mu(v,others)|U.                            (11.4)

Successively replace each slot to obtain full mixed locality whenever the corresponding tuples agree on U. This is ordinary open-set locality on the entire compact normalized valuative boundary. No stronger plurifine topology is asserted without definition and proof. In dimension one, p=0 locality is automatic; dimension zero is empty.
