# n3. Bounded real and actual decreasing-net moment measures

Use n1 and the proved algebraic kernel n2. This note constructs finite positive Radon MOMENT measures $\nu_{\mathfrak a}(u_1,\ldots,u_p)$ on the entire boundary for every tuple in $(D^{\rm bd}_{\mathfrak a})^p$. The name deliberately does not assert the geometric intersection/locality properties still missing in n7. The proof covers arbitrary real continuous and bounded completed functions, not just rational profiles.

For a rational tuple from n2 its positive functional on the rational test lattice has mass
$$B_{\boldsymbol u}(1)=B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(\mathfrak a))=\frac{e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_p,\mathfrak a)}{\prod k_i}.$$
If $q>\|f\|_\infty$ is rational, $q\pm f$ are nonnegative lattice tests. Positivity gives $|B(f)|\le qB(1)$, hence $|B(f)|\le\|f\|_\infty B(1)$. Uniform density from n1 extends this rational functional uniquely to a bounded real-linear functional on all real continuous functions: real linearity follows by rational approximation of scalars and norm continuity. Positivity persists by approximating a nonnegative continuous function and adding a vanishing positive rational constant to the approximation. The compact-Hausdorff Riesz correspondence identifies it with a finite positive regular Borel (Radon) measure. Equivalently, in the convention explicitly used in BFJ Section 4.2, a Radon measure is its positive continuous test functional. The Riesz correspondence is ordinary functional background at exactly compact Hausdorff hypotheses, not an extra geometric positivity theorem.

There is a uniform bound on gauge moments. If $-M\le u_i\le0$ for every rational entry, order comparison with the constant $-M$ (first take rational constants just above $M$) gives
$$0\le B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))\le M^p e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J),\qquad \nu_{\mathfrak a}(\boldsymbol u)(X_{\mathfrak a})\le M^p e_R(\mathfrak a).$$
Here brackets mean repeated ENTRIES, not ideal exponentiation; $e_R(\mathfrak a)=e_R(\mathfrak a^{[n]})$. For $p=0$ the factor is one. No order of measures, as opposed to order on ideal-gauge moments, is asserted.

The decisive error estimate uses legitimate negative constants in $C$. For rational tuples $\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol w$ in $[-M,0]$ with coordinatewise sup-norm difference at most $\varepsilon$, use $u_i-\varepsilon\le w_i$ and the reverse inequalities, with a rational slightly larger error if necessary. Expanding the additive kernel after adding $-\varepsilon=-\varepsilon v(\mathfrak a)$ in each slot gives
$$\left|B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))-B_{\boldsymbol w}(v(J))\right|\le [(M+\varepsilon)^p-M^p]e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J).\tag{1}$$
Indeed each nonempty subset of shifted slots contributes a nonnegative term bounded by $\varepsilon^{|S|}M^{p-|S|}e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J)$; summing them is the binomial expression. For $p=0$ the expression is zero.

Now take arbitrary REAL $C$ inputs and approximate each uniformly by rational profiles using n1. The approximants may lie in $[-M-1,0]$, which supplies a common bound. Estimate (1) makes every ideal-test moment Cauchy and independent of approximation. Every rational lattice test is a difference of such gauges, so its functional values converge. Positivity and the mass bound pass to the limit, hence give a measure by the preceding extension argument. Finite uniform density then proves weak convergence on every continuous test. The same estimate with arbitrarily slightly larger bounds/errors passes to the real $C$ inputs. Symmetry, nonnegative real homogeneity, additivity within the negative cone, and order on ideal-gauge moments pass to the limit. For additivity use rational approximations of each summand and their sum, and for real homogeneity approximate the scalar rationally. These are properties of the proved moment functional; the proof does not cite BFJ 4.4 outside its smooth range.

For each $u_i\in D^{\rm bd}_{\mathfrak a}$ choose an ACTUAL decreasing $C$-net $g_{i,\alpha_i}\downarrow u_i$. By n1 all its terms have the bound $[-M,0]$ if the tuple has that bound. The finite Cartesian product of the directed index sets represents the mixed tuple. For every ideal test $v(J)$, its moments increase under refinement and stay bounded by the preceding estimate. Define $B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))$ as their finite supremum. This is an increasing limit of bounded SCALARS, not an increasing limit in the function class. Values for difference tests converge by subtraction. Positive test functionals and their common mass bound pass to the limit on the rational lattice and extend to a finite positive measure as before.

We prove representation independence without a flattening assumption. Let $g_{i,\alpha_i}$ and $f_{i,\beta_i}$ represent the same bounded tuple. Fix a tuple of indices $\boldsymbol\beta$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Since $u_i\le f_{i,\beta_i}$, the directed open sets
$$\{g_{i,\alpha_i}<f_{i,\beta_i}+\varepsilon\}$$
cover the compact space. A finite subcover and directedness select one $\alpha_i$ for which the inequality holds everywhere. Do this for each of the finitely many slots. Then $g_{i,\alpha_i}-\varepsilon\le f_{i,\beta_i}$. Moment order and the real $C$ negative-shift expansion give
$$B_{\boldsymbol f_{\beta}}(v(J))\le B_{\boldsymbol g_{\alpha}-\varepsilon}(v(J))\le \sup_{\boldsymbol\alpha}B_{\boldsymbol g_{\alpha}}(v(J))+[(M+\varepsilon)^p-M^p]e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J).$$
Take the supremum over $\boldsymbol\beta$, send $\varepsilon$ to zero, and reverse the roles. Equality of every gauge moment, density, and the mass bound give equality of the measures. The same argument, now with limit tuples satisfying $u_i\le w_i$ and fixed continuous terms majorizing $w_i$, proves order on ideal-gauge moments throughout the bounded $D$ domain.

Addition of two actual decreasing $C$-nets, indexed by their finite product, gives an actual decreasing $C$-net to their sum. Thus the sum belongs to $D$ by its definition, with a finite bound. Taking scalar moment limits in the $C$ additivity identity proves additivity on bounded $D$ entries. Scaling is handled the same way; scalar zero produces zero. Symmetry is immediate. In particular the negative-constant shift expansion and (1) are now valid on bounded $D$ inputs. No closure under arbitrary $D$ infima or arbitrary nested-net flattening was invoked.

The useful exact formula, for every ideal $J$, is
$$B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))=\sup\{B_{\boldsymbol g}(v(J)):g_i\in C,\ g_i\ge u_i\text{ for all }i\}.\tag{2}$$
Every such $g_i$ lies in the common bound $[-M,0]$. The order just proved gives the upper bound; the terms of any representing nets give the reverse bound. This is a formula for scalar moments, not a new definition of $D$ by all majorants.

Here is the precise descending-net theorem. Let $\lambda$ range over one directed set, let $u_{i,\lambda}\in D^{\rm bd}_{\mathfrak a}$ decrease pointwise to a SPECIFIED $u_i\in D^{\rm bd}_{\mathfrak a}$, and suppose all entries and limits have a common finite bound $[-M,0]$. Then
$$\nu_{\mathfrak a}(u_{1,\lambda},\ldots,u_{p,\lambda})\ \longrightarrow\ \nu_{\mathfrak a}(u_1,\ldots,u_p)$$
weakly, for the net. The membership of the limit is an actual hypothesis; no new infimum closure is being claimed. To prove it, order gives a bound above by the target gauge moment. Fix a continuous majorant tuple $g_i\ge u_i$. Every $D$ function is upper semicontinuous, so $\{u_{i,\lambda}<g_i+\varepsilon\}$ is open. The decreasing net gives a directed cover; compactness selects one common $\lambda$ for all slots with $u_{i,\lambda}-\varepsilon\le g_i$. Thus
$$B_{\boldsymbol u_\lambda}(v(J))\ge B_{\boldsymbol g}(v(J))-[(M+\varepsilon)^p-M^p]e_R(\mathfrak a^{[p]},J).$$
First send $\varepsilon$ to zero, then take the supremum in (2). The gauge moments converge to the target moments. They determine convergence for the rational difference lattice; density and the uniform mass bound extend it to every continuous test. This proves the actual weak descending-net theorem for this moment construction. It is not convergence in total variation, and it is not locality.

All-zero inputs yield zero for $p\ge1$, and any single globally zero entry annihilates the mixed measure. For mixed-zero patterns on normalization branches, the whole tuple is still in the stated domain if it belongs to G331's class; this construction makes no strict-negativity exclusion. It is not extended here to unbounded or bottom inputs.
