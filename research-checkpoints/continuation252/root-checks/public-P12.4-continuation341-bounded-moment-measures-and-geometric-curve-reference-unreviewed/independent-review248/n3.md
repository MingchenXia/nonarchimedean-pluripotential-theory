## Real C and actual bounded D extension (author n3)

For a rational tuple positivity on L_Q and rational constants q>||f|| imply |B(f)|<=qB(1). Let q decrease to ||f||. Density gives a unique bounded extension to all real continuous tests; rational approximation of scalars proves real linearity. Approximate a nonnegative test by lattice tests and add vanishing positive rational errors to retain nonnegativity, proving positivity of the extension. The compact-Hausdorff Riesz theorem gives a finite positive Radon measure. This functional foundation grants no geometric intersection or locality.

Comparison with the legitimate constant -M=-M v(a), first using rational M' decreasing to M, gives

    0<=B_u(v(J))<=M^p E_J,   mass<=M^p e_R(a),
    E_J=e_R(a repeated p times,J).

This is scalar ideal-moment order, not measure order. For p=0 the factor is one, including M=0, and there are no input variables.

If rational tuples u,w in [-M,0] have slotwise uniform error <=epsilon, compare u_i-epsilon<=w_i and conversely. Expanding the simultaneous negative shift yields

    |B_u(v(J))-B_w(v(J))| <= [(M+epsilon)^p-M^p] E_J.          (R3.1)

For each nonempty subset S of shifted slots its nonnegative term is bounded by epsilon^{|S|} M^{p-|S|} E_J. Sum them for the displayed binomial bound. Rational errors just above the actual error suffice initially; p=0 gives zero error.

For real C inputs choose rational profiles uniformly converging slotwise. They eventually lie in [-M-1,0]. R3.1 makes gauge moments Cauchy and approximation independent. Difference-test values converge; positivity and bounded mass pass to the limit, so the same extension/Riesz argument gives a measure. Dense tests and bounded masses prove weak convergence: a uniform test error eta contributes at most eta times the mass on each side. R3.1 passes to real tuples with slightly enlarged bounds/errors. Approximate summands and their sums to prove additivity, and scalars rationally to prove real nonnegative homogeneity. Order passes by comparing approximants after small negative shifts. Symmetry passes directly. This proves all-real coverage without a finite grid.

For u_i in D^bd choose ACTUAL decreasing C-nets g_{i,alpha_i} to u_i and their finite product directed set. Every term is in [-M,0]. Gauge moments increase under refinement because potentials decrease, and are bounded by M^p E_J. Define their moment as their finite supremum. Difference-test values converge by subtraction. The limit positive bounded lattice functional again yields a finite measure. Increasing scalar limits do not invoke increasing function-class closure.

For representation independence let f,g represent the same tuple. Fix f_{i,beta_i} and epsilon>0. The open sets {g_{i,alpha}<f_{i,beta_i}+epsilon} cover X_a and increase with alpha, because inf g=u_i<=f_{i,beta_i}. Compactness and finite subcovers give one alpha_i valid everywhere and product directedness handles finitely many slots. Thus g_alpha-epsilon<=f_beta, and

    B_fbeta(v(J)) <= sup_alpha B_galpha(v(J))
                        +[(M+epsilon)^p-M^p] E_J.

Take sup over beta, epsilon down to zero, then reverse the roles. Gauge moments and hence measures agree. For limits u<=w, use a representation of u and fixed continuous terms f_beta>=w>=u in exactly this argument. It gives B_w(v(J))<=B_u(v(J)) on bounded D.

Addition preserves the actual domain: sums of two representing C-nets, indexed by their finite product, form an actual decreasing C-net to the bounded sum. Directedness approximates both infima at a point. Passing to scalar limits in C-additivity gives D-additivity; the sum of bounded monotone scalar limits is the limit of the sums. Scaling is identical, with scalar zero giving the zero C-function. Symmetry is retained. Negative-shift expansion and R3.1 now hold on bounded D. No nested-net flattening was used.

One has the exact scalar majorant formula

    B_u(v(J))=sup{B_g(v(J)): g_i in C, g_i>=u_i for every i}.  (R3.2)

All these majorants lie in [-M,0]. Order gives one bound and terms of a chosen representation give the other. This is a scalar formula, not a declaration of D-membership for arbitrary functions.

For net continuity assume u_{i,lambda} in D^bd decrease to a SPECIFIED u_i in D^bd, with common bound [-M,0]. The limit domain is a hypothesis, not a closure theorem. Order gives B_{u_lambda}(v(J))<=B_u(v(J)). Fix C-majorants g_i>=u_i and epsilon>0. D functions are upper semicontinuous, since any infimum of continuous functions has open strict sublevel sets. The open sets {u_{i,lambda}<g_i+epsilon} cover X_a and increase under refinement. Compactness and finite slots give one common lambda and every later lambda with u_{i,lambda}-epsilon<=g_i. Hence

    B_{u_lambda}(v(J))>=B_g(v(J))-[(M+epsilon)^p-M^p] E_J.

Take liminf over lambda, then epsilon down to zero, then the supremum in R3.2. Every gauge moment converges to the target. Dense tests and the mass bound give weak convergence of the NET. This is neither total-variation convergence nor locality, and asserts no arbitrary D infimum/increasing closure. A common bound is automatic for finitely many slots once finite bounded limits are stipulated, but its explicit inclusion is harmless.
