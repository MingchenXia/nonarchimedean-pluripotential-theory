## Complete excellent reduced-curve reference theorem (author n5)

Let R be ANY excellent reduced local curve. Its finite normalization S has local DVR centres S_q. Put f_q=[kappa(q):kappa(m)] and a_q=ord_q(a)>0. A centred semivaluation on a DVR has support zero (maximal support would give infinite primary value), vanishes on units, and is a positive real multiple of its order. G331's surjective normalization boundary presentation therefore gives all points v_q=ord_q/a_q. A regular conductor element is nonzero on every component and has finite DVR order, so all fibres are singletons. The compact quotient of the finite branch set is the same finite discrete set. These are local centres, not merely minimal components.

Define geometrically, BEFORE ideal-moment comparison,

    mu_{a,R}=sum_q f_q a_q delta_{ord_q/a_q}.                  (R5.1)

For EVERY actual primary model Y=Bl_I R, IS is invertible on the semilocal normal curve, since all localizations are nonzero principal DVR ideals. The universal property gives h:Spec S -> Y. Its graph is a closed immersion into Spec S times_R Y, since Y is separated. Projection to Y is finite by base change of finite normalization, so h is finite. G331's primary blowup construction is reduced, all components dominate original components and their generic fields are unchanged. Thus the finite closed image contains every component's generic point and is all Y. This proves surjectivity onto every actual primary model, with no higher-dimensional arbitrary-model cofinality.

Locally this finite map is normal and birational with the same total quotient fields, so it is normalization. Indeed any element integral over the target is integral over this normal source and hence belongs to it; conversely the source is finite integral. Finite surjectivity preserves dimension, and the finite normalization's closed fibre is finite; hence Y's closed fibre is finite. Its closed-fibre local rings T=O_{Y,y} are reduced one-dimensional local rings, with residue fields finite over kappa(m).

For an effective integral exceptional Cartier divisor A with regular local equation g_y define its ordinary proper ZERO-CYCLE degree

    deg_R[A]=sum_y length_{O_{Y,y}}(O_{Y,y}/g_y)[kappa(y):kappa(m)].

The support is finite on that closed fibre. Its local quotient has finite length, because g_y is regular, nonzero in every component field. This is an actual Cartier zero-cycle definition, not a degree defined by moments or nonnormal Weil coefficients.

At y let N be finite semilocal normalization of T and C_0=N/T. This finite module vanishes at all generic points of the reduced curve, so has finite length. Multiplication by g is injective on T,N. The normalization snake sequence is

    0 -> C_0[g] -> T/gT -> N/gN -> C_0/gC_0 -> 0.

Kernel and cokernel of multiplication by g on the SAME finite-length C_0 have equal length. They cancel, giving length_T(T/gT)=length_T(N/gN). The Artinian quotient N/gN splits at its DVR centres q over y, each residue factor having T-length [kappa(q):kappa(y)]. Thus

    length_T(T/gT)=sum_{q -> y}[kappa(q):kappa(y)]ord_q(g).

Multiply by [kappa(y):kappa(m)] and sum. Multiplicativity of field degrees includes inseparable extensions. Consequently

    deg_R[A]=sum_q f_q ord_q(h^*A).                           (R5.2)

No normality or Cohen--Macaulay assumption on Y is used.

G331's actual Cartier order unit writes every exceptional integral F as (F+NE_I)-NE_I, a difference of effective exceptional Cartier divisors. Choice independence follows from additivity: for regular g,h,

    0 -> T/hT --multiply by g--> T/(gh)T -> T/gT -> 0

gives additivity of effective lengths; R5.2 also gives it directly. Thus the degree is a homomorphism on the actual Cartier group, extended by differences and then by tensoring with REAL numbers. Every finite real presentation and its relations are handled; no rationality restriction remains.

G331 evaluation at the branch is f_F(v_q)=ord_q(h^*F)/a_q, including its sign for a negative divisor. Therefore

    integral f_F dmu_{a,R}=sum_q f_q ord_q(h^*F)=deg_R(F).      (R5.3)

For any common primary-model pullback, the map from Spec S to the common model exists by the same proof and composes with maps to the two models. These morphisms are unique: they agree on the dense generic locus, targets are separated and sources reduced. Pullback to S is unchanged, so R5.2 proves actual degrees unchanged on all such models. This is genuine real Cartier comparison/model independence within the stated curve model system.

Only now compare moments. One-variable associativity/additivity, or R4.2 with n=1, gives e_R(J)=sum_q f_q e_{S_q}(JS_q). In a DVR an ideal of order j_q has length(S_q/J^N)=N j_q. For another primary gauge b, branch order b_q sends v_q to ord_q/b_q and multiplies its mass by b_q/a_q. This is exactly R4.1 with one factor. Normalization is R4.2 with f_q. A DVR over its own residue field has mass a_q; its maximal-ideal reference mass is one. Dimension-zero reduction has empty boundary and zero measure, no negative arity.

For the residue-degree example let ANY finite extension k subset l have degree d>1, possibly inseparable. Set R=k+t l[[t]], S=l[[t]], A=k[[t]], and choose k-basis 1,alpha_2,...,alpha_d of l. Then

    R=A direct-sum (sum_{i=2}^d tA alpha_i).

It is a finite A-algebra, hence Noetherian and excellent; A is an excellent DVR and finite algebras preserve these properties. R is a domain of dimension one by integral finiteness over A. A nonzero k-constant gives an inverse still in R; zero-constant elements form maximal ideal tS, and residue field is k. S is finite over A and hence over R. For EVERY s in S, ts belongs to R, so s=(ts)/t is in Frac R; this proves Frac R=l((t)), more than simply showing constants l lie in the field. S is its finite normal DVR normalization: generic degree ONE, residue degree d.

The conductor contains tS. If a conductor element had nonzero constant c in k, multiplying by every alpha in l would force c alpha in k, hence l=k, contradiction. So conductor=tS. For J=t^rS, r>=1, J is an m-primary R-ideal and J^N=t^{rN}S. A k-basis of R/J^N is 1 and alpha_j t^i, 1<=i<rN, 1<=j<=d. Length equals k-dimension since every simple local factor is k. Thus

    length_R(R/J^N)=1+d(rN-1),    e_R(J)=dr.

The reference for a=tS is d delta_ord_t, with J-moment dr. On Spec S, the actual Cartier divisor t^r has R-zero-cycle degree dr. This is on an actual allowed primary model: Bl_J R=Spec S. Its t^r-chart is R[J/t^r]=S; every degree-one gT has square divisible by t^rT, because g^2/t^r belongs to J, so that chart covers Proj. No step uses separability or replaces actual residue degree with generic degree.
