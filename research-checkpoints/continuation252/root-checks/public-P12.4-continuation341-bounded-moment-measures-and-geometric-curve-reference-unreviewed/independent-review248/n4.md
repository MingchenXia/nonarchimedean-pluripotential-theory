## Gauges, normalization and smooth BFJ (author n4)

For primary gauges a,b put d(v)=v(b) on X_a and T(v)=v/d(v). G331 gives bounded positive d,1/d and transports homogeneous inputs by u_b(Tv)=u_a(v)/d(v), preserving C, actual D representations and finite bounds. For rational profiles the ideals and denominators k_i remain the same. Pairing T_*[d nu_a] against v_b(J) gives integral d(v)v(J)/d(v) dnu_a=B_u(v(J)), the required b-gauge moment. Density identifies measures. Transported uniform approximations and actual decreasing representations, with bounded continuous test d(F composed with T), prove for all bounded D:

    nu_b(u_b)=T_*[d nu_a(u_a)].                               (R4.1)

There is exactly ONE factor d. For b=a^r it is r and the potentials divide by r. For a third gauge c the second denominator at Tv is v(c)/v(b); its product with d is v(c), proving the cocycle. No nonprimary denominator appears.

Let S be finite normalization, with local normal centres S_q and f_q=[kappa(q):kappa(m)]. Over a minimal prime P of reduced R, both R_P and S tensor_R R_P are the same component field, so their module lengths are one. SH 17.4.8, or 17.4.4 with the smaller-dimensional conductor cokernel, gives e_R(I_1,...,I_n;R)=e_R(I_1,...,I_n;S). For A=product I_i^{t_i}, S/AS is finite Artinian and splits into local factors. Each factor of an S_q composition series has R-length f_q. Thus

    length_R(S/AS)=sum_q f_q length_{S_q}(S_q/AS_q).

Degree-n coefficients give

    e_R(I_1,...,I_n)=sum_{dim S_q=n} f_q e_{S_q}(I_1S_q,...,I_nS_q).

Moments/density and then uniform/actual descending approximation yield

    nu_{a,R}(u)=sum_{dim S_q=n} f_q rho_{q,*}nu_{aS_q,S_q}(rho_q^*u). (R4.2)

The sum is finite. Smaller-dimensional centres remain in the boundary but have no degree-n term. The f_q are actual vector-space residue degrees, including inseparable ones. Normalization has generic identity on component fields, which is different from residue degree. No vertical generic degree is invented. On the conductor-infinite stratum every lift remains, and pushforward adds masses at identified points. The identity concerns actual pullbacks of downstairs inputs; it proves neither arbitrary upstairs operator descent nor a general finite-map theorem.

For BFJ assume ONLY a smooth affine CLOSED germ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic ZERO, n>=2, initially a=m. G331 gives its full all-semivaluation C/D identification, with bottom separately adjoined and excluded here. BFJ 4.1-3 supply independent Cartier intersections and the nef Weil extension; (4.3) uses mixed multiplicity as the length of a generic parameter quotient.

This convention equals the polynomial coefficient in the regular local ring. Over its infinite residue field choose generic superficial parameters in the given ideals, using successive finite-open choices. They form a parameter sequence and are nonzerodivisors successively because the local ring is Cohen--Macaulay. Repeated SH 17.4.6 removes entries down to the one-dimensional QUOTIENT, where the annihilator is zero. The coefficient is the length of the final parameter quotient. These choices occur in the generic parameter range, yielding BFJ's generic-length convention. Do not literally use the unquotiented colon-module length printed in SH 17.4.7; the correctly stated quotient correction and regular-case argument are given in the source ledger. This is the author's repeated-17.4.6 route, not a replacement theorem.

BFJ 4.7 now gives the rational ideal moments, and dense tests identify measures. For real C inputs, BFJ intersection order/additivity give R3.1, so uniform approximation proves equality. That additivity is a smooth result: 4.4 and Remark 4.5 use 3.13 multiplier approximation essentially. None is transferred to singular or positive-characteristic germs.

For bounded D inputs use their actual decreasing C representations and the finite product net. Weil coefficients decrease to those of the limit at every divisorial point, hence converge in the coefficient topology. BFJ 4.4 is upper semicontinuous and increasing in its divisor entries. Thus for its intersection F, monotonicity gives F(Z_lambda)>=F(Z), while upper semicontinuity gives limsup F(Z_lambda)<=F(Z); these imply convergence for NETS. Bounded inputs and a fixed Cartier test give finite intersection values by 4.6's bound. This conclusion uses the entire 4.4 proof, whose finite-coordinate open-neighbourhood argument is topological, not sequence-only 4.8. Taking the fixed ideal test Z(J), the negative intersections are gauge moments. They agree with R3's limits, and density identifies the measures on ALL semivaluations, rather than only their divisorial restriction. R4.1 transports to other primary gauges without attributing an unstated gauge theorem to BFJ.

BFJ 4.9's atom formula is confined to this smooth model system. It is no singular locality or arbitrary-cofinality grant. No actual open-set locality theorem is derived even in the smooth comparison.
