## All-field mixed kernel (author n2)

SH 17.4.2 gives the multigraded length polynomial for primary ideals and finite modules over Noetherian local rings, of total degree dim M. Definition 17.4.3 uses degree dim R coefficients with factorial denominators, so smaller-dimensional modules have zero coefficients. Write e_R(I_1,...,I_n) for the coefficient of t_1...t_n. Unit slots are separately extended to zero because the length expression is independent of their variable; the theorem's proper-primary hypothesis is not enlarged.

SH 8.4.1-2 allows the algebraic extension R(Z)=R[Z] localized at mR[Z]. It is faithfully flat, has equal dimension and infinite residue field, and preserves primary ideals. Tensoring a finite composition series gives residue-field factors k(Z), so lengths and all mixed polynomials agree. No boundary or geometric equivalence across this extension is asserted.

Introduce separate variables for I,J and then identify them to represent (IJ)^t. A term with shared exponent one has original exponents (1,0) or (0,1). Hence

    e(IJ,I_2,...,I_n)=e(I,I_2,...,I_n)+e(J,I_2,...,I_n).

Replacing t by rt proves power linearity; relabeling proves symmetry. The unit convention is compatible.

For J subset K in one slot, fix the other n-1 primary ideals. When n>1 choose x in I_2 superficial for BOTH tuples (I_2,I_3,...,I_n,J) and (I_2,I_3,...,I_n,K), avoiding every minimal prime. SH 17.2.2's proof makes both allowed sets complements of finitely many proper subspaces of I_2/mI_2. A primary ideal is not contained in a minimal prime of a positive-dimensional local ring: m minimal would force dimension zero. Thus avoidance adds only finitely many proper subspaces. Over an infinite field their combined complement has a rational point. This is the SAME element for both reductions.

The quotient has dimension n-1. Avoiding minimal primes gives the upper bound; lifting a parameter system of the quotient and adjoining x gives an m-primary ideal, and the height theorem gives the lower bound. Primary ideals remain proper primary in this quotient. SH 17.2.4 applies: its radical hypothesis holds for primary ideals and Krull intersection for finite local modules. SH 17.4.6 removes the common fixed entry. Repeat with simultaneous choices in successive quotients, which can be nonreduced and non-Cohen--Macaulay. At dimension one,

    J^N subset K^N => length(R/J^N)>=length(R/K^N) => e(J)>=e(K).

Thus single-slot inclusion gives decreasing multiplicity. The same reductions leave a positive one-dimensional primary multiplicity (SH 11.1.3-5), proving positivity of primary tuples. Unit fixed slots give zero on both sides; K unit is handled by positivity. No coefficientwise inequality of arbitrary multivariate polynomials is used.

For integral-closure invariance suppose K subset bar(J), and put L=J+K. Homogeneous ideal-integrality equations make R[Lt] finite graded over R[Jt]. Generators of degrees at most h yield L^{t+h} subset J^t for t>=1, while J^t subset L^t. After multiplying by A=product_{i>=2} I_i^{t_i},

    length(R/L^t A)<=length(R/J^t A)<=length(R/L^{t+h} A).

On every positive integer ray divide by its common parameter to power n. The fixed shift h does not change the leading homogeneous part; squeeze gives equality of the degree-n parts for J,L on positive rational rays and hence as polynomials. Equal closures of J,K allow comparison of each with J+K. Unit cases are trivial. No finite normalization of a Rees algebra is needed.

For primary-or-unit A,B the exact all-support criterion is

    v(A)>=v(B) on ALL X_a  iff  A subset bar(B).

For proper B apply complete SH 6.8.4 on each positive-dimensional local domain R/P, P minimal. Its centred rank-one DVRs normalize by their finite positive a-value to genuine boundary points with support P. The inequality puts each element of A in the closure of the image of B. SH 1.1.5/6.8.3 assembles the equations over all minimal primes. Conversely quotient by ANY semivaluation support and use the ideal-integrality equation: if v(f)<v(B), its leading term is uniquely smallest, impossible. Infinite f-values cause no difficulty because v(B) is finite. If B=R both assertions hold; if A=R and B proper both fail. Using minimal-support DVRs for the criterion does not remove other supports.

For u_i=-v(I_i)/k_i define

    B_u(v(J))=e_R(I_1,...,I_p,J)/(product k_i).

For p=0 this is e_R(J); a unit potential gives zero for p>0. Equal input presentations, after powers, give equal values and equal integral closures, hence equal scaled multiplicities. Every rational test relation converts to v(A)=v(B) by common denominators and products, so the functional is well defined. If (v(A)-v(B))/k>=0, A subset bar(B), bar(A+B)=bar(B), and monotonicity/invariance give e(...,A)>=e(...,B). Therefore it is positive on the full rational test lattice.

The scalar order direction is u<=w => B_u(v(J))>=B_w(v(J)). Indeed I^l subset bar(H^k) for u=-v(I)/k, w=-v(H)/l gives l e(...,I)>=k e(...,H), and division by kl proves it. Addition of negative rational profiles corresponds to product ideals. Symmetry, additivity and rational nonnegative homogeneity follow.
