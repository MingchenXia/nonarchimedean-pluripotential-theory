# n5. Actual geometric curve reference measure and dimension-zero convention

Use qualified G331 and n1--n4. Here there IS an actual independent Cartier intersection comparison and locality theorem, in the exact range of every excellent reduced local CURVE, with arbitrary characteristic and residue field. Arity is zero, so this is a reference measure and not a positive-arity higher-dimensional product.

Let $S$ be the finite normalization, with local DVR centres $S_q$, residue degrees $f_q=[\kappa(q):\kappa(\mathfrak m)]$, and orders $a_q=\operatorname{ord}_q(\mathfrak a)>0$. Every centred semivaluation of a DVR is a positive multiple of its order: write a nonzero element as a power of a uniformizer times a unit, whose value is zero. A support cannot be its maximal ideal, because that would make the boundary ideal infinite. There is no other nonzero prime support in a DVR. Thus G331's normalization presentation identifies the entire downstairs boundary with the finite discrete set
$$v_q=\operatorname{ord}_q/a_q.$$
A regular conductor element has finite value on every branch, so ALL fibres in this curve case are singletons. These are local centres of normalization, not merely minimal components.

Define the reference measure directly from Cartier order on normalized curves:
$$\mu_{\mathfrak a,R}=\sum_q f_q a_q\,\delta_{v_q}.\tag{5}$$
This definition uses actual residue field degrees; there is no generic degree assigned to a vertical component. Every coefficient is positive and finite. The construction is geometric before any ideal-moment comparison.

We prove comparison with an arbitrary actual primary blowup model $Y=\operatorname{Bl}_I R$ and every exceptional REAL Cartier divisor $F$ on it. Since $I S$ is invertible on the semilocal normal curve, the universal property gives a morphism $h:\operatorname{Spec}S\to Y$. It is finite: its graph is closed in $\operatorname{Spec}S\times_R Y$ because $Y$ is separated over $R$, and the projection of this product to $Y$ is finite as a base change of the finite normalization. Its image contains the generic point of every component of $Y$, which is reduced and generically birational on all components by the primary blowup construction in G331. The finite image is closed, hence the map is surjective. It is the normalization of $Y$: locally it is a finite normal birational extension with the same total quotient fields. This special curve argument establishes domination of these actual primary models. It makes no arbitrary higher-dimensional proper-model cofinality assertion.

For an effective integral exceptional Cartier divisor $A$, its independent one-dimensional intersection degree is the proper zero-cycle degree
$$\deg_R[A]=\sum_{y\in\operatorname{Supp}A}\ell_{\mathcal O_{Y,y}}(\mathcal O_{Y,y}/(g_y))\,[\kappa(y):\kappa(\mathfrak m)],$$
where $g_y$ is a regular local equation. The support is a finite subset of the closed fibre: the finite normalization surjects onto it, and its fibre above $\mathfrak m$ is finite. Each relevant local ring is reduced of dimension one, and $A$ cuts a finite-length quotient. Its residue field is finite over the base residue field because it is a closed point of the proper zero-dimensional fibre. This is the usual local Cartier zero-cycle, not a degree defined from valuative moments.

Here is a direct normalization proof of that degree. Put $T=\mathcal O_{Y,y}$ and let $N$ be its finite semilocal normalization. The cokernel $C=N/T$ has finite length, since normalization is generically identical on a reduced curve. Multiplication by the regular element $g=g_y$ in $0\to T\to N\to C\to0$ gives
$$0\to C[g]\to T/gT\to N/gN\to C/gC\to0.$$
For the finite-length module $C$, its multiplication exact sequence gives $\ell(C[g])=\ell(C/gC)$. Therefore $\ell_T(T/gT)=\ell_T(N/gN)$. The DVR factors of $N$ give
$$\ell_T(N/gN)=\sum_{q\mapsto y}[\kappa(q):\kappa(y)]\operatorname{ord}_q(g).$$
Multiplying by $[\kappa(y):\kappa(\mathfrak m)]$ and summing proves
$$\deg_R[A]=\sum_q f_q\operatorname{ord}_q(h^*A).\tag{6}$$
This proof works without normality or Cohen--Macaulay assumptions on $Y$. It uses local actual Cartier equations and the finite normalization, not a replacement by nonnormal Weil coefficients.

Every integral exceptional Cartier $F$ on a primary model can be written as a difference of effective exceptional Cartier divisors by the actual Cartier order-unit assertion of G331; extend (6) by differences and then by finite real linear combinations. Relations cause no ambiguity, since each local order is a homomorphism on actual Cartier divisors. The actual intersection degree of $F$ is therefore
$$\deg_R(F)=\sum_q f_q\operatorname{ord}_q(h^*F).$$
G331's evaluation on the DVR centre is $f_F(v_q)=\operatorname{ord}_q(h^*F)/a_q$, including the negative sign of a negative divisor. Pairing the independently defined (5) gives the exact comparison
$$\int f_F\,d\mu_{\mathfrak a,R}=\deg_R(F).\tag{7}$$
The normalized degree is unchanged by any common primary-model pullback, since its pullback to $S$ is unchanged. Formula (6) also shows that the independent Cartier zero-cycle degree on each such model is that same degree. This proves actual model independence and exact Cartier comparison for every real core presentation and every Cartier test in this curve range.

For a primary ideal $J$, apply one-variable multiplicity additivity (complete Swanson--Huneke 11.2.3--4) to the finite normalization, whose generic rank is one on every reduced component. Equivalently use n4's already proved degree-one formula. The DVR Hilbert function gives $e_{S_q}(JS_q)=\operatorname{ord}_q(J)$, so
$$e_R(J)=\sum_q f_q\operatorname{ord}_q(J)=\int v(J)\,d\mu_{\mathfrak a,R}.$$
Thus (5) agrees with n3's zero-potential moment measure, but its Cartier construction and proof (7) do not rely on assigning moments and then renaming them intersections.

The bounded geometric function class on this finite set is every finite nonpositive branch vector. Indeed each DVR's continuous cone consists of nonpositive constants, obtained from powers of its parameter with real coefficients; the semilocal class is the finite product. The normalization fibres here are singletons, so G331's positivity descent gives all such vectors downstairs. Its actual decreasing completion is every extended nonpositive vector; bounded completed vectors are still finite and hence already continuous. All-zero and mixed-zero profiles are admissible as boundary functions. This says nothing about literal logarithmic radial extensions at a node.

There are no potential variables in (5). Consequently two zero-tuples give the identical measure on every open region of agreement; this is actual open-set locality, albeit a vacuous zero-arity case. Every bounded decreasing-net continuity statement for the zero-tuple is constant equality of measures. It is not advertised as positive-arity Bedford--Taylor locality. Finite continuous tests vary in the usual finite-set norm, so their integrals are continuous as well. Unbounded or bottom potential inputs are not being added: the tuple has zero entries.

For a primary $\mathfrak b$ with $b_q=\operatorname{ord}_q(\mathfrak b)$, the exact change sends $v_q$ to $\operatorname{ord}_q/b_q$ and multiplies its weight by $b_q/a_q$. Hence it is precisely (3), and normalization is (4) with weights $f_q$. On a smooth DVR over its own residue field, $f_q=1$, and (5) has mass $a_q$ at $\operatorname{ord}/a_q$; for the maximal ideal the mass is one. This is the direct dimension-one normalized-local reference convention. For a zero-dimensional reduced local germ, the centred boundary is empty and the only measure is zero, without negative arity.

A concrete residue-degree check prevents replacing $f_q$ by one. Let $k\subset l$ be any finite field extension of degree $d>1$, possibly inseparable, and
$$R=k+t\,l[[t]]\ \subset\ S=l[[t]].$$
Put $A=k[[t]]$. Choosing a $k$-basis $1,\alpha_2,\ldots,\alpha_d$ of $l$ expresses $R=A\oplus\bigoplus_{i=2}^d tA\alpha_i$, a finite $A$-algebra, so it is Noetherian and excellent. It is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=tS$ and residue field $k$: a nonzero constant in $k$ gives an inverse still having constant in $k$. Its fraction field is $l((t))$ because $t\alpha/t=\alpha$. Thus $S$ is its finite normalization with GENERIC degree one and RESIDUE degree $d$. The conductor is $tS$: it contains that ideal, and a conductor unit would force $l=k$. For $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak m$, (5) is $d\delta_{\operatorname{ord}_t}$. For $J=t^rS$, $J^N=t^{rN}S$ and a $k$-basis of $R/J^N$ consists of $1$ and all $\alpha_j t^i$, $1\le i<rN$, $1\le j\le d$. Hence
$$\ell_R(R/J^N)=1+d(rN-1),\qquad e_R(J)=dr.$$
This is exactly the moment of (5) and the Cartier divisor degree. A generic-degree-only coefficient would give $r$, which is wrong. The argument does not assume separability.
