# n6. Precise degeneracies and a locality diagnostic

Use G331 and n1--n5. These examples distinguish genuine obstructions from missing proofs. They do not assert impossibility of the entire reduced higher-dimensional P12.4 target.

First, nilpotent multiplicity cannot be reconstructed from the reduced boundary and its gauge. Let $R_1=k[[t]]$ and $R_2=k[[t]][\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$, both excellent local rings. The ideal $(t)$ is primary in each, and their reduced gauges and centred semivaluation boundaries are identical: nilpotents have infinite value. Nevertheless
$$\ell_{R_1}(R_1/(t^N))=N,\qquad \ell_{R_2}(R_2/(t^N))=2N.$$
The second quotient has basis $t^i,\epsilon t^i$ for $0\le i<N$, while the first has basis $t^i$. Their Samuel multiplicities are one and two. Thus no assignment depending ONLY on G331's reduced boundary and reduced primary gauge can agree with both unreduced intersection multiplicities. Extra generic-length data are necessary if that different target is requested. Our measure construction deliberately uses $R_{\rm red}$; this is not an obstruction in the explicitly reduced scope.

Second, the degree-$n$ convention has real lower-dimensional degeneracy. Let
$$R=k[[x,y,z]]/(xy,xz)\cong k[[y,z]]\times_k k[[x]],\qquad n=2.$$
It is a reduced excellent complete local ring. The isomorphism sends a series to its restrictions to $x=0$ and $y=z=0$, with equal constant terms; the kernel is $(xy,xz)=(x)\cap(y,z)$. Its normalization is $S=k[[y,z]]\times k[[x]]$, finite because its quotient by $R$ is one copy of $k$. It is normal on both component factors and has the same total quotient fields. Its conductor is the pair of maximal ideals, whose downstairs ideal is $\mathfrak m=(x,y,z)$. Every centred semivaluation has finite conductor value, so the normalized boundary is the disjoint union of the FULL surface boundary and the curve point, with no identified fibres. The pieces are clopen by G331.

Take $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak m$ and $I_N=(y,z,x^N)$. It is proper primary. The rational profile $u_N=-v(I_N)/N$ is constant $-1/N$ on the entire surface piece and $-1$ at the curve point. These profiles converge UNIFORMLY to the finite geometric $C$ function $u$ that is zero on the surface and $-1$ on the curve. This is uniform closure, not an assumed increasing $D$ closure. N4's top-degree normalization formula gives $e_R(I_N,\mathfrak m)=e_{k[[y,z]]}((y,z),(y,z))=1$; the one-dimensional factor has no degree-two coefficient. The last equality follows from the monomial basis count $\ell(k[[y,z]]/(y,z)^t)=t(t+1)/2$. Thus the moment measures of $u_N$ have total mass $1/N$ and their weak limit is the zero measure. By n3, $\nu_{\mathfrak m}(u)=0$ even though $u\ne0$. Formula (4) agrees: the sole top-dimensional input pullback is zero. The lower curve point was retained in the boundary and function class; it was not assigned a vertical generic degree. To seek nonzero curve-local products on that component would require the separate zero-potential arity-one-dimensional convention, not silently adding it to a one-potential dimension-two product.

Third, positivity, multilinearity and bounded continuity do not logically imply actual locality. For a diagnostic independent of any local ring, take the two-point compact discrete space $K=\{x,y\}$, the cone of all nonpositive finite functions, and the positive measure-valued map
$$F(u)=(-u(x)-u(y))\delta_x.$$
It is additive, nonnegative-real homogeneous, uniformly continuous, and continuous for bounded decreasing nets. But $u=(-1,-1)$ and $w=(-1,-2)$ agree on the open set $U=\{x\}$, while $F(u)|_U=2\delta_x$ and $F(w)|_U=3\delta_x$. This is an exact counterexample to the bare implication from those properties to locality. It is NOT claimed to satisfy the primary mixed-multiplicity kernel, to be a geometric singular product, or to disprove locality of n3's measures. It explains why a separate proof, tied to actual local intersections, is still required.

The own program exact_diagnostics.py enumerates only the finite bases and rational gauge arithmetic stated in bounded-evidence-input.json, with no supplied program imported or executed. The observed result was PASS on 48 residue-degree basis cases ($d=2,3$, $r=1,2,3$, $N=1,\ldots,8$), eight nilpotent basis cases ($N=1,\ldots,8$), and 27 two-branch gauge/test combinations, using exact fractions. It checks the residue-degree length slopes, the nilpotent basis counts, and the primary-gauge identity. This is exhaustive on those finite declared arithmetic inputs only. Neither this evidence nor the toy map is a substitute for higher-dimensional Cartier intersection comparison or locality.
