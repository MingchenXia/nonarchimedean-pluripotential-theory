# n4. Exact gauge, normalization, and published smooth comparison for moments

Use G331 and n1--n3. This note concerns the moment measures proved there. The rules are genuine identities for those measures, but do not fill the missing singular Cartier comparison or locality theorem.

For two proper PRIMARY gauges $\mathfrak a,\mathfrak b$, let $d(v)=v(\mathfrak b)$ on $X_{\mathfrak a}$ and $T(v)=v/d(v)$. The function $d$ is continuous and bounded above and away from zero. For each degree-one homogeneous input set $u_{i,\mathfrak b}(T(v))=u_{i,\mathfrak a}(v)/d(v)$. G331 proves that this transports both $C$ and actual $D$ representations. Bounds remain finite, and uniform convergence transports because $1/d$ is bounded. Then the EXACT measure change for the moment construction is
$$\nu_{\mathfrak b}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak b})=T_*\big[d\,\nu_{\mathfrak a}(\boldsymbol u_{\mathfrak a})\big].\tag{3}$$
For a rational ideal tuple and a gauge test $v_{\mathfrak b}(J)$, the right side pairs to
$$\int d(v)\frac{v(J)}{d(v)}\,d\nu_{\mathfrak a}=\frac{e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_p,J)}{\prod k_i},$$
which is the left moment. Density of the test lattice gives equality of measures. Uniform and actual descending continuity from n3 then prove (3) for all bounded inputs in $D$. Multiplication of tests by the fixed continuous bounded $d$ commutes with those weak limits. This is one factor of $d$, not $d^n$ or $d^{-(n-1)}$. For $\mathfrak b=\mathfrak a^r$, the same ray has $d=r$, the potentials on its new section divide by $r$, and the transported measure multiplies by $r$. The cocycle follows by substitution. There is no nonprimary denominator anywhere.

Let $S$ be the finite normalization of reduced excellent $R$, and let $q$ run over all its local normal centres above $\mathfrak m$. Put $S_q$ for the corresponding normal local domain, $f_q=[\kappa(q):\kappa(\mathfrak m)]$, and $\rho_q:X_{\mathfrak a S_q}\to X_{\mathfrak a}$ for restriction. Use only centres with $\dim S_q=n$ in the degree-$n$ formula:
$$\nu_{\mathfrak a,R}(\boldsymbol u)=\sum_{q:\dim S_q=n} f_q\,\rho_{q,*}\nu_{\mathfrak a S_q,S_q}(\rho_q^*\boldsymbol u).\tag{4}$$
All other centres and all semivaluation supports are retained; a centre of smaller dimension has zero top-degree contribution in this convention, rather than a fictitious degree assigned to it.

Here is the algebraic proof. Complete Swanson--Huneke 17.4.4 gives additivity of mixed multiplicity on exact sequences of finite modules, and complete 17.4.8 gives the sum over maximal-dimensional minimal primes with coefficient $\ell(M_P)$. Applied to $M=R,S$, the coefficients at every minimal $P$ of $R$ are both one: a reduced ring localized at a minimal prime is its component field, and normalization is generically the same product of fields. Hence $e_R(\boldsymbol I;R)=e_R(\boldsymbol I;S)$. Alternatively $S/R$ is annihilated by a regular conductor element and has dimension less than $n$, so additivity gives the same identity. For each product $A=I_1^{t_1}\cdots I_s^{t_s}$, the finite Artinian quotient $S/AS$ splits into its local factors. A finite-length $S_q$ composition series has $R$-length $f_q$ per factor, so
$$\ell_R(S/AS)=\sum_q f_q\ell_{S_q}(S_q/AS_q).$$
Compare degree-$n$ homogeneous coefficients using 17.4.2. Terms with $\dim S_q<n$ are zero; this gives
$$e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n)=\sum_{q:\dim S_q=n} f_q e_{S_q}(I_1S_q,\ldots,I_nS_q).$$
Formula (4) follows on rational tuples from the gauge moments and test density, and on $C$ and bounded $D$ tuples by the proved continuities. Pullback of each chosen decreasing representation is an actual bounded decreasing representation on each centre, by G331. There are finitely many centres, so summation commutes with the limits.

This proof checks actual residue degrees, including inseparable ones. It uses generic identity of normalization on reduced component fields, not an invented vertical degree and not a general finite-map trace theorem. On the conductor-infinite stratum every normalization point is still present. Formula (4) pushes all of them down, adding any masses whose fibres are identified. The downstairs inputs restrict with identical values on the entire fibre, by their actual pullback, and the formula is unaffected by descriptions of that fibre. No assertion of automatic descent of arbitrary upstairs operator data is made. General finite maps/P12.6 remain separate.

At the EXACT published BFJ range there is an additional comparison.
