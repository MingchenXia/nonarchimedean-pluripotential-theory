# n2. An unconditional primary mixed-multiplicity kernel

Use the domain and full-boundary conventions of n1. This note proves the algebraic kernel needed for moment measures, with arbitrary characteristic, residue field, non-Cohen--Macaulay rings, and non-equidimensional reduced components allowed. It proves no geometric Monge--Ampere operator.

Here are the precise published applications. In the CURRENT July 2024 author version of Swanson--Huneke, Theorem 17.4.2 says that for a Noetherian local ring, finite module $M$, and primary ideals $I_1,\ldots,I_s$, the function $\ell_R(M/I_1^{t_1}\cdots I_s^{t_s}M)$ is a rational polynomial of total degree $\dim M$ for all sufficiently large independent integer $t_i$. Definition 17.4.3 takes its degree-$\dim R$ coefficients, using factorial denominators, as mixed multiplicities; they are zero if $\dim M<\dim R$. These statements and their full immediate proof were read. We use this degree convention even when components have different dimensions.

The complete 17.2.2 proof gives a nonempty Zariski open set of choices for a superficial element in $I_1/\mathfrak m I_1$, with finitely many prescribed primes avoidable when $I_1$ is not contained in them. Complete 17.2.4 and 17.4.6 show that a superficial element $x\in I_1$ avoiding the minimal primes reduces a mixed multiplicity having at least one $I_1$ entry to the corresponding mixed multiplicity over $R/xR$ with that entry removed, when $\dim R>1$. No regular sequence or Cohen--Macaulay hypothesis is required. The finite annihilator correction in dimension one is part of the source, but we do not need that formula to compare the last ideal.

Finite residue fields cause no restriction. Pass to $R(Z)=R[Z]_{\mathfrak m R[Z]}$ solely for these algebraic proofs. Lemma 8.4.2 gives faithful flatness, equal dimension, preservation of primary ideals and lengths. Explicitly, a composition series of any finite-length $R$-module tensors to a series with factors $\kappa(\mathfrak m)(Z)$, so its length is unchanged. Thus every multigraded length polynomial and its coefficients are unchanged. No identification of boundaries across this transcendental extension is used.

Write $e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_n)$ for the coefficient with one entry of each ideal. Product and power linearity follow directly from the polynomial: replace the variables for $I$ and $J$ by the same variable to represent $(IJ)^t$. The coefficient with exponent one in that variable and one in the other $n-1$ variables receives exactly the terms with exponent one in $I$ or in $J$. Hence
$$e_R(IJ,I_2,\ldots,I_n)=e_R(I,I_2,\ldots,I_n)+e_R(J,I_2,\ldots,I_n),$$
and $e_R(I^r,I_2,\ldots,I_n)=r e_R(I,I_2,\ldots,I_n)$. Relabelling gives symmetry. If an ideal is the unit ideal, its length polynomial is independent of that variable, so the corresponding mixed multiplicity is zero. This convention is compatible with the identities.

We give the single-slot inclusion proof, rather than trying to compare individual coefficients of unequal multivariate polynomials. Suppose $J\subset K$ are primary and the other $n-1$ ideals are fixed. Over an infinite residue field, choose in the second slot an element superficial for BOTH tuples $(I_2,I_3,\ldots,I_n,J)$ and $(I_2,I_3,\ldots,I_n,K)$, and avoiding all minimal primes. The two allowed sets are nonempty opens in the same finite-dimensional vector space $I_2/\mathfrak m I_2$, obtained by removing finitely many proper subspaces in the 17.2.2 proof. Their intersection still has a rational point over an infinite field. The avoidance conditions can be included in that finite union. Each quotient has dimension one less: avoiding the minimal primes gives the upper bound, and lifting a system of parameters from the quotient and using the height theorem gives the lower bound. The quotient images of $J,K$ remain primary and included. Apply 17.4.6 to both tuples. Repeat in the remaining fixed slots, choosing simultaneous superficial elements afresh in each quotient. In dimension one, $J^N\subset K^N$ gives
$$\ell(R/J^N)\ge\ell(R/K^N),\qquad e(J)\ge e(K)$$
by dividing by $N$ and using the one-variable Hilbert--Samuel polynomial. Thus, in every dimension,
$$J\subset K\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e_R(J,I_2,\ldots,I_n)\ge e_R(K,I_2,\ldots,I_n).$$
The same reduction to a one-dimensional primary multiplicity proves positivity for primary slots. The positivity of the last one-variable leading coefficient is Theorem 11.1.3 and Definition 11.1.5, whose immediate proof/context were read. Unit slots give zero. These arguments also work for the nonreduced quotients created by superficial elements.

Integral-closure invariance has a separate proof. If $K\subset\overline J$, put $L=J+K$. Each degree-one generator $xt$ of $R[Lt]$ is integral over $R[Jt]$ by its homogeneous ideal-integrality equation. Thus $R[Lt]$ is a finite graded $R[Jt]$-module. A finite homogeneous generating list, with degrees at most $h$, gives $L^{t+h}\subset J^t$ for all $t\ge1$: its degree $t+h$ coefficients lie in $J^{t+h-d}\subset J^t$. Also $J^t\subset L^t$. After multiplying by any fixed products $A=I_2^{t_2}\cdots I_s^{t_s}$, this gives
$$\ell(R/L^tA)\le\ell(R/J^tA)\le\ell(R/L^{t+h}A).$$
On every ray of positive integral exponents, divide by the common large parameter to the power $n$. The two outside polynomials have the same degree-$n$ homogeneous part; the fixed shift $h$ contributes only smaller degrees. Thus the degree-$n$ parts for $J$ and $L$ agree on every positive rational ray and therefore as polynomials. All mixed coefficients agree. If $J,K$ have equal integral closure, apply this twice to $J+K$. This proves invariance without transferring coefficient inequalities from a length inequality. The finite graded argument is proved here and does not require finite normalization of a Rees algebra.

Finally, the order used in these moments is the order of functions on ALL $X_{\mathfrak a}$. For primary or unit $J,K$,
$$v(K)\ge v(J)\text{ on }X_{\mathfrak a}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\subset\overline J.$$
For proper primary $J$, apply the complete Swanson--Huneke 6.8.4 centred rank-one DVR criterion to each domain $R/P$ for $P\in\operatorname{Min}R$. Each quotient has positive dimension: in a positive-dimensional reduced local ring the maximal ideal cannot be minimal. Normalize these DVRs by the nonzero primary image of $\mathfrak a$; they are actual points of $X_{\mathfrak a}$ with support $P$. Then 6.8.3 (or 1.1.5 componentwise integral dependence) assembles the conclusion. Conversely a monic ideal-integrality equation and the unique-smallest-term argument in the quotient by any semivaluation support give the inequality at that support; primary values are finite. For $J=R$ the conclusion is automatic. For $K=R$ and proper $J$ the inequality is impossible on the nonempty section. These cover unit cases. Nonminimal supports are not removed merely because the criterion can be tested on a subset.

For rational profiles $u_i=-v(I_i)/k_i$ and a primary ideal test $v(J)$, define
$$B_{\boldsymbol u}(v(J))=\frac{e_R(I_1,\ldots,I_p,J)}{k_1\cdots k_p}.$$
For $p=0$ this means $e_R(J)$. A zero input may be represented by the unit ideal and gives zero if $p\ge1$. Equal rational profiles have, after integer powers, equal primary ideal values, hence equal integral closures; invariance and power linearity make the definition independent of presentation. Any rational linear relation among ideal tests becomes $v(A)=v(B)$ after synchronizing denominators and multiplying ideals, so the same argument makes the test functional well-defined. If a rational test function $v(A)-v(B)$ is nonnegative, the criterion gives $A\subset\overline B$. Since $A+B$ has the same integral closure as $B$, inclusion monotonicity and invariance give $e(\boldsymbol I,A)\ge e(\boldsymbol I,B)$. Thus the functional is positive on the whole rational test lattice.

Input order also follows: $u\le w$ implies the moments for $u$ are at least those for $w$ in that slot. For $u=-v(I)/k,w=-v(J)/l$, use $I^l\subset\overline{J^k}$ and divide by $kl$. All slots are symmetric and additive on addition of negative rational profiles. These are the algebraic kernel and order laws, not Cartier intersection comparison or locality.
