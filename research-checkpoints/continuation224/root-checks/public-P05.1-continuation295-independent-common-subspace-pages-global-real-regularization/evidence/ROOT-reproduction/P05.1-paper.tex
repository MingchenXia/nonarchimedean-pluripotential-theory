\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct a regularization theorem for a finite union of complete rational affine pages with one common positive-dimensional affine subspace and independent normal quotients. The weights and the fixed reference are actual real data. The main construction lowers the dual function only on compact minimizing fibers, obtaining an exact changed continuous trace with controlled global error. A primal polyhedral approximation then retains every spatial trace vertex and the exact trace recession rays. Its projected graph faces have rational geometry despite real heights. This yields genuine finite real piecewise affine polyhedral convex functions, glued along the entire common subspace, and a globally decreasing uniformly convergent sequence. The unrestricted envelope of every bounded continuous obstacle belongs to this closure before Monge--Ampere is assigned. Intersecting pages nevertheless have strict negative raw contact witnesses. The latter and the precise mixed completion assertions use the six specified whole grants within their scopes. This draft is not an independent acceptance and does not close original P05.1 or P05.2.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement, conventions, and evidence boundary}

\begin{equation}\label{sector}
1\leq k=\dim W<d=\dim V_i,\quad Q_i\cap Q_j=H\ (i\ne j),\quad
\bigoplus_i V_i/W\longrightarrow N_{\R}/W\text{ is injective}.
\end{equation}

(D_a Z)(\tau)=\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}z_\sigma a_\sigma(n_{\sigma/\tau})

\end{equation}

\section{Elementary finite-dimensional foundations}
The following details fix the ordinary foundations used later. Finite-dimensional linear algebra, completeness of the reals, elementary compactness, the separating-hyperplane theorem, and elementary measure integration are not recursively rebuilt. No additional research-paper theorem is used for convex duality or polyhedral generation.

\begin{lemma}[Finite generation and projection]\label{generation}
A polyhedron given by finitely many rational inequalities is generated by finitely many rational spatial vertices and rational recession rays, with lineality generated by pairs of opposite rational rays. A finite generated cone is closed. Projections of polyhedra are polyhedra; projections with rational coefficients retain rational coefficients.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a homogeneous inequality cone first quotient its lineality, the joint kernel of its defining linear forms. On the resulting pointed cone the sum of the nonnegative defining forms is strictly positive off zero. Its unit level section is compact: otherwise normalize an unbounded sequence to obtain a nonzero cone vector where the sum is zero. A bounded polyhedron is the convex hull of its vertices. To prove this last assertion, if a point is not a vertex its active equations have a nonzero null direction in its affine hull. Move in both signs until an additional inequality becomes active; compactness makes both endpoints exist. Express the original point as a segment combination and repeat on these strictly smaller active faces. Induction on dimension ends at vertices. Each vertex solves a full-rank subsystem; rational data give rational solutions. This proves cone generation after undoing the quotient and restoring opposite lineality rays.

For an inhomogeneous polyhedron use the closed homogenized cone in coordinates $(x,t)$ with $t\geq0$ and inequalities $Ax\leq bt$. Generators with $t>0$ normalize to rational points of the original polyhedron, and those with $t=0$ give its rational recession generators. Adding a rational coordinate arrangement makes its cells pointed when vertices are needed. Equivalently one can first remove lineality. An empty cell is discarded.

For completeness, a finite cone generated by columns $u_j$ is closed even without rational data. Any nonnegative representation can be reduced to linearly independent columns: a dependence permits moving coefficients in one of its two signs until a positive coefficient vanishes, preserving nonnegativity. For a convergent sequence select one independent subset along a subsequence. Its inverse on its span bounds and converges the coefficients, proving closedness.

Projection can be checked directly by eliminating one variable from inequalities. Retain the zero-coefficient inequalities and compare each lower bound with each upper bound. If only one sign occurs there is no paired constraint and a sufficiently large value in the appropriate direction exists. Iteration is Fourier--Motzkin elimination, a finite proof of both assertions. A hull plus a finite cone is therefore polyhedral by eliminating its nonnegative combination coefficients. Rationality is required only when asserting rational output inequalities.
\end{proof}

For a function on a vector space, put $a^*(p)=\sup_x(p(x)-a(x))$ and $\sigma_K(x)=\max_{p\in K}p(x)$. A finite continuous convex function equals its double conjugate. Indeed separate $(x,a(x)-t)$ from its closed epigraph. The separating coefficient of height has the appropriate positive sign: it cannot be zero because the epigraph contains a point over every spatial point. Dividing by it gives an affine minorant whose value at $x$ exceeds $a(x)-t$. Let $t\downarrow0$. The reverse inequality follows directly from the definition. The same argument proves $\phi^{**}=\phi$ for a closed convex extended-valued function with compact domain: at a point $p$ in its domain a strictly separating horizontal functional cannot separate a point directly below the epigraph, because the graph contains the same $p$. Outside its closed convex domain spatial separation and scaling give infinite double conjugate. These arguments also establish a support at each point of a full-domain finite convex function: take supporting epigraph normals at a boundary point; a horizontal normal would bound the entire spatial domain on one side, and is impossible. Normalize the nonzero height coefficient. Continuity of a finite convex function on an open affine space can alternatively be proved by bounding it above on a containing simplex and below using reflected points. Its local secants are bounded, so it is locally Lipschitz.

\begin{lemma}[Continuity on the entire compact polytope]\label{boundary}
A finite lower semicontinuous convex function $\phi$ on a compact polytope $K$ is continuous on all of $K$, including every boundary face.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work in the affine hull. Convexity bounds $\phi$ above by $M=\max_{v\text{ vertex}}\phi(v)$; lower semicontinuity and compactness bound it below. Fix $p\in K$. For all inequalities inactive at $p$ choose $r>0$ so their positive slack exceeds $r$ times the norm of their linear part. For a feasible $y\ne p$ sufficiently close to $p$, the point $z=p+r(y-p)/|y-p|$ is feasible. The inequalities active at $p$ hold on this ray because they hold at $y$; inactive ones hold by the choice of $r$. Thus
$\phi(y)\leq(1-|y-p|/r)\phi(p)+(|y-p|/r)M$.
This gives the upper limit at $p$; lower semicontinuity gives the lower limit. A zero-dimensional polytope is immediate. This proof uses its full boundary and works for real, nonrational vertices.
\end{proof}

\section{Actual recession and the compact dual}
Let $Q$ be one complete rational affine page and choose a rational origin. A continuous finite convex PA function $\gamma_Q$ on a finite carrier is the maximum of finitely many real affine forms. To see this, any affine piece on a full-dimensional cell supports globally: compare a segment from an interior point to an arbitrary point and use convexity as the segment exits the cell. Full-dimensional cells are dense, and continuity handles their boundaries. All such supporting forms suffice. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{actualdual}
\gamma_Q(x)=\max_j(p_j(x)+a_j),\quad h(x)=\max_jp_j(x)=\sigma_\Delta(x),\quad
\Delta=\conv\{p_j\}.
\end{equation}
The coefficients $p_j,a_j$ are actual real coefficients. The polytope $\Delta$ can be irrational. Positive recession gives $h(v)>0$ for $v\ne0$, so $0$ lies in the interior of $\Delta$: spatial separation would otherwise give a nonzero $v$ with $\sigma_\Delta(v)\leq0$. Also $|\gamma_Q-h|$ is bounded by the largest absolute intercept.

\begin{lemma}[Compact conjugate]\label{dual}
If $f$ is finite continuous convex and $|f-\gamma_Q|\leq C$, then $\phi=f^*$ has domain exactly $\Delta$, is finite and continuous on the whole $\Delta$, and is bounded there. Moreover $f=\phi^*$ and its recession is exactly $h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a finite $B$ we have $h-B\leq f\leq h+B$. If $p\in\Delta$, then $p(x)-h(x)\leq0$ for every $x$; evaluating at zero and using these bounds makes $f^*(p)$ finite and between $-B$ and $B$. If $p\notin\Delta$, spatial separation gives $v$ with $p(v)>h(v)$; $tv$ makes the conjugate infinite. A supremum of continuous affine functions is convex and lower semicontinuous, so Lemma~\ref{boundary} applies. The preceding double-conjugate argument proves $f=\phi^*$. Boundedness of $\phi$ gives $f(tv+x)/t\to\sigma_\Delta(v)$ for every $x$. In particular $f(x+v)\leq f(x)+h(v)$ by the supremum formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rational spatial recession generation]\label{rays}
Although $h$ has real slopes, $\epi h$ is generated by the vertical ray and finitely many vectors $(r,h(r))$ with $r$ rational in $Q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The recession cones of the finite rational carrying cells cover $Q$. For a direction $v$, choose an unbounded sequence of points on its ray lying in one fixed cell. Dividing each of the cell's inequalities by the ray parameter shows $v$ lies in its recession cone. On such a cone $h$ is the linear part of the cell's affine formula: move from any point of the cell in its recession direction and use the limit. Generate each rational cone by Lemma~\ref{generation}. On it $h$ evaluates linearly, so each $(v,h(v))$ is a nonnegative combination of the lifted generators. Convexity and homogeneity of $h$ put every such combination in $\epi h$; the vertical ray supplies arbitrary excess height. Both inclusions follow. This does not round or rationalize any height.
\end{proof}

\section{Primal approximation with exact finite interpolation}
\begin{proposition}[Full affine approximation]\label{affine}
For $f$ as in Lemma~\ref{dual}, any $\eta>0$, and any finite set $B\subset Q_\Q$, there is a finite rational-carrier real PA convex $a$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{approx}
f\leq a\leq f+\eta\quad\hbox{globally},\qquad a|_B=f|_B,\qquad \rec a=h.
\end{equation}
It is a genuine element of the finite real scalar extension.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Continuity and rational density give
$\phi(p)=\sup_{x\in Q_\Q}\{p(x)-f(x)\}$.
For each $p\in\Delta$ choose a rational sample making this supremum within $\eta/2$. The error of this fixed sample is continuous on the whole compact $\Delta$. It is less than $\eta$ on a neighborhood of $p$; finitely many neighborhoods cover $\Delta$. Let $A$ contain the resulting finite samples and all of $B$, and set
\[
\phi_A(p)=\max_{x\in A}\{p(x)-f(x)\}\quad(p\in\Delta).
\]
Thus $\phi-\eta\leq\phi_A\leq\phi$ everywhere on $\Delta$, not just its relative interior. Define
\begin{equation}\label{primalepi}
E_A=\conv\{(x,f(x)):x\in A\}+\epi h.
\end{equation}
The compact hull plus the closed cone is closed: for a convergent sum take a convergent subsequence of its compact summand. It projects onto all of $Q$ and has nonempty, upward closed fibers. It lies in $\epi f$ by convexity and $f(x+v)\leq f(x)+h(v)$. Hence every fiber has a finite attained lower boundary, say $a(x)$, and $E_A=\epi a$. It is a finite generated polyhedron by Lemma~\ref{rays}. Direct maximization over its generators shows
$a^*(p)=\phi_A(p)$ on $\Delta$ and is infinite elsewhere: the cone contributes zero precisely for $p\in\Delta$ and otherwise contributes infinity. Double conjugation gives \eqref{approx}. At a retained sample $(x,f(x))\in E_A$ equality follows from $a\geq f$. The bounded global error implies exact recession $h$; alternatively the recession of a compact hull plus this cone is exactly the cone, by dividing along an unbounded ray.

We now justify spatial rationality, which does not follow from a rational dual fan. A graph face supported by height functional $(p,-1)$ is
\[
\conv\{(x,f(x)):x\in A_p\}+\cone\{(r,h(r)):r\in R_p\}
\]
for subsets of the finite spatial samples and rays where equality in the supporting functional holds. The vertical ray is absent. On this face projection is one-to-one and its height is affine. Its projected domain is the hull of rational points plus the cone of rational rays. Thus it has rational spatial inequalities by Lemma~\ref{generation}, despite its real lifted heights. There are finitely many faces, every graph point has a nonvertical support because $a$ is finite on the whole space, and intersections of projected graph faces are projected common faces. Subfaces have the same property. These projections form a finite rational carrying complex for $a$. No maximum of real affine pieces on an irrational spatial subdivision was asserted.

Finally, on a fixed finite rational complex record each cell's affine coefficients in a rational affine coordinate system. Agreement on common faces is a finite homogeneous rational linear system. Its real solution space is the real span of a rational basis, obtained by row reduction over $\Q$. Multiplying each basis vector by a suitable positive integer makes all slopes integral on the cell lattices and all constants rational. Each resulting continuous function is an ordinary PA function on that complex. Therefore $a$ is a finite real linear combination of genuine PA functions on one rational carrier. Convexity then supplies its positive divisor by the support argument below. This proves the scalar-extension assertion, not merely cellwise real affinity.
\end{proof}

\section{One exact changed continuous trace}
Let $H\subset Q$ be one rational linear subspace after the chosen translation. Let $T:Q^*\to H^*$ be restriction. For $\Delta=\dom f^*$ put $\Delta_H=T\Delta$ and
\begin{equation}\label{fiber}
k(q)=\min_{Tp=q}\phi(p)\quad(q\in\Delta_H).
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Restriction duality on all fibers]\label{restriction}
The minimum in \eqref{fiber} is attained on each compact fiber. The extended function $k$ is closed convex, finite and continuous on the whole polytope $\Delta_H$, and $k=(f|_H)^*$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each fiber is a nonempty closed subset of compact $\Delta$, and $\phi$ is continuous. Taking convex combinations of minimizers proves convexity. If $q_j\to q$, take minimizing $p_j$ and a convergent subsequence along the lower limit; lower semicontinuity gives $k(q)\leq\liminf k(q_j)$. Boundedness follows from $\phi$; the extended function is closed also outside its closed compact domain. The image of a polytope is the hull of its finitely many projected vertices, so Lemma~\ref{boundary} gives continuity on its entire boundary. For $x\in H$ grouping the supremum by fibers gives
\[
k^*(x)=\sup_{q\in\Delta_H}(q(x)-k(q))=\sup_{p\in\Delta}(p(x)-\phi(p))=f(x).
\]
The closed convex double-conjugate argument proves the asserted identity. This also identifies the restricted recession $h_H=h|_H=\sigma_{\Delta_H}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Controlled extension of one changed trace]\label{trace}
Let $g$ be a finite continuous convex function on $H$ with
$f|_H\leq g\leq f|_H+\eta$ and the actual restricted recession $h_H$. There exists a finite continuous convex $F$ on $Q$ such that
\[
f\leq F\leq f+\eta,\qquad F|_H=g\text{ exactly on all of }H,\qquad \rec F=h.
\]
No PA conclusion for this intermediate $F$ is claimed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $b=g^*$. Lemma~\ref{dual} applied on $H$ gives domain $\Delta_H$, whole-boundary continuity, and
$k-\eta\leq b\leq k$. The bounded trace error already forces the restricted recession, but recording it prevents a hidden change of reference. The minimizing set
\[
M=\{p\in\Delta:\phi(p)=k(Tp)\}
\]
is closed in compact $\Delta$ because both displayed functions are continuous on their whole domains. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{S}
S=\{(p,b(Tp)):p\in M\}
\end{equation}
is compact and contains a point over a minimizer of every fiber. This is why mere interior dual continuity would be inadequate.

Define
\begin{equation}\label{dualepi}
E=\conv(\operatorname{graph}\phi\ \cup S)+\R_{\geq0}(0,1).
\end{equation}
The convex hull is compact. To see this without assuming a convex-hull compactness theorem, reduce any combination with more than $\dim Q+2$ points by an affine dependence, moving its nonnegative coefficients until one vanishes. Thus this hull is the continuous image of a compact finite product times a closed simplex. Adding the vertical ray is closed, since a convergent sum with its compact component cannot have unbounded ray parameter. The projection is exactly $\Delta$, heights are bounded below, and all fibers have attained lower boundary $\psi$; hence $E=\epi\psi$ is a closed convex epigraph.

Every source in \eqref{dualepi} lies above $\phi-\eta$. This holds on $S$ because there $\phi(p)=k(Tp)$ and $b\geq k-\eta$. Convexity of $\phi-\eta$ therefore gives $\psi\geq\phi-\eta$. The graph of $\phi$ is included, so $\psi\leq\phi$. Every source also lies above $b\circ T$: on the graph use $\phi\geq k\circ T\geq b\circ T$, and on $S$ there is equality. Convexity of $b\circ T$ gives $\psi\geq b\circ T$. For each $q$, a minimizing point $p\in M$ supplies $(p,b(q))\in E$. It follows, with attainment, that
\begin{equation}\label{exactfiber}
\min_{Tp=q}\psi(p)=b(q)\qquad(q\in\Delta_H).
\end{equation}
Take $F=\psi^*$. Compactness of $\Delta$ and boundedness of $\psi$ make $F$ finite and globally Lipschitz. Order reversal and $\phi-\eta\leq\psi\leq\phi$ give $f\leq F\leq f+\eta$. Grouping the dual supremum by fibers in \eqref{exactfiber} gives $F|_H=b^*=g$ at every point. Boundedness of $\psi$ on exactly $\Delta$ gives recession $h$ as before. This completes the continuum proof; no finite check is a substitute for \eqref{exactfiber}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The construction handles one restriction map $T$. Lowering dual values to meet constraints for several distinct overlap subspaces can lower the wrong other fibers. Nothing here asserts their simultaneous feasibility. Nor is any Monge--Ampere operator applied to $F$ before genuine PA regularization.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact finite PA trace and synchronization}
\begin{lemma}[Epigraph generators of a real PA trace]\label{tracegenerators}
A finite rational-carrier convex real PA $g$ on $H$ has, after a pointed rational carrying refinement, finitely many rational spatial vertices $B$ and rational spatial rays generating
\[
\epi g=\conv\{(v,g(v)):v\in B\}+\epi h_H.
\]
All heights and recession values are the actual real values.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Refine by rational coordinate hyperplanes through a rational origin. Each cell is then contained in an orthant and has no lineality; Lemma~\ref{generation} supplies its vertices and rays. On that cell $g$ is affine. A recession ray has lifted height $h_H(r)$ by the limit, as in Lemma~\ref{rays}. Thus every graph point is a convex combination of the lifted vertices of its cell plus a nonnegative sum of its lifted rays. Convexity gives the reverse inclusion for the common hull plus $\epi h_H$. The vertical ray supplies heights above the graph. This proves equality of entire epigraphs, including unbounded cells and all boundary faces.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Genuine PA extension with an exact whole trace]\label{sync}
In Theorem~\ref{trace}, suppose $g$ is finite rational-carrier real PA. There exists a genuine finite rational-carrier real PA convex $F_A$ on $Q$ with
\[
f\leq F_A\leq f+2\eta\text{ globally},\qquad F_A|_H=g\text{ on all }H,\qquad\rec F_A=h.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use Theorem~\ref{trace} first; $F$ need not be PA. Apply Proposition~\ref{affine} to $F$ with approximation error $\eta$, appending every vertex $v$ of the pointed carrying refinement of $g$. These vertices are rational in $Q$ and $F(v)=g(v)$. Thus the new primal epigraph contains all $(v,g(v))$. Its recession cone is $\epi h$, whose intersection with $H\times\R$ is exactly $\epi h_H$. In particular it contains each actual lifted trace recession ray. Lemma~\ref{tracegenerators} now implies
$\epi g\subset \epi F_A\cap(H\times\R)$.
Therefore $F_A|_H\leq g$. The above approximation gives $F_A\geq F$ and $F|_H=g$, proving the other inequality and exact whole trace equality. The global error is $2\eta$. Spatial graph-face rationality, scalar-extension membership, and exact recession follow from the proof of Proposition~\ref{affine}. Retaining finite points alone would not prove this result: the full trace epigraph and its exact recession are used.
\end{proof}

\section{Inherited incidences and all-face book convexity}
\begin{proposition}[Continuous PC equivalence]\label{book}
A continuous function on $X$ is printed-PC if and only if its restrictions to all pages are ordinary convex and agree on all of $H$. In that case it satisfies every legal real-endpoint PCC on every rational refinement, including nongeneric faces.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First a convex polyhedron contained in a finite union of pages is contained in one page. Its relative interior is an open subset of its affine hull; a finite union of proper affine subspaces cannot contain such a set. For an elementary verification choose a linear form vanishing on each proper subspace but not identically on that affine hull. The product is a nonzero polynomial and cannot vanish on an open box, as repeated one-variable evaluation shows. Thus every inherited carrier cell belongs to a page; cells in $H$ may belong to several.

Suppose $f$ is continuous printed-PC. Refine the inherited rational complex by the ambient rational equations defining all pages and $H$. This is a legal refinement, and makes $H$ a union of faces. A cell not on $Q_i$ meets $Q_i$ only in $H$; the induced complex on $Q_i$ is consequently a rational refinement by genuine inherited cells and faces. Inside each cell, rational endpoint Jensen inequalities extend to real endpoints by density and continuity. Rational density on any face follows by solving its rational active equalities and approximating within its relative interior; points on smaller faces are handled on those faces. Thus $f$ is convex on each cell.

Take a segment with rational endpoints in $Q_i$. Its finite cell break parameters are rational: intersect its rational affine line with the finitely many rational inequalities. At a break point the adjacent segment cells contain a common inherited face containing that rational point. Nearby opposite rational points on the line, with equal rational distance from the break point, give a legal two-endpoint PCC. Its midpoint inequality orders the left and right secant slopes. On each side the cellwise convexity makes slopes monotone; taking decreasing rational distances orders the left and right one-sided limiting slopes. Hence every secant ending at the break from the left is at most every secant leaving it to the right. The concatenation is convex: for any three ordered points, split their secants at the finitely many breaks; the ordered side secants imply the usual three-point secant inequality. A segment lying in a lower-dimensional face uses the induced one-dimensional cells and the same argument. Continuity and rational approximation of arbitrary page endpoints then prove ordinary convexity on the entire page. No replacement by a coarser rational box is made. Agreement on $H$ follows from single-valuedness.

Conversely assume page convexity with a common convex trace. Consider any inherited PCC, even with real endpoints and real nonnegative weights. If its center lies outside $H$, every incident endpoint cell containing its center belongs to its unique page. Jensen on that page applies. If its center $x$ is in $H$, assign each endpoint in $H$ arbitrarily to a page and group all endpoints by page. Let $A_i$ be the positive total weight of group $i$ and $y_i$ its barycenter; discard groups of zero weight. In the ambient quotient by $H$ the barycenter equation reads
$\sum_i A_i[y_i]=0$.
The direct-sum assumption forces $[y_i]=0$ for every group, so $y_i\in H$. Pagewise Jensen followed by Jensen on the common trace gives
\[
\sum_j\alpha_jf(x_j)\geq\sum_i A_i f|_H(y_i)\geq f|_H\left(\sum_iA_i y_i\right)=f(x).
\]
No generic position, normal circuit, top-dimensional face, or rationality of these endpoints was used. This proves the claimed real-endpoint completion for continuous data on every inherited face.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Printed endpoint pathology]
On a rational affine space put $s(x)=\gamma(x)$ at rational points and $s(x)=\gamma(x)-1$ at other points. Every endpoint value is $\gamma$; at a rational center use its convexity and at an irrational center the value has only been lowered. Thus $s$ is printed-PC and bounded relative to $\gamma$. Both sets are dense in positive dimension, and no continuous function can approximate $s$ uniformly with error less than $1/2$. This explains the continuous hypothesis and why raw printed-PC continuity or broad literal density is not inferred from BMPS/BBGS. Our envelope argument uses R230's actual continuity result in its exact domain instead.
\end{remark}

\section{Complete supports, positive primitive divisors, and gluing}
\begin{lemma}[Common supports at the entire overlap]\label{support}
Suppose pagewise finite convex functions $a_i$ are bounded relative to $\gamma_i$ and share a common trace $g$ on $H$. At each $x\in H$, any $q\in\partial g(x)$ lifts to $p_i\in\partial a_i(x)$ on every page. The $p_i$ combine to one ambient affine support of all pages at $x$.
