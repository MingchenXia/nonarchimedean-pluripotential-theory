## 1. The theorem and its domain

Let `(X,0)` be a closed normal algebraic germ over an algebraically closed field of characteristicZERO, of dimension at least2. Write `(R,m)` for its local ring and fix an m-primary ideal `a`. Let `B=X_a(R)` be the ENTIRE intrinsic normalized semivaluation boundary, with all nonminimal support primes and all infinity values retained.

Let `C_geo` and `D_geo` be the independently geometric continuous cone and its completion by ONE decreasing net of continuous members, at the full qualified scope of G331. Their geometric origin is the nef real Cartier core on ACTUAL primary models, before its comparison with the ideal family. In particular these are not being defined by a new order completion in this manuscript.

Fix the coherent nonzero correction ideal

`Qideal=d(K_X) Jac_X`.

Here `d(D)=O_X(D) O_X(-D)` is the published defect ideal. An affine representative may be shrunk around the germ to choose this coherent ideal; the primary-normalized boundary and all statements are local. A choice of canonical divisor changes it by a Cartier/principal shift and leaves the defect ideal unchanged. Set

`O={v in B: v(Qideal)<+infinity}`,  `K_L={v in B: v(Qideal)<=L}`.

The ideal value is the minimum of finitely many generator values, continuous into `[0,+infinity]`. Thus `O` is OPEN, each `K_L` is compact, and every divisorial point with minimal support belongs to `O`. The omitted closed subset is exactly the set of support primes containing `Qideal`; it is not discarded from `B` or from the geometric cone.

For a finite continuous real obstacle `f`, define the raw geometric envelope

`p(f)(v)=sup{h(v):h in D_geo, h<=f on ALL of B}`

and the specified ordinary USC regularization `E(f)=p(f)^*` on ALL of `B`. Constants `-M` are geometric minorants for sufficiently large `M`, so `p(f)` is finite. The constraint is global even when the conclusion is local.

**Theorem 1.** For EVERY finite continuous real obstacle `f`, the raw envelope `p(f)` is continuous on `O`, and `E(f)=p(f)` there. On `O` it also agrees with the supremum restricted to `C_geo` minorants. All support primes within `O`, rather than just real-valued field valuations, are included.

For a rational signed primary-ideal obstacle, the proof constructs global continuous geometric minorants with a quantitative estimate on EACH `K_L`. The error consists of one finite determining-model trace error tending to zero, plus `L/(Nk)`, where `N` clears the obstacle's rational coefficients. No global bound on `v(Qideal)` is assumed. The approximation is uniform on `K_L`, not asserted uniform on the entire boundary when `Qideal` is nonprimary.

Consequently any failure of continuity for finite continuous obstacles in this characteristic-zero setting must lie on the closed infinite-correction stratum. This is a location result, not an assertion that such failures exist. It does not imply that the global USC regularization is in `D_geo`, nor does it supply unconditional positive envelopes on the omitted stratum or for all extended USC obstacles.

## 4. The general Section3 multiplier estimate and PRIMARY truncation

## 5. The finite trace supplies a GLOBAL lower competitor

## 6. Cauchy estimates on the finite-correction OPEN set

Subtract the divisorial estimates using the SAME intermediate `W`. For any two indices `k,l`, all normalized centered divisors satisfy

`u_k-u_l<=eta_k+v(Qideal)/(Nl)`,

`u_l-u_k<=eta_l+v(Qideal)/(Nk)`.

Both sides are continuous on `O`, because `v(Qideal)` is finite there. Every centered divisor belongs to `O`, and divisorial density on the whole boundary implies density in this OPEN subspace. Consequently these inequalities hold on ALL of `O`.

On any `K_L`, they show that `u_k` is uniformly Cauchy, since the finite trace errors tend to zero and `L/(Nk)` tends to zero. Write `U` for its pointwise limit on `O`. This limit is continuous there: each point of `O` has an open neighborhood `{v:v(Qideal)<L}` for some finite `L`, on which the sequence is uniformly Cauchy with the same estimates. Uniform limits of continuous functions are continuous on that neighborhood.

On each centered divisor `U=W`. Letting `l` tend to infinity in the two inequalities gives on ALL of `O`

`U-v(Qideal)/(Nk)<=u_k<=U+eta_k`.

Equivalently, for the ACTUAL global minorants from Section5,

`0<=U-g_k<=eta_k+v(Qideal)/(Nk)`.

The nonnegative left inequality follows as well from `g_k<=W` on divisors and continuity on `O`. On `K_L`, the right side is at most `eta_k+L/(Nk)`. Therefore `U<=p(f)` on `O` by the definition of the global raw envelope. Since every `g_k<=f` globally and the limit is taken at points of `O`, also `U<=f` there.

No divisorial density inside the CLOSED subset `K_L` is assumed. The inequalities are first extended on the OPEN set `O`, and then restricted to `K_L`. This distinction is necessary for the estimate.

## 7. Domination of EVERY geometric completed competitor

Let `h in D_geo` satisfy `h<=f` on ALL of `B`. Use its ONE actual decreasing net `h_alpha in C_geo` converging pointwise to `h`. For any `epsilon>0` and each of the finitely many normalized determining vertices `v_i`, one can choose an index past which

`h_alpha(v_i)<=f(v_i)+epsilon`.

A single common index exists by directedness and finiteness. This argument also works if `h(v_i)=-infinity`; no finite lower bound on the competitor is presumed.

The source b-divisor `H_alpha` associated with this ACTUAL continuous majorant is source-nef by Section2. Its trace shifted by `epsilon Z(a)` is no larger than `C_Y`; at every prime outside the closed fiber the traces are zero. Source Corollary2.13 gives

`H_alpha+epsilon Z(a)<=W`.

Thus `h_alpha-epsilon<=W=U` on centered divisors. The two functions `h_alpha-epsilon` and `U` are continuous on `O`, so density in that open subspace extends their inequality to all its support points. Since `h<=h_alpha`, it follows that `h<=U+epsilon` on `O`. Letting `epsilon` decrease to zero shows `h<=U` there.

Every completed competitor is controlled in this way, so `p(f)<=U`. Together with Section6 this proves equality, continuity, and equality with the supremum of CONTINUOUS minorants on `O` for the rational signed obstacle. ## 8. All finite continuous obstacles and the specified regularization

Rational signed primary-ideal profiles form a uniformly dense vector lattice on the WHOLE compact boundary. To spell out the lattice property, for normalized profiles `p_I(v)=v(I)`,

`min(p_A-p_B,p_C-p_D)=p_(AD+CB)-p_(BD)`.

Products, sums, and integer powers in this identity remain primary, and a common integer denominator handles rational coefficients. Rational constants come from powers of `a`. Separation of ALL boundary support points is the full qualified G331 ideal-profile separation, rather than a separation assertion restricted to field valuations. The lattice Stone--Weierstrass theorem consequently gives uniform density; it is ordinary topological background, not an envelope theorem.

The raw operator is nonexpansive on finite continuous obstacles. If `||f-g||<=delta` and `h<=f` is a completed geometric competitor, then `h-delta` is also in `D_geo`, by subtracting the same nonnegative constant from its ONE decreasing continuous net, and `h-delta<=g`. Taking suprema gives `p(f)-delta<=p(g)`. Interchanging the obstacles gives

`||p(f)-p(g)||<=||f-g||`

on ALL of `B`. Existence of negative constant competitors ensures these values and inequalities are finite. The identical argument holds when competitors are restricted to `C_geo`.

Approximate an arbitrary finite continuous `f` uniformly by rational signed profiles `f_j`. Their raw envelopes are continuous on `O` by the preceding whole argument. The nonexpansive estimate makes them converge uniformly, even on all of `B`, to `p(f)`. Hence `p(f)` is continuous on `O`. The analogous estimate for the continuous-competitor suprema, and their equality on `O` for each `f_j`, proves that equality on `O` also for `f`.

Since `O` is OPEN, every point in it has a neighborhood contained in it. Ordinary USC regularization of `p(f)` on the full space can therefore be computed there using only those neighborhoods. Continuity on `O` gives `p(f)^*=p(f)` at every such point. This completes the proof of Theorem1.
