\begin{abstract}
For a normal algebraic local germ over a perfect field of positive
characteristic, we give an explicit uniform approximation of the geometric
envelope of a Cartier obstacle carried by an actual smooth primary blowup.
Asymptotic test ideals and uniform generation give primary ideal profiles
with error at most $c v(J)/m$, at every semivaluation support. No singular
multiplier ideal, global discrepancy bound, numerical b-divisor recognition,
or increasing-limit positivity is used. An extension to every continuous
obstacle requires the stated smooth-primary-model hypothesis. That
hypothesis is not proved here. This is an unreviewed working paper and does
not close original P12.3 or P16.3.
\end{abstract}

\section{Fixed data and the precise model hypothesis}
Let $k$ be perfect of characteristic $p>0$. Let $B=\operatorname{Spec} A$
be a normal integral affine $k$-variety, $x$ a closed point of codimension
$n\geq2$, and $R=A_{\mathfrak m_x}$. Fix a proper $\mathfrak m_x$-primary
ideal $\mathfrak a$. After an affine localization around $x$, all primary
ideals used below are represented by coherent ideals supported at $x$.
Let $X_{\mathfrak a}$ be the entire compact normalized boundary
$\{v:v(\mathfrak a)=1\}$. We retain nonminimal prime supports and $+\infty$
values on the kernel of a semivaluation.

We use the \emph{entire qualified G331} geometric cone: actual negative
effective real Cartier presentations on actual primary blowups, tested on
every contracted integral curve, their uniform closure $\cC$, and infima
of one specified decreasing $\cC$-net, with bottom, denoted $\cD$.
In particular a unit-or-primary ideal profile $-v(I)/r$ belongs to $\cC$;
such rational profiles are uniformly dense in $\cC$ after taking the
allowed finite operations. Negative rational constant shifts remain
single profiles after multiplication by a power of $\mathfrak a$.
These are previously proved class comparisons, with actual geometric
positivity preceding them. No envelope theorem is imported.

For a finite continuous obstacle $f$ set
\[
 P(f)(v)=\sup\{h(v):h\in\cD,\ h\leq f\}.
\]
The fixed-model theorem has the following additional data:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Y=\operatorname{Bl}_{J} B$ is an \emph{actual} integral blowup of a
proper $x$-primary ideal $J$, and $Y$ is smooth over $k$;
\item $Y\to B$ is the identity off $x$, and $\mathfrak a\OO_Y$ is
invertible;
\item $C$ is an integral Cartier divisor on $Y$ supported on the full
closed fibre. Its evaluation on $X_{\mathfrak a}$ is $f_C$.
\end{enumerate}
The tautological bundle
$\mathcal A=J\OO_Y=\OO_Y(-F_J)$ is ample and globally generated, since
the base is affine. Put $H=-F_J$, so $\OO_Y(H)=\mathcal A$ and
$h_H=-v(J)\leq0$. The sign of $H$ is essential.

Evaluation at a point of arbitrary support is performed first in
$\operatorname{Frac}(R/\ker v)$. Its generic point is outside the closed
point, and properness gives its centre on $Y$. Local rational generators
of a divisor supported on the closed fibre are nonzero at this generic
point, so their values are finite. Qualified G331 supplies continuity and
boundedness on the whole section. No map from $\operatorname{Frac}R$ to
the quotient field is used.

\section{The raw envelope and coherent section ideals}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:raw}
For finite continuous $f$, the raw $\cC$ and $\cD$ suprema coincide.
They are bounded and lower semicontinuous. They satisfy
$\|P(f)-P(g)\|_\infty\leq\|f-g\|_\infty$.
If $P(f)$ is a uniform limit of $\cC$ functions below $f$, then
$P(f)\in\cC$ and it is the greatest $\cD$ minorant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A negative constant is a competitor and all competitors are nonpositive.
If $h=\inf_\alpha h_\alpha\leq f$ is its actual decreasing representation,
the increasing open sets $\{h_\alpha<f+\eps\}$ cover the compact boundary.
A finite subcover and directedness give one index valid everywhere.
Then $h_\alpha-\eps\in\cC$ is below $f$ and is at least $h-\eps$.
Thus the two suprema agree; the supremum of continuous minorants is lower
semicontinuous. Subtracting a negative constant from each competitor gives
the two inequalities for nonexpansivity. A uniform limit is in the closed
cone $\cC$, and equality with the raw supremum gives greatest-minorant
dominance directly. No increasing-limit closure of $\cD$ is asserted.
\end{proof}

Define, inside $\operatorname{Frac} A$,
\[
 I_m=\Gamma(Y,\OO_Y(mC)),\qquad
 u_m=-\frac1m v(I_m),\qquad m\geq1.
\]
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sections}
Each $I_m$ is a nonzero coherent unit-or-primary ideal of $A$. The
sequence is graded and
\[
 u_m\leq f_C,\qquad P(f_C)=\sup_{m\geq1}u_m
       =\lim_{m\to\infty}u_m
\]
pointwise at every support. This limit statement alone is not positivity
recognition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A section is regular on $B\setminus\{x\}$ because $C$ is supported on
the closed fibre. Normality and codimension at least two extend it to
$A$. Proper coherent direct image gives coherence. On the complement of
$x$ the direct image is $\OO_B$, by the stated identity of $Y$ there.
Choose an integer $M>0$ with $C\geq-M F_{\mathfrak a}$, where
$\mathfrak a\OO_Y=\OO_Y(-F_{\mathfrak a})$. Such an $M$ exists because
there are finitely many fibre prime divisors and the coefficients of
$F_{\mathfrak a}$ at all of them are strictly positive. Smoothness implies
normality, so the coefficient inequality is actual effectiveness.
Consequently $\mathfrak a^{Mm}\subset I_m$. This proves nonzero and
unit-or-primary, and $0\leq v(I_m)\leq Mm$ at all points of the section.
Products of sections give $I_m I_l\subset I_{m+l}$.

For a section $s$, effectiveness of $\operatorname{div}(s)+mC$ gives
$v(s)+m f_C(v)\geq0$ at every support by valuation-ring pullback. Hence
$u_m\leq f_C$. Each $u_m$ is an actual primary or unit profile in $\cC$.

Conversely let $g=-v(L)/r\leq f_C$ be a rational unit-or-primary profile.
For every prime divisor $E$ of the fibre the centred divisorial test
gives $\operatorname{ord}_E(L)+r C_E\geq0$. At the remaining prime
divisors, $C_E=0$ and sections of $L\subset A$ are regular.
Since $Y$ is normal, these tests imply
$L\OO_Y\subset\OO_Y(rC)$, as an actual inclusion of fractional ideal
sheaves, and hence $L\subset I_r$. Thus $g\leq u_r$.
For any $h\in\cC$ below $f_C$, use the granted uniform density of rational
single profiles and a small negative rational constant shift. The resulting
profile is below $f_C$ and approaches $h$ uniformly. This gives
$\sup_m u_m\geq h$. Lemma~\ref{lem:raw} proves the asserted supremum
identity with all $\cD$ competitors.

For a fixed $v$, $a_m=v(I_m)$ is a finite, nonnegative subadditive
sequence. Its Fekete limit is $\inf_m a_m/m$: explicitly, write an index
as $qr+s$, use $a_{qr+s}\leq q a_r+a_s$, and let the index tend to
infinity. For $s=0$ omit the remainder term; the other finitely many
remainder values are finite. Negating proves the limit of $u_m$ and its
value. This works separately at every semivaluation support and does not
assume monotonicity of the entire sequence.
\end{proof}

\section{The exact test-ideal inputs}
Let $\mathfrak b_m$ be the base ideal of $\OO_Y(mC)$, namely
\[
 \mathfrak b_m=I_m\OO_Y\otimes\OO_Y(-mC)\subset\OO_Y.
\]
It is a nonzero graded family and is the unit ideal off the closed fibre.
Write $\mathfrak t_m=\tau(\mathfrak b_\bullet^m)$ for its asymptotic
test ideal on the \emph{smooth $k$-variety $Y$}. The applied published
algebraic inputs are GJKM Section~4, Definitions~4.1, 4.3, 4.5,
Remarks~4.2--4.4 and Theorem~4.6:
\[
 \mathfrak b_m\subset\mathfrak t_m,\qquad
 \mathfrak t_{ml}\subset\mathfrak t_m^l\quad(m,l\geq1).
\tag{TI}
\]
The ideals are coherent, compatible with restriction, and equal to the
unit ideal off the fibre. These facts concern smooth varieties over a
perfect field of positive characteristic; no singular test-ideal
subadditivity is asserted.

Theorem~4.6 states: if $Z$ is an integral smooth $k$-scheme, projective
over the spectrum of a finite-type $k$-algebra, $D,E,H$ are Cartier
divisors, $\lambda\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}$, $\OO_Z(H)$ is ample globally
generated, some $H^0(Z,mD)$ is nonzero, and $E-\lambda D$ is nef, then
\[
 \OO_Z(K_{Z/k}+E+dH)\otimes\tau(\lambda\Vert D\Vert)
\quad\hbox{is globally generated for }d\geq\dim Z+1.
\tag{UG}
\]
We apply it on $Y$, not on a singular base and not to an analytic
discretely valued equation. Set $D=C$, $E=mC$, $\lambda=m$, and fix
$d=n+1$. The nef difference is exactly zero, and the nonzero-section
hypothesis was proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:sections}. Thus
$\OO_Y(K_{Y/k}+mC+dH)\mathfrak t_m$ is globally generated for every $m$.

Fix $c\geq0$ large enough that
$\mathcal A^c\otimes\OO_Y(-K_{Y/k}-dH)$ is globally generated. This is
ordinary Serre generation for one fixed coherent invertible sheaf and
the ample bundle $\mathcal A$. Tensor the two globally generated sheaves.
It follows that
\[
 \mathcal F_m:=\OO_Y(mC)\otimes\mathfrak t_m\otimes\mathcal A^c
 \quad\hbox{is globally generated for every }m\geq1.
\tag{1}
\]
The integer $c$ is independent of $m$. This step does not assume that
$K_{Y/k}$ has an exceptional Cartier presentation on the singular base;
the fixed canonical twist is cancelled by a globally generated invertible
sheaf on $Y$.

\section{Uniform approximation on the entire boundary}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:fixed}
Under the fixed-model hypotheses, let $J_m=\Gamma(Y,\mathcal F_m)$.
These are nonzero coherent unit-or-primary ideals of $A$. The actual
geometric profiles $g_m=-v(J_m)/m$ satisfy, at every support,
\[
 g_m\leq P(f_C)\leq g_m+\frac{c}{m}v(J).
\tag{2}
\]
If $\mathfrak a^N\subset J$, then
$\|P(f_C)-g_m\|_\infty\leq cN/m$. Consequently $P(f_C)$ is in $\cC$
and is the greatest $\cD$ minorant of $f_C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The sheaf $\mathcal F_m$ is a fractional ideal sheaf equal to $\OO_Y$
off the fibre. Normal Hartogs extension again embeds its global sections
in $A$; proper coherence gives a finite ideal. The inclusion
$\mathfrak b_m\subset\mathfrak t_m$ gives
$I_m J^c\subset J_m$. Hence $J_m$ contains a primary power and is nonzero.
Away from $x$ the direct image is the unit ideal; it is therefore
unit-or-primary. Global generation (1), as an equality of fractional
ideal sheaves, gives
\[
 J_m\OO_Y=\OO_Y(mC)\,\mathfrak t_m\,\OO_Y(cH).
\tag{3}
\]
For any of our semivaluations the coherent ideals in this equality have
finite value: $\mathfrak t_m$ contains $\mathfrak b_m$, whose value is
finite by Lemma~\ref{lem:sections} and the finite Cartier evaluation of
$C$. Evaluate local generators at the proper centre, in the supported
quotient valuation ring. Equality (3) then yields the literal identity
\[
 g_m(v)=f_C(v)-\frac{c}{m}v(J)-\frac1m v(\mathfrak t_m).
\tag{4}
\]
In particular $g_m\leq f_C$ since both ideal values are nonnegative.
Its actual unit-or-primary profile is in $\cC$ by G331, and therefore
$g_m\leq P(f_C)$.

For fixed $m$ and every $l\geq1$, evaluate (TI) and the first inclusion
at $ml$. With the base-ideal identity this gives
\[
 u_{ml}=f_C-\frac{v(\mathfrak b_{ml})}{ml}
 \leq f_C-\frac{v(\mathfrak t_{ml})}{ml}
 \leq f_C-\frac{v(\mathfrak t_m)}m.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:sections} identifies the limit of this actual subsequence
at every support with $P(f_C)$. Equation (4) now proves the other half
of (2). Since $J$ is primary, some $\mathfrak a^N$ is contained in $J$;
$v(J)\leq N$ on the entire section, including all nonminimal supports.
Uniform convergence to $P(f_C)$ follows. Lemma~\ref{lem:raw} proves
geometric positivity and greatest-minorant dominance. We have not inferred
positivity from an increasing limit, nor from a capacity or energy limit.
\end{proof}

\section{The precise extension and the outstanding geometric gate}
Say that $R$ has property (SM) if, for every finite collection of primary
ideals, including the chosen gauge, after shrinking one affine normal
neighbourhood of $x$ there is an \emph{actual smooth primary blowup}
$Y=\operatorname{Bl}_J B$ principalizing that collection. This is a finite
ideal principalization hypothesis; it asserts no cofinality for arbitrary
proper models. A merely regular alteration, a positive-dimensional
birational fibre over another locus, or a nonprimary centre does not meet it.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:SM}
If (SM) holds, every finite continuous obstacle on the entire normalized
local boundary has a continuous geometric envelope in $\cC$. The result
holds over a perfect positive-characteristic field without an algebraic
closure assumption, in the one fixed primary gauge.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A rational signed primary-profile obstacle is determined by finitely many
ideals. Property (SM) supplies $Y$; after clearing one denominator the
obstacle is the evaluation of an integral Cartier divisor $C$ supported
on its fibre. Positive homogeneity of the raw envelope follows by scaling
the entire competitor cone, so Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed} applies also before
denominator clearing. Qualified G331's signed-profile lattice density
approximates any finite continuous obstacle uniformly by these rational
ones. Lemma~\ref{lem:raw} makes their envelopes uniformly Cauchy with
limit exactly the raw envelope. Closedness of $\cC$ finishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
These are local geometric envelopes, not an analytic Monge--Ampere
equation or a finite-energy inverse. No P16.3 closure follows. Theorem
\ref{thm:fixed} is substantive even before (SM) is established: it
constructs actual primary ideals and a uniform error bound, using the
exact published test-ideal inputs rather than assuming envelope continuity.
For example the ordinary blowup of the origin of affine space is smooth
in every characteristic, and the theorem applies to the Cartier obstacles
it actually carries. That example alone does not carry all obstacles.
\end{remark}

The general nonisolated normal germ cannot be covered by an argument
silently choosing a smooth modification unchanged off the closed point:
that would already make its punctured germ smooth. For isolated algebraic
germs in low dimension, published resolution results may provide the
needed model construction, but the construction must also produce an
\emph{actual primary blowup} with an ample exceptional boundary and all
specified ideal principalizations. Neither that construction nor an
unconditional application of Cossart--Piltant is claimed in this paper.
The full arbitrary excellent/nonalgebraic/mixed-characteristic residual
and the characteristic-zero infinite-correction stratum of manuscript370
remain separate. A failure of (SM) is not an envelope counterexample.
