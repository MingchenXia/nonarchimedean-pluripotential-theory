## 2.2. Intersections, energy, and Radon measures

On a common compactified configuration over $\mathbb P^1$, define
$$
 \langle(\theta_0,\varphi_{D_0}),\ldots,(\theta_n,\varphi_{D_n})\rangle
 =\prod_{i=0}^n(\theta_{i,\overline{\mathcal X}}+D_i).
 \tag{4}
$$
Intersection is with the fundamental cycle. Projection formula makes
(4) independent of the dominating model. It is symmetric, multilinear,
and translating its $i$th potential by $c$ adds
$c(\theta_0\cdots\widehat\theta_i\cdots\theta_n)_X$.
For a model test $h$ its decisive testing identity is
$$
 \langle(0,h),(\theta_1,\varphi_{D_1}),\ldots,(\theta_n,\varphi_{D_n})\rangle
 =\sum_E b_E h(v_E)\prod_{i=1}^n(\theta_{i,\mathcal X}+D_i)|_E.
 \tag{5}
$$
The restricted nef classes give nonnegative weights. The central-fiber
cycle and generic-fiber cycle have the same intersection degree, so
the mass is $(\theta_1\cdots\theta_n)_X$. These are actual model
intersection measures.

BJ Theorem 7.1 extends (4) to ample psh tuples by
$$
 \langle(\omega_0,\varphi_0),\ldots,(\omega_n,\varphi_n)\rangle
 =\inf_{\psi_i\in\mathcal H_{\rm dom}(\omega_i),\ \psi_i\geq\varphi_i}
       \langle(\omega_0,\psi_0),\ldots,(\omega_n,\psi_n)\rangle.
 \tag{6}
$$
Monotonicity follows from (5). Upper semicontinuity follows by choosing
a model tuple below a threshold and using continuity of
$\varphi\mapsto\sup(\varphi-\psi)$ to bound nearby tuples by that tuple
plus small constants. The translation rule gives the required bound.
Decreasing approximation proves uniqueness and decreasing continuity.
Define $E$ as in Theorem A and
$$\mathcal E^1(X,\omega)=\{\varphi\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega):E(\varphi)>-\infty\}.$$
Then $E(0)=0$ and $E(\varphi+c)=E(\varphi)+c$ exactly. Energy is
concave, increasing, usc, and decreasing-continuous. Bounded psh
functions have finite energy. Theorem 7.8 proves finiteness of all
mixed finite-energy pairings: subtract constants, compare finitely
many ample classes by positive multiples, and bound the energy of
their sum using Lemmas 7.9--7.10. This argument has no envelope
antecedent. Theorem 7.14 then extends (4) symmetrically and
multilinearly to differences of finite-energy psh functions.

For such differences $\varphi_i$ and numerical classes $\theta_i$,
BJ Theorem 7.18 defines the mixed signed Radon measure $M$ by
$$
 \int h\,dM=\langle(0,h),(\theta_1,\varphi_1),\ldots,(\theta_n,\varphi_n)\rangle,
 \qquad h\in\operatorname{PL}(X)_{\mathbb R}.
 \tag{7}
$$
In the nef-psh case the functional is positive by monotonicity, has
value $(\theta_1\cdots\theta_n)_X$ at $1$, and is therefore bounded
by that mass times $\|h\|_\infty$. PL density in $C^0$ and Radon
representation give existence and uniqueness. Multilinearity gives
the signed case. Proposition 7.19 proves (5), exact mass, normalization
pushforward, integrability of finite-energy tests, and decreasing
continuity. Thus the construction really extends the model operator;
it is not a prescribed inverse-measure parametrization.

For integrability, decrease model test potentials to an $\mathcal E^1$
test. Mixed pairing finiteness and monotone integration give a finite
integral and preserve (7). Multilinearity handles differences and
signed measures. Positive mixed measures charge no pluripolar set.
Weighted decreasing continuity is Proposition 7.21: its weighted
integral is the pairing containing $\varphi_0$ **minus** the pairing
containing $0$. Those terms both converge. Lemma 7.22 then gives weak
convergence of the weighted measures. This supplies the sign needed
in its printed proof. The Hölder pairing estimate of Theorem 7.34
also gives strong continuity of mixed measures. On irreducible $X$,
$\operatorname{MA}_\omega(0)=\delta_{v_{\rm triv}}$.

## 2.3. Comparison and uniqueness before envelopes

For $\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal E^1$, the model formula, locality on
vertical components, and weighted decreasing continuity give BJ
Theorem 7.40:
$$
 \mathbf1_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\max(\varphi,\psi))
 =\mathbf1_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\varphi).
 \tag{8}
$$
On a model the maximum agrees near a component where its value is
strictly larger, and hence has the same intersection weight there.
The weighted limiting test is
$(\varphi-\psi)_+=\max(\varphi,\psi)-\psi$; pluripolar sets have no mass.
Apply (8) to $\max(\varphi,\psi-\epsilon)$ and use equal total masses:
$$
 \operatorname{MA}(\psi)(\{\varphi<\psi-\epsilon\})
 \leq\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)(\{\varphi\leq\psi-\epsilon\}).
$$
Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives the correctly signed comparison
$$\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\psi)
 \leq\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\operatorname{MA}(\varphi). \tag{9}$$

Set
$$
 I(\varphi,\psi)=\int(\varphi-\psi)(\operatorname{MA}(\psi)-\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)),
$$
$$
 J_\psi(\varphi)=E(\psi)-E(\varphi)+\int(\varphi-\psi)\operatorname{MA}(\psi),
 \qquad J(\varphi)=\sup\varphi-E(\varphi).
$$
Mixed pairing algebra and vertical intersection negativity imply
$I\geq0$, $I/(n+1)\leq J_\psi\leq nI/(n+1)$, strict energy concavity,
and the quantitative pairing estimates of BJ Sections 7 and 10.
The energy difference is
$$
 E(\varphi)-E(\psi)=\frac1{(n+1)V}\sum_{j=0}^n
 \int(\varphi-\psi)(\omega+dd^c\varphi)^j\wedge(\omega+dd^c\psi)^{n-j}.
 \tag{10}
$$
It lies between the integrals against $\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)$
and $\operatorname{MA}(\psi)$, in that order.

BJ Corollary 10.4 is in the equidimensional general-variety scope.
Equal MA implies $I=0$. Normalize equal suprema. Theorem 10.3 tested
against $\delta_v$ and $\delta_{v_{\rm triv}}$ gives equality at every
divisorial $v$: these Dirac measures have finite dual energy by
Proposition 11.1 and linear growth. Minimal usc extension gives global
equality. Irreducibility therefore leaves one additive constant.

The global domination principle is also unconditional: if
$\varphi\in\operatorname{PSH}$, $\psi\in\mathcal E^1$ and
$\varphi\leq\psi$ almost everywhere for $\operatorname{MA}(\psi)$,
put $\rho=\max(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathcal E^1$. Then
$$I(\rho,\psi)=-\int(\rho-\psi)\operatorname{MA}(\rho)\leq0.$$
Separation and its a.e. zero difference give $\rho=\psi$. This is BJ
Corollary 10.6 for **global** potentials. It is not a substitute for
proving a merely weak/local singular potential is global.

# 3. A noncircular smooth affine repair

## 3.1. Correct growth and the printed counterexample

W Definition 3.15(1) requires
$$
 \operatorname{FS}(Y)\ni h=max\{c_i\log|f_i|+C_i,\lambda\},
$$
where the $f_i$ generate $k[Y]$, $c_i>0$ are rational, and
$C_i,\lambda$ are rational. Its printed Definition 3.15(2) requires
$h\leq u+O(1)$, a **lower** growth condition. Definition 6.1 instead
requires $u\leq h+O(1)$, an upper condition. We explicitly use (2)
and write $\operatorname{PSH}^{\log,+}$ and
$\operatorname{PSH}^{\log,+}_{\rm reg}$ for this corrected convention.
This does not make the printed assertions true under their printed
definitions.

Indeed take $k=\overline{\mathbb Q}$, $Y=\mathbb A^1$, and
$a=\log|z|$. The functions $a_m=\max(\log|z|,-m)$ are actual affine
FS functions and decrease to $a$. They are finite rational convex PL
functions of $-\log|z|$; identity pullback proves weak pshness.
Also $a\leq\max(\log|z|,0)$. But every FS function has a finite floor
and $a(0)=-\infty$, so no FS function can satisfy the printed lower
bound. Thus W's printed Proposition 6.2(2) and Theorem 6.3 are false
with that membership requirement. The corrected convention permits
logarithmic poles.

W Proposition 4.19 independently invokes Lemma 3.25(1), whose explicit
antecedent is affine regularizability. That antecedent is its desired
conclusion. The following proof replaces this invocation rather than
just changing the growth sign.

## 3.2. Independent smooth local-to-global input

We use the smooth specialization of the local-to-global mechanism
preceding W Proposition 4.19. Its logical inputs are as follows; none
is the disputed affine regularization lemma.

First, a **global PL** locally psh metric is a nef model metric.
For a continuous PL limit, the local decreasing tropical PL
approximation is uniformly convergent on compact neighborhoods by
Dini. Locally, finite rational convex PL functions have convex
smoothing approximants on their finite-coordinate charts; at infinite
coordinates the finite PL expression is independent of those
coordinates on a smaller neighborhood (W Remark 3.2). It therefore
gives a CLD psh function. CLD Definition 8.1.1 is the sheaf generated
from smooth positive-Hessian functions by decreasing limits, not W's
definition by name. CLD Proposition 8.3.1 gives positive curvature for
these locally integrable functions. For an integral model metric,
CLD Theorem 18.8.4 and Corollary 18.8.8 identify positive curvature
with numerical nefness of its residual bundle. Rational metrics are
handled by a tensor power. Applied to model charts this is W Theorem
3.23, hence (3). This direction does not claim equality of all CLD
and W local psh functions.

Second, a bounded locally regularizable function can be embedded
on a smaller neighborhood into a bounded genuine global psh potential.
Here is the useful construction of W Proposition 3.28(2). Choose
nested compact rational tropical domains $P\Subset Q\Subset U$
around the point, with an ample FS potential $v$ such that $v<0$ on
$P$, $v\leq0$ on $Q$, and $Q=\{v\leq0\}$. Clearing poles by a high
power of an ample line bundle realizes the finitely many inequalities
as ratios of sections; add a complete very ample system. Let
$|u|\leq M$ there, with rational $M$ enlarged if necessary. Choose a
rational $A>(2M+1)/\epsilon$ so that
$h=Av+M+1<-M$ on $P$ and $h>M$ on a collar of $\partial Q$.
For the given common-neighborhood decreasing PL approximants $u_i$,
compactness and the strict collar margin ensure $u_i<h$ on that
collar for all sufficiently large $i$. Extend
$\max(u_i-h,0)$ by zero. This is globally PL and locally $A\theta$-psh,
so the preceding model criterion makes it globally psh. These
extensions decrease, are uniformly bounded below, and limit to the
same extension for $u$. This proves the needed genuine globalization
using local PL approximants only. It also proves divisorial
determination for bounded local potentials by BJ Theorem 4.22;
truncation gives the general local assertion used below.

The FS reference just constructed need not equal a previously fixed
model reference, even if their underlying line bundle agrees. This is
accounted for explicitly: write $\theta=\theta_0+dd^c\eta$ with a
global rational PL model potential $\eta$. Replace the glued
$A\theta$-potential $\psi$ by $\psi+A\eta$, and replace its local
potential $h$ by $h-A\eta$. Their sum on the local neighborhood is
unchanged, and the new pair represents $A\theta_0$. Both $\eta$ and
the glued potential are bounded on the compact space. This reference
change, rather than simply identifying different model forms, is
used whenever a prescribed form is required in the next construction.

Third, the bounded local mixed measure can be defined using these
globalizations and BJ's genuine mixed operator. On a small neighborhood
write $C_i u_i=\phi_i+g_i$, with $\phi_i$ global bounded psh and
$g_i$ a model potential. Define the local mixed measure by the
corresponding scaled global mixed measure. For PL inputs CLD Theorem
18.4.7 identifies its intersection weights with the local model
measure. Weighted decreasing continuity in BJ Proposition 7.21 proves
independence of choices on overlaps and localizes the measure. This
is the actual construction in W Theorem 4.2, whose proof uses
Proposition 3.28(2), not smooth affine regularization. It works with
decreasing approximants because the measure is transported from BJ;
mere current positivity is not being asserted to supply that continuity.

Local logarithms of units are pluriharmonic. A finite PL partition of
unity subordinate to a compact finite cover permits integration by
parts, first for local PL approximants and then by weighted continuity.
One can refine the finitely many tropical inequalities of the cover
by a rational simplicial subdivision and use its affine hat functions;
only their restrictions to the compact tropical image matter. Local
PL functions in this calculation need extension only on a neighborhood
of the compact support. The identity
$$\int_X dd^c\phi_1\wedge\bigwedge_{i=2}^n(\theta_i+dd^c\phi_i)=0$$
then gives total local mass $(\theta_1\cdots\theta_n)$. Locality for
maxima is transferred from BJ by the same globalization. Its correct
restriction on $\{u>v\}$ is the measure of $u$, not the measure of $v$
printed in W 4.11(1). Mass and locality give bounded local comparison
with the sign in (9), as in W 4.12--4.15.

On a **smooth** projective irreducible $\bar Y$ over our field, BJ
Theorem 5.20 gives the envelope property for its nef reference class.
For an ample rational trivial reference $\theta$, BJ Theorems 12.8,
12.12 and Proposition 11.1 give a continuous genuine global psh
solution $h_v$ to $\operatorname{MA}_\theta(h_v)=\delta_v$ for every
divisorial point $v$. This replaces the BFJ input in the smooth use of
W 4.16 entirely within its valid scope. For bounded local
$\alpha,\beta$ with $\alpha\leq\beta$ a.e. for the local measure of
$\beta$, subtract a constant from $h_v$ so $h_v<\alpha$. Then
$\alpha_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)\alpha+\epsilon h_v<\alpha$ and its
positive local mixed expansion has an atom at least $\epsilon^n V$
at $v$. Comparison and equal masses force all its mass onto
$\{\alpha_\epsilon<\beta\}$, hence
$\alpha_\epsilon(v)<\beta(v)$. Let $\epsilon\downarrow0$ and use
local divisorial determination. This proves local domination on this
smooth compactification. No singular Dirac existence is used.

Finally let $\alpha$ be bounded locally $\theta$-psh on this smooth
projective space. Take a decreasing **net** of continuous majorants
$f_i\downarrow\alpha$. Such a net exists on a compact Hausdorff space
for every bounded usc function, by normality and finite minima;
countability is unnecessary. Smooth EP gives continuous global
$p_i=P_\theta(f_i)$. The envelope of
$p_i+t(f_i-p_i)$ is $p_i$ for $0\leq t\leq1$. BJ's genuine continuous
energy derivative gives
$$\int(f_i-p_i)\operatorname{MA}_\theta(p_i)=0.$$
Thus the measure is on the closed contact set. Since
$\alpha\leq f_i=p_i$ there, the local domination just proved gives
$\alpha\leq p_i\leq f_i$. The net $p_i$ decreases to $\alpha$ and
has a uniform lower bound, so BJ decreasing closure proves global
pshness. This is the needed smooth instance of W Theorem 4.17, with
its hypotheses established noncircularly. General singular local
domination has not been used.

## 3.3. Smooth compactification and rational parameters

Take an affine embedding $Y\hookrightarrow\mathbb A^r$ and its
projective closure. Characteristic-zero resolution by boundary
blowups gives a smooth projective compactification $\bar Y$ that is
unchanged on $Y$. There is an effective ample boundary divisor: start
with the hyperplane divisor, and at a boundary blowup replace its
pullback by $m$ times that pullback minus the effective exceptional
divisor, for sufficiently large $m$. The negative of that exceptional
divisor is relatively ample. Enlarging $m$ makes the divisor effective
with precisely the inverse-image boundary support. Iteration proves
the assertion; this is the sign-correct form of W Lemma 3.32.

Choose a very ample multiple $L_0$ of that boundary divisor and its
canonical section $s$, so $Y=\bar Y\setminus Z(s)$. Give $L_0$ its
trivial semipositive model metric, with rational form $\theta$.
Including $s$ in a very ample system yields
$$g=-\log|s|_\theta\in\operatorname{FS}(Y),\qquad g\geq0.$$
The ratios of the complete system by a power of $s$ generate $k[Y]$,
as the affine chart of a closed projective embedding. If (2) holds,
polynomial degree estimates between coordinate generators give
$h\leq A g+B'$ for a positive rational $A$ chosen large enough.
Thus assume $u\leq Ag+B$. Choose **rational** $c>1$ and
$\theta'=cA\theta$. This is a rational ample semipositive model form,
not an arbitrary real model coefficient. Choose rational $C$.

## 3.4. Direct truncation and the boundary neighborhood

On $Y^{\rm an}$ set
$$\phi=u-cAg,\qquad\phi_C=\max(u-cAg,C),$$
and set $\phi=-\infty$, $\phi_C=C$ on
$D^{\rm an}=\bar Y^{\rm an}\setminus Y^{\rm an}$.
The upper bound gives
$$\phi\leq B-(c-1)Ag.\tag{11}$$
Choose $R$ with $B-(c-1)AR<C$. The continuous norm $|s|_\theta$
vanishes precisely on $D^{\rm an}$. Its open sublevel set
$\{|s|_\theta<e^{-R}\}$ is a neighborhood of that whole boundary,
and $g>R$ on its intersection with $Y^{\rm an}$. By (11),
$\phi_C=C$ there. This is an actual common boundary neighborhood,
not just a pointwise assertion of limiting values. It proves usc
gluing, and $C\leq\phi_C\leq\max(B,C)$ everywhere.

On $Y$, $cAg$ is a genuine model potential of $\theta'$ in W
Definition 3.10: clear denominators in $cA$ and use the corresponding
power of $s$. Hence
$$\phi_C+cAg=\max(u,C+cAg)$$
is locally psh by the local definition and finite-max closure. To
verify that closure, put the two local decreasing PL sequences over
the same smaller neighborhood and combine their tropicalizations;
their maxima are rational convex PL and decrease to the maximum.
Near the boundary $\phi_C=C$, and every local potential of the
semipositive form $\theta'$ is locally psh; thus $C$ is locally
$\theta'$-psh. Changes of trivialization add a rational multiple of
the logarithm of an invertible section ratio. Refining the
tropicalization adds an affine function in a finite unit coordinate,
which preserves local convexity and decreasing approximation. The
same local definition therefore holds across charts.

It follows that $\phi_C$ is a bounded locally $\theta'$-psh function
on the **smooth** compactification. Section 3.2 makes it genuine
global $\theta'$-psh. Let $C=-m\downarrow-\infty$. The limit $\phi$
is finite wherever $u$ is finite and $g$ is finite. Since $Y$ is
irreducible and $u$ is generically finite, there is such a point, so
the global limit is not identically $-\infty$. BJ decreasing closure
gives $\phi\in\operatorname{PSH}(\bar Y,\theta')$. The identically
$-\infty$ function is outside this generically finite theorem; if
an extended cone includes it, $g/m-m$ is an explicit decreasing FS
sequence tending to it. No generically finite assertion is attached
to that endpoint.

## 3.5. Actual affine FS correspondence

Take a decreasing global rational FS sequence $\phi_i\downarrow\phi$
in the rational class $\theta'$, using BJ Corollary 12.18 followed
by ample FS approximation with summable error tails. Set
$u_i=\phi_i+cAg$ on $Y$. Its FS metric on a sufficiently divisible
power $m cA L_0$ is described by a finite basepoint-free system with
rational weights. Add the complete very ample system on a further
power, including the distinguished section $s^{mcA}$. Assign rational
weights low enough that all added terms are dominated by the
original metric: their relative log norms have finite suprema on the
compact projective space. The metric is unchanged.

On $Y$ these terms are
$$m^{-1}\log|\sigma/s^{mcA}|+\lambda_\sigma.$$
The complete-system ratios generate $k[Y]$; the distinguished section
supplies a rational constant floor. Thus every $u_i$ is in the actual
FS class of Definition 3.15(1), with positive rational coefficients,
rational constants, and coordinate-algebra generators. The sequence
decreases pointwise to $u$. The upper growth (2) was assumed and
proved compatible with this construction. A lower bound at logarithmic
poles was neither assumed nor concluded. This proves Theorem C's
locally psh part.

## 3.6. Smooth weakly psh base

The repaired base needed in W 6.2(2) also starts with a weakly psh
function. Proper birational pullback is local psh by definition; the
proper map is closed, so the descended function is usc. Local
globalization and divisorial determination above show it is the
minimal usc extension of its divisorial values. On a dense affine
principal open $Y_f$ where the birational map is an isomorphism,
the function is locally psh and has the upper growth bound. The
locally psh part of Theorem C gives actual affine regularization there.

For completeness the upper-growth removable extension used here
does not need the disputed base. Suppose $v$ is already regularizable
on $Y_f$ and $v\leq h+B$ for $h\in\operatorname{FS}(Y)$. For
positive rational $\epsilon$, extend $v+\epsilon\log|f|$ by
$-\infty$ across $f=0$. Controlled approximants of $v$, obtained
from a projective compactification by the **legitimate** regularizable
case of W Lemma 3.25, are bounded above by
$2\max(h,-\epsilon\log|f|/3)+O(1)$. Their sums with
$\epsilon\log|f|$ tend to $-\infty$ near $f=0$, uniformly for
each fixed approximant. Truncate at rational floors. The truncations
are PL and locally psh, constant on a neighborhood of $f=0$;
compact model gluing and (3) give global affine FS regularization.
No regularization of $v$ is being proved by assuming it here.

Let $\epsilon_i\downarrow0$. Subtract a common large multiple of an
ample boundary potential $g$ on a smooth compactification. The
resulting genuine global potentials are uniformly bounded above,
equal $v+\epsilon_i\log|f|-2Ag$ on $Y_f$, and $-\infty$ on the
complement. They need not be monotone where $\log|f|>0$. Smooth EP
applied to their tail suprema shows that the usc regularization of
their pointwise limit is global psh. On $Y_f$ this limit is
$v-2Ag$ and is already usc; there the regularization is unchanged.
Section 3.5 supplies a regularizable extension on $Y$. Divisorial
determination makes it unique and identifies it with the original
weakly psh function. This reconstructs the smooth upper-growth base
without using the singular theorem 6.3 or its conclusion.

# 4. An elementary supporting barrier, with its precise extent

Let $\mathbb T=\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$ with extended order topology,
and $r\geq1$. Choose $a>0$ with $\log(r+1)/a\leq1$. Define
$$\chi(t)=a^{-1}\log\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^r e^{-a t_i}\right),
\qquad e^{-a\infty}=0.$$
Then $\chi$ is finite continuous and
$$\max(0,-t_1,\ldots,-t_r)\leq\chi(t)
\leq\max(0,-t_1,\ldots,-t_r)+1.\tag{12}$$
The inequalities bound a sum of $r+1$ nonnegative terms by its
largest term and $(r+1)$ times that term.

Fix $t$, put $Z=1+\sum_i e^{-at_i}$,
$p_0=1/Z$, $p_i=e^{-at_i}/Z$, and $I=\{i:p_i>0\}$. Define
$$C=\chi(t)+\sum_{i\in I}p_i t_i
 =-a^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^r p_i\log p_i,\qquad
 \ell_t(t')=C-\sum_{i\in I}p_i t'_i.$$
Terms with zero coefficient are omitted; if $t'_i=\infty$ for
$i\in I$, the affine expression is $-\infty$. It is finite on a
neighborhood of the contact point and has nonpositive slopes. Write
$p'$ for the probabilities obtained from $t'$. For finite values of
the affine expression,
$$a(\chi(t')-\ell_t(t'))=\sum_{i=0}^r p_i\log(p_i/p'_i)\geq0.$$
Gibbs' inequality follows directly from $\log x\leq x-1$ on the
positive support. Equality also requires no $p'$ mass outside that
support, hence $p'=p$. Since $p_0,p'_0>0$ and
$p_i/p_0=e^{-at_i}$, this means $t'=t$. Thus contact is unique,
including infinite-coordinate strata. The expression must be
evaluated at $t'$, correcting the variable in W Lemma 6.6.

Pointwise strictness is insufficient for uniform separation on this
noncompact space. Put $D(t')=\chi(t')-\ell_t(t')$. If
$m=\min_i t'_i<0$, then $\chi(t')\geq-m$ and
$\ell_t(t')\leq C-(1-p_0)m$, so
$$D(t')\geq-p_0m-C.$$
Consequently $K_b=\{D\leq b\}$ has all coordinates at least
$-B$, $B=(b+C)/p_0$. Since $\chi\geq0$,
$\sum_{i\in I}p_i t'_i\leq b+C$, so for each $i\in I$,
$$t'_i\leq\frac{b+C+B\sum_{j\in I,\ j\ne i}p_j}{p_i}.$$
Coordinates outside $I$ need no upper bound. This puts $K_b$ in a
compact product of finite intervals and intervals $[-B,\infty]$.
The extended nonnegative $D$ is continuous there, so $K_b$ is
compact. Its zero set is $\{t\}$. If $U$ is any neighborhood of $t$,
compactness of $K_1$ and the finite-intersection property give
$K_b\subset U$ for some $b>0$. Hence
$$\ell_t<\chi-b\quad\hbox{on }\mathbb T^r\setminus U.\tag{13}$$
This proves the exact supporting and separation assertions independently
of the cited APW supporting results.

There is also an explicit rational local polyhedral approximation for
this particular function. Its entropy variational formula is
$$\chi(t)=\sup_{q\in\Delta_r}
 \left(-\sum_i q_i t_i-a^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^r q_i\log q_i\right).\tag{14}$$
Rational $q$ give the same supremum, also at infinity, by approximating
inside the face with zero coefficients on infinite coordinates. On
the compact box $Q_B=[-B,\infty]^r$, enumerate rational vectors with
$q_0=1$ and the simplex vertices included. Their finite maxima are
continuous and increase to $\chi$. Dini gives uniform approximation.
Round entropy constants down by rational errors. For rational
$\epsilon>0$, a finite rational convex PL maximum $h_\epsilon$
then satisfies $\chi-\epsilon\leq h_\epsilon\leq\chi$ on $Q_B$.
With $\epsilon_j=4^{-j}$,
$$g_j=h_{\epsilon_j}+2\epsilon_j,\qquad
 \chi+\epsilon_j\leq g_j\leq\chi+2\epsilon_j$$
gives $g_{j+1}<g_j$ and uniform convergence to $\chi$ there.
The same bound holds on the open box $t_i>-B$. At an infinite
coordinate, terms involving it tend to $-\infty$ and are inactive
on a smaller neighborhood; the constant term remains. This checks
the actual finite rational PL definition, rather than treating an
extended affine expression as a finite function everywhere.

Pullback along regular functions $h_1,\ldots,h_r$ yields decreasing
local rational tropical PL approximants wherever those functions
have bounded absolute values. It does **not immediately** give
decreasing global affine FS approximants. Rational slopes can be
cleared to logarithms of monomials. The generator and floor
conditions require the compact gluing/complete-system procedure of
Section 3.5, or W Lemma 3.26's independent PL-exhaustion procedure:
on each compact exhaustion domain, truncate a function minus a
larger ample FS potential; the growth margin makes the boundary
collar constant; glue its given PL approximants; apply (3); then
apply global FS regularization. The sequence's errors are made
summable before diagonalization. This procedure is valid whenever
the **common-domain** local PL sequences and upper growth bound
have actually been established. It is not automatic closure under
every decreasing sequence of merely locally psh functions; W
Remark 4.6 explicitly distinguishes that issue.

We spell out the common-exhaustion step, including real slopes and
the infinite series, so that the barrier does lie in the actual
corrected affine FS regularization class. This step uses the model
criterion (3), rather than a singular envelope theorem.

**PL-exhaustion gluing lemma.** Suppose $Y$ is irreducible affine,
$u\leq g+B$ for an actual affine FS $g$, and on the interior of
each compact exhaustion domain there is a single decreasing rational
convex tropical PL sequence to $u$. Assume $u$ is generically finite.
Then $u\in\operatorname{PSH}^{\log,+}_{\rm reg}(Y)$.
To prove this, choose the projective compactification and rational
ample model potential associated with $g$ by the explicit
affine embedding of W 3.17(2). Rational rescaling and a constant
turn $g$ into that model potential. Fix rational $c>1$ and a rational
floor $C$. The upper growth margin puts
$\{u-cg\geq C-1\}$ in a compact set $E\Subset Y^{\rm an}$.
Take a compact neighborhood of $E$ inside one exhaustion interior.
On its boundary collar $u-cg<C-1$; compactness of the collar and
decrease of the given continuous PL sequence imply that every
sufficiently late approximant is below $cg+C$. Thus its maximum
with the floor, after subtracting $cg$, extends as the constant $C$
outside that neighborhood. It is globally PL and locally
$c\theta$-psh; the local model criterion makes it globally psh.
It decreases to $\max(u-cg,C)$, which is genuine global psh by
decreasing closure. Letting $C\downarrow-\infty$ preserves generic
finiteness and gives the global potential $u-cg$, with boundary
value $-\infty$. Global sequential FS regularization and the
complete-system generator/floor argument of Section 3.5 then give
actual decreasing affine FS approximants for $u$. This proves the
lemma without the disputed 4.19 application or singular EP. It
reconstructs the required upper-growth instance of W 3.26.

For the strict barrier on a countable trivially valued $k$, enumerate
all regular functions $f_j$ and fix coordinate generators $z_i$.
Use $\rho_j=\chi_{r+j}(-\log|z_i|,-\log|f_1|,\ldots,-\log|f_j|)$.
Let $v=\max(0,\log|z_i|)+1$, and choose positive rational
$\gamma_j$ so small that
$0\leq\gamma_j\rho_j\leq2^{-j}(v+1)$. Polynomial degree bounds
and (12) permit this choice. The series $\rho=\sum_j\gamma_j\rho_j$
is continuous, converges locally uniformly, and has upper growth
$\rho\leq v+1$.

Its upper approximants
$R_N=\sum_{j\leq N}\gamma_j\rho_j+2^{-N}(v+1)$ decrease to
$\rho$, because the next term is at most the decrease in the tail
bound. On a **fixed** compact exhaustion neighborhood, all the
regular functions in each finite list have rational log-norm upper
bounds. Formula (14) therefore approximates each $R_N$ uniformly
by a finite rational convex tropical PL $G_N$, with error at most
$\epsilon_N=4^{-N}$. The constants and slopes are rational, since
the $\gamma_j$ are rational. Set
$\widetilde G_N=G_N+2\sum_{m\geq N}\epsilon_m$; this tail is
rational. On that neighborhood,
$$\widetilde G_N-\widetilde G_{N+1}
 \geq R_N-R_{N+1}+\epsilon_N-\epsilon_{N+1}\geq0.$$
The sequence decreases to $\rho$. The gluing lemma proves genuine
affine FS regularizability with its generator requirement.

Fix a contact point $x$ and an open neighborhood $U$. The initial
topology of regular-function norms gives a finite-list tropical
neighborhood $\operatorname{Trop}_j^{-1}(W)$ containing $x$ and
contained in $U$; the enumeration includes that finite list. Use the
supporting expression $\ell$ at $\operatorname{Trop}_j(x)$ from
(13), and put
$$\rho'=\gamma_j\ell\circ\operatorname{Trop}_j
                 +\sum_{i\ne j}\gamma_i\rho_i.$$
It has upper growth, is finite at $x$, agrees there with $\rho$,
and is at most $\rho-\delta$ outside $U$ for some $\delta>0$.
There is no $\infty-\infty$ operation: the continuous series is
finite and the supporting term is either finite or $-\infty$.

To verify actual regularizability of $\rho'$, write its supporting
term as $C+\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_i\log|h_i|$ with real
$\alpha_i>0$. On a fixed exhaustion neighborhood choose rational
$B_i$ with $\log|h_i|\leq B_i$. Choose rational
$\alpha_{i,m}\uparrow\alpha_i$, all positive, and rational
$q_m\downarrow C+\sum_i\alpha_i B_i$. Then
$$L_m=\max\left(q_m+\sum_i\alpha_{i,m}(\log|h_i|-B_i),-m\right)$$
is finite rational convex tropical PL and decreases to that real
logarithmic expression. This monotonicity uses
$\log|h_i|-B_i\leq0$; at a zero of a positive-coefficient $h_i$
the untruncated expression is $-\infty$ and the floor gives a
finite approximant. The remainder series, omitting the fixed $j$,
has decreasing local PL approximants by the preceding tail
construction. Add the two sequences on the same exhaustion
neighborhood. They decrease to $\rho'$, which is generically finite
since it is finite at $x$. The gluing lemma supplies decreasing
**global affine FS** approximants, with rational constants,
rational slopes, and a generating coordinate list. This proves
the supporting replacement used in W 6.7 under upper growth,
including real coefficients, logarithmic poles, and the infinite
series. It does not assert the norm theorem 6.12 or the full
singular Runge chain.

# 5. Rechecking the singular induction: a concrete uncovered field

W Theorem 6.3 uses the smooth weakly psh base 6.2(2), then supporting
functions, Rossi's principle, finite norms 6.12, the local projection
step 6.14--6.16, and the product-domain distance argument 5.7. The
smooth base is repaired above under upper growth. It is insufficient
to validate the next norm application.

In Proposition 6.12 Step 1, for $s\in S^{\rm an}$ the proof sets
$F=H(s)$, takes a line through its $F$-rational lift in $S_F$, and
normalizes the line's preimage. The normalized curve $C_s^\nu$ is
smooth over $F$. Step 3, W LF2331--2334, states that the pulled-back
local psh function on this curve is affine FS regularizable **by
Proposition 4.19**. But 4.19 explicitly assumes a discretely or
trivially valued field. The following instance shows that $F$ need
not satisfy that antecedent.

Take $k=\overline{\mathbb Q}$ trivially valued and
$$
 Y=\operatorname{Spec} k[a,b,y,z]/(b^2-a^3),\qquad
 S=\operatorname{Spec} k[a,y,z],\qquad \pi:Y\longrightarrow S.
 \tag{15}
$$
The source is integral, nonnormal and unibranch, the product of a
cusp and two affine lines. The map is finite flat of degree two:
its algebra has basis $1,b$ over $k[a,y,z]$. It is étale on $a\ne0$;
there $b$ is invertible and the derivative $2b$ is invertible.
This affine source is a chart of
$C_{\rm cusp}\times\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, a projective
geometrically unibranch threefold in the retained domain. Its
normalization is $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$;
the cusp normalization has one point with unchanged residue field
in each geometric fiber, and taking its product preserves that
universal-homeomorphism property. The exterior tensor product of
$\mathcal O_{C_{\rm cusp}}(1)$ and the two $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$
is ample. Thus this field issue arises internally in the proposed
proof on the retained projective domain. It is not a request to
extend the task to a different ground-field axis.

On $S$, let $s$ have kernel $(a)$ and monomial valuation
$$v(y)=1,\qquad v(z)=\sqrt2,$$
with a nonzero polynomial in $y,z$ valued by its least monomial
weight. Distinct monomials have distinct weights, so products have
the sum of their least weights. This defines a multiplicative
seminorm $\exp(-v)$ and an analytic point. Its completed residue
field is the completion of $k(y,z)$ for this valuation. It contains
values $m+n\sqrt2$ for all integers $m,n$, from rational monomials.
This subgroup is nondiscrete: by the pigeonhole principle applied
to fractional parts of $j\sqrt2$, there are nonzero such values
arbitrarily close to zero. Its absolute value is nontrivial.
Completion cannot remove those elements or their values.

The $F$-rational lift of $s$ has coordinates $(0,y_s,z_s)$. The line
$a=\tau$, $y=y_s$, $z=z_s$ is generically in the étale locus. Its
preimage is $b^2=\tau^3$, and its normalization is $\mathbb A^1_F$
with $\tau=t^2$, $b=t^3$. Thus the smooth curve used by Step 3
really occurs over a nondiscrete field, in dimension three, with an
algebraically closed trivial original field and unibranch source.
This is a mathematical witness to the failed application antecedent;
it is not a finite computer search or a counterexample to norm
regularization itself.

Section 6.3.1 and Proposition 6.11 of W do allow **any** complete
field. They prove that the finite-flat norm of an already
regularizable function is regularizable. At the challenged use,
regularizability of the input on $C_s^\nu$ has not been established.
Applying 6.11 therefore cannot supply the missing antecedent. W
Lemma 6.13 states the limiting value of a local psh function at a
closed point of a smooth curve over any complete field; it does not
state existence of affine FS approximants or a norm theorem for
merely local psh inputs. The inspected CLD statements give current
positivity, model positivity and local/model measure compatibility.
They likewise do not state this affine regularization assertion.

A possible repair is to replace Step 3 by an independently proved
one-dimensional pushforward/subharmonic boundary-limit argument,
including compatibility with W's local definition and multiplicities.
Another is to prove upper-growth affine regularization on these
smooth curves over every $H(s)$. Neither has been proved here. This
is a specific mathematical obligation, not an objection based on
v1 status or missing bibliographic identifiers.

With the supporting-function closure supplied by Section 4,
this gap still occurs in 6.12(2), then in 6.15 Step 5 and the
induction of 6.14, then in the singular-stratum induction of 6.3.
Consequently W 6.5's affine-chart argument and the countable-field
proof of body Theorem 7.1 cannot yet be used as a proved singular
envelope antecedent in this reconstruction. Some printed index
errors are harmless (for example the projection preimage should be
taken over the chosen affine target open), but correcting them does
not cover $H(s)$.

# 6. Conditional envelope passage and removal of cardinality

This section checks the useful forward route with its antecedents
visible. It does not claim the missing countable case.

For a rational ample trivial reference, a very ample multiple
embeds $X$ into $\mathbb P^r$. At the center of a valuation one basis
section is a unit and the others have nonnegative order; the trivial
metric on that multiple is the pullback of the trivial projective
FS metric. This verifies the needed reference reduction. It does
not identify an arbitrary nef model metric with the embedding metric.
On chart $Y_j=\{z_j\ne0\}\cap X$, the potential
$$\rho_j=\max_i\log|z_i|-\log|z_j|$$
is nonnegative and $\min_j\rho_j=0$ everywhere. If the missing
singular upper-growth regularization theorem were available, it
would regularize $\varphi+\rho_j$. The legitimate regularizable
extension lemma then makes $\varphi-(c-1)\rho_j$, extended by
$-\infty$, globally $c\omega$-psh for rational $c>1$. Taking its
finite maximum and then letting $c\downarrow1$ proves that weakly
psh chart data are genuinely global. The upper chart bound follows
from bounded-above usc $\varphi$ on the compact projective space.
This is W 6.5 with precisely its currently missing input exposed.

For a resolution $p:\widetilde X\to X$ of a geometrically unibranch
$X$, geometric fibers are connected: factor through the finite
normalization; the latter is a universal homeomorphism in this
domain, and a proper birational map to a normal target has connected
geometric fibers by Stein factorization. BJ 5.20 applies on the
smooth resolution to the **nef**, possibly nonample, $p^*\omega$.
Its envelope descends to an usc function constant on fibers by W
2.24. That proposition first proves constancy over divisorial
points of strata, where residue fields are discrete or trivial,
then uses flat-stratum analytic openness and divisorial determination
to pass to all points. In its zero-psh argument, $m\varphi$ is the
decreasing sequence for $\varphi\leq0$; the printed $-m\varphi$
has the wrong sign. Divisorial nonnegligibility identifies the
descended function with the downstairs usc supremum. It is weakly
psh. Only the missing weak/global identification would now make it
genuinely global downstairs. Fiberwise usc descent alone does not.

Suppose henceforth that the rational ample envelope theorem on
countable algebraically closed subfields **has been proved**. Let
the original $k$ have arbitrary cardinality. Descend the finite
data of $X$, $L$ and a rational PL obstacle $f$ to a countable
algebraically closed $k_1\subset k$. Each such trivial field is
complete. Reducedness and irreducibility descend by faithful field
extension. Over these perfect characteristic-zero fields, normal
finite-type algebras are geometrically normal; one can test $R_1$
and $S_2$ after a separable extension, whose local fibers are
regular. Consequently finite normalization commutes with the field
extension: the extended normalization is finite normal birational
and hence is the normalization. Multiple geometric branches would
persist after extension, so geometric unibranchness descends.
Ampleness descends by the same faithful flat test.

Set $u_1=P_{\omega_1}(f_1)$, continuous and global by the assumed
countable theorem. Pull back to $u=r_{k1}^*u_1$. Model approximants
show $u$ is global psh and $u\leq f$. For $0\leq t\leq1$, the
envelope of $u_1+t(f_1-u_1)$ is $u_1$: the obstacle lies between
$u_1$ and $f_1$. The genuine BJ continuous energy derivative gives
$$\int(f_1-u_1)\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_1}(u_1)=0.\tag{16}$$
Thus the measure is supported on the closed contact set.

A global model psh competitor $\psi\leq f$ descends to a larger
countable algebraically closed $k_2$, with $\psi=r_{k2}^*\psi_2$.
Relative ampleness and nefness descend by faithfully flat base
change. The restriction map $r_{21}$ is surjective: a multiplicative
seminorm extends to a scalar extension by passing to a completed
residue field extension, equivalently using BJ's restriction-map
section. Thus $\psi_2\leq f_2=r_{21}^*f_1$.

The genuine mixed measures satisfy
$$r_{21*}\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_2}(r_{21}^*u_1)
      =\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_1}(u_1).\tag{17}$$
For model tuples this is checked in (5). A regular field extension
preserves vertical $R_1$ and $S_2$: at vertical codimension-one
points regularity is stable under these separable extensions, and
depth with regular fibers preserves $S_2$. BJ's vertical criterion
therefore preserves relative integral closure while retaining the
given nonnormal generic fiber. Over an algebraically closed base,
vertical irreducible components are geometrically integral, so no
component splits. DVR multiplicities of $t$, divisorial restrictions,
and degrees of intersections are unchanged. Flat extension of
cycles gives identical weights in (5). Decrease model psh
approximants of $u_1$ and test continuous functions to obtain (17);
volumes also stay $V$. This argument never absolutely normalizes
away the generic fiber.

Positivity and (17) put the pulled-back measure on
$\{f_2=r_{21}^*u_1\}$. Hence $\psi_2\leq r_{21}^*u_1$ almost
everywhere for that measure. BJ Corollary 10.6 applies to this
**global** competitor on the singular irreducible variety and gives
the inequality everywhere. Supremizing model competitors, as in BJ
Lemma 5.19, proves $P_\omega(f)=r_{k1}^*P_{\omega_1}(f_1)$, hence
continuity. PL density and the envelope's sup-norm contraction
give all continuous obstacles. No BFJ Dirac equation is invoked on
a singular $k_2$-variety.

If this rational envelope antecedent were established, ample real
classes would follow by choosing rational ample $\omega_j\to\omega$
with $\omega_j-\omega$ ample. This is possible by openness of the
ample cone and rational density, perturbing $\omega+\epsilon_j A$.
The inclusions $\operatorname{PSH}(\omega)\subset
\operatorname{PSH}(\omega_j)$ and BJ's actual class closure make
the usc supremum $\omega$-psh. Thus real-class approximation and
cardinality removal are valid conditional passages, not replacements
for the failed rational countable base.

# 7. The exact envelope-to-equation proof

We prove Theorem B and keep its antecedent throughout.

## 7.1. Energy condition and necessity

For a Radon probability $\mu$, (1) is a convex weakly lsc functional;
BJ Proposition 9.4 computes the same supremum on genuine model
potentials. Finite dual energy makes every finite-energy test
integrable and excludes pluripolar mass. Conversely, if every such
test is integrable, integration is uniformly bounded on normalized
$J$-bounded sets. Otherwise choose $J(\psi_j)\leq C$ with
$\int\psi_j\,d\mu\leq-2^j$. The decreasing partial sums
$\sum_{j\leq m}2^{-j}\psi_j$, completed by a zero term to a convex
combination, have energy at least $-C$, so their limit is in
$\mathcal E^1$ but has integral $-\infty$, a contradiction.

For $0\leq t\leq1$, the actual mixed expansion yields
$$I(t\psi,0)\leq t[1-(1-t)^n]I(\psi,0)\leq nt^2I(\psi,0).$$
Together with $J\asymp I(\cdot,0)$ this gives
$J(t\psi)\leq C_n t^2J(\psi)$. Normalize $\sup\psi=0$ and
take $t=J(\psi)^{-1/2}$ when $J\geq1$. Uniform integration on
the rescaled bounded-$J$ set gives
$-\int\psi\,d\mu\leq A\sqrt{J(\psi)}+B$. Subtracting $J(\psi)$
proves finite dual energy. This is BJ Theorem 9.20 with the
necessary minus signs supplied by its own weighted-sum argument.
It does not require EP.

If $\mu=\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)$, mixed integrability and the
tangent inequality following (10) give
$$E(\psi)-\int\psi\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)
 \leq E(\varphi)-\int\varphi\operatorname{MA}(\varphi).$$
Equality holds at $\psi=\varphi$, so the right side is the finite
dual energy. For raw mass $V$, the correct objective is
$VE(\psi)-\int\psi\,d\nu=V(E(\psi)-\int\psi\,d\mu)$.
Translation invariance uses mass one after normalization.

## 7.2. Compactness and differentiation

By BJ Theorem 5.11, EP gives compactness of
$\operatorname{PSH}_{\sup}(\omega)$ for divisorial convergence.
Bounded divisorial values give a Tychonoff subnet; its usc tail
suprema are psh by EP. Divisorial nonnegligibility identifies those
values with ordinary tail suprema. Their decreasing limit has the
desired finite divisorial values and is globally psh. Continuity
of the supremum preserves normalization. This is compactness of
the genuine cone on $X$, not on a fixed skeleton or only upstairs.

BJ Theorem 8.5 proves on a general reduced projective variety,
uniformly in $\varphi\in\mathcal E^1$ and for $f\in\operatorname{PL}_{\mathbb R}$,
$$E^\downarrow(\varphi+\epsilon f)
 =E(\varphi)+\epsilon\int f\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)+O(\epsilon^2).\tag{18}$$
Its proof writes $f$ as a difference of auxiliary ample model
potentials, uses determinant-of-cohomology/sup-norm volumes,
and the mixed positive expansion with fixed masses. The volume
ratio $V_{\omega+\epsilon\omega'}/V_\omega-1$ is $O(\epsilon)$;
multiplication by $\epsilon$ gives the uniform quadratic error.
Decreasing approximation extends from continuous psh to every
finite-energy potential. Apply to $f$ and $-f$.

For continuous $f$, approximate uniformly by $f_m\in\operatorname{PL}_{\mathbb R}$,
with error $\eta_m$. The correct estimate is
$$|E^\downarrow(\varphi+tf)-E^\downarrow(\varphi+tf_m)|\leq|t|\eta_m.$$
The derivative is squeezed between
$\int f_m\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)-\eta_m$ and
$\int f_m\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)+\eta_m$, for either sign by
also using $-f$. Let $m\to\infty$. EP is used in BJ Proposition
8.3 to identify the upper extension with
$E(P_\omega(\varphi+tf))$ for unbounded $\varphi$; bounded shifts
ensure this envelope has finite energy. Thus
$$\left.\frac d{dt}\right|_{0}E(P_\omega(\varphi+tf))
       =\int f\operatorname{MA}_\omega(\varphi).\tag{19}$$
No multiplier ideals on singular $X$ are assumed in (18). The
envelope identity in (19) is conditional, as required.

## 7.3. Attainment and the actual equation

Put $F_\mu=E-\int\varphi\,d\mu$ and
$J_\mu=E^\vee(\mu)-F_\mu$. Choose a model maximizing sequence,
normalized by $\sup\varphi_i=0$. Strict concavity at the midpoint
with $0$ gives BJ Lemma 9.15:
$$J(\varphi_i)\leq C_n(J_\mu(\varphi_i)+E^\vee(\mu)).$$
It has eventually bounded $J$. Compactness gives a weakly
converging subnet. Lower semicontinuity of $J$ puts the limit in
$\mathcal E^1$.

The fixed target integration step is BJ Proposition 9.19, not weak
testing on continuous functions: weak psh convergence with bounded
$J$ implies $L^1(\mu)$ convergence. Its noncircular proof chooses
maximizing model potentials $\rho_j$; Legendre duality and (18)
give $\operatorname{MA}(\rho_j)\to\mu$ **before** attainment.
Lemma 9.17 bounds the target integration error uniformly on a
bounded-$J$ set by a constant times $J_\mu(\rho_j)^{1/2}$.
Each model measure is a finite sum of divisorial evaluations, so
its integration is weakly continuous there. Uniform limits give
target-integral convergence. Apply this also to
$\max(\varphi_i,\varphi)$, whose $J$ stays bounded. The exact
identity is
$$|\varphi_i-\varphi|=2\max(\varphi_i,\varphi)-\varphi_i-\varphi.$$
This proves $L^1$ convergence. Energy usc then gives
$F_\mu(\varphi)\geq\limsup F_\mu(\varphi_i)=E^\vee(\mu)$,
and hence attainment.

For any maximizing net with deficit $\delta_i$, the Legendre
inequality and (18) give for $\epsilon>0$
$$\epsilon\int f(\operatorname{MA}(\varphi_i)-\mu)
       \leq\delta_i+C_f\epsilon^2.$$
Divide by $\epsilon$, pass to the net limit, let $\epsilon\downarrow0$,
and repeat with $-f$. PL density proves convergence of the
**constructed genuine** measures to $\mu$; a constant maximizing
net at the attained potential proves $\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)=\mu$.
This is BJ 9.11--9.12. Necessity and uniqueness were proved before
EP. Multiplying by $V$ gives the raw equation.

## 7.4. Completeness and why the antecedent is essential

BJ Theorem 12.4 identifies strong convergence with divisorial weak
convergence plus convergence of energy, and $I$ defines it modulo
constants. On the full space add
$|\sup\varphi-\sup\psi|$. Theorem 10.14 proves completeness of
the finite-dual-energy target space with quasimetric
$$I^\vee(\mu,\mu')=\inf_\rho(J_\mu(\rho)+J_{\mu'}(\rho))$$
without EP. Proposition 12.7 gives a bi-Lipschitz embedding of
$(\mathcal E^1/\mathbb R,I)$ into that space. Surjectivity just
proved makes it an isomorphism of complete spaces. Cauchy suprema
give completeness of normalized/full potentials.

For the converse, completeness makes the bounded increasing net
of finite maxima of model competitors below a continuous obstacle
$f$ converge: include a constant lower bound, so all competitors
are uniformly bounded and genuinely in $\mathcal E^1$. For
$\varphi_i\leq\varphi_j$, (10) gives
$$0\leq\int(\varphi_j-\varphi_i)\operatorname{MA}(\varphi_i)
 \leq(n+1)(E(\varphi_j)-E(\varphi_i)).$$
Hence $J_{\varphi_i}(\varphi_j)\leq n(E(\varphi_j)-E(\varphi_i))\to0$.
Completeness gives a psh limit. Its divisorial values and the
continuous obstacle identify it with the model supremum envelope.
BJ Lemma 5.19 supplies its lower semicontinuity, so it is continuous;
the continuous-obstacle characterization gives EP. These are the
actual general-variety implications of BJ Theorem 12.8. In the
forward implication its printed Corollary 5.21 citation must be
the general Theorem 5.11 once EP is assumed. The smooth sufficient
theorem 12.9 is not an unconditional singular theorem.

# 8. Nonnormal unibranch normalization and a nodal obstruction

For a reduced irreducible geometrically unibranch finite-type variety,
normalization $q:X^\nu\to X$ is finite birational of degree one.
The geometric unibranch condition makes every geometric fiber a
single point with purely inseparable residue extension; in
characteristic zero it is an equality. Thus it is a universal
homeomorphism, and its analytic seminorms lift uniquely, so
$q^{\rm an}$ is a homeomorphism. This topological assertion does
not prove global psh descent.

One-way pullback is unconditional. Projection formula gives
$(q^*\omega)^n=V$, and BJ Proposition 7.2 gives
$$E_{q^*\omega}(q^*\varphi)=E_\omega(\varphi).$$
Consequently downstairs $\mathcal E^1$ pulls into upstairs
$\mathcal E^1$. BJ Proposition 7.19 gives the actual mixed identity
$$q_*\bigwedge_i(q^*\omega_i+dd^cq^*\varphi_i)
       =\bigwedge_i(\omega_i+dd^c\varphi_i).\tag{20}$$
It follows from model projection formula and decreasing pairing
continuity, or from (7) and PL density. These do not require EP.

Actual **onto** pullback needs more: BJ Lemma 5.13 assumes a sequence
$\omega_m>\omega$ tending to $\omega$, each with EP. Under that
antecedent it uses small pulled-back logarithmic ideal corrections,
increasing closure in the larger classes, and divisorial determination
to descend every upstairs global psh function. Equal energies then
give onto $\mathcal E^1$. Combined with the analytic homeomorphism,
onto pullback would also give equal dual energies and correspondence
of every target. These onto assertions remain conditional here;
the missing singular envelope premise cannot be replaced by the
homeomorphism.

There is an unconditional obstruction outside the unibranch domain.
Let $C$ be an irreducible rational projective nodal curve, $q:\mathbb P^1\to C$
its normalization, and $p_1\ne p_2$ the preimages of the node.
Let $d=\deg L>0$ and $v=\operatorname{ord}_{p_1}$ on the common
function field. It is a divisorial point downstairs, and
$\delta_v$ has finite dual energy by BJ Proposition 11.1.

Blow up $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1$ at $(p_1,0)$, with exceptional
$E$. The model class
$$p^*(q^*L)+d\mathcal X_0-dE$$
has degrees $0$ and $d$ on the two central components, both with
multiplicity one. It is relatively nef: every vertical curve is a
central component and has the indicated nonnegative degree. Its
genuine raw measure by (5) is $d\delta_v$. Its bounded continuous
psh model potential is
$$g(s\operatorname{ord}_{p_1})=d\max(1-s,0),\qquad
g(s\operatorname{ord}_p)=d\quad(p\ne p_1).$$
In particular its two endpoints at $p_1,p_2$ have values $0,d$.

If a downstairs finite-energy solution to $d\delta_v$ existed,
its pullback would be finite-energy by the unconditional energy
identity. By (20), positivity and equal mass force its upstairs
measure to be $d\delta_v$, since a valuation of the common function
field has a singleton analytic lift. Upstairs uniqueness on the
smooth irreducible curve makes its pullback $g+c$. A pullback from
$C$ has the same value at the two endpoints mapping to the node;
$g+c$ differs by $d>0$. Contradiction. This is an actual finite-energy
nonsolvable target for the genuine source operator, also reflected
in BJ Example 12.11. It does not depend on the missing unibranch EP.

For comparison, the cuspidal cubic $y^2z=x^3$ has normalization
$[u:v]\mapsto[u^2v:u^3:v^3]$, finite birational with one geometric
point in every fiber, and $q^*\mathcal O_C(1)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(3)$.
The same upstairs blowup has central degrees $0,3$ and genuine
mass $3\delta_{\operatorname{ord}_{u=0}}$. This upstairs computation
does not prove onto psh descent downstairs. No general cuspidal
existence theorem is inferred from it in this paper.

# 9. Exact disposition and reproducibility

The global model/intersection finite-energy theory, necessary target
energy condition, and uniqueness/comparison replacements are
established in the retained projective ample domain by BJ. The
full existence-and-uniqueness theorem is established here only
with its actual EP antecedent, and unconditionally in the smooth
domain where BJ proves EP. The direct smooth extension has been
repaired noncircularly under the corrected upper-growth convention.
The entropy barrier and its local rational PL approximation are
independently proved. The singular norm step over $H(s)$ still
has the concrete gap in Section 5. The supporting replacement now
includes common-exhaustion and actual affine FS regularization,
but does not remove that norm antecedent. Therefore the rational
countable-field singular EP,
then unconditional arbitrary-cardinality/real-class singular
existence and onto normalization, remain unproved in this assignment.

No claim is made about other valuations, positive characteristic,
nonample classes, nonprojective proper spaces, or nonalgebraizable
analytic spaces. Arbitrary support in Theorem B is genuine arbitrary
support with (1), not replacement by a skeleton. Irreducibility gives
one constant; in the broader equidimensional source scope the
ambiguity is locally constant, and component masses are their
intersection volumes. Disconnected or multibranch varieties do
not acquire an asserted one-constant onto theorem.

## Explicit conventions, rather than literal faulty formulas

Definition3.1(3), actual page14, writes f(x)=l(x)+C but only f(x')>=l(x').
Read literally this holds locally for every continuous real function: choose
a constant lower bound l and set C=f(x)-l. The intended supporting-affine
condition must instead be f(x')>=l(x')+C with equality at x. This correction
is necessary whenever using the genuine convex/psh classes. A proof using
literal membership in the faulty class cannot establish global psh membership.
The corrected condition on finite coordinate charts is ordinary convexity.
At a coordinate +infinity the supporting affine function has coefficient
zero in that coordinate, as required for real-valued continuous affine data.
Every use of a boundary support or rational PL approximation still needs its
actual proof; the correction alone does not supply it.

Proposition3.28's actual page22 defines a metric maximum containing log|s0|,
then identifies v=-log||s0|| with a maximum lacking the corresponding zero
term and concludes v(x)<0. With that unshifted term v>=0, so the displayed
construction cannot be literal. It also changes the semipositive model form
from omega to theta but subsequently calls a theta potential an omega
potential. The following bookkeeping repairs both defects.

## Proposed corrected bounded local construction

Take rational M>0 with |u|<=M on the chosen compact neighborhood. Select
rational coordinate inequalities log|f_i|<C_i, with f_i=s_i/s0 on an affine
chart and x strictly inside every inequality, and add enough sections to
define a globally generated ample mL. A generating list suffices; it need
not simultaneously be a prescribed basis. Use an actual FS metric whose
local potential is

    v=(1/m) max(-mC0, log|f_1|-mC_1,...,log|f_r|-mC_r),

with C0>0 and all thresholds chosen so v(x)<-epsilon<0. The constant floor
is negative, not zero. Make Q={v<=0} compact and contained in the original
open set, and P={v<=-epsilon}. The ample class is unchanged, but its
semipositive model form is theta. Write theta=omega+ddc p for the actual
global rational model-metric difference p, retaining this difference below.

Choose rational A>(2M+1)/epsilon and put h=A v+M+1, a local potential of
A theta up to its rational additive constant. Define

    D N(u)(s)=log max_beta(exp(sum_i beta_i w_i)|a_beta(s)|).

    N(u)=max_beta(D^(-1)log|a_beta|+D^(-1)sum_i beta_i w_i).

    u(y)>=eps_j log|g_j(pi(y))|-M_j.

of sup-normalized potentials at all divisorial points. BJ 4.22 supplies
their minimal usc extension, proving global equality. Domination follows
by applying this to $\max(\phi,\psi)$: its $I$ with $\psi$ is nonpositive
under the a.e. inequality, hence zero. These are global statements, not
local/global identification on singular analytic charts.

For a raw positive Radon target $\nu$, its prescribed mass is exactly $V$.
Put $\mu=\nu/V$. The finite-dual-energy condition is
$$E_\omega^\vee(\mu)=\sup_{\psi\in\mathcal E^1}
       \left(E_\omega(\psi)-\int\psi\,d\mu\right)<\infty.\tag{3.3}$$
The supremum can be computed on genuine model potentials. BJ 9.20, in
its irreducible scope, proves equivalence with integrability of every
$\mathcal E^1$ potential. A sign in its printed integration bound must
be negative: sup-normalized potentials are nonpositive. To check the
proof, unbounded integration on a $J$-bounded set would give
$J(\psi_j)\le C$ and $\int\psi_j\,d\mu\le-2^j$. The weighted decreasing
sum $\sum2^{-j}\psi_j$ has energy at least $-C$ by concavity but integral
$-\infty$, a contradiction. The mixed expansion gives, for $0\le t\le1$,
$$I(t\psi,0)\le t[1-(1-t)^n]I(\psi,0)\le nt^2 I(\psi,0).$$
Since $J$ and $I(\cdot,0)$ are comparable, scaling by $t=J(\psi)^{-1/2}$
yields $-\int\psi\,d\mu\le A\sqrt{J(\psi)}+B$ and (3.3).
For a solution, the mixed energy tangent inequality gives attainment of
(3.3) at the solution, so necessity is unconditional. This characterization
of targets does not assert surjectivity.

**Conditional equation theorem.** Assume the genuine envelope property for
$\omega$: usc suprema of nonempty bounded-above global psh families are
psh. Then every target (3.3), with arbitrary support, is the MA of an
$\mathcal E^1$ potential, unique modulo one constant. Conversely,
surjectivity for every such target implies EP. This is BJ Theorem 12.8,
not its smooth sufficient corollary 12.9.

Here are the operative steps. General Theorem 5.11 supplies compactness of
sup-zero PSH for divisorial convergence. The uniform energy derivative,
Theorem 8.5, is
$$E^\downarrow(\phi+\epsilon f)
 =E(\phi)+\epsilon\int f\operatorname{MA}(\phi)+O_f(\epsilon^2),\quad f\in\operatorname{PL}_{\mathbb R},$$
uniformly for $\phi\in\mathcal E^1$. The source uses model-volume
calculations and mixed positive expansions, not smoothness of $X$.
Apply it to both signs. Uniform approximation of a continuous direction
by PL directions changes energy by at most $|t|\eta$, so the two-sided
derivative is $\int f\operatorname{MA}(\phi)$. EP is required in 8.3 to
identify this with $E(P_\omega(\phi+tf))$ for an unbounded potential.

Normalize a maximizing sequence for (3.3) by $\sup\phi_j=0$. Strict
concavity and BJ 9.15 bound $J$; compactness gives a weak subnet and
lower semicontinuity puts its limit in $\mathcal E^1$. Proposition 9.19
supplies $L^1(\mu)$ convergence on bounded-$J$ sets. Its proof first
approximates $\mu$ by maximizing **model** MA measures using 9.11, before
attainment: the Legendre inequality and uniform derivative imply their
weak convergence. The uniform target-integration estimate 9.17 then
makes integration a uniform limit of divisorial finite-sum evaluations.
For absolute values the correct identity is
$$|\phi_j-\phi|=2\max(\phi_j,\phi)-\phi_j-\phi.$$
Energy usc gives attainment. At an attained maximum, the Legendre
inequality, the derivative, and tests $f,-f$ force the actual constructed
MA measure to be $\mu$. The raw objective is $VE-\int\phi\,d\nu$;
translation invariance uses precisely mass $V$.

BJ 10.14 supplies target-space completeness before EP; 12.7 supplies the
MA embedding. Surjectivity makes the embedding onto and supplies strong
$\mathcal E^1$ completeness. Conversely completeness applied to bounded
increasing finite maxima of model competitors gives continuous envelopes:
for $\phi_i\le\phi_j$, the mixed energy formula bounds the relevant
$J_{\phi_i}(\phi_j)$ by $n(E(\phi_j)-E(\phi_i))$. The net is Cauchy,
and divisorial values identify its psh limit with the model supremum.
This explains why solving only EP-to-equation cannot complete the task.

For $n=0$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}k$, $V=1$, potentials are constants and
$E(c)=c$; the equation is immediate. No $n\ge1$ estimate is applied there.

# 4. Determinants and the exact affine FS trace

Let $B$ be finite locally free of rank $d$ over integral affine $A$.
For an analytic base point $s$, $F=H(s)$ and
$$B_s=B\otimes_A F=\prod_{y\mid s}B_{s,y},\qquad
 m_y=\dim_F B_{s,y},\qquad \sum m_y=d.$$
The residue field of $B_{s,y}$ is $H(y)$, finite over $F$, with its unique
extended absolute value. Nilpotents are retained. Set $N(u)(s)=\sum m_yu(y)$.

Take $u=\max(\lambda,c_i\log|f_i|+C_i)\in\operatorname{AFS}(B)$.
Choose an integer $D$ clearing all $c_i$, and put
$$b_0=1,\quad b_i=f_i^{Dc_i},\quad w_0=D\lambda,\quad w_i=DC_i.$$
The determinant of multiplication by $\sum_iT_i b_i$ on $B$ is a scalar
endomorphism of the invertible module $\det_A B$. It is therefore a
basis-independent homogeneous polynomial
$$P(T)=\det(\operatorname{mult}_{\sum T_i b_i})
       =\sum_{|\beta|=d}a_\beta T^\beta\in A[T],\qquad a_{(d,0,\ldots,0)}=1.$$
No global free basis is required.

Extend $F$ to a complete algebraically closed valued extension. The finite
algebra becomes a product of local Artinian algebras with residue field
the extension. On a local factor of length $\ell$, a composition series
has $\ell$ residue-field quotients, and multiplication acts on each by
$\sum T_i b_i(x)$. Thus
$$P_s(T)=\prod_x\left(\sum_i T_i b_i(x)\right)^{\ell_x}.$$
The sum of geometric lengths above an original $y$ is $m_y$, including
its residue degree. In characteristic zero this also follows by splitting
the separable residue field into its $[H(y):F]$ embeddings. The characteristic
zero case is all that is required here.

For arbitrary positive real radii $r_i$, the weighted Gauss norm
$\|\sum a_\beta T^\beta\|_r=\max|a_\beta|r^\beta$ is multiplicative.
One elementary proof chooses the lexicographically largest exponent among
terms attaining each maximum. At the sum of those exponents, no other
pair of maximal terms can contribute: in the additive lexicographic
order each is at most the chosen term, and equality of the sum forces
both equalities. Contributions of smaller norm cannot cancel the unique
maximal contribution. The resulting coefficient has the product norm;
the opposite inequality is the non-Archimedean triangle inequality.
This proof requires no value-group restriction on the $r_i$. Isometric
scalar extension preserves the norm term by term.

Applying this with $r_i=e^{w_i}$ gives the exact identity
$$DN(u)(s)=\log\max_\beta e^{\sum_i\beta_iw_i}|a_\beta(s)|,$$
$$N(u)=\max_\beta\left\{D^{-1}\log|a_\beta|
                         +D^{-1}\sum_i\beta_iw_i\right\}.\tag{4.1}$$
The $T_0^d$ coefficient supplies the rational floor $d\lambda$.
All intercepts and positive log coefficients are rational; zero
coefficients can be discarded. Cancellation of determinant coefficients
has already been handled by Gauss multiplicativity.

The coefficients $a_\beta$ need not generate $A$. Choose generators $g_j$
of $A$ over the ground field and write their images as polynomials in the
$f_i$. If $c_* =\min c_i$ and $C_* =\min C_i$, then, with
$Q=\max(0,\log|f_i|)$, one has $u\ge c_* Q-M_0$ for a rational $M_0$
large enough to cover the floor and constants. A polynomial expression
of degree $e_j$ gives
$$\log|g_j\circ\pi|\le e_j Q+L_j,$$
where $L_j$ is the logarithm of a coefficient bound. Choose positive
rational $\eta_j$ with $\eta_je_j\le c_*$, and rational $M_j$ large
enough that $u\ge\eta_j\log|g_j\circ\pi|-M_j$ globally. Coefficients
of a nontrivially valued field merely change $M_j$. Summing on a fiber,
$$N(u)\ge d\eta_j\log|g_j|-dM_j.$$
Add these dominated branches to (4.1). Its value is unchanged and its
list now generates $A$. This proves actual AFS membership, not just
local tropical-PL membership.

For $u_j\downarrow u$, a fixed finite fiber sum gives $N(u_j)\downarrow
N(u)$, including a $-\infty$ summand. Every sequence is locally bounded
above by its first AFS term, so no $+\infty-\infty$ occurs. For an upper
growth bound on a finite map, the source coordinates are integral over
$A$. A monic equation gives the root estimate
$$\log|f_i(y)|\le\max_{j<m}\frac{\log|a_j(s)|}{m-j}$$
(the zero root is harmless). Otherwise the leading power would strictly
dominate all other terms and could not sum to zero. Polynomial bounds
in base generators therefore give an upper base-FS growth bound. To
call the trace limit generically finite, one must verify that it is finite
on the required base component; for example, it suffices that it be finite
at all points of one fiber. We do not infer this merely from a formal sum.

If $u$ is finite continuous and $u_j$ are continuous AFS approximants,
Dini on each compact inverse image gives uniform convergence there.
The trace error is at most $d$ times that error. Formula (4.1) shows the
approximant traces continuous, so the trace is continuous. Such compact
sets exist locally on the analytic base, and the finite map is proper.
Dini is used only for a continuous finite limit.

**Trace base change without approximation.** At any scalar-extended base
point $s'$ above $s$, the fiber algebra is $B_s\otimes_FH(s')$.
Its decomposition is obtained by splitting the original idempotents and
then its local factors. For each original factor, dimensions over
$H(s')$ sum to $\dim_F B_{s,y}$. Every refined analytic point restricts
to $y$, since the residue-field norm extension is unique. Hence for any
function $a$ on the original fiber,
$$\sum_{x\mapsto y}\dim_{H(s')}B'_{s',x}=m_y,\qquad
 N_{\pi'}(a\circ r)(s')=N_\pi(a)(s).\tag{4.2}$$
This is an identity of finite Artinian algebras, not a consequence of
regularizable-function base change. It also applies to a finite map
without flatness when using its actual fiber dimensions at that point.


**Worked Artinian-fiber check.** For $A=k[a]$, $B=k[z]$, $a=z^2$,
let $u=\max(\log|z|,-1)$. Its norm polynomial is
$P=T_0^2-aT_1^2$, with radii $(e^{-1},1)$, and formula (4.1) gives
$N(u)=\max(-2,\log|a|)$. At $a=0$ the fiber is $k[z]/(z^2)$,
one point with Artinian dimension two; $2u(0)=-2$ agrees. If the fiber
splits away from zero, two dimension-one points give the same formula;
if it stays a degree-two residue field, its single dimension-two point
also gives it because $|z|=|a|^{1/2}$. Thus neither nilpotent lengths nor
residue degrees can be replaced by an unweighted point count. This is
an exact algebraic example, not an exhaustive finite computation.

# 5. Radial curve boundary equality

Let $F$ be complete with residue characteristic zero, $C$ a smooth curve,
and $q:D\to C$ a finite map from a disjoint union of smooth curves, every
component dominating $C$. It is flat: over each target DVR its finite
module is torsion-free. Let $a\in C(F)$. Let $v$ and $w$ be usc functions
on neighborhoods of $a$ and $q^{-1}(a)$, respectively, with common-neighborhood
decreasing continuous CLD-psh approximants **after the scalar extension
used below**, allowing an identically
$-\infty$ restriction to a component. A sufficient explicit condition is
a decreasing sequence of rational convex PL weights on **ambient tropical
neighborhoods**, pulled back by regular functions. This includes every
actual AFS function and its supplied decreasing sequences. Suppose, on a punctured algebraic
neighborhood of $a$,
$$v(t)=\sum_{x\mid t}\dim_{H(t)}(\mathcal O(D)\otimes H(t))_x\,w(x).\tag{5.1}$$
Then the same equality holds at $a$.

To handle **all** $F$, first choose a complete algebraically closed
nontrivially valued extension $\Omega$. If $F$ is trivial, embed it in
$F((T))$, $|T|=e^{-1}$, then take the completed algebraic closure. If
$F$ is nontrivial, take its completed algebraic closure directly. Residue
characteristic remains zero. Wanner's terminology, LF168--174, is exactly
complete, algebraically closed, nontrivially valued; normalizing its
valuation scale to contain $\mathbb Q$ does not affect the assertions.
The geometric fiber now consists of rational points, and (4.2) transports
(5.1) without any assumption of FS regularizability of $w$.

For the stated sufficient tropical condition, the common-neighborhood PL
sequences pull back under scalar extension and under algebraic maps: pull
back the regular functions defining the tropical chart and retain the same
convex PL expression on its ambient neighborhood. Near a zero coordinate a
finite PL expression is independent of it on a smaller neighborhood.
On the remaining finite coordinates its finite convex affine maximum has
smooth convex regularized maxima converging uniformly on compact subsets.
The $d'd''$ Hessian is positive. This proves continuous PL approximants
are CLD-psh in Wanner Definition 3.16; continuity supplies local
integrability. Wanner Section 4, LF800--805, additionally fixes a **smooth proper**
algebraic curve. Complete each component of the smooth $\Omega$-curves
by the normal projective model of its function field; a regular curve
over the perfect $\Omega$ is smooth, and the original smooth curve is
an open subcurve (its local DVR points identify this open immersion).
Thus the local neighborhoods are open subsets of smooth proper curves,
exactly the scope of Theorem 4.7. No extension of the functions across
the added boundary is necessary. The theorem applies to each continuous
approximant on those open subsets and makes it Thuillier-subharmonic.
Thuillier 3.1.8(ii) passes to the decreasing limit on each connected
neighborhood: it is subharmonic or identically $-\infty$. This use does
not assert a general arbitrary-$F$ affine regularization theorem. For a
convex PL function specified only on a tropical image with local supports,
an ambient convex smoothing theorem is an extra antecedent: a local affine
support alone need not furnish such an extension. That full compatibility
of W's corrected local class is not proved here, and is a concrete remaining
condition for applying this sufficient boundary theorem to every weak input.

For a locally subharmonic $b$ at a rational center with disk coordinate $z$,
take sufficiently small closed disks, contained with a neighborhood in its
domain. Their boundary is the single Gauss point $\xi_R$ for
$R\in|\Omega^*|$. The constant harmonic comparison of Th 3.1.5 gives
$$b(0)\le b(\xi_R).$$
For a nontrivial subharmonic function the latter is finite by Th 3.1.9.
Since $\xi_R\to0$, usc gives $\limsup_{R\to0}b(\xi_R)\le b(0)$.
Thus $b(\xi_R)\to b(0)$, also if $b(0)=-\infty$. The identically
$-\infty$ case is immediate. This is convergence along these boundary
Gauss points, **not** an ordinary punctured-neighborhood limit. For example,
logarithmic singularities at other closed points obstruct such ordinary
limits even for otherwise well-behaved subharmonic functions.

Choose a target coordinate $t$ at $a$ and coordinates $z_i$ at the finitely
many source centers. On sufficiently small disks a finite curve map has
$$t=c_i z_i^{e_i}U_i(z_i),\quad c_i\ne0,\quad U_i(0)=1,$$
where $e_i$ is the order of $q^*t$ in the source DVR. Shrink until
$|U_i-1|<1$. Because residue characteristic is zero, $|e_i|=1$ and
all rational binomial coefficients have norm at most one. The convergent
binomial series $U_i^{1/e_i}$ gives $y_i=z_iU_i^{1/e_i}$.
This is an invertible coordinate on a smaller disk: its linear term is
one, and its higher coefficients become strictly smaller than the
linear term after shrinking; successive substitution is a contraction.
Thus $t=c_i y_i^{e_i}$ there.

For a target Gauss radius $r\in|\Omega^*|$ small enough, each such disk
has exactly one preimage Gauss point of radius
$R_i=(r/|c_i|)^{1/e_i}\in|\Omega^*|$. Rescale both coordinates to unit
radius. The residue equation is $Y^{e_i}=\alpha T$, $\alpha\ne0$,
which is irreducible over $\widetilde\Omega(T)$ by Eisenstein at $T$.
Consequently the Gauss norm on $\Omega(y_i)$ lies above that on
$\Omega(t)$ with residue degree $e_i$. Since the entire map on these
disks has degree $e_i$, the actual analytic fiber algebra is a single
field of dimension $e_i$; there are neither additional points nor
hidden nilpotent lengths at this generic Gauss point. At $t=0$ its
local Artinian dimension is also $e_i$.

Properness is essential to exclude preimages outside the chosen disks.
Take open disks inside the coordinate neighborhoods; the complement is
closed and its image under the finite map is closed, avoiding $a$.
A small target neighborhood therefore has all preimages in these disks.
Choose $r$ further small so its Gauss point avoids the finite algebraic
exceptional set in (5.1). Then
$$v(\xi_r)=\sum_i e_i w(\xi_{R_i}).$$
The separate radial limits give the assertion at $a$. If a summand tends
to $-\infty$, usc bounds the others above near their centers, so the
finite sum tends to $-\infty$. There is no subtraction of infinities.
Finally aggregate the geometric Artinian dimensions by (4.2) to return
to the original $F$-fiber. This proves the boundary equality used here.

# 6. Slicing weights, an independent norm extension, and proper lifting

In this section $k$ is algebraically closed trivially valued of
characteristic zero, $Y$ integral affine of dimension $n$, and $S$ affine
open in $\mathbb A^n$, with a finite surjection $\pi$. Write $A=k[S]$,
$B=k[Y]$, $K=\operatorname{Frac}A$, $L=\operatorname{Frac}B$, and $d=[L:K]$.
Choose a dense principal etale open $W\subset S$.

For $s\in S^{\rm an}$ set $F=H(s)$ and take its rational lift $s'$ in
$S_F$. Choose a line through $s'$ generically in $W_F$, intersected with
$S_F$. Such a line exists because $F$ is infinite and finitely many
nonzero polynomial conditions cannot vanish on every direction. In the
scheme preimage, the line is cut by $n-1$ linear equations. $Y_F$ is
integral of dimension $n$ because $Y/k$ is geometrically integral.
The principal-ideal height bound and the dimension formula for affine
finite-type algebras give every component dimension at least one;
finiteness gives dimension at most one. There are no zero-dimensional
minimal components. Reduce this preimage and normalize it to $D$.
Every component dominates the smooth line $C$, so $q:D\to C$ is finite
flat. Normal curves over the perfect characteristic-zero $F$ are smooth.
Its generic fiber lies in the etale locus, where neither reduction nor
normalization changes it; hence its rank is $d$. Every point over $s'$
is covered by normalization. Summing its positive Artinian dimensions
over points restricting to an original analytic $y\mid s$ defines positive
integer weights $m_{y,C}$ with total $d$.

We now prove independence without invoking an unproved smooth removable
extension. For any global $h\in\operatorname{AFS}(B)$, form the homogeneous
polynomial of Section 4 as the field norm $N_{L/K}(\sum_iT_i b_i)$.
Each $b_i$ is integral over $A$, so $\sum T_i b_i$ is integral over
$A[T]$. All coefficients of its characteristic polynomial are integral
over $A[T]$. The norm polynomial belongs to $K[T]$, and $A[T]$ is
normal because $A$ is a localization of a polynomial algebra over $k$.
Thus its coefficients are in $A[T]$. This algebraic argument extends
(4.1) across the nonflat locus. The base-generator bound of Section 4
makes the resulting maximum $v_h$ an actual global AFS function on $S$.
On $W$ it is the determinant trace of $h$.

Pull $v_h$ and $h$ to the normalized slicing curve. They are continuous
locally convex PL, and (4.2) gives their trace equality on $C\cap W_F$.
The radial theorem of Section 5 yields
$$v_h(s)=\sum_{y\mid s}m_{y,C}h(y).\tag{6.1}$$
The field-norm polynomial depends only on $\pi$ and $h$, not $W$ or $C$.
Consequently all slicing choices have identical integrals against every
actual global AFS test.

These tests determine each individual weight. Their differences form the
rational lattice of Section 2 and contain rational constants. Distinct
analytic fiber points have different regular-function seminorms; choose
$f$ distinguishing them and truncate $\log|f|$ below a rational floor
small enough to retain the distinction. Add low generator branches so
that they are dominated at both points. This is an actual AFS function
separating the pair. On a finite set the closure of this rational lattice
is all real-valued functions: for a pair of points, affine rescaling of a
separating function interpolates two prescribed values in the closure;
finite minima and maxima then give a function with every prescribed
finite-set value. Real rescaling is obtained as a limit of rational
rescalings. Equality of integrals therefore implies equality against
each indicator function. Hence $m_{y,C}$ is independent of both choices;
write it $m_y(\pi)$. On $W$ it agrees with (6.1)'s actual finite-flat
fiber dimension. At a nonflat fiber these weights need **not** be called
the dimensions of the original nonflat fiber algebra; they are the fixed
normalized slicing degrees, totaling the generic degree $d$.

For a supplied decreasing AFS sequence $h_j\downarrow h$, $v_{h_j}$ are
actual AFS functions and their fixed finite-fiber sums decrease to
$\sum m_yh(y)$. Thus finite-surjective trace regularization is proved for
an already globally regularizable input, provided its limit is generically
finite. If the input is finite continuous, the compact uniform convergence
argument of Section 4 proves continuity of this trace too. This is a new
partial strengthening of the finite-flat calculation in the original
normal-target situation; it proves no weak-input approximation by itself.

Now suppose $u$ is weakly local psh, witnessed by a proper birational
$\mu:\widetilde Y\to Y$, and suppose that a genuine globally regularizable
$v$ on $S$ has already been constructed agreeing with its trace on a
suitable etale open. Shrink that open so $\mu$ is an isomorphism over its
preimage. This is possible: the bad image is a proper closed subset of
$Y$; its image under finite $\pi$ is closed of dimension less than $n$.
Choose the slicing line **after** this shrink. Each normalized curve
component then has a generic lift through the inverse of $\mu$. Properness
extends this generic lift across every closed point of the smooth curve
by the valuative criterion for its DVRs; separatedness glues the extensions.
Its pullback $w$ is now an actual decreasing local convex-PL limit, or
identically $-\infty$ on a restricted component. If these restrictions
have the continuous CLD-psh approximants of Section 5 (in particular the
ambient-convex sufficient condition), the radial theorem proves
$$v(s)=\sum_{y\mid s}m_y(\pi)u(y).$$
The preceding independence proof ensures these weights did not depend on
this $u$-specific choice of line. A curve chosen before the witness need
not lift; no such assertion is used.

In Wu 6.12 the preceding extension depends on a smooth weakly-psh
upper-growth base and its removable extension. Those antecedents are
not reconstructed in full here. The missing arbitrary-$H(s)$ affine
regularization in its printed Step 3 can be avoided for this equality under
the explicit curve-positivity condition,
without asserting a stronger arbitrary-field theorem. For instance,
$Y=\operatorname{Spec}k[a,b,y,z]/(b^2-a^3)\to\operatorname{Spec}k[a,y,z]$
is finite flat of degree two. At a point with kernel $(a)$ and weights
$v(y)=1,v(z)=\sqrt2$, $H(s)$ contains the nondiscrete group
$\mathbb Z+\sqrt2\mathbb Z$. The line $a=\tau$ has normalized preimage
$\tau=z_0^2,b=z_0^3$ over that field. Sections 5--6 apply to its
boundary equality when the stated curve-positivity antecedent holds; they do not prove arbitrary-$H(s)$ FS regularization.

# 7. Explicit model-PL collars and their positivity

Let $X$ be the projective closure of an affine embedding $Y$, $L$ a very
ample rationally weighted basepoint-free FS reference with metric $\theta$,
and $s_0$ the distinguished section such that $Y=X\setminus Z(s_0)$.
Assume $g=-\log\|s_0\|_\theta\ge0$ is an actual proper affine FS
exhaustion. The standard coordinate closure with the trivial reference
has $g=\max(0,\log|z_1|,\ldots,\log|z_r|)$. More general rational FS
embedding references work with the same formulas.

Take a global actual affine FS expression
$$a=\max(\lambda,q_j\log|f_j|+A_j),\qquad f_j=F_j/s_0^{d_j},\quad
 F_j\in H^0(X,d_jL).$$
The homogeneous expression is obtained from a polynomial in the affine
embedding coordinates; no arbitrary analytic function is homogenized.
Fix rational $c>0,C,R>0$ and assume $a-cg\le C$ on the entire shell
$R\le g\le R+1$. Define $\psi=\max(a-cg,C)$ for $g\le R$, and $C$
for $g\ge R$, including $Z(s_0)^{\rm an}$.

Here is its finite **algebraic** model-PL formula. Set
$$G=\min(g,R+1)=-\max(\log\|s_0\|_\theta,-R-1),$$
$$H_j=\max(\log\|F_j\|_{d_j\theta},-M_j).$$
These are genuine BJ model-PL functions. For example, if $\phi_\theta$ is
the FS metric's global potential relative to the trivial metric, then
$$H_j=\max(\log\|F_j\|_{\rm triv},d_j\phi_\theta-M_j)-d_j\phi_\theta.$$
After clearing rational weights and taking a suitable power, the first
maximum is an actual basepoint-free FS metric: its second branch contains
the complete basepoint-free system of the reference. The same argument
applies to $G$ with $s_0$. A lone untruncated section norm is not claimed
to be bounded PL. BJ Section 2.1 says the **rational** PL space is a vector
lattice. Its real span need not be closed under max, and is not used here.

Choose rational $M_j>0$ so that
$$-q_jM_j+(q_jd_j-c)G+A_j\le C\quad(0\le G\le R+1).$$
It suffices to check the two endpoints of this rational affine expression.
This is a uniform clipping threshold, including all zeros of $F_j$.
Set
$$A_* =\max\left(C,\lambda-cG,
              \{q_jH_j+(q_jd_j-c)G+A_j\}_j\right).$$
Signed rational coefficients are permitted by the rational vector space.
On $g\le R+1$, clipping changes no branch above the floor, hence
$A_*=\max(a-cg,C)$. Choose rational $K_*>0$ with
$C+K_*\ge\sup_X A_*$, and put
$$F_* =\min\{A_*,\ C+K_*(R+1-G)\}.\tag{7.1}$$
On $g\le R$ the second branch is at least $C+K_*$ and is inactive.
On the shell $A_*=C$ by hypothesis. On $g\ge R+1$, including the whole
boundary divisor, $G=R+1$ and the cap is exactly $C$, while $A_*\ge C$.
Thus $F_*=\psi$ everywhere. This checks each signed coefficient and every
boundary value. It proves model representation for this algebraic input;
it does not identify arbitrary analytic PL with equivariant model PL.

Locally on $Y$, adding $cg$ to $\psi$ gives $\max(a,C+cg)$ where the
inner expression is used. This is corrected locally convex PL. On the
shell it equals $C+cg$, and near the boundary the glued potential is
constant relative to the semipositive reference. Hence the metric is
locally $c\theta$-psh, including the seams. Changes of frame add a
logarithm of an invertible section ratio, an affine harmonic term.

We check that this local assertion makes the represented model metric nef
without a smooth-only equivalence. Clearing rational powers makes the
metric integral PL. For the collar metrics here, the local formulas are explicit finite
maxima of algebraic logarithms on ambient tropical neighborhoods. They
have smooth convex approximants; their uniform convergence on smaller compact
charts gives positive curvature currents. CLD 18.8.4 and 18.8.8 apply to
boundaryless separated analytifications; algebraic projective
analytifications have these properties, also when singular or nonnormal.
To read actual central curve degrees, represent the metric on a dominating
equivariant configuration and extend to $k((t))$, $|t|=e^{-1}$.

This extension really is the pulled-back metric at **every** analytic
point. BJ 2.1 expresses model PL as differences of rational flag-ideal
functions. An equivariant ideal has monomial generators $t^{-\ell}f$;
at any extended analytic $x$ its ideal norm is
$\max e^\ell|f(p(x))|$. This is precisely the pullback of (2.3), without
restricting $x$ to the Gauss section. Integral closure of a flag ideal
does not change this norm: a monic integral equation and the ultrametric
root bound give the inequality for every added integral element, and the
opposite inequality follows from containment. Tensor powers handle the
reference metric and differences. The formal completion of the
projective flat configuration over $k[[t]]$ is an admissible model of
the original generic fiber $X_{k((t))}$; nonnormality of that fiber has
not been normalized away.

CLD 18.8.6 transports positive curvature to this nontrivially valued field;
it imposes no nontriviality restriction on the original field. At each
integral central-fiber curve $Z$, formal reduction is surjective, so take
an analytic point reducing to its generic point. The closure of that
reduction point is $Z$. CLD 17.7.10 identifies $Z$ with the restricted
model line bundle as a residual pre-model. The $d=1$ case of 18.8.4
therefore gives $\deg(\mathcal L|_Z)\ge0$. If one passes to an
algebraically closed complete extension to obtain a reduction point,
projection formula multiplies the degree by a positive field/component
degree and yields the same inequality on $Z$. Nefness concerns reduced
integral curves; nilpotent structure in the special fiber does not change
the test. Nonreduced generic fibers are not introduced. All vertical
curves have nonnegative degree, so the actual model bundle is relatively
nef. BJ Lemma 4.3 returns the metric to the genuine global PSH cone.
No BFJ smooth equivalence on a singular quotient is used.

For comparison, [GM] Theorem 1.1 identifies algebraic, formal and piecewise
linear metrics on proper schemes, including semipositivity, but fixes a
nontrivially valued field throughout. It cannot be applied directly over
$k$. On a **smooth** compactification, a rational analytic PL metric can
instead be pulled to $k((t))$, made strict integral PL by a tensor power
and powers of $t$, and treated by GM, then by residual degrees and BJ
Remark 4.19. That remark is expressly smooth and connected, with
characteristic zero and an ample class. This route proves the model-psh
claim for individual smooth gluing metrics; by itself it does not prove
the later bounded local mixed-measure construction or its comparison.

**Already-regularizable extension.** Suppose $a_i\downarrow u$ is a
supplied sequence of global AFS functions and $u\le g+B$.
For rational $c>1$ and floor $C$, choose $R$ with

If $u$ is finite at a point of $Y$, the limit is not identically

BJ 12.18 supplies decreasing sequential global model approximation
without EP; uniform approximation of nef models by ample FS metrics,

# 8. Hartogs coefficient expressions, with their exact extent

Let $Y$ be integral affine over the original $k$, with coordinate
exhaustion $g\ge0$, and let $D\subset(Y\times\mathbb A^1)^{\rm an}$ be
Runge and contain the zero section. Use the definition that the polynomial
hull of every compact subset of $D$ is contained in $D$. The hull is
compact: bound all coordinate norms by a compact polydisk, and intersect
the closed polynomial inequalities there. A compact polynomially convex
set inside an open set is contained, with a strict margin, in a compact
finite polynomial-inequality polytope inside that open set. To see this,
separate each point of the complement in a larger bounding polydisk by
one polynomial with rational threshold strictly between its value and the
compact supremum; use a finite subcover. The bounding coordinates provide
compactness. This also handles a compact supremum of $-\infty$.

A countable exhaustion, with hulls at each step, gives nested compact
polytopes $P_r$ and strict polynomial-inequality opens $\Omega_r$ with
$P_r\subset\Omega_{r+1}\subset P_{r+1}\subset D$ and union $D$.
We use **strict inequalities**, not the generally false identification
$\operatorname{int}\{|f|\le a\}=\{|f|<a\}$: a constant constraint can
make that identification fail. This avoids an additional literal defect
in the distance calculation. Write
$$\Omega_r=\{|G_j(y,w)|<e^{c_j}\}_j,\qquad
 G_j=\sum_{i=0}^{k_j}f_{j,i}(y)w^i,\quad c_j\in\mathbb Q.$$
On the zero-section domain $O_r=\{|f_{j,0}|<e^{c_j}\}_j$, the distance
radius of the fiber disk is determined by the Gauss sup norm, so
$$H_r(y):=-\log d_{\Omega_r}(y)
     =\max_{j,\ i\ge1}\frac{\log|f_{j,i}(y)|-c_j}{i}.\tag{8.1}$$
Indeed all disk points of radius $R$ satisfy the inequalities exactly when
the Gauss bounds $|f_{j,i}|R^i\le e^{c_j}$ permit their open-disk limits;
Gauss points show the converse for any larger radius. The constant terms
are already strictly below threshold. An absent nonconstant coefficient
contributes $-\infty$. Compactness of $P_r$ prevents all such coefficients
from vanishing on a zero-section fiber, since otherwise the entire
noncompact affine line fiber would lie in $P_r$.

The $O_r$ exhaust $Y^{\rm an}$. On any fixed compact coordinate sublevel,
a whole tail is contained in $O_r$, and the $H_r$ decrease there to
$u=-\log d_D$. Compactness of a smaller closed fiber disk inside $D$
justifies convergence of the radii, even when $d_D=\infty$ and $u=-\infty$.
Formula (8.1) is a finite maximum of rational positive log coefficients
of **global regular functions on $Y$**. It has no generator or floor
condition yet. On the whole affine space set
$$a_r=\max\{H_r,\epsilon_r g-r\},\qquad
       \epsilon_r\in\mathbb Q_{>0},\quad \epsilon_r\downarrow0.$$
Expanding the second branch supplies the original coordinates with positive
rational coefficients and the rational floor $-r$. Thus $a_r$ is actual
AFS globally. On any fixed compact tail domain these functions decrease
to $u$; no global monotonicity of the raw $H_r$ is asserted.

Suppose $u\le Ag+B$, with rational $A>0$, and $u$ is finite somewhere.
Fix rational $c>A,C$ and choose $R$ so $B-(c-A)R<C-1$. On the compact
sublevel $g\le R+1$, a tail of the $a_r$ is defined by (8.1), decreasing
to $u$. On its outer shell the compact nested-level-set argument of
Section 7 yields $a_r-cg<C$ for a whole tail. Formula (7.1) and the
verified model-nef criterion give genuine decreasing global potentials
whose limit is $\max(u-cg,C)$ on the inner sublevel and $C$ outside.
Since $u-cg<C-1$ for $g\ge R$, this is the desired whole-space
truncation. Decrease $C$ and use the nontrivial finite point, then the
actual global FS/generator correspondence of Section 7. This proves
$u$ has a global decreasing AFS regularization. It also proves local
corrected pshness directly from the common-tail local sequences (8.1).

This checks the precise polynomial coefficient use in W 5.7 and in the
Hartogs step of 6.14. It does **not** assert that the Hartogs domain of an
arbitrary weakly psh $u$ is Runge. W 6.15--6.16 is needed for that input;
representation of (8.1) alone cannot prove it.

# 9. Direct entropy supports and their global FS sequences

For $x_i=\log|f_i|\in\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}$, including an algebra-
generating list, fix positive rational $a$ with $\log(r+1)/a\le1$ and set
$$\chi(x)=a^{-1}\log(1+\sum_i e^{ax_i}).$$
It lies between $\max(0,x_i)$ and that maximum plus one. For $x_0=0$,
let $\Delta$ be the probability simplex and
$F(p)=a^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^r p_i\log p_i$, $0\log0=0$. Gibbs' inequality,
derived from $\log t\le t-1$, gives
$$\chi(x)=\max_{p\in\Delta}\{\sum_{i\ge1}p_ix_i-F(p)\}.$$
For an infinite negative coordinate restrict to its zero-coordinate face.

Take rational triangulations of $\Delta$, respecting every face and
including all vertices, with mesh tending to zero. The lower convex
polyhedral envelope of the lifted vertex values lies above $F$ by
convexity, and at most $\eta$ above it by uniform continuity and the
barycentric representation in a small simplex. Its dual is the finite
maximum of $\sum p_ix_i-F(p)$ at these vertices. Hence the error is
at most $\eta$ for **all finite $x$**, without a compact coordinate bound.
Round intercepts downward to rational values with error at most $\eta$;
keep the zero vertex intercepts unchanged. The resulting $h$ satisfies
$\chi-2\eta\le h\le\chi$ globally. The same inequalities hold on every
infinite-coordinate face by taking the face subcomplex or finite limits.
Clearing denominators converts branches to log norms of regular monomials.
Vertex branches retain zero and each original $\log|f_i|$ separately.
Thus $h$ is actual AFS. Choose error $e_m=4^{-m}$ and take
$g_m=h_m+2e_m$. Then $g_m\ge\chi+e_m$ while
$g_{m+1}\le\chi+2e_{m+1}<\chi+e_m$; it is a decreasing global AFS
sequence tending to $\chi$.

At a contact point $x$, put $p_0=1/Z$, $p_i=e^{ax_i}/Z$, $Z=1+\sum e^{ax_i}$,
and omit zero coefficients. The support is
$$\ell(x')=-a^{-1}\sum p_i\log p_i+\sum_{p_i>0}p_ix_i'.$$
It is finite at contact. The gap multiplied by $a$ is
$\sum p_i\log(p_i/p'_i)\ge0$, with equality only at $x'=x$, including
the infinite faces. To verify uniform separation, change signs to
$t_i=-x_i$. With $C=-a^{-1}\sum p_i\log p_i$ and
$D(t')=\chi(-t')-[C-\sum p_it_i']$, $D\le b$ implies
$\min t_i'\ge-(b+C)/p_0$, using $\chi\ge-\min t_i'$ when that minimum
is negative. It also gives $\sum_{p_i>0}p_it_i'\le b+C$ since $\chi\ge0$.
Thus all coordinates are bounded below and positive-support coordinates
bounded above. The remaining coordinates lie in compact intervals
$[-B,+\infty]$. The extended gap is lower semicontinuous on this compact
product (it is $+\infty$ when a positive-support coordinate is infinite).
Its sublevels are compact, with zero set precisely the contact point.
Nested sublevels therefore lie inside any prescribed contact neighborhood
for some positive $b$. This yields the needed uniform strict gap. No
claim that the gap is finite continuous at such pole coordinates is needed.

For a countable coordinate algebra enumerate its nonzero regular functions;
zero functions can be omitted, since their norms distinguish no points.
Use barriers $\rho_j$ incorporating the coordinate generators and the first
$j$ functions. Polynomial degree bounds give
$\rho_j\le C_j(v+1)$ for $v=\max(0,\log|z_i|)$.
Choose positive rational $\gamma_j$ with
$\gamma_j\rho_j\le2^{-j}(v+1)$. The series converges locally uniformly,
and
$$U_N=\sum_{j\le N}\gamma_j\rho_j+2^{-N}(v+1)$$
decreases to it. Finite positive rational combinations have global uniform
AFS approximants $W_N$ with $U_N-e_N\le W_N\le U_N$, $e_N=4^{-N}$.
Then $W_N+2e_N$ decreases to the series by the same error comparison.
Omitting one fixed summand has exactly the same tail argument.

A support has real positive logarithmic coefficients. Here is its direct
global rational sequence, including all poles. For nonzero $f_i$, choose
integers $d_i>0$ with $\log|f_i|\le d_iv$ globally. Set
$s=C+\sum\alpha_i\log|f_i|$, $\alpha_i>0$, and
$A=\sum d_i\alpha_i$. Choose positive rational $q_{i,m}\uparrow\alpha_i$,
rational $B_m\downarrow A$ strictly above $A$, and rational $C_m\downarrow C$.
Put $b_m=B_m-\sum d_iq_{i,m}>0$ and
$$s_m=C_m+\sum q_{i,m}\log|f_i|+b_mv.$$
Away from poles,
$$s_m-s=(C_m-C)+\sum(\alpha_i-q_{i,m})(d_iv-\log|f_i|)+(B_m-A)v\ge0,$$
$$s_m-s_{m+1}=(C_m-C_{m+1})+(B_m-B_{m+1})v
             +\sum(q_{i,m+1}-q_{i,m})(d_iv-\log|f_i|)\ge0.$$
At a pole both have value $-\infty$; no subtraction is used there.
With rational $\epsilon_m\downarrow0$, define
$$H_m=\max(s_m,\epsilon_mv-m).$$
Both branches decrease to $s$, including at a pole. Expanding $v$ and
clearing rational powers gives regular monomial log branches, while the
second branch explicitly retains each original coordinate generator and
the floor $-m$. Thus $H_m$ is actual AFS. The empty support case is a
constant and has the same floor/generator construction.

Combine this support sequence with the decreasing AFS sequence of the
remaining series, retaining single-generator branches under rational sums.
The resulting $\rho'$ is global upper-growth regularizable, finite at
contact, agrees there with $\rho$, and satisfies $\rho'\le\rho-\delta$
outside a prescribed neighborhood. The initial seminorm topology supplies
a finite-list tropical neighborhood at contact; the enumeration includes
it. This proves the exact supporting construction of W 6.7 independently
of its unidentified APW supporting antecedents and independently of a
singular PL-exhaustion theorem.

# 10. Pole-order localization and the nodal obstruction

Let $R$ be integral affine over trivially valued $k$, $f\ne0$, $L$ a
nonempty compact subset of $Y_f^{\rm an}$, and $x$ its $R$-polynomial
hull point in $Y_f^{\rm an}$. Choose $M$ with $|\log|f||\le M$ on
$L\cup\{x\}$. Let $u\in\operatorname{AFS}(R)$, $h\in R_f$, and suppose
$$a\log|h|+b\le\max(u,-c\log|f|)+O(1),\qquad a,c>0.$$
Then
$$a\log|h(x)|+b\le\sup_L(a\log|h|+b)+2cM.\tag{10.1}$$

Take the finite normalization $\bar R$ in the common fraction field.
At every prime divisor $D$ of $\operatorname{Spec}\bar R$, evaluate the
growth inequality on $\tau\operatorname{ord}_D$, $\tau>0$. Elements of
$R$ have nonnegative order, so $u$ stays between its finite floor and a
fixed finite upper bound. All these field valuations belong to $Y_f^{\rm an}$.
Dividing by $\tau$ gives
$$a\max(-\operatorname{ord}_D h,0)\le c\operatorname{ord}_D f$$
when there is a pole; no pole is possible if the right side is zero.
Choose positive integers $p,q$ with $q/p>c/a$. Then $z=h^pf^q$ has
nonnegative order at every divisor of the normal affine algebra $\bar R$,
so its codimension-one intersection description gives $z\in\bar R$.
It is integral over $R$ and belongs to $R_f$. The finite algebra
$B=R[z]$ has $B_f=R_f$; the finite module $B/R$ is killed by one $f^N$.
Consequently for every $\ell\ge1$,
$$z^\ell f^N=h^{p\ell}f^{q\ell+N}\in R.$$
Apply the genuine $R$-polynomial hull inequality to this regular function,
then multiply logarithms by $a/(p\ell)$ and use the two bounds by $M$:
$$a\log|h(x)|+b\le\sup_L(a\log|h|+b)
                         +2a(q/p+N/(p\ell))M.$$
Let $\ell\to\infty$, then rational $q/p\downarrow c/a$.
If $h(x)=0$ the conclusion is immediate; if $h=0$ on all of $L$, the
same polynomial-hull inequality forces $h(x)=0$. This includes the
$-\infty$ endpoint. Normalization is only used to count actual poles;
the hull is always tested on functions of the original nonnormal $R$.
Taking a finite maximum gives the FS version of (10.1).

The printed denominator claim in W 5.11 is false: take
$f=z^2,g=z,h=g/f=z^{-1},a=2,c=1,u=0$. Then
$\max(2\log|h|,0)=\max(-\log|f|,0)$, but $ar=2>1$ although
$g\notin(f)$. The conclusion (10.1) is not refuted; actual pole orders
repair its proof. To deduce W 5.10 for a regularizable $v\le u+O(1)$,
one additionally needs controlled approximants with growth bounded by
$2\max(u,-c\log|f|)+O(1)$. For such a supplied sequence the error is
$4cM$, and the decreasing usc compact suprema converge to $\sup_Lv$:
if their suprema stayed above a larger level, their nested closed
superlevel sets would have nonempty intersection. Let $c\downarrow0$.
This compact argument uses no invalid uniform Dini convergence to a
singular function. The required controlled extension is a separate input
in a full Runge reconstruction.

The genuine nodal obstruction is unconditional. Let $C$ be an irreducible
rational projective nodal curve and $q:\mathbb P^1\to C$ its normalization,
with node preimages $p_1\ne p_2$. For $d=\deg L>0$, the divisorial
$v=\operatorname{ord}_{p_1}$ downstairs has finite dual energy by BJ 11.1.
Blow up $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1$ at $(p_1,0)$, with exceptional $E$.
The model bundle
$$p^*q^*L+d\mathcal X_0-dE$$
has degree zero on the strict central component and degree $d$ on the
exceptional component, both of multiplicity one. These are all vertical
curves, so the bundle is relatively nef; (3.1) gives raw MA $d\delta_v$.
The bounded model potential is $d\max(1-s,0)$ on the $p_1$ ray and
$d$ on other rays. Its two node-preimage endpoints have values $0,d$.
If a downstairs finite-energy solution existed, normalization pullback
would preserve energy and genuine pushforward would preserve its measure.
The function-field valuation $v$ has one analytic lift; positivity and
fixed mass therefore force the upstairs target to be the same Dirac.
Upstairs uniqueness gives the displayed potential plus a constant.
A downstairs pullback has equal values at $p_1,p_2$, a contradiction.
This is an actual nonsolvable finite-dual-energy target for the genuine
operator outside unibranchness, not a change of the problem's target.

# 11. Remaining dependencies and normalization

The preceding repairs do not complete the new singular envelope source.
The following are indispensable unresolved steps in this reconstruction.

1. **Local positivity and smooth weak upper-growth base.** The GM/CLD/smooth-BJ route in
   Section 7 handles individual continuous rational PL metrics on a
   smooth compactification when their local curvature positivity has
   actually been established. Section 5 proves ambient-convex PL
   positivity and applies Wanner in its exact field scope; the full
   corrected local class, defined using functions only on tropical images,
   still needs a compatible current-positivity argument or an independent
   curve slope proof. The finite norm tests themselves are actual FS and
   satisfy the stronger sufficient condition. To deduce the arbitrary locally psh smooth
   affine theorem, direct truncation $\max(u-cAg,C)$ gives a common
   constant boundary neighborhood and local pshness. It still needs a
   complete proof that this bounded locally psh function is global.
   Wu 4.17 reaches that conclusion through 4.2 and 4.16. Here the exact
   bounded-local mixed-measure independence on overlaps, compatibility
   with CLD 18.4.7, total mass, localization, integration by parts, and
   local comparison have not all been reconstructed. Positive currents
   and a model-nef criterion alone do not prove them. The corrected
   negative-floor local extension must also retain the actual background
   difference: if $\theta=\omega+dd^cp$, then the local formula is
   $\Phi_\omega=\Psi/A+p$, $g_\omega=v-p+(M+1)/A$, with
   $\Phi_\omega+g_\omega=u/A$. Merely equating $\theta$ and $\omega$
   is invalid. Accordingly arbitrary smooth **weak** upper-growth
   regularization and the principal-open removable extension are not
   asserted as unconditional new theorems here.

2. **Rossi and the Runge induction.** Section 8 proves the regularization
   consequence when the Hartogs domain is already Runge. W 5.6's Rossi
   proof and 6.15's splitting argument also glue on arbitrary analytic
   neighborhoods or on disjoint analytic pieces $W_1,W_2$. Their inputs
   are not the single global algebraic FS expression of Section 7.
   A separate rational model-PL bump/gluing argument, or an independent
   polynomial maximum proof, must verify those applications. The
   continuous integer trace in W 6.15 Step 3 is locally constant, but a
   single $\gamma$ on the whole chosen open needs connectedness or an
   explicit localization; continuity alone does not imply it. The
   $\gamma=1$ denominators in its Step 4 need the zero-other-weight case
   treated separately. These are repairable-looking obligations, not
   counterexamples to the desired theorem. No complete replacement of
   Rossi, local projection 6.16, or the full 6.14 induction is claimed.
   The norm equality of Section 6 cannot construct its own smooth weak
   regularization antecedent.

3. **Actual singular envelopes.** W 7.1 descends a smooth-resolution
   envelope using connected fibers and W 2.24, then invokes weak/global
   identification 6.5, which invokes the unreconstructed 6.3 induction.
   The zero-psh multiplier in the proof of W 2.25 must be $m\phi$ for
   $\phi\le0$, not $-m\phi$. Resolution connectedness and usc descent
   by themselves do not prove global psh descent. Therefore the
   countable rational singular envelope base remains unproved here.

4. **Onto normalization and cardinality passage.** For the requested
   geometrically unibranch $X$, finite normalization is degree-one
   birational with singleton geometric fibers and purely inseparable
   residue extensions. In characteristic zero those are equalities.
   It is a universal homeomorphism and its analytification is a
   homeomorphism: at each point the unchanged residue field has exactly
   one extended seminorm. These topological facts do not give onto PSH.
   The unconditional identities are
   $$E_{q^*\omega}(q^*\phi)=E_\omega(\phi),\qquad
   q_*\bigwedge_i(q^*\omega_i+dd^cq^*\phi_i)
      =\bigwedge_i(\omega_i+dd^c\phi_i),$$
   by BJ 7.2 and 7.19, hence one-way $\mathcal E^1$ preservation.
   BJ 5.13 makes PSH pullback onto only when larger classes approaching
   $\omega$ have EP. Under that antecedent the logarithmic-ideal
   corrections and increasing closure descend every upstairs potential;
   equal energies then give onto $\mathcal E^1$. The operator and all
   dual-energy targets correspond after onto descent. Its EP antecedent
   has not been proved here. Neither analytical homeomorphism nor
   pullback identities remove it.

If the countable rational envelope base is proved, the conceivable
arbitrary-cardinality passage uses finite model/obstacle/competitor data
over countable algebraically closed subfields, faithful descent, relative
integral closure in the **given** nonnormal generic fiber, vertical
$R_1/S_2$, component and multiplicity preservation, genuine mixed-measure
pushforward, contact orthogonality, and BJ global domination for genuine
global competitors. No singular BFJ Dirac equation may replace these.
Real ample classes require approximation from above and BJ's actual class
closure. This paper has not reconstructed all those passages as an
unconditional singular theorem; the missing base already blocks them.

The conditional theorem in Section 3 retains all target fields and ample

## Unreviewed root proposal: P16.1-model-atoms-and-smooth-globalization-20261005-continuation70

# Model atoms replace a Dirac equation in local domination

This is a new unreviewed proof proposal following the two continuation70
local-support notes. It does not establish singular local globalization or
singular EP. Its first model-atom construction is unconditional in the
genuine global theory; subsequent local domination requires the actual
bounded local measures, their exact total mass, locality and comparison.

## 1. A bounded genuine test with positive mass at each divisorial point

Let X be reduced irreducible projective over algebraically closed
trivially valued k, and let L be ample rational. For any x in Xdiv,
BJ Lemma2.12 gives a flag ideal a whose integrally closed blowup
pi:mathcalX->Xtriv has a central component E with v_E=x. The construction
uses relative integral closure in the prescribed generic fiber, so it
does not replace a nonnormal X by its normalization.

BJ Lemma2.33 gives an ample test configuration mathcalL for L on this
same mathcalX. Concretely choose a vertical rational divisor D relatively
ample over Xtriv and take mathcalL=L_mathcalX+epsilon D for positive
rational epsilon small enough. The actual model potential b is bounded
and genuine global L-psh. Every central component F has

    (mathcalL|F)^n > 0,

because the restriction is ample; b_F>0 is the multiplicity of F. The
actual raw model measure is sum_F b_F (mathcalL|F)^n delta_(v_F),
so MA(b)({x})>0. Normalizing by V=(L^n) changes no positivity. A
coinciding valuation from another component adds another positive
term. The root/trivial valuation is handled either by the trivial
ample configuration or the same construction. Rational scaled divisorial
valuations are exactly the source's Xdiv convention.

This construction uses neither equation surjectivity nor a continuous
Dirac solution. It supplies exactly the positive atom used in the
perturbation argument, with a concrete bounded model test.

## 2. Domination from the stated local measure antecedents

Assume bounded locally omega-psh potentials have genuine compatible
mixed measures, mixed positivity, exact raw mass V, locality on strict
max loci, and the comparison inequality

    MA(psi)({phi<psi}) <= MA(phi)({phi<psi}).

Also assume the corrected local class is determined by its divisorial
values; in the smooth case the common bounded-local representation in
the previous note obtains this from BJ's genuine divisorial determination.
The finite locally psh cone is stable under positive affine combinations.

Suppose phi<=psi MA(psi)-almost everywhere. Fix x in Xdiv and choose
the bounded genuine b from1. Translate b so that b<phi globally, with
a fixed strict margin; both functions are bounded. For 0<t<1 put

    phi_t=(1-t)phi+t b.

Then phi_t<phi everywhere and phi_t<psi MA(psi)-almost everywhere.
Comparison and exact equal masses give

    V=MA(psi)({phi_t<psi})
      <=MA(phi_t)({phi_t<psi})<=V.

Mixed positivity gives MA(phi_t)>=t^n MA(b), hence
MA(phi_t)({x})>0. Since the strict set has full MA(phi_t) mass, x belongs
to {phi_t<psi}. Letting t decrease to zero yields phi(x)<=psi(x).
Divisorial determination then gives phi<=psi globally.

This repairs Wu4.16's use of a continuous Dirac solution with a weaker
explicit ample-model test. It is not an independent proof that its
local measure antecedents hold on singular spaces. Dimension zero is
the separate one-point constant case, not a slope or divisorial test.

## 3. Smooth bounded local/global equality uses continuous majorant nets

Let X be smooth connected projective, characteristic zero, and omega
ample rational. Known smooth EP is the BJ primary theorem; it is not
being inferred from the singular desired conclusion. Suppose the
preceding bounded-local construction and its mass/comparison completion
have been reconstructed on X. Then2 applies.

Take a bounded locally omega-psh phi. On an arbitrary compact Hausdorff
Xan there is a directed decreasing *net* of continuous majorants g_i
with limit phi. A sequence is not asserted for an uncountable k or a
nonmetrizable Xan. This net is obtained from continuous majorants using
finite minima and the usual compact Hausdorff separation of the closed
superlevel sets of the usc phi. The majorants can be truncated to common
finite bounds without changing their limit.

Under smooth EP the genuine envelope P_omega(g_i) is continuous global
psh, and genuine orthogonality gives

    supp MA(P_omega(g_i)) subset {P_omega(g_i)=g_i}.

On this support, phi<=g_i=P_omega(g_i). Domination2 gives
phi<=P_omega(g_i) on Xan. The envelopes decrease, are bounded below by
a common constant, and are at most g_i. Thus their pointwise limit is
phi. Genuine global decreasing closure proves phi is global omega-psh.
The actual envelope-continuity and orthogonality statements, with their
exact ample and smooth hypotheses, must be cited/read in the native
reconstruction; this proof does not extend smooth EP to singular X.

For an unbounded locally psh generically finite phi, truncate with
max(phi,-M). Global usc on compact Xan bounds it above; each truncation
is bounded locally psh and hence global by the preceding argument.
Their decreasing nontrivial limit is global psh. Nef rational classes
can be approached from above by omega+epsilon theta, theta ample;
the exact genuine class-closure statement and known smooth nef EP are
required at this passage.

## 4. Noncircular smooth affine regularization

Let Y be smooth affine over the retained k and choose a smooth

## Unreviewed root proposal: P16.1-relative-tropical-supports-on-curves-20261005-continuation70

# A direct curve slope route for relative tropical supports

This is a new, unreviewed proof proposal for one remaining dependency of
P16.1 round58. It neither proves singular envelopes nor supplies the
higher-dimensional bounded local mixed operator. The original P16.1 task
and its completion criteria remain unchanged.

## 1. Precise local assertion

Let Omega be complete, algebraically closed, nontrivially valued, with
residue characteristic zero. Let C be a smooth algebraic Omega-curve, and
W an analytic open subset. Suppose a finite continuous real-valued
function b on W is locally a finite piecewise-affine function of
(-log|g_1|,...,-log|g_r|), for regular functions on an algebraic
neighborhood. At infinite coordinates retain the finite-valued affine
convention in Wu2609.27610v1 Definition3.1(1)-(2): every affine expression
is defined and finite on its stated neighborhood. In particular a
coefficient multiplying an infinite coordinate must vanish there.

Assume the corrected support condition: at each image point t there is
an affine ell and a relative image neighborhood on which

    f(t') >= ell(t')+c, with f(t)=ell(t)+c.

The same additive constant occurs in the inequality and contact identity.
The assertion to reconstruct is that b is Thuillier-subharmonic on W.
This conclusion uses relative image supports; an ambient convex extension
is not asserted.

## 2. Common skeleton and finite affine selection

At a curve point where all relevant nonzero g_i have finite logarithms,
shrink away from their finitely many zeros. The g_i are analytic units
there. Thuillier2.3.20 says log|g_i| is harmonic. Its proof uses a
semistable affinoid model and2.2.24: a unit log norm factors through the
skeleton, is piecewise affine, and has zero interior weighted Laplacian.
On a smaller strictly affinoid neighborhood take a common dominating
semistable model for the finitely many units. Harmonic model refinements
do not alter their functions. Each h_i=-log|g_i| therefore factors
through this common finite skeleton and is piecewise affine there.

The pullback b also factors through that skeleton. On each subdivided
edge the coordinates h_i are affine, and b is a continuous selection
from finitely many affine functions. A continuous finite affine selection
on an interval is piecewise affine: distinct affine pieces can change
only at their finitely many intersection points; coincident pieces
represent the same function. Subdivide at these points. Thus b is lisse
in Wanner2.7/Thuillier's sense. Real breakpoints inside edges are allowed
in the piecewise-affine function; they need not all be vertices of the
formal model or lie in the value group.

If a coordinate is infinite at a point, finite-valued affine expressions
on a relative neighborhood containing that point have coefficient zero
in that coordinate. With a finite local list, a smaller neighborhood
retains this independence. Discard those coordinates. On a component
where a pulled-back regular function vanishes identically the same rule
applies. At a type I zero, the remaining units have constant norm on a
small disk, hence b is constant there. At type IV points finitely many
unit harmonic functions are locally constant (Thuillier2.3.21 proof),
so b is locally constant. This handles points not appearing on the
common skeleton and does not assign finite values to infinite logarithms.

The common-model existence and the passage from the stated relative
finite-PL convention to this local independence must be checked against
the exact definitions during independent reconstruction; neither is
being supplied by the previously rejected global singular argument.

## 3. Slopes at a vertex

At any interior skeleton point x, pull back its relative affine support.
The resulting h=ell(h_1,...,h_r)+c is harmonic on a neighborhood of x,
and b-h has a local minimum zero at x. On every outgoing edge e its
one-sided derivative therefore satisfies

    slope_e(b-h) >= 0.

On the common skeleton, harmonicity gives

    sum_e weight_e slope_e(h) = 0.

The weighted coefficient of the lisse Laplacian is consequently

    sum_e weight_e slope_e(b)
      = sum_e weight_e slope_e(b-h) >= 0.

All weights are positive. Over algebraically closed Omega the usual
skeletal edge normalization agrees with Wanner's outgoing-slope sum;
retaining weights also covers a finite intermediate field extension.
At type III breakpoints the same argument uses the two outgoing
directions. The finitely many slopes where b is not affine contain
its Laplacian support. On an interior point outside that support its
Laplacian vanishes. No affinoid boundary coefficient is used to judge
subharmonicity inside the open W.

Thus ddc b is a nonnegative lisse measure locally. Wanner2.11, citing
Thuillier3.4.4, implies b is subharmonic. The sheaf property then gives
the assertion on W. If a current statement is wanted, compactify C
smoothly and apply Wanner4.3 only on the original open subset: lisse
subharmonic functions have positive CLD currents. This is not a general
higher-dimensional smooth-approachability theorem.

## 4. Decreasing limits and the round58 trace application

Suppose u_j is a supplied decreasing sequence of corrected locally
tropical-support PL functions on a common neighborhood of an algebraic
variety, and restrict it along an algebraic map from a smooth curve.
After scalar extension the tropical coordinates still restrict to the
original analytic point; relative support inequalities and affine
selection are inherited. Infinite identically-zero coordinates are
handled as above. Each pulled-back u_j is therefore subharmonic by the
preceding slope argument. Thuillier3.1.8(ii) makes the decreasing limit
subharmonic or identically minus infinity on each connected component.

For a complete residue-characteristic-zero field F, use the precise
extension Omega from round58 Section5: for trivial F first embed into
F((T)), then complete an algebraic closure; otherwise complete an
algebraic closure directly. Complete smooth curves to smooth proper
curves only if invoking Wanner's Section4. No extension of the functions
across the projective boundary is required.

This supplies the subharmonicity needed for the disk comparison and
radial Gauss limit in round58, without its extra ambient-convex current
approximation antecedent. The remainder of that boundary proof retains
the actual ramification degree e, Artinian fiber dimensions, proper
exclusion of other preimages, and locally bounded upper estimates in the
minus-infinity case. It does not construct the trace's previously assumed
globally regularizable extension, establish the smooth weak affine base,
or prove Rossi/Runge induction.

## 5. Why the ambient extension is an additional assertion

Local affine supports on a nonconvex image do not automatically give an
ambient convex extension. Take the union S of the three positive rays
generated by (1,0),(0,1),(1,1). Set f=0 on the first two and
f(t,t)=t on the third. It is a finite continuous affine selection, with
support zero at the common origin and an affine contact support at every
nonzero ray point on a smaller relative neighborhood. A convex ambient
extension agreeing near the origin would have

    f(t/2,t/2) <= (f(t,0)+f(0,t))/2 = 0,

contradicting f(t/2,t/2)=t/2. This example is not claimed to be the
tropicalization of a boundaryless variety and does not refute the slope
argument. It shows why a proof of current positivity or subharmonicity
should state its actual harmonic/balancing input rather than infer an
ambient convex extension from relative support alone.
