\begin{abstract}
Under the genuine numerical-cone and obstacle-envelope foundations,
we construct full-section minorants of every bounded continuous
obstacle whose supremum equals its genuine envelope off one actual
Kodaira divisor. The envelope is continuous there. On compact subsets
the increasing section stages converge uniformly. This yields fixed
original continuous functions approximating the bounded relative
section quotient in absolute $L^1$ simultaneously at an arbitrary
weak Radon limit and its moving measures, provided that limit is
algebraically null. No atomicity is imposed on the limit. The theorem
remains conditional on its explicit genuine foundations and does not
prove unbounded test tails or the inverse equation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Actual domain and conditional foundations}
Let $X$ be smooth integral projective of dimension $n\geq1$ over an
algebraically closed trivially valued characteristic-zero field, and
let $L$ be an actual big rational line bundle. Put $K=X^{\mathrm{an}}$,
$\theta=c_1(L)$ and let $v_0$ be the generic trivial valuation.
All supported semivaluations remain on the compact Hausdorff carrier.
Section potentials use the actual trivial reference convention:
logarithmic norms of regular sections are nonpositive and are zero
at $v_0$. Tensor products use the same reference convention.

We assume the genuine foundations proposed in the unreviewed complete
partial original-scope paper. They are not accepted theorem grants:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every genuine $u\in\PSH(\theta)$ is the all-point limit of
one decreasing net of finite, possibly polar, FS stages $h_i$ on
actual rational bundles $M_i$ with $c_1(M_i)\to\theta$ and a finite
generic endpoint. Stages are extended continuous, hence usc. Actual
FS functions on $L$ are genuine in this cone.
\item Every bounded continuous $f$ with a genuine minorant has a
genuine envelope $u_f=P_\theta(f)\leq f$, dominating every genuine
minorant. The reference $R=P_\theta(0)$ is genuine, normalized by
$R(v_0)=0$, with $R\leq0$. Constants shift envelopes in the usual way.
\end{enumerate}
Choose an actual ample rational bundle $A$ and rational $a>0$ with
$L-aA=\OO(E_0)$ for an actual effective rational Cartier divisor $E_0$.
Write $W=q_{E_0}\leq0$ for its single-section FS potential and
$Z=E_0^{\mathrm{an}}$. Then $W$ is finite exactly on the ordinary
open set $U=K\setminus Z$. This is an actual bundle identity.
Kodaira decomposition, openness of the ample cone and eventual global
generation of actual ample bundles are ordinary algebraic background.

\section{All bounded obstacles have section density off the divisor}
Fix $f\in C(K)$ and an integer $C\geq1$ with $|f|\leq C$.
For every cofinal admissible degree $m$, use the full filtration
\[
 W_m(f;s)=\inf_K(mf-\log|s|),\qquad0\neq s\in H^0(X,mL).
                                                        \tag{1}
\]
The bracket is $+\infty$ at a section pole. It is finite at $v_0$,
and its superlevel spaces, including zero, form a finite closed-endpoint
filtration of the full finite-dimensional section space. Choose a basis
adapted to its integer part and set
\[
 \phi_{m,f}=\frac1m\max_j
        \bigl(\log|s_{mj}|+\lfloor W_m(f;s_{mj})\rfloor\bigr).
                                                        \tag{2}
\]
The function is basis independent: a section of weight at least $k$
is a linear combination of adapted basis sections of weight at least
$k$, and the ultrametric inequality applies with trivial scalar norms.
Each term in (2) lies below $f$, so $\phi_{m,f}\leq u_f$ by genuineness.
The powers of sections and superadditivity of weights give
$\phi_{m,f}\leq\phi_{m',f}$ for $m\mid m'$.
Degrees are directed by divisibility, with denominators cleared;
no countability assumption on the field or defining FS net is used.

\begin{theorem}\label{density}
For every $f\in C(K)$,
\[
                  u_f(x)=\sup_m\phi_{m,f}(x)
                    \quad(x\in U).                    \tag{3}
\]
The value is finite continuous on $U$, and the cofinal stages converge
uniformly on every compact subset of $U$. Equality on $Z$ is not claimed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The envelope exists because $R-C\leq-C\leq f$. Monotonicity and
constant shifts give $R-C\leq u_f\leq R+C$.
Tensor the section of $E_0$ with a full generated power of $aA$.
It gives an actual FS potential $W$ on $L$, and $W-C\leq f$.
Hence $u_f$ is finite on $U$.

Take a defining FS net $h_i\downarrow u_f$ on actual $M_i$.
Fix $\epsilon>0$. Compactness gives eventually $h_i\leq f+\epsilon$
\emph{on all of $K$}. Indeed the closed sets
$\{h_i\geq f+\epsilon\}$ decrease and have empty intersection,
since their all-point limit is at most $f$. This uses a continuous
obstacle and a strict margin, including at old poles.

Fix rational $t\in(0,1)$ before taking a late defining stage. The
actual rational bundle
\[
                B_{i,t}=L-(1-t)M_i-t(L-aA)               \tag{4}
\]
is eventually ample since its numerical class tends to $ta[A]$.
After clearing powers, its full generated FS system is identically
zero on the entire original carrier: some section is a unit at the
center of each semivaluation, including a supported one.
Tensor the actual FS stages and this generated factor. The scalar
\[
                g_{i,t,\epsilon}
                    =(1-t)(h_i-\epsilon)+t(W-C)          \tag{5}
\]
is an actual FS function on $L$, with all tensor exponents cleared.
No numerical bundle equality is substituted for (4). Because
$h_i-\epsilon\leq f$ and $W-C\leq-C\leq f$, (5) is below $f$
on the entire carrier. In particular it is a genuine minorant.

Any actual FS minorant $g$ of $f$ is below the supremum in (3).
To verify this with real weights, write
$g=r^{-1}\max_j(\log|s_j|+\lambda_j)$ on an actual power of $L$.
The minorant inequalities imply $W_r(f;s_j)\geq\lambda_j$.
Raise each section to the $\ell$th power; in degree $r\ell$ its
weight is at least $\ell\lambda_j$. The corresponding integer part
loses less than one. Thus
$\phi_{r\ell,f}\geq g-1/(r\ell)$; basis independence just proved
allows use of each of these sections. Let $\ell\to\infty$.

For $x\in U$, $W(x)$ and $u_f(x)$ are finite. Apply this observation
to (5), take the defining tail $i$, then let rational $t\downarrow0$
and finally $\epsilon\downarrow0$. It proves the reverse inequality
in (3); the upper inequality was already proved.

The full section system of one fixed cofinal degree is finite on all
of $U$, by the same Kodaira-section tensor with a generated ample
factor. Since $|f|\leq C$, every filtration weight is in
$[-mC,mC]$, and $\phi_{m,f}\geq\phi_{m,0}-C$; here the integer
choice of $C$ removes any rounding error in this bound. Therefore
all cofinal stages after that degree are finite continuous on $U$.
Their supremum is lsc, and the genuine envelope is usc. Equality (3)
makes it continuous there. On a compact $F\subset U$ the continuous
nonnegative gaps $u_f-\phi_{m,f}$ decrease to zero. Their closed
positive-margin superlevels have empty intersection, so compactness
gives uniform convergence. This is the Dini argument for the
divisibility-directed stages, rather than a metrizability assumption.
\end{proof}

For $f=0$, (3) recovers the reference assertion. For a bounded rational
model obstacle $f_H$, its full time lattice is exactly (1) with cleared
denominators, when the ordinary section-lattice identification has
been reconstructed. Thus the density theorem gives both the reference
and the test-envelope equalities on $U$, instead of assuming a
general negligible-set theorem. It does not give below-approximants
for an arbitrary unbounded obstacle.

\section{Fixed continuous approximants with absolute integral errors}
Write $u=u_f$ and $b=u-R$ on $U$. Monotonicity gives $|b|\leq C$,
and Theorem~\ref{density} makes $b$ continuous on $U$.
Let positive Radon measures $\nu_i\to\nu$ weakly on $K$, with
converging finite masses. Assume $\nu(Z)=0$.
Let $m_i$ tend cofinally in admissible divisibility degrees. Suppose
bounded measurable tests $a_i$ on the supports of $\nu_i$ satisfy
$|a_i|\leq C$ and, wherever both terms are finite,
\[
                  a_i=\phi_{m_i,f}-\phi_{m_i,0}.          \tag{6}
\]
The equality is required on each compact subset of $U$ for all
sufficiently late $i$. It need not define a subtraction at common poles.

\begin{theorem}\label{weighted}
For every $\eta>0$ there is an actual $B\in C(K)$, $|B|\leq C$, with
\[
 \int_U|b-B|\,d\nu<\eta,\qquad
 \int|a_i-B|\,d\nu_i<\eta\quad\text{eventually}.          \tag{7}
\]
Consequently $\int a_i\,d\nu_i\to\int_U(u_f-R)\,d\nu$.
The compact collar and $B$ are fixed before the degree and measure
indices tend to their limits. There is no atomic-support hypothesis.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\nu(K)=0$, take $B=0$ and use $\nu_i(K)\to0$.
Otherwise let $M>\nu(K)$ bound all sufficiently late masses.
Radon inner regularity and $\nu(U)=\nu(K)$ give a compact
$F\subset U$ whose omitted mass is arbitrarily small. Choose
$0\leq\chi\leq1$ continuous, equal to one on $F$, with support
contained in $U$. Its compact support is a fixed collar away from $Z$.
Define $B=\chi b$ on $U$ and zero outside $U$. It is continuous
on $K$: $b$ is bounded and the support of $\chi$ stays inside $U$.
Its limiting error is at most $C\int(1-\chi)\,d\nu$.

Theorem~\ref{density} gives uniform convergence of both section stages
on $\operatorname{supp}\chi$. Fix one degree $q$ after their
individual errors there are at most $\delta>0$. For $m_i$ sufficiently
divisible and large, their monotonicity and envelope upper bounds give
$|a_i-b|\leq2\delta$ on this fixed support. On the full measure support,
\[
                   |a_i-B|\leq2\delta\chi+C(1-\chi).
                                                        \tag{8}
\]
Outside the collar (8) uses only $|a_i|\leq C$; no value of $b$
at a reference pole is required. First choose the compact collar
so the limiting omitted integral is small, then $\delta$ with
$2\delta M$ small, and finally this fixed $q$. Weak convergence of
the masses and of the continuous $\chi$ gives (7) with room in the
strict inequalities. Integrating $B$ and using (7) proves the limit.
\end{proof}

\section{Actual moving-model application and its limits}
For a fixed bounded rational model obstacle $f_H$, suppose its
complete relative stages and the zero stages share the actual generic
moving factor $T_m$:
\[
 \phi_{m,H}\circ p_m^{\mathrm{an}}=q_{T_m}+\psi_{m,H},
 \qquad\phi_{m,0}\circ p_m^{\mathrm{an}}=q_{T_m}.
                                                        \tag{9}
\]
Time-lattice comparison gives $|\psi_{m,H}|\leq C$.
Suppose the actual pushed auxiliary nef measures converge weakly
to a proposed original output $\mu_u$, with its actual raw mass.
At their function-field divisorial atoms, the horizontal factor is
finite and the valuative birational lift is unique; hence their
bounded test is exactly the quotient in (6). If $\mu_u(Z)=0$,
Theorem~\ref{weighted} proves its weighted identity and original
continuous absolute $L^1$ approximants for this fixed bounded relative
test, even when $\mu_u$ is nonatomic.
