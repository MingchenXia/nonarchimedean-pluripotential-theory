which hypotheses belong to their original statements, and what has been
proved inside this text.  Its purpose is logical, not merely historical.
A theorem imported from the literature remains an imported theorem even when
we give a detailed proof architecture; a result proved in a manuscript
supplied by the author is not described as a published theorem; and a new
argument in this first draft is identified as such.

\section{How to read the ledger}
\label{sec:appL-how}

Each entry has one of five statuses.

\begin{description}
\item[Proved.]
The book supplies a complete argument from earlier results in the book or
from standard algebraic facts explicitly stated at the point of use.

\item[Deep import.]
The proof is not reproduced in full.  The theorem is stated with its
source hypotheses and an exact citation or theorem number.

\item[Reconstruction.]
The book proves the result by reorganizing ingredients from the cited
source.  The mathematical credit remains with that source.

\item[Supplied manuscript.]
The argument comes from one of the two TeX archives supplied for this
project.  The text has been checked and rewritten, but the result is not
represented as independently published.

\item[First-draft result.]
The claim is proved in this book and is not assigned to an external source.
Such a claim is kept separate from the historical theorem stream and is
accompanied by its exact scope or by a counterexample.
\end{description}

These labels concern provenance, not importance.  An elementary lemma may
be a deep dependency if the available proof uses resolution, alteration, or
test ideals.  Conversely, a long calculation may be proved here from
definitions.

\section{Proof boundary of the technical appendices}
\label{sec:appL-appendix-proof-boundary}

Every theorem, lemma, proposition, and corollary in
\crefrange{app:valuation-algebra}{app:complex-reference} is followed
immediately by a proof block.  A block headed \emph{foundational input} is
not represented as a proof of that input: it states the imported assertion,
gives its source, and proves only the deductions made from it.  The precise
irreducible cores are as follows.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.5em,labelindent=0pt]
\item[\Cref{app:valuation-algebra}.]
The converse and finite Rees-valuation reduction in the valuative criterion
for integral closure are imported from
\cite[Chapter~VI]{ZariskiSamuelII}; the square--cube and universal
homeomorphism criteria are exactly Stacks Project Tags 0CND, 0EUH, and 04DF;
and the additive-power formula is exactly
\cite[Theorem~4.17, Proposition~4.2, and Corollary~4.3]{RobertsSingh}.

\item[\Cref{app:birational-geometry}.]
The imported scheme-theoretic cores are admissible domination (Tag 081T),
Raynaud--Gruson flattening (Tag 0815), and Noetherian Stein factorization
(Tag 03H0), together with the normalization statement used for its middle
term (Tag 035L).  Resolution and simultaneous principalization are the stated
Hironaka inputs \cite{Hironaka1964I,Hironaka1964II}; the smooth projective
alteration input is \cite[Theorem~4.1]{deJong1996}.

\item[\Cref{app:ideals}.]
The analytic inputs are Nadel coherence
\cite[Theorem~10.20]{Bou17} and strong openness
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{GZ15}.  The algebraic inputs are local vanishing
\cite[Theorem~9.4.1]{PAG2}, subadditivity
\cite[Theorem~2.3]{DEL00}, geometric Skoda division
\cite[Section~9.6]{PAG2}, and the uniform-generation input used in
\cite[Theorem~5.20]{BJ22}.  The positive-characteristic inputs are the
asymptotic test-ideal statements of
\cite[Section~3 and Proposition~3.1]{MustataTest}, Canton's discrepancy
estimate \cite[Definition~3.2 and Proposition~4.1]{Canton}, the
Brian\c con--Skoda theorem
\cite[Theorem~6.9(iii)]{ST12} (with
\cite[Theorem~2.1]{HY03}), and the fixed-datum reduction comparison
\cite[Theorem~6.8]{HY03}.

\item[\Cref{app:convexity}.]
The Lorentzian signature and solvability of the Alexandrov equation used for
Alexandrov--Fenchel are exactly
\cite[Theorems~12.3 and~13.3]{Ber26Convex}, with the classical formulation
\cite[Theorem~7.3.1]{Sch14}.  Continuity of Bedford--Taylor products for
convex superpotentials is imported from
\cite[Theorem~4.2]{Ber26Convex}.  The mixed-subdivision construction is
anchored at \cite[Theorem~5.1.7]{Sch14}; the finite-dimensional area formula
used for the smooth Alexandrov density is
\cite[Theorem~3.8]{EvansGariepy}.  All reductions from those inputs,
including degenerate bodies and the factor \(n!\), are proved in the
appendix.

\item[\Cref{app:nets}.]
The point-set arguments are proved directly.  Compactness of the probability
measure space uses only Tychonoff compactness
\cite[Chapter~5]{Kel75} and the Riesz representation theorem
\cite[Theorem~2.14]{Rud87}; the Portmanteau inequalities are then proved
from continuous minorants.

\item[\Cref{app:intersection-bdivisors}.]
Finite-level intersection theory imports the operational Chow projection
formula \cite[Proposition~2.3(c)]{Ful}.  The extension to nef Weil
\(b\)-classes imports exactly decreasing approximation
\cite[Theorem~2.1]{DF20}, existence and independence
\cite[Proposition--Definition~3.1]{DF20}, and upper semicontinuity and
monotone continuity \cite[Theorem~3.2]{DF20}.

\item[\Cref{app:tropical-algebra}.]
The analytic-calibration theorem imports precisely the local-degree and
gluing statements
\cite[Proposition~14.3.1, \S14.3.2, Lemma~14.3.4, and
Definition~14.3.5]{CLD25}, harmony
\cite[Theorem~15.6.1]{CLD25}, and calibrated pushforward
\cite[Proposition~14.4.2]{CLD25}.  The balancing and lattice-index
deductions are proved in the appendix.

\item[\Cref{app:complex-reference}.]
The nonpluripolar, relative-energy, envelope, strong-openness, geodesic, and
test-curve inputs are isolated by exact internal anchors to
\cref{chap:complex-prerequisites,chap:rays-test-curves}; unrestricted
total-mass monotonicity is imported from
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{DDNL18mono}, with the small-unbounded-locus predecessor
\cite[Theorem~1.16]{BEGZ10}.  In particular,
the analytic-singularity assertion for the ideal envelope uses envelope
maximality and is not deduced from divisorial orders alone.
The multiplier-ideal reconstruction uses the uniform-
\(\eta\) valuative criterion \cite[Corollary~10.18]{Bou17}, not separate
strict inequalities with a divisor-dependent margin.
\end{description}

\Cref{app:stacks,app:normalizations,app:notation,app:provenance} contain

theorem-like environments.  Their displayed formulas are cross-referenced
to proofs or foundational inputs in the preceding appendices and main
chapters.

\section{Audit checklist for future editions}
\label{sec:appL-future}

Before a cited theorem is enlarged in a later edition, the following checks
must all be repeated:

\begin{enumerate}
\item verify the latest primary version and any erratum;
\item compare the base field, characteristic, and singularities of the
  space;
\item compare positivity: ample, nef, big, pseudoeffective, or Kähler;
\item compare regularity: model, smooth, continuous, bounded, or
  finite-energy;
\item determine whether the proof is sequential or valid for arbitrary nets;
\item audit every normalization of valuation, curvature, mass, and energy;
\item check whether the conclusion is equality, a one-way map, a
  correspondence, or an analogy;
\item confirm that zero-volume and infinite-value cases are defined; and
\item record whether the result is published, a dated preprint, a supplied
  manuscript, or a new proof in the book.
\end{enumerate}

This protocol is intentionally conservative.  The subject moves quickly,
but the logical dependencies of a textbook must remain stable even when the
literature changes.
