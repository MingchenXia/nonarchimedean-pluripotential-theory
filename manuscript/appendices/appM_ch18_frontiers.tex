\section{Detailed regularity and descent results}
\label{sec:appM-regularity-results}

Classical pluriharmonicity is defined by curve tests after arbitrary ground
field extension and therefore does not visibly provide a finite local
presentation by logarithms of analytic units.  On the quasi-smooth locus,
altered local uniformization and rigid $\acute{e}$tale descent remove this
gap.  The descent step is included because the uniformizing cover need not be
finite, flat, or boundaryless, so finite-flat descent cannot be applied to it
directly.

quasicompactness, separatedness, and boundarylessness play no role.  The
coefficient group is essential.  By
\Cref{prop:p061-integral-pl-counterexample}, the conclusion cannot be
strengthened to $\mathbf Q$-PL or integral PL.

The same conclusion has a finite-flat cover descent that is independent of
any quotient presentation.

\begin{corollary}[Finite-flat cover descent for classical pluriharmonic regularity]
\label{cor:p061-finite-flat-cover-descent}
Let $K$ be a complete nontrivially valued field and let
$\pi:Y\to X$ be a finite, flat, surjective morphism of good strictly
$K$-analytic spaces.  Suppose that every classically pluriharmonic
function on every ordinary open subspace of $Y$ is $\mathbf R$-PL.
Then every classically pluriharmonic function on every ordinary open subspace
of $X$ is $\mathbf R$-PL and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $U$ be an ordinary open subspace of $X$, let
$h\in\operatorname{PH}(U)$, and fix $x\in U$.  Choose a good strict
affinoid neighbourhood $U_0\subseteq U$ of $x$.  The restricted map
\[
 \pi^{-1}(U_0)\longrightarrow U_0
\]
is finite, flat, and surjective between good strictly $K$-analytic
spaces.  Classical pluriharmonicity is stable under analytic pullback
\cite[Proposition~7.2(6)]{GR25Classical}, so $\pi^*h$ is
$\mathbf R$-PL on $\pi^{-1}(U_0)$ by hypothesis.  Apply
\cite[Proposition~5.11]{GJR26} with the divisible coefficient group
$\Lambda=\mathbf R$.  It descends $\mathbf R$-PL regularity to
$h|_{U_0}$.  Since $x$ was arbitrary, $h$ is $\mathbf R$-PL on
$U$.

Both $h$ and $-h$ are classically psh.  The real-PL comparison
\cite[Theorem~4.3]{GR25Classical} makes both signs semipositive, which is
exactly Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonicity.
\end{proof}

Consequently every ordinary neighbourhood admitting a finite flat surjective
cover by one of the verified P06.1 regularity ranges is itself in that
range.  This is a descent criterion, not a construction of such a cover for
an arbitrary singular germ.

The theorem does not by itself address a non-quasi-smooth point.  Within the
good, separated, strict, boundaryless scope of
\Cref{prob:frontier-pluriharmonic}, the published curve comparison already
covers every strict analytic curve, including singular and nonreduced ones
\cite[\S4.1.2 and Proposition~3.15]{GR25Classical}.  A further genuinely
singular higher-dimensional scope is supplied by normalization in one curve
factor and a parameterized rigid-point argument.

These remain central-fibre criteria.  For instance, the one-step proposition
permits changing fibre equations such as $XY=aZ^2$ as the analytic
coefficient $a$ on the base varies, while the corollary permits a finite
tower over any base already having the local pluriharmonic
$\mathbf R$-PL property.  The standard-etale theorem permits a genuine
source chart over that cone and reduces its central fibre to a verified
base range.  The algebraic-etale theorem manufactures precisely such a chart
over a smooth algebraic base, but only at a point mapping to the displayed
zero section.  Its finite-tower theorem instead places the central domain
at each stage as an ordinary neighbourhood inside an algebraic etale source
over the preceding stage and closes an induction; no common grading among
the stages is needed.  None of these
results controls arbitrary points away from the
relevant central fibre, nor do they improve a base that lacks the required
local property or produce a weighted-cone model for an arbitrary germ.

For example, when $\operatorname{char}K\ne 3$, this includes the non-toric
normal isolated surface singularity $x^3+y^3+z^3=0$ at its vertex.  The
result is a vertex-germ theorem only:
it does not provide a weighted-cone presentation for a general singular germ,
nor a conclusion away from the specified vertex.

Finite quotient singularities admit a different descent argument.  The
point is to descend an arbitrary real PL function, rather than to average a
metric through a finite flat map.  Ordinary-open localization is supplied by
\cite[Proposition~5.8]{GJR26}; clean finite-group slices by
\cite[Lemma~5.2 and Theorem~5.3]{HansenJohansson23}; and the possibly wild,
nonflat formal quotient by
\cite[Remark~3.3.3 and Theorems~3.3.4 and~4.4.1]{Zavyalov24Quotients}.
The tensor-induction step is written out because exactness of invariants and
division by the group order are both unavailable in residue characteristic
dividing that order.

Classical pluriharmonicity is stable under analytic pullback by definition.
Consequently, if a finite constant-group quotient has a separated strict
affinoid source lying in any of the verified regularity scopes above, then a
classically pluriharmonic function on the quotient pulls back to a real PL
function and \Cref{prop:p061-finite-group-rpl-descent-candidate} descends that
regularity.  In particular, P06.1 is settled on finite constant-group
quotients of quasi-smooth strict affinoids and of the verified local
arbitrary-curve-factor models.  The proposition does not uniformize an
arbitrary singular germ by such a quotient.

The constant-group argument does not by itself descend through a
nonreduced diagonalizable group in equal positive characteristic.  The next
proposition supplies the missing Frobenius descent.  It is formulated for a
general finite radicial morphism because only the uniform power condition,
and not the toric presentation, is used in its proof.  Combining it with the
finite constant-group result treats every split simplicial affine toric
domain, including wild quotient singularities.

The cone in \Cref{thm:p061-simplicial-toric-ph-rpl} need not be full
dimensional, and the analytic domain need not contain a closed torus orbit.
Thus the result is genuinely complementary to
\Cref{thm:p061-toric-fixed-point-constancy}: it covers all points of every

convex rational cone.  Neither theorem supplies a local quotient chart for
an arbitrary singular analytic germ.

\begin{example}
\label{ex:p061-simplicial-cover-not-flat}
The finite lattice cover used in the preceding proof need not be flat.
Indeed, the inclusion
\[
                     K[x^2,xy,y^2]\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow K[x,y]
\]
is the two-dimensional simplicial toric cover induced by the index-two
sublattice.  Its generic rank is two.  At the torus-fixed vertex, however,
the fibre algebra is
\[
 K[x,y]/(x^2,xy,y^2),
\]
which has $K$-basis $(1,x,y)$ and hence dimension three.  A finite flat
module of generic rank two would have two-dimensional fibre there, so this
cover is not flat.  This explains why neither finite-flat PL descent nor a
trace argument can replace the two-stage constant-group/Frobenius proof.
The calculation remains valid in characteristic two, where the index-two
diagonalizable factor is nonreduced.
\end{example}

The simpliciality restriction can in fact be removed, and every torus orbit
can be treated at once.  Near an arbitrary orbit, an exact principal toric
open is a product of the fixed-point chart for the transverse cone with a
split torus.  The parameterized statement below proves constancy on every
completed-residue-field fibre of the transverse factor; its semigroup Tate
algebra has a singleton Shilov boundary.  No toric resolution or descent
through a nonflat modification is used.

The orbitwise product decomposition is also stable under a good strict
quasi-smooth parameter: \Cref{cor:p061-split-toric-quasismooth-products}
applies to every ordinary open subspace of a split toric variety times such
a parameter, and to the corresponding analytic local product models.  Its
proof first makes the pointed transverse toric factor visible at the chosen
orbit and then applies the fixed-fibre factorization to the product of the
orbit torus with the parameter.  It does not extend to a nonsplit toric
factor, a non-quasi-smooth parameter, or an analytic germ without an actual

When the toric factor becomes split over a complete analytic extension which
is the completion of an algebraic extension, the same product conclusion
descends by \Cref{cor:p061-algebraic-split-toric-forms}: scalar extension
preserves both classical pluriharmonicity and quasi-smoothness of the
parameter, and $\mathbf R$-PL functions descend to the ground field.  This
still requires such an algebraic splitting extension and an actual local
product model.

The quasi-smooth parameter may also be replaced by an arbitrary finite
product of good strict analytic curves and a good strict factor with
quasi-smooth reduction.  Indeed,
\Cref{cor:p061-toric-arbitrary-curve-factor-products} first exposes the
pointed transverse toric factor at the chosen orbit.  Its remaining
parameter is the product of the orbit torus with the curve factors and the
last factor, so \Cref{thm:p061-arbitrary-curve-factors-ph-rpl} applies.
The same conclusion descends for a toric form split by the completion of an
algebraic extension.  This still requires an actual product model and does
not admit a reduced singular factor of dimension at least two outside the
toric factor.

The algebraicity of the toric form itself is unnecessary for this scalar
extension argument.  If each point has an ordinary analytic neighbourhood
which becomes an ordinary open subspace of a split toric variety after a
complete extension that is the completion of an algebraic extension, then
\Cref{cor:p061-algebraic-split-analytic-toric-forms} gives the same
conclusion, including after product with a good strict quasi-smooth
parameter.  The displayed splitting chart remains a hypothesis; this does
not construct one for an arbitrary analytic germ.

This analytic-local range admits the arbitrary curve factors as well.
\Cref{cor:p061-analytic-toric-arbitrary-curve-factor-products} starts from
the same pointwise splitting charts, carries all curve factors and the factor
with quasi-smooth reduction through scalar extension, and applies
\Cref{cor:p061-toric-arbitrary-curve-factor-products} upstairs.  The
splitting chart and its algebraic extension remain hypotheses; the
corollary does not construct them or globalize a nonproduct germ.

The product need not be visible before passing to an ordinary-etale source
chart.  The next result starts from such a chart, constructs a genuine
affinoid product over a sufficiently deep weighted toric tube, and then
applies fibrewise singleton-Shilov factorization.  The fixed-fibre factor is
retained; it is not silently identified with an open subset of the smooth
target.

such a standard-etale affinoid chart, nor does it cover a non-quasi-smooth
parameter or a non-etale local model.

The pointed toric factor in that statement is enough at every orbit of an
arbitrary split toric variety.  Indeed, on the usual principal toric open,
the orbit factorization separates a pointed full-dimensional toric factor
from a split torus; the latter is absorbed into the quasi-smooth parameter.
Thus \Cref{cor:p061-analytic-standard-etale-all-orbits} applies to an actual
strict standard-etale affinoid chart over a split toric variety times a good
strict quasi-smooth parameter whenever its target affinoid is an ordinary
neighbourhood of the image point.  This is a local reduction of the stated
chart condition, not an assertion about arbitrary analytic etale maps.

If the toric base becomes split only after a complete analytic extension
which is the completion of an algebraic extension, the same chart conclusion
descends by \Cref{cor:p061-analytic-standard-etale-algebraic-split-forms}.
Scalar extension preserves the standard presentation and quasi-smooth
parameter; the all-orbits result applies upstairs, and ground-field descent
returns the $\mathbf R$-PL conclusion.  This still requires such an
algebraic splitting extension and the same ordinary-target chart condition.

The same analytic standard-etale tube retains arbitrary finite external
curve factors.  In
\Cref{cor:p061-analytic-standard-etale-arbitrary-curve-factor-products},
the coefficient-contraction theorem manufactures the toric product before
singleton-Shilov factorization, while the arbitrary-curve theorem applies to
the retained parameter together with a final factor having quasi-smooth
reduction.  The result includes algebraic-split toric bases by the same
completed-algebraic-extension descent, but still assumes the displayed
standard-etale presentation, ordinary-target condition, and external
product.

The algebraic ordinary-etale conclusion has the analogous algebraic-split
descent.  Namely,
\Cref{cor:p061-zariski-etale-algebraic-split-toric-charts} applies whenever
the toric factor of each source-Zariski chart becomes split after a complete
analytic extension which is the completion of an algebraic extension.  The
pulled-back chart is then in the preceding split ordinary-etale range, and
\cite[Corollary~5.12]{GJR26} descends the resulting $\mathbf R$-PL
function.  This does not manufacture an ordinary-etale chart, nor does it
cover toric factors without such an algebraic splitting extension.

Arbitrary finite curve factors may be carried through the manufactured
ordinary-etale tube.  In
\Cref{cor:p061-etale-toric-arbitrary-curve-factor-products},
singleton-Shilov factorization leaves those curves together with the
quasi-smooth fixed-fibre factor and a final factor having quasi-smooth
reduction.  The arbitrary-curve-factor theorem applies to that parameter,
and completed-algebraic-extension descent treats algebraic-split toric
factors.  This still requires the displayed ordinary-etale source chart and
an actual external product; it does not cover a genuinely log-etale or
locally twisted nonproduct germ.

\begin{example}
\label{ex:p061-etale-conifold-tube}
Assume that $\operatorname{char}K\ne2$, and let
\[
 Z=\operatorname{Spec}
 K[x,y,z,w]/(xy-zw),
 \qquad \mathcal Y=\mathbf A^1_{K,s}.
\]
The three-dimensional affine toric variety $Z$ is the cone over a square;
its vertex is the origin.  On the principal open where $2T$ is invertible,
the equation
\[
                         T^2=1+x+s
\]
defines an ordinary-etale morphism
$
 \mathcal X\to Z\times_K\mathcal Y
$.
At the point over the vertex with $s=0$ and $T=1$,
\Cref{prop:p061-v3-etale-toric-product} produces an actual product
$
 \Omega\simeq U\times_KD
$, where $U$ is a sufficiently deep weighted neighbourhood of the
toric vertex and $D$ is a neighbourhood in the fixed-fibre curve
$T^2=1+s$.  Thus every classically pluriharmonic function on a
neighbourhood of that point is pulled back from $D$, and is locally
$\mathbf R$-PL and GJR harmonic.  The example is genuinely source-etale:
no choice of a branch of $T$ on the original target is assumed in the
theorem.
\end{example}

Consequently P06.1 is settled on every ordinary open analytic subspace of
every split toric variety, for arbitrary cones and at every orbit, on every
toric form which becomes split after the completion of an algebraic extension
and its products with a good strict quasi-smooth parameter, and on every
good strict analytic space locally having the displayed algebraic-split
analytic toric form, as well as on every source-Zariski-local ordinary-etale
chart to a product whose toric factor is
split or becomes split after an algebraic splitting extension and whose
other factor is smooth, as well as on the displayed strictly analytic
standard-etale affinoid charts over a split or algebraic-split toric base and
a quasi-smooth parameter.  This includes the rank-zero transverse factor at
the dense torus orbit.  P06.1 remains
active for arbitrary nonproduct
non-quasi-smooth singular germs, products containing a reduced singular
factor of analytic dimension at least two outside the verified ranges,
genuinely log-$\acute{e}$tale charts not covered by an ordinary-etale
chart of the displayed kind, and nonsplit local models outside the verified
algebraic-split descent range.

The integral metric criterion of Chambert--Loir--Ducros and the real residue
formalism of Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff fit together without pulling currents
back to curves.  The following result uses an ample residual class as an
order unit for the finitely many integral PL summands of a real PL function.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:p01-real-pl-positive-current-candidate}
Let $K$ be a complete nontrivially valued non-Archimedean field, let $X$
be a good, separated, boundaryless, pure-dimensional, strictly
$K$-analytic space, and let
$h:X\to\mathbf R$ be an $\mathbf R$-PL function.  The following conditions
are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the Chambert--Loir--Ducros current
      $\dprimeddprime[h]$ is positive;
\item the residue class
      \[
      L_h(x)\in\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{red}(X,x))
                    \otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathbf R
      \]
      is nef for every $x\in X$;
\item $h$ is semipositive in the sense of Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, if the same $X$ is allowed to have boundary, positivity of
$\dprimeddprime[h]$ on $\operatorname{Int}(X)$ is equivalent to
semipositivity of $h|_{\operatorname{Int}(X)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The equivalence of (2) and (3) is
\cite[Proposition~7.1]{GJR26}, and (3) implies (1) by
\cite[Theorem~7.14]{GJR26}.  We prove that (1) implies (2).

Fix $x\in X$.  By \cite[Proposition~5.8 and Lemma~5.10]{GJR26}, after
shrinking to an analytic neighbourhood of $x$, write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:p01-real-pl-decomposition}
                         h=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_i h_i,
\end{equation}
where the $h_i$ are integral PL functions and
$\lambda_i\in\mathbf R$.

We first construct one integral PL function that dominates the finitely
many $h_i$ in both current directions.  Choose a compact strictly analytic
neighbourhood $Y$ of $x$, with $x\in\operatorname{Int}(Y)$, on which all
$h_i$ are defined.  By \cite[Corollary~5.9 and \S6.16]{GJR26}, after passing
to a formal model dominating the finitely many models involved, the closure
$V_0$ of the reduction of $x$ in the special fibre is a model of
$\operatorname{red}(X,x)$, and line bundles $M_{i,0}$ on $V_0$ represent
$L_{h_i}(x)$.  Since $x$ is inner, $V_0$ is proper; this is the inner-point
input cited in the proof of \cite[Proposition~7.10]{GJR26}.  As in that
proof, Chow's lemma gives a proper birational morphism
$\pi:V\to V_0$ with $V$ projective.  By \cite[\S6.2]{GJR26}, properness of
$\pi$ shows that $V$ is again a model of $\operatorname{red}(X,x)$.  Put
$M_i=\pi^*M_{i,0}$ and choose an ample line bundle $A$ on $V$.  The
realization step \cite[\S6.6.5]{CLD12}, equivalently the strict lifting
statement \cite[Theorem~17.5.7(b)]{CLD25}, gives an integral PL germ $a$
with $(V,A)$ a model of $L_a(x)$.  Shrinking once, regard $a$ and all the
$h_i$ as functions on the same neighbourhood.

For each $i$, the line bundles
\[
                         A^{\otimes N}\otimes M_i,
             \qquad     A^{\otimes N}\otimes M_i^{-1}
\]
are ample for all sufficiently large $N$.  Indeed, apply the ample-twist
criterion \cite[Tag~0892]{stacks-project} to the identity morphism of $V$,
the line bundles $M_i^{\pm1}$, and $A$.  Since the family is finite, fix one
positive integer $N$ that works for every $i$.  Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:p01-ample-domination-residues}
                       NA+M_i\quad\text{and}\quad NA-M_i
\end{equation}
are nef on $V$.  By residue linearity \cite[Lemma~6.18]{GJR26} and the
residue criterion \cite[Proposition~7.1]{GJR26}, $a$ and each $Na\pm h_i$
are semipositive at $x$.  The semipositive locus is open by
\cite[Remark~7.3(4)]{GJR26}; after intersecting the finitely many such loci,
all these functions are semipositive on one open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$.
Consequently, \cite[Theorem~7.14]{GJR26} gives positive currents on $U$
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:p01-current-domination}
 T_a:=\dprimeddprime[a]\geq0,
 \qquad NT_a+T_i\geq0,
 \qquad NT_a-T_i\geq0,
 \qquad T_i:=\dprimeddprime[h_i].
\end{equation}

Set $\varepsilon_m=1/m$.  Choose $q_i^{(m)}\in\mathbf Q$ so close to
$\lambda_i$ that, with $r_i^{(m)}=q_i^{(m)}-\lambda_i$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:p01-rational-error-budget}
                    N\sum_{i=1}^{s}|r_i^{(m)}|\leq\varepsilon_m.
\end{equation}
Define the $\mathbf Q$-PL function
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:p01-rational-positive-approximants}
                       g_m:=\sum_{i=1}^{s}q_i^{(m)}h_i
                                  +\varepsilon_m a.
\end{equation}
For a nonzero real number $r$, write $\sigma(r)=r/|r|$; when $r=0$, omit
the corresponding summand.  Linearity of residues and currents, the
assumed positivity of $T_h:=\dprimeddprime[h]$, and
\eqref{eq:p01-current-domination} give
\begin{align}
\label{eq:p01-positive-decomposition}
 \dprimeddprime[g_m]
  ={}&T_h
    +\left(\varepsilon_m-N\sum_i|r_i^{(m)}|\right)T_a \\
   &+\sum_{r_i^{(m)}\ne0}|r_i^{(m)}|
       \left(NT_a+\sigma(r_i^{(m)})T_i\right)\geq0
\end{align}
on $U$.  Notice that this argument tests positivity against every positive
compactly supported form on $U$; no choice of a tropical chart or skeleton
is involved.

Choose a positive integer $d_m$ such that $d_mg_m$ is integral PL.  Apply
\cite[Corollary~18.8.8]{CLD25} to the trivial line bundle with the integral
PL metric $\lVert1\rVert=\mathrm e^{-d_mg_m}$.  Its first Chern current is
$\dprimeddprime[d_mg_m]$.  The neighbourhood $U$ is boundaryless,
topologically separated, and pure-dimensional, so the cited corollary
applies.  In view of
\eqref{eq:p01-positive-decomposition}, its residue at $x$ is nef.  Dividing
by $d_m$ shows that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:p01-nef-approximating-residues}
             L_{g_m}(x)=\sum_iq_i^{(m)}L_{h_i}(x)
                              +\varepsilon_mL_a(x)
\end{equation}
is nef.

On the fixed projective model $V$, the class in
\eqref{eq:p01-nef-approximating-residues} is represented by
$\sum_iq_i^{(m)}M_i+\varepsilon_mA$.  By
\cite[Lemma~6.11]{GJR26}, this representative is nef.  Hence for every
proper integral curve $C\subset V$,
\[
  0\leq
  \sum_iq_i^{(m)}\deg_C(M_i)+\varepsilon_m\deg_C(A).
\]
Here $q_i^{(m)}\to\lambda_i$ by
\eqref{eq:p01-rational-error-budget}, and $\varepsilon_m\to0$.  Taking the
limit yields
\[
                       \sum_i\lambda_i\deg_C(M_i)\geq0.
\]
This holds for every proper integral curve on $V$, so
$\sum_i\lambda_iM_i$ is nef.  It represents
$L_h(x)=\sum_i\lambda_iL_{h_i}(x)$ by
\cite[Lemma~6.18]{GJR26}; hence $L_h(x)$ is nef.  Since $x$ was arbitrary,
(2) holds.

For a pure-dimensional analytic space with boundary, its interior is a
boundaryless pure-dimensional open analytic subspace.  Apply the statement
already proved to $\operatorname{Int}(X)$.  This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

This is a book-level deduction from the cited results; no novelty or priority
claim is made.  The argument uses the ample class only as a finite-dimensional
order unit.  It does not assert a pullback theorem for arbitrary positive CLD
currents.

The vanishing case admits a second proof that exposes the rational lattice
calibration along codimension-one faces.  Keeping the analytic cells of a
skeletal current separate, rather than passing only to their possibly
self-overlapping tropical image, recovers precisely that information.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:ch18-real-pl-zero-current-harmonic}
Let $K$ be a complete nontrivially valued non-Archimedean field, and let
$X$ be a good, separated, boundaryless, strictly $K$-analytic space that is
pure of dimension $n\geq1$.  If $h:X\to\mathbf R$ is an $\mathbf R$-PL
function and its Chambert--Loir--Ducros current satisfies
\[
                         \dprimeddprime[h]=0,
\]
then $h$ is harmonic in the sense of Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff.  Equivalently,
the residue class
\[
 L_h(x)\in\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{red}(X,x))
                  \otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathbf R
\]
is numerically trivial for every $x\in X$.  Consequently, $h$ is
classically pluriharmonic in the sense of Gubler--Rabinoff.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x\in X$.  By \cite[Proposition~5.8 and Lemma~5.10]{GJR26}, after
shrinking to an analytic neighbourhood $W_0$ of $x$, one can write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch18-zero-current-independent}
                         h=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_i h_i,
\end{equation}
where the $h_i$ are integral PL functions on $W_0$ and the real numbers
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s$ are linearly independent over $\mathbf Q$.
By \cite[Proposition~8.3 and \S8.4]{GJR26}, after replacing $W_0$ by a
smaller analytic neighbourhood of $x$, choose a smooth tropicalization
\[
                         F:W_0\longrightarrow\mathbf R^N
\]
simultaneously tropicalizing $h_1,\ldots,h_s$ on $W_0$.  Thus
$h_i=\varphi_i\circ F$, with $\varphi_i$ piecewise
$(\mathbf Z,\Gamma)$-affine.  Choose a compact strictly affinoid
neighbourhood $A\subset W_0$ of $x$ with $x\in\operatorname{Int}(A)$, and
then choose an open neighbourhood $W$ of $x$ with
$W\subset\operatorname{Int}(A)$.  Restrict the $h_i$ and $F$ to $W$.
Since boundarylessness and purity are local here, we may suppose that $W$
is boundaryless and pure of dimension $n$.  We prove that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch18-zero-current-integral}
                         \dprimeddprime[h_i]=0
                         \qquad(1\leq i\leq s)
\end{equation}
on a neighbourhood of $x$.

Let $T_0=[W]$ be the integration current.  By
\cite[Example~7.5.3]{CLD25}, $T_0$ is a closed skeletal current of dimension
$n$, described in each moment map by the canonical tropical calibration.
For each $i$, put
\[
                         T_i:=\dprimeddprime(h_iT_0)
                             =\dprimeddprime[h_i].
\]
By \cite[Theorem~8.10.3]{CLD25}, $T_i$ is a skeletal current of dimension
$n-1$.

We recall the arithmetic in the construction in the proof of that theorem,
using the fixed tropicalization $F$ defined on all of $W$.  Let
$P_F^{T_0}\subset W$ be the minimal
canonically calibrated skeleton describing $T_0$ relative to $F$, as in
\cite[Propositions~7.5.7--7.5.8]{CLD25}.  In particular, $F$ is an immersion
on each cell of $P_F^{T_0}$; distinct analytic cells are not identified in
this description, even if their images overlap in $F(W)$.  Choose a common
polyhedral subdivision $\mathscr C$ of $P_F^{T_0}$ on which all the
$\varphi_i\circ F$ are affine.  A top-dimensional cell $C$ carries its
canonical calibration.  By
\cite[Proposition~14.3.1 and \S14.3.2]{CLD25}, it is rational with respect to
the integral tangent lattice, so it has the form
\[
                         \mu_C=q_C\mu_C^{\mathrm{prim}},
                         \qquad q_C\in\mathbf Q_{>0},
\]
where $\mu_C^{\mathrm{prim}}$ is the primitive lattice volume transported
through $F|_C$.  If $D$ is a codimension-one cell and $\nu_D$ is its
primitive lattice volume, the formula in the proof of
\cite[Theorem~8.10.3]{CLD25} describes $T_i$ along $D$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch18-zero-current-calibration}
                         \mu_{i,D}=a_{i,D}\nu_D,
                         \qquad a_{i,D}\in\mathbf Q.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the analytic degree entering the canonical tropical multiplicity is
an integer, while its comparison with the primitive tangent lattice is
rational by the cited propositions.  The derivative of the integral-affine
function $\varphi_i\circ F|_C$ in a primitive normal direction is an integer,
and every lattice transition factor is rational.  With the orientation sign
used in the boundary integral, the contribution from each cell $C$ adjacent
to $D$ is therefore rational.  Summing the finitely many contributions gives
$a_{i,D}\in\mathbf Q$.  This is the coefficient of the calibrated tropical
divisor of $\varphi_i\circ F$ on the weighted skeleton.  A further common
refinement subdivides these calibrated cycles and preserves rationality.

By linearity and \eqref{eq:ch18-zero-current-independent}, the skeletal
current $\sum_i\lambda_iT_i=\dprimeddprime[h]$ is described on the same
subdivision along $D$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch18-zero-current-sum-calibration}
                         \left(\sum_i\lambda_i a_{i,D}\right)\nu_D.
\end{equation}
The assumed current identity says that this skeletal current is zero.  Let
$|\mathscr Q|\subset W$ denote the source-side support of the resulting
calibrated codimension-one skeleton.  Consequently the calibration in
\eqref{eq:ch18-zero-current-sum-calibration} vanishes on every
codimension-one cell.  Indeed, suppose that it were nonzero on the relative
interior of some $D$.  Choose a compact polyhedral patch
$K_D\Subset D^\circ$ with nonempty relative interior.  In the source-side
skeleton, choose an ambient open neighbourhood $V\subset W$ of $K_D$ such
that
\[
                         V\cap|\mathscr Q|\subset D^\circ.
\]
This is possible because the source-side subdivision is locally finite and
$K_D$ is contained in the interior of its cell.  In particular, overlapping
target images create no additional source-side incidence.  By
\cite[Corollary~6.2.4]{CLD25}, there is a nonnegative smooth function $\chi$
which is equal to $1$ on a neighbourhood of $K_D$ and satisfies
$\operatorname{Supp}(\chi)\subset V$.
Choose a constant Lagerberg $(n-1,n-1)$-form whose evaluation on $\nu_D$ is
nonzero, and change its sign if necessary.  Pairing the current with the
test form obtained by multiplying its pullback by $\chi$, the source-side
formula of \cite[Definition~7.5.1]{CLD25} gives a strictly positive value, a
contradiction.  Notice that distinct analytic cells remain distinct in this
formula even if their images under $F$ overlap.  This source-side separation
is part of the skeletal-current description and its uniqueness in
\cite[Definition~7.5.1 and Propositions~7.5.7--7.5.8]{CLD25}.  Thus
\[
                         \sum_i\lambda_i a_{i,D}=0.
\]
Since $a_{i,D}\in\mathbf Q$ and the $\lambda_i$ are $\mathbf Q$-linearly
independent, $a_{i,D}=0$ for every $i$ and $D$.  Hence the calibrated
skeletal description of each $T_i$ is zero in the fixed chart $F$ and its
polyhedral refinements.

It remains to verify the full current identity, not merely a fixed-chart
pushforward.  Let $\alpha$ be any smooth compactly supported
$(n-1,n-1)$-test form in $W$.  By the compact-support localized partition of
unity of \cite[Corollary~6.2.8]{CLD25}, together with the determination of
smooth forms by localized terms in \cite[Lemma~7.2.5]{CLD25}, it is a
finite sum of terms $\rho\omega$, where $\rho$ is a smooth compactly
supported function and $\omega$ is tropicalized by a moment map $H$ on an
open set $V\subset W$ containing
$\operatorname{Supp}(\rho)$.  Since the fixed $F$ is defined on all of $W$,
the common refinement $(F|_V,H)$ is defined on $V$ and still simultaneously
tropicalizes $h_1,\ldots,h_s$.  The same computation on a common subdivision
again has rational coefficients.  Since the calibrated coefficients of
$\sum_i\lambda_iT_i=0$ vanish and the $\lambda_i$ are $\mathbf Q$-linearly
independent, the coefficient of every $T_i$ on every source-side cell of
this common refinement is zero.  The defining identity in
\cite[Definition~7.5.1]{CLD25} then gives
\[
                         \langle T_i,\rho\omega\rangle=0.
\]
Summing the localized terms proves \eqref{eq:ch18-zero-current-integral}.
Notice that no analytic current is pulled back and no target representative
is required to avoid the image of the boundary of a compact analytic
domain.  Compact support is carried by $\rho$ on the open boundaryless
space, exactly as in the definition of a skeletal current.

For each $i$, apply \cite[Theorem~18.8.4]{CLD25} with $d=1$ to the trivial
line bundle with the integral PL metric determined by $h_i$.  The separated
and boundaryless hypotheses are satisfied, and
\eqref{eq:ch18-zero-current-integral} implies that $L_{h_i}(x)$ is
numerically trivial.  The residue construction is linear, so
\[
                         L_h(x)=\sum_i\lambda_iL_{h_i}(x)
\]
is numerically trivial.  Since $x$ was arbitrary,
\cite[Proposition~7.1 and Definition~7.2]{GJR26} imply that $h$ is harmonic.

Finally, harmonicity means that $h$ and $-h$ are semipositive.  The field is
nontrivially valued, $X$ is good and strict, and both functions are
$\mathbf R$-PL.  Hence \cite[Theorem~4.3]{GR25Classical} makes both functions
classically psh, so $h$ is classically pluriharmonic.
\end{proof}

The proof of \Cref{prop:ch18-real-pl-zero-current-harmonic} is a book-level
consequence of the cited CLD, Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff, and
Gubler--Rabinoff results; no novelty or priority claim is made.  The
rational calibrated coefficients and the refinement by the chart of an
arbitrary test form are essential.  In particular, the proof neither pulls
back a CLD current nor uses a target-side cutoff to avoid the image of the
boundary.

\begin{warning}{The continuous zero-current problem}
\Cref{prop:ch18-real-pl-zero-current-harmonic} settles the implication for
$\mathbf R$-PL functions under its hypotheses.  For an arbitrary continuous
function $u$ in higher dimension, however, it is not known in this
generality whether $\dprimeddprime[u]=0$ forces harmonicity or classical
pluriharmonicity: there is no residual line bundle or integral skeletal
calibration from which to recover the face-balancing law, and arbitrary CLD
currents cannot simply be pulled back to all curve probes.  CLD further note
in \cite[Remark~7.8.3]{CLD25} that it is not even known whether local
integrability plus $\dprimeddprime[u]=0$ forces continuity.  The published
continuous result on smooth proper algebraic curves is recorded in
\Cref{cor:ch19-continuous-zero-current-harmonic}; see also
\Cref{def:ch16-harmonic-function,sec:ch16-harmonic-ambiguity}.
\end{warning}

There is nevertheless an exact continuous factor scope in which proper
pushforward recovers the full Euclidean Hessian.  The result below is a
book-level deduction from the cited sources; no novelty or priority claim is
made.  Its factorization, full-image, properness, and calibration hypotheses
are part of the conclusion's boundary, not a construction available for an
arbitrary continuous function.

\begin{corollary}[Local proper-full-rank moment Bedford--Taylor criterion]
\label{cor:ch18-local-proper-full-rank-moment-bt}
Let $K$ be a complete nontrivially valued non-Archimedean field, let $U$ be
an open set in a $K$-analytic space, let $T$ be a positive closed CLD current
on $U$, and let $u,v$ be continuous psh functions on $U$ with
\[
                    \dprimeddprime[u]=\dprimeddprime[v].
\]
Put $h=u-v$.  Suppose that, for every $x\in U$, there is an open
neighbourhood $U_x\subseteq U$ for which $h|_{U_x}$ factors through a
proper full-rank moment and satisfies every hypothesis of
\Cref{prop:p062-proper-full-rank-moment}, applied with $X=U_x$.  Then
\[
                 \dprimeddprime(uT)=\dprimeddprime(vT)
                 \quad\text{on }U.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The equality of the two Hessian currents restricts to
$\dprimeddprime[h|_{U_x}]=0$ on every $U_x$.  The affine source formula
in \Cref{prop:p062-proper-full-rank-moment} therefore makes $h|_{U_x}$ an
ordinary CLD-smooth function with zero smooth Hessian.  Taking the constant
approximating sequence $h_{x,j}=h|_{U_x}$ verifies the hypotheses of

\paragraph{Strict open-disc descent.}
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:p062-open-disc-descent}
Let $K$ be a complete non-Archimedean field with nontrivial absolute
value, let $m\geq1$, and let $B$ be a good, topologically separated,
pure $m$-dimensional strict $K$-analytic space.  For
$R\in|K^\times|$, put
\[
 D_R^-=\{x\in(\mathbf A^1_K)^{\mathrm{an}}:|T(x)|<R\},
 \qquad X=B\times_KD_R^-,
\]
and write $\pi:X\to B$ and $q:X\to D_R^-$ for the projections.  If
$h\in C^0(X,\mathbf R)$ satisfies
\[
                         \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0,
\]
then there is a unique $b\in C^0(B,\mathbf R)$ such that
\[
                         h=\pi^*b,
                         \qquad
                         \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0.
\]
Moreover, $\mathrm d'[h]=0$ implies $\mathrm d'[b]=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The disc $D_R^-$ is good, topologically separated, strict,
one-dimensional, and boundaryless.  Hence
\Cref{prop:p062-strict-product-fibre-slicing-v4-research} applies.  For
every $x\in B$, it makes the restriction of $h$ to
\[
                     X_x\simeq (D_R^-)_{\mathscr H(x)}
\]
a zero-current function over $\mathscr H(x)$.  The radius still belongs
to $|\mathscr H(x)^\times|$, so
\Cref{prop:p062-arbitrary-field-disc} makes this restriction constant.
The zero section $s(x)=(x,0)$ therefore defines a continuous function
$b=h\circ s$ with $h=\pi^*b$; it also proves uniqueness.

Choose a compactly supported ordinary CLD-smooth $(1,1)$-form
$\eta$ on $D_R^-$ with $\int_{D_R^-}\eta=1$.  Such a form is
obtained by pulling a nonzero nonnegative compactly supported Lagerberg top
form back from a relatively compact logarithmic interval in a strict
subannulus and normalizing; the calibrated integration formula is
\cite[Proposition~11.1 and Example~11.7]{GJR26}.  Let $\beta$ be a
compactly supported ordinary smooth $(m-1,m-1)$-form on $B$.  Since
$\eta$ has top bidegree on the disc,
$\mathrm d'\eta=\mathrm d''\eta=0$, and consequently
\[
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''\bigl(\pi^*\beta\wedge q^*\eta\bigr)
       =\pi^*(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''\beta)\wedge q^*\eta.
\]
The left-hand test form is compactly supported on $X$.  Pairing it with
the zero current of $h$, using $h=\pi^*b$, and applying fibre
integration gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0
 &=\int_X h\,\pi^*(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''\beta)\wedge q^*\eta\\
 &=\left(\int_{D_R^-}\eta\right)
   \int_B b\,\mathrm d'\mathrm d''\beta
  =\bigl\langle\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b],\beta\bigr\rangle.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here the continuous-multiplier form of the fibre-integration identity
follows from uniform ordinary-smooth approximation with a cutoff and the
projection formula \cite[Theorem~16.5.4 and Corollary~16.5.5]{CLD25}; this
is the same compact-support argument used in the proof of
\Cref{prop:p062-strict-product-fibre-slicing-v4-research}.  Since this holds
for every $\beta$, the asserted zero-current equality for $b$ follows.

Suppose in addition that $\mathrm d'[h]=0$.  For every compactly
supported ordinary smooth $(m-1,m)$-form $\beta$ on $B$, the same
top-degree fibre form satisfies
\[
 \mathrm d'\bigl(\pi^*\beta\wedge q^*\eta\bigr)
       =\pi^*(\mathrm d'\beta)\wedge q^*\eta.
\]
The defining pairing for the first current and the same fibre-integration
identity therefore give
\[
 0=\bigl\langle\mathrm d'[h],\pi^*\beta\wedge q^*\eta\bigr\rangle
   =\mathord\pm\left(\int_{D_R^-}\eta\right)
      \bigl\langle\mathrm d'[b],\beta\bigr\rangle.
\]
Thus $\mathrm d'[b]=0$, as claimed.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Strict open-annulus products over a published curve.}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:p062-open-annulus-products-over-curves}
Let $K$, $C$, and $W\subset C^{\mathrm{an}}$ be as in
\Cref{thm:wanner-curve-comparison}.  Let
\[
 A=A^-_{r,R}=
 \{z\in(\mathbf G_{\mathrm m,K})^{\mathrm{an}}:r<|T(z)|<R\},
 \qquad 0<r<R,\quad r,R\in|K^\times|,
\]
and write $\pi:W\times_KA\to W$ and $q:W\times_KA\to A$ for
the projections.  If
\[
 h\in C^0(W\times_KA,\mathbf R),
 \qquad \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0,
\]
then there are unique continuous functions $a,b:W\to\mathbf R$ such
that
\[
 h=\pi^*b+(a\circ\pi)q^*\log|T|.
\]
Moreover, $a$ is locally constant, $\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0$,
and $h$ is classically pluriharmonic.  In fact it is locally
$\mathbf R$-PL and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $X=W\times_KA$ and $L=\log|T|$.  The strict open annulus is
good, topologically separated, strict, one-dimensional, and boundaryless.
Thus \Cref{prop:p062-strict-product-fibre-slicing-v4-research} shows that
the restriction $h_x$ to every fibre
$X_x\simeq A_{\mathscr H(x)}$ has zero current.  The two radii remain
in $|\mathscr H(x)^\times|$, so
\Cref{prop:p062-arbitrary-field-annulus-v4-source-correct} gives unique
numbers $a(x),b(x)$ with
\[
                         h_x=a(x)L+b(x).
\tag{$*$}\label{eq:p062-open-annulus-fibre-affine}
\]
Choose two distinct logarithmic radii in $(\log r,\log R)$.  The
relative Gauss sections at those radii are continuous by the
Shilov-section construction \cite[\S\S12.1.10--12.1.11]{CLD25}.
Evaluating \eqref{eq:p062-open-annulus-fibre-affine} on them and solving
the resulting nonsingular two-by-two system proves that $a$ and $b$
are continuous; it also proves their uniqueness.

Choose compactly supported nonnegative ordinary smooth $(1,1)$-forms
$\eta_0,\eta_1$ on $A$, supported in two disjoint logarithmic
subintervals, and set
\[
 N_i=\int_A\eta_i,\qquad M_i=\int_A L\,\eta_i,
 \qquad H_i=M_i a+N_i b \quad (i=0,1).
\]
The forms can be chosen so that $N_0M_1-N_1M_0\ne0$: normalize two
nonzero nonnegative Lagerberg top forms pulled back from disjoint compact
subintervals of the skeleton.  For every compactly supported ordinary
smooth function $\beta$ on $W$, the form
$\pi^*\beta\wedge q^*\eta_i$ is a compactly supported test form on
$X$, and its mixed differential is
\[
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''(\pi^*\beta\wedge q^*\eta_i)
       =\pi^*(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''\beta)\wedge q^*\eta_i.
\]
Pairing with the zero current of $h$, using \eqref{eq:p062-open-annulus-fibre-affine}, and applying fibre integration therefore gives
\[
 0=\int_Xh\,\pi^*(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''\beta)\wedge q^*\eta_i
   =\int_W H_i\,\mathrm d'\mathrm d''\beta.
\]
The continuous-multiplier fibre-integration step is exactly the
compact-support approximation and projection-formula argument in the proof
of \Cref{prop:p062-open-disc-descent}.  Hence
$\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[H_i]=0$.  Inverting the displayed constant
matrix gives
\[
              \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[a]=0,
              \qquad \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0.
\tag{$**$}\label{eq:p062-open-annulus-coefficients-zero-current}
\]

It remains to use the mixed part of the equation, which is what rules out
a varying annular slope.  Write $u=-L$, choose a nonzero nonnegative
smooth function $\chi$ compactly supported in
$( -\log R,-\log r)$, and put
\[
                         \gamma=\chi(u)\,\mathrm d'u.
\]
This is a compactly supported ordinary smooth $(1,0)$-form on $A$.
Since $u$ is affine in the annular moment coordinate, the calibrated
one-dimensional integration formula and integration by parts give
\[
 c_\chi:=\int_A L\,\mathrm d''\gamma
 =\mathord\pm\int u\chi'(u)\,\mathrm du
 =\mathord\mp\int\chi(u)\,\mathrm du\ne0.
\tag{$***$}\label{eq:p062-open-annulus-nonzero-pairing}
\]
Here one may use the same compactly supported skeleton forms and
calibrated integration as in \Cref{prop:p062-open-disc-descent}; compact
support also gives $\int_A\mathrm d''\gamma=0$.

Let $\beta$ now be an arbitrary compactly supported ordinary smooth
$(0,1)$-form on $W$.  For
$\omega=\pi^*\beta\wedge q^*\gamma$, the base and fibre dimensions,
together with $\mathrm d'\mathrm d''L=0$, give
\[
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''\omega
   =\mathord\pm\pi^*(\mathrm d'\beta)\wedge q^*(\mathrm d''\gamma).
\]
Thus the zero-current equation, \eqref{eq:p062-open-annulus-fibre-affine},
and fibre integration yield
\[
 0=\mathord\pm c_\chi\int_Wa\,\mathrm d'\beta.
\]
The second coefficient $b$ disappears because
$\int_A\mathrm d''\gamma=0$.  As $c_\chi\ne0$, the defining
pairing for first derivatives of currents shows that
\[
                              \mathrm d'[a]=0.
\tag{$\dagger$}\label{eq:p062-open-annulus-slope-current}
\]

By \eqref{eq:p062-open-annulus-coefficients-zero-current} and
\Cref{cor:ch19-continuous-zero-current-harmonic}, $a$ is Thuillier
harmonic.  On a connected strictly affinoid semistable neighbourhood, such
a germ is the pullback of a piecewise-affine function on its skeleton
\cite[Definition~2.3.1 and Definition~2.3.16]{Thu05}.  Testing
\eqref{eq:p062-open-annulus-slope-current} on a compactly supported
$(0,1)$-form in the interior of any skeleton edge makes the affine slope
on that edge zero.  The pullback description, continuity at vertices, and
connectedness then make $a$ constant on that neighbourhood.  Hence $a$
is locally constant on $W$.

Finally, \eqref{eq:p062-open-annulus-coefficients-zero-current} and
\Cref{cor:ch19-continuous-zero-current-harmonic} make $b$ classically
pluriharmonic.  On a neighbourhood on which $a=c$ is constant,
$c\log|T|$ is classically pluriharmonic by
\Cref{thm:p062-arbitrary-field-polycylinder-v4-source-correct}; analytic
pullback preserves classical psh \cite[Proposition~4.2(6)]{GR25Classical}.
Both signs of their sum are therefore classically psh, proving the stated
pluriharmonicity of $h$.  The local $\mathbf R$-PL and harmonic
conclusions follow from \Cref{thm:p061-arbitrary-curve-factors-ph-rpl}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Annular coefficient descent over a strict base.}
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:p062-annulus-coefficient-descent}
Let $K$ be a complete non-Archimedean field with nontrivial absolute
value, let $m\geq1$, let $B$ be a good, topologically separated, pure
$m$-dimensional strict $K$-analytic space, and let
\[
 A=A^-_{r,R}=\{z\in(\mathbf G_{\mathrm m,K})^{\mathrm{an}}:
                         r<|T(z)|<R\},
 \qquad 0<r<R,\quad r,R\in|K^\times|.
\]
Write $\pi:B\times_KA\to B$, $q:B\times_KA\to A$, and
$L=q^*\log|T|$.  If
\[
 h\in C^0(B\times_KA,\mathbf R),\qquad
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0,
\]
then there are unique continuous functions $a,b:B\to\mathbf R$ such
that
\[
                             h=\pi^*b+(a\circ\pi)L.
\]
They satisfy
\[
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[a]=\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0,
 \qquad \mathrm d'[a]=0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The strict-product fibre-slicing proposition applied to the annulus factor,
followed by the arbitrary-field annulus classification, gives unique
numbers $a(x),b(x)$ on every completed-residue-field fibre with

\[
                         h_x=a(x)\log|T|+b(x).
\tag{$\star$}\label{eq:p062-annulus-base-fibre-affine}
\]
Two distinct relative Gauss sections of the annulus are continuous
\cite[\S\S12.1.10--12.1.11]{CLD25}; evaluating \eqref{eq:p062-annulus-base-fibre-affine}
on them proves that $a$ and $b$ are continuous and proves uniqueness.

Choose compactly supported nonnegative ordinary smooth $(1,1)$-forms
$\eta_0,\eta_1$ on $A$, supported in disjoint logarithmic subintervals,
for which
\[
 N_0M_1-N_1M_0\ne0,
 \qquad N_i=\int_A\eta_i,\quad M_i=\int_A\log|T|\,\eta_i.
\]
For a compactly supported ordinary smooth $(m-1,m-1)$-form $\beta$ on
$B$, the test form $\pi^*\beta\wedge q^*\eta_i$ has mixed differential
\[
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''(\pi^*\beta\wedge q^*\eta_i)
   =\pi^*(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''\beta)\wedge q^*\eta_i.
\]
Consequently, compact-support fibre integration gives
\[
 0=\int_{B\times_KA}h\,\pi^*(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''\beta)\wedge q^*\eta_i
  =\int_B(M_i a+N_i b)\,\mathrm d'\mathrm d''\beta.
\]
The continuous-multiplier identity is precisely the uniform
ordinary-smooth approximation with cutoff and the projection formula used in
the proof of \Cref{prop:p062-open-disc-descent}; see
\cite[Theorem~16.5.4 and Corollary~16.5.5]{CLD25}.  Thus both
$\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[M_i a+N_i b]$ vanish, and inversion of the
constant two-by-two matrix proves
\[
              \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[a]=0,
              \qquad \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0.
\tag{$\star\star$}\label{eq:p062-annulus-base-coefficients-zero-current}
\]

Put $u=-\log|T|$, choose a nonzero nonnegative smooth function $\chi$
compactly supported in $(-\log R,-\log r)$, and set
$\gamma=\chi(u)\,\mathrm d'u$.  The calibrated one-dimensional
integration formula gives
\[
 c_\chi:=\int_A\log|T|\,\mathrm d''\gamma
       =\mathord\mp\int\chi(u)\,\mathrm du\ne0,
 \qquad \int_A\mathrm d''\gamma=0.
\]
For every compactly supported ordinary smooth $(m-1,m)$-form $\beta$
on $B$, the compactly supported $(m,m)$-form
$\omega=\pi^*\beta\wedge q^*\gamma$ satisfies
\[
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''\omega
   =\mathord\pm\pi^*(\mathrm d'\beta)\wedge q^*(\mathrm d''\gamma).
\]
Pairing with the zero current of $h$, using
\eqref{eq:p062-annulus-base-fibre-affine}, and integrating along the fibre
now yields
\[
                   0=\mathord\pm c_\chi\int_Ba\,\mathrm d'\beta.
\]
The $b$-term vanishes because $\int_A\mathrm d''\gamma=0$.  Since
$c_\chi\ne0$, the defining pairing of the first differential of a
current gives $\mathrm d'[a]=0$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite strict open-polyannuli over a zero-Hessian-rigid base.}
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:p062-open-polyannulus-rigid-base}
Let $K$ be a complete non-Archimedean field with nontrivial absolute
value, let $m\geq1$, and let $B$ be a good, topologically separated,
boundaryless, pure $m$-dimensional strict $K$-analytic space.  Assume
that
\begin{enumerate}
\item every $b\in C^0(B,\mathbf R)$ satisfying
  $\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0$ is classically pluriharmonic; and
\item every $b\in C^0(B,\mathbf R)$ satisfying
  $\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0$ and $\mathrm d'[b]=0$ is locally
  constant.
\end{enumerate}
For $s\geq0$, let
\[
 A_i=\{z\in(\mathbf G_{\mathrm m,K})^{\mathrm{an}}:
                     r_i<|T_i(z)|<R_i\},
 \qquad 0<r_i<R_i,\quad r_i,R_i\in|K^\times|,
\]
and put
\[
 X_s=B\times_K\prod_{i=1}^sA_i,
 \qquad p:X_s\longrightarrow B,
 \qquad q_i:X_s\longrightarrow A_i.
\]
If $h\in C^0(X_s,\mathbf R)$ satisfies
$\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0$, then there are unique continuous
functions $b,a_1,\ldots,a_s:B\to\mathbf R$ such that
\[
 h=p^*b+\sum_{i=1}^s(a_i\circ p)q_i^*\log|T_i|,
 \qquad \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0,
\]
and every $a_i$ is locally constant.  In particular, $h$ is
classically pluriharmonic.  Moreover, every
$u\in C^0(X_s,\mathbf R)$ satisfying
\[
             \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[u]=0,
             \qquad \mathrm d'[u]=0
\]
is locally constant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $X_0=B$, and write $p_j:X_j\to B$ for the evident projection.
We prove by induction on $j$ both the displayed
coefficient formula and the final first-current rigidity assertion for
$X_j$.  At $j=0$, these are exactly the two hypotheses on $B$.

Suppose the assertions hold for $X_{j-1}$, and write
$X_j=X_{j-1}\times_KA_j$.  Applying
\Cref{prop:p062-annulus-coefficient-descent} with base $X_{j-1}$ gives
unique continuous functions $a,c$ on $X_{j-1}$ with
\[
 h=\pi^*c+(a\circ\pi)q_j^*\log|T_j|,
 \qquad
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[a]=\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[c]=0,
 \qquad
 \mathrm d'[a]=0.
\]
The induction rigidity assertion makes $a$ locally constant.  Every
fibre of $X_{j-1}\to B$ is a connected finite product of standard strict
open annuli.  Evaluating $a$ on the product of fixed relative Gauss
sections therefore gives a unique continuous locally constant function
$a_j:B\to\mathbf R$ with
\[
                             a=p_{j-1}^*a_j.
\]
Applying the induction coefficient formula to $c$ now gives the stated
formula at level $j$, including uniqueness.  The induction hypothesis
makes $c$ classically pluriharmonic.  On a neighbourhood on which all
the $a_i$ are constant, each logarithmic summand is classically
pluriharmonic by
\Cref{thm:p062-arbitrary-field-polycylinder-v4-source-correct}; analytic
pullback preserves classical psh \cite[Proposition~4.2(6)]{GR25Classical}.
Thus $h$ is classically pluriharmonic.

It remains to prove rigidity at level $j$.  Let $u$ have zero Hessian
and $\mathrm d'[u]=0$.  The formula just proved writes
\[
 u=p_j^*b+\sum_{i=1}^j(a_i\circ p_j)q_i^*\log|T_i|.
\]
Fix $i$, regard $X_j$ as $B_i\times_KA_i$, where
$B_i=B\times_K\prod_{k\ne i}A_k$, let $p_i:B_i\to B$ be the
projection, and collect the terms independent of $A_i$ into a continuous
function $v_i$ on $B_i$.  Thus
\[
 u=\pi_i^*v_i+(a_i\circ p_i)q_i^*\log|T_i|.
\]
Choose a compactly supported ordinary smooth top-bidegree form $\alpha$
on $B_i$, put $w_i=-\log|T_i|$, choose a nonzero nonnegative smooth
cutoff $\chi$ in its logarithmic interval, and set
$\bar\gamma_i=\chi(w_i)\,\mathrm d''w_i$.  The calibrated
one-dimensional calculation gives
\[
 \bar c_i:=\int_{A_i}\log|T_i|\,\mathrm d'\bar\gamma_i\ne0,
 \qquad
 \int_{A_i}\mathrm d'\bar\gamma_i=0.
\]
The defining pairing with the zero first current gives
\[
 0=\mathord\pm\bar c_i\int_{B_i}(a_i\circ p_i)\,\alpha.
\]
The full-dimensional top-form detection input for a boundaryless pure space,
recalled in \Cref{rem:ch14-form-injectivity}, shows that top forms supported
in product charts detect the continuous function $a_i\circ p_i$ on the
good, boundaryless, pure strict space $B_i$.  Hence $a_i=0$.  This holds
for every $i$, so $u=p_j^*b$.  Choose compactly supported
ordinary smooth top-bidegree forms of integral one on the annular factors.
Pairing $\mathrm d'[u]=0$ with their product and with an arbitrary
compactly supported ordinary smooth $(m-1,m)$-form on $B$ gives
$\mathrm d'[b]=0$.  The second hypothesis on $B$ makes $b$, and
thus $u$, locally constant.  This completes the induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[First-current rigidity on a published curve]
\label{lem:p062-curve-first-current-rigidity}
Let $K$, $C$, and $W\subset C^{\mathrm{an}}$ be as in
\Cref{thm:wanner-curve-comparison}.  If $b\in C^0(W,\mathbf R)$ satisfies
\[
          \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0,\qquad \mathrm d'[b]=0,
\]
then $b$ is locally constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{cor:ch19-continuous-zero-current-harmonic}, $b$ is Thuillier
harmonic.  On a connected strictly affinoid semistable neighbourhood, its
germ is the pullback of a piecewise-affine function on the skeleton
\cite[Definition~2.3.1 and Definition~2.3.16]{Thu05}.  Testing
$\mathrm d'[b]=0$ on a compactly supported ordinary smooth
$(0,1)$-form in the interior of each skeleton edge makes every affine
slope zero.  The pullback description and continuity at the vertices then
make $b$ constant on that neighbourhood.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite strict open-polyannulus products over a published curve.}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:p062-open-polyannulus-products-over-curves}
Let $K$, $C$, and $W\subset C^{\mathrm{an}}$ be as in
\Cref{thm:wanner-curve-comparison}.  Let $s\geq0$, and for
$1\leq i\leq s$ put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 A_i=A^-_{r_i,R_i}
 =\{z\in(\mathbf G_{\mathrm m,K})^{\mathrm{an}}:
                  r_i<|T_i(z)|<R_i\},\\
 \qquad 0<r_i<R_i,\quad r_i,R_i\in|K^\times|.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Write
\[
 X_s=W\times_K\prod_{i=1}^sA_i,
 \qquad p:X_s\longrightarrow W,
 \qquad q_i:X_s\longrightarrow A_i.
\]
If $h\in C^0(X_s,\mathbf R)$ satisfies
$\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0$, then there are unique continuous functions
$b,a_1,\ldots,a_s:W\to\mathbf R$ such that
\[
 h=p^*b+\sum_{i=1}^s(a_i\circ p)q_i^*\log|T_i|.
\tag{$\heartsuit$}\label{eq:p062-open-polyannulus-curve-formula}
\]
Here $\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0$, every $a_i$ is locally constant,
and $h$ is classically pluriharmonic.  In fact it is locally
$\mathbf R$-PL and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $X_0=W$.  We prove simultaneously by induction on $j\geq0$ the
formula \eqref{eq:p062-open-polyannulus-curve-formula} on $X_j$, and the
following first-current rigidity assertion:
\[
 u\in C^0(X_j,\mathbf R),\quad
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[u]=0,\quad\mathrm d'[u]=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\text{ is locally constant}.
\tag{$\clubsuit_j$}\label{eq:p062-open-polyannulus-first-rigidity}
\]
At level $j$, all projections in the formula are the evident projections
of $X_j$.
For $j=0$, the formula and the pluriharmonic conclusion are
\Cref{cor:ch19-continuous-zero-current-harmonic}, and
\eqref{eq:p062-open-polyannulus-first-rigidity} is
\Cref{lem:p062-curve-first-current-rigidity}.

Suppose the two assertions have been proved through $j-1$, and write
$X_j=X_{j-1}\times_KA_j$.  Applying
\Cref{prop:p062-annulus-coefficient-descent} with base $X_{j-1}$ gives
unique continuous $a,b:X_{j-1}\to\mathbf R$ with
\[
 h=\pi^*b+(a\circ\pi)q_j^*\log|T_j|,
 \qquad
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[a]=\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0,
 \qquad \mathrm d'[a]=0.
\]
The induction hypothesis \eqref{eq:p062-open-polyannulus-first-rigidity}
for $j-1$ makes $a$ locally constant.  The fibres of
$X_{j-1}\to W$ are connected finite products of standard strict open
annuli.  Thus $a$ is constant on those fibres; evaluating it on the
product of fixed relative Gauss sections gives a unique continuous locally
constant $a_j:W\to\mathbf R$ with
$a=p_{j-1}^*a_j$.  Applying the induction formula to $b$ now gives
exactly \eqref{eq:p062-open-polyannulus-curve-formula} at level $j$.
Fibrewise uniqueness followed by the induction uniqueness proves uniqueness
of all displayed coefficients.  The induction conclusion makes $b$
classically pluriharmonic; on a neighbourhood where each $a_i$ is
constant, every summand in
\eqref{eq:p062-open-polyannulus-curve-formula} is classically
pluriharmonic.  Thus so is $h$.  Since $X_j$ is a finite product of the
published curve domain with strict analytic curves,
\Cref{thm:p061-arbitrary-curve-factors-ph-rpl} gives the stated local
$\mathbf R$-PL and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff conclusions.

It remains to prove \eqref{eq:p062-open-polyannulus-first-rigidity} at
level $j$.  Let $u$ satisfy its hypotheses and use the just-proved
formula for $u$.  Fix $i$.  Regard $X_j$ as
$B_i\times_KA_i$, where
$B_i=W\times_K\prod_{k\ne i}A_k$, and collect every term independent of
$A_i$ into a continuous function $v_i$ on $B_i$.  Thus
\[
 u=\pi_i^*v_i+(a_i\circ p_i)q_i^*\log|T_i|.
\]
Let $\alpha$ be any compactly supported ordinary smooth top-bidegree form
on $B_i$.  With the same $u_i=-\log|T_i|$ and cutoff $\chi$ as above,
put $\bar\gamma_i=\chi(u_i)\,\mathrm d''u_i$.  The one-dimensional
calculation dual to the preceding one gives
\[
 \bar c_i:=\int_{A_i}\log|T_i|\,\mathrm d'\bar\gamma_i\ne0,
 \qquad\int_{A_i}\mathrm d'\bar\gamma_i=0.
\]
The compactly supported test form
$\pi_i^*\alpha\wedge q_i^*\bar\gamma_i$ and the defining current
pairing \cite[\S4.4]{CLD25} therefore give
\[
 0=\mathord\pm\bar c_i\int_{B_i}(a_i\circ p_i)\,\alpha.
\]
Top forms supported in product charts detect a continuous function, so
$a_i\circ p_i=0$, and hence $a_i=0$.  This holds for every $i$, so
$u=p^*b$.  If $\theta$ is any compactly supported ordinary smooth
$(0,1)$-form on $W$, choose compactly supported ordinary smooth
top-bidegree forms $\eta_i$ on the $A_i$ with integral one.  Pairing
$\mathrm d'[u]$ with
\[
                    p^*\theta\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^jq_i^*\eta_i
\]
gives $\int_Wb\,\mathrm d'\theta=0$.  Thus
$\mathrm d'[b]=0$, and the $j=0$ rigidity assertion makes $b$
locally constant.  This proves \eqref{eq:p062-open-polyannulus-first-rigidity}
and completes the induction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Locally trivial open-polyannulus bundles over a published curve.}
\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:p062-open-polyannulus-bundles-over-curves}
Let $K$, $C$, and $W\subset C^{\mathrm{an}}$ be as in
\Cref{thm:wanner-curve-comparison}.  Let $s\geq1$, and let
$\pi:Y\to W$ be a morphism, where $Y$ is a good, topologically
separated, pure $(s+1)$-dimensional strict $K$-analytic space.  Suppose
that $W$ has an analytic open cover by $U$'s for which, for radii
\[
       0<r_{U,i}<R_{U,i},
       \qquad r_{U,i},R_{U,i}\in|K^\times|
       \quad(1\leq i\leq s),
\]
there is an isomorphism over $U$
\[
 \pi^{-1}(U)\simeq
 U\times_K\prod_{i=1}^s
 \{z\in(\mathbf G_{\mathrm m,K})^{\mathrm{an}}:
                         r_{U,i}<|T_i(z)|<R_{U,i}\}.
\]
If $h\in C^0(Y,\mathbf R)$ satisfies
$\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0$, then $h$ is classically
pluriharmonic, locally $\mathbf R$-PL, and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff
harmonic on $Y$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The zero-current equation restricts to every displayed analytic open
trivializing chart.  Each $U$ is again an open subset of
$C^{\mathrm{an}}$, so
\Cref{thm:p062-open-polyannulus-products-over-curves} applies to the
restriction of $h$ on $\pi^{-1}(U)$.  Its three conclusions are local
on $Y$, which proves the assertion.  No compatibility of the logarithmic
coordinates or of their coefficient functions on distinct charts is used.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Mixed open-polycylinder bundles over a published curve.}
\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:p062-mixed-open-polycylinder-bundles-over-curves}
Let $K$, $C$, and $W\subset C^{\mathrm{an}}$ be as in
\Cref{thm:wanner-curve-comparison}.  Let $s,r\geq1$, and let
$\pi:Y\to W$ be a morphism, where $Y$ is a good, topologically
separated, pure $(s+r+1)$-dimensional strict $K$-analytic space.
Suppose that $W$ has an analytic open cover by $U$'s for which, for
radii
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 0<r_{U,i}<R_{U,i},\qquad r_{U,i},R_{U,i}\in|K^\times|
       \quad(1\leq i\leq s),\\
 \rho_{U,j}\in|K^\times|\qquad(1\leq j\leq r),
 \end{gathered}
\]
there is an isomorphism over $U$
\[
 \pi^{-1}(U)\simeq
 U\times_K\prod_{i=1}^s
 \{z\in(\mathbf G_{\mathrm m,K})^{\mathrm{an}}:
                         r_{U,i}<|T_i(z)|<R_{U,i}\}
 \times_K\prod_{j=1}^rD^-_{\rho_{U,j}}.
\]
If $h\in C^0(Y,\mathbf R)$ satisfies
$\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0$, then $h$ is classically
pluriharmonic, locally $\mathbf R$-PL, and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff
harmonic on $Y$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Restrict to one displayed analytic product chart.  Successively applying
\Cref{prop:p062-open-disc-descent} to its $r$ strict open-disc factors
writes the restriction of $h$ as the pullback of a continuous
zero-current function on
\[
 U\times_K\prod_{i=1}^s
 \{r_{U,i}<|T_i|<R_{U,i}\}.
\]
\Cref{thm:p062-open-polyannulus-products-over-curves} makes that function
classically pluriharmonic.  Analytic pullback preserves classical psh
\cite[Proposition~4.2(6)]{GR25Classical}, so both signs of the restriction
of $h$ are classically psh.  The displayed chart is a good strict finite
product of good strict analytic curves, so
\Cref{thm:p061-arbitrary-curve-factors-ph-rpl} makes the restriction
$\mathbf R$-PL and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.  The displayed charts
cover $Y$, proving all three assertions.  No global splitting of the
annular and disc coordinates or global logarithmic coefficient formula is
used.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Open-disc products over a published curve.}
\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:p062-open-disc-products-over-curves}
Let $K$, $C$, and $W\subset C^{\mathrm{an}}$ be as in
\Cref{thm:wanner-curve-comparison}, let $r\geq0$, and choose
$R_1,\ldots,R_r\in|K^\times|$.  If
\[
 h\in C^0\left(W\times_K\prod_{i=1}^rD_{R_i}^-,\mathbf R\right)
 \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0,
\]
then there is a unique continuous $b:W\to\mathbf R$ with
\[
 h=\operatorname{pr}_W^*b,
 \qquad
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0.
\]
In particular, $h$ is classically pluriharmonic, locally
$\mathbf R$-PL, and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $r=0$, this is
\Cref{cor:ch19-continuous-zero-current-harmonic}.  For $r>0$, repeatedly
apply \Cref{prop:p062-open-disc-descent}; at each step the remaining base

\begin{proof}

                         W\times_K\prod_{i=1}^sA_i.
\]
\Cref{thm:p062-open-polyannulus-products-over-curves} gives the displayed

conclusions.  Finally, $X$ is a good strict finite product of good strict

\paragraph{Locally trivial open-polydisc-bundle descent.}
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:p062-open-disc-bundle-descent}
\label{prop:p062-open-polydisc-bundle-descent}
Let $K$ be a complete non-Archimedean field with nontrivial absolute
value, let $m\geq1$, and let $B$ be a good, topologically separated,
pure $m$-dimensional strict $K$-analytic space.  Let
$\pi:Y\to B$ be a morphism, and let $q\geq1$.  Suppose that $B$
has an analytic open cover by $U$'s for which, for some
$R_{U,1},\ldots,R_{U,q}\in|K^\times|$, there is an isomorphism over
$U$
\[
              \pi^{-1}(U)\simeq
              U\times_KD_{R_{U,1}}^-\times_K\cdots\times_KD_{R_{U,q}}^-.
\]
If $h\in C^0(Y,\mathbf R)$ satisfies
$\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0$, then there is a unique
$b\in C^0(B,\mathbf R)$ such that
\[
                         h=\pi^*b,
                         \qquad
                         \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0.
\]
Moreover, $\mathrm d'[h]=0$ implies $\mathrm d'[b]=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On a displayed trivializing chart, successively apply
\Cref{prop:p062-open-disc-descent} to the $q$ open-disc factors.  At every
step, the remaining base is a good, topologically separated, pure strict
analytic space of positive dimension.  Thus this produces a unique
continuous zero-current function $b_U$ on $U$ with
$h|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}=\operatorname{pr}_U^*b_U$.  On an overlap, the two
pullbacks agree.  Since $\pi$ is surjective there and its fibres are
nonempty open polydiscs, this equality forces the restrictions of $b_U$
and $b_V$ to agree.  They therefore glue to a unique continuous function
$b$ on $B$, and the displayed pullback identity holds globally.  The
local zero-current identities glue to $\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0$
because currents form a sheaf (\Cref{prop:ch14-currents-sheaf}).
If $\mathrm d'[h]=0$, the strengthened conclusion of
\Cref{prop:p062-open-disc-descent} gives $\mathrm d'[b_U]=0$ on every
displayed product chart.  These local identities likewise glue to
$\mathrm d'[b]=0$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite open-polydisc-bundle towers.}
\begin{corollary}[Iterated open-polydisc-bundle transfer of zero-current regularity]
\label{cor:p062-iterated-open-polydisc-bundle-regularity}
Let $K$ be a complete non-Archimedean field with nontrivial absolute
value, let $B_0$ be a positive-dimensional good, topologically separated,
pure strict $K$-analytic space, and suppose that every
$b\in C^0(B_0,\mathbf R)$ satisfying
$\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0$ is classically pluriharmonic.  Let
$\ell\geq1$, and suppose that
\[
 B_\ell\xrightarrow{\pi_\ell}B_{\ell-1}
 \longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow B_1
 \xrightarrow{\pi_1}B_0
\]
is a tower in which every $B_j$, $1\leq j\leq\ell$, is a good,
topologically separated, pure strict $K$-analytic space.  For every
$1\leq j\leq\ell$, suppose that there are an integer $q_j\geq1$ and
an analytic open cover of $B_{j-1}$ by $U$'s such that, for radii
$R_{j,U,1},\ldots,R_{j,U,q_j}\in|K^\times|$, one has an isomorphism
over $U$
\[
 \pi_j^{-1}(U)\simeq
 U\times_KD_{R_{j,U,1}}^-\times_K\cdots\times_KD_{R_{j,U,q_j}}^-.
\]
If $h\in C^0(B_\ell,\mathbf R)$ satisfies
$\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0$, then there is a unique
$b\in C^0(B_0,\mathbf R)$ such that
\[
 h=(\pi_1\circ\cdots\circ\pi_\ell)^*b,
 \qquad
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0.
\]
In particular, $h$ is classically pluriharmonic on $B_\ell$.
If, in addition, $B_0$ is analytically locally isomorphic to an open
analytic subspace of a finite product of good strict $K$-analytic curves,
then $B_\ell$ has the same local description and $h$ is locally
$\mathbf R$-PL and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.
Moreover, if every $u\in C^0(B_0,\mathbf R)$ with
\[
             \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[u]=0,
             \qquad \mathrm d'[u]=0
\]
is locally constant, then the same implication holds with $B_\ell$ in
place of $B_0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{prop:p062-open-polydisc-bundle-descent} first to $\pi_\ell$
and then successively to $\pi_{\ell-1},\ldots,\pi_1$.  At every stage
it produces a unique continuous zero-current function on the preceding
base, and the pullback identities compose.  This gives the asserted unique
function $b$ on $B_0$.  By the hypothesis on $B_0$, both $b$ and
$-b$ are classically psh.  Their pullbacks along the composite morphism
are classically psh by \cite[Proposition~4.2(6)]{GR25Classical}, so $h$
and $-h$ are classically psh.

Assume now the additional local-product hypothesis.  We first propagate
that local description through the tower.  It holds at level zero by
assumption.  If it holds at level $j-1$, fix $y\in B_j$, choose a
displayed trivializing open $U\subseteq B_{j-1}$ around $\pi_j(y)$,
and shrink inside $U$ to an open $V$ which is analytically isomorphic
to an open analytic subspace of a finite product of good strict analytic
curves.  Then
\[
 \pi_j^{-1}(V)\simeq
 V\times_KD_{R_{j,V,1}}^-\times_K\cdots\times_KD_{R_{j,V,q_j}}^-.
\]
The right-hand side is an open analytic subspace of the product obtained by
adjoining these strict open-disc curves to that finite curve product: it is
the base change of the open immersion defining $V$.  Thus the same local
description holds at level $j$, and induction reaches $B_\ell$.
Since $h$ is classically pluriharmonic there,
\Cref{thm:p061-arbitrary-curve-factors-ph-rpl} makes it locally
$\mathbf R$-PL and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.

For the final assertion, let $h$ also satisfy $\mathrm d'[h]=0$.
Applying the strengthened first-current conclusion of
\Cref{prop:p062-open-polydisc-bundle-descent} successively through the
tower gives $\mathrm d'[b]=0$.  The additional hypothesis makes $b$
locally constant, and then so is its pullback $h$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Endpoint-base classification for open-polydisc bundles.}
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:p062-open-polydisc-bundle-endpoint-base}

value, and let $P$ be a positive-dimensional standard strict endpoint

\Cref{prop:p062-open-polydisc-bundle-descent}.  If
$h\in C^0(Y,\mathbf R)$ satisfies
$\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0$, then there are unique real constants
$c,\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_a$ such that
\[
                  h=\pi^*\left(c+\sum_{j=1}^a\lambda_j\log|S_j|\right).
\]
In particular, $h$ is classically pluriharmonic, locally
$\mathbf R$-PL, and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.

\begin{proof}

\Cref{thm:p062-endpoint-polycylinder-v4-research}.  It gives the displayed

makes both $b$ and $-b$ classically psh; their analytic pullbacks are
classically psh by \cite[Proposition~4.2(6)]{GR25Classical}.  On every
displayed trivializing chart, the restriction of $h$ is therefore
classically pluriharmonic on an open analytic subspace of the good strict
finite product of the endpoint-polycylinder curve factors with $r$ strict
open discs.  Hence
\Cref{thm:p061-arbitrary-curve-factors-ph-rpl} makes that restriction

are local on $Y$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Bedford--Taylor independence on endpoint towers.}
\begin{corollary}[Endpoint-tower Bedford--Taylor criterion]
\label{cor:p213-endpoint-tower-bt}
Let $K$ be a complete non-Archimedean field with nontrivial absolute
value, and let $B$ be a good, topologically separated, pure,
boundaryless strict $K$-analytic space of dimension $n\geq1$.  Suppose
that $B=B_\ell$ is the total space of a tower as in
\Cref{cor:p062-iterated-endpoint-open-polydisc-bundles}, and write
$\Pi:B\to P$ for the composite map to its endpoint-polycylinder base.
If $T$ is a
positive closed CLD current of bidegree $(p,p)$,
$0\leq p\leq n-1$, and $u,v$ are continuous psh functions on $B$
with
\[
                         \dprimeddprime[u]=\dprimeddprime[v],
\]
then
\[
                 \dprimeddprime(uT)=\dprimeddprime(vT).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Put $h=u-v$.  By
\Cref{cor:p062-iterated-endpoint-open-polydisc-bundles}, there are real
constants $c,\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_a$ such that
\[
 h=\Pi^*
 \left(c+\sum_{j=1}^{a}\lambda_j\log|S_j|\right).
\]
On the standard tropical chart of each annular factor, $\log|S_j|$ is
the pullback of an affine coordinate, hence is an ordinary CLD-smooth
function with zero smooth Hessian.  Thus the function in parentheses is
ordinary CLD-smooth with zero Hessian on the endpoint polycylinder.
The pullback theorem for CLD forms
\Cref{prop:cld-form-pullback} makes $h$ ordinary CLD-smooth on $B$
with zero smooth Hessian.  Taking the constant sequence $h_j=h$ in
\Cref{cor:ch16-local-pluriharmonic-criterion} gives
$\dprimeddprime(hT)=0$, which is the asserted equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}

\end{proof}

\paragraph{Invariant toric bases for open-polydisc bundles.}
\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:p062-invariant-toric-open-polydisc-bundles}
Let $K$ be a complete non-Archimedean field with nontrivial absolute value,
let $X_\Sigma$ be a split toric $K$-variety, and let $O$ be an open
subset of its extended tropicalization.  Put
$V=\operatorname{trop}^{-1}(O)$, and assume that $V$ is good,
topologically separated, strict, pure, and positive-dimensional.  Let $S$
be the maximal compact torus of $X_\Sigma$, and
let $\pi:Y\to V$ be a locally trivial standard strict open-polydisc
bundle as in \Cref{prop:p062-open-polydisc-bundle-descent}.  Suppose that
the $S$-action on $V$ lifts to an action on $Y$ for which $\pi$ is
equivariant.  If $h\in C^0(Y,\mathbf R)$ is $S$-invariant and satisfies
\[
                         \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0,
\]
then $h$ is classically pluriharmonic.  It is also locally
$\mathbf R$-PL and Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{prop:p062-open-polydisc-bundle-descent}, there is a unique
continuous $b$ on $V$ with
\[
                         h=\pi^*b,
                         \qquad \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[b]=0.
\]
For $s\in S$ and $x\in V$, choose $y\in\pi^{-1}(x)$.  Equivariance and
invariance give
\[
 b(sx)=h(sy)=h(y)=b(x),
\]
so $b$ is $S$-invariant.  Apply
\Cref{prop:p062-invariant-split-toric-boundary-zero-current-candidate} to
$b$.  It makes $b$ locally $\mathbf R$-PL,
Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic, and classically pluriharmonic.  Locally its
real-PL expression is a real linear combination of logarithms of analytic
units, whose pullbacks are again analytic units.  Thus $h=\pi^*b$ is
locally $\mathbf R$-PL.  Finally, analytic pullback preserves classical
psh by \cite[Proposition~4.2(6)]{GR25Classical}; applying this to both $b$
and $-b$ proves that $h$ is classically pluriharmonic.  The same
two-sign criterion, through \cite[Theorem~4.3]{GR25Classical}, gives its
Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonicity: the displayed local product charts make
$Y$ good and strict.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Iterated algebraic-split invariant-toric bundle regularity]
\label{cor:p062-iterated-algebraic-split-invariant-toric-open-polydisc-bundles}
Let $K$ be a complete non-Archimedean field with nontrivial valuation, and
let
\[
 B_\ell\xrightarrow{\pi_\ell}B_{\ell-1}
 \longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow B_1
 \xrightarrow{\pi_1}B_0=V
\]
be a tower of good strict $K$-analytic spaces.  Suppose that a complete
analytic extension $L/K$, which is the completion of an algebraic
extension, a rational fan $\Sigma$, an open $O\subseteq N_\Sigma$, and
an $L$-analytic isomorphism
\[
                 \iota:V_L\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{trop}^{-1}(O)
\]
to the corresponding split toric $L$-domain are given.  Assume that every
$V_L$ is good, topologically separated, strict, pure, and
positive-dimensional, that every $(B_j)_L$ is good, topologically
separated, strict, and pure, that every
$(\pi_j)_L$ is a locally trivial standard strict open-polydisc bundle, and
that the maximal compact torus $S$ acts on every $(B_j)_L$, compatibly
with the transported action on $V_L$, so all $(\pi_j)_L$ are
equivariant.  If $h\in C^0(B_\ell,\mathbf R)$ is finite, $h_L$ is
$S$-invariant, and
\[
                         \mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h_L]=0,
\]
then $h$ is classically pluriharmonic, locally $\mathbf R$-PL, and
Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
After transport through $\iota$, apply
\Cref{cor:p062-iterated-invariant-toric-open-polydisc-bundles} over $L$
to the scalar extension of the tower and to $h_L$.  Thus $h_L$ is
classically pluriharmonic and locally $\mathbf R$-PL.  Ground-field
descent for the latter \cite[Corollary~5.12]{GJR26} and the converse
scalar-extension implication for the former
\cite[Proposition~7.2(6)]{GR25Classical} give the first two conclusions on
$B_\ell$.  Applying the two-sign criterion on the good strict space
$B_\ell$ gives Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonicity.
\end{proof}

             \iota_U:V_L\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{trop}^{-1}(O)

The three conclusions therefore hold on $U$.  Since $y$ was arbitrary,

\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Scalar-stable algebraic-split weak-form-closure Bedford--Taylor criterion]
\label{cor:p213-algebraic-split-scalar-stable-weak-form-bt}
Let $K$ be a complete non-Archimedean field with nontrivial valuation,
let $W$ be a good, topologically separated, pure-dimensional,
boundaryless strict $K$-analytic space, and let $T$ be a positive
closed CLD current of bidegree $(p,p)$ on $W$,
$0\leq p\leq\dim W-1$.  Suppose that $T$ is locally
weak-form-approximable in the sense of
\Cref{def:ch18-weak-approximation-classes}.  Let $u,v$ be finite
continuous psh functions on $W$ with
\[
                         \dprimeddprime[u]=\dprimeddprime[v],
\]
and put $h=u-v$.  Assume that every point of $W$ has a good strict
open neighbourhood $U$ on which $h|_U$ satisfies all hypotheses of
\Cref{cor:p062-local-iterated-algebraic-split-invariant-toric-open-polydisc-bundles}.
In particular, the displayed scalar extension, split-toric tower,
invariance, and zero-current equality are required for $h|_U$ after
that extension.  Then
\[
                         \dprimeddprime(uT)=\dprimeddprime(vT)
                         \quad\text{on }W.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x\in W$ and choose $U$ as in the hypotheses.  The cited local
algebraic-split tower corollary makes $h$ locally
Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic on $U$.  After shrinking about $x$, it
is therefore $\mathbf R$-harmonic, so
\cite[Proposition~10.17]{GJR26} makes it a weakly smooth function with zero
weakly smooth Hessian.  The constant net with this value makes $h$
locally weak-harmonically approximable, while the restricted current remains
locally weak-form-approximable.  Thus
\Cref{prop:ch18-weak-form-closure-bt} gives the vanishing of
$\dprimeddprime(hT)$ on this smaller neighbourhood.  Such neighbourhoods
cover $W$, and the sheaf property of ordinary currents and $uT-vT=hT$
give the asserted equality.
\end{proof}

The preceding statements cover standard strict \emph{open} polydisc products
and the stated locally trivial open-polydisc bundles, as well as locally
trivial projective-space bundles over the explicitly locally holomorphically
separated paracompact bases of
\Cref{prop:p062-projective-bundle-descent}, locally trivial smooth proper
split-toric bundles over the bases of
\Cref{prop:p062-smooth-proper-split-toric-bundle-descent}, and locally
trivial proper smooth connected curve bundles over the algebraically closed bases of
\Cref{prop:p062-proper-smooth-curve-bundle-descent}.  Under the explicit
rigid-fibre scalar-stability hypothesis,
\Cref{prop:p062-scalar-stable-proper-curve-bundle-descent} removes the
algebraic-closure restriction from this last descent statement, without
asserting automatic scalar ascent of a continuous Hessian current.  Over a
published curve, \Cref{thm:p062-mixed-bundle-towers-over-curves} permits standard
strict open-polydisc, projective-space, smooth proper split-toric, and
proper smooth curve-bundle stages in any finite order while retaining every
stagewise base hypothesis.  The
underlying transfer mechanism is isolated in
\Cref{prop:p062-mixed-exact-bundle-tower-transfer}: unique zero-current
descent, classical pluriharmonicity, $\mathbf R$-harmonicity, and
first-current rigidity all pass from an arbitrary admissible base through
any such mixed tower.  Consequently, a globally coordinated
curve-polyannulus product may occur at the bottom of the tower while
retaining its unique coefficient formula, by
\Cref{cor:p062-polyannulus-base-mixed-bundle-towers}; if that bottom space
is instead a locally trivial curve-polyannulus bundle or locally trivial
mixed curve-polycylinder bundle, the three regularity conclusions persist
by
\Cref{cor:p062-open-polyannulus-bundle-mixed-bundle-towers,cor:p062-mixed-open-polycylinder-bundle-mixed-bundle-towers}.
The boundaryless top of such a tower may moreover be multiplied by globally
coordinated standard strict open annuli: in
\Cref{cor:p062-open-polyannulus-over-mixed-bundle-towers}, the unique base
and slope coefficients all descend to the original curve.  The preceding
two orders admit a common extension.  In
\Cref{thm:p062-interleaved-polyannulus-mixed-bundle-towers}, any finite
number of globally coordinated standard strict open-polyannulus product
stages may occur at arbitrary levels among the four exact bundle types.
Every continuous zero-current function then has a unique formula containing
one logarithmic term for every annular coordinate introduced anywhere in the
tower; its base function and all slope coefficients live on the original
curve.  The coefficient formula requires those global coordinates, but the
three regularity conclusions do not.  By
\Cref{cor:p062-curve-local-annular-mixed-bundle-towers}, they persist when
one analytic open cover of the original curve makes every annular stage a
coordinated product after base change and makes every restricted exact stage
retain the hypotheses of the interleaved theorem.  The annular coordinates,
radii, and trivializations need not agree on overlaps.  This does not cover
annular charts available only over arbitrary opens of an intermediate tower
level, nor does it produce a global coefficient formula in the presence of
coordinate monodromy.
For one locally trivial curve-polyannulus bundle,
\Cref{prop:p062-polyannulus-bundle-slope-local-system} identifies that
monodromy intrinsically: logarithmic coordinate changes give a rank-$s$
local system of lattices and a zero-current affine translation cocycle in
its realification, while the local slope vectors of every zero-current
function form a global parallel section of the dual real local system.  No
global-coordinate claim is made.  The translation part has an exact
cohomological obstruction:
\Cref{prop:p062-polyannulus-radiance-obstruction} defines its canonical
zero-current radiance class and proves that its vanishing is equivalent,
after refinement, to purely linear overlap laws under zero-current affine
gauges.  In that case every zero-current function has one global base
coefficient and pairs its parallel slope with a global continuous section
of the associated flat vector bundle.  The gauges need not come from
analytic units, so this still does not produce global annular coordinates.
Without assuming that the full radiance class vanishes,
\Cref{prop:p062-polyannulus-slope-exact-sequence} identifies the realizable
parallel slopes as the kernel of scalar contraction with that class.  It
places the zero-current functions, base zero-current functions, parallel
slopes, and scalar obstruction in one exact sequence.  Thus injectivity of
the contraction---in particular, absence of parallel dual sections---forces
every zero-current function to descend uniquely to the curve; when the full
radiance class vanishes, a chosen affine gauge splits the sequence.
The preceding statements also cover the displayed
maximal-compact-torus-invariant bundles over split-toric bases and their
finite equivariant towers, together with their explicitly scalar-stable
algebraic-split forms and towers, including their analytic-local versions.
For the algebraic-split statements, the lifted action, invariance, and
zero-current equation are imposed after the chosen extension; they are not
automatically inherited from downstairs.  The tower results additionally
require actions on every level and equivariance of every bundle map, even in
the analytic-local form.  The locally twisted annular-bottom results require
the displayed local bundle charts and do not by themselves permit an annular
stage at a later level.  Later locally twisted stages are now covered when
their trivializations come from one cover of the original curve and the
entire restricted tower satisfies the interleaved theorem; arbitrary
intermediate-base local trivializations remain outside the result.  These
statements do not handle arbitrary annular fibrations, disc fibrations
without the stated local product charts, noninvariant functions on the new
toric-bundle range, arbitrary nonproduct germs, or a current equation known
only on a strict radial core.

\paragraph{Summary of provenance.}
Delta-forms, tropical intersections, Poincar\'e--Lelong, Chern delta-forms,
model measures, and local heights come from \cite{GK17}.  The formal
semipositivity equivalences and tropical-frame positivity criterion are
\cite{GK19}.  Harmonic tropicalizations and weakly smooth forms are from
\cite{GJR26}.  The residue-nef curve test and its stability theorem are from
\cite{GR25PL}; the classically psh comparison is \cite{GR25Classical}.
The real-PL zero-current converse of
\Cref{prop:ch18-real-pl-zero-current-harmonic} is a book-level deduction from
the calibrated skeletal-current results of \cite{CLD25}, the residue and
common-tropicalization results of \cite{GJR26}, and the classical comparison
of \cite{GR25Classical}; no novelty or priority claim is made.

\cite[Proposition~7.8]{GR25Classical} with altered local uniformization

\paragraph{Order of discovery.}
The chronology is worth stating because it explains the shape of the
theory.  Lagerberg's superforms and the CLD calculus came first, and were
designed for a differential-geometric purpose.  Gubler and K\"unnemann's
delta-forms \cite{GK17} were designed for an Arakelov-theoretic purpose:
the local heights of \Cref{sec:ch18-green} are the target, and the
enlargement of coefficients is what makes a $*$-product available.  Their
positivity paper \cite{GK19} then compared the resulting notion with
Zhang's semipositivity \cite{Zhang1995} and with the metric-theoretic
notions of \cite{GM19}.  Gubler, Jell and Rabinoff \cite{GJR26} started from
a cohomological question---the failure of Poincar\'e duality for the smooth
Dolbeault complex, whose local theory is Jell's \cite{Jell16}---and enlarged
the charts instead.  The curve-test papers \cite{GR25PL,GR25Classical} are
later still, and they belong to a different tradition, that of Thuillier's
potential theory on curves and its comparison with the CLD sheaf
\cite{Wanner19}.  A reader who keeps these four purposes apart will not be
tempted to look for a single theory containing all four constructions.
