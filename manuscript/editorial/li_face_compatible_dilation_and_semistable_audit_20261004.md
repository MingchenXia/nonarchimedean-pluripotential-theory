## Mathematical consequence and its limits

The dossier gives a direct specialization of the imported ALT
projective resolution to a supplied finite conical complex with
compatible integral heights positive away from zero. The target
ray alteration is one lattice cZ. The induced source lattices
are the height-preimages of cZ. Lattice surjectivity must be
applied to every source ray to obtain generator height c;
surjectivity on a maximal cone alone would only give a gcd.
Regular cone bases then give unimodular height-c simplices by
subtracting one basis vertex. The point-slice case is included.
Original coordinate rays force each b_i to divide c. Further
dilation preserves the difference lattice ker h.

This records an application of a known theorem with an explicit
projectivity import, not a new theorem or a completed proof of
all ALT antecedents. Rationalization of already supplied compatible
heights uses finite rational linear equalities and open inequalities;
it does not solve the height-existence requirement.

The remaining application checks are the actual finite log/conical
category, chart monodromy, the convergent-germ/quasi-excellent
comparison, a scheme atlas of the stack with the right cyclic
parameter, a global finite vertical normalized-blowup presentation,
strict reduced SNC and preservation of the fixed generic
polarization. The existing positive-model derivation remains
conditional on that presentation. Generic modifications in
earlier Abramovich–Karu work and arbitrary desingularization
are not silently substituted for these requirements.

## Actual-PDF diagnostics

HPPS page 73 prints a box-height upper bound V-1. The index-two
tetrahedron conv(0,e_1,e_2,(1,1,2)) has the nonzero box point
(1,1,1) with all fractional barycentric coordinates 1/2 and
height 2. The correct elementary dimension bound is at most
dim Delta. The same paragraph has an ambient-lattice coset
instead of the vertex-difference coset. Page 75 reverses the
box-point lattice difference and switches k+1 to k in the
barycentre/shell proof notation. All are present in the actual
arXiv PDF. They are auxiliary correction requirements, not a
claimed disproof of KMW or a checked correction of every
line in its new proof.

ALT Theorem 4.2(i), page 19, prints an ill-typed reversed
fibre-product formula. More substantially, Theorem 4.5 on page
20 omits the log-regular-base hypothesis required by its own
preceding semistability definition. The identity on the singular
complex cusp with trivial log structures is log smooth; monoidal
alterations are isomorphisms on its entire triviality locus and
cannot make the cusp regular. This diagnoses the unrestricted
literal auxiliary statement. Theorem 4.7 explicitly resolves
the intermediate base to be log regular before using it, so
neither that theorem nor the principal conical theorem is
declared disproved. The source text and actual page agree.

Ferry–Joswig–Rambau page 3's claimed rotated half-triangle
identification has an area factor of two, and its F(d/2) notation
is not the page-1 standard-triangle definition without further
interpretation. Odd d gives a nonlattice half-triangle corner.
Lemma 9(2), page 7, gives 2d+1 boundary points for odd d,
where the displayed triangle and the lattice-point convex hull
both have 2d. The dossier gives a general edge count and the
six explicit points for d=3. Page 6 calls an upper estimate
a lower bound. These actual-PDF auxiliary defects do not alone
disprove the reported enumeration or its broad quadratic-order
growth claim. The counting-capacity limit is distinct from
pluripotential capacity, and bounding its liminf/limsup does
not establish limit existence.

These diagnostics are direct symbolic/geometric arguments.
