## Mathematical and source scope

The attributed real subharmonic theorem recovers the envelope's own upper
regularization, not every obstacle. Its Poisson-difference correction is
ordinary harmonic; no higher-dimensional pluriharmonic correction is inferred.
The explicit Hartogs construction works in every complex dimension at least
two. Its tangential Levi form, cuspidal peaks and negative exhaustion are
explained. Global boundary smoothness and a product-with-discs construction
are not asserted. The approximants increase off the axis with growing bounds.

A strong plurisuperharmonic majorant may have poles. Recovery holds where it
is finite; necessity retains the finite pluriharmonic-obstacle hypothesis.
Every majorant in the Hartogs example has poles on the entire defect disc.
Tameness therefore does not imply pointwise recovery there. The boundary
Jensen formula is a one-sided estimate with its prescribed limit/infimum
order. A fixed harmonic measure detects the axis loss at every truncation.
