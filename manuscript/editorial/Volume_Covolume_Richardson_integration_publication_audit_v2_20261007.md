## Mathematical change and source boundaries

**D.8.1** distinguishes exact rational geometric realization from closed
coefficient limits. The controlling Grund--Huh--Michalek--Suess--Wang
version 1 uses reduced irreducible projective varieties over an
algebraically closed field, semiample Cartier divisors and a nonnegative
rational scale of the top intersection divided by `d!`. Covolume
differential operators use cutoff reversal in divided powers, not direct
factorial normalization. Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4 give the stated
constant-coefficient preservation equivalences. Theorem 1.12 supplies a
sufficient symbol condition for a homogeneous linear operator with fixed
degree shift; a converse is not asserted. The dual converse requires
auxiliary variables. Product normalization is not multiplicative on
overlapping variables. Partial symmetrization retains multiaffinity and
the rational mixing range `0 <= t <= 1`.

**D.8.2** imports Jiang--Li's reverse Khovanskii--Teissier inequality
for nef Cartier divisors over any algebraically closed field, with the
binomial constant, strict ample case and sharp nef constant. Its proof
uses multipoint Okounkov bodies, not a characteristic-zero resolution
assumption. The source cubic in Huh's survey is Lorentzian but violates
the necessary volume inequality: the divided-power coefficients give
`3 * 12 * 12 = 432 < 84 * 6 = 504`. This separates Lorentzianity
from membership in the closed volume cone. The June survey's other
questions are historical to that exact revision, not certified present-day
open problems. Its attributed exact five-dimensional convex-projection
counterexample is not promoted to an independently proved certificate or
an answer about closed realizations.

**F.9.2** treats the normalized total Chern intersection polynomial of
globally generated bundles on a complex projective variety. For each
positive-rank kernel, its dual is used in the quotient projective bundle.
The pushforward identity is `h_j(G_i) = c_j(F_i)`; the tautological
volume includes a monomial shift of degree `s_i - 1` in each variable.
Divided-power differentiation removes the shift with the displayed
factorials. This is numerical intersection theory. It does not assert
positivity of every metric-dependent Chern form, smooth realization over
every field, or an arbitrary b-divisor product.

**F.9.3** imports Nguyen-Dang's normalized skew Schur and skew Schur
P/Q realizations over the complex numbers. Nonzero inputs have smooth
models; the zero and empty-shape conventions are explicit. Ordinary
Richardson varieties use opposite flags, rectangle duality and the
globally generated quotient bundle. The Lagrangian argument uses modified
Q classes; the spinor argument uses modified P classes, quotient rank
`N + 1`, and `c_j = 2 tau_j`. Neither substitution treats the ordinary
symmetric polynomials in roots as those modified classes. The appendix's
cycle constructions over algebraically closed fields retain their weaker
smoothness scope. In characteristic two, ambient finite flatness is not
silently inherited by singular Richardson restrictions. Radicial degrees
and the integer powers of two in cycle comparison are recorded separately.

Philip B. Zhang's independent finite-variable Lorentzian proof uses the
complement `(L^n, mu)/lambda` with `L >= lambda_1`, inversion and
normalization. This differs from the Grassmannian rectangle dual used in
the geometric model. Deleting an alphabet variable is not asserted to
commute with complement choice. Ordinary skew-Schur support is described
by the partition conjugate to column lengths and its subset-rank formula;
that explicit formula is not transferred to arbitrary skew P/Q support.
The factorial-weighted coefficient law is distinct from uniform tableaux
and from negative association; cumulative Littlewood--Richardson
inequalities do not give individual coefficient log-concavity.

**G.9.8** imports the projection-dimension criterion for positive
multidegrees of a reduced irreducible multiprojective variety over any
field. All subset inequalities and total degree are required. For a
reducible variety, one component must satisfy the whole system. The
controlling published Castillo--Cid-Ruiz--Li--Montano--Naizhen Zhang
locators are Theorem 3.12, Corollary 3.13 and Proposition 5.1. GHMSW
Proposition 5.4 answers their Question 5.8 over any field; the old question
is not left marked open. Exact algebraic support, support of coefficient
limits, and arbitrary fan Chow classes have separate scopes. Matroid
intersection retains its dual-union interpretation and loop/coloop
conventions; no general dual-algebraicity conjecture is solved.

**L.79** supplies exact editions, theorem/proof locators and all these
boundaries. The later five-author operator paper's irreducibility-gap
observation about Ross--Suess Proposition 2.6 is attributed as such. It
does not disprove every preservation result; the integration uses the
alternative component argument checked in GHMSW and Huang et al.
