# Local mathematical audit

The packet proves a universal obstruction in the literal integral-slope
coefficient category.

- The obstacle is continuous at the vertex and at `t=N-1,N`, bounded, and
  has only the integral slopes `0,-1,0`.
- The candidate envelope is raywise convex.  Its initial ray slopes are
  `g_j-1/N>0` and `g_i>0`, so every nonnegative balancing relation has
  nonnegative vertex Jensen defect.
- The obstacle comparison is exact outside `[N-1,N]`; on that interval it is
  equivalent to `t<=N`.
- The chord through the endpoint bounds at `0` and `N u_j` gives maximality
  on the only segment where the obstacle is not already the candidate.
- The integral left derivative at `t=1` yields the exhaustive dichotomy
  `m<=g_j-1` or `m>=g_j`.  One of the two endpoint-error differences then has
  magnitude at least `(N-1)/N`, giving the stated uniform lower bound.
- Eventual raywise affine replacement differs boundedly from the original
  finite-subdivision reference in the same literal coefficient category, so
  the established bounded-reference-shift proposition applies.

The affine-line slope-`1/2` example is the case `N=2`.  The conclusion is
negative only for literal regularity.  It neither weakens the book's real-PA
results nor extends a Monge--Ampere operator to the nonregularizable envelope.
No model-verifier verdict is claimed.
