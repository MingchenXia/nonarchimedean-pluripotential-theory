## Canonical form and the cohomological category

The earlier section-module construction applies to an invertible
canonical extension even though it is not ample: ampleness was used
only in the later generation step. Finite free cohomology and the
finite coefficient/germ realization give constant section rank on all

rank one. Its generator is nowhere zero on every such fibre, hence
gives a meromorphic relative canonical form and a meromorphic total
top form with vertical divisor. The projective proper-support

## Exact Skoda hypothesis and normalization

The controlling source is Yang Li, *Uniform Skoda integrability and
Calabi–Yau degeneration*, Analysis & PDE 17 (2024), 2247–2256,
DOI `10.2140/apde.2024.17.2247`. Its Theorem 1.3 assumes an
algebraic polarized CY family over a smooth affine curve. The new note
does not use that statement to cover an arbitrary meromorphic family.
The Section 2 analytic setup instead gives Theorem 2.9 for a flat
projective reduced-SNC disc model and a fixed total-space Kähler form.

On such a model the divisor coefficients of the total canonical form
are integers, so shifting their minimum to zero uses an honest power
of the parameter. The local relative form has the same displayed
exponents as the total form; wedging with dt checks this. The published
BJ asymptotic gives a positive total-mass lower bound of order T^m.
Consequently every finite adapted chart satisfies the source's (4),
including charts with nonessential components. The mass estimate
on generic top-stratum charts alone would not have sufficed.

The positive rational projective presentation gives the fixed total
Kähler form theta/2+i dt wedge d-bar-t, restricting to theta/2.
Dividing by T matches the norm-potential convention used in the FS
composition exactly. Theorem 2.9 supplies fixed alpha and A; Theorem
3.1 then supplies the uniform C0 estimate. A bounded normalized
background difference transfers the estimate with multiplier at most
exp(2 alpha B), after normalizing the translated test's supremum.
An arbitrary parameter-dependent O(T) bound is not called a continuous
hybrid reference change.

## Punctured continuity and the scalar normalization

Classical smooth CY existence and uniqueness, Ehresmann trivialization,
Banach implicit-function theory and the compact Laplacian/Schauder
inverse are explicit imports, not newly primary-certified proofs.
The dossier checks the parameter-dependent equation with a scalar
volume multiplier and mean-zero normalization. Its derivative is
(v,a) mapping to (2 Delta v-a, integral v). The probability volume
is proportional to the metric volume at a solution, so the Laplacian
has zero mean against it. Choose a as minus the target's mean, solve
the zero-mean equation, and restore the requested constant. This is
an invertible derivative at each fixed nonzero parameter; no uniform
elliptic inverse at the degeneration is claimed.

Uniqueness patches the local smooth mean-normalized solutions. Subtracting
the fibre supremum preserves continuity and fibrewise smoothness;
the supremum itself need not depend smoothly on the parameter. Dividing
by T gives exactly the punctured-family continuity needed by the hybrid
proof, without claiming parameter-psh dependence. The factor (2T)^n
cancels in the normalized MA probability, and the uniform Skoda C0
estimate gives O(T) oscillation of the unscaled norm potential.
