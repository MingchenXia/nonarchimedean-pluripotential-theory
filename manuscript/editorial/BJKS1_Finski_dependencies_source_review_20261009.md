## Exact mathematical signature

The ground field is algebraically closed, trivially valued and of arbitrary
characteristic unless a particular assertion adds a restriction. X is a
projective reduced irreducible variety; L is an ample Q-line bundle.
Additive norms chi are scalar-invariant, finite on nonzero sections and
ultrametric, with multiplicative interpretation exp(-chi). Graded norms
are compatible with original degree, superadditive on products and linearly
bounded in positive degree. Exhaustive separated left-continuous flags
recover chi by a maximum. The unit has degree zero and translation adds
c*m in degree m, leaving it unchanged.

Passing to R(X,dL) is a restriction of original section degrees. The source
direct limit identifies representatives on a further sufficiently divisible
Veronese. Graded distances divide original degree m; their limsup is over
divisibility. Original degree d*j and native degree j must therefore be
distinguished when comparing volumes or distances for L^d. Basepoint-free
systems and degree-one generation are available in the stipulated eventual
ample range, not every small degree.

Definition 1.8 and Lemma 1.4 separate finite-type graded norms, generation
in degree one after a Veronese, and weighted polynomial quotient norms.
The finite-dimensional lifting proof terminates because the flag has only
finitely many jumps. An arbitrary linear projection is not that quotient.
An arbitrary filtration-adapted basis is not a multiplicative toric basis.

The R-positive action is radial: (t dot phi)(v)=t*phi(v/t). It is distinct
from multiplying a potential at a fixed valuation, and from multiplying the
polarization. This paper expressly counts the trivial valuation as
divisorial, allowing t=0 in t*ord_E. Finski's printed definition on his
physical page 36 uses positive rational t; those conventions differ. The
product vertical divisor has trivial restriction to X, so the projection
statement needs that convention stated. This observation does not reject
the projection under the present paper's enlarged divisorial convention.

## Raw FS and canonical extension

Definition 2.13 defines raw FS(chi) as the bounded lower semicontinuous
supremum of sufficiently divisible finite-degree FS functions. Equation
(2.9) identifies these with FS of the canonical degree-d approximants.
Proposition 2.14 proves raw FS invariance under homogenization by the
commutative root/Fekete construction and three epsilon choices.
Theorem 2.16 proves IN(FS(chi))=chi_hom, using the compact Berkovich
spectrum and the homogeneous affine cone. IN itself is unchanged by LOWER
semicontinuous regularization of an arbitrary bounded obstacle. Its
regularization is not the UPPER regularization FS-star used later.

Example 2.21 is an explicit primary anchor for the book's Appendix L.99
diagnostic. On P1 with O(1) and a point p, set chi_m(s)=m when s(p)=0 and
zero otherwise. It is homogeneous and bounded multiplicative. Its raw FS
is 1 off p and 0 at the supported analytic point p. Positive finite Gauss
weights tending to infinity approach p; raw FS has a downward jump and
cannot belong to the class defined by decreasing nets of continuous FS
functions. Its upper regularization is constant 1. Translation by -1 and
scaling reproduce the book's negative-jump convention. The example is
attributed to the literature and makes no new priority claim.

Definition 1.12 also fixes a small normalization omission in the printed
Example 2.21 proof. The degree-d approximant is generated by the original
unrescaled restriction to R_d. On P1 a monomial of original degree d*k
and vanishing order j has quotient weight d*min(j,k). A simply vanishing
section has approximant weight d, rather than the printed value 1 unless
native weights have been rescaled. The gap (d*k-d)/(d*k) still tends to
one for fixed d, so the source's nonuniformity conclusion survives. The
old note reproducing the printed value is retained and this fresh
normalization clarification is explicit.

The exact controlling gate is Lemma 6.17, physical 75, printed 902:

| Conclusion | Necessary stated input |
| --- | --- |
| Raw FS and upper FS-star agree on divisorial valuations | Lemma 4.2, including the original non-negligibility/determination inputs |
| They agree on all linear-growth valuations, with equal extended energies | Weak envelope property, Lemma 4.8; boundedness below for the energy conclusion |
| FS-star is in bounded psh functions regularizable from below, and the canonical approximants converge strongly in E1 | FS-star is L-psh; full envelope property is a sufficient input |

Lemma 4.3 requires an increasing bounded-above net already in E1 and
assumes the upper-regularized supremum is psh. It then uses energy
monotonicity and divisorial convergence to obtain strong convergence.
Neither boundedness nor multiplicativity proves the missing psh membership.
Weak envelope property is defined via a birational model with a dominating
ample polarization and full envelope property; it is not identical with
full envelope property on the original variety.

Thus Finski's equation (6.15), written as a raw supremum on all
semivaluations, must be distinguished from the canonical psh extension
used by his Theorem 6.6. The P1 diagnostic refutes literal raw membership;
it does not refute the intended maximal-ray theorem after its canonical
representative and hypotheses are specified. The original BBJ21 maximal-ray
proofs and the original holomorphic extension theorem remain separate
source tasks and are not certified by this selected BJ reading.

## Homogenization, endpoint and finite-p separation

Theorem 2.3's degree-independent additive comparison and finite-generation
conclusion require a norm generated in degree one. The proof uses reduced
affinoid completion, non-Archimedean GAGA, spectral-radius equivalence and
Temkin graded reduction; those originals are not recursively proved here.
Theorem 2.19 characterizes continuous graded norms by uniform canonical
approximation and continuous raw FS, using an increasing divisibility net
and Dini's lemma. Example 2.22 shows continuous constant raw FS need not
mean literal finite type: its degreewise sqrt(m) discrepancy is unbounded
but sublinear. Example 2.25 shows the opposite endpoint obstruction:
noncontinuous ideal-truncated norms can have d-infinity distance 1/2 from
homogenization. General finite-p equivalence therefore does not imply an
endpoint equivalence.

Theorem 3.18, physical 51–53, printed 878–880, gives canonical convergence
for finite p; p=infinity holds precisely in the continuous case. Its proof
uses an ordered volume difference, uniformly bounded spectral support,
dominated convergence in a threshold and approximation of graded
subalgebras. Theorem 5.1 identifies graded probability volume with
EXTENDED energy of raw FS; it does not silently treat raw FS as psh.

The printed Okounkov proof steps for arbitrary graded subalgebras need a
value-lattice convention. S=k[x^2,y^2] inside the ordinary polynomial
section ring of P1 has even-degree dimensions k+1 at m=2k and no odd
degree sections. Its even-degree Hilbert coefficient tends to 1/2, while
its body in the ambient valuation lattice is [0,1], of length one. At
even d, the interior value sets omit odd valuation values, contrary to
the unqualified d^(-1)Z intersect B equality. This flags the printed
body-volume and interior-lattice steps. It is not a counterexample to the
intended approximation theorem: the even Veronese approximants recover
this S. The original [12] Bourbaki survey and [13] Boucksom–Chen paper
must be consulted before presenting an arbitrary-subalgebra proof with
the omitted lattice hypotheses removed.

## Energy, mass, distance and toric signs

Theorem 5.4, physical 61–62, printed 888–889, is the exact cited d1 input.
On finite-type FS functions it characterizes d1 by ordered energy
differences and the infimum through common lower FS functions. It pulls
back through IN, applies volume-energy and canonical approximation, then
uses an original E1 convergence input to prove nondegeneracy. It is not
an unordered signed-energy identity. Theorem 5.5 extends d1 to E1, but
completeness is equivalent to the envelope property. A finite-energy
rooftop formula additionally requires that property; the continuous
formula uses extended energy. The full continuation on physical 65 was
actually read in the supplementary selection.

The source intends probability MA: MA(0)=delta_triv, E(c)=c, and test
configuration energy is intersection divided by (n+1)*(L^n). The mixed
central-fibre intersection sum printed on physical 54 lacks an explicit
1/(L^n). A product test configuration for (P1,O(2)) has ordinary
intersection mass two; dividing by its volume restores probability mass
one. This is a displayed normalization omission, not an intended change
to the source's energy theory. The book's unnormalized MA convention
must continue to retain the explicit volume factor.

Theorem 5.8 compares d1 to absolute difference integrated against the
two probability MA measures. Its arithmetic-mean/common-lower-function
argument is fully read, including 2*rho-sigma-tau >= |phi-psi|/2 and the
positive expansion of MA(rho). Locality, domination and strong continuity
are imported original inputs. The quasi-metric I needs its supremum
anchor to distinguish constants; quotient by constants is a separate
infimum operation. For joint weights {-1,1}, signed mean is zero but
probability L1 distance is one.

Appendix B's printed toric formulas need a consistent coordinate sign.
The paper defines v_xi(z^alpha)=<xi,alpha> but defines g-vee(xi) using
sup_alpha(<xi,alpha>+g(alpha)). Direct section evaluation then gives
g-vee(-xi)-0-vee(-xi). For g(alpha)=alpha on [0,1], corresponding to
ord_0, the direct value at xi=1 is zero; the printed positive-sign
formula gives one. Likewise real MA of the plus-sign Legendre transform
needs reflection to coincide with the separately correct positive
gradient pushforward in valuation coordinates. Example B.2's Legendre
maximum is consistent with its express plus-sign definition. The intended
dictionary can be stated with either convention after the identification
is made explicit; it cannot be imported without that identification.

## Test configuration interface

Lemma A.12, physical 94, printed 921, proves that the metric of an AMPLE
test configuration equals raw FS of its associated norm and its
homogenization. It uses Theorem A.6, finite-type FS membership and the
finite-type envelope identity. Theorem A.6 and its displayed proof were
subsequently read at physical 91–92, with the preceding Proposition A.3
continuation; the beginning of Proposition A.3 and all earlier model
prerequisites remain outside this selection.

Relative integral closure is taken inside the generic fibre and should
not be replaced by absolute normality on a nonnormal original X.
Lemma A.7 traces an integral equation coefficient in original degree im;
the printed Laurent coefficient space H0(X,mL) on physical 92 must be
H0(X,imL). Associated graded equals the full central-fibre section ring
only after a sufficiently divisible Veronese, as explicitly stated in
(A.6). Flatness alone supplies no every-small-degree surjectivity.
Corollary A.8 imports the reduced-central-fibre result after sufficiently
divisible base change. Pullback to an equivariant resolution can be
semiample and need not remain ample.

Proposition A.15's assertion that every rank-r test configuration induces
a norm of exact rank r needs effectiveness/minimality. Definition A.14
permits the flat product X times A^r with the standard torus action only
on A^r and a positive rationally independent direction. Its induced norm
is zero, of rank zero. The intended finite-type realization and a rank
at-most-r statement survive; removing redundant torus directions gives
the appropriate minimal rank. The rank-one gloss in Section 1.4 also
needs its trivial-case convention. These rank issues do not contradict
the ample rank-one FS metric identity used in Finski.
