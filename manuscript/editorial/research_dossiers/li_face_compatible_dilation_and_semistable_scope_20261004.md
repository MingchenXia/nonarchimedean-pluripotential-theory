# Face-compatible dilation and the semistable application category

Date: 4 October 2026. This is a book-external source-supported reduction.
It follows the [normalized cyclic-cover calculation](li_cyclic_normalization_and_height_one_subdivision_20261004.md)
and the [positive-model calculation](li_normalized_blowup_model_presentation_20261004.md).
Known source statements, direct lattice consequences, literal source
diagnostics and remaining application hypotheses are distinguished below.
There is no independent model-verifier receipt, new book theorem, numerical
experiment, priority claim or change to the 42 open-problem statuses.

## 1. Four statements that must remain distinct

For a lattice polytope P, a unimodular triangulation uses the ambient
affine difference lattice, rather than just the lattice generated by its
original vertices. Coherence means that a height function induces the
triangulation by lower faces. It supplies projectivity in the toric
application. Compatibility means agreement on every identified common
face. These are separate requirements.

Haase–Paffenholz–Piechnik–Santos, arXiv:1405.1687v3, Theorem 4.1,
physical page 63, states that some positive integral dilation cP has a
regular unimodular triangulation. Theorem 4.5, page 64, gives an
effective bound and a regular refinement of a dilated supplied
triangulation. A regular refinement of a triangulation does not make
the original triangulation regular. Their Theorem 4.11, pages 70–71,
preserves regularity when the initial triangulation is regular.

Liu, arXiv:2112.04654v1, Theorem 1.2, physical page 2, states that
all sufficiently large integral dilations admit unimodular
triangulations. The same page says that the triangulation can be
made regular but that the paper does not prove this assertion.
Thus its displayed theorem is not treated here as a checked proof
of the coherent version for every sufficiently large dilation.
Page 3 explains compatibility with faces and extension to polytopal
complexes. Theorem 2.9, pages 10–11, proves canonicity from local
confluence, termination and facial compatibility. Only the recorded
scoped parts of this 40-page paper have been freshly read.

Adiprasito–Liu–Temkin, arXiv:1810.03131v2, Theorem 2.7, physical
page 4, directly treats finite abstract conical complexes and their
lattices. Its Theorem 3.3, page 7, treats maps of lattice polytopal
complexes and asserts a projective compatible resolution after a
dilation and alteration. This is a closer source for the simultaneous
application than a separate invocation of KMW on each maximal cell.
The proof and its projectivity qualification are discussed below.

Ferry–Joswig–Rambau, arXiv:2605.14150v1, concerns reflection-invariant
unimodular triangulations of the standard triangle d Delta_2 in the
plane. It counts all such triangulations, without imposing coherent
heights. It supplies a related 2026 combinatorial research direction,
not a theorem producing projective semistable models in arbitrary
dimension.

## 2. What the canonical construction actually provides

The HPPS construction starts with a global order of vertices. Its
canonical triangulation of a dilated simplex restricts to the
canonical triangulation of each ordered face. In the vertex-generated
lattice it is a type-A unimodular triangulation. A simplex of index
greater than one in the ambient lattice does not thereby become
ambient-unimodular. The separate box-point reduction addresses that
index.

Theorem 4.11 proves coherence using a small perturbation of supplied
initial coherent weights and type-A dicing weights. Lemma 4.16,
pages 75–76, specifies the boundary triangulation and permits its
weights to extend over a star. Corollary 4.18, page 77, performs the
construction on disjoint open stars with matching boundary weights.
Theorem 4.19, pages 77–78, lowers maximal simplex volume. The
carrier and A-equivalence bookkeeping in Lemmas 4.21–4.25 and
Corollary 4.26 controls compatible iteration. Theorem 4.27,
pages 81–82, concludes a regular unimodular refinement of a
dilated triangulation.

These are genuine common-face and height-extension ingredients.
They are not a license to assign unrelated coherent triangulations
to maximal cells and glue afterwards. Nor does local coherence alone
produce a single compatible projective height function.

ALT explicitly formalizes the stronger requirement. Remark 3.4,
physical pages 7–8, says that projectivity of intermediate subdivisions
of individual cells does not guarantee projectivity of the whole
subdivision. It asks for canonical height functions restricting to
the same functions on faces, and leaves verification of those height
functions in the subsequent constructions to the reader. The fresh
reading includes the recursive Cayley constructions, confluence,
box-point index reduction and the log-geometric lifting. It does not
replace this omitted check with an independent proof.

If a compatible projective height function on a finite rational
subdivision is already supplied, it can be made rational without
changing its subdivision. Values on the finite set of rays satisfy
rational linear equalities for linearity and face identification,
and strict rational linear inequalities for the prescribed bends.
The solution space of the equalities has a rational affine basis;
a sufficiently close rational point preserves the finitely many
strict inequalities. Clearing denominators gives integral values.
This direct finite-dimensional observation assumes the compatible
height function exists. It does not construct missing compatible
heights in ALT's recursive proof.

## 3. Direct consequence for a supplied height-cone complex

Assume a finite conical complex C in the exact sense of ALT Section
2.1.1 is supplied. Each cell sigma has a lattice N_sigma; lattices
on faces are the intersections with their linear spans. Assume
compatible integral linear functions

    h_sigma : N_sigma -> Z

which are strictly positive on sigma minus its origin. They define
a map C -> (R_{>=0}, Z). Strict positivity excludes unbounded
height slices and rules out extra height-zero rays. This is the
complex appropriate to the central monomial coordinates; ordinary
transverse smooth coordinates do not supply additional such rays.

Import the projective resolution conclusion of ALT Theorem 2.7
as a known source input, with the coherence qualification just
recorded. A subdivision of the target ray leaves its underlying
ray unchanged. A lattice alteration of that ray replaces Z by
cZ for one positive integer c. Its induced source lattice is

    N_sigma(c) = {v in N_sigma : h_sigma(v) in cZ}.

The source resolution is regular in these lattices, and weak
semistability holds on every cone, including its one-dimensional
faces. If v is a primitive generator of a resolved source ray,
the lattice-surjectivity condition on that ray gives

    h_sigma(v) = c.

It is essential to apply the condition to the rays. Surjectivity
from a higher-dimensional cone's entire lattice only gives a gcd
condition on its generator heights and would not suffice.

Let rho be a full-dimensional resolved cone of dimension r, with
primitive basis vectors v_1,...,v_r of N_sigma(c). Its height-c
slice is the simplex conv(v_1,...,v_r). Write

    H_sigma = ker(h_sigma : N_sigma -> Z).

The vectors v_j-v_1, j=2,...,r, are a basis of H_sigma: in the
basis v_1,...,v_r a vector has height c times the sum of its
coefficients. Their sum is zero precisely for elements of H_sigma.
Thus the slice is unimodular in its affine difference lattice.
The same calculation includes r=1, where the slice is a point
and H_sigma is zero. Resolved cones agree on identified faces,
so their height slices give compatible triangulations.

For the original monomial orthant with h(v)=sum b_i v_i, every
original coordinate ray survives subdivision. Its primitive vector
in N_sigma(c) is

    (c/gcd(c,b_i)) e_i.

Its height divided by c is b_i/gcd(c,b_i). Semistability on this
ray forces b_i to divide c. This recovers the required common
divisibility, rather than assuming it follows from a gcd condition
on a maximal cone.

If a prior cover of degree q divisible by all b_i was already
made, replacing its parameter by a further degree-a cover replaces
the level q slice by the level aq slice. The affine difference
lattice remains ker h. Relative to any level-q lattice vertex,
the new slice is the dilation aP_q, up to a lattice translation.
An origin for an affine dilation must be specified; literal
multiplication in an arbitrary translated affine lattice is not
an intrinsic operation.

Consequently, under the stated imported projective complex theorem,
there is a simultaneous coherent ambient-unimodular subdivision
after a common cover for this supplied complex. This is a direct
specialization of a known theorem, not a new combinatorial theorem.
The conclusion remains conditional on the exact conical-complex
category and on the projectivity import. It is stronger and more
appropriate than independent single-polytope triangulations.

## 4. Geometric realization still needs its own hypotheses

The previous local calculation turns a supplied coherent rational
subdivision into a normalized monomial blowup on an affine toric
chart. Its parameter is a unit away from the central fibre, and
the monomial centre can be chosen vertical on that chart. A
simultaneous complex subdivision does not by itself prove global
descent of these ideals in the analytic family.

ALT Section 4 supplies a primary route for log schemes. Theorem
4.4, physical page 20, lifts the quasi-local conical construction
to a projective monoidal resolution compatible with surjective
strict morphisms. Its proof uses toric stacks, independence of
charts and etale descent. A monoidal alteration can be a root
stack rather than a scheme. Example 4.3, page 19, distinguishes
root stacks from representable subdivisions and states that
monoidal alterations are isomorphisms on the trivial-log locus.

Theorem 4.7, page 21, assumes a dominant finite-type morphism of
quasi-excellent integral schemes in characteristic zero and a
proper closed subset Z of the source. It gives a stack-theoretic
base modification and a projective modification of the proper
transform. Its preservation clause assumes a regular open base
B_0, smoothness over B_0 and a relative normal-crossings divisor
there. The proof uses relative desingularization [ATW19], which
the v2 references list as in preparation. That dependency has
not been acquired and checked at this checkpoint.

Remark 4.6 and the paragraph after Theorem 4.8 warn that replacing
the stack base by a scheme using Kawamata's trick may alter the
previous good open part. Thus a statement about a stack resolution
does not automatically give a scheme over a prescribed cyclic
cover with an unchanged polarized generic fibre. Over a complex
trait, a root-stack atlas is a promising specific route, but its
exact base change and global normalized-blowup presentation must
be shown in the chosen category.

The remaining checks are these: the actual family defines the
required finite log/conical object, including any chart monodromy;
the convergent-germ scheme is in the stated quasi-excellent
category or an applicable comparison has been proved; a scheme
cover of the stack has the specified parameter normalization;
the projective source map has the required finite vertical
normalized-blowup presentation; and the resulting central divisor
is globally reduced strict SNC while the fixed generic
polarization is preserved. These are explicit application inputs,
not an assertion that semistable reduction is unknown.

Once these inputs are supplied, the earlier positive-model and
ramified-descent calculations apply. The argument here does not
quietly substitute arbitrary desingularization, which can introduce
central multiplicities, or an alteration of the generic CY family.

## 5. Literal source diagnostics

In HPPS v3, physical page 73, the height of a nonzero box point
is stated to lie between 1 and V-1, where V is the simplex
index. Consider the lattice tetrahedron

    Delta = conv(0,e_1,e_2,(1,1,2)).

Its index is 2. The lattice point (1,1,1) has barycentric
coordinates (-1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2). The sum of their fractional parts
is 2, exceeding V-1=1. The general dimension-based range for a
nonzero box point is 1,...,dim Delta. This explicit lattice
calculation concerns the printed auxiliary range. It is not a
counterexample to KMW.

The same paragraph's coset m+Lambda cannot locate a nonzero box
class when m is already in the ambient lattice Lambda; that
coset is Lambda itself. The vertex difference lattice is the
relevant quotient lattice. Lemma 4.16 on page 75 also prints
m in L_F minus Lambda_F, reversing the stated ambient-versus-
vertex lattice inclusion. Its statement uses divisibility by k+1
for a k-dimensional face, while the proof switches to k in its
barycentre and shell description. The actual PDF contains these
expressions. They are retained as local notation/correction
requirements, without silently certifying every corrected proof
line or rejecting the principal known theorem.

ALT v2, physical page 19, Theorem 4.2(i) prints an ill-typed
base-change formula with a fibre product over Y, although the
supplied morphism is Y -> X. The intended base-change expression
would be Y times_X L^{log}(X). The later Theorem 4.4 uses the
appropriate base-change direction.

A substantive missing hypothesis occurs in ALT Theorem 4.5,
page 20. It assumes only a log smooth morphism between fine log
schemes, but the immediately preceding definition of semistable
also requires a log regular base. Let

    B = Spec C[x,y]/(y^2-x^3),   X=B,

with trivial fine log structures and f the identity. The
identity is log smooth. The cusp is singular: its local dimension
at the origin is one and its cotangent-space dimension is two.
Example 4.3(iii) makes every monoidal alteration an isomorphism
on the triviality locus, which here is all of B. Such an
alteration cannot make B regular. Therefore Theorem 4.5 cannot
hold in its unrestricted literal form with that definition.
Adding a log-regular base is the natural required premise, not a
proof of the rest of the theorem. Theorem 4.7's proof explicitly
resolves its intermediate base to be log regular before applying
4.5, so this diagnostic is not a disproof of Theorem 4.7 or the
main combinatorial resolution.

## 6. Related May 2026 counting direction

The full nine-page Ferry–Joswig–Rambau v1 text was read. Its
standard reflection is x=y. The reported enumeration reaches
d=9; the authors credit an independent implementation through
d=8. Neither computation was reproduced here. Their enumeration
method invokes Rambau [Ram26], listed without an arXiv identifier
in the references; that dependency has not been checked.

Physical page 3 defines the half-triangle

    T_d = conv((0,0),(d/2,d/2),(d,0)).

It then calls T_d a rotated copy of (d/2) Delta_2 and denotes
its count by F(d/2), although F was defined on page 1 for the
full standard triangle. The normalized Euclidean areas of these
two triangles are d^2/2 and d^2/4, respectively. A rotation
cannot identify them, and there is no unimodular lattice
equivalence. When d is odd, the top vertex of T_d is not a
lattice point, so T_d itself cannot be the union of a lattice
triangulation. One can instead study the lattice point set in
T_d and its convex hull; this interpretation requires an
explicit definition and is not silently substituted here.

Lemma 9(2), physical page 7, gives 2d+1 boundary lattice points
for odd d. Directly counting the displayed triangle's edges
gives 2d for both parities. Its base contributes d+1 points.
Each sloping edge contributes floor(d/2) additional points;
the top point is counted twice only when d is even. Thus

    n_boundary = d+1+2 floor(d/2)-1_{d even} = 2d.

For d=3 these six points are (0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0),(1,1)
and (2,1). The convex hull of the lattice point set has the
same boundary count: for odd d the rational corner is cut by
one primitive edge connecting the two top lattice points.
The printed proof replaces floor(d/2) by d/2 in the odd case.
These are literal auxiliary defects. They do not by themselves
disprove its reported enumerations or quadratic-order asymptotic
conclusion, whose source dependencies and interpretation still
require checking. The page-6 rectangle paragraph also calls
an estimate a lower bound while using it as an upper bound.

The source's Conjecture 11 asks for the asymptotic symmetric
counting capacity to equal one. The word capacity here concerns
logarithmic triangulation counts; it is not pluripotential
capacity. Two quadratic upper/lower bounds alone do not prove
that a normalized count has a limit. A useful related direction
would separate the limit-existence question, the value question,
and the coherent symmetric subclass. This is an external research
lead, without adding an unverified item to the book's fixed
42-atom ledger.
