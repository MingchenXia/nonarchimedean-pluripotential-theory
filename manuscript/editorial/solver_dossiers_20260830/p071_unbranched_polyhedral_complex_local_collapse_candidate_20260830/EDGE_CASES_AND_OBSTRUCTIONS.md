# Edge cases and hostile obstruction ledger

Date: 30 August 2026

This ledger separates cases actually proved in `statement.tex` from nearby
claims for which the proof gives no support.

## Cases inside the theorem

| Case | Why the proof covers it |
|---|---|
| Interior of a maximal cell | Take the convex chart inside that cell and apply the pinned one-chamber theorem. |
| An internal face shared by two or more cells of a subdivision | The chart target is one convex polyhedron and the incident cell pieces are only a subdivision.  One model approximation is constructed across the entire face. |
| Boundary and corner points | Chart images and approximation domains are relatively open in the convex polyhedron, hence are ordinary neighbourhoods in the complex topology. |
| Lower-dimensional cells / empty ambient interior | All affine spans, Hessians, convexity, and relative neighbourhoods are taken in `Aff(P)`, never in a larger ambient affine space. |
| Dimension zero | The chart polyhedron is a point.  Convexity is vacuous; the Stone quotient and decreasing approximation remain meaningful.  The pinned proof has no empty maximum or polar-coordinate convention. |
| Empty complex | All asserted sheaf equalities are vacuous.  Every nonempty germ still uses a nonempty chart polyhedron. |
| Unbounded cell or complex | The pinned theorem shrinks around the germ and uses a compact bounded polyhedral core.  No global compactness of the chart target is required. |
| Nonmetrizable or non-second-countable `Z` | The pinned theorem uses a compact metrizable quotient generated by countably many clopens; it never metrizes `Z`.  The chart-transport argument adds no countability assumption. |
| Circle or other loop with affine monodromy | Each germ is handled in one interval/polyhedral chart.  No chart is analytically continued around the loop. |
| Orientation-reversing or other generator-preserving holonomy | The transition transports stage zero and therefore every locally generated stage.  The conclusion remains local. |
| Embedded nonconvex shape with an intrinsic convex unfolding | It is covered if, and only if, the chosen generator sheaf is transported from the one-chamber convex model by that unfolding.  Embedded nonconvexity by itself is not the decisive condition. |

Finiteness of the complex is harmless and is part of the requested scope.
The proof itself is germ-local and would also apply to a locally finite
complex with the same generator-convex atlas; that enlargement is not needed
for the present statement.

## Minimal cross-face obstruction

Let

```text
L = R_{≥0} e_1 ∪ R_{≥0} e_2 ⊂ R^2.
```

It is finite, face-to-face, and topologically a line at the vertex.  Give it
the sheaf obtained by restricting ambient smooth functions whose Hessian is
positive semidefinite on each ray.  The ambient affine function
`F(x,y)=-x-y` is a stage-zero generator.  Every cellwise affine unfolding of
the two rays to opposite sides of an interval has positive left slope and
negative right slope for the transported `F`.  It is therefore nonconvex at
the origin.  In the unit-speed unfolding it is `-|t|`.

Consequences:

1. face-to-face incidence and “two chambers at a codimension-one face” do
   not supply the cross-face slope inequality;
2. equality of values on the common face is insufficient;
3. cellwise Hessian positivity is not the same generator sheaf as smooth
   convexity on an intrinsic unfolded interval;
4. the pinned single-chamber theorem cannot be transported to this ambient
   cellwise structure.

This example does **not** prove that the CLD hierarchy for every such
cellwise structure fails to collapse.  It proves only that the proposed
convex-chart reduction and its fibrewise globally convex comparison sheaf
would be false without the generator-preserving hypothesis.

## Cross-face Jensen relations

There is a second, complementary reason that “convex on every cell” is too
weak as a comparison definition.  Suppose a smooth generator near a vertex
`p` is the restriction of one ambient smooth function `F`.  Choose incident
cell points `x_j` so that each segment `[p,x_j]` stays in one cell, and let
positive weights `alpha_j` satisfy

```text
Σ alpha_j = 1,          Σ alpha_j (x_j-p) = 0.
```

Positivity on the cell makes `F` convex along `[p,x_j]`, hence

```text
F(x_j) ≥ F(p) + dF_p(x_j-p).
```

After weighting and summing, the derivative terms cancel and give the
cross-face Jensen inequality

```text
F(p) ≤ Σ alpha_j F(x_j).
```

Every pointwise decreasing limit preserves this inequality, including
extended-real limits, so every stage of the generated psh sheaf satisfies
it.

On a straight line subdivided into two rays, the function `u(t)=-|t|` is
affine, hence convex, on each closed ray, but

```text
u(0) = 0 > -s = (u(-s)+u(s))/2.
```

It is therefore excluded from the generated sheaf although a merely
cellwise-convex comparison would include it.  Likewise, on a balanced
tripod with `v_1+v_2+v_3=0`, the branchwise affine function
`u(t v_i)=-t` violates the three-way Jensen inequality at the vertex.
These are obstructions to branchwise-only gluing, not counterexamples to
hierarchy collapse: they identify functions which were never in the
generated psh sheaf.

## Genuine branching

A tripod made from three rays meeting at one vertex is not locally
homeomorphic at the vertex to a relatively open subset of any
one-dimensional convex polyhedron.  It therefore has no chart of the
required kind, irrespective of the generator sheaf.  Higher-dimensional
products of the tripod give the same obstruction.

The frequently used pseudomanifold condition “each codimension-one face is
incident to at most two maximal cells” is weaker than local unbranching in
the theorem.  For example, the cone on a finite triangulation of
`RP^2` satisfies the codimension-one incidence condition (allowing its base
as boundary), but the apex has link `RP^2`, not a sphere or ball.  It cannot
have a neighbourhood homeomorphic to a relatively open subset of a convex
three-dimensional polyhedron.  The theorem deliberately excludes this
singular lower-link germ.

## Loop and monodromy firewall

On a polyhedral circle, local charts may all be intervals although the
composition of affine transitions around the loop is nontrivial.  The proof
still yields:

- equality of the three **sheaves**;
- for each point, a decreasing generator sequence on one fixed interval
  neighbourhood.

It does not yield:

- one convex chart covering the circle;
- termwise agreement of sequences chosen in different charts;
- one global decreasing sequence of global smooth psh generators.

A partition of unity is not an available formal repair: multiplying convex
functions by variable cutoffs can destroy convexity.  Taking an ordinary
maximum preserves convexity in one affine chart but generally loses
smoothness, while a regularized maximum would require quantitative overlap
control and a common generator calculus not supplied by the hypotheses.
Accordingly, global-sequence gluing is recorded as unproved rather than
silently inferred.

## Nonconvex unions

A union of chambers can be nonconvex in an ambient affine space but still
admit a piecewise-affine homeomorphism to a convex polyhedron.  The theorem
applies only if this homeomorphism also identifies the **complete generator
germ**.  Conversely, even a topological manifold can fail this condition,
as the two-ray example shows.

Thus none of the following is a sufficient replacement for the hypothesis:

- cellwise convexity;
- positivity tested independently on maximal cells;
- agreement of functions or forms on common faces;
- face-to-face incidence;
- codimension-one nonbranching;
- existence of a merely topological or merely piecewise-affine unfolding.

## Exact release decision

The local generator-convex theorem is proved as a formal consequence of the
pinned model theorem.  The unrestricted finite unbranched/cellwise-positive
complex problem remains **open in this dossier**.  The two-ray example blocks
the naive reduction but is not advertised as a counterexample to every
possible formulation of hierarchy collapse.
