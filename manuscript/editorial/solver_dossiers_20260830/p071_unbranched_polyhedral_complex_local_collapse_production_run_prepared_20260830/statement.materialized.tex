Let \(Z\) be a locally compact Hausdorff space with a basis of clopen
subsets.  Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real affine space, let
\(P\subset E\) be a nonempty convex polyhedron, possibly unbounded and
possibly of dimension smaller than \(E\), and put
\(A=\operatorname{Aff}(P)\).  Set
\[
                              X=Z\times P .
\]

We use the following smooth psh generator sheaf on \(X\).  A real-valued
function \(f\) on an open subset of \(X\) is smooth if, locally at every
\((z,x)\), there are a clopen neighbourhood \(D\) of \(z\), an open
neighbourhood \(O\) of \(x\) in \(A\), and \(F\in C^\infty(O)\) such that
\[
                  f(z',x')=F(x')
       \quad\text{on }D\times(P\cap O).
\]
It is a smooth psh generator if the Hessian of \(F\) is positive
semidefinite at every point of \(P\cap O\).  Denote this sheaf by
\(\operatorname{Psh}_{X,0}\), denote by
\(\operatorname{Psh}_{X,1}\) its first local pointwise-decreasing-limit
stage, and denote by \(\operatorname{Psh}_X\) the smallest subsheaf of
\(\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\}\)-valued functions which contains
\(\operatorname{Psh}_{X,0}\) and is stable under local pointwise decreasing
limits of sequences.

Define \(\mathscr C_X\) by the following local condition.  A section \(u\)
belongs to \(\mathscr C_X\) near \((z_0,x_0)\) if there are
\begin{itemize}
\item a compact clopen neighbourhood \(C\subset Z\) of \(z_0\);
\item a relatively open convex neighbourhood \(V\subset P\) of \(x_0\);
\item a compact metrizable zero-dimensional space \(Y\) and a continuous
  surjection \(q:C\twoheadrightarrow Y\); and
\item an upper semicontinuous function
  \(\bar u:Y\times V\to\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\}\)
\end{itemize}
such that
\begin{equation}
 u(z,x)=\bar u(q(z),x),
 \qquad
 x\longmapsto\bar u(y,x)\text{ is extended-real convex for every }y\in Y.
 \label{eq:p071-polyhedral-factorization}
\end{equation}
Here extended-real convexity uses no \(+\infty\) values and omits terms
with zero coefficient.

Then \(\mathscr C_X\) is a sheaf and
\begin{equation}
             \operatorname{Psh}_{X,1}
             =\operatorname{Psh}_X
             =\mathscr C_X.
 \label{eq:p071-polyhedral-collapse}
\end{equation}
More precisely, if \(u\in\operatorname{Psh}_X(W)\) and
\((z_0,x_0)\in W\), there are a compact clopen neighbourhood
\(C\ni z_0\), a relatively open polyhedral neighbourhood \(V\ni x_0\),
and smooth psh functions \(G_k\in\operatorname{Psh}_{X,0}(C\times V)\)
such that
\begin{equation}
                         G_{k+1}\leq G_k,
             \qquad      G_k\longrightarrow u|_{C\times V}
             \quad\text{pointwise}.
 \label{eq:p071-polyhedral-fixed-domain}
\end{equation}

The same conclusion holds on every Hausdorff space equipped with a
generator sheaf which is locally identified, by generator-preserving
charts, with one of the preceding product models.  In particular it holds
on locally finite polyhedral manifolds with corners, with an arbitrary
locally compact clopen-base transverse parameter carrying only locally
constant affine directions.

For the standing \(K=\mathbf Q\), \(G=\mathbf R\) conventions of
Chambert--Loir--Ducros, a rational polyhedron \(P\) with its induced
affine tropical structure and \(Z\) with its locally constant affine
structure give the displayed generator sheaf through their product
construction.  The assertion is confined to this convex single-chamber
model.  It makes no claim for a nonconvex union of chambers, a branched
polyhedral complex, nonconstant affine directions on \(Z\), or an
arbitrary affine \(G\)-tropical space.  It therefore narrows but does not
retire P07.1.

Let \(\Sigma\) be a finite Hausdorff face-to-face polyhedral complex,
equipped with a sheaf \(\mathcal G_{\Sigma,0}\) of real-valued smooth psh
generators.  In this statement, saying that the pair
\((\Sigma,\mathcal G_{\Sigma,0})\) is \emph{generator-convexly unbranched}
means the following stronger and explicit condition.  Every
\(p\in|\Sigma|\) has an open neighbourhood \(U\), a finite-dimensional real
affine space \(A\), a nonempty convex polyhedron \(P\subset A\), a relatively
open convex subset \(V\subset P\), and a homeomorphism
\begin{equation}
                         \varphi:U\xrightarrow{\sim}V
 \label{eq:p071-complex-chart}
\end{equation}
such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the images under \(\varphi\) of the cell pieces in \(U\) form the
  restriction to \(V\) of a finite face-to-face polyhedral subdivision of
  \(P\), and \(\varphi\) is affine on every such cell piece;
\item the complete germ of \(\mathcal G_{\Sigma,0}|_U\) is carried by
  \(\varphi\) to the single-chamber generator sheaf on
  \(V\): locally, its members are restrictions of functions
  \(F\in C^\infty(O)\), where \(O\) is open in
  \(\operatorname{Aff}(P)\), and the Hessian of \(F\) is positive
  semidefinite on \(P\cap O\);
\item on every overlap, the transition homeomorphism preserves this
  generator sheaf in both directions.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the cells occurring inside a chart are a subdivision of one convex
chamber, rather than separate chambers across which positivity is tested
independently.  This convention implies the required absence of local
branching.  The weaker condition that every codimension-one face belong to
at most two maximal cells is not being used as a substitute for
\eqref{eq:p071-complex-chart}.

Let \(Z\) be a locally compact Hausdorff space with a basis of clopen
subsets and put
\[
                              X=Z\times|\Sigma|.
\]
On \(X\), let \(\operatorname{Psh}_{X,0}\) be the product generator sheaf:
after shrinking in \(Z\) to a clopen set and using a chart
\eqref{eq:p071-complex-chart}, a generator is independent of the
\(Z\)-variable and is one of the preceding single-chamber generators.
Let \(\operatorname{Psh}_{X,1}\) be the first local pointwise-decreasing
limit stage, and let \(\operatorname{Psh}_X\) be the smallest subsheaf of
\(\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\}\)-valued functions containing
\(\operatorname{Psh}_{X,0}\) and stable under local pointwise decreasing
limits of sequences.

Define a comparison germ at \((z_0,p_0)\) as follows.  Choose a chart
\eqref{eq:p071-complex-chart} at \(p_0\).  A section \(u\) is a comparison
germ if, after shrinking, there are
\begin{itemize}
\item a compact clopen neighbourhood \(C\subset Z\) of \(z_0\);
\item a relatively open convex neighbourhood \(W\subset V\) of
  \(\varphi(p_0)\);
\item a compact metrizable zero-dimensional space \(Y\) and a continuous
  surjection \(q:C\twoheadrightarrow Y\); and
\item an upper semicontinuous function
  \(\bar u:Y\times W\to\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\}\)
\end{itemize}
such that
\begin{equation}
 u(z,p)=\bar u\bigl(q(z),\varphi(p)\bigr),
 \qquad
 x\longmapsto\bar u(y,x)
 \text{ is extended-real convex for every }y\in Y.
 \label{eq:p071-complex-comparison}
\end{equation}
No \(+\infty\) values are allowed, and zero-weight terms are omitted in
extended-real convex combinations.  On every open subset of \(X\), let
\(\mathscr C_X\) consist of the functions all of whose germs have this
property.

Then the condition \eqref{eq:p071-complex-comparison} is independent of the
chosen generator-convex chart, \(\mathscr C_X\) is a sheaf, and
\begin{equation}
              \operatorname{Psh}_{X,1}
              =\operatorname{Psh}_X
              =\mathscr C_X.
 \label{eq:p071-complex-collapse}
\end{equation}
More precisely, if \(u\in\operatorname{Psh}_X(\Omega)\) and
\((z_0,p_0)\in\Omega\), one may choose one chart
\eqref{eq:p071-complex-chart}, one compact clopen \(C\ni z_0\), and one
relatively open convex polyhedral neighbourhood
\(W\ni\varphi(p_0)\), with
\(C\times\varphi^{-1}(W)\subset\Omega\), together with
\[
 G_k\in\operatorname{Psh}_{X,0}
       \bigl(C\times\varphi^{-1}(W)\bigr)
\]
such that
\begin{equation}
                 G_{k+1}\leq G_k,
 \qquad          G_k\longrightarrow
                 u|_{C\times\varphi^{-1}(W)}
                 \quad\text{pointwise}.
 \label{eq:p071-complex-fixed-star}
\end{equation}
The single domain in \eqref{eq:p071-complex-fixed-star} crosses, at once,
all cell faces represented in the chart.  No partition-of-unity or
regularized-maximum gluing of independently chosen chamberwise
approximants is involved.

The assertion is local as a sheaf statement.  It does not assert that one
sequence of global generators on all of \(Z\times|\Sigma|\) decreases to
every global psh section.  In particular, affine monodromy around a circle
or another loop is harmless for \eqref{eq:p071-complex-collapse}, because a
single chart is used at each germ, but no global convex development or
global approximation theorem is inferred from this fact.

The generator-convex hypothesis cannot be replaced by face-to-face and
topological unbranching together with cellwise positivity.  Indeed, let
\[
 L=\mathbf R_{\geq0}e_1\cup\mathbf R_{\geq0}e_2\subset\mathbf R^2
\]
with the two-ray face-to-face decomposition and with the ambient-induced
cellwise smooth psh generators.  The ambient affine function
\begin{equation}
                         F(x,y)=-x-y
 \label{eq:p071-complex-v-function}
\end{equation}
is such a generator.  Under the intrinsic unfolding
\(te_1\mapsto t\) and \(te_2\mapsto -t\), its restriction becomes
\(-|t|\), which is not convex at the origin.  Hence that complete germ is
not generator-preservingly identifiable with the single convex interval
model.  The same example shows why compatibility merely on the common
face, where it is vacuous, supplies no cross-face slope inequality.

The theorem includes dimension zero, lower-dimensional and unbounded
cells, boundary and corner points, and finite complexes with loop
monodromy, provided the displayed generator-convex charts exist.  It makes
no claim for branched links, for a nonconvex chamber union without such a
chart, for a merely codimension-one-unbranched pseudomanifold with singular
lower links, or for a generator sheaf defined only by independent
cellwise positivity.
