# Scope firewall

This repaired candidate establishes two different conclusions and does not
conflate them.

## Full every-open conclusion

The abstract proposition and its polycylinder corollary require a genuine
basis for the ordinary topology. Therefore, for each open `W`, point `x in
W`, and equal-curvature pair on `W`, one may choose a basis member `Q` with
`x in Q subset W`. This refinement is the entire reason the full
continuous-pair Bedford--Taylor predicate follows.

A polycylinder atlas, one polycylinder neighbourhood per point, or an open
cover by polycylinders is not substituted for this basis hypothesis.

## Fixed-domain conclusion

For one standard strict open polycylinder `P`, the candidate proves the
identity only when the pair `u,v` is defined on all of `P`. It does not say
that every ordinary open subset of `P` is a standard polycylinder, has a
standard-polycylinder basis, or satisfies P06.2 zero-current regularity.
Accordingly, it does not call every positive closed current on a single `P`
a Bedford--Taylor current.

## Ambient and geometric scope

- The analytic framework is good, topologically separated, pure-dimensional,
  and without boundary. These hypotheses put the order-zero multiplier,
  current sheaf, and printed CLD Section 8.5 calculus in a source-supported
  common category.
- Strictness is imposed only in the concrete polycylinder statements, where
  it belongs to the P06.2 dependency.
- The field is complete and nontrivially valued in the polycylinder results.
- Every disc and annulus is standard, strict, and open; all radii lie in
  `|K^times|`. Closed, half-closed, and non-strict endpoint cases are absent.
- No etale, log-etale, moment-map, toroidal, or quasi-smooth chart is replaced
  by an analytic product isomorphism.
- Potentials are continuous. Nothing here proves the literal CLD predicate
  for discontinuous psh functions.
- Currents may have arbitrary support. The argument uses only positivity,
  symmetry, order zero, and `d'`/`d''` closedness.
- The bidegree range is `0 <= ell <= n-1`, so applying `d'd''` remains within
  the current bicomplex.

This is a boundary result for P21.3, not a retirement of the general atom.
