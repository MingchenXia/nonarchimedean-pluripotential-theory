\begin{proof}[Proof of
\cref{prop:p213-hessian-regular-basis-candidate}]
Put \(h=u-v\).  Multiplication of the positive current \(T|_W\), which is
of order zero, by the continuous functions \(u,v,h\) is defined, and
linearity gives
\[
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''(uT|_W)
 -\mathrm d'\mathrm d''(vT|_W)
 =\mathrm d'\mathrm d''(hT|_W).
\]
The assumed equality of curvature currents says
\(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0\) on \(W\).

Fix \(x\in W\).  Choose \(Q\in\mathscr B\) with
\(x\in Q\subseteq W\).  By the defining property of \(\mathscr B\), the
restriction \(h|_Q\) is ordinary smooth and
\(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''h|_Q=0\) as a smooth form.  Since a positive closed
current is symmetric and both \(\mathrm d'\)- and
\(\mathrm d''\)-closed, the smooth-current Leibniz identity gives
\[
 \mathrm d'\mathrm d''\bigl(hT|_Q\bigr)
 =\mathrm d'\mathrm d''h\wedge T|_Q=0.
\]
This identity is local in \(Q\).  As \(x\) was arbitrary, the sheaf property
for currents yields
\(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''(hT|_W)=0\) on \(W\), proving the assertion.  The
psh specialization uses no additional argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:p213-polycylinder-basis-candidate}]
Let \(Q\) be a member of the stated basis, and transport a continuous
function \(h\) with zero curvature current to its standard strict open
polycylinder model.  The arbitrary-field strict-open-polycylinder theorem
\cref{thm:p062-arbitrary-field-polycylinder-candidate} gives unique
constants \(c,\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_b\in\mathbf R\) such that
\[
                    h=c+\sum_{j=1}^{b}\lambda_j\log|S_j|.
\]
Each \(S_j\) is an analytic unit on its annulus factor.  Its tropicalization
\(-\log|S_j|\) is an ordinary CLD-smooth coordinate, and an affine function
of these coordinates is ordinary CLD-smooth with zero smooth Hessian.
Consequently the displayed basis satisfies the hypothesis of
\cref{prop:p213-hessian-regular-basis-candidate}, which proves the global
and local assertions.

For the last assertion take \(Q=P\) directly.  This last use does not claim
that arbitrary ordinary open subsets of \(P\) are standard polycylinders.
\end{proof}
