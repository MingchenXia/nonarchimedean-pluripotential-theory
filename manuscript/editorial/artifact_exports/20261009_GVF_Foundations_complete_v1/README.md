## Main scope and proof boundaries

Theorem 7.7's complete printed dictionary proof and its local-measure,
positivity and height predecessors have been read. The dictionary uses
normalized pseudo-valuation patches, positive functionals on lattice
divisors, representative measures modulo homogeneous renormalization,
and densely generated L1 lattices. One fixed common triangle-error bound
is required by the continuous-logic language and bounded-height
ultraproduct. GVF_e means ht(2)<=e; it does not normalize ht(2) to equal e.

Corollary 7.11 represents every countable MVF by an ordinary adelic
structure and every countable GVF by a proper one. Countability permits
one common full-measure set where all field elements have finite
valuations. The arbitrary-cardinality statement instead uses
pseudo-valuations and local measure families; it does not provide an
ordinary absolute-value representation without further hypotheses.

The complete finite-Galois proof, Proposition 10.5, gives existence and
uniqueness **under Aut(F/K)-invariance**. Its norm calculation and
integrability proof retain that qualifier. Example 10.6 expressly gives
other nonsymmetric extensions. The special curve and number-field
uniqueness statements fix their scalar heights. None asserts arbitrary
transcendental-extension uniqueness.

The relative b-divisor quotient, Theorem 8.3/Corollary 8.4, factors out
the ideal generated by base-field lattice divisors. Lemma 8.5's full
ample-twist argument makes positive global functionals vanish on
numerically trivial divisors. Intersection constructions require explicit
base polarization and correct geometric/arithmetic powers. The density
and existential-closure inputs attributed to BH22, Sza23 and DF22 are

## Literal definitions, examples and normalization diagnostics

1. Definition 4.11's printed disjointness condition is incorrect. For any
   alpha, the pair alpha,-alpha satisfies alpha meet beta<=0<=alpha join
   beta; asserted norm additivity would force 0=2||alpha||. Use the standard
   condition |alpha| meet |beta|=0 for an L1 lattice. This repairs the
   intended definition, rather than refuting the standard dictionary.
2. Example 11.12 omits the origin from its meromorphic counting term while
   allowing every eta(r)->infinity. For r>1 its literal formulas give
   ht(z)=0 and ht(1/z)=log(r)/eta(r). With eta=sqrt(max(1,log r)), the
   stated set includes z but excludes its inverse, so it is not a field.
   With eta=max(1,log r), inverse heights tend to zero and one, incompatible
   with a global projective height. Include the origin order and leading
   coefficient, or require stronger growth making that term vanish.
   The printed universal-theory comparison inherits this example defect;
   no main dictionary or existential-closure theorem is refuted by it.
3. Corollary 12.8 sets Q[r] with r=ht_K(2), whereas Definition 10.2 fixed
   ht_Q[r](2)=r log2. Embeddings preserve the height of the constant 2.
   The intended parameter must be ht_K(2)/log2. K=Q[1] makes the literal
   mismatch log2 versus (log2)^2 explicit.
4. Equation (4) needs the closed-point residue degree when ht(x) denotes
   normalized absolute Weil point height. For z^d-2, Gauss contribution
   is zero and the negative circle integral is -log2; normalized closed
   point height is log2/d. Degree-weighting gives zero as required by
   the Weil-model intersection identified in Section 8. A cycle-height
   convention could absorb the degree, but that convention must be stated;
   this qualification prevents an unambiguous false-formula claim based
   merely on ambiguous height terminology.
5. Proposition 10.5's root bound is established only where v(a)<=0. Its
   global dominating function needs max(0,-min(nonzero coefficient
   valuations)+log[n]). Degree-one a=1/2 at the real place makes the
   untruncated right side negative while the left negative-part magnitude
   is zero. Zero coefficients and extended-value exceptions must be
   excluded before division. The positive-part repair retains integrability.
6. Theorem 11.10's unbounded-sequence dichotomy must be interpreted along
   the ultrafilter. Negative cutoff witnesses have uniformly bounded
   representatives after modifying a U-small set. Merely being globally
   unbounded does not imply the heights tend to infinity along U.
