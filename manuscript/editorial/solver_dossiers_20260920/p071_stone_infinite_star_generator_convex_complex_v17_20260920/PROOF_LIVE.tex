\begin{proof}
Fix $(z_0,x_0,p_0)\in X$ and choose a convex-development chart
$\varphi:U\xrightarrow{\sim}V\subset P$ at $p_0$.  By the two-sided
generator hypothesis, the product homeomorphism
\[
 \Psi=\operatorname{id}_Z\times\operatorname{id}_{X_B}\times\varphi:
 Z\times X_B\times U\longrightarrow Z\times X_B\times V
\]
identifies the complete product generator sheaves in both directions.

Every stage of the transfinite psh hierarchy commutes with restriction to
an open subset.  This follows by transfinite induction: it is immediate for
the generator stage; at a successor stage both the formation of a local
pointwise decreasing sequence and sheafification are local; and at a limit
stage the hierarchy is the sheaf-local union of the preceding stages.
After stabilization, the same conclusion holds for the full generated
sheaf.  Consequently $\Psi$ identifies both $\mathcal P_{X,1}$ and
$\mathcal P_X$ near the chosen point with the restrictions to
$Z\times X_B\times V$ of the corresponding sheaves on
$Z\times X_B\times P$.

Those two ambient sheaves agree by
\Cref{thm:p071-stone-infinite-star-polyhedral-collapse}.  Its
fixed-neighbourhood assertion, after a convex shrinking inside $V$, gives
one decreasing generator sequence on a fixed smaller product
neighbourhood.  Transporting this sequence through $\Psi^{-1}$ proves
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-infinite-star-generator-convex-collapse} and the
approximation assertion at $(z_0,x_0,p_0)$.  When $x_0$ is the node, the
same theorem supplies one countable active-branch set independent of the
compact Stone parameter and of the polyhedral variable.  The transported
set therefore has the asserted uniformity on the developed chart.

On each chart, the transported comparison sheaf equals the intrinsic full
generated sheaf just identified.  Hence two chartwise definitions agree on
their overlap, so they glue to a well-defined sheaf $\mathcal C_X$, and the
three displayed sheaves coincide.  The proof uses one chart at a time and
no finite subdivision of it.  It therefore requires neither local
finiteness nor a bound on the number of cells meeting the chosen
neighbourhood.
\end{proof}
