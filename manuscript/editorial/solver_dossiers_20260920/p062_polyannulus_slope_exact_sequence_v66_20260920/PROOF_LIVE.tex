\begin{proof}
Let $a\in H^0(W,\mathbb V_{\pi,\mathbf R}^{\vee})$.  On a triple overlap,
the affine cocycle and dual transition laws give
\begin{align*}
 r_{\alpha\gamma}(a)
 &=a_\gamma^{\mathsf T}c_{\alpha\gamma}\\
 &=a_\gamma^{\mathsf T}c_{\beta\gamma}
   +a_\gamma^{\mathsf T}M_{\beta\gamma}c_{\alpha\beta}\\
 &=r_{\beta\gamma}(a)+r_{\alpha\beta}(a),
\end{align*}
because
$a_\beta=M_{\beta\gamma}^{\mathsf T}a_\gamma$.  Thus
$(r_{\alpha\beta}(a))$ is a scalar Cech one-cocycle.

We check that its class is intrinsic.  On a common refinement, suppose a
second set of annular coordinates is related to the first by
\[
 \widehat L_\alpha
 =\pi^*e_\alpha+(N_\alpha\circ\pi)L_\alpha.
\]
The change-of-frame identities from the radiance construction are
\[
 \widehat M_{\alpha\beta}
 =N_\beta M_{\alpha\beta}N_\alpha^{-1},
 \qquad
 \widehat c_{\alpha\beta}
 =N_\beta c_{\alpha\beta}
  +e_\beta-\widehat M_{\alpha\beta}e_\alpha.
\]
The dual coordinates are
$\widehat a_\alpha=N_\alpha^{-\mathsf T}a_\alpha$.  Hence
\[
 \widehat a_\beta^{\mathsf T}\widehat c_{\alpha\beta}
 =a_\beta^{\mathsf T}c_{\alpha\beta}
  +\widehat a_\beta^{\mathsf T}e_\beta
  -\widehat a_\alpha^{\mathsf T}e_\alpha.
\]
The two scalar cocycles differ by a Cech coboundary.  Refinement does not
change their direct-limit class, so $\partial_{\rho_0}$ is well defined and
is the contraction of $a$ with the canonical class $\rho_0(\pi)$.
Linearity is immediate.

The coefficient uniqueness in \eqref{eq:p062-slope-exact-local-form} makes
$\sigma_\pi$ well defined and linear.  The map $\pi^*$ in
\eqref{eq:p062-polyannulus-slope-exact-sequence} is injective because the
locally trivial map $\pi$ is surjective.  A pullback has zero local slope.
Conversely, if $\sigma_\pi(h)=0$, then
\eqref{eq:p062-slope-exact-local-form} gives $h=\pi^*b_\alpha$, and the
coefficient transition reduces to $b_\alpha=b_\beta$.  The $b_\alpha$
therefore glue to a unique $b\in\mathscr P_W(W)$.  This proves exactness at
$\mathscr P_W(W)$ and $\mathscr Z(Y)$.

For $h\in\mathscr Z(Y)$ with slope $a=\sigma_\pi(h)$, the second identity
in \eqref{eq:p062-slope-exact-local-form} reads
\[
 r_{\alpha\beta}(a)=b_\alpha-b_\beta.
\]
Thus $\partial_{\rho_0}(a)=0$, and
$\operatorname{im}\sigma_\pi\subseteq\ker\partial_{\rho_0}$.

Conversely, suppose
$a\in\ker\partial_{\rho_0}$.  By the direct-limit definition of Cech
cohomology, after refining the atlas there are
$b_\alpha\in\mathscr P_W(U_\alpha)$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:p062-slope-obstruction-coboundary}
 b_\alpha-b_\beta=a_\beta^{\mathsf T}c_{\alpha\beta}.
\end{equation}
Define on $\pi^{-1}(U_\alpha)$
\[
 h_\alpha=\pi^*b_\alpha
 +(a_\alpha\circ\pi)^{\mathsf T}L_\alpha.
\]
On an overlap, the logarithmic transition law, the dual transition law,
and \eqref{eq:p062-slope-obstruction-coboundary} give
\begin{align*}
 h_\beta
 &=\pi^*b_\beta
   +(a_\beta\circ\pi)^{\mathsf T}
     \bigl(\pi^*c_{\alpha\beta}
            +(M_{\alpha\beta}\circ\pi)L_\alpha\bigr)\\
 &=\pi^*b_\alpha
   +(a_\alpha\circ\pi)^{\mathsf T}L_\alpha
 =h_\alpha.
\end{align*}
They therefore glue to a continuous function $h$ on $Y$.  The standing
local converse makes $h$ a zero-current function, and its unique slope is
$a$.  Hence
$\ker\partial_{\rho_0}\subseteq\operatorname{im}\sigma_\pi$, proving
exactness at the parallel-slope term.

If $\partial_{\rho_0}$ is injective, exactness makes
$\sigma_\pi$ identically zero, so the already proved exactness at
$\mathscr Z(Y)$ gives the asserted descent.  The vanishing of the space of
parallel dual sections is a sufficient special case.

Finally, if $\rho_0(\pi)=0$, then $\partial_{\rho_0}=0$, so exactness makes
$\sigma_\pi$ surjective.  Choose the vectors $d_\alpha$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:p062-slope-exact-radiance-gauge}, and let $\Lambda_\pi$ be the
global continuous logarithmic-radius section obtained from the
$\widetilde L_\alpha=L_\alpha+\pi^*d_\alpha$.  The standing local converse
shows that the map
\[
 H^0(W,\mathbb V_{\pi,\mathbf R}^{\vee})\longrightarrow\mathscr Z(Y),
 \qquad
 a\longmapsto\langle\pi^{-1}a,\Lambda_\pi\rangle,
\]
takes values in $\mathscr Z(Y)$; its local formula has slope $a$, so it is
a linear right inverse to $\sigma_\pi$.
Together with the kernel computation, it gives the displayed direct-sum
decomposition.  Its dependence on the affine gauge and the final scope
qualification are exactly those of the radiance construction.
\end{proof}
