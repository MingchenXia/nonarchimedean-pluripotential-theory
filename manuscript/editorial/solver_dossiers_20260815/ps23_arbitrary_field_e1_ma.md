**Packet:** `refs/ps23_arbitrary_field_e1_ma.md` (source: Pille-Schneider, arXiv:2209.04879v2, Remark 4.4). **Date run:** 2026-08-15. **Solver protocol:** stages 1–7, maximal rigor. **Standing setup.** \(K\) an arbitrary complete non-Archimedean (rank-one) valued field, value group \(\Gamma=\log|K^\times|\subset\mathbb R\) (possibly trivial, discrete, or dense; residue field \(\tilde K\) arbitrary, possibly imperfect); \(X/K\) a projective scheme of pure dimension \(n\); \(L,L_1,\dots,L_n\) ample (semiample suffices for most FS-level statements). \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is compact Hausdorff.

## 2. Formalization

**Target (T).** Construct a pairing \((\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n)\mapsto\mathrm{MA}(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n)\), a positive Radon measure on \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\), on the largest justified domain \(\mathcal D\subset\prod_i\mathcal E^1(X,L_i)\), satisfying: (MA1) agreement with PS23 Thm. 4.3 on \(\prod_i\mathrm{CPSH}(L_i)\) (hence with the model operator); (MA2) symmetry, multilinearity, positivity, total mass \((L_1\cdots L_n)\); (MA3) continuity along decreasing nets, truncation continuity, independence of approximants; (MA4) integration by parts and first variation of energy where finite; (MA5) uniqueness of the extension and comparison; (MA6) functorial scalar extension (and descent where meaningful).

**Definition of \(\mathcal E^1\) over arbitrary \(K\) (confirming the packet claim).** PS23 does not spell out the definition (Rem. 4.4 asserts it; line 1648 defers to [BFJ15, §6] in the hybrid section). The canonical definition, specializing to BJ22 Def. 7.6 when \(K\) is trivially valued and to BFJ's when \(K\) is discretely valued of residue char 0, is the *monotone* one. Fix \(\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathrm{FS}^\tau(L)\). For \(\phi,\psi\in\mathrm{FS}^\tau(L)\) set
\[\mathrm E(\phi;\psi):=\frac1{(n+1)V}\sum_{j=0}^n\int(\phi-\psi)\,\mathrm{MA}(\phi^{(j)},\psi^{(n-j)}),\qquad V=(L^n),\]
(well defined by PS23 Prop. 4.2). For \(\phi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,L)\), write \(\phi=\lim_i\phi_i\), \(\phi_i\in\mathrm{FS}^\tau(L)\) decreasing (PS23 Def. 2.10), and set \(\mathrm E(\phi):=\lim_i\mathrm E(\phi_i;\phi_{\mathrm{ref}})\in\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}\); then
\[\mathcal E^1(X,L):=\{\phi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,L):\ \mathrm E(\phi)>-\infty\}.\]
Net-independence is proved in §4 (step B1). \(\mathcal E^1\supset\mathrm{CPSH}\supset\mathrm{FS}^\tau\).

## 4. The construction, line by line

Throughout, "pairing" means the *relative* energy pairing on FS/DFS data over arbitrary \(K\), defined by telescoping from (I1):
\[\Delta\big((\phi_i)_{i=0}^n;(\phi_i')_{i=0}^n\big):=\sum_{j=0}^n\int(\phi_j-\phi_j')\,\mathrm{MA}(\phi_0,\dots,\phi_{j-1},\phi_{j+1}',\dots,\phi_n'),\quad \phi_i,\phi_i'\in\mathrm{DFS}(L_i).\]

**B0 (formal layer; unconditional).**
1. *Well-definedness/symmetry.* Independence of the telescoping order reduces to the adjacent-swap identity \(\int(\phi_0-\phi_0')\,\mathrm{MA}((\mathcal O,\phi_1-\phi_1'),\Phi)=\int(\phi_1-\phi_1')\,\mathrm{MA}((\mathcal O,\phi_0-\phi_0'),\Phi)\), which is exactly the \(\mathcal O_X\)-slot IBP of Prop. 4.2 (bullet 4), multilinearly expanded. The cocycle identity \(\Delta(\phi;\phi'')=\Delta(\phi;\phi')+\Delta(\phi';\phi'')\) follows by re-telescoping. Hence a symmetric relative pairing, i.e. the analogue of BJ22 §3.2 over any \(K\).
2. *Slotwise monotonicity.* If \(\psi\ge\phi_0\) in \(\mathrm{FS}^\tau(L_0)\), then by the cocycle property the increment equals the single term \(\int(\psi-\phi_0)\,\mathrm{MA}(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n)\ge0\) (positivity bullet). This is the analogue of BJ22 Lemma 3.15/7.15.
3. *Constants and mass.* \(\Delta\) shifts by \(c\,(L_1\cdots L_n)\) under \(\phi_0\mapsto\phi_0+c\) (mass bullet): the analogue of (7.2).

**B1 (energy and \(\mathcal E^1\); unconditional).** For a decreasing net \(\phi_i\downarrow\phi\) in \(\mathrm{FS}^\tau(L)\) and any FS majorant \(\tau\ge\phi\): the sets \(\{\phi_i\ge\tau+\varepsilon\}\) are compact, decreasing, with empty intersection, hence eventually empty (**Dini for nets** on the compact \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\); same device as GJKM19's use of "Dini's Theorem for nets [37, p. 239]", line 496). With B0.2–3 this gives \(\lim_i\mathrm E(\phi_i;\phi_{\mathrm{ref}})=\inf\{\mathrm E(\tau;\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}):\tau\in\mathrm{FS}^\tau(L),\ \tau\ge\phi\}\): the limit is independent of the net. So \(\mathrm E\) and \(\mathcal E^1(X,L)\) are well defined over any \(K\), \(\mathrm E\) is increasing, continuous along decreasing nets, \(\mathrm E(\phi+c)=\mathrm E(\phi)+c\); this *is* BJ22 Thm. 7.1/Def. 7.6 in the trivially valued case and recovers the BFJ energy in the discretely valued residue-char-0 case (both endpoints' FS classes coincide with the model classes there — PS23 Ex. 2.12, Ex. 4.1). **This confirms and makes precise the packet's assertion that \(\mathcal E^1\) exists over any \(K\).**

**B2 (the pure operator on \(\mathcal E^1\); unconditional). CANDIDATE-PROPOSITION A.**
*Statement.* Let \(K\) be any complete non-Archimedean field, \(X/K\) projective of pure dimension \(n\), \(L\) ample. There is a unique operator \(\mathrm{MA}:\mathcal E^1(X,L)\to\mathcal M(X^{\mathrm{an}})\) (positive Radon measures of mass \(V=(L^n)\)) which agrees with the BE operator (PS23 Thm. 4.3) on \(\mathrm{CPSH}\) and is continuous along decreasing nets (weak topology of measures). It satisfies (MA2) for equal slots, (MA3), and: \(\mathrm E\) is differentiable along \(t\mapsto(1-t)\phi+t\psi\) in the sense of the two-sided bounds (7.23)-analogue \(\int(\phi-\psi)\mathrm{MA}(\phi)\le \mathrm E(\phi)-\mathrm E(\psi)\le\int(\phi-\psi)\mathrm{MA}(\psi)\); moreover the mixed pairs \(\mathrm{MA}(\phi^{(j)},\tau^{(n-j)})\), \(\tau\in\mathrm{FS}^\tau(L)\), all exist on \(\mathcal E^1\).
*Proof.* (i) *Finiteness chain.* WLOG \(\phi\le\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}=:\tau_0\) (B0.3). For \(\phi_i\downarrow\phi\) FS, slotwise monotonicity makes \(j\mapsto\langle(L,\phi_i)^{(j)}(L,\tau_0)^{(n+1-j)}\rangle\) (relative to all-\(\tau_0\)) decreasing in \(j\), with minimum the pure term \(=(n+1)V\,\mathrm E(\phi_i;\tau_0)\ge(n+1)V\,\mathrm E(\phi)>-\infty\). So all reference-mixed pairings converge to finite limits, decreasing in \(i\).
(ii) *Test functionals.* Every \(\psi\in\mathrm{DFS}(X)\) is \(\sigma_1-\sigma_2\) with \(\sigma_l\in\mathrm{FS}^\tau(M)\) on a **common** bundle \(M\) (PS23 Def. 2.15); replacing \(\sigma_l\) by \(\sigma_l+\rho\), \(\rho\in\mathrm{FS}^\tau(mL-M)\) (\(m\gg0\), \(L\) ample; Prop. 2.14(3)), we may take \(M=mL\). For \(\sigma\in\mathrm{FS}^\tau(mL)\), the functional \(F_\sigma(\phi):=\langle(mL,\sigma)(L,\phi)^{(n)}\rangle\) (relative to \((\sigma,\tau_0^{(n)})\)) is increasing in \(\phi\) (B0.2 applied in the \(L\)-slots), and by \(\mathbb Q\)-homogeneity and the same-bundle comparison \(\tau_0-c\le m^{-1}\sigma\le\tau_0+c\), \(c=\sup|m^{-1}\sigma-\tau_0|\), it is squeezed between reference-mixed pairings \(\pm cV\) — finite on \(\mathcal E^1\) by (i). Hence \(F_\sigma(\phi):=\lim_iF_\sigma(\phi_i)\) exists, finite, net-independent (Dini as in B1).
(iii) *Riesz.* \(\psi\mapsto F_{\sigma_1}(\phi)-F_{\sigma_2}(\phi)\) is well defined (identities linear in the \(\sigma\)-slot hold at each \(i\) and pass to the limit), positive for \(\psi\ge0\) (monotonicity of \(F_\sigma\) in \(\sigma\), B0.2 in the \(mL\)-slot), bounded by \(V\sup|\psi|\); by density (I2) and the Riesz representation theorem on the compact Hausdorff \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) (as in BJ22 §7.3, lines 3952–3996) it defines \(\mathrm{MA}(\phi)\in\mathcal M\), of mass \(V\) (constants). Continuity along decreasing nets and uniqueness are immediate from the construction and density; agreement on \(\mathrm{CPSH}\) follows since a decreasing net of FS metrics with continuous limit converges uniformly (Dini) and PS23 Thm. 4.3 is continuous for uniform convergence. Truncations \(\max(\phi,\tau_0-t)\in\mathcal E^1\cap L^\infty\) decrease to \(\phi\) (max-stability: Prop. 2.14(2)/2.20), giving truncation continuity, with \(\mathrm E(\max(\phi,\tau_0-t))\downarrow\mathrm E(\phi)\). \(\square\)
*Remark.* The proof of (7.23) is the formal telescoping (BJ22 Prop. 7.27 (7.22)–(7.23) uses only symmetry+monotonicity). The measure-valued statement "the pairing extends to \(\mathrm{PSH}\times\mathrm{CPSH}^n\)" (BJ22 Thm. 7.4-analogue) also transplants using PS23 Lem. 4.10 in place of (7.6). *What does NOT come free:* mixed tuples with **distinct** \(\mathcal E^1\)-entries; see B3.

**B3 (tiers for the full mixed theory).**
- **Tier 1.5 (concavity (C)) — CANDIDATE-LEMMA C.** *Claim:* over any \(K\), \(t\mapsto \mathrm E((1-t)\phi_0+t\phi_1;\tau_0)\) is concave on \([0,1]\cap\mathbb Q\) for \(\phi_0,\phi_1\in\mathrm{FS}^\tau(L)\). *Proposed proof:* by [BE20, Thm. A] (as invoked in BJ22 lines 4880–4897, and stated there to hold over arbitrary NA fields, line 353), \(\mathrm E(\rho;\cdot)=\mathrm{vol}_L(\rho)=-\lim m^{-1}h^0(mL)^{-1}\log\det\|\cdot\|_{m\rho}\) on \(\mathrm{CPSH}\). Sup-norms interpolate: \(\|s\|_{m\rho_t}\le\|s\|_{m\rho_0}^{1-t}\|s\|_{m\rho_1}^{t}\) (Hölder, pointwise). **B4 (curves; unconditional). CANDIDATE-PROPOSITION B.**
*Statement.* Let \(K\) be any complete non-Archimedean nontrivially valued field, \(X/K\) a smooth projective curve, \(L\) ample of degree \(V=\deg L\). Then, with \(\mathcal E^1(X,L)\) as in B1:
1. The operator \(\mathrm{MA}:\mathcal E^1(X,L)\to\mathcal M(X^{\mathrm{an}})\) of Candidate-Proposition A satisfies the *full* package: symmetric "mixed" theory (trivial for \(n=1\); the pairing \((\phi_0,\phi_1)\mapsto\langle(L_0,\phi_0)(L_1,\phi_1)\rangle\) is finite and symmetric on \(\mathcal E^1(L_0)\times\mathcal E^1(L_1)\)), quasi-metric \(\mathrm I\), strong topology, bi-Lipschitz embedding \((\mathcal E^1/\mathbb R,\mathrm I)\hookrightarrow(\mathcal M^1,\mathrm I^\vee)\), **uniqueness up to constants**, mass \(\deg L\), agreement, monotone/truncation continuity, IBP and first variation.
2. The envelope property holds (GJKM19 Thm. 1.1/3.1) — hence, by the Thm. 12.8-analogue: \((\mathcal E^1/\mathbb R,\mathrm I)\) is complete and \(\mathrm{MA}:\mathcal E^1\to\mathcal M^1\) is onto: **the curve Calabi–Yau theorem in finite energy over an arbitrary \(K\)**, \(\mathrm{MA}(\phi)=\mu\) solvable, uniquely mod \(\mathbb R\), for every \(\mu\ge0\) of mass \(\deg L\) with \(\mathrm E^\vee(\mu)<\infty\).
3. For \(\mu\) supported on the skeleton of a strictly semistable model, the solution is continuous (LITERATURE: Thuillier [50, Cor. 3.4.13] + GJKM19 Prop. 1.2, lines 240–248).
*Proof of the missing input (N) on curves.* By multilinearity and density it suffices to prove \(\int f\,dd^cf\le0\) for \(f\) a \(\mathbb Z\)-model function. As in GJKM19 (proof of Prop. 3.7) reduce by base change and the projection formula to \(K=\mathbb C_K\) algebraically closed, and choose a strictly semistable model \(\mathscr X\) with skeleton \(\Delta\) on which \(f\) is determined; then \(f=F\circ\tau\) with \(F\) piecewise affine (Prop. 3.5), and by the slope description (Prop. 3.7 with \(\theta=0\)) \(dd^cf=\sum_{x}\big(\sum_{\nu\in T_x(\Delta)}w_x(\nu)\lambda_{x,\nu}(F)\big)\delta_x\). Summation by parts edge by edge (each edge \(e=[x,y]\) of length \(\ell_e\), weight \(w_e\), slope \(\lambda_e\) contributes \(-w_e\lambda_e^2\ell_e\)) gives
\[\int f\,dd^cf=-\sum_{e\subset\Delta}w_e\,\lambda_e^2\,\ell_e\ \le\ 0,\]
with equality iff \(F\) is constant. Extension to tropical FS with real constants: absorb \(\Gamma_{\mathbb Q}\)-constants into sections (value group dense), approximate real constants uniformly with the *same* sections; the quadratic form is continuous under such approximation because \(\big|\int f\,dd^cf-\int g\,dd^cg\big|=\big|\int(f-g)\,dd^c f+\int(f-g)\,dd^cg\big|\le\sup|f-g|\cdot(\text{total variations})\) (symmetry (I1)), and the total variations \(2\deg M\) are fixed along the approximation. This proves (N)\(_{n=1}\); tiers 1.5 and 2 then run as in B3 (concavity is a consequence of (N)). The trivially valued curve case is BJ22 (Thm. 5.20 with \(\dim\le2\), any characteristic; Thm. 12.9). The envelope property input for item 2 is GJKM19 Thm. 1.1 (any \(K\), smooth curve); the surjectivity argument is the Thm. 12.8-analogue (i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii), whose ingredients — weak compactness of \(\mathrm{PSH}_{\sup}\) (Thm. 5.11-analogue, equivalent to the envelope property; its proof (BJ22 lines 2905–2932 and Thm. 5.11) is formal given envelope continuity and Dini), differentiability (Cor. 8.6-analogue: [BGM20] holds over arbitrary \(K\)), maximizing nets (Thm. 9.11-analogue) — are available on curves. *Flag:* the transplant of the compactness equivalence (Thm. 5.11) uses the topology of pointwise convergence on a determining dense set of points; on curves, psh functions are determined on skeletal points by the retraction description (GJKM19 Props. 3.5–3.8), which supplies the needed substitute for BJ22 Cor. 4.23. This is the one step of item 2 written out in the book rather than quoted. \(\square\)
*Regularity caveats (curves):* Cor. 3.11's hypothesis \(\Gamma\subset\mathbb Q\) shows that even on curves the envelope of a *model* metric need not be a model metric for dense irrational \(\Gamma\) — the operator theory is insensitive to this, but PL-regularity statements are not. Regular non-smooth curves (imperfect \(K\)) are **not** covered by GJKM19 Thm. 1.1; item 1 still applies (it needs no smoothness), item 2 is open there.

**(MA6) Scalar extension/descent.** For an isometric embedding \(K\hookrightarrow K'\) with \(\pi:X_{K'}^{\mathrm{an}}\to X^{\mathrm{an}}\): FS/DFS pull back (Prop. 2.14(7)); decreasing nets pull back to decreasing nets; if (I6) holds at the DFS level, then \(\mathrm E(\pi^*\phi)=\mathrm E(\phi)\), \(\pi^*\mathcal E^1(X,L)\subset\mathcal E^1(X_{K'},L_{K'})\) and \(\pi_*\mathrm{MA}(\pi^*\phi)=\mathrm{MA}(\phi)\) by monotone limits. ## 5. Self-adversarial audit

1. **Nets vs sequences.** \(\Gamma\) dense/uncountable \(\Rightarrow\) \(\mathrm{FS}^\tau(L)\) has no countable cofinal subfamily below a given \(\phi\); all of B1–B2 is net-based; Dini-for-nets on compact \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is the only compactness used. Sequences reappear legitimately only after a quasi-metric exists (countable uniformity base). Checked: no hidden sequential argument in B1–B2.
2. **Trivially valued degeneration.** Over trivially valued \(K\), \(\mathrm{FS}^\tau=\mathrm H\)-metrics, the construction reduces to BJ22 Thm. 7.1/Def. 7.24 with \(\mathrm{MA}(\phi_{\mathrm{triv}}\text{-tuple})=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\delta_{v_{\mathrm{triv},\alpha}}\) (BJ22 Ex. 7.26): masses match.
3. **Total mass at endpoints.** \(\int\mathrm{MA}(\phi)=V\) survives all monotone limits (constants test function); mixed masses \((L_1\cdots L_n)\) at tier 1.5 (Prop. 7.19-analogue).
4. **\(-\infty\) loci.** \(\mathcal E^1\) metrics can take \(-\infty\) (already on curves: a potential with \(\mathrm{MA}\)-masses \(m_k\) at depth \(d_k\to\infty\) with \(\sum m_k^2d_k<\infty\), \(\sum m_kd_k=\infty\) is finite-energy and \(-\infty\) at a type-1 end). Whether \(\mathrm{MA}(\phi)\) charges pluripolar sets for \(\phi\in\mathcal E^1\) over general \(K\) is open (tier-2+; BJ22 needs the envelope property for the negligibility theory; CLD25 Cor. 16.9.3/16.9.4 covers only continuous approachable entries). Also PS23 lines 994–996: Zariski-dense pluripolar points exist (trivially valued \(\mathbb P^2\)) — no conflict, but forbids naive "generic point" arguments.
5. **Mixed characteristic, e.g. \(K=\mathbb C_p\).** B1–B2 and tiers depend on no residue-characteristic input; (N) via Route 2 is residue-char-free; the *envelope property* is the sole residue-char-sensitive frontier for \(n\ge2\) (multiplier ideals: residue char 0; test ideals: equichar \(p\) + (A1)–(A3); mixed characteristic: no known route — not even a conjectural ideal-theoretic mechanism in the greppable record).
6. **Imperfect residue field.** Enters only through (A1) (perfect \(k\)) in the GJKM route and through smoothness vs regularity of curves (B4 caveat). The operator tiers are insensitive.
7. **Nondiscreteness.** Kills model-function monotone regularization as a tool (no analogue of GJKM Cor. 9.1 known), makes \(K^\circ\) non-noetherian (models exist — GJKM19 §2.1 works over any \(K^\circ\); algebraization on curves via non-noetherian Grothendieck existence, GJKM19 Rem. 3.3 — but Hodge-index-type inequalities on models are unverified), and injects rationality phenomena (GJKM Cor. 3.11). It does **not** affect B1–B2.
8. **Singular \(X\).** BJ22 Ex. 12.11 (nodal curve): surjectivity fails; envelope property forces unibranch (Thm. 5.12). Any general-\(K\) claim of the CY/isomorphism package must carry a unibranch (or smoothness) hypothesis; the operator itself (A, R) does not.
9. **Negative regularity constraints (book ledger).** [BJGreen24, Thms. A,B] (ch71 lines 1698–1708) + GJKM19 App. (Burgos–Sombra): no retraction/PL shortcut in \(\dim\ge2\); consistent with our architecture, which never factors through skeleta. On curves the retraction *is* legitimate (GJKM Prop. 1.2) — this is exactly why B4 closes.
10. **Could (N) fail over some \(K\)?** No candidate mechanism: it holds on curves (B4), under discreteness, and trivially valued; the CLD route sketches a field-free proof. We therefore do **not** register a counterexample-candidate on this axis; the honest risk is (V1)/(V2)-type bookkeeping, not sign failure.
11. **What PS23's "unclear" most plausibly refers to** (reconstructed, not sourced): not the monotone extension (B2, which is soft), but the *canonicity* of the resulting theory — strong topology, uniqueness, solvability, independence from the FS-approximation scheme vs local (CLD) definitions — i.e. tiers 1.5–2 plus the envelope property. The dossier keeps these strictly separated.

## 6. Boundary examples

**(a) \(\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C_p}\), Dirac mass at a type-4 point.** \(X^{\mathrm{an}}\) is an \(\mathbb R\)-tree; \(\Gamma=v(\mathbb C_p^\times)=\mathbb Q\) dense; \(\tilde K=\overline{\mathbb F}_p\); \(\mathbb C_p\) is not spherically complete, so there are decreasing families of discs \(D(a_i,r_i)\) with empty intersection and \(\inf r_i=r>0\): the corresponding type-4 point \(x\) lies at *finite* hyperbolic distance \(\log(r_{\mathrm{Gauss}}/r)\) from the Gauss point. Let \(L=\mathcal O(1)\), \(\tau_0=\max(\log|z_0|,\log|z_1|)\) the Weil metric, \(\mathrm{MA}(\tau_0)=\delta_{x_G}\). The function \(g(y)=-d\big(x_G,\,y\wedge x\big)\) (distance from the Gauss point to the meet of \(y\) and \(x\), i.e. the negative Gromov product) is continuous and bounded on the tree since \(d(x_G,x)<\infty\), and \(\phi:=\tau_0+g\) satisfies \(\mathrm{MA}(\phi)=\delta_x\) with \(\phi\in\mathcal E^1\cap C^0\), \(\mathrm E(\phi)=-\tfrac12 d(x_G,x)\). This is a solution over a densely valued, mixed-characteristic field lying in *neither* established endpoint; it is produced by Candidate-Proposition B (and directly by hand). It is not a model/PL metric (its kink locus accumulates at \(x\)); consistent with the ledger's negative-regularity entries. By contrast \(\delta_y\) for \(y\) of type 1 has \(\mathrm E^\vee(\delta_y)=\infty\) (infinite distance): no \(\mathcal E^1\) solution — the mass-vs-support frontier inside \(\mathcal M^1\) is the hyperbolic metric, exactly as in BJ22 Thm. 12.12's boundedness condition.
**(b) Tate curve over a field with dense value group.** Let \(K=\mathbb C((t^\Gamma))\) be a Hahn field with \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z+\theta\mathbb Z\) dense (\(\theta\) irrational; \(K\) is spherically complete, residue char 0, valuation of rank 1 dense), \(E=E_q\) with \(v(q)=\theta\): \(E^{\mathrm{an}}\) retracts onto a circle skeleton \(S\) of irrational length \(\theta\); no model has all skeletal data rational (GJKM Cor. 3.11's hypothesis fails). For \(L\) of degree 1 and \(\mu=\) normalized length measure on \(S\): the solution of \(\mathrm{MA}(\phi)=\mu\) is the metric whose potential on \(S\) is the (quadratic-along-edges) Green potential of \(\mu-\delta_{x_0}\), continuous, in \(\mathcal E^1\); solvability is B4 item 3 (skeletal support) — over a field where BFJ (discreteness) does not apply even though the residue characteristic is 0: it isolates *nondiscreteness* as the only failing classical hypothesis, and shows it is harmless on curves. An elliptic curve with good reduction over \(\mathbb C_p\) gives the atomic case \(\mathrm{MA}(\phi_{\mathrm{can}})=\delta_{x_0}\) (single Shilov point), again inside B4.
