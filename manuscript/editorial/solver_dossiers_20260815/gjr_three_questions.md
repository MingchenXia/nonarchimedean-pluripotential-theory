## Q1. Semipositive analogue of the local integral decomposition of harmonic \(\Lambda\)-PL functions

### 1.1 Status audit
[GJR, Prop. 7.5] (= intro Prop. 1.6(2)): \(\Lambda\)-harmonic functions are locally finite \(\Lambda\)-linear combinations of \(\mathbf Z\)-harmonic functions. The question posed immediately before it (exact wording UNCONFIRMED; scope per packet): does the analogue hold with "harmonic/numerically trivial" replaced by "semipositive/nef", with the coefficient conditions this forces? [GR25] develops the semipositive theory (curve-testing, limits, FS-approximation, maximum principle) but — as far as retrievable — does **not** prove or refute a finite positive-cone integral decomposition; uniform FS-approximation (their Thm 1.5) is *not* an exact finite decomposition.

### 1.2 Formalization
For \(x\in X\) (good strictly \(K\)-analytic), let \(\mathrm{SP}_\Lambda(x)\) = germs at \(x\) of \(\Lambda\)-PL functions semipositive on a neighborhood.

**(SP\(_\Lambda\)) statement.** *Every \(h\in\mathrm{SP}_\Lambda(x)\) admits a neighborhood \(W\ni x\), finitely many semipositive \(\mathbf Z\)-PL functions \(h_1,\dots,h_m\) on \(W\), and \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m\in\Lambda_{\ge 0}\), with \(h|_W=\sum_i\lambda_i h_i\).*

Sign discipline (packet warning): with unrestricted signs \(\lambda_i\in\Lambda\) the statement degenerates — every \(\Lambda\)-PL function is a \(\Lambda\)-combination of \(\mathbf Z\)-PL ones ([GJR, §5], sheaf-of-\(\Lambda\)-modules structure), and semipositivity of the *pieces* is then unrelated to that of \(h\). The harmonic case tolerates all signs only because \(-h\) harmonic \(\Leftrightarrow\) \(h\) harmonic; the semipositive cone is not symmetric, so \(\lambda_i\in\Lambda_{\ge0}\) is the only formulation whose truth would be "the semipositive analogue". For \(\Lambda=\mathbf Z\), (SP\(_{\mathbf Z}\)) is **trivially true** (\(h=1\cdot h\); \(h\) is itself integral semipositive). All content is in divisible \(\Lambda\supsetneq\mathbf Z\)... with one caveat: a strengthened variant (SP\('\)) demanding the \(h_i\) be *elementary* (harmonic or single-max Fubini–Study pieces) is nontrivial even for \(\Lambda=\mathbf Z\); the packet's formulation is (SP\(_\Lambda\)), which we adopt, and we prove the curve case in the strengthened elementary form anyway.

### 1.3 One-dimensional case: complete proof (CANDIDATE-PROPOSITION)

**Proposition A.** *Let \(X\) be a smooth strictly \(K\)-analytic curve, \(K\) algebraically closed with nontrivial valuation, and \(\Lambda=\mathbf Z\) or divisible with \(1\in\Lambda\). Then (SP\(_\Lambda\)) holds; moreover the pieces \(h_i\) can be taken \(\mathbf Z\)-harmonic except for at most one, which can be taken with nef residue divisor of degree \(\le\deg\) of the residue of \(h\).*

*Proof.* The statement is local; PL functions have locally finite kink loci, so it suffices to decompose the germ at each \(x\) on a small star-shaped neighborhood on which \(h\) is linear along each branch at \(x\).

(i) \(x\) of type 1 or 4: PL germs are constant (tubes/ends carry no kinks: a \(\mathbf Z\)-PL germ is \(-\log\|1\|_{\mathfrak L}\) and every formal model is trivial near such \(x\) after shrinking); decomposition trivial.

(ii) \(x\) of type 3: \(\mathbf Z\)-PL germs are linear across \(x\) (Shilov points of strictly affinoid domains have type 2 when \(|K^\times|\) is dense; kinks of model functions occur at type-2 points), hence so are \(\Lambda\)-combinations. A linear germ of slope \(s\in\Lambda\) equals \(s_+\cdot(\log|f|)+s_-\cdot(-\log|f|)\) with \(s_\pm\in\Lambda_{\ge0}\), \(f\) a unit realizing slope \(1\) (coordinate on the annulus). Both pieces are \(\mathbf Z\)-harmonic, hence semipositive. ∎(ii)

(iii) \(x\) of type 2 with residue curve \(C_x\) (smooth proper over \(\tilde K\)); branches at \(x\) \(\leftrightarrow\) closed points of \(C_x\). The germ of \(h\) is its slope family \((\lambda_v)_{v\in C_x(\tilde K)}\), \(\lambda_v\in\Lambda\), almost all \(0\); semipositivity at \(x\) is \(S:=\sum_v\lambda_v\ge 0\), and near \(x\) it is automatic (linear on branches). [Semipositive \(\Leftrightarrow\) Thuillier-subharmonic for PL germs on curves: the residue bundle of the germ is \(\mathcal O_{C_x}(\sum_v\lambda_v[v])\), nef iff \(\deg\ge0\); anchor: [GJR, §6, §15], [GR25, §5]; on curves this is classical.]

*Realization lemma.* For any \(v\ne w\in C_x(\tilde K)\): (a) the germ \(g_{v,w}\) with slopes \(+1\) at \(v\), \(-1\) at \(w\), else \(0\), is \(\mathbf Z\)-harmonic; (b) the germ \(p_w\) with slope \(+1\) at \(w\), else \(0\), is semipositive \(\mathbf Z\)-PL. Construction: choose a strictly semistable formal model \(\mathfrak Y\) of a compact strictly analytic neighborhood \(Y\) of \(x\) in which \(C_x\) appears as the component of \(x\), with \(v,w\) away from the nodes. Let \(\mathfrak L\) be the line bundle on \(\mathfrak Y\) obtained by gluing \(\mathcal O_{C_x}(v-w)\) (resp. \(\mathcal O_{C_x}(w)\)) on the \(C_x\)-component with the trivial bundle on all other components via trivializations near the nodes (possible: the divisors avoid the nodes and the special fiber is an sncd curve). Then \(\mathfrak L_s\) is numerically trivial (resp. nef: degrees \(1,0,\dots,0\ge0\)) and the germ of \(-\log\|1\|_{\mathfrak L}\) at \(x\) has the stated slopes by the slope formula for model functions (slope along the branch at \(v\) = local degree of \(\mathfrak L_s\) at \(v\); standard, cf. [GJR, §6]). ∎(lemma)

Now fix any \(w\) with some \(\lambda_v\neq0\) (if all vanish, \(h\) is constant). Then
\[ h \;=\; \sum_{v\neq w,\ \lambda_v>0}\lambda_v\,g_{v,w}\;+\;\sum_{v\neq w,\ \lambda_v<0}(-\lambda_v)\,g_{w,v}\;+\;S\cdot p_w \]
as germs at \(x\): the slope at \(v\ne w\) is \(\lambda_v\); at \(w\) it is \(-\sum_{v\ne w}\lambda_v+S=\lambda_w\). All coefficients lie in \(\Lambda_{\ge0}\) (note: **no divisibility is used** — the identity is integral-affine in the \(\lambda_v\), so it works verbatim for \(\Lambda=\mathbf Z\)); all pieces are \(\mathbf Z\)-harmonic except \(p_w\), which is semipositive. Gluing is unnecessary (local statement). ∎

**Tripod check (packet's candidate counterexample).** Slopes \((2,-1,-1)\) at a type-2 point, branches \(v_1,v_2,v_3\): here \(S=0\) and the decomposition above with \(w=v_1\) gives
\[(2,-1,-1)=1\cdot(1,-1,0)+1\cdot(1,0,-1),\]
both pieces \(\mathbf Z\)-harmonic (balanced), hence semipositive, with coefficients in \(\mathbf Z_{\ge0}\). **Not a counterexample.** Convex-geometry reason (exhaustive on the finite-dimensional slope cone): the germ cone \(\{\lambda\in\mathbf R^{(\mathrm{Br})}:\sum_v\lambda_v\ge0\}\) is a *rational* polyhedral half-space; its integral points \(e_v-e_w\) and \(e_v\) generate it over \(\mathbf Z_{\ge0}\) in each finitely-supported stratum, and all of them are realizable by the lemma. No enumeration can produce a 1-dimensional counterexample: a rational polyhedral cone is the \(\Lambda_{\ge0}\)-span of its integral points (Carathéodory over \(\mathbf Q\) + divisibility; for \(\Lambda=\mathbf Z\) trivial). The genus of \(C_x\) is irrelevant because nefness on a *curve* is the rational linear condition \(\deg\ge0\) — this is the structural reason dimension 1 is safe and the exact point where higher dimension is at risk.

Base-field audit: non-algebraically closed \(K\): branches correspond to closed points of \(C_x\) of degree \(d_v\ge1\); the germ cone becomes \(\{\lambda:\sum_v d_v\lambda_v\ge0\}\), still rational polyhedral, with realizable integral generators \(d_w e_v-d_v e_w,\ e_v\); the proof adapts. Trivially valued \(K\) is excluded (the [GJR]/[GR25] PL formalism assumes nontrivial valuation). Discretely valued \(K\): type-3 step (ii) changes (kinks of model functions at type-3 possible when \(|K^\times|\) discrete); there, \(\mathbf Z\)-PL germs at type 3 form the rank-2 cone \(\{(s_+,s_-):s_++s_-\ge0\}\) (two branches, convexity), with integral generators \((1,-1),(-1,1),(1,0),(0,1)\) realizable by annulus model functions; same conclusion.

### 1.4 Higher dimension: attack map, first unsupported implication, failed counterexample ledger

*Reduction (following [GR25, §5]/[GJR, §6]):* \(h\) semipositive at \(x\) \(\Leftrightarrow\) residue datum on Temkin's reduction \(\mathrm{red}(X,x)\) nef. (SP\(_\Lambda\)) at \(x\) reduces to: the cone of semipositive germs, inside the finite-dimensional space of \(\Lambda\)-PL germs subordinate to a fixed kink structure, is generated over \(\Lambda_{\ge0}\) by its integral points. By the Carathéodory lemma above this follows from:

**(∗) Missing input (first unsupported implication in my attempted general proof).** *For each point \(x\) and each finite collection \(\underline g=(g_1,\dots,g_m)\) of functions tropicalizing a neighborhood of \(x\) (in the harmonic-tropicalization sense of [GJR, §8]), the cone of germs \(\Phi(\,\cdot\,)\circ\mathrm{trop}_{\underline g}\), \(\Phi\) PL, that are semipositive at \(x\) is cut out by finitely many linear inequalities with rational (integral wall-crossing/degree) coefficients in the slope parameters of \(\Phi\).*

I could not prove (∗) in general without [GJR, §6] in hand; it is exactly the kind of statement the germ-residue theory there is built for. Everything below is evidence *for* (∗), obtained by attempting to refute it.

*Attempted counterexample 1 (round nef cones; the packet's feared mechanism, one dimension up).* If germ-semipositivity at some \(x\) were equivalent to nefness of a freely varying class on a fixed residue surface \(Z\) with non-rational-polyhedral nef cone — e.g. \(Z\) an abelian surface with real multiplication by \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt d]\), \(\rho=2\), Néron–Severi form \(x^2-dy^2\) anisotropic over \(\mathbf Q\), or a K3 with two \((-2)\)-curves \(D_1\cdot D_2=3\) (irrational isotropic rays with coefficients in \(\mathbf Q+\mathbf Q\sqrt5\)) — then for divisible \(\Lambda\) containing the relevant irrationals, a germ with residue on an irrational extremal nef ray \(\xi\) could not decompose: if \(\xi=\sum\lambda_i\theta_i\) with \(\theta_i\) nef integral, \(\lambda_i>0\), extremality forces \(\theta_i\in\mathbf R_{\ge0}\xi\cap N^1(Z)_{\mathbf Z}=\{0\}\), contradiction. **This mechanism is real as convex geometry but I could not realize it by a germ.** Two independent collapse phenomena block it:

(a) *Shrinking past kinks.* At a divisorial point \(\beta\) (reduction = generic point of a component \(Z\)), the germ of the model function of a blowup-exceptional vertical divisor (e.g. \(\mathfrak L=\mathcal O(-E)\), \(E\) exceptional over a curve \(C\subset Z\); function \(x\mapsto\min(v_x(g_C),1)\) in local charts) becomes **pluriharmonic** on the open neighborhood \(\Omega=\{v(g_C)<1\}\ni\beta\) (chart-local Poincaré–Lelong: the local equations are invertible on \(\Omega\)). So model-function germs at \(\beta\) do not carry nef-cone classes at all; the germ residue of such \(h\) is numerically trivial. Kinks *through* \(\beta\) arise only from data whose reduction is a nonconstant rational function on \(Z\) (walls \(\{v(g)=0\}\) with \(\overline g\in \tilde K(Z)^\times\) nonconstant), i.e. from a **fixed finite integral tropical structure** on \(\mathrm{red}(X,\beta)\); the semipositivity constraints on a PL germ subordinate to it are degree/wall-crossing inequalities with coefficients from that fixed integral matrix — rational. This is (∗) at divisorial points over good reduction, verified at the level of the mechanism (not written as a formal proof).

(b) *Sign rigidity.* For a germ with wall structure \(\sum_i c_i\cdot(\text{wall pieces along } \{v(g_i)=0\})\): every neighborhood of \(\beta\) contains full tubes over cofinitely many closed points of each wall divisor, and at such tube points the germ is locally \(c_i\cdot(\text{FS-piece})\)+harmonic; \(dd''\ge0\) there forces \(c_i\ge0\). So mixed-sign combinations are never semipositive near \(\beta\), and semipositive germs decompose with the **signs already sorted** — precisely the positive-cone structure (SP\(_\Lambda\)) needs.

*Attempted counterexample 2 (irrational data at skeleton points of mixed-reduction abeloids).* At Abhyankar points of the skeleton \(S(A)\) of a mixed-reduction abeloid, the residue data involves \(\operatorname{Pic}^0\)-classes of the residual abelian variety (\(Q_s\)-classes of the residual Raynaud extension, cf. Q3 below); these are numerically trivial, hence impose **no** open nef-cone conditions; the remaining conditions are lattice-rational. No obstruction manufactured.

### 1.5 Disposition Q1
- \(\dim X=1\) (all \(\Lambda\)): **CANDIDATE-PROPOSITION** (Proposition A, complete proof above; elementary-pieces form). The tripod \((2,-1,-1)\) decomposes; **no 1-dimensional counterexample exists** (rational-polyhedrality proof).
- \(\Lambda=\mathbf Z\), any dimension: trivially TRUE in the formulation (SP\(_{\mathbf Z}\)).
- Divisible \(\Lambda\), \(\dim X\ge2\): **CONDITIONAL-REDUCTION** to (∗) (germ-cone rationality via [GJR, §6] residue theory), plus the Carathéodory-over-\(\mathbf Q\) lemma (proved). The packet's feared positive-cone failure via non-polyhedral nef cones **does not materialize in any configuration I could construct**; two attempted counterexamples collapsed for documented structural reasons (shrinking past kinks; sign rigidity). Not claimed as a theorem: OPEN-AFTER-ATTEMPT for a full proof, with (∗) as the recorded gap.

## Q2. Is \(\Lambda^{p,q}M_{\mathbf R}\hookrightarrow H^{p,q}(A)\) an isomorphism?

### 2.1 Setup and audit
\(A\) abelian over \(K\), \(\dim A=g\); Raynaud uniformization (after a finite extension splitting the situation; [CLD, §19.2.1, App. B.5–B.6]): \(0\to\Lambda_A\to E\xrightarrow{p}A\to0\), \(E\) an extension of a good-reduction abeloid \(B\) (\(\dim b\)) by a split torus \(T\) (\(\dim r\)), \(g=b+r\). Skeleton \(S(A)=T_{\mathrm{trop}}/\tau(\Lambda_A)\cong\mathbf R^r/\Gamma\), a real torus of dimension \(r\); \(A^{\mathrm{an}}\) deformation-retracts onto \(S(A)\) (Berkovich; [CLD, §19.2.2]). \(M\) = character lattice of \(T\), \(\operatorname{rk} M=r\); \(\Lambda^{p,q}M_{\mathbf R}:=\Lambda^pM_{\mathbf R}\otimes\Lambda^qM_{\mathbf R}\) = translation-invariant \((p,q)\)-Lagerberg forms on \(S(A)\); \(\dim=\binom rp\binom rq\), zero if \(p>r\) or \(q>r\). \(H^{p,q}(A)\) = Dolbeault cohomology of [GJR] weakly smooth forms on \(A^{\mathrm{an}}\). The injection (per [GJR, 1.15], attributed to **Prechtel's thesis** — thesis bibliographically UNCONFIRMED in this session: no trace found on Gubler's page/Regensburg EPub through my searches) sends an invariant form to its pullback class; injectivity is forced by the nondegenerate pairing with invariant \((r-p,r-q)\)-forms under \(\int_{S(A)}\) (mechanism standard; attribution to Prechtel per packet). Confirmed by fetch: [GJR, §1.15] states the canonical tropicalization from Raynaud uniformization *is* a harmonic tropicalization, and announces the §17 mixed-reduction abelian threefold with non-smooth canonical tropicalization.

Key literature anchors (what they give, exactly):
- **[JW]** Jell–Wanner, *Poincaré duality for the tropical Dolbeault cohomology of non-archimedean Mumford curves* (arXiv:1612.01889): for Mumford curves \(X_g\), CLD-forms: \(h^{0,0}=h^{1,1}=1\), \(h^{1,0}=h^{0,1}=g\). Tate curve (\(g=1\)): all four numbers \(=1\).
- **[J-Hodge]** Jell, *Tropical Hodge numbers of non-archimedean curves*, Israel J. Math. (2019; arXiv:1706.05895): tropical Hodge numbers of curves; per [CLD, §0.3.1], Jell also showed CLD-Dolbeault cohomology of a proper curve **can be infinite-dimensional** (bidegree of the infinite-dimensionality UNCONFIRMED here; [CLD] ref. [88]).
- **[JSS]** Jell–Shaw–Smacka, *Superforms, tropical cohomology and Poincaré duality*: superform Dolbeault cohomology of polyhedral spaces = tropical cohomology; for the tropical torus \(\mathbf R^r/\Gamma\): \(H^{p,q}\cong\Lambda^pM_{\mathbf R}\otimes\Lambda^q M_{\mathbf R}\) (invariant representatives; with Mikhalkin–Zharkov tropical (co)homology of tropical abelian varieties as the homological counterpart).
- **[J-Poincaré]** Jell's \(d''\)-Poincaré lemma (thesis, Regensburg, `epub.uni-regensburg.de/34788`; and per [CLD, §0.3.1]): the CLD \((0,\bullet)\)-complex resolves \(\mathbf R_X\); hence \(H^{0,q}_{\mathrm{CLD}}(X)\cong H^q_{\mathrm{sing}}(X;\mathbf R)\).
- **[GJR2]**: weakly smooth Dolbeault cohomology of arbitrary compact rig-smooth curves via weighted metric graphs with boundary, with Poincaré duality (abstract confirmed; numbers not retrievable this session).

### 2.2 Bidegree row \((0,q)\): isomorphism for ALL reduction types (CONDITIONAL-REDUCTION)
\(H^{0,q}(A)\cong H^q(A^{\mathrm{an}};\mathbf R)\cong H^q(S(A);\mathbf R)\cong\Lambda^q M_{\mathbf R}^{\ }\) — the first isomorphism needs the \(d''\)-Poincaré lemma **for weakly smooth forms** (the CLD case is [J-Poincaré]; the weakly smooth case is presumably in [GJR, §13], UNCONFIRMED — this is the single missing cohomological input for this row); the second is Berkovich's retraction; the third is invariant-form cohomology of a real torus. Under that input, the injection restricted to \((0,q)\) is an isomorphism for every reduction type — including good reduction, where both sides vanish for \(q\ge1\). Symmetric considerations do *not* apply to \((p,0)\): \(H^{p,0}=H^0(A^{\mathrm{an}},\Omega^p_{d''\text{-closed}})\) is not topological; see 2.4/2.5.

### 2.3 Top bidegree: the injection is NOT surjective whenever \(A\) is not totally degenerate (CANDIDATE-PROPOSITION)
**Proposition B.** *If \(r<g\) then \(\Lambda^{g,g}M_{\mathbf R}=0\) while \(H^{g,g}(A)\neq0\); hence the injection is not an isomorphism in bidegree \((g,g)\) for any abelian variety of non-maximal degeneration (in particular for good reduction and for the §17 mixed-reduction threefold).*

*Proof (modulo the standard integration theory of [GJR, §11]).* \(\Lambda^gM_{\mathbf R}=0\) since \(\operatorname{rk}M=r<g\). For \(H^{g,g}\neq0\): choose a smooth point region with a moment map \(\varphi_U=(\log|f_1|,\dots,\log|f_N|)\) whose tropical image has dimension \(g\) (exists: \(\dim A^{\mathrm{an}}=g\); Bieri–Groves/tropical multiplicity theory [GJR, §§3–4]). Pull back a bump \((g,g)\)-superform \(\omega\) supported in the locus of positive tropical multiplicity with \(\int_{A^{\mathrm{an}}}\omega\neq0\) ([GJR, §11]: integration is well defined and computes a positive multiple of the Euclidean integral against multiplicities). \(\omega\) is \(d''\)-closed (top bidegree on a \(g\)-dimensional space). If \(\omega=d''\eta\) with \(\eta\) weakly smooth of bidegree \((g,g-1)\), then \(\int_{A^{\mathrm{an}}}\omega=0\) by the theorem of Stokes for weakly smooth forms on the compact boundaryless \(A^{\mathrm{an}}\) ([GJR, §11–12]; UNCONFIRMED number). Contradiction. ∎

Audit of this argument: it needs (i) existence of charts of full tropical dimension — true on any rig-smooth \(g\)-dimensional space; (ii) Stokes without boundary terms — [GJR] prove Stokes/Green-type identities as the backbone of their current theory (Poincaré–Lelong, §12), so the dependency is real but low-risk; flagged, not smoothed.

### 2.4 Totally degenerate case (\(r=g\))
- \(g=1\) (**Tate curve; mandatory boundary example, fully worked in §4 below**): \(h^{p,q}=1\) for all \(p,q\in\{0,1\}\) by [JW] for CLD forms; on Mumford curves the weakly smooth and CLD sheaves agree — the residue curves of a semistable model are rational, so numerically trivial classes on the special fiber are (degree-zero on \(\mathbf P^1\)-components, hence) principal-after-refinement, making every \(\mathbf Z\)-harmonic germ a combination of \(\log|\text{units}|\); [GJR, §15] is the systematic statement (agreement of harmonic sheaves with Thuillier's on curves; UNCONFIRMED number). \(\Lambda^{p,q}M_{\mathbf R}\) has the same dimensions \(\binom1p\binom1q\). **LITERATURE-RESOLVED: isomorphism holds for Tate curves.**
- \(g\ge2\): The target computation \(H^{p,q}(S(A))=\Lambda^{p,q}M_{\mathbf R}\) is known ([JSS]). Missing input: a \((p,\bullet)\)-Poincaré lemma with coefficients for weakly smooth forms identifying \(H^q(A^{\mathrm{an}},\Omega^p)\) with the skeleton computation (equivalently: the retraction \(A^{\mathrm{an}}\to S(A)\) induces a quasi-isomorphism on \((p,\bullet)\)-Dolbeault complexes; equivalently \(\Omega^p\)-acyclicity of tubes). This is exactly the input available in dimension 1 through [GJR2] and open in higher dimension. **CONDITIONAL-REDUCTION**, with the required statement isolated. No obstruction is visible: on totally degenerate \(A\) all residue varieties of semistable models are rational (toric strata), so weakly smooth \(=\) CLD locally (same collapse as in the Mumford-curve case; sketch, not a proof).

### 2.5 Good reduction (\(r=0\))
Injection target: \(\Lambda^{p,q}M_{\mathbf R}=0\) for \((p,q)\neq(0,0)\). So "isomorphism" \(\Leftrightarrow\) \(H^{p,q}(A)=0\) for all \((p,q)\neq(0,0)\). Status: \((0,q)\), \(q\ge1\): vanishes conditionally (2.2). \((g,g)\): **nonzero** (Prop. B) — so the *unrestricted* good-reduction statement is **refuted** (modulo the flagged Stokes/integration inputs). Intermediate bidegrees (e.g. \((1,1)\), \((p,0)\) with \(1\le p\le g\)): OPEN; note that for CLD forms Jell's infinite-dimensionality phenomenon for proper curves ([CLD, §0.3.1], ref. [88]) warns that \(H^{p,q}\) may even be infinite-dimensional; whether weak smoothness restores finiteness on good-reduction abelian varieties is precisely the kind of question [GJR2] answers in dimension 1 (graph with one vertex weighted by the reduction genus) — the higher-dimensional analogue is open. **Good-reduction elliptic curve** (from [GJR2]-type graph computations, dimension 1): expected \(h^{0,1}=h^{1,0}=0\) or governed by the vertex weight — exact values not retrievable this session; recorded as needing [GJR2] Thm extraction; \(h^{1,1}=1\ne0\) by Prop. B's argument. So already for a good-reduction elliptic curve the injection fails to be onto at \((1,1)\).

### 2.6 Mixed reduction; the §17 threefold (mandatory test)
For the [GJR, §17] abelian threefold (\(g=3\); mixed reduction; canonical tropicalization not smooth — the specific Raynaud data \(b,r\) UNCONFIRMED this session; the mechanism requires the residual extension non-isotrivial, hence \(b\ge1\), \(r\ge1\); see Q3): Prop. B applies (\(r<3\)): **not an isomorphism at \((3,3)\)**. Rows \((0,q)\): isomorphism conditionally (2.2) — note \(H^{0,q}=\Lambda^q M_{\mathbf R}\) is *insensitive* to the non-smoothness of the canonical tropicalization. Bidegree \((1,1)\): the invariant class of the canonical polarization form \(\tau^*(d'd''\tfrac12 q_L)\) lies in the image by construction; whether classes attached to the residual abelian variety \(B_s\) (invisible to \(M\)) appear in \(H^{1,1}\) is OPEN and is, in my assessment, the sharpest test of whether the injection "sees everything" beyond the trivial failures of Prop. ## Q3. Are canonical metrics on abelian varieties ordinary \(\delta\)-metrics?

### 3.1 Status audit and formalization
Canonical (cubist) metrics: existence/uniqueness up to scalar for any line bundle \(L\) on an abeloid \(A\) [CLD, Lem. 19.1.6, verified locally]. \(\delta\)-metrics: [GK17] (metrics whose first Chern current is locally a \(\delta\)-form in the chart-based calculus of smooth tropicalizations and tropical intersection theory; with positivity theory in [GK19]). [GJR, Rem. 16.4] (text not retrievable; content per packet + [GJR, §1.15] fetch + [CLD, Rem. 19.2.3]) retracts an argument of earlier work that assumed the canonical tropicalization of every abelian variety is a *smooth* tropicalization map; the identity of the earlier work (presumably [GK17] and/or Gubler's older treatment) is UNCONFIRMED. The corrected state: the canonical tropicalization \(\tau:E\to T_{\mathrm{trop}}\) is always a **harmonic** tropicalization ([GJR, §16]); it is smooth iff the residual Raynaud extension is isotrivial, i.e. iff the classifying bundles \(Q_{i,s}\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(B_s)\) are torsion — [CLD, Rem. 19.2.3], verified locally, including: automatic whenever \(\tilde K\) is algebraic over a finite field, and false in general otherwise. Structure formula (split case), verified locally: \(p^*L\cong q^*M\otimes(\text{metric factor }e^{-f\circ\tau})\), \(f\) quadratic with pure quadratic part \(\tfrac12 q_L\) [CLD, Prop. 19.2.5]; hence
\[ c_1(p^*\bar L)=c_1(q^*\bar M)+\tau^*\bigl(d'd''\tfrac12 q_L\bigr) \quad\text{[CLD, Cor. 19.2.6(b)]}. \]
Also verified locally: the canonical metric is always d-psh = "formal \(\otimes\) G-smooth" [CLD, Cor. 19.2.6(a)]; positivity: cubist metric psh \(\Leftrightarrow q_L\ge0\) and \(M_s\) nef \(\Leftrightarrow L\) nef (algebraic case) [CLD, Prop. 19.3.1]; \(c_1(\bar L)^g\) = Haar on \(S(A)\) of mass \(\deg_L(A)\) [CLD, Cor. 19.2.8] (Gubler's Compositio 146 (2010) theorem, reproved).

### 3.2 Good reduction: YES (LITERATURE-RESOLVED)
The canonical metric is the formal metric of a model line bundle on the abelian \(K^\circ\)-scheme (= Néron model here) [CLD, Prop. 19.1.8]. Formal/model metrics are \(\delta\)-metrics with Chern \(\delta\)-forms given by tropical intersection theory — [GK17] (theorem number UNCONFIRMED; the statement is one of the two constitutive examples of that theory, alongside smooth metrics). Positivity bonus: the Chern current is \(\ge0\) iff \(L_s\) nef [CLD, Rem. 19.1.9(b)]. Trivially valued base: [CLD, Rem. 19.3.3].

### 3.3 Totally degenerate: YES, and more — the canonical metric is CLD-smooth (LITERATURE-RESOLVED)
\(B=0\), \(E=T\), \(M\) trivial: \(-\log\) of the canonical metric is locally \(f\circ\mathrm{trop}_T+\log|u|\) with \(f\) quadratic and \(\mathrm{trop}_T\) a genuine moment map (characters are units). Compositions of smooth functions with smooth tropicalizations are CLD-smooth; hence the canonical metric is a smooth metric, a fortiori a \(\delta\)-metric, with curvature the invariant form \(\mathrm{trop}^*(d'd''\tfrac12 q_L)\). (Classical; cf. Gubler, Compositio 2010; [CLD, ch. 19] with \(b=0\).)

### 3.4 Mixed reduction, isotrivial residual extension — includes ALL \(K\) with residue field algebraic over a finite field: YES (CONDITIONAL-REDUCTION, low risk)
If every \(Q_{i,s}\) is torsion, \(\tau\) is a smooth tropicalization after passing to a suitable multiple/cover ([CLD, Rem. 19.2.3]); then \(\tau^*(d'd''\tfrac12q_L)\) is a CLD-smooth form and \(c_1(p^*\bar L)\) = (\(\delta\)-form of a formal metric) + (smooth form), which descends along the local isomorphism \(p\); the canonical metric is a \(\delta\)-metric. In particular **over \(p\)-adic fields (finite residue field) and over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_q\)-residue situations the answer to Q3 is unconditionally YES**; the retracted argument is *correct* there. (Descent along the finite splitting extension: canonical metrics are Galois-equivariant by uniqueness; \(\delta\)-forms descend along finite base change in [GK17]'s calculus — routine, flagged.)

### 3.5 Mixed reduction, non-isotrivial residual extension (the §17 threefold): OPEN-AFTER-ATTEMPT
This requires \(\tilde K\) transcendental-or-infinite over the prime situation (e.g. \(K=\mathbf C((t))\)); note \(\mathbf C_p\) is **excluded** (residue \(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\) ⟹ automatic isotriviality) — an audit point fixing where the hard case can live. Line-by-line attempt on the naive argument:
1. \(c_1(q^*\bar M)\) is a \(\delta\)-form: OK (formal metric, §3.2).
2. \(\tau^*(d'd''\tfrac12q_L)\): write \(f\circ\tau=\ell(\tau)+\tfrac12\sum a_{ij}\tau_i\tau_j\). Linear part: \(\ell(\tau)\) is an \(\mathbf R\)-PL (model-type) function, its \(dd''\) is a \(\delta\)-form ([GK17]'s PL calculus): OK.
3. Quadratic part: as currents, \(d'd''(\tau_i\tau_j)=d'\tau_i\wedge d''\tau_j+d'\tau_j\wedge d''\tau_i\) (using \(dd''\tau_i=0\): the \(\tau_i\) are harmonic — [CLD, Rem. 19.2.3]; the product rule for continuous PL × pluriharmonic-PL needs, and has, a Bedford–Taylor-type justification, cf. [CLD, Cor. 8.11.2 as used in Cor. 19.2.8]).
4. **FIRST UNSUPPORTED IMPLICATION:** "\(d'\tau_i\wedge d''\tau_j\) is a \(\delta\)-form." The [GK17]/[GK19] \(\delta\)- and piecewise-smooth calculi are chart-based over *smooth* tropicalizations; the germs of \(\tau_i\) at the points of \(A^{\mathrm{an}}\) reducing to positive-dimensional subvarieties \(Y\subseteq B_s\) with \(Q_{i,s}|_Y\) non-torsion are **not subordinate to any smooth tropical chart**: smoothness of a formal metric at such points is *equivalent* to torsion of the restricted bundle on every integral subvariety through the reduction point ([CLD, Prop. 17.4.13], verified locally). Hence no local \(\delta\)-form *representation via these charts* exists, and I found no other representation; nor a disproof — ruling the metric out as a \(\delta\)-metric would require a chart-independent invariant separating \(\delta\)-currents from weakly smooth currents (e.g. a restriction-to-curves criterion detecting the non-torsion residue class), which does not exist in the confirmed literature. Alternative attack (Zhang/Tate limits of model metrics) fails structurally: \(\delta\)-forms are not closed under uniform limits of metrics.
5. What survives unconditionally: the canonical metric **is** a weakly smooth (harmonically tropicalized) metric with weakly smooth curvature form — [GJR, §16] and [CLD, §7.8.7, Cor. 19.2.6]; all measure-theoretic consequences (\(c_1^g\) = Haar, local heights) survive [CLD, Cor. 19.2.8].

**Sharp reduction.** *The canonical metric of \((A,L)\) in the non-isotrivial mixed case is a \(\delta\)-metric iff the finitely many currents \(d'\tau_i\wedge d''\tau_j\) \((1\le i\le j\le r)\) on \(E\) are \(\delta\)-forms (equivalently, descend to \(\delta\)-forms on \(A\)).* Disposition: **OPEN-AFTER-ATTEMPT**, with the failure point and the equivalent statement isolated. My assessment (not a claim): the chart-obstruction is representation-theoretic, not current-theoretic, so an extension of the \(\delta\)-formalism admitting harmonic charts (in the spirit of [CLD]'s "G-formes harmoniquement tropicalisées", §7.8.7, or Mihatsch's refinement, [CLD, §0.3.2 ref. 103]) would resolve it — but that is exactly the enlarged weakly-smooth world the question distinguishes from.

## 4. Boundary example: the Tate curve, fully worked (dimension 1, Q2 and Q3)

\(A=E_q=\mathbf G_m^{\mathrm{an}}/q^{\mathbf Z}\), \(0<|q|<1\), \(v=-\log|\cdot|\), \(v(q)=:\lambda>0\). Skeleton \(S=\mathbf R/\lambda\mathbf Z\); \(r=g=1\), \(M\cong\mathbf Z\).
- **Q2:** \(\Lambda^{p,q}M_{\mathbf R}\cong\mathbf R\) for all \(p,q\in\{0,1\}\). \(H^{p,q}(E_q)\): by [JW] (CLD forms; weakly smooth = CLD on Mumford curves, §2.4): \(h^{0,0}=1\) (constants), \(h^{1,0}=1\) (class of \(d'x\), \(x\) the skeleton coordinate: the invariant \((1,0)\)-form \(\mathrm{trop}^*(dx)'\)), \(h^{0,1}=1\) (class of \(d''x\); nonzero by pairing with \(d'x\): \(\int_{E_q}d'x\wedge d''x=\lambda\neq0\)), \(h^{1,1}=1\) (class of \(d'x\wedge d''x\); nonzero by Stokes). All four generated by invariant forms: **isomorphism in every bidegree**. Contrast: good-reduction elliptic curve \(E'\): \((0,q)\)-row: \(H^{0,1}=H^1(\text{point})=0=\Lambda^{0,1}(0)\) ✓; \((1,1)\): \(h^{1,1}=1\neq0\): **not** an isomorphism — the dimension-1 instance of Prop. B.
- **Q3:** \(L=\mathcal O_{E_q}(e)\), theta uniformization: \(p^*L\) trivialized by the Tate theta function \(\theta(w)=\prod_{n\ge0}(1-q^nw)\prod_{n>0}(1-q^n/w)\). Canonical metric: \(-\log\|\theta\|(w)=\tfrac1{2\lambda}v(w)^2+(\text{linear})\) on \(v\)-coordinates, i.e. \(-\log\|\cdot\|=(\tfrac12 q_L)\circ\mathrm{trop}\) with \(q_L(x)=x^2/\lambda\): a CLD-**smooth** metric; curvature \(c_1(\bar L)=\mathrm{trop}^*(d'd''\tfrac{x^2}{2\lambda})\) descends to the invariant \((1,1)\)-form of total mass \(1=\deg L\) — the Haar measure on \(S\). Ordinary \(\delta\)-metric (even smooth). Good reduction \(E'\): canonical metric = formal metric of \(\mathcal O_{\mathcal E'}(\bar e)\) on the Néron/abelian model: model metric, \(\delta\)-metric; curvature current = Dirac-type mass \(1\) at the Shilov point (a \(\delta\)-form, not smooth). Dimension 1 has **no mixed case** (\(r\in\{0,1\}\)): consistent with §3.5's hard case starting at \(g\ge2\); [GJR] exhibit it at \(g=3\) (§17; whether \(g=2\) examples exist is not settled by the retrievable text — the isotriviality mechanism suggests \(g=2\), \(b=r=1\) candidates over \(\mathbf C((t))\); UNCONFIRMED against [GJR, §17]'s choice).
