# Positive pluriharmonic currents: Siu analyticity and proof boundaries

Date: 7 October 2026. Local baseline and last successfully fetched
`origin/work`: `082c6ca3fb38fc471bf2e4b0a24cceea4d2e3805`; last fetched
`origin/main`: `00957114db21b627e97369563032ceda9cea09c7`.

Dinh--Nguyen--Nguyen arXiv:2606.29680v2 attributes Siu analyticity and a
fixed analytic-component decomposition to positive pluriharmonic currents
on compact Kähler manifolds, in every bidimension. Its thirteen-page proof
replaces the forty-page, two-author, projective bidimension-one route in v1.
Both versions were read completely. Eleven dependencies were read in exact
scopes: **213 distinct source/page pairs on 111 individually displayed
sheets**, all with complete selected text and sequential notes. Five PDFs
were read completely and eight partially. This records actual reading,
not a model receipt or a certificate for every recursive external input.

The distinction between positivity, `dd^c`-closedness and `d`-closedness is
essential. The main analyticity induction does not use the unpublished
density-mass theorem cited for a different movable-class application.
The latter remains an uncleared external input. Several auxiliary literal
formula and locator defects are recorded below; none is presented as a
counterexample to the controlling main theorem.

This source-only checkpoint preserves the 4,486-page book, all active
inputs, canonical problem statuses and the historical native catalogue.
In particular, it supplies no non-Archimedean repair of BJ22 Theorem 6.21,
no critical division `(SD)_k` proof and no general envelope closure theorem.

## Main theorem and dependency records

**D01 — Signature and attribution.** v2 Theorem 1.2, actual p.2,
proof pp.11--12, concerns a positive `dd^c`-closed current T of bidimension
(q,q) on a compact Kähler manifold. For c>0, `E_c(T)={nu(T)>=c}` is
analytic of dimension at most q. Its fixed q-dimensional analytic
components have nonnegative constant coefficients, and the remaining
positive pluriharmonic current has threshold sets of dimension at most
q-1. Noncompact or arbitrary compact non-Kähler manifolds are not included.
Dimension-one and endpoint bidimensions require their elementary separate
interpretations rather than an invalid blow-up reduction.

**D02 — Normalization.** v2 uses `dd^c=i partial barpartial/pi`, whereas
the book uses `i partial barpartial/(2pi)`. With the source operator its
plane-one Lelong convention has factor `2^(-q)r^(-2q)` in the ball trace
integral. Positive threshold sets, vanishing and closedness survive a
consistent positive rescaling. A numerical tangent coefficient does not
automatically survive copying conventions.

**D03 — Analytic restriction.** v2 Theorem 2.1 and its cited
support/extension theory provide positivity and pluriharmonicity of the
restriction to analytic components. A current supported on a pure
q-dimensional compact analytic component is a constant multiple of its
integration current. Finite mass makes the nonzero components countable.
The constants are fixed once, rather than adjusted during a residual limit.
The older external support theorems remain imported, not independently
recertified by this reading.

**D04 — Total and strict transforms.** v2 Proposition 2.2 is anchored
in AB03 Theorems 3.9 and 3.11, actual pp.10--13 / printed 725--728.
The total positive pluriharmonic (1,1)-transform has an Aeppli-class
characterization, composition and pushforward properties. AB03 Theorem
3.11 is not a theorem numbered 2. On a general noncompact modification,
the strict transform is only plurisuperharmonic; compact exceptional
components force the weakly psh coefficient functions to be constant,
making that strict transform pluriharmonic. Proposition 3.13, actual
p.13 / printed 728, gives the separate psh strict-mass statement.

**D05 — Point coefficient and locator firewall.** v2 Lemmas 2.3--2.4
use the exceptional coefficient in a point blow-up and the inequalities
`a<=nu(T,x)<=a+nu(strict,y)`. The acquired Vigny preprint Lemma 5.2,
actual p.13, is an integration-by-parts identity, not that point-pullback
lemma. Its Lemma 2.1 and Section 5 supply related exceptional-mass and
pluriharmonic-transform content. The cited journal Lemma 5.2 cannot be
authenticated or disproved as a locator from an unavailable journal PDF.

**D06 — Cone equality and closed representative.** v2 Lemma 3.1 uses
Fu--Xiao together with Tosatti's 2026 compact-Kähler cone duality.
Fu--Xiao Lemma 3.3, actual pp.11--12, has a strict positivity-on-nonzero-
positive-pluriharmonic-currents hypothesis; its closure duality is in
Remark 3.4. Its appendix Theorem 5.2, actual pp.18--19, is not the
journal locator A.2. Generated movable cones and their closures must be
distinguished. An Aeppli/de Rham identification under the `dd^c` lemma
does not turn the original current into a `d`-closed current.

**D07 — Simultaneous Lelong domination.** v2 Lemma 3.2 selects a
positive closed (1,1)-current S in the class of T with
`nu(S,x)>=nu(T,x)` at every point. Finite sets of point blow-ups first
give simultaneous constraints; compactness of the fixed-class closed
positive-current set and the finite intersection property give all
points. This is not a pointwise choice of unrelated representatives.
Exceptional cohomology classes identify the point coefficients.

**D08 — Pluriharmonic Lelong--Skoda reduction.** Vigny preprint
Theorem 5.1, actual p.12, extends the transform to positive pluriharmonic
currents; the closed case is Theorem 3.1. The extension uses compact
pluriharmonic strict transforms and continuous-potential wedge products.
DS04 Corollary 4.2 and Theorem 5.1, freshly read in their official PDF,
provide these inputs. An arbitrary singular wedge, or an individually
positive smooth approximation to T, is not asserted.

**D09 — Compact regularization route.** DS04 Theorem 4.1, actual
pp.7--8 / printed 965--966, supplies bounded-mass differences of positive
smooth pluriharmonic currents. Corollary 4.2 supplies compact
pluriharmonic strict-transform mass and closedness. This gives an
inspected compact route around the uncleared local Appendix B estimates
in Nguyen's tangent preprint. The approximation is a difference; neither
positive piece need converge individually to T.

**D10 — Continuous-potential wedge.** DS04 Theorem 5.1, actual
pp.9--11 / printed 967--969, requires a closed positive (1,1)-factor with
continuous potential and a positive pluriharmonic factor. Formula (12)
retains cross derivatives; it is not simply `dd^c(uT)` when T is
nonclosed. Uniform potential convergence controls the gradient via the
L2 estimate. Arbitrary local pluriharmonic wedges are not supplied.
The ancillary Proposition 5.5 proof has a degree mismatch in a printed
omega exponent and is not imported uncorrected.

**D11 — Tangent existence versus numerical formulas.** v2 Theorem
4.3 cites Nguyen tangent Theorem 1.18 / Corollary 1.21, actual pp.16--17,
and intersection Theorem 2.14(2), actual pp.13--14. Compact, boundaryless
settings remove the local boundary regularity conditions. The compact
pluriharmonic cohomology statement is explicit in the latter theorem.
The qualitative class and dimension argument are separate from exact
normalizations in its later numerical formulas, which are not cleared.

**D12 — Horizontal dimension.** v2 Definition 4.4 uses the largest
j for which the horizontal wedge is nonzero. Using the smallest j gives
zero for every nonzero positive current and does not recover the required
dimension. Proposition 4.5 uses minimum horizontal dimension to identify
a conic current as a base pullback. The source's cited surface Lemma 3.7
is authentic; any higher-dimensional extension requires its own argument.

**D13 — Whole-fibre Liouville.** DS surface Lemma 3.7, actual p.13,
concerns positive pluriharmonic vertical currents on the whole normal
line bundle. Extremal fibre currents correspond to positive harmonic
functions on all of C, hence are constant. Compact fibre-slice Harnack
bounds support the cone argument. A positive harmonic function on a
proper fibre domain need not be constant. DNS surface Lemma 3.15,
actual p.24, literally calls positive psh functions on C2 constant;
`|z|^2` disproves that phrase, while the required positive
pluriharmonic Liouville statement is valid.

**D14 — Divisor tangent.** v2 Lemma 4.6 uses a positive (1,1)-current
with no divisor mass. Shrinking transverse trace kills the extra
horizontal component; the tangent is a pullback of a positive
pluriharmonic current R on the divisor. Lelong semicontinuity gives
`nu(R,y)>=nu(T,y)`. This combines qualitative tangent inputs, not
the uncleared cup-product mass formula from another application.

**D15 — Full-fibre locus in the induction.** v2 proof, pp.11--12,
starts with the minimal analytic hull of `E_c(T)` inside `E_c(S)`.
An offending component is resolved and blown up (identity for a
divisor), with `total=strict+a[D]` and a point showing a<c. Every
exceptional fibre over the offending threshold set lies wholly in
`E_(c-a)(R)`. By induction this is a proper analytic subset Y of the
projective bundle D. The required proper analytic set on the base is
`{x: D_x subset Y}`, equivalently its maximum-fibre-dimension locus.
The image of a proper Y alone need not be proper: a section surjects.
The full-fibre inclusion is therefore retained explicitly in any
explanatory integration. Resolution is an imported analytic input.

**D16 — Fixed decomposition and rectifiability.** Once threshold
analyticity is known, Theorem 2.1 gives fixed component restrictions and
positive finite partial remainders. Corollary 1.4, actual pp.2--3,12,
uses support on a countable union of Borel sets of finite
2q-dimensional Hausdorff measure. The residual's positive-Lelong set
has zero residual trace mass, while that support forces positive
density almost everywhere, hence residual zero. Finite Hausdorff
dimension is not the same assumption as finite Hausdorff measure.

**D17 — Movable application has an additional dependency.** v2
Lemma 4.7 and Theorem 1.5 concern a bidimension-one pluriharmonic
current paired with a positive **closed** (1,1)-current. Closedness
of the second factor makes the mixed tensor pluriharmonic. DS density
Lemma 5.5, actual pp.43--44, applies to a measure and a closed current;
zero measure mass on its positive-Lelong set gives the zero density
needed in the horizontal wedge argument. The final density mass equals
cup product via **Ngu26 Theorem 1.9**, for which no source PDF was
obtained. This general-dimensional application is attributed with that
explicit uninspected input. It is not used in D15's main induction.

**D18 — Surface energy is different.** DNS surface Theorem 2.2,
actual pp.8--9, treats two pluriharmonic (1,1)-currents. Their tensor
need not be pluriharmonic, and tangent mass has two L2-energy cross
terms. For a closed factor its energy vanishes; Lemma B.2, actual
p.40, characterizes this. For a nonclosed self-pair the mass is
`{T}^2-2E(T)`. These terms cannot be discarded by copying the
closed-current tensor argument. Surface nef/big Corollary 2.4 retains
its positive-Lelong measure hypothesis. The stronger foliation iff
statement additionally uses hyperbolicity.

**D19 — Surface kernel hypotheses.** DNS Appendix A, actual
pp.38--39, requires sectionwise kernel integrability and support in
`dist(x,y)<=A/|lambda|`, together with the scale-dependent coefficient
bounds. The shrinking support is essential. Weak convergence plus a
large coefficient bound alone does not give the L2-product limit.
Appendix B's slice/L2 proof retains the appropriate lower exponents;
generic hyperbolic-foliation coordinates on p.42 are ancillary.

## Tosatti dependency and literal defects kept separate

**D20 — Morse and cone scope.** Tosatti v2 Theorem 1.1 concerns
nef real (1,1)-classes alpha,beta on compact Kähler X:
`Vol(alpha-beta)>=int alpha^n-n int alpha^(n-1) beta`.
Its corollaries give the closed movable/pseudoeffective duality and
balanced-cone comparison used in D06. Volume differentiability is
inside the big cone; boundary local Lipschitz continuity is separate.
No representative-level positive subtraction is implied by a class
inequality.

**D21 — Null transition set and closed current.** Proposition 2.1
uses maxima of finite local potentials and generic constants to produce
a globally positive closed current. The transition set is closed and
Lebesgue-null. Lebesgue-null does not mean pluripolar. The proof controls
that null set by concentrated smooth approximants and mixed-mass
estimates; it does not assert that the difference of two arbitrary
positive currents is positive. Its smooth approximation input includes
an older Błocki--Kołodziej theorem not acquired here.

**D22 — Fixed absolutely continuous lower bound.** Proposition 2.3
needs preservation of a common, fixed determinant-density lower bound
under weak limits of positive currents. Determinant-root concavity,
convolution of the positive matrix-valued measures and Lebesgue
differentiation give that specific preservation. The common bound is
fixed before passing to the limit. This is enough for the inspected
Tosatti step and does not require the stronger pointwise limsup claim
printed in Boucksom 2002 Proposition 2.1.

**D23 — Literal limsup claim fails.** Boucksom 2002 Proposition 2.1,
actual p.6 / printed 1048, prints `T_ac^n>=limsup T_(k,ac)^n` a.e.
Its convolution/Fatou proof gives a liminf; point-dependent subsequences
do not supply the asserted limsup. On a flat elliptic curve,
`T_k=(1+sin(2pi kx)) omega` are positive closed currents converging
weakly to omega, while their density limsup is 2 for almost every x
(irrational rotations), exceeding the limiting density 1. The fixed
common lower-bound conclusion in D22 remains valid. This literal
auxiliary counterexample does not refute Tosatti's main inequality.

**D24 — Quasi-positive boundary convergence needs domination control.**
Nguyen tangent Definition 2.3/Lemma 2.4 and intersection
Definition 3.2/Lemma 3.3 assert a boundary convergence inference for
differences of positive currents. `R_n=delta_(1/n)-delta_(-1/n)`
converges weakly to zero, whose mass on the boundary of (0,1) is zero,
but `R_n((0,1))=1`. The positive dominating limits have boundary mass.
Avoiding that mass, rather than just the signed limit's boundary mass,
is needed. Suitable radius choices may avoid it; this does not refute
the compact boundaryless qualitative theorem.

**D25 — Local regularization estimates remain uncleared.** Nguyen
tangent Appendix B, actual pp.232--239, has a translated kernel with
a printed argument-sign mismatch; its alternative B.7 bound in y alone
does not give the claimed uniform unit-measure-to-Lp bound. A kernel
`phi(x)chi(y)|y|^(2-N)` illustrates the defect in real dimension N>2.
The difference kernel also contains a diagonal delta, which cannot be
treated as an ordinary L1 kernel. An approximate identity minus the
identity does not map every discontinuous L-infinity input into a
Lipschitz function with the displayed small norm. The last bootstrap
still invokes those estimates. DS04's compact pluriharmonic difference
regularization is used instead; the general local psh chain is not
certified here.

**D26 — Projective bundle and radial factors.** Nguyen intersection
Section 10, actual pp.52--54, has an inclusion direction/dimension
mismatch between the normal bundle and its infinity projectivization;
the cohomology basis of rank r+1 belongs to `P(E direct-sum C)`.
The zero-section/top-index term is separate. In Theorems 8.4--8.5,
actual pp.45--47, with `dd^c=i partial barpartial/pi`,
`r^(-2)int_(|t|<r)dd^c|t|^2=2`, not 1. Thus literal numerical
coefficient formulas are not cleared. Qualitative class independence
and a nonzero positive rescaling are distinct questions.

**D27 — Original closed conic argument.** DS density Proposition
3.10 and Lemmas 3.11--3.15, actual pp.25--29, concern closed currents.
Blowing up the zero section gives the whole-fibre argument and separates
the zero-section term. Finite mass eliminates radial conic terms and
positivity eliminates mixed terms. Some algebraic steps are independent
of closedness, but extending the full theorem to pluriharmonic currents
requires the corresponding strict-mass/support inputs; resemblance is
not a certificate. Only the listed eight density-preprint pages were read.

## Superseded forty-page route

**D28 — Older scope and dimension definitions.** v1 Theorem 1.1 is
projective and bidimension one; the compact-Kähler question remained
open in its text. It uses the erroneous smallest-index horizontal
dimension wording. Its movable-closure conclusion is not an actual
covering family of curves. The comparison reading never licenses
transferring the v2 theorem to v1 or retaining its older scope as current.

**D29 — Threshold and degree slips.** v1 Proposition 2.2 estimates a
tail by c/2 and then needs threshold `c/(2|I|)`, not `c/|I|`.
Corollary 2.4's no-mass dimension direction is reversed. Later tests
and omega exponents sometimes use surface degrees in general dimension.
Its mass-free threshold set has an upward-closed range, so a finite
maximal threshold as printed is not the intended infimum/essential
supremum. Constant-density normalization claims also require scaling.

**D30 — Analytic estimates and diagonal support.** v1's scaled tensor
of two pluriharmonic currents is neither automatically pluriharmonic
nor of pure bidegree. Singular test forms need regularization. Some
displayed annular powers lose the cancellation from the radius scale,
and a support description near the diagonal omits fixed smooth parts.
An ambient exact current supported on the diagonal requires the Thom
class/injection argument to conclude the desired coefficient vanishes;
support alone is insufficient. These are recorded defects in the older
proof route, not certified counterexamples to its conclusion.

**D31 — Old lifting and local appendices.** v1 Lemma 6.3 does not
provide the asserted individually positive smooth pluriharmonic
approximants for its lifts; shrinking-tube concentration is not supplied
by weak convergence alone. Several derivative claims use C2 estimates
under a C1 heading or copy the wrong coordinate. Its Appendix A has
an ambient `dd^c log` expansion dropping transverse curvature and a
copied logarithmic kernel; bounded convolution does not cover a
singular annular kernel without the needed estimates. These steps are
not retained as the controlling proof. The explicit older
Dinh--Lawrence rectifiability reference is not a model-verification receipt.

## Integration boundary and preservation

An attributed Appendix H continuation may explain the v2 theorem,
fixed coefficients, total/strict transforms, closed Lelong domination
and full-fibre induction. It must name the inspected alternative compact
regularization route and keep uninspected external support/resolution
inputs attributed. A movable-class application must additionally expose
Ngu26; a surface energy formula must retain the nonclosed cross terms.
Tosatti's Morse inequality and closed fixed-density lower-bound step may
be explored in their own scope. None identifies a positive
pluriharmonic representative with a closed representative pointwise.
