Classical pluriharmonicity is defined by curve tests after arbitrary ground
field extension and therefore does not visibly provide a finite local
presentation by logarithms of analytic units.  On the quasi-smooth locus,
altered local uniformization and rigid \(\acute{e}\)tale descent remove this
gap.  The descent step is included because the uniformizing cover need not be
finite, flat, or boundaryless, so finite-flat descent cannot be applied to it
directly.

Thus \Cref{thm:p061-quasismooth-ph-rpl} removes the global strictly
semistable-model hypothesis from the quasi-smooth part of the classical
pluriharmonic regularity problem.  It is local on \(X\): global
quasicompactness, separatedness, and boundarylessness play no role.  The
coefficient group is essential.  By
\Cref{prop:p061-integral-pl-counterexample}, the conclusion cannot be
strengthened to \(\mathbf Q\)-PL or integral PL.

The theorem does not address a non-quasi-smooth point.  Within the good,
separated, strict, boundaryless scope of
\Cref{prob:frontier-pluriharmonic}, the published curve comparison already
covers every strict analytic curve, including singular and nonreduced ones
\cite[\S4.1.2 and Proposition~3.15]{GR25Classical}.  Consequently, the
residual P06.1 question is local regularity at non-quasi-smooth points in
analytic dimension at least two.
