## Three exact auxiliary calibrations

1. The source's same-index comparison in Lemma 54, Proposition 55 and
   Lemma 56 fails for its permitted translated P1 boundary metric a=2.
   For f(z)=|z|²/(1+|z|²), source probability integral is
   4^n/(1+4^n), target 4/5: the gap tends to **1/5**, not O(1/n).
   The metrized pullback instead changes D_m to D_mn. Theorem 61 uses
   the flagged estimate, so subsequent main proofs are not cleared.
2. For the canonical O(1) metric on P2 restricted to (z,1+z), the local
   psh weight is log max(1,|z|,|1+z|). Product corners, primitive
   saturated rank-one characters and continuous boundary weights hold.
   Curvature on a left unit-circle arc requires a positive e1 term, but
   the entire same Haar slice adds unwanted mass near z=1, where the
   original weight is harmonic. The printed whole-slice formula in
   Propositions 90/9.5 omits the actual polyhedral cell inequalities.
3. On (z,1+z) with the standard product boundary metric, the source
   probability is half Haar on |z|=1 plus half Haar on |1+z|=1.
   The ambient monomial beta=z2 pushes the first part to a circle
   centered at 1, with non-atomic tropical distribution. Proposition
   9.6's general fixed-modulus Haar pushforward conclusion therefore
   needs actual factorization through the active quotient or a more
   general density/support description.

## Restriction, normalization and dependency boundaries

Quotienting by the connected stabilizer leaves finite stabilizers. Likewise
B intersect T can include a finite part; its identity component is the
torus, and the abelian quotient maps isogenously to pi(B). The proof of
Lemma 115 incorrectly treats this torus as dense in a semiabelian B with
positive-dimensional abelian quotient. A functorial intrinsic norm/tropical
restriction construction must fill that step. Common defining fields are
needed for a single affine envelope to contain every orbit. Torsion
translation preserves Neron–Tate height; literal bundle/metric identities
require compatible power or rational identifications. General-fan inputs
must survive restriction and cannot disappear from Theorem 106's signature.
