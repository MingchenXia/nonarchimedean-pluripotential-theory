## Two distinct settings and the meaning of height

Theorem A on page 3, proved as Theorem 5.5 on page 19, concerns the
special fibre of a smooth proper model over the integers of a finite
extension of `Q_p`. Its geometric generic fibre is a projective
irreducible hyperKähler variety of dimension `2n >= 4`; the smooth
proper model and special fibre may be algebraic spaces. The source
states the equivalence of quasi-F-splitting, F-splitting, a degree-two
crystalline slope-zero summand, and finite generation of every
`H^j(X,W O_X)` over `W(k)`. The quasi-F-splitting height is consequently
one or infinity. The theorem does not assume Hodge-goodness, integral
crystalline torsion-freeness, or a small absolute ramification index.
Those hypotheses appear in different statements and should not be
silently added to, or removed from, other conclusions.

The intrinsic setting of Definition 4.10 and Corollary 4.16 on pages
15–17 instead requires a smooth proper geometrically connected variety
with trivial canonical bundle and the complete graded algebra

\[
 H^*(X,\mathcal O_X)=k[\eta]/(\eta^{n+1}),\qquad |\eta|=2.
\]

This Hodge-good condition includes nonzero cup powers, not only the
dimensions of the groups. In dimension at least four the source obtains
the same quasi-F-splitting/F-splitting dichotomy by formal-group cup
products. This argument has no characteristic-zero lifting assumption.
The crystalline height in the good-reduction argument and the height
of a prorepresentable degree-two Artin–Mazur group must remain distinct
until the hypotheses identifying them have been supplied.

Definition 6.14 on page 29 additionally requires that `H^0(Omega^2)` be
one-dimensional and generated by a nowhere-degenerate two-form. This
working notion of primitive symplectic variety is not asserted to be
equivalent to Fu–Li's irreducible symplectic notion. It imposes neither
étale simple connectedness nor closedness of the two-form explicitly;
E1 degeneration supplies closedness when available. Étale simple
connectedness alone leaves room for nonzero `H^1(O)` from a nonreduced
Picard scheme. The Enriques discussion on page 30 illustrates why the
characteristic-zero definitions cannot simply be transported.

In small characteristic, a nondegenerate alternating two-form still
has a nonzero Pfaffian volume. Its ordinary nth exterior power is
`n!` times that volume and vanishes for `p <= n`. Thus the usual
characteristic-zero exterior-power proof of canonical triviality is
not available in that range; the source explicitly uses the Pfaffian.

## The good-reduction proof and its finite-generation input

Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.2 on pages 11–12 use weak admissibility and
the one-dimensional degree-zero Hodge graded piece in degree two. The
Newton slopes below one either disappear or form a block of slope
`1-1/h` and multiplicity `h`. The slope-defect budget is at most one.
The symmetry of Figure 1 is consistent with its displayed Newton and
Hodge polygons; it is not used as a substitute for the argument.

Proposition 4.7 and Corollary 4.8 on page 14 carry the characteristic-zero
cup injection `Sym^i H^2 -> H^(2i)` through the relevant comparisons.
For `i >= 2` and `h >= 2`, the minimum symmetric-power slope is at
least `i/2 >= 1`; in the ordinary case only the power of the slope-zero
line contributes below one. This is not an arbitrary positive-
characteristic cup-injectivity assertion.

Proposition 5.1 on page 18 assumes that **all** Witt-vector cohomology
groups are finitely generated. It then identifies the coherent Euler
characteristic with the alternating slope defects. The proof keeps
the finite torsion terms in the Verschiebung exact sequence until
their kernel and cokernel lengths cancel. Rationalizing first would
discard that bookkeeping. Propositions 5.2–5.3 give possible values
`n+1`, `2` and `1`, according as the degree-two height is one, finite
and greater than one, or infinite. Because `chi(O_X)=n+1 >= 3`, only
the ordinary branch survives under finite generation. Proposition 5.4
then uses top Witt cohomology, its torsion-free quotient and
Verschiebung separation to obtain a nonzero top coherent Frobenius
map and hence F-splitting.

The implication from quasi-F-splitting to finite generation uses
Nakkajima in Lemma 2.4; the top-degree passage uses Olsson in
Proposition 5.4. These external inputs have not been fully audited in
this checkpoint. No unconditional proof certificate is attached here.
The source also warns that failure of ordinary height does not imply
all crystalline slopes are the supersingular slopes. For K3 surfaces
(`n=1`), the intermediate value `2` equals `chi(O)=2`, and finite
nonordinary height is compatible with quasi-F-splitting. The
higher-dimensional dichotomy must not be extended to surfaces.

## Formal-group products: the selected citation does not cover every degree

Proposition 4.14 and Corollaries 4.15–4.16 on pages 16–17 use the
**relative** Cartier product over `W`, its bilinear Witt cup product,
and the differential of that product. They do not use the absolute
Cartier tensor product with unit `W(Z)`. The cup map on the
one-dimensional tangent spaces is nonzero by Hodge-goodness. The
source obtains height one in every even degree on the ordinary branch;
otherwise the higher even formal groups have infinite height.

The author-hosted Mondal–Reinecke *Unipotent homotopy theory of schemes*
PDF has 95 pages. Only actual pages 1, 76–77 and 89–90 were displayed
and read. Propositions 7.2.17 and 7.2.21 give the relative product's
dimension and the additive-group result for two one-dimensional
height-greater-than-one factors; their displayed proofs were read.
Remark 7.2.18 relates this product to the Cartier-module construction.
Earlier internal inputs and Antieau–Nikolaus's construction are not
fully checked here, and the 108-page journal version is not substituted
for these locators.

There is a specific scope limitation in the modern Artin–Mazur
citation. Mondal–Reinecke Proposition 6.3.3 on page 77 works under
the standing condition (*) on page 76, which includes
`H^i(O_X)=0` for every `0<i<q`, where `q` is the degree of its formal
group. Hodge-good varieties have `H^2(O_X)=k`, so this special-case
statement does not itself supply the required identification for
`q=2i>2`. Zou also cites the general Artin–Mazur criterion. That
primary criterion and its general Cartier-module identification have
not been checked here. This records an uncleared citation route, not
a counterexample or a claim that the main result is false.

## Families require lifting and the stated characteristic bounds

Section 6 uses smooth proper scheme families with geometrically
connected fibres and a **relative** trivialization of the canonical
bundle. The vanishings and the base hypotheses remain attached to
their individual results. Theorem 6.8 on page 25 controls finite
height when the generic fibre is non-F-split and there is a
Hodge-good fibre. It does not rule out finite quasi-F-split,
non-F-split fibres outside the Hodge-good locus when the generic
fibre is F-split.

Lemma 6.9 on pages 25–26 uses locally constant `dim H^2(T)`—condition
(ct2)—to make the lifting obstruction a section of a vector bundle.
Dense-open fibrewise liftability then gives relative W2 liftings
locally on the smooth base. Proposition 6.11 on pages 27–29 uses
these **relative** liftings, low-degree de Rham decomposition,
Frobenius pullback of determinantal ideals, and Nakayama's lemma to
propagate ranks. Fibrewise lifting alone is not its initial relative
decomposition hypothesis.

Selected actual pages 1, 4–6 and 17–20 of Deligne–Illusie's published
Inventiones 89 (1987), pages 247–270, were read and displayed. The
scan verifies the antisymmetrization denominator `1/i!` for `i<p`,
Corollary 3.7(a)'s truncated relative decomposition, and the
base-change/local-freeness argument of Section 4.1. Remark 4.1.6
explicitly requires lifting the Frobenius twist `X'`. For an affine
smooth base over a perfect field, lifting base Frobenius lets a
relative lift of `X` give such a lift of `X'`. Corollary 3.7(b)'s
full decomposition in relative dimension at most `p` has its own
additional cohomological condition; it should not be conflated with
the unrestricted truncated statement.

Theorem 6.12 requires `2 <= n < p`, a smooth connected finite-type
base, a Hodge-good fibre, fibrewise `H^1(O)=0`, (ct2), and the
standing relative canonical hypothesis. Proposition 6.11's
all-degree Hodge assertions instead require `2n<p`. The middle
Serre pairing extends coherent cup-algebra propagation to `n<p`;
it does not extend all Hodge assertions to that range.

Remark 6.13 identifies two source-level open directions: constancy
of Hodge-goodness at `n=p`, and removal of (ct2) from the family
theorem. At the endpoint the coherent dimensions remain constant,
but the cup product across degrees `p-1` and `p+1` is outside the
controlled range. These are research leads, not additions to the
canonical book inventory or solved problems.

## Standard deformation classes and integral inputs

Theorem C on page 4, proved on pages 32–33, concerns Hodge-
deformation equivalence through the specified smooth proper
families with constant Hodge numbers. Its K3 Hilbert schemes
require `p>n`; its generalized Kummer varieties require `p>n+1`.
The alternative chain criterion retains `p>2n` and (ct2). These
are not conclusions for every smooth proper symplectic family.
Formal W lifting or an actual W2 lifting is not automatically an
actual projective W lifting of every final fibre.

Theorem A.1 distinguishes all-degree torsion-freeness and E1
degeneration under `p>n+1` from the separate H3/H4 statement under
`p>=5` and `p` not dividing `n+1`. The two bounds have different
uses. In particular the latter does not handle Kummer varieties
at `p=3`. Lemma A.11 on pages 38–40 separates rational transport
of the Beauville–Bogomolov form from its integral primitivity and
unimodularity. Integral perfection is not inferred merely from
rational nondegeneracy.

Only pages 1–3 of Yu Min's 31-page author-hosted *Integral p-adic
Hodge theory of formal schemes in low ramification* were displayed.
Theorem 0.3 there explicitly recalls Fontaine–Messing, assumes
`0<=i<=p-2`, and preserves invariant factors. This selected check
confirms the quoted comparison's degree bound; it does not read
its complete proof or verify a separate journal locator. The
motivic diagonal, torsion and deformation inputs of Appendix A
remain incompletely audited.
