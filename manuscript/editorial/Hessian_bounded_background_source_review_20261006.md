## What the bounded operator and comparison actually supply

The published paper's Sections 2--5 now have a complete reading:
elementary symmetric-polynomial inequalities and torsion estimates
on actual 5--19; signed measure coefficients and inductive currents
on 19--24; weighted Sobolev estimates and nonclosed integration
by parts on 25--34; capacity convergence and quasicontinuity on
34--37; and bounded monotone measure convergence on 38--42.
Order-zero currents allow bounded multiplication before another
ddc is taken. Intermediate wedge products need not all be positive
as bare forms; the reference powers and appropriate trace bounds
matter. Quasicontinuity and convergence in capacity do not themselves
make a potential continuous.

Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.2, actual 42--43, use
H_m(u)=(ddc u)^m wedge omega^(n-m), with functions defined in a
neighborhood of the comparison domain. The strict psh perturbation
and the nonclosed error constants belong to that domain. Polar and
negligible sets, actual 44--48, support the later measure-limit
argument. These proofs still have the cited smooth estimates and
classical antecedents; reading them in full is not an independent
verification of every external antecedent.

Theorem 8.2, actual 49, gives a continuous solution for local Lp
data, p>n/m, by stability and the cited smooth Dirichlet theorem.
Theorem 8.7, actual 52--56, states bounded local existence and
uniqueness for a measure with a bounded zero-boundary subsolution.
Its reductions retain compact support, an extension to a fixed
neighborhood and uniform total mixed masses. The solution is bounded;
continuity for general measure data is not its conclusion. The
balayage reduction on actual 53 speaks of a continuous replacement
agreeing outside the ball with a bounded obstacle; that language
requires a separate treatment of rough boundary/obstacle data.
The printed comparison in the preceding extension step must apply
to the original homogeneous potential, rather than merely reiterate
the tautological inequality for its maximum with the defining function.
Neither observation is promoted here to a counterexample to Theorem 8.7.

Section 9, actual 56--58, works with a general smooth real
alpha in Gamma_m(omega). On a chart choose rho with ddc rho>=alpha
and retain tau=alpha-ddc rho. The background operator is the
binomial expansion of ddc(u+rho)+tau, not the zero-background
operator of u+rho alone. The source explicitly states local transfer
of the preceding results. Lemma 9.3 states unique local background
Dirichlet solvability; its proof is omitted as an extension of
Theorem 8.7. Theorem 9.1 gives bounded existence on a compact
manifold with boundary, using a bounded subsolution. Remark 9.2
keeps global boundary uniqueness open in general and states it for
Stein manifolds or closed reference and background forms. This is
not a nonuniqueness claim for the closed compact manifold used in
Fang's separate global domination argument.

Fang's Proposition 1.8, actual 6, is printed for bounded omega-psh
potentials, while later steps and the new smoothness paper use the
larger bounded m-subharmonic class. Its local negative quadratic
perturbation is compatible with that larger cone by mixed positivity,
but its comparison invocation must concern the background operator,
not silently replace the zero-background Corollary 6.2. Section 9's
explicit transfer is relevant evidence; the exact bounded background
comparison proof remains a separate application gate. The strict-gap
exponential comparison on actual 7 does correctly give a uniform
factor exp(-lambda delta), as the book's continuous passage explains.

## Continuity chain and integrability gates

Fang's Proposition 1.17, actual 8--9, states contact support for
**continuous** obstacles. Lemma 1.18 immediately uses it with
min(u,v) for merely bounded admissible potentials. The required
bounded/quasicontinuous obstacle contact theorem is a further input,
with its precise Hessian class and antecedents; it is not supplied
by changing the word continuous. The ensuing infimum envelopes on
actual 19 likewise need that input.

Section 2 constructs local Monge--Ampère subsolutions and global
bounds, then reduces a Hessian bound to a psh envelope. The local
exponential integrability theorem is external; its exponent and
mass normalization retain their separate primary scope. Lemma 2.5,
actual 13--14, also needs the exact bounded-density auxiliary
convergence theorem and global bounded domination to identify its
limit. Its Step 2 cites the 2015 Monge--Ampère item for Theorem 3.19,
whereas the checked result with that number is in the 2016 Hessian
paper. These version and operator distinctions were already recorded.
The later L1 argument cites Lemma 1.5 for a psh envelope whose
supremum has not been normalized; the lemma only bounds potentials
with supremum zero. The relation to the prescribed normalized
Hessian obstacle must be used separately before that citation can
bound the envelope's unnormalized L1 norm.

Sections 3.1--3.2 retain the strict sublevel stability argument,
normalizing constants, positive-exponent and zero-exponent limits,
and identification of any other bounded solution. The earlier
diagnostic of Proposition 3.4 remains valid: a measure lower bound
does not identify the frozen auxiliary limit with the given
potential. The exp(lambda u_j/m) factor in the normalizer estimate
is also retained as an earlier qualification. Positive-exponent
existence and the normalized zero-exponent equation are distinct.

The source defines L^chi by integrability at **some** scale. General
membership is weaker than integrability at every scale and weaker
than convergence of Luxemburg norm tails. Its all-scale dominated
convergence argument therefore needs an additional growth or
integrability condition. For bounded densities, all scaled modulars
are finite and this particular obstruction disappears. The regularity
equation with a given bounded solution and smooth positive density
lies in that restricted density regime; the remaining comparison,
auxiliary convergence and specialized second-order inputs still
have to be checked there. No discontinuous bounded PDE solution
has been constructed by these norm diagnostics.

## Seven exact literal diagnostics

The machine-readable elementary calculations are embedded in the
source manifest and retained in the ignored primary cache, SHA-256
`02ff970423311dc41b11f5ed0072e7a33d3dd1b4f2ea3b8d2ed3684070032762`.
They distinguish statements as printed from their main PDE conclusions.

1. **Endpoint product factor**, published actual 7, (2-11)--(2-12).
   At n=m=2 and eigenvalues (1,1), the indicated product is one,
   but the displayed (n-m)^(m-1) factor is zero. A positive dimension
   constant is needed. The stated main lemma is not thereby refuted.
2. **Torsion wedge degree**, published actual 11, (2-17). For
   n=3,m=2,k=s=0, the first two expressions have bidegree (3,3),
   while the last printed expression has degree (2,2). The lemma's
   statement has the degree-correct reference power n-k-s.
3. **Difference-of-powers range**, published actual 50, before
   (8-3). The sum must run from zero to m-1. For n=m=2, A=2 beta
   and B=beta, A^2-B^2 is 3 beta^2; the printed range gives
   2 beta^2. For n=3,m=2 it even includes a negative power.
4. **Maximum-lemma exponential**, Fang actual 9, Lemma 1.18(1).
   Its second hypothesis is printed with exp(lambda u)g. On a
   compact Kähler background take constants u=0,v=1,lambda=1,
   f=g=1. Both printed hypotheses hold, but the claimed maximum
   lower bound says 1>=e. The proof uses the corrected
   exp(lambda v)g hypothesis. This printed auxiliary statement
   is false; no main continuity-theorem counterexample follows.
5. **Norm/modular scale**, Fang actual 10. For the author's
   normalized chi(t)=t^2/2 on a probability space, constant
   f=epsilon has norm epsilon and modular epsilon^2/2. Their ratio
   2/epsilon is unbounded, so one fixed C0 cannot give the asserted
   linear bound for all f. The threshold chi(1) is essential:
   the normalized power norm is exactly L2. The source's normalized
   Hölder inequality does not have a missing factor of two.
6. **Dimension-dependent convex coefficient**, Fang actual 13.
   At n=3, A=10 beta and B=beta, (A^3+B^3)/4 has coefficient
   1001/4, exceeding 1331/8 for ((A+B)/2)^3. Mixed positivity
   instead gives the coefficient 2^(-n); an application of strict
   domination additionally needs one uniform contraction below one.
7. **Norm-tail convergence**, Fang actual 12--13 and 20. Let
   chi(t)=(exp(t)-t-1)/(e-1), a convex normalized weight, and let
   f(x)=-log x on the probability interval (0,1). At every scale
   0<s<1 its modular is finite, so f belongs to L^chi. For any
   A>0 and 0<r<=1, the modular of 1_{f>A}f/r diverges because
   integral from zero to exp(-A) of x^(-1/r) is infinite. Every
   tail norm is at least one. Such exponential growth fits the
   stated Monge--Ampère Condition (K) asymptotic by an eventually
   increasing, rapidly growing h; its exact formula is in the
   diagnostic. General membership alone does not justify the
   claimed norm-tail or all-scale convergence. Bounded densities
   do not exhibit this obstruction.
