\begin{proof}
Write $\mathcal J_T$ for the exact product-comparison sheaf in
\Cref{thm:p071-infinite-star-polyhedral-collapse}.  We use three outputs
of its proof.  First, $\mathcal J_T$ is a sheaf, contains
$\mathcal G_{T,0}$, and is closed under pointwise decreasing sequential
limits.  Second, on a compact reduced core $D_K\times Q$, where the
exceptional branch set is finite, the active full-branch set $K$ is
countable, and $Q$ is a compact convex polytope, the exact comparison
envelope $C^{\mathrm{ex}}$ is finite, continuous, and nonexpanding in the
uniform norm; it dominates every upper-semicontinuous comparison minorant
of its obstacle.  These are
\Cref{lem:p071-exact-star-polyhedral-envelope,lem:p071-arbitrary-exact-star-polyhedral-insertion}.
Third, if $H$ is a finite continuous comparison function on a fixed
compact standard core $D\times Q$, then for every $\varepsilon>0$ the
finite-activity and partial-Legendre construction in the cited theorem
gives a generator $g$ on one prescribed smaller product neighbourhood
$U'\times V'$ such that
\begin{equation}
                         H\leq g\leq H+6\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-one-step}
\end{equation}
The smaller neighbourhood depends only on the two nested geometric cores,
not on $H$ or $\varepsilon$, so the estimate applies on the same domain to
every member of a finite family.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{The comparison class is decreasing-sequentially closed.}
The definition of $\mathcal C_X$ is local.  Restriction is immediate, and
gluing follows from the sheaf property of $\mathcal J_T$ after shrinking
the Stone variable to compact clopen sets.  Every generator belongs to
$\mathcal C_X$: after a clopen shrinking it is independent of $z$, so one
takes $Y$ to be a point.

Let $u_n\downarrow u$ on an open $W\subset X$, with
$u_n\in\mathcal C_X(W)$, and first fix a node point
$(z_0,\eta,y_0)\in W$.  Choose a compact clopen neighbourhood
$C\ni z_0$, a standard star neighbourhood $U_0$, and a compact convex
polytope $Q$ containing $y_0$ in a relatively open convex neighbourhood,
so that
\[
                         C\times U_0\times Q\subset W.
\]
After shrinking only the finitely many exceptional collars, choose a
compact closed standard core $D\subset U_0$.

For fixed $n$, compactness of $C\times\{\eta\}\times Q$ gives finitely
many node factorization charts for $u_n$.  Intersect their standard star
neighbourhoods, refine their polyhedral neighbourhoods over a finite cover
of $Q$, and let $K_n\subset B$ be the union of their finite exceptional
sets, their common countable activity sets, and the exceptional set of
$U_0$.  Then $K_n$ is countable and
\begin{equation}
 u_n(z,p_b(t),y)=u_n(z,\eta,y)
 \quad
 \bigl(z\in C,\ b\in B\setminus K_n,\
       t\in[-\infty,0],\ y\in Q\bigr).
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-limit-inactive}
\end{equation}
For every branch in $K_n$, compactness of its branch prism with
$C\times Q$ supplies finitely many further factorization charts.  Together
with the node charts, these form a countable family describing $u_n$ on
$C\times D\times Q$.

Choose a countable clopen base in every metrizable quotient appearing in
these charts, for all $n$, and let $\mathcal B$ be the Boolean algebra of
clopen subsets of $C$ generated by their inverse images and by the domains
of the charts.  It is countable.  The evaluation map
\begin{equation}
 q:C\longrightarrow
 Y:=q(C)\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathcal B}
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-common-quotient}
\end{equation}
is a continuous surjection onto a compact metrizable zero-dimensional
space.  The chosen bases separate points in every original quotient, so
each $u_n$, and hence $u$, is constant on the fibres of
$q\times\operatorname{id}_{D\times Q}$.

Write $u_n=\bar u_n\circ(q\times\operatorname{id})$.  Every
$\bar u_n$ is upper semicontinuous.  Indeed, if
$\bar u_n(\zeta,x)<s$, compactness of the fibre $q^{-1}(\zeta)$ gives a
finite product-neighbourhood cover inside $\{u_n<s\}$, and closedness of
the compact-to-Hausdorff quotient $q$ gives a product neighbourhood of
$(\zeta,x)$ inside $\{\bar u_n<s\}$.  The same holds for
$\bar u=\inf_n\bar u_n$, since
$\{\bar u<s\}=\bigcup_n\{\bar u_n<s\}$.

Every fibre $\bar u_n(\zeta,\cdot)$ belongs to $\mathcal J_T$.
Decreasing closure of that sheaf gives the same for
$\bar u(\zeta,\cdot)$.  Moreover
\[
                              K=\bigcup_{n\geq1}K_n
\]
is countable, and
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-limit-inactive} passes to the
limit.  Thus $u\in\mathcal C_X$ near the node.  At an ordinary branch
point or a full endpoint, the same Boolean-algebra construction uses one
compact branch--polytope core and no activity set.  Hence
$\mathcal C_X$ is decreasing-sequentially closed, and minimality gives
\begin{equation}
                          \mathcal P_X\subseteq\mathcal C_X.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-minimality}
\end{equation}

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Regularization on a fixed node neighbourhood.}
Let $u\in\mathcal C_X$ near $(z_0,\eta,y_0)$ and use a factorization as in
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-factorization} on
$C\times U\times V_0$, with finite exceptional set $A$ and common
countable activity set $K$.  Shrink the exceptional collars once and choose
a compact closed standard core $D\subset U$.  Choose a compact convex
polytope $Q\subset V_0$ and a relatively open convex neighbourhood
$V'\ni y_0$ with $\overline{V'}^{\,P}\subset Q$.  Fix a smaller standard
neighbourhood $U'$ whose closure lies in the interior of $D$.  All
subsequent approximants will be defined on the single product
$C\times U'\times V'$.

Collapse every full branch outside $A\cup K$ to the node.  The resulting
compact exact-star core $D_K$ is metrizable, and $\bar u$ factors through
$Y\times D_K\times Q$.  Give this compact product a compatible metric
$d$.  For $k\geq1$, put
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_k(\xi)=\max_{\omega\in Y\times D_K\times Q}
       \bigl(\max\{\bar u(\omega),-k\}-k\,d(\xi,\omega)\bigr).
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-upper-regularization}
\end{equation}
Then $\Phi_k$ is finite and continuous and
\begin{equation}
          \Phi_{k+1}\leq\Phi_k,
   \qquad \Phi_k\geq\bar u,
   \qquad \Phi_k\downarrow\bar u.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-upper-limit}
\end{equation}

For $\zeta\in Y$, apply the exact comparison envelope only in the
$D_K\times Q$ variables:
\begin{equation}
 c_k(\zeta,\cdot)
    =C^{\mathrm{ex}}\bigl(\Phi_k(\zeta,\cdot)\bigr).
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-fibre-envelope}
\end{equation}
The envelope lemma and the insertion lemma give
\begin{equation}
 \bar u\leq c_k\leq\Phi_k,
 \qquad c_{k+1}\leq c_k,
 \qquad c_k\downarrow\bar u.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-envelope-limit}
\end{equation}
The nonexpanding estimate for $C^{\mathrm{ex}}$ and the uniform continuity
of $\Phi_k$ show that $c_k$ is jointly continuous on
$Y\times D_K\times Q$.

Choose nested finite clopen partitions $\mathscr Q_k$ of $Y$ whose meshes
tend to zero.  For $R\in\mathscr Q_k$, define
\begin{equation}
 a_{k,R}(x)=\max_{\zeta\in R}c_k(\zeta,x),
 \qquad
 a_k(\zeta,x)=a_{k,R_k(\zeta)}(x).
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-clopen-max}
\end{equation}
Each $a_{k,R}$ is a finite continuous comparison function.  Branchwise
joint convexity and the junction inequalities survive a supremum.  The
exact endpoint trace survives because the family is jointly continuous on
the compact terminal prism.  Lift $a_{k,R}$ to $D\times Q$ by making every
branch outside $A\cup K$ equal to its node slice; the two-stage barycentre
calculation in
\Cref{lem:p071-arbitrary-exact-star-polyhedral-insertion} shows that the
lift is still an exact comparison function.  Refinement and
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-envelope-limit} give
\begin{equation}
                              a_{k+1}\leq a_k,
                 \qquad      a_k\downarrow\bar u.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-clopen-limit}
\end{equation}
For the upper limit, choose a maximizing point in $R_k(\zeta)$ and use its
convergence to $\zeta$ together with
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-upper-limit}; this also covers
the value $-\infty$.

Fix positive numbers $\varepsilon_k$ with
$\sum_k\varepsilon_k<\infty$.  For every $k$, apply
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-one-step} to the finite family
$\{a_{k,R}:R\in\mathscr Q_k\}$.  On the one fixed domain
$U'\times V'$ this gives generators $g_{k,R}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
                   a_{k,R}\leq g_{k,R}
                       \leq a_{k,R}+6\varepsilon_k.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-family-smoothing}
\end{equation}
Define
\[
 w_k(z,x)=g_{k,R}(x)
 \quad\text{when }q(z)\in R\in\mathscr Q_k.
\]
The inverse images $q^{-1}(R)$ form a finite clopen partition of $C$, so
$w_k\in\mathcal G_{X,0}(C\times U'\times V')$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-clopen-limit} and
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-family-smoothing} give
\[
                         w_{k+1}\leq w_k+6\varepsilon_{k+1}.
\]
Set
\[
             \tau_k=6\sum_{j>k}\varepsilon_j,
             \qquad G_k=w_k+\tau_k.
\]
Then $G_{k+1}\leq G_k$ and
$G_k\downarrow u$ on $C\times U'\times V'$.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{The remaining strata.}
At a full endpoint, use a compact terminal branch--polytope core.  The
terminal insertion operator and the partial-Legendre smoothing in the
proof of \Cref{thm:p071-infinite-star-polyhedral-collapse} give the same
nonexpanding fibre envelope and a uniform one-step estimate, now with
constant $5$.  Repeating the compact-metric regularization, finite clopen
partition, and summable-tail argument above gives one decreasing generator
sequence on a fixed terminal product neighbourhood.  At an ordinary point
of a branch, a smaller branch--polytope chart is one convex polyhedral
model, so \Cref{thm:p071-stone-polyhedral-collapse} gives the required
fixed-neighbourhood sequence directly.

We have proved $\mathcal C_X\subseteq\mathcal P_{X,1}$ at every point.
Together with \eqref{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-minimality} and the
tautological inclusion $\mathcal P_{X,1}\subseteq\mathcal P_X$, this
proves \eqref{eq:p071-stone-infinite-polyhedral-collapse} and the stated
fixed-neighbourhood assertion.
\end{proof}
