## Dominated divisorial approximation

We use analytic resolution and principalization of coherent ideals and the
ordinary divisorial-density theorem [MP24, Theorem 2.3.6].  The latter is
used only to lift a semivaluation across a modification; the order inequality
is proved separately.

### Step 1 — lifts, including proper support

Let `pi:Y -> X` be a proper bimeromorphic morphism of connected compact
Kähler manifolds.  Pullback gives a continuous map
`pi^beth:Y^beth -> X^beth`.  Since `Y^beth` is compact and `X^beth` is
Hausdorff, its image is closed.  By birational invariance of divisorial
valuations [MP24, Remark 2.3.2], every member of `X^div` is in that image.
The density theorem therefore gives `pi^beth(Y^beth)=X^beth`.  Thus every
`v in X^beth` has a lift `v_tilde in Y^beth`.

This is only an existence claim.  It does not attempt to extend the
valuation-only bijection of [MP24, Proposition 2.1.6] by subtracting
infinite values.

### Step 2 — monomial domination on all coherent ideals

Let `B_1,...,B_k` be distinct smooth SNC prime divisors on `Y`, assume
`v_tilde(B_i)>0`, and choose rational weights
`0<a_i<=v_tilde(B_i)`.  Infinite values are allowed; here
`v_tilde(B_i)` is the value of `O_Y(-B_i)`.  Let
`Z=Z_Y(v_tilde)` be the irreducible central variety of [MP24, Lemma 2.1.1].
Its ideal is maximal among coherent ideals of positive value, so
`Z subset B_i` for all `i`.  Let `W` be the connected component of
`B_1 intersection ... intersection B_k` containing `Z`.  The monomial
valuation `q` along `W` with weights `(a_1,...,a_k)` is independent of the
chosen point of `W` by [MP24, Section 2.3.1 and Proposition 2.3.4], and is
rational divisorial by [MP24, Remark 2.3.5].

We show `q(j)<=v_tilde(j)` for every coherent ideal `j` on `Y`.  For `s>0`
set

`c_s = sum_{beta in Z_{>=0}^k, sum_i a_i beta_i >= s} O_Y(-sum_i beta_i B_i).`

After discarding redundant generators, this is a finite coherent sum: the
exponent set is upward closed and a minimal exponent satisfies
`beta_i<=ceil(s/a_i)`.  In extended-real order,

`v_tilde(c_s) = min_beta sum_{i:beta_i>0} beta_i v_tilde(B_i)
              >= min_beta sum_i beta_i a_i >= s.`

The positive-exponent restriction is what avoids an undefined product of
zero with `+infinity`.

Let `j` be nonzero and let `0<s<q(j)`.  Near a point of `W`, choose
coordinates with `B_i=(z_i=0)`.  The ideal `c_s` is generated by monomials
`z^beta` of weight at least `s`.  A holomorphic germ has monomial value at
least `s` precisely when all transverse Taylor-coefficient germs of weight
less than `s` vanish identically along `W`.  There are finitely many such
exponents, so this is exactly membership in `c_s`.  The tangent coordinates
have weight zero: the argument does not replace vanishing of a coefficient
germ along `W` by vanishing at a single point.  Point-independence and local
finite generation therefore give `j subset c_s` on a neighbourhood of `W`.

Put `Q=(j+c_s)/c_s` and `k=Ann_{O_Y}(Q)`.  These are coherent, `Q` vanishes
near `W`, and hence `k=O_Y` near `W`; moreover `k j subset c_s` globally.
We have `v_tilde(k)=0`: otherwise maximality of the central ideal would give
`k subset I_Z`, impossible because `k` is the unit ideal near `Z subset W`.
Multiplicativity and the preceding bound now give

`v_tilde(j)=v_tilde(kj)>=v_tilde(c_s)>=s.`

Letting `s` tend to `q(j)` proves `q(j)<=v_tilde(j)`.  The case `q(j)=0` is
immediate, and the zero ideal has infinite value on both sides.  Thus this
order inequality covers every coherent ideal, including ideals whose
behaviour away from `W` was removed through the annihilator.

### Step 3 — approximation on each finite set of ideals

Fix nonzero coherent ideals `a_1,...,a_l` on `X`.  Simultaneous analytic
principalization supplies a proper modification `pi:Y->X`, with `Y` smooth,
such that

`a_j O_Y=O_Y(-D_j),  D_j=sum_{i=1}^r d_{ji}B_i,  d_{ji} in Z_{>=0},`

where the distinct `B_i` form an SNC divisor.  It may be taken as a finite
sequence of blowups of smooth centres, so `Y` remains compact Kähler;
principalizing the product and resolving the pair makes each factor
invertible.

Lift `v` using Step 1 and put `w_i=v_tilde(B_i) in [0,+infinity]`.  Then

`v(a_j)=sum_{i:d_{ji}>0}d_{ji}w_i.`

Retain exactly the indices with `w_i>0`.  For each retained index choose a
finite positive rational `a_i<=w_i`; set every other `a_i=0`.  If none is
retained, take the trivial valuation.  Otherwise Step 2 yields a rational
divisorial `q_Y<=v_tilde`, and `q_X=pi^beth(q_Y)` belongs to `X^div`, obeys
`q_X<=v`, and satisfies

`q_X(a_j)=sum_{i:d_{ji}>0}d_{ji}a_i.`

Here is the v3 repair.  If `w_i=0`, then `B_i` cannot contain `W`: since
`Z subset W`, an inclusion `W subset B_i` would imply
`O_Y(-B_i) subset I_Z`.  Positivity of `v_tilde(I_Z)` and monotonicity would
then imply `w_i>0`, a contradiction.  Thus the monomial value of such a
zero-weight component is zero, exactly as required in the displayed
formula.  This direct central-ideal implication replaces the v2 appeal to
simple-normal-crossing geometry.

For each finite positive `w_i`, let `a_i` increase rationally to `w_i`; for
`w_i=+infinity`, send `a_i` to infinity.  The finite sums converge to the
formula for `v(a_j)` in `[0,+infinity]`.  More explicitly, for each integer
`N>0` choose the weights so that

- `0<=v(a_j)-q_X(a_j)<1/N` when `v(a_j)<+infinity`; and
- `q_X(a_j)>N` when `v(a_j)=+infinity`.

The zero and unit ideals already have their prescribed values.  Basic
pointwise neighbourhoods mention finitely many ideals, so this produces a
dominated rational divisorial point in every basic neighbourhood of `v`.
For a convergent net, direct pairs `(S,N)`, where `S` is a finite set of
nonzero coherent ideals and `N>=1`, by inclusion in `S` and the usual order
on `N`, and choose the preceding point for each pair.  No comparison between
two chosen points is required.  This proves the theorem.

## Comparison corollary

An MP potential is upper semicontinuous as a decreasing limit of continuous
PL functions.  For `v`, take the dominated divisorial net `q_lambda<=v`
converging to it.  Antitonicity and divisorial agreement give
`F(v)<=F(q_lambda)=phi(q_lambda)`.  Upper semicontinuity for nets yields
`F(v)<=phi(v)`, also if `phi(v)=-infinity` (apply every finite upper
threshold).  Conversely [MP24, Theorem 4.3.8] applied to `phi` and the
upper-semicontinuous `F`, together with divisorial equality, gives
`phi<=F`.  Therefore `F=phi`.

## Conditional finite-energy corollary

The finite-energy correspondence provides an MP finite-energy potential
`phi in E^1_MP([omega])` agreeing with `F_Gamma` on rational divisorial
points, by [DXZ23, Theorem 3.17], [MP24, Theorems 5.1.4 and 5.1.7, Section
5.2], and the frozen Chapter 71 normalization and divisorial-identification
interface.  At the trivial valuation both values are `c=Gamma_max`.

The raw transform is bounded above by `c`.  If `q<=v`, then
`q(I(mGamma_tau))<=v(I(mGamma_tau))` for every `m,tau`; taking the first
supremum, changing sign, and taking the second supremum gives
`F_Gamma(q)>=F_Gamma(v)`.  Under the stipulated P14.2 upper
semicontinuity, it meets the comparison corollary and hence equals `phi`
on all of `X^beth`.  This is equality for the literal ideal formula,
including semivaluations with proper support, not a replacement by a
regularization.
