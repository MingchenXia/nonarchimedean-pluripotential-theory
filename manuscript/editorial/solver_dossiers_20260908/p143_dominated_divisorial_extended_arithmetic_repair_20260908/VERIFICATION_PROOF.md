## The dominated divisorial approximation theorem

We use analytic resolution and principalization of coherent ideals, and the ordinary divisorial-density theorem [MP24, Theorem 2.3.6]. The latter is used only to lift a semivaluation through a modification; the order inequality is established separately.

### Step 1 — existence of a lift, including proper support

Let `π:Y→X` be a proper bimeromorphic morphism of connected compact Kähler manifolds. Pullback of coherent ideals gives a continuous map `π^beth:Y^beth→X^beth`. Since `Y^beth` is compact and `X^beth` is Hausdorff, the image is closed. Each point of `X^div` lies in that image by the birational invariance of divisorial valuations [MP24, Remark 2.3.2]. The ordinary divisorial density theorem then forces `π^beth(Y^beth)=X^beth`. Thus every `v∈X^beth` has a lift `v_tilde∈Y^beth`.

This is an existence statement only. It does not extend the valuation-only bijection of [MP24, Proposition 2.1.6] by any undefined subtraction of infinite values.

### Step 2 — monomial domination on every coherent ideal

Let `B_1,…,B_k` be distinct smooth SNC prime divisors on `Y`, assume `v_tilde(B_i)>0`, and choose rational weights `0<a_i≤v_tilde(B_i)`. Infinite values are allowed. Here `v_tilde(B_i)` means the value of `O_Y(-B_i)`.

Let `Z=Z_Y(v_tilde)` be the irreducible central variety from [MP24, Lemma 2.1.1]. Its ideal is maximal among coherent ideals of positive value. Hence `Z⊂B_i` for every `i`. There is a unique connected component `W` of `B_1∩…∩B_k` containing `Z`, because that intersection is smooth with disjoint connected components. Let `q` be the monomial valuation along `W` with weights `(a_1,…,a_k)`. Its construction and independence of the chosen point of `W` are in [MP24, Section 2.3.1 and Proposition 2.3.4]. Since the weights are rational, `q` is divisorial with the rational scaling permitted in the statement [MP24, Remark 2.3.5].

We prove `q(j)≤v_tilde(j)` for every coherent ideal `j` on `Y`. For `s>0`, define the monomial ideal

`c_s=sum_{β∈Z_{≥0}^k, sum_i a_iβ_i≥s}O_Y(-sum_iβ_iB_i).`

After redundant generators are discarded this is a finite coherent sum: the exponent set is upward closed, and a minimal exponent has `β_i≤ceil(s/a_i)`. The semivaluation rules give, in extended-real order,

`v_tilde(c_s)=min_β sum_{i:β_i>0}β_i v_tilde(B_i) ≥ min_β sum_iβ_i a_i ≥s.`

The restriction to positive exponents is precisely the stated extended-value convention.

Take a nonzero coherent ideal `j` and `0<s<q(j)`. Near any point of `W`, take coordinates in which `B_i=(z_i=0)`. The ideal `c_s` is generated by monomials `z^β` of weight at least `s`. A holomorphic germ has monomial value at least `s` iff every transverse Taylor coefficient of weight less than `s` vanishes identically as a germ along `W`. There are finitely many such exponents, so this is exactly membership in `c_s`. The coordinates tangent to `W` have zero weight; vanishing at one point of `W` is not being substituted for vanishing of the corresponding coefficient germ. Point-independence of the monomial valuation and local finite generation of `j` therefore give `j⊂c_s` on a neighbourhood of `W`.

This inclusion need not be global if the divisor intersection has other connected components. Put `Q=(j+c_s)/c_s` and `k=Ann_{O_Y}(Q)`. They are coherent. Since `Q` vanishes near `W`, `k=O_Y` there, while `k j⊂c_s` globally. Also `v_tilde(k)=0`: if it were positive, the maximal-central-ideal property would give `k⊂I_Z`, impossible because `k` is the unit ideal near `Z⊂W`. Multiplicativity and the preceding lower bound now yield

`v_tilde(j)=v_tilde(kj)≥v_tilde(c_s)≥s.`

Letting `s` tend to `q(j)` proves `q(j)≤v_tilde(j)`. If `q(j)=0`, the inequality is immediate; the zero ideal has infinite value on both sides. Thus the domination holds for every coherent ideal, including those whose behaviour away from `W` was removed by the annihilator.

### Step 3 — approximation on any finite set of ideals

Fix nonzero coherent ideals `a_1,…,a_l` on `X`. Simultaneous analytic principalization supplies a proper modification `π:Y→X`, with `Y` smooth, such that `a_jO_Y=O_Y(-D_j)`, where `D_j=sum_{i=1}^rd_{ji}B_i`, `d_{ji}∈Z_{≥0}`, and the distinct `B_i` form an SNC divisor.

The modification can be a finite sequence of blowups of smooth centres, so `Y` remains compact Kähler. One may principalize the product and resolve the pair; locally, invertibility of a product of nonzero ideal factors yields invertibility of the individual factors.

Lift `v` to `v_tilde` by Step 1 and set `w_i=v_tilde(B_i)∈[0,+∞]`. Multiplicativity gives

`v(a_j)=sum_{i:d_{ji}>0}d_{ji}w_i.`

Retain exactly the indices with `w_i>0`. Choose a finite positive rational `a_i≤w_i` for each retained index, and put `a_i=0` for all others. If none is retained, use the trivial valuation. Otherwise, Step 2 produces a rational divisorial `q_Y≤v_tilde`; its pushforward `q_X=π^beth(q_Y)` lies in `X^div`, satisfies `q_X≤v`, and has

`q_X(a_j)=sum_{i:d_{ji}>0}d_{ji}a_i.`

Indeed, a component with `w_i=0` cannot contain the generic point of the selected stratum, for otherwise SNC would fail there; its monomial value is therefore zero.

For finite positive `w_i`, increase the rational `a_i` to `w_i` from below. For `w_i=+∞`, send `a_i` to infinity. The finite sums then converge to the displayed formula for `v(a_j)` in `[0,+∞]`. For each integer `N>0`, choose the weights so that

- `0≤v(a_j)-q_X(a_j)<1/N` when `v(a_j)<+∞`; and
- `q_X(a_j)>N` when `v(a_j)=+∞`.

The zero and unit ideals already have their prescribed values. Basic neighbourhoods in the pointwise topology use finitely many ideals, so we have a dominated rational divisorial point in every basic neighbourhood of `v`. For an explicit convergent net, direct the pairs `(S,N)`, with `S` a finite set of nonzero coherent ideals and `N≥1`, by inclusion in `S` and the usual order on `N`, and choose the point above for each pair. No comparison between two different chosen points is required. This proves the theorem.

## Proof of the comparison corollary

MP potentials are upper semicontinuous as decreasing limits of continuous PL functions. Fix `v` and take dominated divisorial points `q_λ≤v` converging to it. Antitonicity and divisorial agreement give `F(v)≤F(q_λ)=φ(q_λ)`. Upper semicontinuity of `φ` for nets gives `F(v)≤φ(v)`. This remains valid when `φ(v)=-∞`: every real upper threshold eventually bounds `φ(q_λ)`, so it also bounds `F(v)`.

For the reverse inequality apply [MP24, Theorem 4.3.8] to the MP potential `φ` and the upper-semicontinuous function `F`. Equality on `X^div` yields `φ≤F` everywhere. The two inequalities give `F=φ`.

## Proof of the conditional finite-energy corollary

The finite-energy correspondence supplies an MP finite-energy potential `φ∈E^1_MP([ω])` agreeing with `F_Γ` at all rational divisorial points. This uses [DXZ23, Theorem 3.17] and [MP24, Theorems 5.1.4 and 5.1.7, and Section 5.2], together with the normalization and divisorial-identification interface in frozen Chapter 71, equations (71.39)–(71.40). At the trivial valuation both values equal `c=Γ_max`.

The raw transform is bounded above by `c`. If `q≤v`, then `q(I(mΓ_τ))≤v(I(mΓ_τ))` for every `m,τ`. Taking the first supremum, changing sign, and then taking the second supremum gives `F_Γ(q)≥F_Γ(v)`. By the stipulated P14.2 conclusion, `F_Γ` is upper semicontinuous. It thus meets the comparison corollary, and equals `φ` throughout `X^beth`. The equality is for the literal ideal formula, including semivaluations supported on a proper subvariety; it does not replace that formula by a regularization.
