## Topological and integral conventions

The field K is still complete and nontrivially real-valued. Its norm topology and rank-one ring K-circ are fixed. The additional valuation ring K-plus contained in K-circ may have higher rank; k=K-circ/K-topologically-nilpotent and V=K-plus/K-topologically-nilpotent. The analytic base Spa(K,K-plus) has the nongeneric prime chain of K-plus. Choosing K-plus changes the integral adic data; it does not replace the real norm by a real-valued higher-rank valuation.

Section 3 separates several recovery interfaces. Proposition 3.3 gives full faithfulness for partially proper objects under rank-one base change. Proposition 3.5 preserves the topological rings and ordinary structure sheaf on the rank-one locus, but localizes the integral subring; it does not identify all integral sheaves. The reflection statements in 3.8 require partially proper maps; 3.9 additionally retains the local positive weak finite-type assumptions. The optional single-localization hypothesis of 3.4 is not assumed universally. Proposition 3.10 glues admissible formal models from their rank-one models and compatible flat, locally finitely presented V-reductions. Smoothness and properness of these reductions are tested over Spec(V), not only over its generic field k. Theorem 3.12 is an equivalence for proper finite-type adic objects, with the precise relative base assumptions of that theorem. It does not prescribe a unique smooth integral model for every recovered proper analytic object.

These proofs use Huber's adic foundations, formal-model and blowup theorems, Fujiwara–Kato formal geometry, Nagata compactification, and valuation-ring flatness. ## Uniformization and model hypotheses

An abeloid is smooth, proper and geometrically connected as an analytic group; projectivity is an additional condition. Theorem 4.7 passes to a finite separable affinoid extension (L,L-plus), retaining L-plus intersect K=K-plus. Its functorial Raynaud cross has a split torus, a closed full lattice and a **generically good** abeloid quotient. Its universal-cover characterization uses vanishing of analytic-site H1 with constant Z coefficients, not étale simple connectedness. Classical rank-one uniformization and duality remain attributed external inputs. Theorem 6.9 distinguishes generically good from good reduction over the entire higher-rank integral base: the torus pushout initially covers the open subgroup arising from the integral model, and extends to the full Raynaud extension; it equals that extension exactly in the good-reduction case specified there.

The three Néron notions must remain separate:

- Section 5's full algebraic Néron model maps all smooth algebraic test schemes and need not be quasi-compact. Proposition 5.3 supplies a marked model for a semiabelian scheme over a normal integral base. The extension comparison in 5.5 requires a regular local base or a valuation ring with separably closed fraction field, and an abelian generic fiber; it is not an unrestricted semiabelian statement.
- Theorem 5.7 assumes V Henselian and every nongeneric residue field algebraically closed. Its gluing of translates gives a full locally finite-type Néron model after the required finite separable extension. Theorems 5.10–5.11 give additional algebraically closed fraction-field cases and component-group consequences. They do not solve arbitrary-valuation full Néron existence without these hypotheses.
- Definition 6.3's marked formal model models an open analytic subgroup and has fiberwise connected formal fibers; its tests are pointed smooth quasi-separated fiberwise connected formal schemes. A strong marked model is connected, allows more than one fiber component, and tests pointed smooth connected formal schemes. Its maximal connected open subgroup need not be the whole proper abeloid or the completion of a full algebraic Néron model. Theorem 6.7 supplies a marked model after finite separable affinoid extension; its strong conclusion assumes k algebraically closed or V a DVR. Theorem 6.12's finite-flat multiplication assertion has its stated algebraic/formal algebraic-closedness premises; it is not asserted for arbitrary DVR Néron models.

Example 6.14 assumes K algebraically closed for its full classification. In its totally degenerate case the algebraic component group is the value group of the localized K-plus modulo the period value; the strong formal component group is the value group of the residual localized valuation ring. Those formulas concern different models. In the rank-one algebraically closed Tate example the latter group is zero, whereas the algebraic group can be Q modulo the period value. Neither formula identifies all real points of the Berkovich skeleton with algebraic components.

For K=Q-p((t)) with its real t-adic norm, compose the t-order with the p-order of the first nonzero coefficient. The resulting lexicographic value is w(f)=(ord-t(f), ord-p(first coefficient)). Its integral ring is Z-p+t Q-p[[t]], contained in K-circ=Q-p[[t]], with residual valuation ring V=Z-p. Its prime chain is (0) strictly contained in t Q-p[[t]] strictly contained in p Z-p+t Q-p[[t]]. Thus Spec(K-plus) has three points and Spa(K,K-plus) has two. The intermediate ideal is not finitely generated: for any finite nonzero generators choose t/p^N with value smaller than their minimum. A real ordered embedding of the full lexicographic group is impossible: the positive image of (1,0) would have to exceed every positive integer multiple of the image of (0,1). The rank-one coarsening instead kills the second direction.

For a prime p>=5, put a=1/(1728p−1) and take y²=x³+3ax+2a. Direct symbolic computation gives Delta=−1728²p/(1728p−1)³ and j=1/p. Delta is a unit in Q-p[[t]], so this equation supplies good t-adic reduction. But w(j)=(0,−1), so j is not integral over K-plus. The standard necessary j-integrality criterion for good elliptic reduction excludes good reduction over that integral ring. Only this necessary attributed criterion is used; the direct calculation is not a proof of the general elliptic reduction criterion or the source's algebraically closed classification. Its V=Z-p satisfies the DVR alternative in 6.7, after the required extension. Its closed residue field F-p is not algebraically closed, so 5.7 is not applied unchanged.
