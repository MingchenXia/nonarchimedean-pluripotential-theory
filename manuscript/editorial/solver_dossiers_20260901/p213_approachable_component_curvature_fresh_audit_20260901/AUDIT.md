## 3. First-stage approximation is sufficient and is not full CPsh

The exact definition at lines 9–11 requires one ordinary neighborhood on which the entire sequence is defined. It correctly excludes a collection of approximants on unrelated successively shrinking domains. On a smaller relatively compact neighborhood, compact-uniform convergence is uniform on its closure, so the two formulations used in this note agree in the present good separated analytic setting.

The corrected error argument is sound. After removing any zero-error case, choose a subsequence with e_(j+1)<e_j/3. For g_j=f_j+2e_j, the inequalities displayed at line 11 give g_(j+1)<=g_j, and g_j converges uniformly to f. Adding real constants preserves smooth psh. Thus every member of A is locally a decreasing limit of smooth psh functions, and also belongs to the continuous uniform-closure sheaf CPsh. These are exactly the aspects of CLD local approachability needed below.

This argument starts with a direct smooth sequence. It does not show that a function at an arbitrary successor or limit stage of the CPsh hierarchy has such a sequence. The note and ledger explicitly decline to use the hierarchy-collapse assertion in CLD Proposition 8.1.11. The proof reviewed here has no hidden use of that assertion: the direct first-stage hypothesis is used for analytic nullity, while the minimal-sheaf principle is used separately for positivity in Section 4.1.

The distinction matters even though the final formula involves only one derivative dd'': continuity of u_j times a positive current gives weak convergence of curvature, but weak convergence alone does not preserve zero mass on a fixed closed set. Therefore a hypothetical iteration of the nullity claim through the full CPsh hierarchy would require additional uniform control not supplied by this argument.

## 4. The actual Corollary 16.9.4 import

CLD Corollary 16.9.4, printed page 467, is an analytic-nullity theorem for locally approachable continuous potentials on a boundaryless n-space. In Chapter 16, an n-space means a topologically separated pure n-dimensional analytic space; it does not mean a quasi-smooth or reduced space. The present assumptions satisfy the goodness and separation interfaces needed in its proof. There is no hypothesis of a nontrivial absolute value, discreteness, residue characteristic zero, or algebraic closure in this theorem.

Only the one-potential case m=1 is required. The coefficient test degree is n−1. The exceptional analytic set is allowed to have dimension n−1, not just <=n−2. This is substantially stronger for these particular curvature coefficients than the dimension-only support argument in the preceding scalar-classification packet. It does not say that every positive closed (1,1)-current has zero mass on every divisor.

I checked the route from the primary source rather than inferring this statement from a generic slogan about pluripolar sets. CLD 16.9.2 applies the weighted integrability theorem of 8.12, and 16.9.3 turns logarithmic integrability into zero polar mass. Locally, prime avoidance gives an analytic f vanishing on the chosen proper analytic set but on no top-dimensional component. Canonical local integrability of log|f| is precisely the input used in the source proof; it is compatible with canonical multiplicities and does not presume geometric reducedness. Corollary 16.9.4 follows using its polar set.

There is a useful logical safeguard in the frozen note's line 39. The printed last paragraph of 16.9.4 mentions continuous psh truncations. A merely decreasing sequence of arbitrary continuous CPsh functions would not, on its own, establish *first-stage smooth* approachability. The note additionally requires smooth convex approximation of these particular truncations, which is sufficient here. To check that this is a real special construction, one can use a fixed smooth convex nondecreasing regularized maximum chi of t and 0, with derivative between 0 and 1, equal to 0 for t sufficiently negative and equal to t for t sufficiently positive. The functions chi(log|f|+R)−R are smooth on the invertibility locus and extend constantly near f=0; for increasing R they decrease to log|f|. Their smoothness uses moment coordinates only where f is invertible. This is a direct common-neighborhood sequence for this logarithm, not an appeal to the full hierarchy. Thus the note's stated extra construction covers the needed source interface without importing the disputed general collapse.

The nontrivial estimate remains an external theorem. In Proposition 8.4.3 the logarithmic surrogate and the other potentials are smooth on the required neighborhoods. The argument in 8.12 keeps a fixed smooth negative approximant of the singular potential while passing the continuous potentials' uniform smooth approximation; it then uses monotone convergence for the singular sequence. This review checked those approximation hypotheses and the use of the resulting estimate; it does not claim a new proof or extension of weighted CLN to every CPsh function.

An upstream arithmetic caveat must be disclosed. CLD 8.4.2 omits the second term in beta_k when k=n, so beta_n=r^(n+a), with r=−rho on the negative sublevel. At this endpoint its displayed factor 2(k+a) in the derivative formulas is incorrect: d''beta_n=−(n+a)r^(n+a−1)d''rho, and dd''beta_n=(n+a)[−r^(n+a−1)dd''rho+(n+a−1)r^(n+a−2)d'r wedge d''r]. The factor is 2 only in the non-endpoint calculation. This was independently checked by differentiation after the author drew attention to it while this report was still unfrozen. The frozen auditee does not assert or use these derivative identities as new equalities; it explicitly imports the weighted-integrability/nullity theorem in its stated first-stage scope. The endpoint typo is not a reason to turn that import into a full-CPsh claim, and this report does not certify every printed equality in the proof of the imported theorem. A rederivation of the estimate must retain the endpoint factor 1 (and may enlarge a harmless upper-bound constant). No new general weighted-estimate packet is an input to this audit.

The arbitrary-field assertion is consequently sound for (I) and the source-nullity specialization of Section 4.1. In particular, no discretely valued uniformizer, strict-affinoid basis over a trivial field, regular-locus argument, or algebraization is inserted into this proof. ## 5. Ordinary component geometry and canonical weights

Lines 47–57 use the right type of component. These are the actual reduced analytic irreducible components over the original complete K, not geometrically irreducible components after an extension. Ducros's component family is G-locally finite: every affinoid domain meets finitely many members. Goodness upgrades this to finiteness near every compact test support, by a finite affinoid-neighborhood cover. It does not require global finiteness, countability, or paracompactness.

For each i, the union of all other components is closed analytic. Distinct components are incomparable, and a proper analytic closed subset of an irreducible component has smaller dimension, by the primary component/dimension results. Consequently B_i=X_i intersect union_(j!=i) X_j is closed analytic both in X_i and in X, and has dimension <n. The family is locally finite on compact sets, so B=union_i B_i is likewise closed analytic with local dimension <n. No argument here uses regularity of X_i, smoothness over K, or geometric connectedness. This remains valid over imperfect fields.

The closed immersion j_i is finite, topologically proper, and boundaryless relative to X. Its source is good, separated, usual-strict and pure n-dimensional; the relative-boundary formula gives absolute boundarylessness from that of X. For compact pullback tests, closedness of X_i in the Hausdorff space X also verifies topological properness directly. CLD's associated tropical structure and Stokes give positivity and separate d'- and d''-closedness of the integration current on a possibly singular pure component. Proper pushforward preserves these properties.

The normalization of delta_X at lines 53–57 is correct and essential. The source formula is delta_X=delta_[X]=sum_i m_i (j_i)_*delta_(X_i,red), where m_i is the positive generic local length. This is not replaced by the reduced-space integration current. In the proof of CLD 16.6.3, the reciprocal calibrated degree 1/m_i of the reduction immersion is exactly what gives the factor m_i in canonical integration. The current decomposition used here is therefore compatible with both reduced and nonreduced X over the full field range of (I).

The proof does not need to identify all currents on a nilpotent thickening with currents on its reduction. It only uses the already established fundamental-cycle current formula, continuous multiplication of its positive summands, and ordinary differentiation. Possible embedded nilpotent structure does not introduce a new top-dimensional canonical summand beyond this formula. Nor does the proof need to pull an unknown current through normalization or a singular immersion.

## 6. Proper coefficient pushforward and signed total variation

Lines 59–76 correctly supply the actual current and measure comparison. Pullback of a smooth psh function through j_i is smooth psh: ordinary pullback commutes with the two differentials and preserves positivity. A uniform sequence remains uniform on an inverse-image neighborhood, and compact-uniform convergence is preserved because a compact set has compact image. Thus w in A(X) restricts to the required A-function on X_i, even though X_i need not be smooth.

For S_i^w=dd''(w delta_i), continuous multiplication commutes with pushforward of the positive integration current. Proper pullback of test forms and commutation of differentials then identify S_i^w with j_i*dd''[w|X_i]. This is the same projection identity as CLD 8.5.6 and can also be checked directly on the distributional pairing. Only a function and ordinary test forms are pulled back; there is no postulated pullback of S_i^w itself.

For a compact ordinary (n−1,n−1)-form alpha, j_i*alpha is compactly supported. Testing against a smooth scalar multiplier phi gives the pushforward identity for the coefficient measure. Positive currents admit the required Radon coefficient measures; for general symmetric alpha, compact local decomposition into a difference of positive forms produces signed Radon measures. Smooth compact scalar tests are dense in continuous compact tests on these good separated ordinary domains, so equality on the former identifies the measures. This excludes a possible confusion between distributional identity and equality of Borel coefficients.

The nullity needed in the proof is absolute, not just signed cancellation. For positive alpha the source coefficient is positive and has zero mass on B_i by Corollary 16.9.4. For a symmetric alpha=alpha_+−alpha_−, its coefficient's total variation is bounded by the sum of the two positive coefficient measures. The antisymmetric part has zero pairing against the symmetric current. Compact partitions and cutoffs extend the local reasoning to all compact ordinary forms without assuming global paracompactness. The applicable cone is the positive-form cone from CLD's compact decomposition, not an unsupported assertion that every form is a difference of two strongly positive forms.

Finally, for a proper pushforward of signed measures, |j_i*mu| is bounded by j_i*|mu|. Since the inverse image of the ambient crossing B_i is precisely its closed subset on X_i, zero total variation passes to S_i^w wedge alpha on X. The note applies this separately to u and v. It never assumes their difference is positive, and never substitutes mu(B_i)=0 for |mu|(B_i)=0.

The case n=1 is included: the tests in this paragraph have degree (0,0), and the crossing sets have dimension zero. The primary analytic-nullity theorem still applies, so the argument does not require the n>=2 scalar-classification theorem in this case.

## 7. The support argument proves (I) without classification

For a continuous equal-canonical-curvature pair, linearity of compactly locally finite sums gives sum_i m_i R_i=0. The support reduction in lines 84–88 is exact. R_i is zero off X_i because it is the derivative of a multiplier of a current supported in X_i. On the ordinary open complement of all other components, the canonical current is m_i delta_i, and hence m_i R_i=0 there. As m_i>0, R_i vanishes there too. Those two ordinary opens cover X minus B_i. Therefore Supp(R_i) is contained in B_i.

This is not an assertion that the different component currents are linearly independent on the crossing. It does not divide a weighted current identity pointwise at a shared point. It only isolates a component on the open where all others are absent.

For each compact test alpha, R_i wedge alpha is a signed Radon measure. The current vanishes on the open complement of B_i, so smooth-test density implies that this coefficient measure is zero there. Its total variation on B_i is zero by the separate nullity statements for S_i^u and S_i^v. It is therefore zero as a measure. Pairing with a compact smooth scalar cutoff equal to one near Supp(alpha) recovers <R_i,alpha>=0. Testing all alpha proves R_i=0. Conversely componentwise equality implies canonical equality by the same locally finite weighted sum.

This is an actual source-current proof: neither characteristic-skeleton adjacency nor a target-only tropical pushforward is being used to identify the remaining current. Every ordinary compact test is accounted for. The new divisorial conclusion follows from analytic-nullity of these curvature coefficients, not from an incorrect test-support dimension bound at degree n−1.

## 8. Section 4.1: full CPsh positivity and the stronger criterion

The new section is mathematically stronger than (I) and remains justified. Its key source is the proof, not merely the statement, of CLD Lemma 8.5.5. Fix any positive closed current T on an ordinary domain and let P be the subsheaf of continuous functions w for which dd''(wT) is positive. It contains smooth psh functions. It is stable under uniform limits because continuous multiplication of a positive current is continuous under locally uniform convergence, differentiation is continuous on currents, and the positive cone is closed. Thus the defining minimality of CPsh puts the *whole* CPsh sheaf inside P. This argument does not assert that a general member possesses a direct smooth sequence.

Apply that lemma on X with T=delta_i. It gives positive closed S_i^u and S_i^v for arbitrary continuous CPsh u,v without first restricting these potentials to a component or assuming any current pullback. Multiplying and differentiating the canonical decomposition yields S=sum_j m_j S_j^u=sum_j m_j S_j^v. On a fixed compact support these are finite sums. For positive alpha, the complementary sum after removing m_i S_i^u is positive. Therefore S wedge alpha−m_i(S_i^u wedge alpha) is a positive measure, and likewise for v.

The inequalities in (4.4) are consequently genuine positive Radon-measure inequalities, not formal comparisons of densities on a chosen chart. Nonnegative smooth scalar testing proves them distributionally; nonnegative continuous compact testing follows using smooth density with compact cutoffs, giving the asserted Borel-measure domination. The constants are correctly 1/m_i, with the actual generic multiplicities still present.

If the positive coefficients of S give zero mass to B, domination gives zero mass to B_i for each positive component coefficient. Decomposition into positive forms supplies signed total-variation nullity for every coefficient. The support argument in Section 7 needed only continuous multipliers and equality of canonical curvature, so it applies unchanged. Thus all R_i vanish. This proves the criterion under exactly its stated extra hypothesis and does not use the weighted CLN estimate for general CPsh potentials.

The two useful sufficient conditions at line 111 also check out. If one of the original potentials is locally in A, then its curvature is S, and the primary theorem supplies B-nullity directly. If S has an ordinary-local A-potential a that is not one of u,v, the same argument applies to a. For each compact alpha, a finite subordinate smooth partition reduces the measure statement to such neighborhoods. All local restrictions of canonical integration use the same calibration, by CLD 15.7.2. On nonreduced X the source theorem is applicable as stated; alternatively, applying first-stage nullity on the reduced components and summing with the m_i gives the identical conclusion. Thus neither sufficient condition silently imposes reducedness.

For S=0, positivity alone already forces every S_i^u and S_i^v to vanish. This limiting example in line 113 is correct and requires no analytic-nullity theorem.

The accepted extension is a *pair/common-curvature criterion*. In particular, an arbitrary second CPsh member is allowed when the first is locally in A. This does not imply that the arbitrary second member lies in A, does not prove that CPsh equals its first stage, and does not imply that an arbitrary common curvature has a local A-potential. Those possible inferences would be invalid; the frozen note makes none of them.
