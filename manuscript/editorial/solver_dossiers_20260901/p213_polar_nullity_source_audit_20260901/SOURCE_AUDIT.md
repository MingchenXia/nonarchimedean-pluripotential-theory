# Source audit: polar nullity and component curvature cancellation

Date: 1 September 2026.

## 1. Verdict

CLD Corollary 16.9.4 DOES directly exclude lower-dimensional analytic mass for continuous psh potentials that are locally approchable. It DOES NOT state the same result for arbitrary CPsh potentials without that qualification. Definitions 8.1.7, 8.1.8 and 8.1.13 do not identify the two classes, and the proof in Section 8.12 begins by choosing local smooth approximating sequences on a common neighbourhood.

The component-curvature deduction is rigorous once the common positive ambient curvature has zero trace mass on the component crossings. This hypothesis holds if AT LEAST ONE of the two continuous CPsh potentials is locally approchable. It holds, in particular, if both are locally uniform limits of smooth psh functions. This deduction does not need scalar-current classification, normalization, residue characteristic zero, or a nontrivial valuation.

The extension to two arbitrary CPsh potentials is not obtained merely from weak continuity or from the definition of the uniform-closure sheaf. An additional weighted polar estimate is required. A separate new proof of that estimate is being developed at root's request; it is NOT an input theorem or an accepted result of this source audit.

## 4. Original-field and component interfaces

For the following consequence let K be ANY complete non-Archimedean field and X be good, separated, strict and boundaryless, pure of dimension n>=1. Strictness may be read in CLD's convention; the usual strict class is a safe smaller class even when the valuation is trivial. Goodness is retained because the local-barrier proposition uses it. No smoothness, normality or reducedness of X is needed here.

Let X_i be its reduced analytic irreducible components, j_i:X_i->X the closed immersions, and delta_i=j_i*delta_(X_i). The canonical current is locally the finite sum

    delta_X = sum_i m_i delta_i,   m_i>0.

Compact subsets meet only finitely many components in this good setting. The existing component note supplies the locally finite indexing; CLD 16.6.3 supplies the generic-multiplicity normalization.

Analytic pullback by j_i preserves the smooth form operations and positivity. Consequently smooth psh functions pull back to smooth psh functions, and a local uniform smooth approximation pulls back to one on the inverse image ordinary open. This uses the given analytic map, not an unknown-current pullback through normalization or scalar extension.

A closed immersion is finite, topologically proper and has no relative boundary. The component spaces inherit boundarylessness from X. Their pure dimension is n. Thus the positive integration currents and the proper-pushforward formula in CLD 8.5.6 apply to their continuous CPsh potentials.

For any continuous CPsh h on X, put

    A_i(h)=dd(h delta_i).

These are positive closed currents of bidegree (1,1). Their positive order-zero nature follows from 8.5.5 (equivalently from component pullback and 8.5.6), not from arbitrary derivatives of Radon measures being measures. Here dd=d'd''.

## 5. Classification-free cancellation lemma

Let u,v be real continuous CPsh functions on X and suppose

    S=dd[u]=dd[v],  [u]=u delta_X.

Put R_i=A_i(u)-A_i(v), and let B_i be the intersection of X_i with the union of the other components. Local finiteness and the positive m_i give

    S=sum_i m_i A_i(u)=sum_i m_i A_i(v).

As in Section 6 of the frozen component note, R_i vanishes outside B_i: outside X_i it is zero by support, while on the complement of all other components delta_X=m_i delta_i and the common-curvature equality gives m_i R_i=0. This reasoning takes place on ordinary opens of X.

Assume that for every compactly supported positive smooth (n−1,n−1)-form alpha, the positive trace measure

    mu_alpha = S wedge alpha

has zero mass on the component crossings in its support. This condition is local, so finitely many components suffice for each alpha. Define

    lambda_i^u=A_i(u) wedge alpha,
    lambda_i^v=A_i(v) wedge alpha.

They are positive Radon measures. Positivity of every summand in the displayed decomposition gives the genuine measure inequalities

    0 <= lambda_i^u <= mu_alpha/m_i,
    0 <= lambda_i^v <= mu_alpha/m_i.

Hence both measures vanish on B_i. Their difference nu_i=R_i wedge alpha has total variation bounded by lambda_i^u+lambda_i^v, so its total variation is zero on B_i as well. On the other hand its support lies in B_i. Therefore nu_i=0, and in particular <R_i,alpha>=0.

Every symmetric compact test form is the difference of two positive compact test forms, by CLD 6.5.2; antisymmetric forms pair trivially with symmetric R_i. Thus R_i=0 as a current.

This proof does not use injectivity of proper pushforward. If one instead applies 16.9.4 on X_i, the relevant positive trace is j_i*(dd[u_i] wedge j_i*alpha); proper pushforward carries zero mass on j_i^(-1)(B_i) to zero mass on B_i. For a signed trace the total-variation bound, not just a signed integral equal to zero, is the correct statement.

### Direct source consequence

If AT LEAST ONE of u and v is locally approchable, Corollary 16.9.4 on X itself gives the required nullity of the common S on B_i, because dim B_i<n. Thus all R_i vanish. The other potential need only be a continuous CPsh function, so its component curvatures are positive.

In particular the desired componentwise equality holds when both potentials are locally uniform smooth-psh limits, on reduced or nonreduced X and for codimension-one crossings as well. This conclusion is independent of scalar-current classification and of the field restrictions used in that classification.

If, in addition, a separate theorem classifies every positive closed (0,0)-current on the particular ordinary domain as sum_i c_i delta_i, then

    dd(uT)-dd(vT)=sum_i c_i R_i=0.

That last implication is conditional on the classification on the ACTUAL ordinary domain and retains its field and dimension restrictions. This audit does not extend that classification to arbitrary K.

## 6. Why the general CPsh step cannot be skipped

If both u and v are arbitrary CPsh, all the positive measures in Section 5 still exist, but the nullity of mu_alpha on B_i has not yet been supplied by the cited polar theorem. Positive weighted sums give domination only AFTER that common ambient nullity is known. They cannot prove it out of positivity alone.

Nor is zero mass on a fixed closed set stable under weak convergence. Even uniform potential convergence alone does not justify an abstract sheaf-closure claim. On the tropical real line consider

    u_j(x)=sqrt(x^2+j^(-2)),   u(x)=|x|.

The functions u_j are smooth convex, hence psh, and converge uniformly with error at most 1/j. Their second-derivative measures have densities

    j^(-2)/(x^2+j^(-2))^(3/2),

so each gives zero mass to {0}, while the limit is 2 delta_0. This is an exact obstruction to the NAIVE closure inference.

This example does not refute the desired non-Archimedean analytic-nullity theorem: {0} here is a tropical point, not the lower-dimensional analytic polar subset under consideration. No counterexample satisfying the full continuous-CPsh/analytic-polar hypotheses is established in this audit.

To extend the polar result legitimately one needs a uniform estimate, for example the nonsmooth version of the source's weighted 8.4.3 bound with constants depending only on fixed local barrier data and sup norms, not on a smoothing neighbourhood or ordinal stage. That estimate would allow fixed smooth polar truncations first, followed by their monotone limit. The existence of currents and weak continuity alone do not provide the necessary bound.

## 7. Separate new proof route, not a source attribution

The source's dpsh calculus suggests a genuine route: for fixed positive closed S define

    Gamma_S(h)=1/2 dd(h^2 S)-h dd(hS).

Local square/product decompositions into CPsh functions make these expressions differences of positive currents. Minimal-subsheaf arguments can then be used to prove positivity and continuity of this energy current, as well as exponential identities, without assuming stage-one approximation. Mixed-energy Schwarz and the smooth-barrier integration-by-parts calculation would extend 8.4.3.
