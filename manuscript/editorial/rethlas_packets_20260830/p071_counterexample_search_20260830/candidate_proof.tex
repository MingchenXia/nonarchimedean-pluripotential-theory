Choose a smooth nondecreasing function
\(q:\mathbf R\to[0,1]\) such that \(q=0\) on
\((-\infty,-2]\) and \(q=1\) on \([-1,\infty)\), and put
\[
                         \psi(t)=\int_0^t q(s)\,\mathrm ds
                         \quad(t\in\mathbf R),
 \qquad
                         \psi(-\infty)=
                         \lim_{t\to-\infty}\psi(t).
\]
Then \(\psi\) is smooth and convex, it is constant near \(-\infty\),
\(\psi(0)=0\), and \(\psi'(0)=1\). On \(U_n\), define \(f_n\) branchwise
by
\begin{equation}
 (f_n)_{b_+}=\psi,
 \qquad
 (f_n)_{b_n}(t)=-t,
 \qquad
 (f_n)_b=0\quad(b\notin\{b_+,b_n\}).
 \label{eq:p071-counterexample-search-profile}
\end{equation}

The branch \(b_n\) is exceptional in \(U_n\), so its domain is
\((-1,0]\) and no condition at \(-\infty\) is involved. Every full branch
profile in \eqref{eq:p071-counterexample-search-profile} is constant near
its included endpoint \(-\infty\), and only the two branches \(b_+\) and
\(b_n\) are nonconstant. The endpoint derivatives at the centre are
\[
                   (f_n)'_{b_+}(0)=1,
                   \qquad (f_n)'_{b_n}(0)=-1,
\]
and all the others vanish. Their sum is zero, which is exactly the smooth
balance condition in the CLD star. Finally,
\(\psi''=q'\geq0\), while every other branch profile is affine. The exact
description of smooth psh functions on \(X_B\) therefore gives
\[
                  f_n\in\operatorname{Psh}_{X_B,0}(U_n).
\]

Suppose that a common open neighbourhood \(W\ni\eta\) and representatives
\(F_n\in\operatorname{Psh}_{X_B,0}(W)\) existed. Shrinking \(W\), choose
a standard neighbourhood \(W_0\subseteq W\), with finite exceptional
branch set \(A\). Choose \(n\) with \(b_n\notin A\). The branch
\((W_0)_{b_n}\) is then the full interval \([-\infty,0]\). Since
\([F_n]_\eta=[f_n]_\eta\), the restriction of \(F_n\) to this branch
agrees with \(-t\) on a collar of \(0\), and hence
\[
                              (F_n)'_{b_n}(0)=-1.
\]
On the other hand, CLD smoothness on a full branch forces
\((F_n)_{b_n}\) to be constant near \(-\infty\). Thus its derivative is
zero sufficiently far to the left. Positivity of
\(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''F_n\) makes this branch profile convex, so its
derivative is nondecreasing. It cannot increase from \(0\) to \(-1\).
This contradiction proves the failure of a common representative
neighbourhood.

Since \(0\leq q\leq1\), one has \(-2\leq\psi\leq0\). The profile \(-t\)
takes values in \([0,1)\) on \((-1,0]\), and all remaining profiles
vanish. Thus \(f_n+3>0\) on \(U_n\). Put \(C=3\). Positive scaling and
addition of a constant preserve smooth psh functions, so
\[
                         v_{n,k}=2^{-k}(f_n+3)
\]
is smooth psh on \(U_n\). Since \(f_n+3>0\), the sequence is pointwise
decreasing and converges to \(0\).

Suppose that all the germs \([v_{n,k}]_\eta\) had representatives in
\(\operatorname{Psh}_{X_B,0}(W)\) on one neighbourhood \(W\). Restrict
to \(k=1\), multiply each representative by \(2\), and subtract the
smooth constant \(3\). The resulting smooth psh functions on \(W\) would
represent all the germs \([f_n]_\eta\), contradicting the preceding
paragraph. Equivalently, the derivative on \(b_n\) is already
\(-1/2\) for \(k=1\), and the same convexity contradiction applies.

Nevertheless, the row limits are all the restriction of the global
function \(0\), and the outer sequence \(0,0,\ldots\) has the same limit.
The global constants \(2^{-k}\) form a decreasing smooth psh approximation
to \(0\) on all of \(X_B\). The shrinking-domain obstruction therefore
belongs to the chosen presentations, not to the psh function being
presented. This proves all assertions.
