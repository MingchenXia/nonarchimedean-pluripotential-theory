Let
\[
             B=\{b_+\}\sqcup\{b_n:n\geq1\}\sqcup B',
\]
where the \(b_n\) are pairwise distinct, and let \(X_B\) be the affine
tropical star of Chambert--Loir--Ducros, Section~6.7.3, with centre
\(\eta\). For each \(n\geq1\), let \(U_n\) be the standard open
neighbourhood of \(\eta\) whose exceptional branches are
\(b_1,\ldots,b_n\), with
\[
 (U_n)_{b_j}=(-1,0]\quad(1\leq j\leq n),
 \qquad
 (U_n)_b=[-\infty,0]\quad
       (b\notin\{b_1,\ldots,b_n\}).
\]

Then \(U_{n+1}\subseteq U_n\), but their intersection is not a
neighbourhood of \(\eta\). There are smooth psh functions
\[
                  f_n\in\operatorname{Psh}_{X_B,0}(U_n)
\]
such that the countable family of germs
\[
                         [f_n]_\eta\in
                 (\operatorname{Psh}_{X_B,0})_\eta
\]
has no simultaneous realization on one neighbourhood: there do not exist
an open \(W\ni\eta\) and smooth psh functions
\[
                  F_n\in\operatorname{Psh}_{X_B,0}(W)
\]
with \([F_n]_\eta=[f_n]_\eta\) for every \(n\).

The same construction can be embedded into deliberately badly chosen
first-stage presentations of the global zero function. More precisely, one
may choose \(C>0\) such that, for every \(n\),
\[
                         v_{n,k}=2^{-k}(f_n+C),
                         \qquad k\geq1,
\]
is a pointwise decreasing sequence of smooth psh functions on \(U_n\)
with limit \(0\). The selected two-index family of germs
\(\bigl([v_{n,k}]_\eta\bigr)_{n,k}\) still has no common representative
neighbourhood, even though every row limit and the outer decreasing limit
are the global smooth psh function \(0\) on \(X_B\).

Consequently, neither the sheaf axiom nor passage to stalks supplies the
fixed neighbourhood needed to flatten an arbitrarily chosen array of local
first-stage presentations. This is not a function in
\[
 \operatorname{Psh}_{X_B,2}\setminus\operatorname{Psh}_{X_B,1},
\]
does not settle P07.1, and does not contradict first-stage collapse on
\(X_B\): the same zero function has a global decreasing approximation by
positive constants.
