% Source-aware proof for the exact isolated statement.

\begin{proof}
The proper strict Berkovich space \(X\) is compact and Hausdorff, hence
paracompact. The strict Berkovich--rigid comparison recalled in
\cite[\S2.2]{GM19} therefore associates to it a proper rigid analytic
curve. Under this comparison, smoothness agrees with rigid smoothness in
the present proper, hence boundaryless, setting. Connectedness is preserved
because an open-and-closed decomposition on either side gives one on the
other. Since \(K\) is algebraically closed, connectedness is geometric
connectedness.

The algebraization theorem
\cite[Theorem~1.8.1(a)]{Lutkebohmert2016RigidCurves} now gives a smooth
projective connected algebraic \(K\)-curve \(C\) and an isomorphism
\(X\simeq C^{\An}\). This conclusion is also consistent with the broader
projectivity theorem for proper one-dimensional rigid spaces
\cite[Theorem~8.12]{Mitsui2011RigidSurfaces}.

View \(L\) as an invertible coherent sheaf on the associated rigid space.
Proper rigid GAGA
\cite[Theorem~1.6.11(b),(c)]{Lutkebohmert2016RigidCurves}
algebraizes it to a coherent sheaf \(M\) on \(C\). Apply the same theorem to
\(L^\vee\). Full faithfulness algebraizes the analytic evaluation
isomorphism \(L\otimes L^\vee\simeq\mathcal O_X\) and its inverse. Hence
\(M\) is invertible and \(L\simeq M^{\An}\), rather than merely the
analytification of an arbitrary coherent sheaf. The cohomological part of
the same GAGA theorem identifies the analytic and algebraic degree
conventions; in the statement we simply take \(\deg M\) as the definition.

The metric and its local CLD-psh weights are unchanged under the analytic
isomorphism \((X,L)\simeq(C^{\An},M^{\An})\). Thus
\Cref{thm:p081-curve-local-global} applies. Its four numbered conclusions,
its degree-zero numerical-triviality clause, and its independent existence
statement for degree-zero line bundles give every assertion above.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:p082-smooth-strict-curve-transfer-scope}
The corollary algebraizes the analytic curve and its line bundle. It does
not assert that a specified formal model of \(X\) is itself the completion
of an algebraic model. Raynaud localization identifies formal models only
up to admissible formal blow-up; after the generic fibre is algebraized,
\cite[Lemma~2.4]{GM19} supplies a dominating completion of an algebraic
model. No assertion is made for a singular curve, a non-algebraically
closed ground field, a trivially valued field, higher dimension, or the
distinct question whether every real parlinear metric is globally dpsh.
\end{remark}
