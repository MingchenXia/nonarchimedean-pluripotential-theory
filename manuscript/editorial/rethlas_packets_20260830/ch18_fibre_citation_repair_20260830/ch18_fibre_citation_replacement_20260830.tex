\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:ch18-fibre-cylinder}
Let $W$ be an affinoid domain in a pure $n$-dimensional analytic space,
let $g:W\to T^{\An}$ be an analytic moment map, and let $f$ be a nonzero
analytic function which defines an effective Cartier divisor on $W$.
Write
\[
 W_t:=W\mathbin{\times}_{\mathbf A^{1,\An}}\mathcal M(\mathscr H(t))
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 W_I:=\{x\in W:|f(x)|\in I\}.
\]
There are $r>0$ and a weighted integral polyhedral complex
$(\mathscr C,m)$, either empty or pure of dimension $n-1$, such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every analytic point $t$ of the closed disc $|t|\leq r$, the
 $(n-1)$-skeleton of $g_{\trop}(W_t)$, with its canonical calibration, is
 $(\mathscr C,m)$;
\item for every closed interval $I\subset(0,r]$, the $n$-skeleton of
 \[
              (-\log|f|,g_{\trop})(W_I)
 \]
 is the weighted product $(-\log I,1)\times(\mathscr C,m)$;
\item at $t=0$, the same $(\mathscr C,m)$ is the $(n-1)$-skeleton of the
 calibrated tropicalization of $\divis(f|_W)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $Z=W_0$.  If $Z$ is empty, compactness of $W$ gives
$\min_W|f|>0$; choosing $r<\min_W|f|$ proves all three assertions with
$(\mathscr C,m)$ empty.  We may therefore suppose that $Z$ is nonempty.

At every point of $Z$, the germ of $f$ is a regular element of the local
ring.  Hence, on a neighbourhood of $Z$, the space has Serre's property
$(\mathrm S_1)$ and the map $f$ is purely of relative dimension $n-1$.
Indeed, the first assertion follows from depth at a regular element, and
the second from upper semicontinuity of fibre dimension followed by the
analytic Hauptidealsatz.  This is the local reduction written out in the
proof of \cite[Lemma~16.7.5]{CLD25}.  Since $Z$ is compact, there is
$R>0$ such that the compact analytic tube
\[
                 W_{\leq R}:=\{x\in W:|f(x)|\leq R\}
\]
is contained in that neighbourhood.  It is pure of dimension $n$, has
property $(\mathrm S_1)$, and the restriction
$f:W_{\leq R}\to\mathbf A^{1,\An}$ is purely of relative dimension
$n-1$.

Apply \cite[Theorem~14.7.2]{CLD25} with $d=1$,
$X=W_{\leq R}$, $Y=\mathbf A^{1,\An}$, and $y=0$.  After decreasing
$r<R$, part~(a) of that theorem gives the first assertion.  Part~(b),
restricted to a closed interval $I\subset(0,r]$, gives the second after
permuting the two factors and replacing the coordinate $\log|f|$ by
$-\log|f|$; the standard calibration has weight one and is unchanged by
this sign change.

Finally, $W_0$ is the closed analytic subspace defined by $f$.  Its
associated cycle is $\divis(f|_W)$ by \cite[\S13.7.1]{CLD25}, and
integration over a pure closed analytic subspace agrees with integration
over its associated cycle by \cite[Proposition~16.6.3]{CLD25}.  Thus its
canonical calibration contains exactly the generic lengths occurring in
the Cartier divisor, which proves the third assertion.
\end{proof}
