## Mathematical and expository review

reviewed. Every assertion has a proof or exact import. All 23 reusable
environments and seven sections have semantic labels; there are 63 labels.
Inline mathematics and equation navigation follow the book conventions.

The principal repairs are:

- Ideal valuation is independent of local generators by the ultrametric
  rules and the vanishing of valuations on units. The extended-real Fekete
  argument proves the full asymptotic supremum and decreasing dyadic formula,
  retaining zero ideals and infinite values. Background independence is
  stated for all quasi-psh potentials through a sufficiently ample class.
- The exact divisorial multiplier-ideal inequalities retain log discrepancy
  and the squared-norm normalization. Ideal equivalence uses every real
  positive exponent and is equivalent to equality of the analytified
  functions on all semivaluations. The upper-regularized valuative theorem
  and strong openness have distinct roles; ideal and ordinary model
  equivalence are not conflated.
- Parameter translation and the trivial-valuation endpoint have explicit
  formulas. The Gauss-extension identity uses the published relative-curve
  convention. Such curves need not have model-fixed levels; the source's
  convention is distinguished from the book's maximal test curves.
- Concavification changes the initial potential to the maximum of the
  original finite potentials. It preserves the divisorial Legendre function
  without an ideal or ordinary model projection. A two-pole projective-line
  example computes the function at ordinary divisors, point supports and
  the trivial valuation, and shows the change from raw mass 1-lambda to 1.
- The fixed-potential construction proves integral reduction on every
  semivaluation, uniform global generation, and the precise varying-class
  closedness argument. The Demailly theorem is tied to the July 2011 author
  PDF rather than silently assigned to a different book edition.
- A separate affine-compatibility proposition uses fixed-potential
  analytification and divisorial determination. It proves the PL Legendre
  identity on all semivaluations, including infinite endpoint values,
  without a finite-level multiplier-ideal equality claim.
- Family algebraization and generation retain their exact analytic source
  interfaces. Dyadic GF approximants keep all coefficient ideals, including
  those vanishing on proper supports. Noncollapse is checked at the trivial
  valuation, and uniqueness applies domination in both directions.
- The zero-mass case uses the actual positive-mass rationally perturbed
  components. Extensions are compared in a common larger background before
  varying-class closedness is applied. The chapter's conclusion identifies
  which compatibilities it has proved and leaves the comparison, limits,
  operations and trace to Chapter 57.
