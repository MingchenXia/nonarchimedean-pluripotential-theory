## Repair and mathematical review

The blowup universal-property statement already required an invertible
extended ideal, but its proof only said locally principal. A principal ideal
generated by a zero divisor need not be invertible. The proof now identifies
the chart algebra as the degree-zero localized Rees algebra, states that
its generator is a nonzerodivisor, and uses the nonzerodivisor generator on
the test morphism to obtain unique regular quotients and gluing. The exact
source is Stacks Lemmas 31.33.4–31.33.6, Tag 01OF. Only this proof changes;
all statements and the other 38 formal environments remain byte-identical.

Krull valuations remain finite on nonzero field elements; ideal values use
finite nonzero generators and reverse ideal inclusion. The valuative
integral-closure converse and finite Rees-valuation reduction are explicitly
imported from Swanson–Huneke 6.8.3 and 10.2.2. Rees divisors need not be
exceptional. The monomial example establishes both ideal inclusions directly.
Normalized Rees pieces require a normal integral base; the nonnormal
degree-zero part is its normalization. Finite normalization keeps the Nagata
hypothesis. The conductor square does not assert bijectivity on its support.

The square-cube theorem keeps its elementary finite-stage and polynomial
base-change domains. A universal homeomorphism does not automatically give
an injective ring extension; the characteristic-zero qualification and
dual-number counterexample remain explicit. Determinant norms retain finite
local freeness and the denominator determinant in the line-bundle formula;
the polynomial-law norm is not a product over geometric points of a
nonreduced fibre. Roberts–Singh uses one fixed coefficient system for all
large integer m, exponent zero, and N at least p. Its ideal-power calculation
remains in Chapter 44 and is not extended to arbitrary integral extensions.
