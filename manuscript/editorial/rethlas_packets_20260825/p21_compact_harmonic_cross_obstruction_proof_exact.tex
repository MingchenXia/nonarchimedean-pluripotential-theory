The image of a compact topological space under a continuous map is compact.
Thus \(g(W)\) is compact in \(\mathbf R^2\).  The set \(\Sigma\) is not
compact: for every \(R>0\), it contains \((R,0)\), so it is unbounded, and
compact subsets of the Euclidean space \(\mathbf R^2\) are bounded.
Consequently \(g(W)\neq\Sigma\).

This argument uses only the compact-source and continuity clauses of the
stated definition.  It does not exclude a proper tropicalization from a
noncompact analytic space, nor an exhaustion of such a map by compact
harmonic-tropicalization charts.
