## Scope and mathematical distinctions

The introduction permits a common countable dense subfield or discrete
sigma-algebra for section spaces. Section 3 explicitly uses the narrower
assumptions: perfect K; countable K or discrete sigma-algebra; standard
Archimedean normalization on Q; a measurable set of finite positive
measure; projective reduced X. Section 4 additionally requires geometric
integrality and normality. The ambiguous three-item “two conditions”
list on p.4 is not treated as permission to discard these hypotheses.

“Ample”, “semiample”, “nef” and “big” in Definition 3.1 describe the
underlying geometric bundle. Local semipositivity is separate, as is
global arithmetic positivity of point heights. Total Arakelov degree
defines chi-volume; positive degree defines arithmetic volume. The
denominator is n^(d+1)/(d+1)!.

An integrable metric shift changes rank-r total degree by r times the
integral of the shift, and normalized minimal slope by the integral
alone. Proposition 3.4 gives the resulting chi-volume shift
(d+1) vol(L) times that integral. Positive total slope supplies no
simultaneous all-place norm bound on a section.

The finite-generator argument gives an intended uniform linear lower
minimal-slope bound for finitely many semiample directions. The
projective-bundle construction packages the multigrading into one
semiample tautological bundle. Actual adelic line bundles also have the
cited finite asymptotic maximal-slope bound; this extra input matters
for the spectral norm.

## Literal v1 repairs and diagnostics

1. The HN degree error in Proposition 1.9 drops the Archimedean-measure
   factor of Proposition 1.6. It must not be copied without reconciling
   the measure normalization and cited determinant-norm comparison.
2. A norm/filtration dictionary needs finite separated/exhaustive
   filtrations and the appropriate endpoint convention. An arbitrary
   decreasing filtration is not sufficient.
3. Definition 2.6 reverses the product-norm inequality used in the
   subsequent proofs. Those proofs require an upper bound on the norm
   of a product by the product of norms times rank losses.
4. Even after fixing that direction, finite/subfinite algebra type alone
   does not give a nondegenerate finite spectral norm. Rank-one norms
   exp(n^2) satisfy the literal printed direction but have infinite
   spectral limit; exp(-n^2) in the corrected direction have zero
   spectral limit. Linear upper slope boundedness is the missing
   general-series qualification, supplied separately for the intended
   adelic line-bundle application.
5. The binomial spectral-norm proof needs the middle-index bound as well
   as endpoint indices. Its mixed product and finite-generator displays
   have degree/rank-index slips; the latter's intended fixed-degree
   factor is dim(E_lambda)^C rather than dim(E_n)^C.
6. Under ordinary lattice Lebesgue measure, the roof integral for
   chi-volume needs (d+1)!. On P1 with constant shift c, G=c on [0,1],
   whereas chi-volume is 2c. If another measure convention is intended,
   it must be stated.
7. Lemma 3.6 reverses its section bundle and quotient-volume difference.
   An injection H0(mL) into H0(mM) uses a section of M-L; the quotient
   rank has geometric limit vol(M)-vol(L). Theorem 3.7 actually uses
   this intended direction in its application with M=nL+A.
   A one-trivial-place canonical P1 model with L=O(2), M=O(1), both
   shifted by 1, meets the literal lemma assumptions but has chi(L)=4
   and printed bound 0. Reversing the source/target data gives the
   intended equality 2=4-2.
8. The metric-distance display needs a consistent absolute logarithmic
   convention, and the real-divisor norm needs |f(x)|. Several isolated
   intersection powers, variable names and limsup/liminf labels are
   local notation slips.
9. The rational-homogeneity proof prints an upper scaled limsup slope
   bound where its positivity step needs a lower bound. That passage
   needs reconstruction in its stated real-divisor scope.
10. The continuous-extension argument requires a continuous strict
    lower slope bound, not a globally constant bound on an unbounded
    cone. An offset handles strictness at the origin. Coefficient
    formulas should first be used on parameter space; descent to a
    dependent generated cone needs justification.

These diagnoses are attached to the exact 2023 arXiv version. They do
not assert that the intended main Hilbert–Samuel, birational-invariance
or semiample-continuity conclusions are false.

## Main proof routes and remaining import boundary

The semiample semipositive Hilbert–Samuel proof compares nL with nL+A,
then uses a fixed section of rL-A for the reverse inequality. Auxiliary
metric shifts only arrange nonnegative total section degree. Uniform
linear slope estimates make the error subleading; the ample
Hilbert–Samuel and arithmetic intersection inputs are imported.

Theorem 3.10's birational chi-volume proof assumes a finite lower
asymptotic minimal slope on the pullback. Pointwise metric compatibility
must be combined with surjectivity of the proper analytic map to get
equality of sup norms. The coherent quotient is supported in lower
dimension; an auxiliary section avoiding its associated points compares
it with an ample semipositive case. Its rank is subleading, and the
lower slope estimates control its degree. Birational projection for
intersections is another imported dependency. Neither this proof nor
the introduction supplies an all-place small-section theorem.

The semiample continuous extension is over finitely generated rational
polyhedral directions with the stated geometric and adelic assumptions;
it is not unrestricted continuity on the big cone. The general imported
Okounkov, Chen–Moriwaki, Bost–Chen and intersection/Hilbert–Samuel inputs
have not been recursively read in this audit.

## Toric normalized-concavity candidate

Proposition 3.11 derives superadditivity of chi-volume divided by
geometric volume from Minkowski equality and a pointwise roof inequality.
The own candidate uses a triangle and a thin rectangle on a common
smooth toric surface, with specified affine and zero roofs. Exact
lattice sums and polygon integrals yield geometric volumes 64,16,224
and signed chi-volumes -512,0,-2048, so the proposed normalized
