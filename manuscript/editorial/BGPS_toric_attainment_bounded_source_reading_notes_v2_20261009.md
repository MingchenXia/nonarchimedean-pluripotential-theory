## Signature and metric

Let K=Q(sqrt(2)), with real embeddings sigma_+(sqrt(2))=sqrt(2) and
sigma_-(sqrt(2))=-sqrt(2). Use standard absolute values and weights
n_+=n_-=1/2, with the usual weighted finite places. Let T=Gm and
X=P1_K, with ample degree-one toric Cartier divisor D=[infinity].
Set a=log(2), u_+=-a, u_-=a, u_v=0 at every finite place. Thus
sum_v n_v u_v=0. In the affine coordinate z and frame nonvanishing away
from infinity, give D the continuous metric with psh potential

    g_v(z)=log max(1, exp(u_v)*|z|_v).

Equivalently its concave metric function is
psi_v(t)=min(0,t-u_v), for t=-log|z|_v. At finite places use the
canonical model metric. At real places these are translated canonical
continuous positive toric metrics; their local curvature is Haar measure
of mass one on radius exp(-u_v). They are invariant under complex
conjugation and extend continuously as line-bundle metrics at zero and
infinity. This is a finite-support adelic semipositive metric, with ample
underlying divisor; it is not restricted to smooth archimedean metrics.

The local roof on Delta=[0,1] is theta_v(x)=u_v*x, so the global roof is
identically zero. At an interior x, each local sup-differential is the
singleton {u_v} and the global one is {0}. This meets the actually read
monocritical criterion in Proposition 4.16. A direct alternative is that
for any centered probability family the energy

## Direct height formula and nonattainment in the torus

For alpha in T(Kbar) and any finite defining extension F/K, the actual
height convention gives

    h_D(alpha)=sum_{w in M_F} n_w max(0, log|alpha|_w+u_{v(w)}).

This is nonnegative. The weighted sum of the arguments of max is zero,
by the product formula and centering. Hence height zero forces

    |alpha|_w=2  for every w above sigma_+,
    |alpha|_w=1/2  for every w above sigma_-,
    |alpha|_w=1  for every finite w.

Let d=[F:K] and q=N_{F/K}(alpha) in K*. The norm identity at a real
place, including every conjugate with multiplicity, then gives
|sigma_+(q)|=2^d and |sigma_-(q)|=2^(-d).
Since q lies in the real quadratic field, the first equality implies
sigma_+(q)=2^d or -2^d. Injectivity of sigma_+ and its identity on Q
then force q=2^d or -2^d as an element of K. Its other embedding has
the same absolute value 2^d, contradicting 2^(-d) for d>=1.

Thus the direct calculation gives no height-zero **torus** point, even
over a finite extension. It does not claim the boundary has no
height-zero points; for this metric the boundary point zero does have
height zero. BGPS special points in Definition 5.10 and the proof of
5.13 are points in the principal torus.
