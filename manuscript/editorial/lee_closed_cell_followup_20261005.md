## Candidate closed cells

A cell has label `(J,F,I,C)`: its minimal sedentary stratum is `(J,F)`,
its positive generators are the independent coordinates `I` disjoint from
`J`, and `C` is a chain of original flats strictly below `F` containing
`I union J`. Its finite cone has generators

\[
 e_i\quad(i\in I),\qquad -e_{F\setminus G}\quad(G\in C).
\]

The candidate closed cell is the closure of this cone in the coordinate
product of tropical projective lines for that stratum. Positive coordinates
are independent of the negative blocks. On the negative blocks, nested
flats give weakly ordered levels between zero and minus infinity. This
motivates a simplex for the negative chain and a cube for the positive
coordinates, rather than the cube obtained by compactifying each ray
independently in its own recession fan. This geometric motivation is not
treated here as a completed general identification proof.

The candidate facets are generated by four rules:

1. Remove one positive coordinate from `I` at its finite endpoint.
2. Remove one flat from the negative chain.
3. Send a positive coordinate `i` to its infinite endpoint, moving it from
   `I` to the sedentary label `J`.
4. Send the largest negative-chain flat `G` to infinity, replacing `F` by
   `G` and removing this last member of the chain.

The original coordinates and original flat labels remain fixed throughout.
They distinguish isomorphic minors and retain loop coordinates. Orient a
cell by positive rays in increasing coordinate order, followed by negative
rays in increasing chain order. The finite deletion of generator in position
`k` (starting at zero) has sign `(-1)^(k+1)`; its positive infinite facet
has sign `(-1)^k`. The largest negative generator has infinite-facet sign
`(-1)^(dimension-1)`. These signs are checked by the exact incidence-square
calculation, not inferred from an unsigned face count.

## Face-poset computation

The same basis-exchange enumerator covers all 92 labelled matroids on at
most four elements. For each, the script lists all candidate cells and
facets, then recursively computes each cell's complete face downset. Every
pairwise intersection of downsets is either empty or exactly the downset
of one cell. Every signed boundary squares to zero.

There are 6,538 candidate cells and 1,015,221 unordered pairs in total.
These are exact checks of the specified **candidate face posets**. They
do not independently prove that every geometric intersection in an
arbitrary-rank tropical compactification has this face poset, or that the
resulting cells form charts for the required affine sheaf. Those general
identifications remain necessary before a book import relying on this route.

## Direct multi-tangent calculation

The second calculation differs from the preceding wedge complex. For a
cell `tau`, it constructs `F_p(tau)` as the sum of the exterior tangent
spaces of all same-sedentarity cofaces. Exterior coordinates are determinants
of the actual ray vectors in the original ambient coordinate space. Exact
rational row reduction chooses a basis. A facet map deletes coordinates
lost to its new sedentarity, then expresses the projected wedge in the
target basis. Every map lands in that basis and the full signed boundary
squares to zero. Boundary ranks use exact rational elimination.

All cells in this candidate are closed. The chain groups used are

\[
 C_{p,q}=\bigoplus_{\dim\sigma=q}F_p(\sigma).
\]

For a finite complex over `Q`, dual cochain groups have the same homology
dimensions. This does not establish integral torsion-freeness, a cup-product
identification or an arbitrary-rank sheaf comparison.

| Example | Candidate cells by dimension | Computed diagonal dimensions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty ground set | 1 | 1 |
| Boolean `U_(3,3)` | 27, 64, 54, 16 | 1, 3, 3, 1 |
| Parallel pair, one coloop and one loop | 12, 18, 7 | 1, 2, 1 |
| Uniform `U_(2,4)` | 20, 33, 14 | 1, 4, 1 |
| Uniform `U_(3,4)` | 48, 123, 110, 34 | 1, 4, 6, 1 |
