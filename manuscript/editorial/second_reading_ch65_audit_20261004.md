## Mathematical continuity

The parameter-pole filtration uses minus ceiling of the real index and
retains attained integer weights. Its equivariant finite free lattice and
multiplication are explicit. Finite generation comes from the relatively
ample section algebra. Conversely, rational integral jumps and a common
Veronese give a degree-one relative Proj over C[varpi]. Chart domains are
torsion-free over the parameter PID, hence flat. Normalization and section
saturation can enlarge pieces; the unique representative is normal and
ample after the stated equivalence, not the original unsaturated algebra.

Fubini–Study values use a finite maximum after degree-one generation and
are bounded globally, including proper-support semivaluations. Integral
closure preserves ideal values by the unique least term argument, including
infinite values. The graded Gauss valuation first quotients by the support;
integrality preserves the finite maximum. Saturation preserves the metric
through powers in large degrees without preserving individual base ideals.
The two P1 examples show respectively normalization changes degree-one
pieces, and ramified base change plus normalization changes odd monomial
weights in arbitrarily large divisible degrees. Passing to an even
Veronese does not remove the latter discrepancy.

The squared-norm obstacle error is (log C+n log m)/m uniformly over filtered
subspaces. The local mean-value estimate uses radius m^(−1/2) and a
first-order normalized smooth frame. Sup obstacles increase under powers;
tail envelopes and factorial cofinal degrees are distinguished from an
unsupported all-degree limit. Concavity is proved by products, then
regularized and extended to real parameters. Strict interior levels are
nontrivial and have small unbounded locus. Maximality imports RWN's
division/envelope theorem; multiplicativity alone does not supply it.

The closed terminal level is the left limit. Scalar and parity examples
show that raw terminal spaces can vanish or oscillate although the closed
test curve and ray are well defined. Scalar Fenchel–Moreau recovers this
closed curve, using the initial-time limit as well as positive times.

adds cs to ordinary norm weights and ct to squared-norm potentials;
