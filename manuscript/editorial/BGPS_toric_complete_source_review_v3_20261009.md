## Exact point-attainment candidate remains unverified

Proposition 5.13 literally assumes u_v belongs to val_v(T(K)) tensor Q
**separately** at every place of the ground number field. The proof's
commensurability claim between independently chosen local valuation
groups and one simultaneous S-unit image is not cleared by Dirichlet's
unit theorem. A complete adversarial candidate over K=Q(sqrt(2)) tests
this distinction for P1 and O(1): u_+=−log 2, u_−=log 2, all finite values
zero, both real weights 1/2, and local translated canonical metrics.
The global roof is zero and the interior superdifferentials give the
monocritical vector u. The separate local witnesses are 2 and 1/2.

The direct all-place height formula is nonnegative. A hypothetical
height-zero torus point alpha over F/K would have all real-conjugate
moduli 2 above the first real place and 1/2 above the other, with all
finite moduli 1. Its norm q in K would have moduli 2^d and 2^(−d).
Since K is real quadratic, the first value forces q=±2^d in K and hence
the same absolute value at the second real place, a contradiction.
Roots of suitable powers of the unit 1+sqrt(2) have heights tending to
zero, giving a generic small subsequence and essential minimum zero.
Boundary point zero is not the torus special-point notion and is not
claimed to have positive height. Abstract energy minimizers need not be
the Galois-orbit distribution of one algebraic point.

Later actual source pages permit finite independent local metric choices
and introduce no hidden global-translation condition excluding this
model. Explicit smooth semipositive approximations also confirm the
metric-admissibility direction. The full dossier and seven illustrative

## Further full-source diagnostics

The notes distinguish weak probability convergence from finite moments,
require compactness/tightness and full place weights, and retain proper
vs projective and divisor-vs-real-divisor hypotheses. In Example 6.2 the
roof domain is [−1,1], despite a later printed [0,2]. Example 6.3 has a
plateau of roof maximizers and nevertheless is monocritical: uniqueness
of the roof maximizer is not the definition of monocriticality.

Theorem 6.4 and Example 6.5 give a useful ordinary Cartier-divisor case:
standard P1_Q, Fubini–Study at infinity, canonical metrics at all finite
places. Small generic orbits tend to unit-circle Haar at infinity and
Gauss Dirac at finite places, whereas the original Fubini–Study curvature
is smooth area. Modifying finite models can preserve or destroy
monocriticality. This classical example is a potential explanatory book
addition, pending a separate normalization and integration audit.

Propositions 7.3–7.4 construct points in shrinking adelic neighborhoods
and identify equilibrium limits at capacity one; they establish
approximation and do not clear exact point attainment. The phrase
“does not charge singletons” must mean classical type-I points, because
the finite-place equilibrium measure can be a Gauss-point atom.

Proposition 7.5's written cross-place argument needs attention before
reuse. Its capacities c and 1/c give global capacity c^(n_v0−n_v), unless
the unspecified extension equalizes selected local weights or the scale
is compensated. The Archimedean inverse-image display omits the lower
conjugate arc, although Figure 4 correctly shows both. Shrinking
neighborhoods do not give exact zero valuations at the Archimedean
places: uniform annular bounds and limiting KR/product-formula arguments
are needed instead. A later expectation display repeats the wrong place
and omits weights. These are written-proof diagnostics and repair
directions, not verified refutations of the intended proposition.
Theorem 7.2's degree-one divisor statement must not be silently applied
to every higher-degree example without the corresponding reduction.
Remark 7.7 explains a genuine exception to cross-place phase effects
when the tropical fiber is a single type-III point. Historical 2019
open questions, including Remark 5.9, are not asserted open in 2026
without an additional current-literature search.
