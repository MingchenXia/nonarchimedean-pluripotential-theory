## Result and conventions

This document gives a complete integration argument in the curve scope using
the supplied exact supporting theorems n700 and n701 and the stated primary
foundations. The new integration is an unverified draft, with an author audit.
The independent receipts for the supporting theorems are preserved at their
own scopes; no earlier verdict certifies this integration. Original P06.2
remains open in arbitrary dimension, so its status is **partial** and
`candidate=false`. P05.2 is not addressed.

All spaces are actual Berkovich analytic spaces, with their specified
structures. A curve means a separated analytic space pure of analytic
dimension one, as in Gubler--Rabinoff (GR) 3.1. An ordinary open is open in
the underlying topology. Strictness always means strict analyticity over
the current ground field. Put $D=d'd''$. All tests and currents in the
initial equation are **ordinary** Chambert--Loir--Ducros (CLD) tests and
currents. On a curve,
\[
 D[h](\psi)=\int_X h(D\psi)_{\mathrm{Rad},K},
 \qquad \psi\in A_c^{0,0}(X).                         \tag{1}
\]
Canonical integration retains all actual generic Artinian lengths of $X$,
including unequal lengths on different components and nilpotent structure.
The smooth top form $D\psi$ has a signed Radon measure. We do not assume
that $D[h]$ itself is a Radon measure.

GR-subharmonicity means GR Definition 3.4; GR-harmonicity means both signs
are subharmonic (Definition 3.13). Classical pluriharmonicity means both
signs are classically psh in Definition 4.1 (Definition 7.1). These
definitions include extension fields and curve maps. Real PL below has
the **G-local** convention of GR 2.16 and GJR Definition 5.1, on the
G-topology generated by strict analytic domains. GJR-harmonicity means
Definition 7.2, equivalently the numerical triviality criterion in
Remark 7.3(1). It is a different regularity assertion from ordinary CLD
smooth harmonicity.

**Theorem (arbitrary-field intrinsic curve recognition).** Let $K$ be any
complete nontrivially valued non-Archimedean field. Let $X$ be a good,
separated, strict, boundaryless, pure one-dimensional $K$-analytic space.
Every finite continuous $h:X\to\mathbb R$ satisfying (1) equal to zero
for every ordinary compact smooth scalar test is GR-harmonic and
classically pluriharmonic. It is real PL in the stated G-local convention,
and GJR-harmonic. The result holds on every ordinary open subspace of $X$.

More explicitly, for **every** analytic extension field $L/K$, **every**
$L$-analytic curve $Y$, and **every** $L$-analytic morphism
$f:Y\to X_L$, the finite continuous function
\[
                         h\circ\pi_{L/K}\circ f           \tag{2}
\]
is GR-harmonic. No smoothness, strictness, boundarylessness, reducedness,
or separability hypothesis is added to these test curves or maps beyond
being an analytic curve and morphism in the published definition. If
$Y$ is smooth and strict, (2) is also Thuillier-harmonic. The ground-field
theorem includes imperfect and positive-characteristic fields,
singular curves, arbitrary nilpotents, and unequal generic lengths.
There is no global algebraic-model or properness hypothesis.

## The valued completed algebraic closure

**Standard finite-field foundation.** A finite algebraic extension $E/K$
of a complete non-Archimedean field admits a unique extending absolute
value, is complete in that absolute value, and every embedding of such
finite extensions over $K$ is isometric. This includes inseparable
extensions. This is BGR, *Non-Archimedean Analysis*, 3.2.4/2, used at
exactly this scope in n700, S2, and proved in n701, Section 3.1. The
uniqueness assertion follows by finite-dimensional norm equivalence and
applying a comparison bound to arbitrarily high powers. We use this
standard foundation on finite fields over the complete fields $K$ and,
in the next lemma, $L$; it is not a current theorem.

**Lemma 1.** An algebraic closure $\overline K$ has a compatible absolute
value extending that of $K$, and $C=\widehat{\overline K}$ is an
algebraically closed complete nontrivially valued analytic extension of
$K$, in every characteristic.

**Proof.** Give each finite intermediate $E/K$ its unique norm. Two
choices agree on intersections because their restrictions extend the
norm on a finite complete intermediate field. On their finite compositum
they agree with its unique norm. These norms therefore define a
multiplicative ultrametric absolute value on the union $\overline K$.
The embedding of $K$ is isometric. Its metric completion is a field:
for a nonzero limit $a$ of elements $a_m$ of $\overline K$, eventually
$|a_m|=|a|>0$, and
\[
 |a_m^{-1}-a_j^{-1}|=|a_m-a_j|/(|a_m||a_j|)
\]
shows that the inverses converge. Multiplication and the norm extend
continuously, retaining multiplicativity and the ultrametric inequality.
Completeness follows by construction. An element of $K^\times$ with
absolute value different from one retains that value in $C$, so the
valuation is nontrivial. This verifies the analytic-extension definition
in GR 2.1--2.2: a complete non-Archimedean field with an isometric
embedding of the ground field.

Here is a proof of algebraic closure, rather than an invocation of a
completion theorem. Take a monic polynomial
\[
 P(T)=T^d+\sum_{i<d}a_iT^i\in C[T],\qquad d\geq1,
 \quad R=\max(1,\max_{i<d}|a_i|).
\]
For $m\geq1$, choose monic $P_m\in\overline K[T]$ whose coefficients
have errors at most $\delta_m=2^{-md}R^{-(d-1)}$ from those of $P$.
Density allows these simultaneous choices. Every root of $P_m$ has norm
at most $R$: if $|z|>R$, the leading term has norm strictly greater
than every other term. All $P_m$ split, with repetitions allowed, in
$\overline K$.

Choose a root $b_1$ of $P_1$. Given a root $b_m$ of $P_m$,
\[
 |P_{m+1}(b_m)|\leq\delta_m R^{d-1}=2^{-md}.
\]
Factor $P_{m+1}(T)=\prod_{j=1}^d(T-c_j)$. At least one factor has
$|b_m-c_j|\leq2^{-m}$; otherwise their product would have norm
greater than $2^{-md}$. Choose it as $b_{m+1}$. The ultrametric
inequality gives $|b_j-b_m|\leq2^{-m}$ for $j>m$, so $b_m$ converges
in $C$ to $b$. Coefficient convergence and the bound $|b_m|\leq R$
give $P(b)=\lim_m P_m(b_m)=0$. Thus every nonconstant polynomial has
a root, after dividing by its leading coefficient; repeated division
gives algebraic closure. No derivative, separability, discrete valuation,
or spherical completeness was used. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (actual common field for this integration).** For every analytic
extension $L/K$ there is an actual complete nontrivially valued field $F$
with isometric embeddings of $C$ and $L$ whose restrictions to $K$ agree.

**Proof.** Choose an algebraic closure $\overline L$ and endow it with
the compatible extension norm using the complete field $L$ as in
Lemma 1. A $K$-embedding $\overline K\to\overline L$ exists: partially
defined embeddings on intermediate fields, ordered by extension, have
unions on chains. A maximal one missing an algebraic element extends by
choosing a root of that element's minimal polynomial in $\overline L$,
a contradiction. Each finite $K$-subfield embeds isometrically by the
finite-field foundation. Thus this embedding is isometric on all of
$\overline K$. Take $F=\widehat{\overline L}$. The embedding extends
isometrically from $C$ into this complete field, and $L$ already embeds
isometrically. They agree on $K$. Nontriviality is inherited from $K$.
$\square$

Lemma 2 is a direct construction for the fields appearing here. It
does not assert that $C\widehat\otimes_K L$ is a field or define any
pullback of a continuous current. It can be used in the function-level
scalar descent below. In that use we make no claim that $F/C$ is
completed-algebraic, and no current equation is transported through it.

**GR subharmonicity and harmonicity.** Definition 3.4 starts with an
upper semicontinuous function on a separated pure analytic curve $Z$.
For every nontrivially valued analytic extension $E/k$ and every map
from a smooth connected strict $E$-curve into $Z_E$, its composite is
Thuillier-subharmonic or identically $-\infty$. Definition 3.13 says a
real-valued function is harmonic if both signs are subharmonic.
GR 3.4.2 and 3.12 identify these finite two-sign functions on smooth
strict curves over nontrivially valued fields with Thuillier harmonic
functions. A finite function never uses the identically-$-\infty$

**GR Proposition 3.14(6).** Precisely: if $Z$ is a $k$-analytic curve,
$u:Z\to\mathbb R$ is continuous, and $E/k$ is an analytic extension
field, then $u$ is harmonic if and only if
$u\circ\pi_{E/k}:Z_E\to\mathbb R$ is harmonic. There is no assumption
of reducedness, smoothness, strictness, perfectness, characteristic,
or algebraicity of $E/k$. This is the two-sign specialization of
Proposition 3.5(7), for upper semicontinuous functions. We apply it
first with $Z=X$ and $E=C$, **after** Gate R recognizes $h_C$.

We have checked the complete scalar-extension proof, rather than
reading “if and only if” as current functoriality. Its reverse direction
uses a simultaneous analytic extension and, on a smooth strict probe,
the comparison definition of Thuillier subharmonicity. In our application
the simultaneous extension of $C$ and any other $L/K$ is explicitly
constructed in Lemma 2. The proof then compares already subharmonic
functions, never continuous currents.

The exact smooth foundations in that proof are: Thuillier Definition

## Actual geometry, compact tests, and calibration at the current gate

Choose $C$ as in Lemma 1, with $M=\overline K$. This is an actual
algebraic valued extension and its metric completion, so Gate T applies
with $n=1$. On a strict affinoid $V=\mathcal M(A)$ the source upstairs
is $\mathcal M(A\widehat\otimes_K C)$, with the actual completed
algebra, including nilpotents.

Goodness follows from inverse images of ordinary interiors of affinoid
neighbourhoods; strict presentations remain strict after scalar
extension. Separatedness follows by base change of the closed diagonal.
Boundarylessness follows from the relative-interior base-change
inclusion, CLD 11.4.1 or Berkovich 1993, Proposition 1.5.5(iii),(iv),
as used in Gate T. We use only that inclusion, not equality of arbitrary
absolute interiors. Purity of **every** actual upstairs component and
local dimension one follow from n701's separate componentwise purity
proof in Sections 3.2, 6 and 9. Merely knowing
$\dim X_C=\dim X=1$ would be insufficient.

The function $h_C=h\circ\pi_{C/K}$ is finite and continuous. A compact
set downstairs has compact inverse image: take a finite cover by
ordinary interiors of affinoid neighbourhoods, whose scalar inverse
images are compact; the inverse image of the compact set is closed in
their finite union since the carriers are Hausdorff. This is the
compact-support assertion in n701 Section 3.3, with no finite-fibre
assumption on $C/K$.

For an arbitrary $\beta\in A_c^{0,0}(X_C)$, n701 Theorem A gives a
finite embedded $E\subseteq\overline K$ and
$\alpha_E\in A_c^{0,0}(X_E)$ with $\beta=q_E^*\alpha_E$ **as
smooth sections**, on the entire space. This includes zero regions,
ordinary presentation opens and compact zero extensions. Its smooth
Hessian is therefore exactly $q_E^*D\alpha_E$. Finite-stage transport
in n701 gives $D[h_E]=0$. Canonical smooth-top-measure projection,
CLD Proposition 15.3.1, gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{X_C}h_C(D\beta)_{\mathrm{Rad},C}
 &=\int_{X_C}(h_E\circ q_E)(q_E^*D\alpha_E)_{\mathrm{Rad},C}\\
 &=\int_{X_E}h_E(D\alpha_E)_{\mathrm{Rad},E}=0.
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{5}
\]
The multiplier is bounded on the compact support. This is an
application of a theorem proved for all compact tests. It is not a
claim that an arbitrary continuous current has been pulled back.

The length bookkeeping is essential. In the calibration underlying
(5), a finite Artinian moment-fibre algebra $B$ over a complete field
$H$ has dimension $d$. After a complete extension $H'/H$, its tensor
is already complete as a finite-dimensional vector space and decomposes
into local factors $B_j$. A composition series in each factor gives
\[
 d=\dim_{H'}(B\otimes_H H')
   =\sum_j\operatorname{length}_{B_j}(B_j)[\kappa_j:H'].     \tag{6}
\]
Each composition factor is the residue field of its local Artinian
ring, so this calculation permits nilpotents, inseparable residue
extensions, and unequal lengths. It is the actual degree sum in CLD
13.3.3 and 14.3.6, used in n701 Section 10, and supplies the canonical
projection formula with **no field-degree factor**. Individual generic
weights may change under scalar extension; their appropriate calibrated
sums are retained. They are never normalized to one or divided out.

In contrast, CLD Remark 14.4.3 concerns a finite $E$-analytic space
viewed as $K$-analytic on the **same carrier**: its $K$-calibration is
$[E:K]$ times the $E$-calibration. n701 uses that different statement
only after constructing an actual local $E$-analytic structure in
its finite-Galois induction. We do not replace (5) by that statement,
and never assign an infinite degree factor to $C/K$.

## Proof of the theorem and all quantifiers

If $X$ is empty the conclusions are vacuous. Otherwise take the valued
completed algebraic closure $C$ of Lemma 1. The original $K$ and $X$
satisfy every hypothesis of Gate T: complete nontrivial valuation,
goodness, separatedness, strictness, boundarylessness, and pure
dimension one. The preceding application checks give a good separated
strict boundaryless **pure analytic curve** $X_C$, with its actual
structure, and the equation $D[h_C]=0$ against every ordinary compact
scalar test.

The field $C$ is algebraically closed, complete, and nontrivially valued.
Gate R now applies directly to $X_C$ and its finite continuous multiplier.
It gives GR-harmonicity of $h_C$. Its singular and nonreduced cases are
part of that exact theorem, so no smoothness or reduction assumption on
$X_C$ is needed and none is silently introduced. In particular, possible
new nilpotents from geometric inseparability cause no scope failure.

Apply GR Proposition 3.14(6) with $Z=X$, $k=K$, $k'=C$, and $u=h$.
$X$ is a separated pure analytic curve, $h$ is continuous, and $C/K$
is an analytic extension. The proposition detects harmonicity of this
**already recognized** upstairs function and gives GR-harmonicity of $h$.
This is precisely the published reverse function-level implication;
it does not detect or ascend an arbitrary current.

Consequently $h$ and $-h$ are subharmonic in Definition 3.4. GR 4.1.2
applies on the analytic curve $X$ to each sign, making both signs
classically psh. Definition 7.1 gives classical pluriharmonicity. To
spell out its full scope, fix any analytic extension $L/K$, any
$L$-analytic curve $Y$, and any $f:Y\to X_L$. Definition 4.1 for each
sign gives subharmonicity on $Y$ of the corresponding sign of (2).
The composite is finite and continuous; Definition 3.13 makes it
GR-harmonic. This includes the further extension-field and smooth-probe
quantifiers already inside subharmonicity on $Y$. If $Y$ is smooth and
strict, GR 3.4.2 and 3.12 identify it with Thuillier harmonicity. No
current equation on $X_L$, on $Y$, or on any common extension has been
used in this quantifier step. The field $L$ may be transcendental over
$K$, and the map $f$ may be inseparable, nonfinite, or constant.

Now apply GR Proposition 3.15 on the original strict $K$-curve. Its
nontrivial-valuation hypothesis and continuity hypothesis hold, and
GR-harmonicity was just established. It gives real PL in the published
G-local convention and numerical triviality of every residual class.
GJR Definition 7.2 and Remark 7.3(1), with the complete nontrivially
valued $K$ and good strict $X$, give GJR-harmonicity.

Finally let $\Omega\subseteq X$ be an ordinary open. It is an analytic
domain (CLD 11.1.7). It is good, separated and strict by ordinary-local
restriction and the strict-neighbourhood statement CLD 11.7.7; local
dimension is preserved by domain restriction (11.2.3), so it is pure
one-dimensional if nonempty. Its open inclusion has full relative
interior, and composition with the boundaryless structural morphism
makes it boundaryless (11.4.1). A compact scalar test on $\Omega$
extends by zero to a compact ordinary scalar test on $X$: its support
is a compact closed subset of the Hausdorff $X$ contained in the open
$\Omega$, and every exterior point has a neighbourhood disjoint from
that support. Canonical integration and differentials restrict to
domains (CLD 14.3.5, 15.7.2 and Section 7.7), so (1) restricts.
Apply the same theorem to $\Omega$, or use harmonicity's sheaf property
GR 3.14(1). These are actual ordinary opens at every analytic point,
not merely one affinoid or an admissible cover. This proves all claims.
$\square$
