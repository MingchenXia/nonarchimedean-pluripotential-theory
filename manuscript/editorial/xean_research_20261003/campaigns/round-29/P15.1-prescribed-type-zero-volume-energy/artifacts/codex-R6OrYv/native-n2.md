Mixed masses and weights. Normalize any finite family u_i<=psi. Its average w belongs to the weighted full-mass class by convexity. Exact truncation proof. For u<=psi, let z=psi-u, u_L=max(u,psi-L), mu_a=<T_u^aT_psi^(d-a)>omega^r and mu_a,L=<T_uL^aT_psi^(d-a)>omega^r. Both masses are M.  integral(psi-u_L)dmu_a,L=integral min(z,L)dmu_a.
Boundary equality sets are harmless because the weight is constant there; poles have zero mass. Monotone convergence gives E(u_L) decreasing to the finite displayed energy. This proves existence and independence of any real levels tending to infinity. Ordered potentials have ordered truncations, so E is increasing; all mixed masses equal M, so constant shifts are cM.

Bounded decreasing continuity needed for the extension. The standard scalar capacity fact is that decreasing quasi-psh functions in a fixed background, with a non-identically-minus-infinity quasi-psh limit, converge in Kähler capacity (Guedj–Zeriahi 2005, Proposition 3.7 as cited in the supplied DDNL Theorem 2.4 proof, p.2057). DDNL Proposition 2.2 states that uniformly bounded local psh potentials converging in capacity, multiplied by uniformly bounded quasicontinuous weights converging in capacity, have convergent weighted Bedford–Taylor top products. Quasi-psh functions and bounded truncations are quasicontinuous. These are standard foundations; the big-class-only DDNL Theorem 2.3 is not applied to the pseudoeffective background.

Here is the full tail argument: if v_j decreases to v with all differences from psi bounded uniformly by B, all mixed products of v_j,psi and fixed smooth slots have the same mass m as the limiting product. Put eta_L=min(1,max(psi+L,0)). On its nonzero set the potentials plus smooth local theta-potentials have uniform lower bounds, so replace them by uniformly bounded local psh truncations. Locality preserves the product there. Capacity convergence and Proposition 2.2 give convergence both of integral eta_L dnu_j and of integral eta_L(v_j-psi)dnu_j, using a smooth partition of unity. These are strictly local bounded applications in every current slot. The omitted weighted integral is bounded by B integral(1-eta_L)dnu_j. Since total masses equal m, this tail tends, for fixed L, to B integral(1-eta_L)dnu. Let L tend to infinity; the latter tends to zero because nu has no polar mass. Thus the entire bounded energy converges, even with unbounded psi.

For arbitrary v_j in F^1 decreasing to u in F^1, monotonicity gives E(u)<=E(v_j)<=E(max(v_j,psi-L)). For fixed L the upper potentials converge within a uniform bounded type and the preceding lemma applies. Taking j to infinity and then L to infinity proves E(v_j)->E(u). In particular every decreasing bounded-type approximation of u has the same value. Concavity follows by taking simultaneous truncations u_L,v_L: their convex combinations decrease to (1-t)u+tv, which belongs to the domain by Vu convexity, so decreasing continuity passes bounded concavity to the limit. For d=0 the formula is affine. No increasing-sequence or unbounded-velocity theorem is claimed.
