Partial P15.1 theorem, with no fibration hypothesis. Let X be a connected smooth compact Kähler n-fold, n>=1, alpha=[theta] pseudoeffective with vol(alpha)=0, and psi an actual theta-psh potential. Set dc=i(barpartial-partial)/(4pi), ddc=i partial barpartial/(2pi), T_u=theta+ddc u, and C_psi={u theta-psh: psi-C<=u<=psi+C for some C}. Differences and sup norms are taken off the common polar locus. This retains bounded singularity type, not merely ideal equivalence.

Foundations and checked scope. BEGZ, Acta Math. 205 (2010), 199–262, arXiv:0812.3674v2, Definition 1.1, Propositions 1.4 and 1.6, Theorem 1.8: actual-current nonpluripolar products on compact Kähler X are finite closed positive, symmetric, multilinear on positive sums, plurifinely local, and their traces have no pluripolar mass; smooth factors multiply ordinarily. DDNL, Anal. PDE 11 (2018), 2049–2087, Proposition 2.1: for n pseudoeffective smooth backgrounds, replacing each potential by one of the same bounded type preserves the top mixed integral. Theorem 2.4: making every potential less singular increases that integral. We apply these with k current slots and n-k smooth Kähler slots of potential zero, so the stated pseudoeffective hypotheses suffice. BEGZ Definition 1.17 defines big volume by minimal-singularity mass, gives the boundary limit vol(alpha+epsilon lambda)->vol(alpha), and identifies nef positive products with cup products. An arbitrary big-class actual-current mass is bounded above by volume by DDNL monotonicity; equality is never assumed.

Vu, arXiv:2005.13241v3 (25 March 2022), Lemmas 2.5–2.6 and Theorem 2.7: for a closed positive (n-1,n-1)-current R and bounded R-admissible dsh a,b, the intrinsic diagonal Dirichlet product is positive and
 integral b<ddc a dot-wedge R> = integral a<ddc b dot-wedge R> = -integral<db dc a dot-wedge R>.
Admissibility means a quasi-psh difference representation whose poles have zero R-mass. Proposition 2.1(iii) identifies iterated relative products with the ordinary products used here. Every R below is a nonpluripolar product times smooth positive closed factors, or is entirely smooth, so all differences and smooth tests are admissible and all products finite.

Convention and defect check: Vu's source operator is twice ours, so source potential u/2 represents the same current; DDNL uses i partial barpartial, so its source potential is u/(2pi). Positive rescaling preserves mass/type statements. Vu's source gradient of a/2 is half our gradient; multiplying source IBP by 2 gives the displayed formula. Intrinsically da dc a R=(1/2)ddc(a^2)R-a ddc a R. Vu v3 Eq. (2.3) has this correct 1/2, but repeated page-6/page-11 identities omit it. We preserve their raw quotations and never use those repetitions as definitions. The corrected local square coefficients are 1,1,-1/2. A direct cutoff check verifies the needed IBP: for negative quasi-psh representations a=phi1-phi2,b=phi3-phi4 with ddc phi_j>=-Omega, put chi_k=1+max(sum phi_j,-k)/k. Then 0<=chi_k<=1, ddc chi_k>=-4Omega/k, and correct bounded Stokes gives
 0<=integral dchi_k dcchi_k R=-integral chi_k ddc chi_k R<=4 integral Omega R/k.
The gradients of bounded difference truncations have mass <=2||a_k||_infty integral Omega R. Locality on {chi_k>0}, dominated convergence off the representation poles, and Cauchy–Schwarz make every cutoff cross term tend to zero. Bounded IBP therefore proves symmetry and the negative diagonal identity; polarization proves the full formula. Thus concavity below uses a positive intrinsic gradient, with the correct factor.

Define M_k=integral<T_psi^k>omega^(n-k), with M_0=integral omega^n, and d=max{k:M_k>0}. The carrier is X, of dimension n, and the nonpluripolar product has bidegree (d,d). The raw leading mass M_d is positive and is never normalized to one.

Degree proof. For epsilon>0, T_psi+epsilon omega is strictly positive and its class is big. Minimal singularities and DDNL monotonicity give
 0<=M_n<=P_epsilon:=integral<(T_psi+epsilon omega)^n><=vol(alpha+epsilon[omega]).
Actual-current multilinearity gives P_epsilon=sum_k binom(n,k) epsilon^(n-k) M_k. The volume bound tends to zero; hence M_n=0 and d<n. Any two Kähler forms are pointwise comparable, so their positive trace tests vanish for exactly the same degrees; d is auxiliary-form independent. DDNL bounded-type equality gives integral<T_u1...T_uk>omega^(n-k)=M_k for u_i in C_psi. For k>d this positive current has zero strictly positive trace and is therefore zero (its coefficient measures are dominated by its trace). Closedness makes M_k a cohomological polynomial of degree n-k in [omega]. There is no numerical-dimension identification.

Energy theorem. For lambda=[omega], r=n-d, define
 E_psi,lambda(u)=1/(d+1) sum_(a=0)^d integral (u-psi)<T_u^a T_psi^(d-a)>omega^r.
It is representative independent, increasing and concave, and satisfies
 E_psi(u)-E_psi(v)=E_v(u), E_psi(psi)=0,
 E_psi(u+c)=E_psi(u)+cM_d,
 |E_psi(u)-E_psi(v)|<=M_d||u-v||_infty.

Proof of the cocycle and variation, also valid at every fixed degree q with W=omega^(n-q), without the zero-volume assumption. On the convex hull of finitely many same-type potentials write u_t=u0+sum t_i h_i and A_i(t)=integral h_i<T_ut^q>W. These are polynomials with finite coefficient integrals. For q>=1, partial_j A_i=q integral h_i<ddc h_j T_ut^(q-1)>W is symmetric in i,j by the admissible IBP above; for q=0 the A_i are constant. Thus this polynomial one-form is closed. Its line integral from v to u equals
 integral_0^1 integral (u-v)<((1-t)T_v+tT_u)^q>W dt
 =1/(q+1)sum_a integral(u-v)<T_u^aT_v^(q-a)>W,
since binom(q,a) integral_0^1 t^a(1-t)^(q-a)dt=1/(q+1). Ordinary Stokes on the finite triangle psi,v,u proves the cocycle, including boundary points by polynomial continuity. Along u_t=(1-t)u+tv, h=v-u,
 E'(t)=integral h<T_ut^d>omega^r,
 E''(t)=-d integral<dh dc h dot-wedge (<T_ut^(d-1)>omega^r)><=0 for d>=1.
This is a polynomial identity with continuous endpoint derivatives. Pullbacks to finite simplices are polynomial and have the ordinary chain rule along C1/C2 parameter paths. No arbitrary unbounded or discontinuous velocity is covered. For d=0 the energy is affine and no gradient formula is asserted. Order, constant shifts, and the sandwiches v-delta<=u<=v+delta prove the remaining laws.

Representative independence proof. The compact Kähler ddc-lemma gives omega1-omega0=ddc g for cohomologous Kähler forms, with smooth real g. The exact used statement is that cohomologous smooth closed real (1,1)-forms on compact Kähler X differ by ddc of a smooth real function; a standard published reference is Deligne–Griffiths–Morgan–Sullivan, Invent. Math. 29 (1975), 245–274, §5, principle of two types. Expand omega1^r-omega0^r as ddc g sum_b omega1^b omega0^(r-1-b). For h=u-psi, Q=<T_u^aT_psi^(d-a)>, each R=Q omega1^b omega0^(r-1-b) is closed positive of degree n-1 with no representation-pole mass, also for d=0 when it is smooth. IBP gives integral h ddc g R=integral g<ddc h dot-wedge R>. The latter signed current is the difference of two degree-(d+1) products of same-type currents, both zero. This proves independence without illicit signed products. Changing one factor in a mixed product of r Kähler forms gives the same argument. Choosing fixed smooth representatives of a finite cohomology basis near any Kähler class makes the energy a homogeneous polynomial of degree r in that class, hence continuous. Changing theta to theta+ddc g and u,psi to u-g,psi-g changes no actual current or difference. Changing the actual anchor within its type has the cocycle, not absolute anchor independence.

Perturbative energy theorem. Define the unnormalized relative top energy in theta+epsilon omega, anchored at psi, by the analogous (n+1)-term sum, denoted E_epsilon,omega(v). It is defined for (theta+epsilon omega)-psh v of the bounded type psi; T_v alone need not be positive. The degree-n cocycle argument above applies to the positive perturbed currents and gives Lipschitz constant P_epsilon, the actual anchor mass. For fixed u in C_psi, write E_psi^(k),omega(u) for the degree-k relative sum. Actual-current expansion gives exactly
 E_epsilon,omega(u)=sum_(k=0)^d binom(n,k)epsilon^(n-k)E_psi^(k),omega(u),
 |E_psi^(k),omega(u)|<=||u-psi||_infty M_k.
The mixed-sum coefficient identity is sum_j binom(j,a)binom(n-j,k-a)=binom(n+1,k+1), proved by marking the (a+1)-st member of a (k+1)-subset of {0,...,n}; division by n+1 gives binom(n,k)/(k+1).

Let ANY Kähler forms omega_epsilon have [omega_epsilon]->lambda, with lambda Kähler, without representative convergence. If u_epsilon is perturbed-psh, of the same bounded type, and ||u_epsilon-u||_infty=delta_epsilon->0 for a fixed u in C_psi, then
 E_epsilon,omega_epsilon(u_epsilon)/(binom(n,d)epsilon^(n-d))->E_psi,lambda(u).
Indeed the replacement error is <=delta_epsilon P_epsilon. The M_k are bounded cohomological polynomials on a compact neighborhood of lambda, so P_epsilon=O(epsilon^(n-d)) uniformly. The normalized k<d terms are O(epsilon^(d-k)); the leading energy is representative independent and class-continuous. This is only a leading-coefficient assertion. The full positive-epsilon energy can depend on representatives: on P1xP1, theta=p1*kappa, psi=0, u=p1*v smooth nonconstant, omega0=p1*kappa+p2*kappa, replacing omega0 by omega0+t ddc u changes its degree-zero coefficient by -2t integral dv dc v, although the leading degree-one coefficient is unchanged.

Auxiliary differential check, not a P15.2 claim. For a fixed omega, the measure expansion gives <(T_u+epsilon omega)^n>/(binom(n,d)epsilon^(n-d))-><T_u^d>omega^r. For moving same-type u_epsilon, factor the difference into n signed mixed terms. Smooth-test IBP bounds each by C_f delta_epsilon times the mixed mass with one omega factor. DDNL bounded-type equality identifies that mass with Q_epsilon=sum_(k=0)^d binom(n-1,k)epsilon^(n-1-k)M_k=O(epsilon^(r-1)). Therefore the normalized test error is O_f(delta_epsilon/epsilon); o(epsilon) is a sufficient potential rate. This includes n=1,d=0 using the empty product. Smoothly convergent positive auxiliary forms permit uniform constants. Merely class-convergent forms do not supply the needed test bound or a full-form-free measure limit.
