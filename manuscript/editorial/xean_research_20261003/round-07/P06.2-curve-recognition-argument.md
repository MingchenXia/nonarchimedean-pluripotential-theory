## Status, conventions, and exact statements

This is a new, unverified partial submission for P06.2. Its proof bytes have
an author self-audit only. No old review verdict is transferred to them, and
no formal verification or priority claim is made. The original atom remains
open. The blocked preceding workspace has been preserved without edits.

All spaces and morphisms are Berkovich analytic spaces over the field
specified in each statement. An ordinary open is open in the underlying
topology. An analytic neighbourhood contains an ordinary neighbourhood;
an analytic domain alone need not do so. A curve is separated and pure of
analytic dimension one. Put $D=d'd''$. All forms and currents in an equation
below are **ordinary** Chambert--Loir--Ducros (CLD) forms and currents,
defined on ordinary opens. For a pure space $Z$, $[u]=u\delta_Z$ uses
canonical integration with the actual generic artinian lengths of its
structure, including nilpotents. In dimension one,
\[
                 D[u](\psi)=\int_Z uD\psi,
                 \qquad \psi\in A_c^{0,0}(Z).
\]
The equation is tested against every compact ordinary smooth scalar test.
Smooth top forms define signed Radon measures, so these integrals with a
continuous multiplier are well defined. This is not an a priori assertion
that the differentiated current $D[u]$ is a measure.

Here ``harmonic'' on an arbitrary curve means Gubler--Rabinoff (GR)
Definition 3.13: both signs are subharmonic in GR Definition 3.4, which
includes analytic field extensions. On a smooth strict curve over a
nontrivially valued field this agrees with Thuillier harmonicity. Classical
pluriharmonicity means that both signs are classically psh in GR Definition
4.1, with its **every analytic extension and every analytic curve map**
quantifiers. Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff (GJR) harmonicity is the real-PL
residual numerical-triviality notion; it is not ordinary CLD smoothness.
Real PL is used in the precise GR 2.16/GJR convention: G-locally on
strict analytic domains the function is a finite real linear combination
of logarithms of analytic units. The local real-PL conclusions below
refer to that convention, not to one ordinary smooth moment expression.

**Theorem A (intrinsic curve recognition).** Let $K$ be algebraically
closed, complete, and nontrivially valued. Let $Z$ be a good separated
strict boundaryless pure analytic curve. If $u:Z\to\mathbb R$ is continuous
and $D[u]=0$, then $u$ is GR-harmonic, classically pluriharmonic, locally
real PL, and GJR harmonic. If $Z$ is smooth, $u$ is Thuillier harmonic.
There is no reducedness, normality, properness, or global algebraic-model
hypothesis. The theorem also applies on every ordinary open of $Z$.

**Proposition B (local images, arbitrary field).** Let $K$ be complete and
nontrivially valued, $X$ good separated strict boundaryless, and
$f:Y\to X$ a $K$-analytic morphism from a good separated smooth strict
boundaryless pure curve. At each analytic point $y\in Y$, either:

1. on an ordinary neighbourhood of $y$, $f$ factors analytically through
   $\mathcal M(E)$ for a finite field extension $E/K$, mapping to a single
   rigid point of $X$; or
2. there are strict affinoid neighbourhoods $V$ of $y$ and $U$ of $f(y)$,
   with both points in their ordinary interiors, such that $V$ is connected
   and normal and $V\to U$ is finite. Its reduced kernel image
   $C\hookrightarrow U$ is irreducible and pure one-dimensional,
   $V\to C$ is finite surjective, and it admits a finite analytic lift to
   the finite normalization of $C$.

**Theorem C (ground-field ambient probes).** Let $K$ be algebraically
closed, complete, and nontrivially valued. Let $X$ be good separated strict
boundaryless and pure of dimension $n\geq1$. If $h:X\to\mathbb R$ is
continuous with $D[h]=0$, then, for every $K$-analytic map $f:Y\to X$ as
in Proposition B, $h\circ f$ is Thuillier harmonic on $Y$.

Theorem C is fieldwise. It does **not** say that $h$ is classically
pluriharmonic on $X$, or that the equation ascends to $X_L$. Even when
$K$ is algebraically closed, analytic extensions $L/K$ can be
transcendental. The universal conclusion of Theorem A is justified instead
by the already-recognized curve harmonicity and the precise GR curve
functoriality theorem.

### S2. Affinoid neighbourhoods, algebra, dimensions, and smoothness

CLD means A. Chambert-Loir and A. Ducros, arXiv:1204.6277v2,
25 July 2025. The exact consequences needed here are these.

* CLD 11.1.2: an affinoid algebra is noetherian, its ideals are closed,
  $\mathcal M(A)\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is surjective, and the spectral
  seminorm vanishes precisely on the nilpotents. On a reduced affinoid
  algebra it is an equivalent Banach norm. A strict affinoid algebra has
  a bounded quotient presentation from a Tate algebra with unit radii.
* CLD 11.1.5--7: a good analytic space has an affinoid-neighbourhood
  basis, and ordinary opens are analytic domains. CLD 11.7.7 explicitly
  adds that every point of a good strict space over a nontrivially valued
  field has a **strict affinoid neighbourhood**. Applying it inside a
  prescribed ordinary open gives such a neighbourhood there.
* CLD 11.2.1--3: a closed analytic subspace of $\mathcal M(A)$ is
  $\mathcal M(A/I)$ for an ideal $I$; on an affinoid the Zariski closed
  sets are such zero sets. The reduction has the same carrier. Analytic
  domains preserve local analytic dimension, and the dimension of a
  strict affinoid equals the Krull dimension of its algebra. CLD 11.2.5:
  affinoids have finitely many irreducible components; an irreducible
  analytic space is pure, and its proper closed analytic subsets have
  smaller dimension.
* CLD 11.3.1: the stated local-ring regularity conditions persist on
  analytic domains; the normalization is finite surjective, and locally
  is the integral closure of the reduced algebra in its total quotient
  ring. CLD 11.4.8: quasi-smoothness is geometric regularity, hence implies
  regularity and normality here. CLD 11.4.9: on good spaces smoothness
  means quasi-smoothness together with relative interior. Thus a
  boundaryless rig-smooth curve is smooth.

We also use the strict affinoid Nullstellensatz, in its exact form from
Bosch--Güntzer--Remmert (BGR), *Non-Archimedean Analysis*, 6.1.2/1:
over a complete nontrivially valued field, the residue field at a maximal
ideal of a strict affinoid algebra is finite over the ground field. Thus
every nonempty Zariski closed subset of a strict affinoid contains a rigid
point. This follows by choosing a maximal ideal of its nonzero quotient
and using the finite-extension valuation. A zero-dimensional strict
affinoid algebra is finite over $K$: it is noetherian artinian, its
semisimple quotient is a finite product of the preceding finite fields,
and its nilpotent radical has a finite filtration with finite-dimensional
successive quotients. A fresh BGR PDF is not in the supplied cache; this
standard primary theorem is explicitly stated and cited, not claimed to
have been verified by a PDF-byte check. Its strict/nontrivial hypotheses
are checked at every application.

The other valued-field fact is BGR 3.2.4/2: a finite algebraic extension
of a complete non-Archimedean field has a unique extending absolute value
and is complete. It includes inseparable extensions. In the application
to a finite field inside a reduced affinoid, uniqueness also follows by
equivalence of finite-dimensional ultrametric norms: for two extending
norms, $|z|_1\leq C|z|_2$; applying this to $z^m$ and taking $m$th roots
gives $|z|_1\leq|z|_2$, and then the reverse inequality. The usual
finite-dimensional completeness and norm-equivalence proof is by
induction, using that a complete codimension-one subspace is closed and
has positive distance from any fixed vector outside it.

### S3. Boundary and local finiteness, with different quantifiers

CLD 11.4.1 states that for an analytic-domain inclusion the relative
interior is the ordinary topological interior. An algebraic
analytification is boundaryless. For $f:Y\to X$ with **separated target**
$X$, formula 11.4.1.3 states exactly
\[
 \operatorname{Int}(Y/X)\cap f^{-1}(\operatorname{Int}(X))
       =\operatorname{Int}(Y),\qquad
 \partial Y=\partial(Y/X)\cup f^{-1}(\partial X).
\]
Boundaryless maps are stable under composition and analytic base change.
The equality is also proved in the author-hosted Conrad--Temkin,
*Descent for non-Archimedean analytic spaces*, arXiv:1912.06230v2,
Lemma 2.9: for $\psi:A\to B$ and $\phi:B\to C$, the inclusion
$\operatorname{Int}(A/B)\cap\psi^{-1}\operatorname{Int}(B/C)
\subset\operatorname{Int}(A/C)$ is equality when $\phi$ is locally
separated. We inspected its actual proof. Only this boundary equality,
not a current-ascent or general descent result from that paper, is used.

CLD 11.4.4 states that finite maps are boundaryless, finite maps to an
affinoid have affinoid inverse images, and finite analytic maps are
equivalent to finite Banach algebra maps on affinoids. Finite Banach
$A$-algebras and finite ring $A$-algebras are equivalent categories; the
finite-module quotient topology determines the Banach topology. Its
footnote explicitly adds: if $|K^\times|\ne\{1\}$ and $A$ is strict,
a Banach $A$-algebra finite as a ring algebra is finite Banach. A map is
**finite at $y$** precisely when it is finite from an analytic
neighbourhood $V$ of $y$ to an analytic neighbourhood $U$ of its image.

CLD 11.4.5 states that
$\dim_y f=\dim_y(Y_{f(y)})$, with the actual fibre over
$\mathcal H(f(y))$, is Zariski upper semicontinuous (citing Ducros,
*Variation de la dimension relative*, Theorem 4.9), and that $f$ is
finite at $y$ if and only if $\dim_y f=0$ and
$y\in\operatorname{Int}(Y/X)$. This is the local criterion used below.
CLD 11.6.6 concerns finiteness over a target neighbourhood of an **entire
fibre** and has a compactness hypothesis. We neither use that different
criterion nor assert compactness of an arbitrary original fibre.

### S4. The checked affinoid embedding theorem

M. van der Put, *The class group of a one-dimensional affinoid space*,
Ann. Inst. Fourier 30(4) (1980), 155--164, Theorem 1.1, printed page 156:

> Every 1-dimensional, normal, connected affinoid space $Y=\mathrm{Sp}(A)$
> is an affinoid subspace of a non-singular complete curve.

The field assumption on printed page 155 is that $k$ is **complete and
algebraically closed**, with a non-Archimedean valuation. Its affinoid
convention is an actual quotient of $k\langle T_1,\ldots,T_m\rangle$;
these are strict affinoids. The proof defines normal/connected by
integral closedness and absence of nontrivial idempotents. It glues finitely
many affinoid pieces to $Y$ and explicitly concludes that $Y$ is an
affinoid domain of the resulting **algebraic** complete nonsingular curve.
Over our algebraically closed field that curve is smooth and proper.
We use this theorem only over the same algebraically closed ground field.

For clarity about analytic categories, the exact Berkovich-domain
formulation is also explicitly used in Thuillier, HAL tel-00010990v1,
the proof of Theorem 2.3.8, printed page 53: a pure one-dimensional
rig-smooth strict affinoid, after a finite separable extension $k_1/k$,
is isomorphic to an affinoid domain of a smooth proper algebraic curve
over $k_1$, with a citation to van der Put's theorem. This is an actual
assertion in that proof, not an inference from its bibliography. In our
application $k=K$ is algebraically closed, so that extension is trivial.
The affinoid interpretation identifies
$\mathrm{Sp}(A)$ with $\mathcal M(A)$, bounded algebra maps with analytic
maps, and an affinoid subdomain of the rigid algebraic analytification
with the affinoid domain of its Berkovich analytification. This is the
affinoid-domain compatibility in Berkovich 1993, §1.6, and is precisely
the interpretation used by Thuillier in the inspected passage. We do
not invoke an unspecified equivalence of arbitrary nonparacompact spaces.
All translation here is on one affinoid and an algebraic curve. It
preserves the affinoid algebra, its morphism, and its analytic-domain
structure. The primary input for this proof is the **explicit
Berkovich-domain assertion in Thuillier's proof just quoted**, used only
over algebraically closed $K$, with its embedding premise independently
checked in van der Put. No separately unchecked general equivalence
theorem is needed as a premise. The interpretation does not make the
entire affinoid ordinary open; the
ordinary-interior step is separately proved below.

### S5. Wanner comparison and the included-test issue

V. Wanner, arXiv:1801.04713v3, 8 November 2018, Section 4 fixes a
**smooth proper algebraic** curve over an **algebraically closed complete
nontrivially valued** field. Theorem 4.7 states: for an ordinary open
$W\subset C^{\mathrm{an}}$, a continuous real-valued function that is
psh is Thuillier-subharmonic. Corollary 4.8 states the equivalence for
continuous real-valued functions. In that source, Definition 3.16 means
by psh positivity of the current $D[u]$, not membership in an
approximation-generated hierarchy.

Here only Theorem 4.7's forward implication is needed. Its compact scalar
tests from Definition 3.11 have presentations in algebraic very-affine
unit moments. Those regular units are analytic units, so each such test
is an ordinary CLD test on the same ordinary open, with the same compact
support. The differentials commute with this inclusion. The integration
pairing is the canonical CLD pairing used explicitly before Wanner
Definition 3.16:
$D[u](\psi)=\int_W uD\psi$, described there as a current in the sense
of CLD. On these unit moments the calibration is the canonical one;
algebraic tropical chart integration is that construction restricted to
the algebraic moments. Thus a zero equation against every ordinary
analytic test implies Wanner-current positivity for both signs. We do
not need, and do not claim to prove, equality of the entire algebraic
and analytic test sheaves. Canonical integration and its calibration are
unchanged by an analytic isomorphism and are compatible with analytic
domains (CLD 14.3.5, 15.7.1--2 and §7.7). In our comparison application
the curves are smooth and reduced; there is no multiplicity normalization
or probability normalization to change.

### S6. Already-recognized harmonicity, not current pullback

GR is W. Gubler and J. Rabinoff, arXiv:2506.13548v2,
17 September 2025. The needed exact statements are:

* 3.4.1--2: on a smooth connected strict analytic curve over a
  nontrivially valued field, a finite upper semicontinuous function is
  GR-subharmonic if and only if it is Thuillier-subharmonic. The
  identically $-\infty$ alternative in 3.4.1 is irrelevant here.
* 3.12 and Definition 3.13: on a smooth strict curve, harmonicity agrees
  with both signs being subharmonic; on an arbitrary analytic curve,
  harmonicity is defined by that two-sign property. The proof in 3.12
  uses the strict affinoid Dirichlet solution (Thuillier Lemma 2.3.9)
  and comparison for both signs.
* Proposition 3.14(1,6,7): harmonicity is a sheaf; it is preserved and
  detected by every analytic field extension; for any morphism of
  analytic curves, harmonicity pulls back, and the converse holds when
  that morphism is finite surjective. These statements have no
  reducedness or separability restriction. They concern functions
  **already** harmonic, not arbitrary continuous zero currents.
* Lemma 3.9(1--2): the finite normalization of a reduced strict analytic
  curve is rig-smooth if the field is perfect, and is an isomorphism
  outside a discrete rigid set. We use the smoothness assertion only
  when $K$ is algebraically closed and hence perfect; finiteness and
  boundarylessness make the normalization smooth in the good-space
  sense of S2.
* 4.1.2: on an analytic curve, classical psh equals GR-subharmonicity.
  Its extension-field and curve-map implication uses Proposition
  3.5(7,8), not a scalar-current assertion. Consequently a GR-harmonic
  curve function is classically pluriharmonic.
* Proposition 3.15: on **any strict analytic curve** over a
  nontrivially valued field, a continuous function is harmonic if and
  only if it is real PL and every residual class
  $L_u(x)\in\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{red}(Z,x))_{\mathbb R}$
  is numerically trivial. This is precisely GJR harmonicity. GJR,
  arXiv:2111.05741v3, Definition 7.2 and Remark 7.3(1), define
  harmonicity by both-sign semipositivity and equate it to this numerical
  triviality for an R-PL function. Nonreduced curves are permitted in
  GR Proposition 3.15; its proof explicitly passes through reduction.

These sources justify the universal curve conclusions only after
recognition. In Theorem C the pullback is a pullback of a recognized
harmonic function; no arbitrary finite-map current pullback is imported.

## Smooth curve recognition on actual ordinary neighbourhoods

**Lemma 1 (ordinary local algebraic model).** Under the field hypotheses
of Theorem A, every point $z$ of a good separated smooth strict
boundaryless pure curve $Z$ has an ordinary neighbourhood analytically
isomorphic to an ordinary open of a smooth proper algebraic curve's
analytification over the same $K$.

**Proof.** Choose a strict affinoid neighbourhood $Q_0$ of the arbitrary
analytic point $z$ inside $Z$, using S2. It is normal and pure
one-dimensional by geometric regularity and domain invariance. An
affinoid algebra has finitely many connected components, represented by
idempotents. Select the component $Q$ containing $z$. Since it is open
and closed in $Q_0$, it is still a strict affinoid neighbourhood of $z$
in $Z$. Its algebra is a normal domain of dimension one: in a
noetherian normal ring distinct minimal primes cannot meet, because
every localization is a domain. The finitely many minimal components
therefore give an idempotent decomposition; connectedness leaves one.

S4 now supplies an isomorphism $j:Q\simeq Q'$, where $Q'$ is an
affinoid domain in $C^{\mathrm{an}}$ for a nonsingular complete
algebraic curve $C$ over $K$. If necessary take its connected component
containing $Q'$; $C$ is a smooth proper algebraic curve. This is a domain
embedding, so an additional step is required.

Apply S3 to $Q\hookrightarrow Z$. Since $Z$ is separated and
boundaryless,
\[
      \operatorname{int}_Z(Q)=\operatorname{Int}(Q)=Q\setminus\partial Q.
\]
Apply S3 again to $Q'\hookrightarrow C^{\mathrm{an}}$. The latter is
separated and boundaryless, so
\[
      \operatorname{int}_{C^{\mathrm{an}}}(Q')
             =\operatorname{Int}(Q')=Q'\setminus\partial Q'.
\]
Absolute analytic interior is invariant under the isomorphism $j$.
Therefore $j$ identifies these two **ordinary opens**. The first contains
$z$ because $Q$ was a neighbourhood. On both sides the open analytic
structure is the restriction of the domain structure, so the induced
map is an analytic isomorphism of ordinary neighbourhoods. This proof
treats every analytic point, including types three and four; it uses
neither a rigid-point-density cover nor an admissible cover in place of
an ordinary one. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (smooth recognition).** Under these hypotheses, a continuous
$u$ with $D[u]=0$ on $Z$ is Thuillier harmonic and GR-harmonic.

**Proof.** At any $z$, use Lemma 1 to identify an ordinary neighbourhood
$O$ with $W\subset C^{\mathrm{an}}$. Restriction to $O$ is a legitimate
restriction of a current. For a compact scalar test $\psi$ on $W$, its
pullback is a compact scalar test on $O$ and can be extended by zero to
$Z$; its support is compactly contained in the open. Canonical
integration and $D$ are invariant under the analytic isomorphism. Thus
the transported continuous function $v=u\circ j^{-1}$ satisfies
$D[v]=0$ on $W$ against every ordinary test. In particular both signs
satisfy Wanner's positive-current hypothesis by S5. All hypotheses are
now met: same algebraically closed complete nontrivially valued field,
smooth proper algebraic curve, actual ordinary open, finite continuous
function. Theorem 4.7 makes $v$ and $-v$ Thuillier-subharmonic.

The two-sign criterion S6 makes $v$ harmonic. Pull back the already
recognized function by the open analytic isomorphism. GR 3.4.2 and 3.12
identify this with Thuillier harmonicity on $O$. The ordinary opens $O$
cover $Z$ and harmonicity is a sheaf, so $u$ is harmonic throughout
$Z$. No current has been pulled through a scalar extension or a
nonisomorphic finite map. $\square$

## Singular and nonreduced intrinsic recognition

**Proof of Theorem A.** First suppose $Z$ reduced. Its finite
normalization $\nu:\widetilde Z\to Z$ is a good separated strict pure
curve. It is boundaryless since the finite map is boundaryless and $Z$
is boundaryless. The algebraically closed field is perfect, so S6 makes
it rig-smooth; S2 then makes it smooth. The normalization-transfer
theorem of n100 gives the actual equation
\[
                         D[u\circ\nu]=0
\]
on this normalization. Its hypotheses match exactly: reduced strict
boundaryless pure curve, complete nontrivially valued field, and a
continuous real multiplier. Lemma 2 recognizes $u\circ\nu$ as harmonic.
GR Proposition 3.14(7), for the finite surjective curve morphism $\nu$,
descends this **recognized** harmonicity to $u$. We have not deduced
the upstairs equation by inversion of a direct image.

For arbitrary $Z$, the closed immersion
$i:Z_{\mathrm{red}}\hookrightarrow Z$ is finite, surjective on carriers,
and its source is good separated strict boundaryless and pure
one-dimensional. Apply n100's closed-curve restriction with ambient
dimension $n=1$ and $C=Z_{\mathrm{red}}$. It yields
$D[u\circ i]=0$ with the reduced source's canonical integration.
This explicitly respects the original equation's possibly unequal
generic nilpotent lengths; they have not been divided out. The reduced
case proves $u\circ i$ harmonic. GR Proposition 3.14(7) applies to this
finite surjective morphism as well and descends harmonicity to $u$ on
the original nonreduced curve.

Finally apply GR 4.1.2 to each sign. This gives classical
pluriharmonicity with the full extension-field quantifiers on the
**curve**. GR Proposition 3.15 gives local real-PL regularity and
numerical triviality of all residual classes, which is GJR harmonicity.
All constructions apply after ordinary-open restriction. This proves
Theorem A. No ordinary CLD smooth harmonic regularity or ordinary
smooth approximation is asserted. $\square$

## The affinoid branch lemma, including Banach factorization

**Lemma 3.** Let $K$ be complete nontrivially valued, $\mathcal M(B)$
a connected normal strict affinoid pure curve, and
$f:\mathcal M(B)\to\mathcal M(A)$ an analytic map to a strict affinoid.
Either its bounded algebra map factors through a finite field $E/K$
and a rigid point, or $\dim_y f=0$ at every analytic source point.

**Proof.** As in Lemma 1, connected normality makes $B$ an integral
domain, of Krull dimension one. Fix an actual bounded Tate presentation
$K\langle T_1,\ldots,T_N\rangle\twoheadrightarrow A$ and write
$b_j=f^*(T_j)\in B$.

If every $b_j$ is algebraic over $K$, then
$E=K[b_1,\ldots,b_N]\subset B$ is finite-dimensional over $K$ and a
domain, hence a finite field. Every point of $\mathcal M(B)$ restricts
to the unique extending absolute value on $E$. Thus
$\rho_B|_E=|\cdot|_E$. Since $B$ is reduced its Banach norm is equivalent
to $\rho_B$; $E$ is therefore a closed complete finite-dimensional
subspace of $B$ with its usual finite-field topology. For every Tate
series, polynomial partial sums evaluated at the $b_j$ converge in $B$
under the bounded map and belong to $E$. Closedness puts the limit in
$E$. Consequently the **entire** bounded map, not just its polynomial
subalgebra, factors through $E$. Boundedness to $E$ follows from
$|e|_E=\rho_B(e)\leq C\|e\|_B$ and the bound of the original map;
descent to $A$ uses its quotient norm. Its image contains every $b_j$,
so $A\to E$ is surjective. The inclusion $E\to B$ is bounded by the
same norm equivalence. Hence $f$ factors analytically through the
singleton $\mathcal M(E)$, mapping to a rigid target point. No
separability is required for $E/K$.

Otherwise some $b=b_j$ is transcendental over $K$. Let $a$ be any rigid
source point. The element $b(a)\in\mathcal H(a)$ is algebraic over $K$;
let $P_a\in K[t]$ be its monic minimal polynomial. The element
$P_a(b)\in B$ is nonzero by transcendence, vanishes at $a$, and is not
a unit. A quotient of a noetherian one-dimensional domain by a nonzero
nonunit has dimension zero: every prime containing it is nonzero and
therefore maximal. S2's strict Nullstellensatz and the artinian
argument make $B/(P_a(b))$ finite over $K$, retaining its possible
nilpotents.

Put $x=f(a)$. The isometric embedding
$\mathcal H(x)\hookrightarrow\mathcal H(a)$ makes $x$ rigid, with
finite residue field over $K$. In the actual analytic fibre
$\mathcal M(B)\times_{\mathcal M(A)}\mathcal M(\mathcal H(x))$, the
coordinate $b$ equals the evaluated target coordinate. Its polynomial
$P_a$ is zero there, since it is zero after the injective residue-field
map into $\mathcal H(a)$. The universal property of a closed zero
subspace therefore factors the fibre as a closed subspace of
\[
                 \mathcal M(B/(P_a(b)))_{\mathcal H(x)}.
\]
This last space is finite over $\mathcal H(x)$ and zero-dimensional.
Equivalently, the completed fibre algebra is the quotient of the
scalar-extended finite zero algebra by the target-value relations.
Thus $\dim_a f=0$. This argument compares fibres over their actual
field and applies also when that field extension is inseparable.

The locus $F=\{y:\dim_y f\geq1\}$ is Zariski closed by S3. On a strict
affinoid any nonempty such locus is the zero set of a proper ideal and
has a rigid point by S2. Such a point would contradict the preceding
calculation. Hence $F$ is empty and $\dim_y f=0$ for **every** analytic
$y$. This uses Zariski upper semicontinuity, not an extension by
ordinary-topological density. $\square$

## Proof of the local image theorem

**Proof of Proposition B.** Fix $y\in Y$ and $x=f(y)$. Choose a strict

## Exact result

Let $K$ be complete and nontrivially valued. All analytic spaces and morphisms below are Berkovich spaces and morphisms over $K$. All smooth forms, tests, and currents are **ordinary** Chambert--Loir--Ducros (CLD) forms and currents on ordinary opens. Put $D=d'd''$. For a pure-dimensional space, $[h]=h\delta_X$ uses canonical integration with the **actual generic artinian lengths**, including generic nilpotents. A current of bidimension $(p,q)$ acts on compact tests of bidegree $(p,q)$.

**Theorem A (closed analytic curve restriction).** Let $X$ be good, separated, strict, boundaryless, and pure of dimension $n\geq1$. Let $\Omega\subset X$ be an ordinary open, and let $h:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ be finite and continuous, with
\[
 D(h\delta_\Omega)=0.
\]
For every closed analytic subspace $C\hookrightarrow X$ that is pure of dimension one,
\[
 D(h|_{C\cap\Omega}\,\delta_{C\cap\Omega})=0
 \quad\text{intrinsically on }C\cap\Omega.                 \tag{A}
\]
Neither the ambient space nor the curve is required to be reduced, normal, regular, or geometrically reduced. Inseparable residue fields are allowed. The statement is also local for a closed analytic curve in an ordinary open of $X$.

**Theorem B (rigid puncture removal).** Let $Y$ be a good, separated, strict, boundaryless reduced pure analytic curve. Let $F$ be a locally finite set of rigid points. If $g:Y\to\mathbb R$ is finite and continuous and $D[g]=0$ on $Y\setminus F$, then $D[g]=0$ on $Y$. No assertion that $D[g]$ is a measure is assumed.

**Theorem C (normalization, in the curve scope).** Let $Y$ be as in Theorem B and let $\nu:\widetilde Y\to Y$ be its finite normalization. A continuous $g$ with $D[g]=0$ satisfies
\[
 D[g\circ\nu]=0\quad\text{on }\widetilde Y,
 \qquad \nu_*[(g\circ\nu)]=[g].                            \tag{C}
\]
The second identity is against **every ordinary compact $(1,1)$ test**. The first is proved from the equation on the actual isomorphism locus and rigid puncture removal. It is not a premise about arbitrary finite-map current pullback.

In particular Theorem A applies to closed algebraic curves in ordinary opens of separated pure finite-type $K$-scheme analytifications, retaining the actual scheme structures. This passes beyond cone vertex tubes, simple-root charts, regular ambient varieties, and regular curve embeddings. It gives an intrinsic current equation, not yet classical pluriharmonicity on every curve probe. No scalar-current ascent or two-sign regularization theorem is claimed.

Apply this assertion to any given ordinary open to place the affinoid inside that open. In a strict zero-dimensional affinoid, the algebra is artinian and finite over $K$, so its carrier consists of finitely many rigid points. This last standard affinoid Nullstellensatz consequence is also implicit in CLD 13.3.1: an isolated point in a zero-dimensional analytic fibre has a local algebra finite over the completed residue field of the target. ## Elementary reducedness and prime avoidance

A reduced noetherian ring $R$ satisfies $S_1$. Indeed let $P_1,\ldots,P_t$ be its minimal primes. If $u$ avoids them and $uv=0$, the domain $R/P_i$ makes $v$ vanish in every quotient, so $v\in\bigcap_iP_i=0$. In a positive-dimensional localization the maximal ideal is not contained in any minimal prime; finite prime avoidance supplies a non-zero-divisor in that maximal ideal. Its depth is therefore at least one. Zero-dimensional localizations impose only depth at least zero. CLD 11.3.1 carries this property to every analytic domain of a reduced component. It does not make the **original** nonreduced or embedded-prime ambient source $S_1$.

Here is also the particular avoidance argument needed in the cuts. Let $A$ be a strict affinoid algebra, $J=(g_1,\ldots,g_s)$ an ideal, and $P_1,\ldots,P_t$ primes such that $J\not\subset P_i$ for every $i$. The coefficient sets
\[
 L_i=\{(a_1,\ldots,a_s)\in K^s:\ \textstyle\sum a_jg_j\in P_i\}
\]
are proper $K$-linear subspaces. A finite union of proper linear subspaces cannot cover $K^s$ when $K$ is infinite: choose a nonzero linear form vanishing on each subspace, take their product, and use that a nonzero polynomial over an infinite field cannot vanish on all $K^s$ (induction on the number of variables). A nontrivially valued field is infinite, for the powers of an element of nonunit absolute value are distinct. Thus there is an actual $f=\sum a_jg_j\in J$ outside all $P_i$. No separability, geometric point transitivity, or smoothness is involved.

## Continuous multiplication of positive weak limits in every degree

**Lemma 1.** On a good separated space, if positive currents $T_m,T$ of bidimension $(p,p)$ satisfy $T_m\to T$ weakly and $u$ is continuous, then $uT_m\to uT$ weakly.

**Proof.** Fix a compact $(p,p)$ ordinary smooth test $\omega$. Positive currents are symmetric, so replace the test by $(\omega+J\omega)/2$. Use finitely many ordinary moment charts near its compact support and a nonnegative compact subordinate partition $\theta_i$ with sum one there. On each chart choose a Euclidean smooth representative and symmetrize that representative before estimating it. With moment coordinates $s_1,\ldots,s_b$ put $Q=\sum_jd's_j\wedge d''s_j$. The matrix of $Q^p$ on $\Lambda^p\mathbb R^b$ is $p!$ times the identity, with the stipulated CLD coefficient sign. A symmetric representative of $\omega$ has a symmetric coefficient matrix $M(s)$, bounded in operator norm on the compact moment image of $\operatorname{supp}\theta_i$. Choose $c_ip!\geq\sup\|M(s)\|$. Then $c_iQ^p\pm\omega$ are middle-positive wherever $\theta_i$ is nonzero. If $p=0$, use $Q^0=1$ and the scalar bound; if $p>b$ the represented test is zero.

Extend $\beta_i=\theta_ic_iQ^p$ by zero after pullback, and put $\beta=\sum_i\beta_i$. It is compact and positive, and $\beta\pm\omega$ are positive. For any positive $S$, its coefficient measures consequently satisfy
\[
 |\mu_{S,\omega}|\leq\mu_{S,\beta}.
\]
The masses $T_m(\beta)$ are bounded because they converge to $T(\beta)$. Choose an ordinary smooth $v$ approximating $u$ uniformly within $\varepsilon$ on $\operatorname{supp}\beta$. For fixed $v$, weak convergence gives $T_m(v\omega)\to T(v\omega)$. The respective errors on replacing $v$ by $u$ are at most $\varepsilon T_m(\beta)$ and $\varepsilon T(\beta)$. Let $m$ tend to infinity and then $\varepsilon$ tend to zero. Antisymmetric tests vanish before and after scalar multiplication. This proves the claim. In particular, for a **fixed** positive current and a compact test, uniform scalar errors are bounded by a finite coefficient-measure mass. This bound alone would not make a differentiated pairing zero; our subsequent cuts also use a closed smooth wedge and its ambient equation. $\square$

## Closed-embedding test lifting

**Lemma 2.** If $i:Z\hookrightarrow M$ is a closed analytic embedding of good separated spaces, every compact ordinary $(p,q)$ test $\eta$ on $Z$ is $i^*\widetilde\eta$ for a compact ordinary test on $M$. Hence $i_*$ is injective on currents of every bidimension.

**Proof.** For $z$ in the compact support of $\eta$, write its ordinary local presentation using finitely many unit germs on $Z$ and a Euclidean smooth superform. The quotient sheaf $i_*\mathcal O_Z=\mathcal O_M/\mathcal I$ lifts those germs to ambient analytic germs. Their values at $z$ are nonzero, so after an ordinary shrinking they are units. Shrink further so their moment values belong to the coefficient domain and the intersection with $Z$ lies in the presentation open. The same Euclidean superform pulled back by the lifted moments is an ambient smooth form $\eta_z$ restricting to $\eta$ throughout that intersection.

Finitely many such ambient opens $W_i$ cover the compact support. Choose an ambient compact smooth cutoff $\chi$, equal to one nearby and supported in $W=\bigcup_iW_i$. On the compact support of $\chi$ choose compact nonnegative smooth partition functions $\theta_i$ subordinate to the $W_i$, with sum one nearby. The compact construction may be made in $W$ and extended by zero. Then
\[
 \widetilde\eta=\chi\sum_i\theta_i\eta_i
\]
is compact and ordinary smooth. Every contributing lift equals $\eta$ on its intersection with $Z$, including transition regions; hence $i^*\widetilde\eta=\eta$ everywhere, also outside its support. Evaluation on such lifts proves current injectivity. No continuous right inverse for a test topology, and no distributional current pullback, is asserted. $\square$

## Poincare--Lelong on an S1 source

**Lemma 3.** Let $M$ be good, separated, boundaryless, pure of dimension $r\geq1$, and satisfying $S_1$. Let $f$ be analytic and stalkwise a non-zero-divisor. With the log current extended from the unit locus,
\[
 D[\log|f|]=\delta_{\operatorname{div}_M(f)}.              \tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** The actual zero fibre is pure of dimension $r-1$. Compact top tests have support disjoint from it, so the log current is unambiguously defined by integration on the unit locus (CLD 15.2.1(b), 16.7.2). Outside the zero set the unit logarithm is ordinary smooth and its Hessian is zero. Near each zero, input 8 supplies an ordinary neighbourhood on which $f:M\to\mathbb A^1_K$ is pure of relative dimension $r-1$. The already imposed $S_1$ condition persists on all compact analytic domains. A compact partition reduces the formula to such neighbourhoods.

Fix a symmetric compact $(r-1,r-1)$ test $\omega$ in one of them. Choose finitely many compact analytic domains $V_i$ tropicalizing it, whose ordinary interiors cover its support, and write $V=\bigcup_iV_i$. For each nonempty index set $E$, the compact analytic domain $V_E=\bigcap_{i\in E}V_i$ is pure and $S_1$. Its fibres are analytic domains in the original fibres, so restriction preserves the pure relative dimension. Take a unit moment $b_E$ on $V_E$ and a superform $\alpha_E$ representing $\omega$. CLD 14.7.2 with $d_{\rm target}=1$ gives, for a common sufficiently small $R>0$,
\[
 (b_E,f)_{\rm trop}(V_E\setminus V_{E,0})^{(r)}
     \big|_{\log|f|<\log R}
 =P_E\times(-\infty,\log R),                              \tag{4}
\]
including product calibration. Here $P_E=b_{E,\rm trop}(V_{E,0})^{(r-1)}$ has the **actual zero-fibre** calibration, and the last factor has standard primitive calibration. Empty domains contribute zero.

Choose a smooth $u_1$ equal to one sufficiently far to the left and zero for $s\geq\log t$, with $0<t<R$, and put $u_2=1-u_1$. Extend $u_1(\log|f|)$ by one at zero, and $u_2$ by zero there. Write $\omega_1=u_1\omega$ and $\omega_2=u_2\omega$. For $\omega_2$ both proposed pairings vanish, as its support lies in the unit locus. The compact support of $d''\omega_1$, of maximum degree $r$, avoids the zero fibre. Choose $0<s<t$ smaller than the minimum of $|f|$ on that support and with $u_1=1$ near $\log s$. On $V_{\geq s}=V\cap\{|f|\geq s\}$ the logarithm is a smooth unit logarithm. Green's formula gives
\[
 \langle D[\log|f|],\omega_1\rangle
 =\int_{V_{\geq s}}\log|f|D\omega_1
 =-\int_{V_{\geq s}}^\partial d''\log|f|\wedge\omega_1.     \tag{5}
\]
The interior Hessian term is zero. The other boundary term contains $d''\omega_1$ and is zero: its support lies in the original interior of $V$ and away from the new inner boundary.

Boundary inclusion-exclusion reduces (5) to each $V_{E,\geq s}$. Because $u_1$ is zero near $t$, compute its boundary integral on $V_E\cap\{s\leq|f|\leq t\}$. Equation (4) identifies its top calibrated image with $P_E\times[\sigma,\tau]$, where $\sigma=\log s$, $\tau=\log t$. The inner face has negative fibre calibration and contributes $-\int_{P_E}\alpha_E$, because $u_1(\sigma)=1$. The outer face contributes zero. A side face is a fibre face of dimension at most $r-2$ times the interval. Contracting the last-coordinate $d''$ leaves an $(r-1)$-form coming from that fibre face, so its contribution is zero by dimension. For $r=1$ there are no such side faces. Thus the integral is $-\int_{V_{E,0}}\omega$. Ordinary inclusion-exclusion on the zero fibre proves
\[
 \langle D[\log|f|],\omega_1\rangle
 =\int_{V_0}\omega
 =\langle\delta_{\operatorname{div}(f)},\omega_1\rangle.
\]
Associated-cycle integration is input 3; the zero quotient itself need not be reduced or $S_1$. Symmetry suffices for all tests. This proves (3), without the excluded regular-element-to-$S_1$ sentence. $\square$

## Cutting positive cycles while preserving the ambient equation

**Lemma 4.** Let $U$ be good, separated, strict and boundaryless. Let
\[
 S=\sum_i m_i(i_i)_*\delta_{Z_i},\qquad m_i>0,
\]
be a finite effective integration cycle of dimension $r\geq2$, where the $Z_i$ are reduced pure closed analytic subspaces of $U$. Suppose $h$ is continuous and $D(hS)=0$. If $f$ is an ambient analytic function nonzero on every $Z_i$ component, let
\[
 S_f=\sum_i m_i(i_i)_*\delta_{\operatorname{div}_{Z_i}(f|_{Z_i})}.
\]
Then $S_f$ is an effective cycle of pure dimension $r-1$ and
\[
 D(hS_f)=0.                                               \tag{6}
\]
It is the stated sum of component divisor cycles, with their actual lengths. It is not identified with an arbitrary quotient scheme's associated cycle.

**Proof.** Each reduced source is $S_1$ and boundaryless, and $f|_{Z_i}$ is regular. To see the latter on analytic domains, a nonzero function on a reduced irreducible component avoids its minimal prime; local analytic rings and domains inherit regularity of that element, by faithful flat analytification/domain restriction or the Cartier sheaf property in CLD 13.6--7. Lemma 3 applies component by component.

Let $\kappa$ be a nonnegative even smooth kernel of integral one supported in $[-1,1]$, and put $\rho(s)=\int\max(s-t,0)\kappa(t)dt$. This is smooth convex, zero for $s\leq-1$, and $s$ for $s\geq1$. Define
\[
 w_m=\rho(\log|f|+m)-m
\]
on the unit locus, and set it equal to $-m$ near zero. The definitions agree on $|f|<e^{-m-1}$, so $w_m$ is ordinary smooth. Its Hessian $\alpha_m=Dw_m$ is doubly closed and strongly positive; on the unit locus it is
\[
 \rho''(\log|f|+m)d'\log|f|\wedge d''\log|f|.
\]
Set $S_m=\alpha_m\wedge S$. These are positive currents of bidimension $(r-1,r-1)$. On each component $S_m=D(w_mS)$ by boundarylessness. For every compact top test on that component, its support avoids the zero fibre, and eventually $w_m=\log|f|$ there. Hence $w_m\delta_{Z_i}\to[\log|f|]_{Z_i}$ weakly. Lemma 3 and weak continuity of differentiation give $S_m\to S_f$ after summing the finitely many components and using closed-embedding direct image.

Coefficient measures give $hS_m=\alpha_m\wedge(hS)$. The legitimate smooth product rule, using both closed differentials of $\alpha_m$, gives $D(hS_m)=\alpha_m\wedge D(hS)=0$. Lemma 1, in degree $p=r-1$, now gives $hS_m\to hS_f$. Differentiate the limit to obtain (6). The last cut has $r=2$, so its positive limit has bidimension $(1,1)$ and its differentiated tests are $(0,0)$; no negative degree occurs. $\square$

## Finite-residue-field scalar germs and uniformly bounded radial cutoffs

**Lemma 5.** Every ordinary smooth scalar germ at a rigid point $a$ is locally constant, including when $\mathcal H(a)/K$ is inseparable.

**Proof.** A scalar moment presentation uses finitely many analytic units $u$. For one of them put $q=|u(a)|>0$, and let $P(T)=T^e+\sum_{j<e}c_jT^j$ be the monic minimal polynomial of $u(a)$ over $K$. Uniqueness of the finite-extension absolute value makes every root, including inseparable repetitions, have norm $q$. Thus $|c_j|\leq q^{e-j}$ and $|c_0|=q^e$. On the ordinary neighbourhood $|P(u)|<q^e$ of $a$, a point with $|u|>q$ would have dominant leading term and $|P(u)|=|u|^e>q^e$. A point with $|u|<q$ would have dominant constant term and $|P(u)|=q^e$. Both contradict the defining inequality. Hence $|u|=q$ throughout that neighbourhood. A finite intersection makes every moment coordinate constant, hence the scalar presentation constant. No field extension of the current occurs. $\square$

**Lemma 6.** Let $Y$ be a reduced good separated strict boundaryless pure curve and let $a$ be rigid. There exist compact ordinary smooth functions $\chi_m$, equal to one near $a$, such that their supports shrink to $a$ and
\[
 \int_Y D\chi_m=0,
 \qquad \| (D\chi_m)_{\rm Rad}\|_{\rm TV}\leq M             \tag{7}
\]
for one finite $M$ independent of all sufficiently large integers $m$.

**Proof, including the compact and relative-dimension conditions.** Take a strict affinoid neighbourhood $N$ of $a$ inside $Y$, with $a$ in its ordinary interior. Its reduced affinoid algebra $B$ is pure of dimension one. The rigid point gives a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_a$. It is not contained in any minimal prime of $B$: otherwise the corresponding one-dimensional component would be contained in the zero-dimensional point. The preceding avoidance argument chooses $f\in\mathfrak m_a$ outside every minimal prime. Thus $f$ is regular on the reduced curve. Its zeros on $N$ form a pure zero-dimensional strict affinoid, so they are finitely many rigid points.

Remove its other zeros and shrink around $a$ into $\operatorname{Int}(N/Y)$. Input 8 gives an ordinary neighbourhood on which $f$ has pure relative dimension zero. Choose a smaller strict affinoid neighbourhood $V$ of $a$ contained there. Its zero fibre has **support exactly $\{a\}$**, though its artinian scheme structure may have positive length $\ell$. The compact pure one-dimensional domain $V$ is $S_1$ by reducedness and domain invariance. Therefore CLD 14.7.2 applies to $f:V\to\mathbb A^1_K$, with $d=1$, $r=1$ and the trivial moment $b$ into $\mathbb G_m^0$. It gives the exact calibrated negative interval, for $0<|f|<R$,
\[
 f_{\rm trop}(V\setminus V_0)^{(1)}
    =(-\infty,\log R),\qquad
 \text{calibration }D_0|e_1|,
 \quad D_0=\ell[\mathcal H(a):K]>0.                       \tag{8}
\]
Here the mass includes the full finite degree, inseparable or not. Compactness makes $f$ a pleasant proper moment on the punctured source for this calculation. The theorem permits boundary on $V$ and treats its calibration; it does not require us to assert $V$ boundaryless.

Choose $\chi\in C^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with $\chi=1$ for $s\leq-1$, $\chi=0$ for $s\geq0$, and $0\leq\chi\leq1$. Set $\chi_m=\chi(\log|f|+m)$ on $V\setminus\{a\}$ and extend it as one near $a$. For large $m$ this extends by zero to all $Y$ as a compact ordinary smooth function. Here is the support justification. Choose an ordinary neighbourhood $H$ of $a$ with closure contained in $\operatorname{Int}(V/Y)$. The compact $V\setminus H$ has no zero of $f$, so its positive minimum of $|f|$ is larger than $e^{-m}$ for large $m$. Thus the closed support of $\chi_m$, contained in $V\cap\{|f|\leq e^{-m}\}$, is inside $H$. It vanishes in a neighbourhood of the boundary of $V$, making extension by zero smooth. The same compact-minimum argument for any neighbourhood of $a$ proves shrinking supports. No density argument is used.

On the transition annulus,
\[
 D\chi_m=\chi''(\log|f|+m)d'\log|f|\wedge d''\log|f|.      \tag{9}
\]
Choose one smooth compact nonnegative $b$ on $\mathbb R$ with $b\geq|\chi''|$, and put $\beta_m=b(\log|f|+m)d'\log|f|\wedge d''\log|f|$. Its support is compact inside the same ordinary interior for large $m$. Both $\beta_m\pm D\chi_m$ are positive top forms, so their measures give $|(D\chi_m)_{\rm Rad}|\leq(\beta_m)_{\rm Rad}$. Calibrated integration in (8) gives exactly
\[
 \int_Y\beta_m=D_0\int_\mathbb R b(s)ds=M.
\]
This proves the uniform variation bound without identifying an unknown continuous Hessian with a measure. The same calibrated integration gives $\int D\chi_m=D_0\int\chi''=0$, since $\chi'$ has compact support. Independently Stokes gives zero because $\chi_m$ is compact smooth on the boundaryless $Y$. Thus the total integral has no omitted boundary term. This proves (7). $\square$

## Proof of rigid puncture removal

It suffices to remove one point $a$ in an ordinary neighbourhood avoiding the other points of $F$. Let $T=D[g]$ there; its restriction outside $a$ is zero. Choose any compact ordinary smooth cutoff $\theta$ equal to one near $a$. Lemma 5 says that a compact scalar test $\varphi$ is constant near $a$. Consequently $\varphi-\varphi(a)\theta$ has compact support disjoint from $a$, and
\[
 T(\varphi)=c\varphi(a),\qquad c=T(\theta).
\]
This proves $T=c\delta_a$ **as a functional on ordinary tests**, without an order-zero or measure hypothesis on $T$. For the functions of Lemma 6, $T(\chi_m)=c$ because $\chi_m(a)=1$. By the definition of differentiation and the zero total integral in (7),
\[
 |c|=\left|\int_Y(g-g(a))D\chi_m\right|
 \leq M\sup_{\operatorname{supp}\chi_m}|g-g(a)|
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Continuity at $a$ and the proved shrinking supports give the limit. Thus $c=0$. Locality proves Theorem B for locally finite $F$. Notice that this proof works directly on a reduced singular curve: a regular function avoiding all branches replaces a normal-curve uniformizer. $\square$

## Proper effective cuts containing a prescribed analytic curve

Fix $x\in C\cap\Omega$. Choose a strict affinoid neighbourhood $Q=\mathcal M(A)\subset\Omega$ with $x$ in $U=\operatorname{Int}(Q/X)$. This strict neighbourhood exists by input 1 applied inside $\Omega$. Every component of $Q$ has dimension $n$, with the original ambient generic length, and $C_Q=C\cap Q$ is a closed pure one-dimensional strict affinoid subspace. If it is empty there is nothing to prove.

Let $Z_i$ be the reduced components of $Q$ and $m_i=\operatorname{length}(A_{P_i})>0$ their actual generic lengths. On $U$,
\[
 \delta_\Omega|_U=S_0:=\sum_i m_i\delta_{Z_i\cap U},
 \qquad D(hS_0)=0.                                       \tag{10}
\]
This is associated-cycle integration and analytic-domain compatibility. Embedded primes do not add support components and are not assigned artificial multiplicity one. At subsequent stages only the constructed effective cycles are used.

Let $J=\mathcal I_{C_Q}(Q)\subset A$. Suppose $S_j$ is a finite effective **affinoid** cycle of pure dimension $d=n-j>1$, restricted to $U$, whose support on $Q$ contains the support of $C_Q$. If $P$ is the prime defining a current reduced component of dimension $d$, $J\subset P$ would imply that this entire component is a closed subspace of the one-dimensional $C_Q$, contradicting $d>1$. Thus choose $f_{j+1}\in J$ nonzero on every component of $S_j$, by the explicit finite avoidance argument. Define the next cycle on $Q$ by the weighted sum of its component divisor cycles and their reduced irreducible decompositions. CLD 13.7.1 and input 2 make **every** resulting component dimension $d-1$, with positive integer coefficient. There are finitely many on the affinoid. Since $f_{j+1}$ vanishes on $C_Q$, every point of $C_Q$ in the support of $S_j$ is in the support of the next divisor cycle. This preserves the containment. We do not assert a scheme embedding of $C_Q$ in each individual reduced component.

Lemma 4 on the boundaryless ordinary open $U$ gives $D(hS_{j+1})=0$ there. The functions are analytic on all $Q$ and hence on $U$; each reduced component intersected with $U$ is a closed boundaryless subspace of $U$. Domains disjoint from $U$ can stay in the affinoid cycle and contribute zero on this open. At the next step avoidance still treats all affinoid components. No operation uses tests meeting the boundary of $Q$.

After exactly $n-1$ cuts, or none if $n=1$, we have
\[
 S_{n-1}=\sum_{k=1}^t b_k\delta_{D_k},\quad b_k\in\mathbb Z_{>0},
 \qquad D(hS_{n-1})=0\quad\text{on }U,                    \tag{11}
\]
where the $D_k$ are distinct reduced pure one-dimensional closed affinoid subspaces of $Q$, and their union contains $C_Q$. This is a constructed cycle. An arbitrary iterated quotient scheme might have embedded primes; neither its $S_1$ property nor equality of its associated cycle with (11) is assumed.

Every reduced irreducible component of $C_Q$ equals some $D_k$. Indeed it is an irreducible closed subset contained in the finite union of the $D_k$, hence contained in one of them. A proper closed subset of an irreducible analytic curve has dimension less than one, whereas this component has dimension one. Equality follows, with the reduced structures.

Distinct $D_k$ intersect in a zero-dimensional closed strict affinoid, since a proper closed subset of an irreducible curve has dimension zero. Thus
\[
 F_Q=\bigcup_{k\ne l}(D_k\cap D_l)
\]
is a finite set of rigid points. On the ordinary ambient open $U\setminus\bigcup_{l\ne k}D_l$, only $b_k\delta_{D_k}$ remains in (11). Its closed embedding into that open, coefficient-measure multiplication, and compact direct-image differentiation give
\[
 0=b_k(i_k)_*D(h|_{D_k\cap U}\delta_{D_k\cap U})
 \quad\text{off }F_Q.
\]
Lemma 2 in bidegree $(0,0)$ and division by the **actual** positive $b_k$ give zero intrinsic Hessian on $D_k\cap U\setminus F_Q$. This is an equation on an actual ordinary open, not its extension by density. Theorem B removes the finite rigid points, proving it on all $D_k\cap U$.

For the original possibly nonreduced $C_Q$, its canonical current is the sum of the reduced component currents weighted by its own actual generic lengths, by CLD 16.6.3. Every one of those multipliers has zero intrinsic Hessian, so differentiation and closed-embedding direct image give (A) on $C\cap U$. The opens $C\cap U$ constructed for **every analytic point** $x$ cover $C\cap\Omega$. Intrinsic currents are local; (A) follows everywhere. This proves Theorem A. $\square$

## Normalization: isomorphism first, all-test direct image second

We give the additional argument for Theorem C independently of an arbitrary finite-map pullback rule. Locally take a reduced strict affinoid curve $\mathcal M(B)$. Its normalization is $\mathcal M(\overline B)$ for the finite integral closure in its total quotient ring, by input 3. A common denominator for finitely many $B$-module generators of $\overline B$ supplies a non-zero-divisor $q\in B$ with $q\overline B\subset B$. Hence $B[q^{-1}]=\overline B[q^{-1}]$. The finite normalization is an actual isomorphism away from the zero-dimensional support of $q$; on this affinoid that support is a finite rigid set. Such exceptional sets are locally finite on the curve, and their finite preimages are rigid. The normalized curve is reduced, strict, separated and boundaryless; boundarylessness follows from the finite map and that of $Y$.

On this actual isomorphism open, the equation transports by an analytic isomorphism, which preserves ordinary forms, differentials and canonical integration. The function $g\circ\nu$ is finite and continuous everywhere. Theorem B on $\widetilde Y$ removes the exceptional points. This proves the upstairs equation only **after** this step.

For every compact ordinary $(1,1)$ test $\alpha$ on $Y$, its support avoids the locally finite rigid exceptional set by input 5. Locally only finitely many points need be removed; $\nu^{-1}(\operatorname{supp}\alpha)$ is compact, and $\nu^*\alpha$ vanishes near their preimages. Integration on the isomorphism open is identical on the two spaces. Therefore
\[
 \langle\nu_*[(g\circ\nu)],\alpha\rangle
 =\int_{\widetilde Y}(g\circ\nu)\nu^*\alpha
 =\int_Yg\alpha
 =\langle[g],\alpha\rangle.
\]
A finite compact partition justifies the same argument when the test meets several affinoids. This proves the identity against all ordinary tests and includes continuous multiplication. Differentiation commutes with finite compact direct image, so $\nu_*D[g\circ\nu]=D[g]$. The isomorphism-locus/removability argument, rather than inversion of this latter identity, proved the upstairs equation. For nonreduced curves one first uses their reduced components and keeps their generic weights; their normalization alone is not asserted to have the nonreduced curve's unweighted current. $\square$

## Optional generic-length associativity, and the quotient distinction

The main proof never makes an arbitrary actual quotient $S_1$ and never identifies a constructed multi-cut cycle with it. For comparison, if an actual pure noetherian source has a **regular** Cartier function, the usual generic divisor associativity can be checked without that invalid inference. At a generic divisor prime $Q$, set $R=A_Q$; codimension one makes $R$ one-dimensional. For a finite $R$-module $M$ put
\[
 \chi_f(M)=\operatorname{length}(M/fM)
             -\operatorname{length}(\ker(f:M\to M)).
\]
All lengths are finite because $f$ avoids the minimal primes. The multiplication-by-$f$ snake sequence makes $\chi_f$ additive. A finite prime filtration has factors $R/P$ for minimal primes and $R/\mathfrak m$ for the maximal prime. The latter factors contribute zero (equal kernel and cokernel lengths); a minimal-prime factor is a domain and contributes $\operatorname{length}((R/P)/f)$. Localization at $P$ shows that its number of occurrences is $\operatorname{length}(R_P)$. Since $f$ is regular on $R$, its own kernel is zero. Thus
\[
 \operatorname{length}(R/fR)
 =\sum_{P\text{ minimal}}\operatorname{length}(R_P)
                         \operatorname{length}((R/P)/f).  \tag{12}
\]
These are the actual generic divisor coefficients; analytic/algebraic length agreement is input 3. Embedded-prime factors contribute zero to this one-dimensional Euler characteristic. Formula (12) only identifies the regular **one-cut** Cartier cycle in its precise setting. In the main iterative construction a later $f$ need not be regular on an unrelated embedded prime of any proposed quotient scheme, so no such identification is used there.

## The regular algebraic embedded-curve cover correction

The proposed small globalization of the prior regular-source corollary is valid, and is separately understood. In a noetherian regular local ring $(R,\mathfrak m,E)$ of dimension $n$, with a regular one-dimensional quotient $R/J$ and the same residue field, the kernel of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\to\mathfrak m_{R/J}/\mathfrak m_{R/J}^2$ has dimension $n-1$. Select that many independent classes among actual generators of $J$, extend them to a regular parameter system, and quotient by them. The result is a regular one-dimensional local domain surjecting onto $R/J$. Its prime kernel must be zero: a nonzero prime in that one-dimensional local domain is maximal and has zero-dimensional quotient. Thus the selected generators give the actual ideal. This uses the regular parameter theorem (Stacks Project, *Algebra*, 10.106.2--3, tags 00NP--00NQ), not $K$-differentials.

For a reduced regular pure finite-type $K$-scheme $V$ and a reduced regular closed algebraic curve $C$, at each scheme closed point $a\in C$ this construction selects actual generators. Coherence removes the ideal-quotient support on a Zariski neighbourhood. Excellence of finite-type algebras over a field makes the regular loci open; remove the bad regular/dimension loci of the finitely many actual cuts, as closed subsets of their closed subschemes and hence of $V$. The dimension at a closed point equals its component dimension, so on the retained open every actual cut is reduced regular and has the expected pure dimension. These are standard EGA IV 5.6.5 and 7.8.3 / Stacks excellent-ring inputs; they are not needed for Theorem A's singular analytic proof.

These are **Zariski** opens $A_a\subset V$. Their union intersects $C$ in a Zariski open containing all scheme closed points. A finite-type scheme over a field is Jacobson: a nonempty closed subset has a closed point. Hence the complement is empty. Analytification turns this scheme open cover into an ordinary open cover of **all** $C^{\rm an}$, because the scheme image of every analytic point lies in an $A_a$. Lemmas 2--4 apply on the actual regular flag. Thus the former local regular statement does globalize on $C^{\rm an}\cap\Omega$. This is not the claim that arbitrary ordinary neighbourhoods of rigid points cover a Berkovich curve, and does not use derivative density. Theorem A already contains the stronger singular analytic closed-curve case, so this correction is not counted as the main new result.

## What remains for universal recognition

Theorem A deals with closed subspaces of the ground-field space, including curves lying entirely in its singular locus. Theorem C treats their normalizations by a proved isomorphism-locus/removability argument. They remove a specific obstruction in the previous regular-cut route, without retiring the atom.

For arbitrary analytic curve morphisms there is a further image and pullback problem. CLD 11.6.5(a) states that a morphism is finite at a source point where it has relative dimension zero and no relative boundary. CLD 11.6.6(a) gives finiteness above a target neighbourhood when the entire zero-dimensional fibre avoids the relative boundary and the map is compact above a neighbourhood. These are useful routes to a finite factorization onto a closed curve image, but their conditions must be established on the actual source and target neighbourhoods; compactness is not automatic for an arbitrary curve map. The present proof does not supply that factorization for every probe. Where such a factorization is supplied, finite affinoid algebra maps do give a closed analytic image by their kernel. One still needs curve harmonic recognition and harmonic pullback in the relevant field and boundary scope. Theorem C is about normalization and does not handle arbitrary finite covers or arbitrary curve morphisms. No example with higher-dimensional analytic-Zariski-dense local image is asserted here.

Classical psh is defined by **every analytic complete-field extension and every curve map**: Gubler--Rabinoff, arXiv:2506.13548v2, Definition 4.1 and Definition 3.4. Both signs must pass these tests for classical pluriharmonicity (Definition 7.1). The precise definitions are checked in `sources/GR-2506.13548.txt`. Our equation on $X$ supplies none on a newly base-changed $X_L$. Theorem A can be applied upstairs only once the upstairs equation has been separately proved. A finite ground-field restriction on the same carrier, or a top-form measure pushforward computation, would not supply that ascent. No graph/product-current ascent is used.

The published smooth-proper-curve comparison described in the catalogue is not imported here as a theorem on all analytic curves or all residue fields. We have proved an intrinsic equation on the closed curve and its normalization; recognition of every such equation as harmonic, with the required extension quantifiers, is a separate obligation. The GJR real-PL and weakly smooth results likewise cannot be invoked before their regularity hypotheses hold. Ordinary smooth, weakly smooth, and G-local real-PL conclusions remain distinct; none is asserted for the general $h$ in Theorem A.

Finally, no two-sign positive-current regularization has been constructed. The smooth convex functions in Lemma 4 are truncations of a **unit logarithm** that construct a divisor cycle; they are not approximants to $h$ or $-h$. Fixed-test scalar approximation in Lemma 1 only controls an error and is not a positivity-to-psh recognition theorem. Thus the scalar-current ascent gate and the two-sign regularization gate remain separately unresolved.

The theorem is pure-dimensional and strict and works on an actual boundaryless ordinary interior around every analytic point. It neither discards a lower-dimensional component in a mixed-dimensional integration convention nor uses the non-strict invisible-boundary bidisc as a counterexample.
