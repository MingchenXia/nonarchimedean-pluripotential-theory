This is a fallible author audit of the current argument, not independent
review. The claimed new result is automatic ordinary zero-Hessian ascent
under every separable quadratic extension on good separated strict
boundaryless pure spaces of every dimension n >= 1. It includes
Q2(sqrt2)/Q2. Original classical recognition and ordinary two-sign
regularization are not proved.

## Load-bearing scalar construction

The old norm-one trace argument is not used. An arbitrary generator a
and c = a - sigma(a) give F = (a+b)/c and sigma(F) =
-(sigma(a)+b)/c, so F + sigma(F) = 1 even in characteristic two.
The conjugate pair lies on an actual analytic unit-moment tripod.
Its squared logarithmic difference is the positive-part square of the
logarithm of the invariant norm. This formula is checked at every
analytic point. It is not an ordinary smooth scalar descent assertion.

Zero v coefficients are retained by full-fibre strict inequalities;
nonzero v coefficients become actual units after shrinking. The same
construction is made for every pair product before any polynomial
approximation. Polarization uses real coefficients, not division by two
in K. Nilpotent coefficients belong to the zero-seminorm case; the
analytic algebra itself is not reduced.

## The fold and differentiated measures

The positive-part square is C1, with a bounded jump in its second
derivative, and no first-derivative jump. The smooth convolution has
uniform zeroth and first convergence and second derivative between zero
and two. Second tangential error is supported in a shrinking strip on
nonconstant moment cells. On a whole cell contained in the fold, the
norm coordinate and the upstairs logarithmic difference are constant
zero, so the tangential first and second derivatives vanish.

This last case cannot be dismissed by saying the fold has measure zero:
it can contain a top-dimensional cell. The proof handles it separately.
Nonconstant hyperplane sections have zero top measure and strip measure
O(epsilon), with the explicit orthogonal-slicing proof included.

The actual top-form measure is evaluated on the supporting skeleton and
bounded before pushing forward, so signed fibre cancellation is not used
to infer a carrier total-variation estimate. Positive canonical
calibrations multiply the target and the dominating form together.
All actual generic lengths remain. Lower dimensional strata have no
top measure; all top cells, including singular top cells and cells
contained in the fold, are included. No normalization of the analytic
space is used to remove a conductor.

## The form gate and high derivative cancellation

The ordinary tripod one-form nullity is proved by its restriction to
every actual compact moment image and the precise CLD nullity criterion.
The resulting orbital identity remains valid after dividing normalized
units by c: the difference unit is upstairs, but its logarithm is a
downstairs unit logarithm plus the real constant log|c|. Conjugation
changes the difference unit's sign without changing its logarithm.

Every pair with a zero coefficient or zero difference at the chosen
base point is handled before taking a logarithm. There is no division
by delta or a vanishing coefficient.

The orbital one-form O has affine coefficient functions in fixed
logarithmic coordinates. Therefore d'd''O = 0 and its first
derivatives have constant coefficients. With L_epsilon =
(dQ_epsilon + O)/2, d'd''L_epsilon = 0 exactly, by exterior
square-zero and anticommutation. All the differential-pair generators
obtained by differentiating L_epsilon are closed for both primes.
Thus differentiating a polynomial in these generators twice needs at
most the second derivatives of Q_epsilon. It does not differentiate
q_epsilon'' as an unconstrained scalar coefficient and then pretend
the resulting third and fourth derivatives are bounded.

The convergence family includes each generator, its two first exterior
derivatives and its mixed second exterior derivative. The product rule
therefore establishes differentiated top-form convergence for arbitrary
products and wedges. The parity pairing covers scalar/differential,
pure-prime alternating and ordered mixed differential pairs. It does
not rely on symmetric Hessians spanning all invariant forms.

## Supports and the two approximation limits

Invariant smooth superforms are first approximated by invariant
polynomial superforms in fixed upstairs coordinates in C2, including
their whole differential basis. This Euclidean approximation is proved
by compact extension, Gaussian convolution and uniform Taylor truncation.

For each fixed polynomial, replacement by the smoothed fold generators
converges in differentiated total variation with all charts fixed.
The constants may grow with polynomial degree. A diagonal choice of
epsilon, after choosing the polynomial degree, is sufficient; no
degree-uniform estimate is asserted. All moment lists, compact domains
and cutoff functions are fixed before either limit.

Finite downstairs cutoffs give one compact support D for every
approximant. Their full inverse image is compact. Products extend by
zero because the cutoffs' closed supports are compactly contained in
their ordinary open charts. The sum-one relation is needed only near
the original support, giving the global error identity. All first and
second cutoff terms are included in the differentiated-family argument.
Boundedness of every continuous multiplier on the fixed support turns
the total-variation convergence into the required pairing convergence.
