# Research notes and rejected routes

The task is original P06.2, whose full objective remains classical
pluriharmonic recognition in the original strict ambient scope. The
assignment selected the next finite scalar gate beyond quadratic
towers, permitting an all-finite-separable curve theorem if the full
higher-dimensional form gate remained out of reach. The result drafted
here uses that curve option. No imported quadratic result or published
recognition receipt is assumed.

## Route A: generalize quadratic differential parity

The quadratic construction pairs every anti-invariant scalar or
differential symbol. A general finite group has more representations
and correlations. Symmetric power sums of conjugate logarithms give
scalar invariants; they do not themselves produce orbital one-forms,
alternating pure-prime factors, or ordered mixed forms. There is no
complete all-dimensional cubic argument here. No abstract invariant
tensor defect is promoted to an analytic counterexample.

## Route B: recover each original unit's symmetric logarithms

Separate multisets of logarithms for each unit lose correlations under
simultaneous group action. The repair is real polarization: a mixed
scalar monomial is an exact linear combination of powers of logarithms
of product units. Averaging these products preserves orbit
correlations. This solves the scalar obstruction only, sufficient for
ordinary curve tests because their bidegree is (0,0).

## Route C: ignore coefficients whose value vanishes

An invariant polynomial coefficient may vanish in a cancellation
direction, including in characteristic p. Simply taking its logarithm
or deleting it globally is invalid. Locally a full-fibre unit bound
fixes M, and coefficients zero at the point are shrunk below
exp(-dM-1). Their valuations then lie above the endpoint chord and
cannot affect the lower hull. All other coefficients become actual
units on that neighbourhood. The root rule itself uses strict gaps
and unique dominant elementary-symmetric summands, so cancellation
within equal-valuation blocks causes no incorrect inference.

## Route D: C0 or ambient C2 descent

The Newton trace is C1 and piecewise polynomial in coefficient heights;
it generally has a second derivative jump. Uniform scalar
approximation does not control Hessian pairings, and ambient C2
convergence at a collision cannot be asserted. Bounded piecewise
Hessians, matched gradients, and tangential mollification give the
required calibrated total-variation convergence. Entire top segments
in collision faces must be handled separately by their common tangent
Hessian, rather than thrown away by a measure-zero statement.

## Route E: one shrink for all polynomial degrees

Polarization introduces more product units as the approximation degree
grows. Their characteristic-polynomial coefficient neighbourhoods
need not admit one common shrink over infinitely many degrees. The
proof avoids this unjustified demand. It fixes outer cutoffs and
upstairs unit tuples for the C2 polynomial error, and handles each
fixed polynomial by its own finite inner cover and smoothing limit.
Final supports are still inside the same outer compact. The inner
cutoffs do not differentiate the polynomial error, so growing cutoff
derivatives do not spoil that first limit.

## Route F: same-carrier and scalar projection normalization

Field restriction has factor [E:K], while scalar projection has none.
Conflating them would make averaging or descent formulas incorrect.
The group action is an actual K-analytic automorphism of the
restricted carrier. Its calibrated isomorphism formula cancels the
restriction factor. Scalar projection is applied only to differentiated
smooth top forms, and their signed measures are integrated against
the bounded continuous multiplier. No arbitrary current pullback is
defined or presumed.

## Route G: pass immediately to a completed algebraic closure

Density of finite coefficients is not enough to establish unit-chart
descent, full-fibre shrink, equality of logarithmic norms, or fixed
support control after completion. This route remains unproved and
is not needed for the finite-separable curve theorem. Even such a
completion theorem would leave universal analytic extensions and
two-sign regularization as distinct original obligations.

## Result and limitations

The complete draft in P06.2-argument.md claims forward zero-Hessian
ascent under every finite Galois extension on good separated strict
boundaryless pure curves, retaining singular and nonreduced structure.
An explicit normal closure and actual reverse descent give every
finite separable extension. The degree-three extension of Q2 built
from Z^3+Z+1 is cyclic and falls outside finite quadratic towers.
This is a partial scalar gate. No all-dimensional cubic theorem,
general recognition theorem, two-sign regularization theorem,
positive-current transport theorem, or analytic counterexample is
claimed. P05.2 is untouched.

All computations are bounded exact diagnostics, and typesetting is
performed with installed tools. They cannot verify the analytic theorem.
There is no external model review, inherited verdict, or novelty claim.
