## Status and exact scope

This is a **partial** result for P15.1. It proves the supplied-fibration construction below, including bounded potentials, perturbative potentials that need not descend, uniform convergence with no rate relative to the perturbation parameter, and critical or higher-dimensional fibres. It is not a solution for an arbitrary pseudoeffective zero-volume class. No result about P15.3 is claimed. The measure statements below are supporting estimates for the energy and its first variation, not a claim to solve the separate general P15.2 target. No literature-priority claim is made.

Throughout,
$$dd^c=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial,
\qquad d^c=\frac{i}{4\pi}(\bar\partial-\partial).$$
In particular, for a real smooth function $w$, $dw\wedge d^cw=(i/2\pi)\partial w\wedge\bar\partial w\geq0$.

Let $\pi:X\longrightarrow B$ be a supplied surjective holomorphic map of connected smooth compact Kähler manifolds, with
$$\dim_{\mathbb C}X=n,\quad\dim_{\mathbb C}B=\nu,
\quad 1\leq\nu<n,\quad m=n-\nu.$$
There is no assumption of a product, flatness, equidimensionality, or smooth fibres. Connected fibres are used only for the optional continuous-descent assertion proved below. Fix a Kähler form $\beta$ on $B$, and put
$$\theta=\pi^*\beta,\quad \alpha=[\theta],\quad
V_B=\int_B\beta^\nu>0,\quad K=\binom n\nu.$$
A bounded $q$-psh function means an everywhere finite, globally bounded, upper semicontinuous quasi-psh representative $v$ satisfying $q+dd^cv\geq0$. Norms are pointwise suprema, not essential suprema with respect to a measure. All products of bounded-potential currents in this paper are ordinary Bedford--Taylor (BT) products.

For a smooth Kähler form $q$ on a compact $d$-dimensional manifold $Y$, set
$$T_v=q+dd^cv,\qquad
E_q(v)=\frac1{d+1}\sum_{j=0}^d\int_Yv\,T_v^j q^{d-j},$$
with reference potential $0$. Its relative form is
$$R_q(v,w)=\frac1{d+1}\sum_{j=0}^d
\int_Y(v-w)T_v^jT_w^{d-j}.$$
The same expressions on $B$ use $q=\beta$, $d=\nu$, and $C_\varphi=\beta+dd^c\varphi$.

## Precise foundations and source audit

The only pluripotential foundations imported for the main result are the following bounded statements.

**BT foundation.** On a complex manifold, locally bounded psh potentials $p_1,\ldots,p_r$ define a closed positive product $dd^cp_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dd^cp_r$ by the recursion $dd^c(p_1 S)$ for the remaining closed product $S$. These products are symmetric and depend only on the currents. If, on coordinate domains, each $p_{i,k}$ decreases to a locally bounded psh $p_i$, the mixed products converge weakly to the corresponding mixed product. One may use smooth local psh decreasing regularizations on smaller domains. These assertions concern locally bounded potentials and local regularizations; they do not assert global smooth regularization in a fixed Kähler class. Citation: E. Bedford and B. A. Taylor, *A new capacity for plurisubharmonic functions*, Acta Math. 149 (1982), 1--40, Section 2; see also *Fine topology, Shilov boundary, and $(dd^c)^n$*, J. Funct. Anal. 72 (1987), 225--251. The bounded construction is expressly stated in BEGZ, arXiv:0812.3674v2, LF175--188, PDF pp. 4--5; the local decreasing approximation/continuity application also appears in its proof of Theorem 1.14, LF668--679. We use this standard published bounded theorem, not an unstated theorem for unbounded products.

Symmetry and multilinearity needed here can also be checked directly from this foundation: choose simultaneous smooth decreasing local regularizations; the smooth identities hold at every level; each term has a BT limit. Differences of positive bounded-potential currents are treated by finite expansion into positive products. Adding local smooth potentials for smooth closed forms gives the same construction and identifies products on chart overlaps.

**Bounded integration by parts.** On a compact Kähler manifold $Y$, let $\Theta$ be a closed positive current of bidimension $(1,1)$. Let $a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2$ be globally bounded quasi-psh functions, and put $a=a_1-a_2$, $b=b_1-b_2$. In the bounded-potential products used here,
$$\int_Y a\,dd^cb\wedge\Theta
=\int_Y b\,dd^ca\wedge\Theta
=-\int_Y db\wedge d^ca\wedge\Theta. \tag{IBP}$$
The signed measures are finite; the gradient products are defined by BT polarization, and $da\wedge d^ca\wedge\Theta$ is a positive finite measure. Citation: S. Boucksom, P. Eyssidieux, V. Guedj, A. Zeriahi, *Monge--Ampère equations in big cohomology classes*, Acta Math. 205 (2010), 199--262, Theorem 1.14 and Lemma 1.15; the primary copy is arXiv:0812.3674v2, LF626--762, PDF pp. 14--16. The statement just used is its special case with the closed complete pluripolar set $A=\varnothing$. At every application below, $\Theta$ is a mixed BT product of bounded-potential positive currents and smooth semipositive forms, is closed and positive by the BT foundation, and has exactly bidimension $(1,1)$. All functions are globally bounded quasi-psh or explicitly their differences. In particular, no boundary around a critical fibre or singularity locus is discarded.

For completeness, the positivity convention for the gradient square is given locally by
$$dd^c p^2=2\,dp\wedge d^cp+2p\,dd^cp$$
for a nonnegative locally bounded psh function $p$ and a closed positive $\Theta$, followed by polarization. The finite-integral and global integration-by-parts conclusion is precisely the bounded special case of the cited theorem; it is not inferred by taking weak limits against arbitrary discontinuous test functions.

**Nef-volume convention.** For nef real $(1,1)$ classes on a compact Kähler manifold, their positive intersection product is the ordinary cup product. Their volume is the top positive product, including the boundary limit from Kähler perturbations. We use the nef numerical dimension
$$\operatorname{nd}(\alpha)=\max\{k:\int_X\alpha^k[\kappa]^{n-k}>0\}$$
for a Kähler form $\kappa$. Citation: BEGZ, Definition 1.17 and subsequent discussion, arXiv:0812.3674v2, LF810--840, PDF pp. 17--18 (and the usual nef numerical-dimension convention). The classes used below are nef because they have a smooth semipositive representative, and their positive perturbations have smooth Kähler representatives with minimal singularities. Thus this convention applies. The actual intersections and perturbation volumes are computed in the proof.

**Ordinary differential foundations.** We use Stokes' theorem for currents, and Sard's theorem: the critical values of a smooth map between finite-dimensional smooth manifolds, with smoothness exceeding the dimension threshold, have measure zero. Citation for Sard: A. Sard, *The measure of the critical values of differentiable maps*, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 48 (1942), 883--890. Here the holomorphic map is smooth, and surjectivity onto the positive-dimensional manifold $B$ ensures a nonempty regular-value set and a nonempty full-rank open set. Fibre integration at a regular value is ordinary integration in local submersion coordinates and a partition of unity, not a singular-fibre theorem.

The optional continuous-descent paragraph imports one additional standard foundation, stated with its exact needed scope there. It is not needed for the main construction on supplied pullbacks.

## The theorem

Let $\omega_\epsilon$ be Kähler forms on $X$ converging smoothly, as $\epsilon\downarrow0$, to a Kähler form $\omega_0$. A fixed arbitrary Kähler form is included. Define
$$c_\epsilon=\pi_*(\omega_\epsilon^m),\quad
q_\epsilon=\theta+\epsilon\omega_\epsilon,\quad
s_\epsilon=Kc_\epsilon\epsilon^m.$$
Here the degree-zero pushforward is a strictly positive constant, as proved below. For a fixed form write $c_\omega$ instead. If $\varphi$ is any bounded $\beta$-psh function, set $U=\varphi\circ\pi$. Let $u_\epsilon$ be any globally bounded $q_\epsilon$-psh functions with
$$\delta_\epsilon=\|u_\epsilon-U\|_\infty\longrightarrow0.$$
No descending assumption on $u_\epsilon$, no monotonicity in $\epsilon$, and no bound on $\delta_\epsilon/\epsilon$ are imposed.

Then
$$\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{E_{q_\epsilon}(u_\epsilon)}{s_\epsilon}
=E_\beta(\varphi)
=\frac1{\nu+1}\sum_{a=0}^\nu\int_B\varphi\,C_\varphi^a\beta^{\nu-a}. \tag{1}$$
More generally, if $v_\epsilon$ uniformly approximates $\psi\circ\pi$ for bounded $\beta$-psh $\psi$, and is bounded and $q_\epsilon$-psh, then
$$\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{R_{q_\epsilon}(u_\epsilon,v_\epsilon)}{s_\epsilon}
=R_\beta(\varphi,\psi). \tag{2}$$
One may instead calibrate by $Kc_{\omega_0}\epsilon^m$, since $c_\epsilon\to c_{\omega_0}>0$.

The supporting first-variation measures satisfy
$$\frac{\pi_*\big((q_\epsilon+dd^cu_\epsilon)^n\big)}{s_\epsilon}
\ \rightharpoonup\ C_\varphi^\nu. \tag{3}$$
The limiting measure has raw mass $V_B$. The energy has reference $0$, value $E_\beta(0)=0$, and constant-shift law
$$E_\beta(\varphi+b)=E_\beta(\varphi)+bV_B. \tag{4}$$
It is monotone, concave, and $V_B$-Lipschitz for the uniform norm on bounded $\beta$-psh potentials. Its relative form is a cocycle and has the exact formula already displayed for $R_\beta$. Its variational scopes are bounded affine segments and continuously differentiable paths in the uniform norm of continuous $\beta$-psh functions, proved below. No differentiability for arbitrary bounded time-dependent paths is asserted.

The conclusions are the same for every Kähler representative and every Kähler class of the auxiliary direction, after its own specified $c$ calibration. The constant $c$ itself is independent of the representative of a fixed class but generally changes with the class. No arbitrary degenerate auxiliary path is included.

The retained data are the supplied carrier $B$ of dimension $\nu$, the map $\pi$, the base Kähler class and chosen reference current $\beta$, the zero reference potential (including its constant), and the bounded potential/singularity data. The raw reference mass is $V_B$, not one. The quantity $s_\epsilon$ is a positive transverse calibration; it is not division by $\operatorname{vol}(\alpha)=0$. The full leading class-volume coefficient is $Kc_{\omega}V_B$, and dividing by that coefficient would lose the stated raw base scale.

## Pushforward, bounded projection, and numerical data

First fix any Kähler form $\omega$. Pushforward of currents is defined by
$$\langle\pi_*S,\eta\rangle=\langle S,\pi^*\eta\rangle$$
for compactly supported smooth test forms on $B$. Properness follows from compactness. For $S=\omega^m$ this is a positive current of bidegree $(0,0)$, since pullback of positive top-degree test forms and wedging with $\omega^m$ is positive. Stokes gives $d\pi_*S=\pi_*dS=0$. A degree-zero distribution with all distributional derivatives zero is constant on a connected manifold: on a ball convolve with a smooth approximate identity, obtaining functions with zero gradient, and pass to the distributional limit; constants agree on overlapping balls. Thus $\pi_*\omega^m=c_\omega$ on **all** of $B$. This also rules out an additional distribution supported on critical values.

There is a full-rank point of $\pi$. Otherwise every value would be critical, contrary to Sard's theorem and surjectivity. Consequently $\theta^\nu\omega^m$ is strictly positive on a nonempty open set and nonnegative everywhere. Pairing the constant current with $\beta^\nu$ proves
$$c_\omega=\frac{\int_X\theta^\nu\omega^m}{V_B}>0. \tag{5}$$
At any regular value $b$, local submersion fibre integration identifies it with
$$c_\omega=\int_{\pi^{-1}(b)}\omega^m,$$
including the sum over all components if the regular fibre is disconnected. No assertion that each singular fibre's naive integral is this number is needed. Formula (5) gives its cohomological meaning and proves fixed-class representative independence by Stokes. Scaling the auxiliary form gives $c_{t\omega}=t^m c_\omega$, so class independence of $c$ would be false.

Here is a bounded projection identity that does not assume smooth fibres or a preexisting pullback of arbitrary singular currents. On a base coordinate neighbourhood choose locally bounded psh potentials $p_1,\ldots,p_k$ for positive bounded-potential currents $C_1,\ldots,C_k$. Define their pullback product by the BT products of $p_i\circ\pi$. Holomorphic composition is psh, and local boundedness is retained. Local decreasing smooth psh approximations $p_{i,l}$ on smaller base domains pull back to decreasing smooth psh approximations on their preimages. BT continuity shows that the definition agrees with the usual smooth pullback limit and is independent of potentials. Smooth products of more than $\nu$ base factors vanish before taking this limit; hence the same is true for their bounded pullback products, even at critical points.

For $k\leq\nu$, the exact projection identity is
$$\pi_*\left(\pi^*C_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi^*C_k\wedge\omega^m\right)
=c_\omega C_1\wedge\cdots\wedge C_k. \tag{6}$$
To prove it without any discontinuous-weight weak-limit inference, put $P_0=\omega^m$, $P_r=dd^c((p_r\circ\pi)P_{r-1})$. These are closed positive BT currents. For any order-zero current $S$ and bounded Borel function $g$ on the base,
$$\pi_*((g\circ\pi)S)=g\,\pi_*S$$
whenever the indicated products have order zero: test in local coordinates, integrate the coefficient measures against $g\circ\pi$, and use the definition of their pushforward. In our use the currents are positive, so coefficient measures are dominated by the trace measure and these bounded products are defined. Proper pushforward also commutes with $dd^c$. Inductively,
$$\pi_*P_r=dd^c(p_r\pi_*P_{r-1})
=c_\omega dd^cp_r\wedge\cdots\wedge dd^cp_1.$$
The recursion uses exactly the bounded BT definition. This proves (6) locally on every base chart; the identities patch. It is an equality on all of $B$, not merely off the critical values. In particular, for any bounded Borel $g$ and $a+b=\nu$,
$$\int_X(g\circ\pi)(\theta+dd^cU)^a\theta^b\omega^m
=c_\omega\int_Bg\,C_\varphi^a\beta^b. \tag{7}$$
Take $g=\varphi$ for the energy. This proves the weighted projection required for possibly discontinuous bounded $\varphi$, without testing a weak limit against $\varphi$. Smooth factors $\beta$ are included using their smooth local psh potentials. Identity (6) is claimed only for bounded local potentials and the specified smooth transverse form; no arbitrary singular-current pullback/projection theorem is assumed.

We will repeatedly use the following cohomological mass identity. If $Q_i=q_i+dd^cw_i\geq0$ have bounded global potentials and smooth closed backgrounds, their mixed BT product has cohomology class $[q_1]\cdots[q_r]$. Indeed, replacing one factor at a time changes the product by
$$dd^c(w_i S),$$
where $S$ is the remaining closed product. This identity is local BT recursion and patches globally; $w_iS$ has order zero because $w_i$ is bounded. Pairing with any smooth closed complementary form gives zero by Stokes. The argument also proves the top-degree mixed mass identities, including those involving $\theta$, and does not require strict positivity of $\theta$. All positive top-degree products below are finite measures on compact $X$ or $B$.

Since $\theta\geq0$ is smooth and $\theta+\epsilon\omega>0$, $\alpha$ is nef and pseudoeffective. One has $\theta^{\nu+1}=0$ and, by (5), $\int_X\theta^\nu\omega^m=c_\omega V_B>0$. The stated nef convention therefore gives $\operatorname{nd}(\alpha)=\nu$ and
$$\operatorname{vol}(\alpha)=\int_X\theta^n=0.$$
For every Kähler direction, its perturbative volume is exactly
$$\int_X(\theta+\epsilon\omega)^n
=\sum_{k=0}^\nu\binom nk\epsilon^{n-k}\int_X\theta^k\omega^{n-k}
=Kc_\omega V_B\epsilon^m+O(\epsilon^{m+1}). \tag{8}$$
This also directly verifies the boundary volume limit. Smooth convergence of $\omega_\epsilon$ makes these finitely many coefficient integrals uniformly bounded, and (5) shows $c_\epsilon\to c_{\omega_0}>0$.

## Bounded energy identities, with their actual variational scopes

Work first with $(Y,q,d)$ as in the definition. Mixed masses of $T_v$ and $q$ are all $V_q=\int_Yq^d$, by the mass identity above. Thus $E_q(0)=0$ and $E_q(v+b)=E_q(v)+bV_q$.

Let $v_t=(1-t)w+tv$ for bounded $q$-psh endpoints, and write $h=v-w$. Positivity and boundedness hold for $0\leq t\leq1$. Multilinearity makes each term defining $E_q(v_t)$ a finite polynomial in $t$ with integrable bounded weights; differentiation is therefore algebraic, not a differentiation of an uncontrolled weak limit. For the term with $j\geq1$, apply (IBP) with $a=v_t$, $b=h$, and $\Theta=T_{v_t}^{j-1}q^{d-j}$. These satisfy every antecedent listed above. It replaces
$$\int_Y v_t\,dd^ch\,T_{v_t}^{j-1}q^{d-j}
\quad\hbox{by}\quad
\int_Y h\,(T_{v_t}-q)T_{v_t}^{j-1}q^{d-j}.$$
Consequently, on the open segment, with one-sided derivatives at endpoints,
$$\frac{d}{dt}E_q(v_t)
=\frac1{d+1}\sum_{j=0}^d\left[(j+1)\int_YhT_{v_t}^jq^{d-j}
-j\int_YhT_{v_t}^{j-1}q^{d-j+1}\right]
=\int_YhT_{v_t}^d. \tag{9}$$
The second term at $j=0$ is absent. The finite sum telescopes.

Integrating (9) and expanding $T_{v_t}^d$ gives
$$E_q(v)-E_q(w)
=\frac1{d+1}\sum_{j=0}^d\int_Y(v-w)T_v^jT_w^{d-j}
=R_q(v,w), \tag{10}$$
because $\int_0^1\binom dj t^j(1-t)^{d-j}\,dt=1/(d+1)$; integration by parts in the ordinary one-variable integral, or the elementary beta-integral formula, proves this coefficient. Equation (10) proves antisymmetry and the exact cocycle
$$R_q(v,w)+R_q(w,z)=R_q(v,z).$$
For ordered endpoints $w\leq v$, (9) is nonnegative, proving monotonicity. If $\delta=\|v-w\|_\infty$, then $w-\delta\leq v\leq w+\delta$. Monotonicity and the shift law give
$$|E_q(v)-E_q(w)|\leq\delta V_q. \tag{11}$$
This holds for arbitrary bounded, possibly discontinuous, endpoints.

Differentiating the polynomial in (9) once more and applying (IBP) with $a=b=h$ and $\Theta=T_{v_t}^{d-1}$ gives
$$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}E_q(v_t)
=d\int_Yh\,dd^ch\,T_{v_t}^{d-1}
=-d\int_Ydh\wedge d^ch\wedge T_{v_t}^{d-1}\leq0. \tag{12}$$
The gradient square is a finite positive measure by the cited bounded theorem, so (12) proves concavity throughout the bounded convex domain. It also specifies exactly what the second variation means for nonsmooth bounded endpoints.

There is an additional useful first-variation scope. If $t\mapsto v_t$ is a $C^1$ map into the Banach space $C(Y)$ with the sup norm, and every $v_t$ is $q$-psh, then
$$\frac{d}{dt}E_q(v_t)=\int_Y\dot v_t T_{v_t}^d. \tag{13}$$
Here $\dot v_t$ is continuous. To verify it, first note that uniform convergence of bounded $q$-psh $a_l$ to $a$ implies weak convergence of each mixed measure $T_{a_l}^jT_a^{d-j}$ to $T_a^d$. For a smooth test $f$, telescope one differing factor at a time and use BT recursion and Stokes to bound the difference by
$$j\|a_l-a\|_\infty C_f V_q,$$
where $-C_fq\leq dd^cf\leq C_fq$. All mixed masses are $V_q$. Smooth test approximation and positivity extend this convergence to continuous tests. Apply (10) to $v_{t+h},v_t$, divide by $h$, use the uniform convergence $(v_{t+h}-v_t)/h\to\dot v_t$ and the just proved mixed weak convergence, and obtain (13). No conclusion with an arbitrary discontinuous velocity or a merely measurable path is inferred. For smooth paths of smooth admissible potentials this specializes to the usual formula; only affine bounded paths have the second-variation formula (12) asserted here.

All these arguments apply to $q_\epsilon$ on $X$ for each fixed positive $\epsilon$, and to $\beta$ on $B$. They give the exact reference, shift, cocycle, monotonicity, concavity, Lipschitz, and variational assertions of the theorem.

## Leading energy and arbitrary uniform perturbative approximants

Put $H=\theta+dd^cU$, a positive bounded-potential current by holomorphic pullback. Products of more than $\nu$ factors chosen from $H$ and $\theta$ vanish by the local pullback argument. Expand
$$E_{q_\epsilon}(U)
=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^n\int_XU\,(H+\epsilon\omega_\epsilon)^j
(\theta+\epsilon\omega_\epsilon)^{n-j}.$$
Every surviving term with $a$ factors $H$ and $b$ factors $\theta$ has $a+b\leq\nu$ and power $\epsilon^{n-a-b}$. The coefficient at the least possible power $\epsilon^m$ is
$$\frac1{n+1}\sum_{a=0}^\nu
\left[\sum_{j=0}^n\binom ja\binom{n-j}{\nu-a}\right]
\int_XU H^a\theta^{\nu-a}\omega_\epsilon^m.$$
The bracket equals $\binom{n+1}{\nu+1}$. To prove it, count subsets of size $\nu+1$ of $\{1,\ldots,n+1\}$ by their $(a+1)$-st element $j+1$; the earlier and later choices are precisely the two binomial factors. A binomial factor outside its admissible range means zero. Since
$$\frac1{n+1}\binom{n+1}{\nu+1}=\frac K{\nu+1},$$
the exact weighted projection (7) gives leading term $s_\epsilon E_\beta(\varphi)$.

All remaining terms have order at least $\epsilon^{m+1}$. Their absolute integrals are bounded by
$$\|\varphi\|_\infty\int_XH^a\theta^b\omega_\epsilon^{n-a-b}
=\|\varphi\|_\infty\int_X\theta^{a+b}\omega_\epsilon^{n-a-b}.$$
The equality follows from the cohomological mass identity, including when $a+b=0$. There are finitely many terms, and the smooth auxiliary limit bounds their coefficient integrals uniformly. Thus
$$E_{q_\epsilon}(U)=s_\epsilon E_\beta(\varphi)
+O(\|\varphi\|_\infty\epsilon^{m+1}). \tag{14}$$
This is a bounded-potential calculation using weighted projection on all fibres, not the earlier smooth fixed-class formula.

Now apply (11), with $q=q_\epsilon$, to $u_\epsilon$ and $U$:
$$|E_{q_\epsilon}(u_\epsilon)-E_{q_\epsilon}(U)|
\leq\delta_\epsilon\int_Xq_\epsilon^n. \tag{15}$$
By (8), $\int_Xq_\epsilon^n/s_\epsilon=V_B+O(\epsilon)$. Dividing (14)--(15) by $s_\epsilon$ gives the quantitative conclusion
$$\left|\frac{E_{q_\epsilon}(u_\epsilon)}{s_\epsilon}-E_\beta(\varphi)\right|
\leq\delta_\epsilon(V_B+O(\epsilon))
+O(\|\varphi\|_\infty\epsilon). \tag{16}$$
The constants in the $O$ terms are bounded for the stipulated auxiliary family. This proves (1) with no epsilon-relative convergence rate. Apply it twice and use (10) to prove (2). One may use any bounded reference $\rho$ on the base and approximating reference family: the limit then is $R_\beta(\varphi,\rho)$ rather than $E_\beta(\varphi)$.

The hypotheses admit genuinely non-descending families and convergence slower than $\epsilon$. For example, with $\varphi=0$, choose a nonconstant smooth base function $f$ and a sufficiently small smooth $\zeta$ on $X$ that varies along a regular fibre and satisfies $\omega+dd^c\zeta>0$. Then
$$u_\epsilon=\sqrt\epsilon\,(f\circ\pi)+\epsilon\zeta$$
is $\theta+\epsilon\omega$-psh for all sufficiently small $\epsilon$, because $\beta+\sqrt\epsilon\,dd^cf>0$, and is not a pullback. Its uniform error has order $\sqrt\epsilon$ (choose $f$ with nonzero norm). Existence of $\zeta$ follows by taking a small smooth bump varying on the positive-dimensional smooth regular fibre. This example is only an admissibility illustration; the proof does not limit approximants to this form.

## Supporting measure estimate and its role in the energy

Write $A=q_\epsilon+dd^cu_\epsilon$, $D=q_\epsilon+dd^cU=H+\epsilon\omega_\epsilon$. Multilinearity in the bounded BT sense yields
$$A^n-D^n=dd^c(u_\epsilon-U)\wedge
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^jD^{n-1-j}. \tag{17}$$
For a real smooth test $f$ on $B$, put $F=f\circ\pi$ and choose $C_f$ such that $-C_f\beta\leq dd^cf\leq C_f\beta$. Pullback gives the same inequality with $\theta$ on $X$, even where it is degenerate. For each positive mixed current $S_j=A^jD^{n-1-j}$, apply (IBP) with $a=u_\epsilon-U$, $b=F$, $\Theta=S_j$; all functions and currents meet its bounded antecedents. Equivalently for this smooth test one may use the recursive current identity and Stokes. It follows that
$$\left|\int_X F(A^n-D^n)\right|
\leq n C_f\delta_\epsilon I_\epsilon,\qquad
I_\epsilon=\int_X\theta q_\epsilon^{n-1}. \tag{18}$$
Indeed each $\int_X\theta S_j$ equals $I_\epsilon$ by the cohomological mass identity. Its expansion starts at the required order:
$$I_\epsilon=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu-1}\binom{n-1}k\epsilon^{n-1-k}
\int_X\theta^{k+1}\omega_\epsilon^{n-1-k}
=\binom{n-1}{\nu-1}c_\epsilon V_B\epsilon^m
+O(\epsilon^{m+1}). \tag{19}$$
Thus $I_\epsilon/s_\epsilon=(\nu/n)V_B+O(\epsilon)$, and the scaled error in (18) is $O_f(\delta_\epsilon)$, not $O_f(\delta_\epsilon/\epsilon^m)$.

The exact pullback expansion is
$$\pi_*D^n=s_\epsilon C_\varphi^\nu+
\sum_{k=0}^{\nu-1}\binom nk\epsilon^{n-k}
\pi_*(H^k\omega_\epsilon^{n-k}). \tag{20}$$
The remainder is a positive measure of total mass $O(\epsilon^{m+1})$, by the cohomological mass identity; (6) supplies the exact leading term across all critical values. After dividing by $s_\epsilon$, its total variation is $O(\epsilon)$. Combining (18)--(20) proves convergence on smooth tests. The scaled positive measures have total masses
$$\frac{\int_X A^n}{s_\epsilon}
=\frac{\int_Xq_\epsilon^n}{s_\epsilon}=V_B+O(\epsilon),$$
so uniform smooth approximation of any continuous test gives the asserted weak convergence (3). Total-variation convergence is proved only for the exact pullback family $D$, not for arbitrary $A$.

This explains the compatibility with the first variation of the base energy: (9) and (13) pair the raw measure $C_\varphi^\nu$ with the allowed base velocities. There is no passage to a derivative of an arbitrary perturbative family $u_\epsilon(t)$ without additional time-regularity hypotheses. Next let $\beta'=\beta+dd^ch$ be a cohomologous Kähler representative, with smooth real $h$; on a compact Kähler manifold such a potential exists by the $dd^c$ lemma, or the displayed relation may simply be included as the specified change. ## Optional continuous descent with connected fibres

This paragraph is not required for (1)--(3), which begin with a supplied bounded base potential. Assume additionally that every fibre is connected and let $W$ be a continuous $\theta$-psh function on $X$. Then there exists a unique continuous $\beta$-psh $\varphi$ on $B$ with $W=\varphi\circ\pi$.

Here the sole additional geometric foundation is complex analytic resolution: a compact irreducible reduced complex analytic space admits a proper surjective holomorphic map from a smooth connected compact complex manifold, which is an isomorphism over a dense nonsingular open subset. This is the analytic desingularization consequence of E. Bierstone and P. D. We invoke it only for irreducible components of the reduced compact fibre, not for resolving the map $\pi$. Compactness of the resolution follows from properness; connectedness follows by taking the resolving component dominating the irreducible space.

Fix a fibre and an irreducible component $Z$ of its reduction. In local base coordinates $\beta=dd^c\rho$, so $W+\rho\circ\pi$ is psh near the fibre. On a resolution of $Z$, its pullback differs from $W$ by the constant $\rho(b)$, hence $W$ pulls back to an ordinary psh function on a connected compact complex manifold. Holomorphic pullback is legitimate because $W$ is continuous and finite. The ordinary maximum principle, applied at a maximum on this smooth compact manifold, makes it constant. Surjectivity of the resolution makes $W$ constant on $Z$. There are finitely many fibre components; components joined by intersections have the same value, and connectedness of the reduced fibre makes their intersection graph connected. Thus $W$ is constant on the entire fibre, including singular and higher-dimensional components.

A continuous surjection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a quotient map. Therefore the uniquely defined fibrewise function $\varphi$ is continuous. It remains to prove psh positivity on the base, without assuming a submersion at critical values. For any fixed auxiliary $\omega$, bounded multiplication of order-zero currents and Stokes give
$$\pi_*\big((\theta+dd^cW)\wedge\omega^m\big)
=c_\omega\beta+dd^c\pi_*(W\omega^m)
=c_\omega(\beta+dd^c\varphi).$$
The left side is a positive current, and $c_\omega>0$, so $\beta+dd^c\varphi\geq0$ distributionally. A continuous function whose sum with a smooth local potential has positive $dd^c$ is psh: on smaller coordinate balls convolve it with radial positive approximate identities, obtaining smooth psh functions converging locally uniformly, and apply the submean inequalities on complex lines in the limit. This proves the assertion through all critical values. No descent statement for arbitrary unbounded potentials or disconnected fibres is made.
