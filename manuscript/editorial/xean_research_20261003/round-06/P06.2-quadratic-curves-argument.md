## 1. Result and status

This packet proves a new special-case scalar-ascent gate. It does not solve P06.2, does not give a counterexample, and does not assert an independent review, formal verification, or priority claim. All new mathematical claims are unverified author drafts.

Let $K$ be a complete nontrivially valued field. Let $E/K$ be a quadratic Galois extension, with nonidentity automorphism $\sigma$ and its extending absolute value. We impose the following explicit **field hypothesis**:

$$
\text{there is }a\in E\text{ with }|a|=1\text{ and }|a-\sigma(a)|=1. \tag{H}
$$

This is an actual restriction on the field extension, not a hypothesis about test descent or current ascent. In particular, it includes the usual unramified quadratic extensions of local fields. No assertion for every quadratic extension is made. The proof works in characteristic two as well: all averages and polynomial coefficients are real, and no division by two in $K$ is used.

**Theorem A (scalar polynomial descent and compact approximation).** Let $X$ be a good topologically separated $K$-analytic space, let $\pi:X_E\to X$ be scalar projection, and assume (H).

1. Near a point $x$ with a singleton scalar fibre, let $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ be analytic units on a scalar-extended affinoid neighbourhood. Every real polynomial in the $2m$ logarithmic coordinates of $f_i$ and $\sigma f_i$ that is invariant under their simultaneous interchange is, after shrinking the downstairs affinoid neighbourhood, the exact pullback of an ordinary CLD-smooth scalar function downstairs.
2. If $\beta$ is a compactly supported, $\sigma$-invariant ordinary smooth real function on $X_E$, there are ordinary $\alpha_j\in A_c^{0,0}(X)$ with their supports in one fixed compact set $D\subset X$. On a finite collection of fixed upstairs ordinary moment neighbourhoods covering $\pi^{-1}(D)$, the error $\beta-\pi^*\alpha_j$ is a finite sum of products of fixed smooth cutoffs and Euclidean smooth coefficient errors converging with all derivatives through order two to zero. The unit lists, cutoffs, coefficient domains, and compact supports in this statement are independent of $j$.

The precise construction and estimate are in Sections 5--7. We claim neither exact descent of every invariant smooth scalar function nor a topological isomorphism of the full ordinary test sheaves. We do not replace ordinary forms by weakly smooth forms.

**Theorem B (one-dimensional scalar ascent).** Assume (H). Suppose $X$ is good, separated, strict, boundaryless, and pure of analytic dimension one, with its actual analytic structure. For every continuous real $h:X\to\mathbb R$,

$$
d'd''[h]=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d'd''[h\circ\pi]=0. \tag{1}
$$

Both equations use ordinary CLD compact tests and their own canonical calibrations: the $K$-calibration downstairs and the $E$-calibration upstairs. Nilpotents and actual generic lengths are retained. The conclusion holds on every ordinary open subspace, with the corresponding actual structures.

Theorem B applies to arbitrary curves in this ambient scope, including singular and nonreduced ones; it does not require algebraization, normalization, a product chart, or prior harmonic recognition. It establishes only the scalar-ascent gate. It constructs no ordinary psh regularization of either sign and gives no classical-pluriharmonic conclusion.

**Corollary C.** The equivalence (1) iterates along any finite tower of quadratic Galois field extensions each satisfying (H) over its preceding complete field, for these pure one-dimensional spaces. The composite extension need not be Galois over $K$. This is not a theorem for arbitrary nongalois finite separable extensions or for the completion of an infinite algebraic extension.

The original finite-Galois gate for all pure dimensions $n\geq1$ and all finite Galois extensions remains open in this packet. The full recognition atom P06.2 remains open.

## 2. Definitions and inspected primary results

Write $t_f=-\log|f|$ for an analytic unit $f$. An ordinary smooth function is a section of the ordinary CLD sheaf $A^{0,0}$: locally in the ordinary Berkovich topology it is a smooth real function of a finite list of $t_f$. An ordinary compact test means its support is compact in that topology. An affinoid neighbourhood contains an ordinary open neighbourhood. The ordinary topological interior of an affinoid domain $V$ in $X$ is denoted $\operatorname{int}_X(V)$; it is not being identified with the absolute analytic interior of $V$.

For a pure curve with canonical calibration, put

$$
\mathcal B_h(\alpha)=\int_X h\,(d'd''\alpha)_{\mathrm{Rad},K},\qquad \alpha\in A_c^{0,0}(X).
$$

The equation $d'd''[h]=0$ is equivalent to $\mathcal B_h(\alpha)=0$ for every such test. A possible fixed duality sign is irrelevant to this criterion. We use the pairing $\mathcal B$ consistently rather than selecting a duality-sign convention.

### 2.1. Analytic neighbourhoods, scalar extension, and dimension

CLD §11.1.5 states that a point in a $k$-affinoid space has a rational affinoid neighbourhood inside any prescribed ordinary open neighbourhood; the rational inequalities can be chosen strict at that point. CLD §11.1.7 states that affinoid neighbourhoods form a basis in a good space and that ordinary open subsets are analytic domains. Affinoid spectra are compact Hausdorff. CLD §11.2.2 states that an analytically separated space is topologically separated.

CLD §11.1.9 states that fibred products of affinoids are computed by completed tensor products, that $\mathcal M(A)_E=\mathcal M(A\widehat\otimes_K E)$, and that the natural scalar projection is surjective. Inverse images of analytic domains have the corresponding base-change structure. A finite extension is analytically separable exactly when it is separable. We use only finite scalar extension here and prove the specific compactness, fibre-orbit, invariant-algebra, and neighbourhood assertions in Section 3.

CLD §11.4.4 states that a finite Banach algebra over an affinoid algebra is affinoid, with its finite-module topology, and that finite analytic morphisms are boundaryless. CLD §11.2.3 states that strict affinoid dimension equals Krull dimension, that scalar extension preserves dimension, that domains preserve local dimension, and that purity of dimension $d$ means local dimension $d$ at every point. Section 3 verifies purity in the curve case used here; we do not infer it merely from equality of global dimensions.

GR §2.11(5') states, for any analytic extension $L/K$,

$$\partial(X_L/Y_L)\subseteq\pi^{-1}(\partial(X/Y)).$$

In particular $\partial X_E\subseteq\pi^{-1}(\partial X)$. Thus the upstairs space is boundaryless. We use no equality of absolute analytic interiors under scalar extension. Goodness, strictness, and separatedness upstairs are checked from the affinoid presentations and the diagonal.

### 2.2. Ordinary scalar charts and compact cutoffs

CLD Corollary 15.1.2 states the local smooth-function criterion using a neighbourhood $U$, analytic units $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ on $U$, and a smooth real $\varphi$ near their logarithmic values at $x$, with $u=\varphi\circ f_{\mathrm{trop}}$ on $U$. Its opposite logarithmic sign convention is immaterial. Proposition 15.1.1(b) gives ordinary forms an ordinary **open** moment-chart cover. If its coefficient is initially written on a paralinéaire subset, CLD Lemma 1.9.4 gives an ambient extension when that subset is closed in the ambient open; one chooses such a local closed neighbourhood, or applies it to the compact moment image after shrinking. Thus the scalar coefficient in our charts can be taken Euclidean smooth. Sections 6.2.1--2 define the ordinary smooth sheaves and their algebra operations. Smooth pullback commutes with $d',d''$ and the algebra operations (CLD §3.3.12).

CLD §15.2.2 states that a good topologically separated analytic space is locally holomorphically separated. Corollary 15.2.9 states: for a locally holomorphically separated analytic space, a closed subset $F$ countable at infinity (in particular compact), and an ordinary open cover $(U_i)$ of $F$, there is a locally finite family of nonnegative smooth $\theta_i$, each of compact support contained in $U_i$, whose sum is one near $F$. We apply it only to finite covers of compact sets. This requires no global paracompactness. Extension by zero across the complement of $U_i$ is smooth for a product having compact support inside $U_i$; the elementary neighbourhood argument is given in Section 6.

CLD Proposition 3.4.1 states, on a compact G-tropical space with a chart $f$, that a coefficient form is null on $f(X)$ exactly when its pullback is zero as a tropical-form presheaf section. Corollary 3.4.2 proves separation on a compact G-cover. Proposition 3.4.4 then states, on an arbitrary G-tropical chart, equivalence of coefficient nullity on every compact domain image, pullback nullity on every compact domain, and zero image in the G-form sheaf. These are used solely to check equality of the ordinary form sheaves on the same carrier under finite restriction of the ground field in Section 3.3; no ordinary descent through $\pi$ is inferred from them.

### 2.3. Actual canonical measures and the two field normalizations

CLD Theorem 15.7.1 and §15.7.2 give a topologically separated pure-dimensional analytic space its canonical calibrated tropical structure and current theory. CLD §7.3.5 defines the integration current with locally integrable multipliers; a continuous multiplier is locally integrable. In particular $[h]$ pairs with a compact smooth top form $\omega$ by $\int h\,\omega_{\mathrm{Rad}}$.

CLD Proposition 14.3.6 states: if $\Sigma$ is a skeletal subset in an analytic space of level $n$, $\mu$ is its canonical calibration, and $\Sigma_L$ and $\mu_L$ are obtained by complete scalar extension, then $\pi_*\mu_L=\mu$. Its proof uses actual degree identity

$$
\deg_x(q\circ f)=\sum_{y\in\pi^{-1}(x)\cap\Sigma_{f_L}}\deg_y(q\circ f_L)
$$

from CLD Lemma 13.3.3. These degrees are dimensions of actual finite fibre algebras, not counts of points. CLD §§14.3.2--5 construct the calibration by these actual degrees on convenient cells. CLD Lemma 13.2.8 computes generic lengths under scalar extension by tensoring a length filtration; no reduction is silently taken in that argument.

CLD Proposition 15.3.1 states: for an analytic space of level $n$, a complete extension $L/k$, and a $(n,n)$-form $\omega$ on $X_G$ with tropically Borel coefficients,

$$\pi_*(\omega_{L,\mathrm{Bor}})=\omega_{\mathrm{Bor}};$$

if $\omega$ is locally integrable, also

$$\pi_*(\omega_{L,\mathrm{Rad}})=\omega_{\mathrm{Rad}}. \tag{2}$$

The proof derives these measure equalities from Proposition 14.3.6 and the definitions of the associated volume forms. We use (2) only for a smooth differentiated compact test. We then multiply its signed measure by the continuous scalar. This is not a pullback theorem for an arbitrary current.

CLD Remark 14.4.3 gives a different identity: if $Z$ is an $F$-analytic space of level $n$, with $F/k$ finite, and $Z_0$ is the same carrier with restricted $k$-analytic structure, then the skeletal subsets are the same, dimensions are the same, and

$$\mu_{Z_0}=[F:k]\mu_Z. \tag{3}$$

We will prove that the ordinary form sheaves on this same carrier agree, and then (3) implies that their top Radon measures differ by $[F:k]$. Here $[E:K]=2$. This factor is absent from (2).

CLD Proposition 15.4.3 states that for $f:Y\to X$ between spaces of level $n$ finite away from a Zariski closed subset of dimension less than $n$, and a locally integrable $(p,n)$-form $\omega$ on $X$, $p\in\{n-1,n\}$,

$$f_*((f^*\omega)_{\mathrm{Rad}})=\mathrm{dc}f\,\omega_{\mathrm{Rad}}.$$

We use its degree-one isomorphism case on the restricted $K$-structure, where $\mathrm{dc}f=1$, to check invariance of the $E$-calibrated pairing under $\sigma$. Its proof uses 14.4.2, including the calibrated rather than point-counting degree.

CLD Proposition 5.10.1 and §§5.8, 14.3--5 give the top-form integration formula on a compact moment skeleton: contract the coefficient form with the positive standard calibration and integrate on its finitely many cells. CLD §5.4.4 states that every G-tropical chart on a compact space is pleasant; its tropical polyhedron is the top-dimensional part of its compact image. Thus the moment on a compact affinoid is pleasant; its one-dimensional skeleton and standard calibration admit a finite cell refinement. For a fixed moment $g$ on a compact curve affinoid, the positive form $\sum_k d't_{g_k}\wedge d''t_{g_k}$ therefore has a finite Radon measure. The coefficient estimate needed below is proved directly from the cell contraction, rather than assuming a continuity theorem for a stronger unspecified topology.

## 3. Finite tensor products, actual fibres, and neighbourhoods

### 3.1. The finite algebra and its invariants

Let $A$ be a $K$-affinoid algebra, with an ultrametric Banach norm defining its topology; no reducedness or spectral-norm condition is imposed. The algebraic tensor $B=A\otimes_K E$ is already complete with the completed tensor topology, hence is $A\widehat\otimes_K E$.

Here is the topological verification. Choose a $K$-basis of $E$. The absolute-value norm on $E$ is equivalent to the coefficient maximum norm. One proof is induction on the dimension: the span of all but the last basis vector is complete by induction and hence closed, so the last vector has a positive distance from that span; this bounds its coefficient, and the ultrametric inequality bounds the remaining ones. Thus the coordinate functionals on $E$ are bounded. On the algebraic tensor these functionals give bounded maps to $A$, while the tensor-norm inequality bounds a finite sum by a constant times its coefficient maximum norm. The tensor topology is therefore that of the finite complete sum of copies of $A$.

The semilinear involution acts by $\sigma(a\otimes e)=a\otimes\sigma(e)$. Its fixed algebra is exactly $A$. Indeed the kernel of the finite-dimensional $K$-linear map $\sigma-1$ on $E$ is $K$; tensoring its linear kernel calculation with the $K$-vector space $A$ gives $B^\sigma=A$. This proof uses no Reynolds operator in $K$, so it also applies when $\operatorname{char}K=2$. If an invariant $b\in B$ is a unit of $B$, its inverse is invariant as well; both belong to $A$, so $b\in A^\times$.

On an affinoid, an analytic element is a unit if it has no zero on its spectrum. The implication needed here follows from the surjectivity $\mathcal M(B)\to\operatorname{Spec}B$ (CLD §11.1.2): a nonunit is in a maximal ideal, and there is an analytic point whose kernel is that ideal. This applies also to nonreduced $B$.

### 3.2. Fibre orbits and compactness

The fibre over $x\in X$ is the spectrum of the finite algebra $\mathcal H(x)\otimes_K E$. Since $E/K$ is separable, this is a product of finite separable field extensions of the complete field $\mathcal H(x)$. Each field factor, being finite, is a Banach affinoid algebra over $\mathcal H(x)$ by CLD §11.4.4. Surjectivity of the affinoid spectrum onto its prime spectrum supplies an extending absolute value. It is unique: two such absolute values are equivalent finite-dimensional norms over the complete field by the norm argument of Section 3.1; a fixed equivalence constant applied to powers $z^r$ and then raised to $1/r$ forces the two values of $z$ to be equal. Thus each factor has one analytic point, and the fibre has either one point or two points.

The group is transitive on these points. For completeness, the invariants of $\mathcal H(x)\otimes_K E$ equal $\mathcal H(x)$ by the same linear kernel calculation as above. If its primitive field factors had more than one group orbit, the sum of the primitive idempotents of one orbit would be a nontrivial invariant idempotent, which cannot belong to a field. In the two-point case $\sigma$ interchanges the points; in the one-point case it fixes the point. No topological covering or local isomorphism assertion has been used.

The separable-algebra decomposition follows by factoring a separable quadratic minimal polynomial over $\mathcal H(x)$ and the Chinese remainder identity. The norm argument proves uniqueness of the points, and the algebraic kernel and idempotent argument proves their orbit assertion independently of a general quotient theorem.

The upstairs carrier is Hausdorff even under the weaker topological separatedness hypothesis of Theorem A. Points with different images have disjoint preimages of downstairs Hausdorff neighbourhoods. Points with the same image lie in the ordinary open $\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{int}_X V_0)$ inside the Hausdorff affinoid $V_{0,E}$; they can be separated there by opens contained in this ordinary open. Thus all the compact and closed-set assertions below take place in Hausdorff carriers.

If $C\subset X$ is compact, finitely many interiors of affinoid neighbourhoods $V_\ell$ cover it. Then $\pi^{-1}(C)$ is a closed subset of the finite union of the compact $V_{\ell,E}$, hence is compact. In particular compact pullbacks of tests are legitimate.

We need a slightly more precise shrinking fact. Fix an affinoid neighbourhood $V_0$ of $x$. Suppose $N$ is a relatively ordinary open subset of $V_{0,E}$ containing its entire fibre over $x$. The compact set $V_{0,E}\setminus N$ has compact, hence closed, image in the Hausdorff space $V_0$. That image excludes $x$. Intersect its complement with $\operatorname{int}_X(V_0)$, and choose a downstairs affinoid neighbourhood $V$ of $x$ inside the resulting ordinary open. Then $V_E\subset N$. This is the compact-complement argument used in every local construction below. It does not descend an arbitrary upstairs domain.

### 3.3. The two carrier structures and ambient properties

Let $Z=X_E$ and let $Z_0$ be its restricted $K$-analytic structure. On an affinoid $V_E$ the structural algebra is the same Banach algebra $B$ in both structures. Their structural sheaves on ordinary opens are the same: restrict the identical affinoid analytic sections and glue in the ordinary topology. An ordinary moment chart in either structure is just a finite list of units on such opens. An $E$-affinoid neighbourhood is also $K$-affinoid under finite restriction of scalars; these give a common local basis, so its unit-coordinate map has exactly the same compact image in both structures. CLD 3.4.1--4 show that its coefficient-nullity relations are identical. By CLD 15.1.1(b), the ordinary CLD form sheaves on $Z$ and $Z_0$ consequently agree in every bidegree, with the same real charts, differentials, and supports. This is a comparison on one carrier, not descent through $\pi$.

Finite $E/K$ makes $B$ a finite Banach algebra over $A$; $Z_0\to X$ is finite. Goodness upstairs follows because $\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{int}_X V)$ is an ordinary open contained in the affinoid $V_E$. An $E$-strict presentation is obtained by base changing a $K$-strict presentation. Analytic separatedness follows by base changing the closed diagonal. Boundarylessness follows from GR 2.11(5').

For the curve theorem, each downstairs strict affinoid neighbourhood has dimension one at every point. All its minimal-prime components have dimension one: a zero-dimensional minimal-prime component would be disjoint from all the other components (otherwise its maximal ideal would contain another minimal prime), and would give an isolated analytic point of local dimension zero. The finite algebra $B=A\otimes E$ is étale and faithfully flat over $A$.

Here is an explicit minimal-prime check that avoids presuming purity from global dimension. Write $J$ for the nilradical of $A$, nilpotent since $A$ is noetherian. The injection of $A/J$ into the finite product of the fraction fields $\operatorname{Frac}(A/P)$ of its minimal components remains injective after tensoring with $E$, because tensoring vector spaces over $K$ is exact. Each resulting field tensor is a finite product of fields by separability. Consequently $(A/J)\otimes E$ is reduced, and $J\otimes E$ is exactly the nilradical of $B$. The kernels of the finitely many maps of $(A/J)\otimes E$ to those field factors are prime ideals with intersection zero. Any minimal prime contains one of those kernels: the product of the finitely many kernel ideals is contained in their zero intersection, and primality supplies a factor. Minimality forces equality. Each such kernel contracts to the corresponding $P$, since $A/P$ injects into that field factor. Thus every minimal prime $Q$ of $B$ is over a minimal prime $P$ of $A$. The finite integral injection $A/P\to B/Q$ preserves dimension: going-up lifts a maximal prime chain and incomparability ensures that contracting a chain does not identify distinct primes. These elementary integral-algebra properties follow by localizing the monic equations for integral elements, or by the usual lying-over proof with maximal ideals. Hence every upstairs component has dimension one. The domain dimension formula in CLD §11.2.3 gives local dimension one everywhere on $Z$. No reduction is used in the actual integration, even though minimal primes are used to check dimension.

These arguments establish every ambient hypothesis needed for Theorem B and for its iteration.

## 4. Norms and two trace coordinates

Since $\sigma$ is isometric, (H) gives $|\sigma(a)|=1$. Also $|a+1|=|\sigma(a)+1|=1$: if $|a+1|<1$, the isometry gives $|\sigma(a)+1|<1$, contradicting $|a-\sigma(a)|=1$.

Consider the three coefficient pairs

$$ (c,\sigma(c)),\qquad c\in\{1,a,a+1\}.$$

All their entries have absolute value one. The determinant of any two distinct pairs has absolute value one: it is $\sigma(a)-a$ or its negative. Consequently each two-by-two inverse matrix has entries of absolute value one.

Let $f\in B^\times$ on a scalar-extended affinoid, and set $\bar f=\sigma f$. The invariant elements

$$N=f\bar f,\qquad T_c=cf+\sigma(c)\bar f$$

belong to $A$; $N$ is a unit. At any analytic point $z$, put $M=\max\{|f(z)|,|\bar f(z)|\}$. Each $|T_c(z)|\leq M$. Any two cannot both be strictly smaller than $M$, because their inverse coefficient matrix would imply $|f(z)|,|\bar f(z)|<M$. Thus at least two of the three traces have absolute value $M$ at every point.

At a singleton fibre $\{y\}$, $|f(y)|=|\bar f(y)|$. Choose two traces of absolute value $M>0$ there. The compact-complement shrinking argument makes both traces nonvanishing on a downstairs affinoid neighbourhood, hence units there. On that neighbourhood write

$$
t=-\log|f|,\quad t'=-\log|\bar f|,\quad S=-\log|N|,
\quad A=-\log|T_c|,\quad B=-\log|T_d|.
$$

The following identity is exact on the **whole** upstairs neighbourhood:

$$
(t-t')^2=(S-2A)(S-2B). \tag{4}
$$

If $t\ne t'$, unequal magnitudes prevent cancellation in either trace; all four coefficients are units of norm one, so $A=B=\min(t,t')$, and both right-hand factors equal $|t-t'|$. If $t=t'=t_0$, then $S=2t_0$, both $A,B\geq t_0$, and at least one equals $t_0$ by the inverse-matrix argument. The right-hand product and the left side are both zero. This proves (4), including all cancellation directions. It is not just a calculation on a selected tropical skeleton.

Define the ordinary smooth downstairs function

$$Q(f)=(t_N-2t_{T_c})(t_N-2t_{T_d}).$$

It pulls back to $(t_f-t_{\sigma f})^2$. For finitely many $f_i$, apply the construction to each $f_i$ and to each $f_if_j$, $i<j$, then shrink once for the finite list of chosen traces. With

$$s_i=t_{f_i}+t_{\sigma f_i},\qquad \delta_i=t_{f_i}-t_{\sigma f_i},$$

we obtain exact smooth downstairs representatives of all $s_i$ and all products

$$
q_{ii}=\delta_i^2=\pi^*Q(f_i),\qquad
q_{ij}=\delta_i\delta_j=\tfrac12\pi^*\bigl(Q(f_if_j)-Q(f_i)-Q(f_j)\bigr). \tag{5}
$$

The division in (5) is division of real smooth functions. No assertion about traces of differential forms has been made.

An invariant real polynomial $P(t,t')$ becomes a polynomial $\widetilde P(s,\delta)$ under the invertible real-linear substitution $t=(s+\delta)/2$, $t'=(s-\delta)/2$. Its invariance means $\widetilde P(s,-\delta)=\widetilde P(s,\delta)$. Hence every monomial in it has even total $\delta$-degree. Pair the $\delta$ factors in each monomial; (5) expresses it as a polynomial in $s_i,q_{ij}$. This proves Theorem A(1). We have proved only this polynomial algebra assertion, and do not invoke a smooth invariant-theory theorem to upgrade it to exact smooth descent.

The field restriction matters. For a ramified quadratic extension where every candidate $a$ has $|a-\sigma(a)|<1$, the inverse coefficient matrices need not have norm-one entries. Two trace magnitudes can both be smaller than $M$, and (4) is not proved. The argument below makes no claim in that situation.

## 5. Local invariant scalar tests

Let $\beta$ be an invariant ordinary smooth function, defined on an ordinary neighbourhood of the full fibre over $x$.

### 5.1. A singleton fibre

Let its fibre be $\{y\}$. By CLD 15.1.2 choose an ordinary neighbourhood $N$ of $y$, units $f_1,\ldots,f_m$, and a Euclidean smooth $\gamma$ with $\beta=\gamma(t_f)$ there. Replace $N$ by $N\cap\sigma N$. Choose a downstairs affinoid neighbourhood $V$ with $V_E\subset N$ by Section 3.2. Invariance gives, on $V_E$,

$$
\beta=G(t_f,t_{\sigma f}),\qquad
G(t,t')=\tfrac12\bigl(\gamma(t)+\gamma(t')\bigr). \tag{6}
$$

The Euclidean domain of $\gamma$ can be taken to contain the compact logarithmic images in question, after shrinking $V$. Multiply by a Euclidean smooth cutoff equal to one near these images to extend $\gamma$ to a smooth function on all of $\mathbb R^m$. This changes neither (6) nor any derivatives of its coefficients near the images. The function $G$ is smooth and invariant on $\mathbb R^{2m}$.

Apply Section 4 to the finite lists $f_i,f_if_j$; shrinking $V$ further leaves (6) valid. All downstairs moment units and the upstairs tuple $F=(f,\sigma f)$ are now fixed. Let $L\subset\mathbb R^{2m}$ be a compact rectangular box containing $t_F(V_E)$, stable under the simultaneous interchange, and with these images in its interior. There are invariant polynomials $P_j$ such that

$$
\max_{|\nu|\leq2}\sup_{t\in L}|\partial^\nu(G-P_j)(t)|\longrightarrow0. \tag{7}
$$

An elementary proof, avoiding an invariant-theory theorem, follows in Section 5.3. Each $P_j(t_F)$ has the exact downstairs smooth representative $b_j$ supplied by (4)--(5). Thus $\pi^*b_j=P_j(t_F)$, and the coefficient error in (6) converges through two derivatives on one fixed upstairs chart.

### 5.2. A two-point fibre

Let the fibre be $\{y,\sigma y\}$. Choose disjoint ordinary neighbourhoods $N_1,N_2=\sigma N_1$ of these points, small enough that $\beta=\gamma(t_f)$ on $N_1$. The compact-complement argument gives $V$ with $V_E\subset N_1\cup N_2$. The sets $V_E\cap N_i$ are disjoint ordinary opens covering $V_E$. Glue a unit $g_i$ by using $f_i$ on the first and its conjugate on the second. These are analytic sections on the whole affinoid $V_E$ by the structural sheaf axiom; their inverses glue too. They are invariant, so Section 3.1 identifies them with $K$-analytic units of $V$. Invariance of $\beta$ gives

$$\beta=\pi^*\bigl(\gamma(t_g)\bigr)\quad\hbox{on }V_E.$$

The same smooth $\gamma$ is used on both pieces; its domain contains $t_g(V)$ because $\pi$ is surjective. This gives exact local scalar descent in this case. We proved the needed gluing; we did not assume a finite étale map is an ordinary topological covering.

### 5.3. Polynomial approximation with differentiated control

Here is a proof of (7). A smooth function on a Euclidean neighbourhood of a box extends, after a cutoff, to a compactly supported smooth function equal to it near the box. For such a function $H$, its convolution $H_\varepsilon$ with a Gaussian approximate identity converges uniformly, together with all derivatives of order at most two, to $H$ on the box. This follows by differentiating under the integral, using uniform continuity of the compactly supported derivatives, and splitting the normalized Gaussian integral into a small ball and its tail.

For fixed $\varepsilon$, write

$$
H_\varepsilon(t)=c_\varepsilon\int H(u)\exp(-|t-u|^2/\varepsilon^2)\,du.
$$

Both $t$ and $u$ range over compact sets. The power series of the exponential and its derivatives through order two converge uniformly there. Replacing the exponential by a finite Taylor sum and integrating gives a polynomial in $t$ converging to $H_\varepsilon$ in the same $C^2$ norm. Choose $\varepsilon_j\to0$ and a sufficiently long finite Taylor sum at each stage. For an invariant $H$ on the invariant box, average the resulting polynomial with its coordinate-interchanged polynomial. The error bound is unchanged and the averaged polynomial is invariant. This proves (7). Increasing the derivative order at stage $j$ would give all fixed finite orders; only order two is needed and claimed in Theorem A.

These approximations are not claimed to converge with controlled derivatives in the downstairs moments $S,Q$. The argument requires derivative control in fixed upstairs moment coordinates, which (7) supplies. This distinction prevents an unjustified claim of exact smooth descent by passing to a limit downstairs.

## 6. Compact supports and global approximation

Now let $\beta\in A_c^{0,0}(X_E)$ be invariant. Its support has compact image $C$ in the Hausdorff space $X$. Cover $C$ by finitely many ordinary opens $U_i=\operatorname{int}_X(V_i)$ from Section 5, with their fixed local constructions. Section 2.2 gives nonnegative ordinary smooth $\chi_i$ on $X$, with compact support inside $U_i$, and $\sum_i\chi_i=1$ near $C$. Set

$$D=\bigcup_i\operatorname{supp}\chi_i.$$

This is a fixed compact set, and $C\subset D$. For a two-point-fibre chart let $b_{i,j}$ be its exact smooth descendant, independent of $j$. For a singleton chart choose $b_{i,j}$ from (7). Define

$$\alpha_j=\sum_i \widetilde{\chi_i b_{i,j}}, \tag{8}$$

where each summand is extended by zero from $U_i$. This is a compactly supported ordinary smooth function on $X$, with support in $D$. Indeed the closed compact support of $\chi_i$ is contained in $U_i$; every point outside that support, including points outside $U_i$, has an ordinary neighbourhood on which the product is zero. Thus zero extension creates no nonsmooth boundary or derivative contribution.

On $X_E$ write $e_{i,j}=\beta-P_{i,j}(t_{F_i})$ on the singleton charts, and $e_{i,j}=0$ on the exact charts. We have the global equality

$$
\beta-\pi^*\alpha_j=\sum_i (\chi_i\circ\pi)e_{i,j}, \tag{9}
$$

with the same zero extensions. To check (9), $\beta=(\sum_i\chi_i\circ\pi)\beta$ near its support by the partition identity, and off its support both sides of that particular identity are zero. All differentiated errors have support in $\pi^{-1}(D)$, compact by Section 3.2.

For each $i$, cover the compact $\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{supp}\chi_i)$ by finitely many upstairs affinoid neighbourhoods $W_{i,k}\subset V_{i,E}$ on which $\chi_i\circ\pi$ has a fixed ordinary scalar moment representation. Combining that moment with $F_i$ represents every term on the right of (9) on the same fixed chart. The product rule and (7) bound every coefficient of its $d'd''$ by $C_i\varepsilon_j$, with $\varepsilon_j\to0$; $C_i$ depends on the fixed cutoffs, charts, and compact coefficient domains, not on $j$. This is the precise differentiated topology used in the proof. It proves Theorem A(2). No $C^0$ density assertion is substituted for it.

## 7. The measure estimate on a curve

We now assume the pure one-dimensional hypotheses. On a fixed compact moment neighbourhood with coordinates $t_1,\ldots,t_r$, write an error top form as

$$\eta=\sum_{k,l} H_{kl}(t)\,d't_k\wedge d''t_l,$$

where $|H_{kl}|\leq M\varepsilon_j$ on the compact moment image. Set

$$\xi=\sum_k d't_k\wedge d''t_k.$$

On a calibrated one-cell, contraction with its tangent vector $v$ gives the scalar $\sum_{k,l}H_{kl}v_kv_l$, times the positive standard calibration. The elementary inequality

$$
\left|\sum_{k,l}H_{kl}v_kv_l\right|
\leq M\varepsilon_j\left(\sum_k|v_k|\right)^2
\leq rM\varepsilon_j\sum_k v_k^2
$$

therefore gives an inequality of measures

$$|\eta_{\mathrm{Rad},E}|\leq rM\varepsilon_j\,\xi_{\mathrm{Rad},E}.$$ 

The latter positive measure is finite on the compact neighbourhood, by the top-form integration formula with its actual finite canonical cell calibration. Lower-dimensional cells carry no top measure. This argument also handles nonsymmetric coefficient matrices: their antisymmetric part contracts to zero. Nilpotents enter the calibration through the actual degrees; positivity of the calibration and finiteness, not a reduction or normalized point count, are used in the inequality.

Applying this on the finite fixed cover from Section 6, and summing the bounds (overlaps only increase the upper bound), proves

$$
\bigl\|(d'd''(\beta-\pi^*\alpha_j))_{\mathrm{Rad},E}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\longrightarrow0,\qquad
\operatorname{supp}\subseteq\pi^{-1}(D). \tag{10}
$$

Here $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$ is the total variation of the signed top Radon measure. Since $h\circ\pi$ is bounded on the fixed compact set $\pi^{-1}(D)$, (10) implies

$$\mathcal B_{h\circ\pi}(\beta-\pi^*\alpha_j)\longrightarrow0. \tag{11}$$

This states and proves the topology controlling the differentiated pairings against the continuous multiplier. Uniform approximation of scalar values alone would not imply (10) or (11).

The dimension-one hypothesis is essential to what we have proved: the tests of a Hessian current are scalar $(0,0)$ functions, and their Hessians are top forms. For dimension $n\geq2$, the tests have bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$. Polynomial descent of scalar coefficients does not supply invariant differential-form descent or density in that bidegree. We make no such inference.

## 8. Averaging, field normalization, and scalar ascent

First check the averaging identity with the correct ground field. The semilinear $\sigma$ is a $K$-analytic automorphism of $Z_0$, so smooth pullback commutes with $d',d''$ there. The common ordinary form sheaf on $Z_0$ and $Z$ has already been checked. By CLD 15.4.3 for this isomorphism and CLD 14.4.3,

$$
\sigma_*((\sigma^*\omega)_{\mathrm{Rad},K})=\omega_{\mathrm{Rad},K},
\qquad \omega_{\mathrm{Rad},K}=2\omega_{\mathrm{Rad},E}
$$

for any compact smooth top form on the carrier. Dividing this equality of real signed measures by two gives

$$\sigma_*((\sigma^*\omega)_{\mathrm{Rad},E})=\omega_{\mathrm{Rad},E}. \tag{12}$$

Since $h\circ\pi$ is invariant, signed-measure integration and (12) show

$$\mathcal B_{h\circ\pi}(\sigma^*\beta)=\mathcal B_{h\circ\pi}(\beta).$$

For an arbitrary upstairs compact scalar test put

$$\beta^{\mathrm{av}}=\tfrac12(\beta+\sigma^*\beta).$$

Its support is contained in the finite saturation $\operatorname{supp}\beta\cup\sigma^{-1}\operatorname{supp}\beta$, which is compact. We do not claim equality of supports, because cancellation may occur. It is invariant and has the same $\mathcal B$ pairing as $\beta$.

Apply Theorem A and (11) to $\beta^{\mathrm{av}}$. For each downstairs $\alpha_j$ in (8), smooth functoriality and CLD 15.3.1 give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_{h\circ\pi}(\pi^*\alpha_j)
&=\int_{X_E}(h\circ\pi)\,(\pi^*d'd''\alpha_j)_{\mathrm{Rad},E}\\
&=\int_Xh\,(d'd''\alpha_j)_{\mathrm{Rad},K}
=\mathcal B_h(\alpha_j).
\end{aligned} \tag{13}
$$

Both differentiated forms have compact support, and $h$ is bounded on the relevant compact set, so the signed pushforward integration formula is applicable. No arbitrary-current pullback is defined.

If $d'd''[h]=0$, the right side of (13) is zero. Taking the limit in (11), then using (12), shows $\mathcal B_{h\circ\pi}(\beta)=0$ for every upstairs compact scalar test. This proves ascent.

For the reverse implication, a downstairs compact test $\alpha$ has compact pullback by Section 3.2, and the same identity (13) directly gives $\mathcal B_h(\alpha)=0$ from the upstairs equation. This reverse implication in fact needs no hypothesis (H) or quadratic restriction for a finite separable extension; the same finite étale component argument checks curve purity there. The forward proof does need the stated quadratic field condition.

All arguments localize to ordinary open subspaces, whose inverse images are their scalar extensions. The field condition is unchanged, the affine neighbourhood constructions are local, and the ambient hypotheses persist. Iterating Theorem B proves Corollary C. No first-current equivalence is asserted: its curve tests have non-scalar bidegrees, and were not covered by the approximation theorem.

## 9. A concrete included extension and checks on the trace identity

Take $K=\mathbb Q_2$, with $|2|=1/2$, and $E=\mathbb Q_2(a)$, where $a^2+a+1=0$. This polynomial has no $\mathbb Q_2$-root: any root would have norm one (compare its leading and constant terms if the norm were larger or smaller), and its residue would be a root of $Z^2+Z+1$ in $\mathbb F_2$, which has none. It is therefore irreducible of degree two and separable in characteristic zero. Its other root is $\sigma(a)=-1-a$. The root equation gives $|a|=1$, and $a-\sigma(a)=2a+1$ has norm one by the ultrametric inequality because $|2a|<1$. Thus (H) holds. This supplies a nonempty arithmetic scope without relying on terminology about unramified extensions.

For a quadratic extension with a separable degree-two residue extension, the usual unramified case, (H) can also be checked directly. Choose a nonzero residue generator and lift its monic separable quadratic polynomial to $K^\circ[Z]$. Choose a lift $a_0\in E^\circ$ of that generator. The lifted polynomial has $|F(a_0)|<1$ and $|F'(a_0)|=1$. Newton iteration in the complete field $E$ stays within distance less than one of $a_0$, keeps derivative norm one, and squares the error bound at each step (expand the quadratic polynomial at each iterate). It converges to a root $a$ with the same nonzero residue. Irreducibility of the residual polynomial shows that $F$ is irreducible over $K$: any $K$-root would be integral and give a residual root, and a quadratic factorization would have a root. Thus its other root is $\sigma(a)$. The derivative at $a$ is $a-\sigma(a)$; separability of the residue polynomial makes its norm one. This proves (H). The exact theorem hypothesis remains the displayed norm condition; no general ramified extension has been added.

It also checks the three determinant identities in the symbolic quadratic algebra $\sigma(a)=-1-a$. These are finite algebraic diagnostics, not checks of an analytic current equation or a proof verifier. The complete analytic proofs are Sections 3--8. The saved output is `evidence/checks.json`.

## 10. Comparison with the original atom and remaining gates

The catalogue's original P06.2 concerns every continuous zero-Hessian function on a good, separated, strict, boundaryless space over every complete nontrivially valued field, with classical curve-test recognition and its universal scalar-extension quantifiers. Theorem B proves neither that recognition nor all finite-Galois ascent. It is a new special case of one missing gate: a quadratic extension satisfying (H), in pure dimension one, without imposing scalar stability as an input. It is consequently `status=partial` and `candidate=false`.

This is not a reproving of the catalogue's algebraically closed smooth-proper-curve recognition case, coordinate-product classification, or invariant toric result. It proves current transport on arbitrary analytic curves under its field restriction, without recognizing the functions first. No catalogue recognition statement is used as a mathematical premise. The new scalar polynomial descent lemma works in arbitrary dimension but is only a scalar lemma; the Hessian ascent conclusion uses curve scalar tests.

The following remain unproved:

* arbitrary finite Galois $E/K$, including quadratic extensions lacking (H), and the requested general pure dimension $n\geq2$;
* ordinary invariant compact-form descent or differentiated-test density in bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$ for that higher-dimensional gate;
* arbitrary nongalois finite separable ascent (apart from the explicitly stated finite quadratic towers);
* ascent to the completion of a specified infinite algebraic extension, with full upstairs compact tests and supports;
* transcendental analytic extension, curve-current pullback, normalization transfer, and universal ambient classical recognition;
* regularization of both signs into the ordinary CLD-psh hierarchy.

The accepted imported purely inseparable note n100 is not used in this proof and is not relabelled or extended. No new conclusion about completed perfection or algebraic closure follows. The earlier curve-repair draft, its review, and the earlier closed-curve manuscript are not premises. No counterexample has been constructed; an unresolved descent proof is not a counterexample. The non-strict arbitrary-radius example from the catalogue is outside this theorem and the original strict counterexample scope.
