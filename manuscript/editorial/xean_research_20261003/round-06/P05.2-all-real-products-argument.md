## Result and exact scope

The original arbitrary-balanced-pair classification P05.2 remains open. This draft settles the specified **all-real-reference converse on products of rank-one balanced ray stars times complete affine space**. The new argument removes the actual ambient-extension assumption from the previous real-reference draft. It does not assert that such an extension exists. The mechanism is an exact finite convex decomposition of a real PA PC reference into rational PA PC references, followed by addition of their rational-domain modifications. No independent review, formal verification, or priority claim is made for this draft.

For each $1\leq l\leq k$, let $N_l$ be a lattice, $V_l=N_l\otimes\mathbb R$, and let $v_{li}\in N_l$, $1\leq i\leq r_l$, be primitive generators of distinct rays spanning $V_l$. Assume
$$
r_l\geq2,\qquad \dim V_l=r_l-1,\qquad
\ker\bigl((a_i)\mapsto\textstyle\sum_i a_iv_{li}\bigr)
=\mathbb Rw_l,\quad w_l\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^{r_l}.
\tag{S}
$$
Set $G_l=\bigcup_i\mathbb R_{\geq0}v_{li}$ and assign its $i$th ray weight $\alpha_{li}=c_lw_{li}$, where $c_l>0$ is real. Let $E=N_E\otimes\mathbb R$ have dimension $m$ and constant weight $e>0$. Use the actual direct lattice and carrier
$$
N=\bigoplus_{l=1}^kN_l\oplus N_E,\quad W=N\otimes\mathbb R,
\quad X=\prod_{l=1}^kG_l\times E,\quad d=k+m.
$$
The top product weights are $e\prod_l\alpha_{li_l}$. Empty products are permitted. A factor is **branched** if $r_l\geq3$. The tangent lattice of a cell is always its intersection with $N$, and primitive quotient normals are computed in these saturated lattices. The subgroup collectively generated by a star's rays need not be saturated.

Here $\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb Q}$ means continuous functions with rational affine coefficients on a finite rational spatial complex. The class $\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}$ permits real coefficients on a finite **rational spatial** complex. The literal source class $\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb Z}$ requires integral tangent slopes and rational constants. These three conventions are distinct.

**Theorem (all-real-reference product criterion; partial P05.2).** Let $\gamma\in\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}(X)\cap\mathrm{PC}(X)$ be positive at infinity. The following package holds if and only if at most one star is branched:

For every bounded $u\in\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}(X)$, the raw envelope $P_\gamma(u)$ is real-regularizable and
$$
\int_X\bigl(P_\gamma(u)-(\gamma+u)\bigr)
\mathrm{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(P_\gamma(u))=0.
\tag{T}
$$
The positive implication is the precise real-reference positive theorem supplied in caller-imported note **n100**. Its hypotheses are checked below; its negative rational-reference quantifier is not used as an all-real-reference theorem.

The new negative implication is stronger than failure of whole-pair regularity. If at least two stars are branched, **every** reference in the stated real class has a bounded, genuinely nonseparable real-PA obstacle $u$, on finite rational spatial domains, whose envelope is an explicitly constructed
$$
P_\gamma(u)=P\in\mathrm{PAPC}_{\mathbb R}(X,\gamma)
\subset\mathrm{PC}_{\mathrm{reg},\mathbb R}(X,\gamma),
$$
and whose **complete** contact defect is
$$
\int_X(P-(\gamma+u))\mathrm{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(P)
=-\epsilon\lambda^d\kappa<0.
\tag{N}
$$
Here $\epsilon\in\{1,2\}$, $\lambda>0$ is rational, and the positive constant computed directly below is
$$
\kappa=2^{m+2}d!\,CC'e\prod_{l=3}^k M_l.
\tag{K}
$$
No ambient convex extension of $\gamma$, global PC approximation theorem, or all-obstacle regularity of a negative pair is assumed.

## Elementary finite-dimensional facts

The following proofs make the new finite support and decomposition arguments independent of an unstated polyhedral extension or approximation theorem.

**Lemma 1 (finite generation and closedness).** A rational polyhedral cone, including one with lineality, has finitely many rational generators. A nonempty pointed rational polyhedron has the form $\operatorname{conv}(A)+\operatorname{cone}(R)$ for finite nonempty rational $A$ and finite rational $R$. A finitely generated real cone is closed. Projection of a finite system of nonstrict real linear inequalities is a finite system of nonstrict linear inequalities; rational data remain rational.

**Proof.** Write a pointed cone as $\ell_i(z)\geq0$, including equality constraints as pairs. Its defining forms have zero common kernel, since a vector in that kernel would give a line. Therefore $\ell=\sum_i\ell_i$ is positive on every nonzero cone vector. The section $\ell=1$ is bounded: an unbounded sequence, normalized and passed to a convergent subsequence on the unit sphere, would yield a nonzero cone vector with $\ell=0$. The section is a compact polyhedron. A point in a positive-dimensional minimal face is on a line in that face with two terminal points in smaller faces; induction expresses it as a convex combination of vertices. There are finitely many vertices, each obtained from a full-rank selection of active equations in the affine hull, and rational equations give rational vertices. Their directions generate the cone. For a cone with lineality, quotient by its rational lineality, apply this argument to the pointed quotient, choose rational lifts, and append both signs of a rational lineality basis. This yields finite rational generators.

Homogenize a nonempty pointed polyhedron $P=\{x:Ax\geq b\}$ to $\{(y,t):Ay\geq bt,\ t\geq0\}$. A line in this cone would have $t=0$ and give lineality of $P$. Apply the pointed cone result. Generators with $t>0$, after dividing by $t$, give $A\subset P$; those with $t=0$ give $R\subset\operatorname{rec}(P)$. Taking the section $t=1$ proves the assertion, with $A$ nonempty.

For projection, eliminate a variable by requiring each of its finitely many lower bounds to be at most each upper bound, retaining inequalities independent of that variable. A one-sided finite list of bounds is always satisfiable. This is exact over the reals, preserves nonstrict inequalities, and preserves rational coefficients. Repetition proves the projection statement. A cone generated by $g_i$ is the projection of $x=\sum_i t_ig_i$, $t_i\geq0$, so is a closed polyhedron by that statement. The argument applies to real generators as well. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (finite support alternative).** Given finite vectors $d_i$ and real numbers $b_i$, there exists a linear form $p$ with $p(d_i)\leq b_i$ for all $i$ if
$$
\sum_i t_ib_i\geq0\quad\text{whenever }t_i\geq0,
\quad\sum_i t_id_i=0.
\tag{F}
$$
The converse is immediate.

**Proof.** Let $K=\operatorname{cone}\{(d_i,b_i),(0,1)\}$, a closed cone by Lemma 1. Condition (F) excludes $z=(0,-1)$ from $K$. Let $k$ be a nearest point of $K$ to $z$; existence follows by restricting to a sufficiently large compact ball. With $r=k-z$, the nearest-point inequality gives $\langle r,w-k\rangle\geq0$ for all $w\in K$. Taking $w=0,2k$ gives $\langle r,k\rangle=0$. Thus $\langle r,w\rangle\geq0$ and $\langle r,z\rangle=-\|r\|^2<0$. Write $r=(-p_0,\alpha)$. The last inequality gives $\alpha>0$, and each generator gives $p_0(d_i)\leq\alpha b_i$. Set $p=p_0/\alpha$. $\square$

## A finite rational system for PA polyhedral convexity

Let $\Omega$ be **any** fixed finite rational polyhedral complex in a lattice vector space $W$, with carrier $Y$. For a face $\tau$ and an incident cell $\sigma\succeq\tau$, put
$$
T_{\sigma,\tau}=\{v:x+tv\in\sigma\text{ for all sufficiently small }t\geq0\},
\qquad x\in\operatorname{RelInt}(\tau).
$$
This does not depend on the chosen $x$: it is given by the constraints of $\sigma$ active on $\tau$. It is a rational polyhedral cone, with finite rational generators $d_{\sigma,\tau,q}$ by Lemma 1. Both signs of the tangent directions of $\tau$ are included through the cone's lineality. If $a$ is PA on $\Omega$, write $a_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}$ for its derivative on the tangent space of $\sigma$.

**Lemma 3 (local support characterizes actual PCC convexity).** A continuous real PA function $a$ on $\Omega$ is PC on $Y$ if and only if, for every $\tau\in\Omega$, there exists an ambient linear form $p_\tau\in W^*$ with
$$
p_\tau(d_{\sigma,\tau,q})
\leq a_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}(d_{\sigma,\tau,q})
\quad\text{for all }\sigma\succeq\tau,q.
\tag{L}
$$
These are finitely many rational linear inequalities in the affine coefficients of $a$ and the coefficients of $p_\tau$, together with rational linear continuity equations for $a$.

**Proof, necessity.** Pick $x\in\operatorname{RelInt}(\tau)\cap N_{\mathbb Q}$. A common rational $t>0$ makes every $x+td_i$ rational and in its incident cell, where $i$ runs through the finite generators at $\tau$. For any $t_i\geq0$ with $\sum t_id_i=0$ and $S=\sum t_i>0$, these points and coefficients $t_i/S$ have centre $x$. The face $\tau$ is incident to every endpoint cell, so this is an actual PCC on $\Omega$. Affinity gives $0\leq\sum_i t_i a_{\sigma_i,\mathrm{lin}}(d_i)$. The case $S=0$ is trivial. Lemma 2 supplies $p_\tau$. The argument explicitly allows real coefficients in this PCC, exactly as Definition 3.2 does, and uses only rational endpoints. There is no intermediate centre off the carrier.

**Proof, sufficiency.** At every $x\in\operatorname{RelInt}(\tau)$, (L) extends by nonnegative linear combination to all of $T_{\sigma,\tau}$. For every incident affine piece and sufficiently small feasible $v$, it gives
$$
a(x+v)\geq a(x)+p_\tau(v).
\tag{G}
$$
All cells containing sufficiently near points contain $\tau$: cells not containing $x$ are a positive distance from $x$, there are finitely many cells, and a cell containing a relative-interior point of $\tau$ contains $\tau$. Thus (G) is a simultaneous local support inequality on the carrier germ.

Consider any line segment $[x,y]\subset Y$. Restriction of $a$ is continuous PL with finitely many intervals. At each interior breakpoint $z$, use its minimal face and its form $p$. If $v=y-x$, the left derivative $b_-$ and right derivative $b_+$ satisfy $b_-\leq p(v)\leq b_+$ by (G) in the two directions. Hence slopes along the segment are nondecreasing and the restriction is convex. At its initial point, the first directional derivative is at least $p_\tau(y-x)$. Integration of the nondecreasing slopes yields
$$
a(y)\geq a(x)+p_\tau(y-x).
\tag{S1}
$$
Now take **any** actual PCC on **any** rational structure of $Y$. Each endpoint is in a convex cell containing the centre, so every segment from the centre to an endpoint lies in $Y$. Apply (S1) with the single local form at the centre, multiply by the PCC coefficients and sum. The linear terms sum to zero. This proves exactly the PCC inequality, including arbitrary real centres and coefficients, without a routing assumption.

For rationality of the system, choose rational basepoints and rational tangent bases in the affine hulls of the cells. Intrinsic affine assignments are finite vectors of real coefficients. Agreement on each shared face is given by rational linear equations (evaluate at a rational basepoint and rational tangent basis). Each derivative at a rational $d_i$ is a rational-linear expression in these variables. The $p_\tau(d_i)$ expressions are also rational-linear in the ambient coefficients of $p_\tau$. This proves the asserted finite system. $\square$

The lemma is a statement about **PA functions on a fixed finite rational complex**. It makes no approximation or regularization claim for non-PA PC functions or arbitrary envelopes. The local forms need not be global ambient supporting forms for $a$.

**Lemma 4 (real PC implies real PAPC at the scope used here).** A continuous real PA PC function on finite rational domains has nonnegative divisor against every nonnegative real local balanced cycle of every positive dimension.

**Proof.** Take a common rational refinement of the function and a local cycle. At a tested codimension-one face $\tau$ and a relative-interior point, Lemma 3 gives a simultaneous local support form $p$. Both tangent directions along $\tau$ show $p=a_{\tau,\mathrm{lin}}$ on its tangent space. Every inward normal derivative of $a_\sigma-p$ is nonnegative by (G); tangential changes of a normal do not change this conclusion. Formula (D), with the ambient linear form subtracted, becomes
$$
(a\cdot z)(\tau)=\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}z(\sigma)
(a_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}-p)(n_{\sigma/\tau})\geq0.
$$
This applies on ordinary open sets as well, because each tested germ is unchanged by restriction to the open set. It tests all dimensions, all local cycles and real weights. $\square$

## Exact rational convex decomposition of a real reference

**Lemma 5 (exact decomposition, preserving strict growth).** If $\gamma\in\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}(Y)\cap\mathrm{PC}(Y)$ on a fixed finite rational complex is positive at infinity, then there are finitely many $\gamma_j\in\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb Q}(Y)\cap\mathrm{PC}(Y)$, each positive at infinity on that same complex, and real $a_j>0$ with $\sum_j a_j=1$, such that
$$
\gamma=\sum_{j=1}^s a_j\gamma_j\quad\text{exactly on all }Y.
\tag{R}
$$
There is no approximation error, including at infinity.

**Proof.** By Lemma 3, choose a feasible lifted vector $y$ consisting of all affine coefficients of $\gamma$ and all support forms $p_\tau$. It satisfies finitely many rational homogeneous equations $Ey=0$ and nonstrict inequalities $Ay\geq0$.

Every affine reference cell is pointed: nonzero lineality would give two opposite recession directions with opposite slopes, contradicting strict positivity. By Lemma 1, each recession cone has finitely many rational generators. Strict positivity at infinity is therefore exactly a finite list of strict rational-linear inequalities $By>0$, one for every nonzero generator. Indeed positive linear values on these generators are positive on every nonzero nonnegative combination. Bounded cells add no condition.

Add to $Ey=0$ the equalities given by precisely those rows of $A$ that vanish at $y$, and call the resulting kernel $L$. It is a rational linear subspace. Within $L$, all remaining $A$ inequalities and all $B$ inequalities hold strictly near $y$. Choose a rational basis of $L$ and a sufficiently small coordinate box about $y$, with rational endpoints strictly enclosing each coordinate of $y$. Its entire box satisfies those strict inequalities. All active $A$ rows remain zero. Every vertex $y_j$ is rational, satisfies the lifted support and continuity equations, and has strict growth. Standard box barycentric weights are products of the one-dimensional weights $ (b_i-y_i)/(b_i-a_i)$ and $(y_i-a_i)/(b_i-a_i)$; they are positive real numbers summing to one and reproduce every coordinate of $y$. Thus $y=\sum_j a_jy_j$ exactly. If $L$ has dimension zero, $y=0$ is rational and the singleton representation suffices whenever the strict conditions are empty; with nonempty strict growth this case is impossible.

Project each $y_j$ to its affine coefficients $\gamma_j$. These give rational affine functions on the same spatial complex. Lemma 3 proves their actual PC inequalities; the finite strict generator tests prove their growth. Projection of the exact vector identity gives (R). $\square$

Although separate $\gamma_j$ can have different recession slopes, their **exact weighted sum** is the original reference. The construction below adds exact modifications of all components and therefore restores the prescribed exterior function identically. Merely approximating $\gamma$ by a rational reference would not do this.

## Recursive divisor algebra and normalizations

We give the algebra needed for the explicit mass computation. Formula (D) is bilinear. Normal-lift changes are tangent to $\tau$ and cancel by continuity. For an ambient affine function, its linear part pulls out of the sum in (D), so its divisor on a balanced cycle is zero. The formula depends only on incident germs, which proves recursive locality.

Refinement is checked locally. If a new tested face lies in the interior of an old supported cell, the two adjacent subdivisions have equal inherited weights, equal affine pieces and opposite primitive normals, so contribute zero. If its minimal old face has the same dimension as the tested face, the saturated tangents, normal quotient classes and inherited incident contributions agree with the old formula. If that minimal face has dimension larger than the original cycle's dimension, all inherited weights vanish. These exhaust the possibilities and prove refinement compatibility, including local cycles and generalized fans.

For balancing and commutativity, quotient a codimension-two face by its saturated tangent lattice, subtract the functions' common ambient linear extensions along that tangent, and refine to pointed two-cones if necessary. For each two-cone with boundary primitive rays $u_r,u_s$, write its weight $\omega_{rs}$ and its saturated plane index
$$
\chi_{rs}=[N_{C_{rs}}:\mathbb Zu_r+\mathbb Zu_s].
$$
The inward primitive normal at $r$ is $u_s/\chi_{rs}$ modulo $\mathbb Ru_r$. Balancing at $r$ has the form
$$
\sum_{C_{rs}\succ r}\frac{\omega_{rs}}{\chi_{rs}}u_s=t_ru_r.
$$
Tangential terms in normal lifts cancel in (D), leaving first divisor weight
$$
D_a(r)=\sum_{C_{rs}\succ r}\frac{\omega_{rs}}{\chi_{rs}}a(u_s)-t_ra(u_r).
$$
Summing $D_a(r)u_r$ and substituting the balancing expression for $t_ru_r$ cancels, for each unordered cone, the two terms $a(u_s)u_r+a(u_r)u_s$. Thus the first divisor is balanced. A second divisor at the quotient origin is
$$
\sum_{\{r,s\}}\frac{\omega_{rs}}{\chi_{rs}}
\bigl(a(u_s)b(u_r)+a(u_r)b(u_s)\bigr)
-\sum_rt_ra(u_r)b(u_r),
$$
which is symmetric in $a,b$. This proves commutativity at every tested codimension-two face. Refinement allows the computation even when the initial germ has lineality. Saturation is retained in the quotient, so $\chi_{rs}$ is the actual normal index. These arguments work directly for real coefficients and weights; no cycle pushforward is used.

For cycles $Z,Y$ in actual direct lattice summands, product tangent lattices split. A codimension-one product face loses a dimension in exactly one factor, with normal $(n,0)$ or $(0,n)$. For a function pulled back from the first factor, (D) gives $(a\cdot Z)\times Y$ on the first type and zero on the second type. Hence
$$
(a\circ p_Z)\cdot(Z\times Y)=(a\cdot Z)\times Y.
\tag{PR}
$$
This includes zero cycles, lineality, refinements and every successive intersection. The direct ambient lattice splitting prevents any extra product index.

## The two-branched-star trap and its full mass

Relabel two branched stars as $G_1,G_2$. Write their relations $w_i,w'_j$, weights $\alpha_i=c_1w_i$, $\beta_j=c_2w'_j$. Partition each set of rays into three nonempty groups. Put $W_g=\sum_{i\in g}w_i$, $W'_g=\sum_{j\in g}w'_j$, and
$$
u_1=(1,0),\quad u_2=(0,1),\quad u_3=(-1,-1).
$$
The assignment $L_0(v_i)=u_g/W_g$ annihilates the unique relation and defines a rational linear map. Likewise define $L'_0$. Choose one positive integer $D$ clearing both matrices in lattice bases, and set $L=DL_0$, $L'=DL'_0$. Then
$$
L(v_i)=k_iu_g,\quad k_i=D/W_g\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad
L'(v'_j)=\ell_ju_g,\quad\ell_j=D/W'_g\in\mathbb Z_{>0},
$$
where scalar integrality follows from integrality of the maps and primitivity of $u_g$. Each group satisfies
$$
\sum_{i\in g}\alpha_ik_i=C=c_1D,\qquad
\sum_{j\in g}\beta_j\ell_j=C'=c_2D.
\tag{G1}
$$

On $G_1\times G_2$, set $F_1=(La)_1-(L'b)_1$, $F_2=(La)_2-(L'b)_2$, $A=|F_1|$, $B=|F_2|$, $q=A+B$. These restrict ambient rational convex homogeneous functions. In quadrant $(a,b)=(tv_i,sv'_j)$, $F_1=p_it-p'_js$, where $p_i=k_i,0,-k_i$ according to groups 1,2,3. Interior bends of $A$ occur precisely when both rays belong to group 1 or both to group 3. Put $g_{ij}=\gcd(k_i,\ell_j)$ and
$$
h_{ij}=(\ell_jv_i/g_{ij},\ k_iv'_j/g_{ij}).
$$
The saturated quadrant tangent lattice is $\mathbb Z(v_i,0)\oplus\mathbb Z(0,v'_j)$. The displayed diagonal vector is primitive there and in the ambient direct lattice. The primitive quotient covector is $(k_i/g_{ij},-\ell_j/g_{ij})$. Bezout supplies a primitive inward normal lift on which $k_it-\ell_js$ has value $g_{ij}$; opposite normals and the absolute-value bend give weight $2g_{ij}\alpha_i\beta_j$.

On a nonzero second-factor axis in group 2 the outgoing normal derivatives give $\beta_j\sum_i\alpha_i|p_i|=2C\beta_j$. On group 1 or 3 axes, the sign is fixed and the derivative sum is $\pm\beta_j\sum_i\alpha_ip_i=0$. First-factor axes give $2C'\alpha_i$ in group 2 and zero otherwise. Other quadrant interiors are affine. This includes every first-divisor face:
$$
\begin{split}
A\cdot[G_1\times G_2]={}&
\sum_{g=1,3}\sum_{i,j\in g}2g_{ij}\alpha_i\beta_j[h_{ij}]\\
&+\sum_{i\in2}2C'\alpha_i[(v_i,0)]
+\sum_{j\in2}2C\beta_j[(0,v'_j)].
\end{split}
\tag{D1}
$$
Here $[h]$ is the ray of primitive generator $h$. The first projections of its diagonal weighted vectors sum to $2C'\sum_{i\in1,3}\alpha_iv_i$, and the axis terms complete this to zero; the second projections similarly complete $2C\sum_{j\in1,3}\beta_jv'_j$ to zero. Thus balancing is also checked directly.

The function $A$ vanishes on its entire divisor. The slopes of $B$ on its diagonals are zero, on its first axes $k_i$, and on its second axes $\ell_j$. Therefore
$$
A^2\cdot[G_1\times G_2]=0,\qquad
B\cdot A\cdot[G_1\times G_2]=4CC'\delta_0.
$$
Coordinate exchange gives $B^2=0$. Bilinearity and the two mixed orders give
$$
q^2\cdot[G_1\times G_2]=8CC'\delta_0.
\tag{D2}
$$
This is a direct divisor proof, not a pushforward by the nonproper difference map.

There is an actual positive balanced **zero curve** for $q$: take all $h_{ij}$ with both rays in the same group, now including group 2, and ray weights
$$
\theta_{ij}=\alpha_i\beta_jg_{ij}.
\tag{Z}
$$
Its first projection sums to $C'\sum_i\alpha_iv_i=0$ and its second to $C\sum_j\beta_jv'_j=0$. Thus $\sum\theta_{ij}h_{ij}=0$. Its weights have the common real factor $c_1c_2$ times rational numbers. The function $q$ is zero on all its rays. The curve will only manufacture an actual PCC.

For every other star, put $h_l(tv_{li})=t$. An ambient rational convex extension is the maximum of forms $p_{li}$ determined by
$$
p_{li}(v_{li})=1,\qquad
p_{li}(v_{lj})=-w_{li}/\sum_{a\ne i}w_{la}\quad(j\ne i).
$$
These assignments annihilate the unique relation, define rational forms, and their maximum restricts to $h_l$. Primitive outgoing slopes one give $h_l\cdot[G_l]=M_l\delta_0$, where $M_l=\sum_i\alpha_{li}>0$. A second intersection vanishes. Choose a lattice basis of $N_E$ with dual coordinate functions $z_a$. On each corresponding affine coordinate line, $|z_a|$ has first divisor $2\delta_0$ and second divisor zero.

On the full product define
$$
Q=q+\sum_{l=3}^kh_l+\sum_{a=1}^m|z_a|,
\qquad \rho=\sum_{l=1}^kh_l+\sum_{a=1}^m|z_a|.
\tag{Q}
$$
Both are rational homogeneous continuous PA PC functions. The function $\rho$ is proper, positive off zero, and comparable with an ambient norm on the finite product cones. There is a rational $K>0$ with $0\leq Q\leq K\rho$, by taking finite rational-generator slope bounds. Appending zero coordinates, the curve (Z) remains a zero curve for $Q$.

\tag{M}

## Gluing the trap to every real reference with exact exterior growth

Fix an arbitrary reference $\gamma$ in the negative theorem. Apply Lemma 5 on any finite rational structure for it:

\tag{C}

Hence every raw competitor $h\leq\Gamma+u$ has $h(0)\leq0$. At a rational $x$ with $0<\rho(x)<R$, the rational point $y=Rx/\rho(x)$ is in the same closed quadrant, and $h(y)\leq q(y)$. The cellwise PCC with endpoints $0,y$ gives $h(x)\leq q(x)$. For an arbitrary real point in this ball, take rational simplices containing it inside its rational affine domain of $q$ and inside the ball. Such simplices exist in the minimal face containing the point; a zero-dimensional face is already rational. Their real barycentric coefficients are permitted, and the rational-point bounds give the same exact inequality, since $q$ is affine there. Outside the ball, $u=0$. Since $\Gamma$ is itself a competitor, (2) gives $P_\Gamma(u)=\Gamma$ without any continuity assumption on $h$.

On $\rho<R$, $\Gamma=q$ and (4) applies by (D)-locality. The integrand is $-u$, zero outside this ball, so the **complete** defect is
$$
\int_X(P_\Gamma(u)-(\Gamma+u))\mathrm{MA}_{\mathrm{poly}}(P_\Gamma(u))=-8R.
\tag{6}
$$
This particular envelope is literal PAPC and directly regularizable. It does not establish all-obstacle literal or real regularity of the pair. The compact roof $u$ is nonseparable: its mixed rectangle difference at $0,(Ru_1,0),(0,Ru_1),(Ru_1,Ru_1)$ equals $R$.

## 2. Two arbitrary branched rank-one stars

Relabel two branched factors as $G_1,G_2$, with generators $v_i,v'_j$, relations $w_i,w'_j$, and weights $\alpha_i=c_1w_i$, $\beta_j=c_2w'_j$. Partition each ray set into three nonempty groups, indexed by $g=1,2,3$. Put $W_g=\sum_{i\in g}w_i$, $W'_g=\sum_{j\in g}w'_j$. Since the only relation among the $v_i$ is $w$, the prescription $L_0(v_i)=u_g/W_g$ defines a rational linear map $V_1\to\mathbb R^2$: the unique relation maps to $\sum_g u_g=0$. The same holds for $L'_0$. Choose a common positive integer $D$ clearing their matrices in lattice bases, and set
$$
L=DL_0,\quad L'=DL'_0,\quad
L(v_i)=k_i u_g,\ k_i=D/W_g\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\quad
L'(v'_j)=\ell_j u_g,\ \ell_j=D/W'_g\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
$$
Integrality of $k_i,\ell_j$ follows also because $u_g$ is primitive and $L,L'$ are integral lattice maps. For every group,
$$
\sum_{i\in g}\alpha_i k_i=C:=c_1D,\qquad
\sum_{j\in g}\beta_j\ell_j=C':=c_2D.
\tag{7}
$$
These maps are actually proper on their carriers: no ray is collapsed, and $\|L(tv_i)\|=tk_i\|u_g\|$ uniformly controls the ray parameter. The product map $\pi=(L,L')$ is proper on $G_1\times G_2$ and $\pi^{-1}(0)=\{0\}$. On a ray the saturated image index is $k_i$, and on a product quadrant it is $k_i\ell_j$; the weighted sums on each target ray and quadrant are $C,C'$ and $CC'$. We do not invoke a proper-cycle projection formula: the following direct divisor calculation supplies the full mass with all indices.

Set $F_1=(La)_1-(L'b)_1$, $F_2=(La)_2-(L'b)_2$, $A=|F_1|$, $B=|F_2|$, $q=A+B$. On $C_{ij}$ the coefficients of $F_1$ are $p_i t-p'_j s$, with $p_i=k_i,0,-k_i$ according to group $1,2,3$ and $p'_j=\ell_j,0,-\ell_j$. An interior bend occurs precisely when both rays are in group 1, or both are in group 3. Set $g_{ij}=\gcd(k_i,\ell_j)$ and
$$
h_{ij}=(\ell_jv_i/g_{ij},\ k_iv'_j/g_{ij}).
$$
The saturated quadrant lattice is $\mathbb Z(v_i,0)\oplus\mathbb Z(0,v'_j)$; $h_{ij}$ is its primitive diagonal generator and is primitive in the ambient direct lattice. The primitive quotient covector is $(k_i/g_{ij},-\ell_j/g_{ij})$. Bezout gives a normal lift on which $k_it-\ell_js$ takes value $g_{ij}$, and its negative is the other lift. The bend weight is therefore $2g_{ij}\alpha_i\beta_j$.

On a second-factor axis with $j$ in group 2, the three-group outgoing derivative sum is $\beta_j\sum_i\alpha_i|p_i|=2C\beta_j$. If $j$ is in group 1 or 3, the sign of $F_1$ is fixed near that nonzero axis point and the coefficient is $\pm\beta_j\sum_i\alpha_i p_i=0$. Here $\sum_i\alpha_i p_i=0$ follows either from the original balancing relation under $L$ or from (7). On first-factor axes, the weights are $2C'\alpha_i$ for group 2 and zero otherwise. These are all possible first-divisor faces; in the other quadrant interiors $A$ is affine. Consequently
$$
A\cdot[G_1\times G_2]
=\sum_{g=1,3}\sum_{i,j\in g}2g_{ij}\alpha_i\beta_j[h_{ij}]
+\sum_{i\in2}2C'\alpha_i[(v_i,0)]
+\sum_{j\in2}2C\beta_j[(0,v'_j)].
\tag{8}
$$
This is balanced directly: the first projections of its diagonal weighted generators sum to $2C'\sum_{i\in1,3}\alpha_iv_i$, completed to zero by the first-axis terms; the second projections give $2C\sum_{j\in1,3}\beta_jv'_j$ and are completed similarly. All weights are strictly positive on their listed rays.

$A$ is zero on its entire divisor. $B$ has zero slope on every diagonal in (8), slope $k_i$ on its first axes, and slope $\ell_j$ on its second axes. Therefore
$$
A^2\cdot[G_1\times G_2]=0,\quad
B\cdot A\cdot[G_1\times G_2]=4CC'\delta_0.
$$
Exchanging groups 1 and 2 proves $B^2=0$. Commutativity and linearity now give
$$
q^2\cdot[G_1\times G_2]=8CC'\delta_0.
\tag{9}
$$
Every saturated index, axis term and mixed-order coefficient in (9) has been computed in the source convention.

There is also an actual balanced **zero curve** for $q$. Take all diagonals $h_{ij}$ with the two rays in the same group, now including group 2, and weight each by $\theta_{ij}=\alpha_i\beta_jg_{ij}$. Its first weighted projection is $C'\sum_i\alpha_i v_i=0$ and its second is $C\sum_j\beta_j v'_j=0$. Its weights are positive and have a common real factor times rational numbers. Thus its weighted generators have sum zero, and $q$ vanishes on every ray of this curve. This curve is used only to manufacture an actual PCC; it is not substituted for the top-dimensional carrier or operator.

## 3. Extra factors and the complete recursive mass

On any factor $G_l$ define $h_l(tv_{li})=t$. This is rational PA (indeed has integral intrinsic ray slopes) and is the restriction of an ambient convex function. To see this explicitly, for each $i$ choose the rational linear form $p_i$ with
$$
p_i(v_{li})=1,\qquad
p_i(v_{lj})=-w_{li}/\sum_{a\ne i}w_{la}\quad(j\ne i).
$$
The prescribed values annihilate the unique relation, hence define a linear form on $V_l$. The maximum of these forms restricts to $h_l$ on all rays. Moreover
$$
h_l\cdot[G_l]=M_l\delta_0,\qquad M_l=\sum_i\alpha_{li}>0.
\tag{10}
$$
This follows from primitive outgoing slopes one. A second intersection by $h_l$ vanishes.

Choose a lattice basis $z_1,\ldots,z_m$ on $E$ and write $b_a=|z_a|$. Define on the full product
$$
Q=q+\sum_{l=3}^k h_l+\sum_{a=1}^m b_a,\qquad
\rho=\sum_{l=1}^k h_l+\sum_{a=1}^m b_a.
\tag{11}
$$
Both are homogeneous continuous rational PA and restrictions of ambient convex functions; $\rho$ is proper on $X$, strictly positive off zero, and comparable with any ambient norm on the finite set of product cones. $Q$ is nonnegative, and $Q\leq K\rho$ for some finite rational $K>0$.

We prove the product divisor rule used here, rather than invoke a projection theorem. For balanced cycles $Z,Y$ in direct lattice summands, saturated tangent lattices of product cells are $N_\sigma\oplus N_\tau$. A codimension-one product face is a codimension-one face of exactly one factor, with normal $(n,0)$ or $(0,n)$. Applying (D) to a function pulled back from the first factor gives $(a\cdot Z)\times Y$ on the former faces and zero on the latter; the latter derivatives and correction term cancel because the function is affine along the unchanged first face. Thus
$$
(a\circ p_Z)\cdot(Z\times Y)=(a\cdot Z)\times Y.
\tag{12}
$$
The rule remains true for cycles of every dimension and their rational refinements, including zero cycles and affine lineality. The ambient lattice splitting prevents any additional product index. Iteration proves all separated mixed intersections in (11).

By (9), more than two $q$ intersections vanish, while two give $8CC'$ times the origin in the first two factors. By (10), each extra graph factor permits exactly one nonzero intersection by $h_l$. A full affine coordinate line permits exactly one by $|z_a|$, giving $2\delta_0$; two vanish. In the multinomial expansion of $Q^d$, the only potentially nonzero exponent vector is therefore $(2,1,\ldots,1)$, and its multiplicity is $d!/2!$. Consequently the **full** measure is
$$
Q^d\cdot[X]=\kappa\delta_0,\qquad
\kappa=\frac{d!}{2}\,8CC'\,e\,2^m\prod_{l=3}^k M_l
=2^{m+2}d!\,CC'e\prod_{l=3}^k M_l>0.
\tag{13}
$$
This proves the mass formula recursively in all dimensions of the advertised product class. For two tripods and $m$ affine directions of weight one it is $2^{m+2}(m+2)!$. No factorial has been omitted.

## 4. Every rational reference admits the obstruction

Fix the reference $\gamma$ in the theorem, a rational $R>0$, and $Q,\rho$ as above. First prove a coercive lower bound, not merely properness. Take a common finite rational structure for $\gamma,\rho$. A cell with nonzero lineality is impossible for $\gamma$: the two opposite recession directions would have opposite affine slopes, both required to be positive. Minkowski--Weyl supplies finitely many rational vertices and recession rays for each cell. On every nonzero such ray, the slope of $\rho$ is positive and that of $\gamma$ is positive. Choose $\eta>0$ below their finite set of slope ratios. On each cell, $\gamma-\eta\rho$ is minimized at some vertex because its ray slopes are nonnegative. Taking a minimum over the finite vertex set yields
$$
\gamma\geq\eta\rho-B\quad\hbox{on }X
\tag{14}
$$
for finite $B$. Bounded cells contribute only their vertices. This checks the pointed-polyhedron hypotheses of the external input.

Choose rational $\lambda>0$ with $\lambda K<\eta$. Then $\lambda Q-\gamma$ is bounded above by (14) and tends to minus infinity as $\rho\to\infty$. Choose rational $M>0$ so large that $\gamma-M<\lambda Q$ on the compact set $\rho\leq2R$. Set
$$
P=\max(\lambda Q,\gamma-M),\quad
b_R=(1-\rho/R)_+,\quad
f=P+\epsilon b_R,\quad u=f-\gamma.
\tag{15}
$$
$P$ is PC by the maximum inequality, and $P-\gamma=\max(\lambda Q-\gamma,-M)$ is bounded both above and below. All functions in (15) have rational coefficients. Equality walls between their affine pieces have rational coefficients, so a common finite **rational** spatial subdivision exists. $P$ is PAPC by the direct effectivity observation and has a uniform decreasing approximation $P+1/j$ in the same growth class. The bounded obstacle $u$ is rational PA and hence admissible in the real category. It is not claimed to have integral slopes.

For each zero-curve ray from Section 2, append zero in all remaining factors and form
$$
x_{ij}=\frac{R}{\rho(h_{ij})}h_{ij}.
$$
These are rational points on $\rho=R$ with $Q=0$. Choose endpoint coefficients proportional to $\theta_{ij}\rho(h_{ij})$ and normalize their sum to one. Because the $\theta_{ij}$ share one real scalar times rational values, the normalized coefficients are positive rational numbers. Their barycentre is zero, by the balancing identity for the zero curve. Every endpoint belongs to a closed product cone incident to the origin face, so this is a PCC on the original product structure. At these endpoints $f=P=\lambda Q=0$. Every raw competitor $h\leq f$ consequently satisfies $h(0)\leq0=P(0)$.

If $x$ is rational with $0<\rho(x)<R$, then $y=Rx/\rho(x)$ is rational, lies in the same closed product cone, and has $f(y)=P(y)=\lambda Q(y)$. The endpoints $0,y$ and centre $x$ form a PCC in that cell; homogeneity gives
$$
h(x)\leq\frac{\rho(x)}R h(y)+\left(1-\frac{\rho(x)}R\right)h(0)
\leq\lambda Q(x)=P(x).
$$
For an arbitrary real $x$ in the ball, work in its minimal rational face of a common affine-domain structure for $Q,\rho$. Take rational simplices containing $x$ in that face and in $\rho<R$; their vertices can be made arbitrarily close to $x$. The same-cell PCC inequality with real barycentric coefficients and the rational-point bounds yields $h(x)\leq\lambda Q(x)$ exactly by affinity. A zero-dimensional face is rational and was already treated. For $\rho\geq R$, $b_R=0$ and the obstacle itself gives $h\leq P$. Since $P$ is a competitor, this proves $P_\gamma(u)=P$ for the raw envelope (2).

$P=\lambda Q$ on $\rho\leq2R$, so the recursive measure on $\rho<R$ is $\lambda^d\kappa\delta_0$, by (D)-locality and (13). The integrand $P-f=-\epsilon b_R$ vanishes outside this ball and equals $-\epsilon$ at zero. This proves the exact defect (1), including all mass elsewhere because its integrand is zero.

The obstacle can be guaranteed nonseparable, rather than only the local envelope. Fix rays of the first two factors and the rectangle with first parameter $R$, second parameter $R$, and all other coordinates zero. The mixed rectangle difference
$$
\Delta g=g(0,0)+g(Rv_i,Rv'_j)-g(Rv_i,0)-g(0,Rv'_j)
$$
vanishes for every sum of functions of separate factors. Here $\Delta b_R=1$. Take $\epsilon=1$ unless $\Delta(P-\gamma)=-1$, in which case take $\epsilon=2$. Then $\Delta u\ne0$, so $u$ is genuinely nonseparable. All four evaluation points are rational and lie in $\rho\leq2R$, and the chosen $\epsilon$ remains rational. This completes the new negative direction for every rational reference in the theorem.

## 5. Reconstruction of the positive direction

It remains to establish the positive package independently on one rank-one star times a complete affine space. Let $G$ have $r\geq2$ primitive rays $v_i$, relation $w>0$, weights $cw_i$, and $\dim V=r-1$. Let $X=G\times E$, $d=m+1$, with any positive constant affine weight absorbed into $c$. The closed cells are $C_i=\mathbb R_{\geq0}v_i\times E$, with common face $S=\{0\}\times E$. The reference $\gamma$ may be real PA and positive at infinity, and $u$ may be any bounded real PA function on a finite rational subdivision. Put $f=\gamma+u$.

### Global rational Jensen and ambient majorants

Every raw PC function $h$ satisfies the usual Jensen inequality for all rational-coefficient combinations of rational points of $X$ whose centre is rational and belongs to $X$. For a centre in $S$, the endpoints lie in cells incident to $S$, so this is already a PCC. For a centre $x=(tv_a,z)$, $t>0$ rational, assign endpoints with zero graph coordinate to cell $a$. Write each endpoint as $(t_jv_{i(j)},z_j)$ and its coefficient as $a_j\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}$. Set $T_i=\sum_{i(j)=i}a_jt_j$. The graph-coordinate equation and the rank-one kernel give
$$
T_i=s w_i+t\delta_{ia},\qquad s\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}.
\tag{16}
$$
Nonnegativity of $s$ follows from an index other than $a$. If $s=0$, all positive-coefficient endpoints lie in $C_a$ and the inequality is a cellwise PCC. If $s>0$, set $b_i=sw_i/T_i$. These equal one off $a$ and are strictly between zero and one at $a$. Split endpoint coefficients as $b_{i(j)}a_j$ and $(1-b_{i(j)})a_j$. The first set has total $B\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1)$ and graph-coordinate moment zero. Its normalized centre $y\in S$ is rational. A PCC at $y$ bounds $Bh(y)$ by the first average. The residual endpoints and $y$ all lie in $C_a$, and their PCC with centre $x$ gives the desired Jensen inequality after substitution. Both totals are nonzero since $s>0$ and $t>0$.

Every rational ambient point has a rational-coefficient barycentric representation by rational points of $X$: express its graph coordinate as $\sum_i a_iv_i$ with rational $a_i$, add a large rational multiple of $w$ to make all $a_i\geq0$, and use equal weights $1/r$ on $(ra_iv_i,z)$. Define $H_{\mathbb Q}(y)$ to be the infimum of the $h$-averages over all such representations of rational ambient $y$. It is finite above. A fixed representation of $-y$ with average $A_{-y}$, combined with any representation of $y$, has centre zero; the preceding Jensen inequality gives the finite lower bound $2h(0)-A_{-y}$. Concatenating representations proves rational convexity of $H_{\mathbb Q}$, and Jensen plus the one-point representation gives $H_{\mathbb Q}=h$ at rational points of $X$.

For completeness, rational convexity provides a locally Lipschitz extension. On a rational box, vertex values give an upper bound. On a smaller symmetric box about a rational centre $a$, reflection gives a lower bound $2H_{\mathbb Q}(a)-M$. Thus there are finite bounds $L,M$ on the rational points of a ball of radius $R_0$. For rational $y,z$ in its concentric radius-$R_0/4$ ball, with $0<\delta=\|z-y\|\leq R_0/4$, choose rational $T\geq1$ between $R_0/(4\delta)$ and $R_0/(2\delta)$. The rational point $p=y+T(z-y)$ stays in the larger ball, and convexity gives
$$
H_{\mathbb Q}(z)-H_{\mathbb Q}(y)\leq(M-L)/T\leq4(M-L)\delta/R_0.
$$
Reverse $y,z$ for the other bound; if $\delta>R_0/4$, the bounds alone suffice. Completion gives a finite continuous ambient convex function $H_h$. At any real point in a cell or in $S$, shrinking rational simplices in that same face, with real barycentric coefficients, show $h\leq H_h|_X$. If $h$ is continuous, equality follows by density. If $h\leq f$ and $h\leq\gamma+A$, continuity of these two upper bounds gives $H_h|_X\leq f,\gamma+A$. Thus every raw competitor has an ambient continuous convex majorant preserving its obstacle and growth bounds. Applying this to $\gamma$ itself supplies a finite convex ambient extension $g$ of $\gamma$.

### Epigraph and rational spatial domains

Take $C\geq\|u\|_\infty$. The convex function $g-C$ lies below $f$ on $X$. On a common finite rational structure for $f,\gamma$, all cells are pointed: otherwise the opposite nonzero lineality directions contradict reference positivity at infinity. Write each spatial cell by Minkowski--Weyl as a finite rational convex hull plus a finite rational cone. Its obstacle epigraph is generated by the lifted points $(a,f(a))$, lifted rays $(b,f_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}(b))$, and the upward vertical ray. Let $\mathcal A,\mathcal R$ be their finite unions over all cells. Then
$$
K=\overline{\operatorname{conv}}\{(x,t):x\in X,\ t\geq f(x)\}
=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal A)+\operatorname{cone}(\mathcal R).
\tag{17}
$$
The expression on the right is closed by the stated finite-generation theorem and contains every original epigraph piece. Conversely, adding any selected recession ray to any convex combination of points can be obtained in the closure of the original convex hull by giving a far-out point on that ray a coefficient tending to zero while keeping coefficient times distance fixed. This proves both inclusions in (17).

The spatial projection is all $V\oplus E$, by the barycentric construction above, and $K\subset\operatorname{epi}(g-C)$. Its vertical fibres therefore have finite lower endpoints and define a finite convex polyhedral function $H$ with $\operatorname{epi}H=K$. It is the largest ambient finite convex function below $f$ on $X$, since the epigraph of any such function is closed and convex and contains $K$. The majorant construction proves, including for raw competitors,
$$
P_\gamma(u)=H|_X,\qquad \gamma-C\leq H|_X\leq f\leq\gamma+C.
\tag{18}
$$

Although generator heights in (17) can be real, their spatial coordinates are rational. Every lower graph face is the convex hull of a subset of the lifted point generators plus the cone of a subset of the lifted rays. Its spatial projection is consequently a rational polyhedron. Projection is injective on a graph face, and common graph faces project to faces because height is affine on either graph face. These projected faces form a finite rational complex of affine domains of $H$. Intersect with the original product structure to obtain a finite rational structure on $X$. This proves the required spatial rationality, rather than inferring it from real polyhedrality alone.

The restriction of ambient $H$ is PC, and the direct effectivity observation gives PAPC on every nonnegative local balanced cycle. The bounds (18) give the right growth. The functions $P+1/j$ decrease uniformly to $P$ and have identical divisors. Thus the envelope is in the actual operator domain and the real regularity requirement holds for every obstacle under discussion.

### Two recursive vanishing statements

If a local balanced $d$-cycle $Z$ carries a PA function $a$ which, after subtraction of an ambient affine function, is the pullback of a conical PA function defined on an entire rational quotient vector space of dimension $q<d$, with a finite complete rational fan of affine domains, then $a^d\cdot Z=0$ in this germ. To prove this, let $T_j$ be the union of faces of codimension at least $j$ in that quotient fan. After one divisor the support lies in the inverse image of $T_1$, because the function is ambient affine away from its walls and balancing cancels its divisor there. Inductively, near the relative interior of a face of codimension $j-1$, the preceding cycle is supported in the inverse image of that face, where the function is ambient affine. Its next divisor vanishes by (D). Thus after $j$ divisors support lies in the inverse image of $T_j$. Since $T_{q+1}$ is empty, the asserted vanishing follows. Formula (D) and its refinement identity apply at each step. All applications below supply precisely such an everywhere-defined quotient function, namely the maximum of finitely many active linear forms.

If both a local rational fan cycle and its function-domain fan have a common nonzero rational lineality space $L$, their top zero-cycle vanishes. Compute on their common generalized fan retaining $L$: every face contains $L$, and there are no zero-dimensional faces. Successive divisor cycles stay on this fan, so after $d$ steps its zero-cycle is zero. Refinement compatibility precludes creating an atom by later subdivision. An ambient affine term can be subtracted before this argument by (D). These statements concern the whole local cycle, not only its top-face interiors.

### Noncontact implies zero mass

The top measure of $P=H|_X$ is finite positive atomic and supported at rational vertices of a common rational structure. Fix such a rational $x$ with $H(x)<f(x)$. Its graph point admits a representation from (17), retaining only positive coefficients:
$$
(x,H(x))=\sum_j a_j(y_j,f(y_j))+\sum_b t_b(r_b,s_b),
\quad a_j>0,\ \sum_j a_j=1,\ t_b>0.
\tag{19}
$$
Every active supporting affine plane of $H$ at $x$ has nonnegative slack on these generators. Its total slack in (19) is zero, so all selected points lie on that plane and all selected rays have zero slack. The upward vertical ray has positive slack and cannot be selected. The rational subspace
$$
L=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{y_j-x,\ r_b\}
\tag{20}
$$
is nonzero: if it were zero, all selected points would have spatial coordinate $x$, and all selected rays would be zero, contradicting $H(x)<f(x)$. All differences of active slopes annihilate $L$. Inactive planes stay below near $x$, so the function germ after subtraction of one active affine plane factors through the quotient by $L$.

This quotient has rational domains. Indeed the coarsest local active-domain cones are convex unions of the rational cones from the domain structure proved above, so are generated by finitely many rational rays. They contain $L$ as lineality since all active slope differences annihilate $L$. Their quotient cones are rational, giving the rational quotient fan required in the vanishing statements.

Write $x=(tv_a,z)$, with $t\geq0$, choosing $a$ arbitrarily if $t=0$. Every selected spatial point or ray in (19) has graph part a nonnegative multiple of some $v_i$. Summing its weighted graph coordinates produces
$$
T_i=sw_i+t\delta_{ia},\qquad s\geq0.
\tag{21}
$$
There are three cases.

If $s>0$, the projection of $L$ onto $V$ is all $V$. To check this, let a linear form $\ell$ annihilate that projection and put $A=\ell(tv_a)$. A selected point with graph coordinate $t_jv_i$, $t_j>0$, gives $\ell(v_i)=A/t_j$; a selected ray on ray $i$ gives $\ell(v_i)=0$; a selected point with zero graph coordinate forces $A=0$. Every index appears in a selected point or ray with positive graph coordinate by (21). If $A\ne0$, all $\ell(v_i)$ have its sign or are zero, and at least one is nonzero since (19) has a point generator. This contradicts $\sum_iw_i\ell(v_i)=0$. Therefore $A=0$, all $\ell(v_i)=0$, and $\ell=0$. Hence $\dim L\geq r-1$, and the ambient quotient has dimension at most $((r-1)+m)-(r-1)=m<d$. The quotient vanishing statement gives zero top mass at $x$, also on $S$.

If $s=0,t>0$, all selected generators lie in $C_a$. Thus $L$ is a nonzero subspace of the $d$-dimensional tangent space $\mathbb Rv_a\oplus E$. Locally the carrier is an open complete affine piece with constant weight $cw_a$. Restrict the quotient germ to this tangent space; its dimension is at most $d-1$, so the same vanishing statement applies.

If $s=t=0$, all selected graph coordinates vanish and $0\ne L\subset E$. At $x\in S$ the local carrier is $G\times E$, with lineality containing $L$, and the function domains retain $L$ as just proved. Their common generalized fan therefore satisfies the lineality vanishing statement. If $m=0$ this case is impossible. These three cases exhaust (21).

Thus every noncontact potential atom has zero top coefficient. Positivity and finite atomicity imply
$$
\operatorname{supp}\mathrm{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(P)\subset\{P=f\},
$$
and the exact contact integral is zero for every bounded real PA obstacle. This reconstructs the positive theorem without using the earlier verdict or a separability assumption.

### Completion of the product criterion

A bivalent factor has opposite primitive generators in a rank-one lattice and equal weights, so it is the entire affine line with constant weight. If there is at most one branched factor, merge every bivalent factor with $E$ and multiply their constant weights. The carrier becomes one rank-one star times a complete affine space, as just proved. If there is no branched factor, it is complete affine space; in positive dimension represent one coordinate line as a bivalent star and apply the same proof. In dimension zero the envelope at the sole point is the obstacle value, and the contact identity is immediate with its positive zero-cycle weight. The negative direction for two or more branched factors is Section 4. This proves the theorem exactly as stated.

## Comparison, limitations and self-audit boundary

The controlling Chapter 72 and its included proofs already settle one-dimensional real orthogonality using local relation cones, complete affine real orthogonality, and separable product envelopes. The one-star-times-affine draft gives the positive nonseparable cylinder range. The contribution developed here is the all-rational-reference negative direction for any number of rank-one star factors once two are branched, its full recursive mass formula, and the resulting criterion on this product class. The previous Bergman obstruction and its positive-growth repair are not counted as progress. No assumption of top-cycle uniqueness, balancing, positive total mass or positive growth has been used as a contact theorem.

The negative all-reference quantifier is **rational**, not arbitrary real. The operation $\max(\lambda Q,\gamma-M)$ can introduce an irrational spatial wall for a real-coefficient $\gamma$. In one dimension rational chordal interpolation can repair this; here such interpolation has not been supplied with all local PCC inequalities, exact trap germ and growth preserved. Hence the converse for all real references is unresolved. The positive direction remains valid for real references because its epigraph proof establishes rational projected domains directly.

No literal all-obstacle classification is claimed. Even (15) with a literal $\gamma$ generally has nonintegral rational slopes.
