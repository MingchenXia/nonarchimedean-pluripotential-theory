257: Definition 2.1 (Rational polyhedron). A rational polyhedron in NR is a subset σ ⊆ NR defined
258: by finitely many affine integral equalities and inequalities, i.e.,
259:                            
260:                       σ = n ∈ NR | f1 (n) + b1 = . . . = fr (n) + br = 0,
261:                                       fr+1 (n) + br+1 ≥ 0, . . . , fN (n) + bN ≥ 0
262: for some linear forms f1 , . . . , fN ∈ M and bi ∈ Z.
263:     We define
264:       • Nσ,R the linear space spanned by all x − y for x, y ∈ σ;
265:       • Nσ = Nσ,R ∩ N the induced sublattice, and Mσ its dual lattice;
266:       • Vσ,R = x + Nσ,R , x ∈ σ, the smallest affine subspace of NR containing σ;
267:       • the relative interior of σ, denoted by relint(σ) is the interior of σ as a subset of Vσ,R ;
268:       • the dimension of σ as the dimension of Nσ,R .
269: Definition 2.2 (Rational polyhedral complex). A rational polyhedral complex is a finite
270: collection Π of rational polyhedra satisfying two conditions:
271:       • if σ is in Π, then so is any face of σ;
272:       • if σ and σ ′ are in Π, then σ ∩ σ ′ is either empty or a face of both σ and σ ′ .
273:    The polyhedra of Π are called faces and we write τ ≺ σ whenever τ is a face of σ. We denote
274: the set of all k-dimensional faces of Π by Π(k) . The union of all faces in Π is denoted |Π| ⊆ NR
275: and called the support of Π.
276:   The dimension of Π is defined as the maximum of the dimensions of the faces of Π, and Π is
277: pure dimensional if every inclusion-maximal face in Π has the same dimension.
278: Definition 2.3 (Refinement). A refinement Π′ of a rational polyhedral complex Π is a rational
279: polyhedral complex such that
280:       • |Π′ | = |Π|
281:       • for every σ ′ ∈ Π′ , there exists σ ∈ Π such that σ ′ ⊆ σ;
282: we write Π′ ≥ Π. Two rational polyhedral complexes are equivalent if they admit a common
283: refinement.
284: Definition 2.4 (Rational polyhedral space).
285:       • Let X ⊆ NR be a topological space. A rational polyhedral complex Π is a rational polyhedral
286:         structure on X if |Π| = X.
287:       • A polyhedral space X ⊆ NR is a topological space together with an equivalence class of
288:         rational polyhedral structures on X.
289:       • A polyhedral space X is of dimension d (respectively of pure dimension d) if some (and
290:         hence any) representative of the equivalence class of rational polyhedral structures on X has
291:         dimension d (respectively pure dimension d).

206: ## The two-branched-star trap and its full mass
207: 
208: Relabel two branched stars as $G_1,G_2$. Write their relations $w_i,w'_j$, weights $\alpha_i=c_1w_i$, $\beta_j=c_2w'_j$. Partition each set of rays into three nonempty groups. Put $W_g=\sum_{i\in g}w_i$, $W'_g=\sum_{j\in g}w'_j$, and
209: $$
210: u_1=(1,0),\quad u_2=(0,1),\quad u_3=(-1,-1).
211: $$
212: The assignment $L_0(v_i)=u_g/W_g$ annihilates the unique relation and defines a rational linear map. Likewise define $L'_0$. Choose one positive integer $D$ clearing both matrices in lattice bases, and set $L=DL_0$, $L'=DL'_0$. Then
213: $$
214: L(v_i)=k_iu_g,\quad k_i=D/W_g\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad
215: L'(v'_j)=\ell_ju_g,\quad\ell_j=D/W'_g\in\mathbb Z_{>0},
216: $$
217: where scalar integrality follows from integrality of the maps and primitivity of $u_g$. Each group satisfies
218: $$
219: \sum_{i\in g}\alpha_ik_i=C=c_1D,\qquad
220: \sum_{j\in g}\beta_j\ell_j=C'=c_2D.
221: \tag{G1}
222: $$
223: 
224: On $G_1\times G_2$, set $F_1=(La)_1-(L'b)_1$, $F_2=(La)_2-(L'b)_2$, $A=|F_1|$, $B=|F_2|$, $q=A+B$. These restrict ambient rational convex homogeneous functions. In quadrant $(a,b)=(tv_i,sv'_j)$, $F_1=p_it-p'_js$, where $p_i=k_i,0,-k_i$ according to groups 1,2,3. Interior bends of $A$ occur precisely when both rays belong to group 1 or both to group 3. Put $g_{ij}=\gcd(k_i,\ell_j)$ and
225: $$
226: h_{ij}=(\ell_jv_i/g_{ij},\ k_iv'_j/g_{ij}).
227: $$
228: The saturated quadrant tangent lattice is $\mathbb Z(v_i,0)\oplus\mathbb Z(0,v'_j)$. The displayed diagonal vector is primitive there and in the ambient direct lattice. The primitive quotient covector is $(k_i/g_{ij},-\ell_j/g_{ij})$. Bezout supplies a primitive inward normal lift on which $k_it-\ell_js$ has value $g_{ij}$; opposite normals and the absolute-value bend give weight $2g_{ij}\alpha_i\beta_j$.
229: 
230: On a nonzero second-factor axis in group 2 the outgoing normal derivatives give $\beta_j\sum_i\alpha_i|p_i|=2C\beta_j$. On group 1 or 3 axes, the sign is fixed and the derivative sum is $\pm\beta_j\sum_i\alpha_ip_i=0$. First-factor axes give $2C'\alpha_i$ in group 2 and zero otherwise. Other quadrant interiors are affine. This includes every first-divisor face:
231: $$
232: \begin{split}
233: A\cdot[G_1\times G_2]={}&
234: \sum_{g=1,3}\sum_{i,j\in g}2g_{ij}\alpha_i\beta_j[h_{ij}]\\
235: &+\sum_{i\in2}2C'\alpha_i[(v_i,0)]
236: +\sum_{j\in2}2C\beta_j[(0,v'_j)].
237: \end{split}
238: \tag{D1}
239: $$
240: Here $[h]$ is the ray of primitive generator $h$. The first projections of its diagonal weighted vectors sum to $2C'\sum_{i\in1,3}\alpha_iv_i$, and the axis terms complete this to zero; the second projections similarly complete $2C\sum_{j\in1,3}\beta_jv'_j$ to zero. Thus balancing is also checked directly.
241: 
242: The function $A$ vanishes on its entire divisor. The slopes of $B$ on its diagonals are zero, on its first axes $k_i$, and on its second axes $\ell_j$. Therefore
243: $$
244: A^2\cdot[G_1\times G_2]=0,\qquad
245: B\cdot A\cdot[G_1\times G_2]=4CC'\delta_0.
246: $$
247: Coordinate exchange gives $B^2=0$. Bilinearity and the two mixed orders give
248: $$
249: q^2\cdot[G_1\times G_2]=8CC'\delta_0.
250: \tag{D2}
251: $$
252: This is a direct divisor proof, not a pushforward by the nonproper difference map.
253: 
254: There is an actual positive balanced **zero curve** for $q$: take all $h_{ij}$ with both rays in the same group, now including group 2, and ray weights
255: $$
256: \theta_{ij}=\alpha_i\beta_jg_{ij}.
257: \tag{Z}
258: $$
259: Its first projection sums to $C'\sum_i\alpha_iv_i=0$ and its second to $C\sum_j\beta_jv'_j=0$. Thus $\sum\theta_{ij}h_{ij}=0$. Its weights have the common real factor $c_1c_2$ times rational numbers. The function $q$ is zero on all its rays. The curve will only manufacture an actual PCC.

758: ## Comparison, limitations and self-audit boundary
759: 
760: The controlling Chapter 72 and its included proofs already settle one-dimensional real orthogonality using local relation cones, complete affine real orthogonality, and separable product envelopes. The one-star-times-affine draft gives the positive nonseparable cylinder range. The contribution developed here is the all-rational-reference negative direction for any number of rank-one star factors once two are branched, its full recursive mass formula, and the resulting criterion on this product class. The previous Bergman obstruction and its positive-growth repair are not counted as progress. No assumption of top-cycle uniqueness, balancing, positive total mass or positive growth has been used as a contact theorem.
761: 
762: The negative all-reference quantifier is **rational**, not arbitrary real. The operation $\max(\lambda Q,\gamma-M)$ can introduce an irrational spatial wall for a real-coefficient $\gamma$. In one dimension rational chordal interpolation can repair this; here such interpolation has not been supplied with all local PCC inequalities, exact trap germ and growth preserved. Hence the converse for all real references is unresolved. The positive direction remains valid for real references because its epigraph proof establishes rational projected domains directly.

The explicit example (5)--(6) does give a particular literal PA envelope defect, but it does not imply literal regularity.
