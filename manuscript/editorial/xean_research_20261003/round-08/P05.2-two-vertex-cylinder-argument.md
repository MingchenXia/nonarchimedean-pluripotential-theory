## Exact result and scope

The original classification P05.2 remains open. The following is a new, nonconical special-case theorem claimed in this unverified draft. It includes every reference and obstacle specified in the assignment, without a separability assumption. No independent verification, formal verification, or priority claim is made.

Put $A=(0,0)$, $B=(1,0)$ in the actual lattice $\mathbb Z^2$. Let $G$ consist of $[A,B]$, the rays from $A$ in directions $(0,1),(-1,-1)$, and the rays from $B$ in directions $(0,-1),(1,1)$. Give all five edges weight one. For $m\geq0$ let
$$
X=G\times\mathbb R^m,\qquad N=\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z^m,
\qquad d=m+1,
$$
with product weight one. In particular the requested cylinder is $m=1$.

**Theorem.** For every continuous $\gamma\in\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}(X)\cap\mathrm{PC}(X)$ positive in every nonzero recession direction of every affine reference cell, and every bounded continuous $u\in\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}(X)$, the raw envelope $P=P_\gamma(u)$ is itself in $\mathrm{PAPC}_{\mathbb R}(X,\gamma)$ on a finite rational spatial complex. Consequently it is real-regularizable, and, for the full recursive intersection operator,
$$
\operatorname{supp}\bigl(P^d\cdot[X]\bigr)\subset\{P=\gamma+u\},
\qquad
\int_X(P-\gamma-u)\,\mathrm{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(P)=0.
\tag{T}
$$
The operator is $P^d\cdot[X]$, with primitive normals in saturated tangent lattices; its factorial is retained. For $m=1$ it is the full two-fold divisor. Both $\gamma$ and $u$ may be genuinely nonseparable and have arbitrary real affine coefficients on finite rational spatial subdivisions.

This theorem does not cover arbitrary finite rank-one graphs, cycles, general singular fans, or the literal integral-slope category. It therefore does not solve the original atom. The accepted note n100 concerns stars and is not used as a theorem premise below. The prior all-real-reference product draft was read in full, together with its author audit and research notes, as fallible material. Its finite support/decomposition arguments are not imported. No review result for old or changed bytes is used.

## Definitions and exact primary inputs

BMPS means Botero--Mazzon--Pille-Schneider, *Monge--Ampère measures on balanced polyhedral spaces*, arXiv:2603.08664v1, submitted 9 March 2026, manuscript dated 10 March 2026. The cached PDF and extracted text are retained as `sources/BMPS2603.08664v1.pdf` and `.txt`.

Our $\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}$ consists of continuous functions affine with real coefficients on a **finite rational spatial complex**. It is distinct from Definition 2.28's integral linear parts and rational constants. Definitions 3.2--3.3 use PCCs
$$
x=\sum_j a_jx_j,\quad a_j\geq0,\quad\sum_j a_j=1,
\quad x_j\in X\cap N_{\mathbb Q},
$$
with real coefficients and cells $\tau\preceq\sigma_j$ on some rational structure, $x\in\tau$, $x_j\in\sigma_j$. A finite raw PC function satisfies the corresponding Jensen inequality for every such PCC. We do not assume that a raw competitor is continuous.

Definitions 2.18--2.20 take $N_\sigma=N\cap\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(\sigma-\sigma)$, and normals $n_{\sigma/\tau}$ primitively generating $N_\sigma/N_\tau$, directed into $\sigma$. Source cycles have rational weights; here weights are explicitly extended to real scalars. Balancing is $\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}z(\sigma)n_{\sigma/\tau}\in N_{\tau,\mathbb R}$. Generalized fans with lineality are allowed by Definition 2.22.

The essential external algebra result is precisely BMPS Definition 2.31, Remark 2.33 and Lemmas 2.34--2.35: for a literal PA function $a$ and rational balanced cycle $z$, the divisor is
$$
(a\cdot z)(\tau)=\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}z(\sigma)a_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}(n_{\sigma/\tau})
-a_{\tau,\mathrm{lin}}\left(\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}z(\sigma)n_{\sigma/\tau}\right).
\tag{D}
$$
It is lift independent, balanced, compatible with rational refinement, linear, and commuting under successive divisors. The inspected source proof establishes lift independence by tangential cancellation, refinement compatibility by opposite normals or inherited normals, and balancing/commutativity by quotient local fans and Allermann--Rau Proposition 3.7. We use the stated BMPS lemma as an external theorem, not its catalogue description. Its applicability to real coefficients follows as follows: on a fixed rational complex, continuity equations for affine pieces and balancing equations for cycles are rational linear equations. Rational solutions are dense in their real solution spaces. Rational affine pieces become literal after clearing denominators; rational cycle vectors can likewise be cleared of denominators before applying the source identities and then rescaled. Formula (D) and its iterates are polynomial in these finite coefficient vectors. All the listed identities therefore pass to real coefficients and weights. This argument also applies to finite rational local generalized fans. No nonrational subdivision is introduced.

Definition 3.1 tests PAPC against all nonnegative local balanced cycles of every positive dimension on ordinary open sets. Effectivity at this real scope is proved below. Definition 4.4 defines the PA measure as the finite atomic zero-cycle $a^d\cdot[X]$, not an Alexandrov measure divided by $d!$. Proposition 3.22 and Definition 3.23 motivate the explicit real regularizable class used here: uniform decreasing PAPC approximation with bounded reference difference. For an already PAPC $P$, the sequence $P+1/j$ supplies this approximation, with identical divisors. We need no extension theorem for a non-PA envelope.

Definition 6.2 gives exactly
$$
P_\gamma(u)(x)=\sup\{h(x):h\in\mathrm{PC}(X),\ h\leq\gamma+u,
\ h\leq\gamma+C_h\text{ for some }C_h\in\mathbb R\}.
\tag{E}
$$
Definition 4.15 is the recession positivity hypothesis in the theorem. We use no source continuity theorem for raw PC functions, no uniform density theorem, no analytic realization, no projection formula, and no total-mass-to-contact implication.

## Elementary convex facts used in the proof

We record proofs to specify the finite-dimensional inputs, including rationality.

**Finite generation and closedness.** A rational polyhedral cone has finitely many rational generators, including both signs of a rational basis of its lineality. A nonempty pointed rational polyhedron has the form $\operatorname{conv}(A_0)+\operatorname{cone}(R_0)$ with finite rational sets and $A_0\ne\varnothing$. A finite convex hull plus a finitely generated cone of real vectors is a closed polyhedron.

For a pointed cone written $\ell_i\geq0$, the sum $\ell=\sum_i\ell_i$ is positive on its nonzero vectors: otherwise all $\ell_i$ vanish and give lineality. The section $\ell=1$ is bounded, since an unbounded sequence, normalized on the unit sphere, would limit to a nonzero cone vector with $\ell=0$. This bounded polyhedron is the convex hull of its vertices: a point in a positive-dimensional minimal face lies between terminal points in smaller faces, and induction reduces to vertices. Vertices are finitely many solutions of full-rank active equations and are rational. Quotienting a general cone by its rational lineality and lifting a rational basis proves the first claim. Homogenize a pointed polyhedron to $\{(y,t):Ay\geq bt,t\geq0\}$; its generators with $t>0$ supply points and those with $t=0$ supply recession rays. Finally eliminate variables in any finite nonstrict linear system by comparing every lower bound with every upper bound. One-sided bounds impose no further restriction. This exact elimination proves that projections are closed polyhedra; rational data stay rational. Apply it to $x=\sum a_ip_i+\sum t_jr_j$, $a_i,t_j\geq0$, $\sum a_i=1$ for the closedness assertion, also when generator heights are real.

**Rational convex completion and subgradients.** A finite rationally convex function on $\mathbb Q^m$ has a unique finite continuous convex extension to $\mathbb R^m$. On a rational box, its vertex values bound it above. Reflection about a rational centre bounds it below on a smaller box. If $L\leq F\leq M$ on a rational ball of radius $R$, then for rational $y,z$ in a concentric ball of radius $R/4$, extend the segment to $y+T(z-y)$ with rational $R/(4\|z-y\|)\leq T\leq R/(2\|z-y\|)$. Convexity bounds the difference by $4(M-L)\|z-y\|/R$; reverse the points for the opposite bound. Large differences are controlled by $M-L$. Thus completion is locally Lipschitz and convex, and is unique by density. The same proof makes every finite everywhere-defined convex function continuous.

Such a function has a supporting affine plane at every point. Here is a finite-dimensional proof. Its epigraph is closed and convex. Separate the point $(x,F(x)-\varepsilon)$ by the normal obtained from its nearest point in the epigraph. The normal's height coefficient is positive: it is nonnegative because of the upward ray, and if zero its horizontal form would bound all of $\mathbb R^m$, an impossibility for a nonzero normal. Normalizing gives an affine minorant $l_\varepsilon$ with $F(x)-\varepsilon<l_\varepsilon(x)\leq F(x)$. Evaluations at $x\pm Re_i$ bound its slopes uniformly for $0<\varepsilon\leq1$. A convergent subsequence as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives the desired supporting plane. Existence of the nearest point follows by restricting the distance minimization to a large compact ball; the normal separation inequality follows by differentiating distance squared on segments. The case $m=0$ is immediate.

These arguments supply the ordinary convex facts below, rather than relying on an unstated ambient extension theorem.

## A rational lift that preserves the actual PCCs and lattices

Use a new lattice $\widetilde N_G=\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zb\oplus\mathbb Zd$ and put
$$
c=-a-b,\qquad e=a-d.
$$
The lifted graph $\widetilde G$ has vertices $0,a$, edge $[0,a]$, rays $\mathbb R_{\geq0}b,\mathbb R_{\geq0}c$ from zero, and $a+\mathbb R_{\geq0}d,a+\mathbb R_{\geq0}e$. Its leaves satisfy the unique relation $b+c+d+e=0$ and span three dimensions. Define $\iota:G\to\widetilde G$ by preserving the parameter on each of these five edges, with the corresponding endpoints. Let $\widetilde X=\widetilde G\times\mathbb R^m$.

Every convex polyhedron in either cylinder is contained in one closed edge strip or a vertex fibre. Indeed its graph projection is a convex subset of the graph. A segment in this graph cannot pass through a trivalent vertex and continue onto another edge, since no two outgoing directions there are opposite. Thus a convex graph subset lies on a single edge. This also proves the assertion for lower-dimensional cells and arbitrary spatial subdivisions.

At the first vertex, the lift on all three incident strips is the restriction of the linear isomorphism sending the original basis $(1,0),(0,1)$ to $a,b$. At the second vertex it is the affine map sending $B$ to $a$ and the original basis $(1,0),(0,-1)$ to $a,d$. On the common edge the maps agree. They are lattice isomorphisms onto the saturated planes $\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zb$ and $\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zd$, respectively; the affine factor is unchanged.

Consequently finite rational structures transfer in both directions. At a PCC centre in a vertex fibre all endpoint cells belong to these incident strips, so the single local affine map preserves the barycentre, rational endpoints, and incidences. At an edge-interior centre they all belong to that edge strip, with the same property. The inverse maps do the same. Hence **all actual PCCs**, even on a different rational structure, are preserved, and raw PC classes are equivalent under $\iota$.

The same local lattice maps identify every saturated cell tangent lattice and primitive quotient normal. Formula (D) is unchanged in each germ, including all subdivision and lower faces. All weights remain one. Iterating (D) identifies the full $d$-fold zero-cycles under the lift. This is a local isomorphism argument, not a pushforward along a nonproper map.

## Scalar supports on the lifted two-vertex tree

**Lemma 1.** Suppose $k$ is a finite function on $\widetilde G\cap\widetilde N_{G,\mathbb Q}$ satisfying rational-coefficient PCC inequalities. It has a continuous extension along every edge, finite one-sided derivatives at both vertices, and a global ambient affine support touching it at every graph point.

Write $k_0$ for the edge function on $[0,1]$, $p_0=k_0'(0+)$, $p_1=k_0'(1-)$, and $m_b,m_c,m_d,m_e$ for the outgoing ray derivatives at their vertices. Then
$$
p_0\leq p_1,\qquad p_0+m_b+m_c\geq0,
\qquad -p_1+m_d+m_e\geq0.
\tag{S}
$$

**Proof.** Rational Jensen on each interval gives its finite convex continuous completion in the interior. Its limit at an endpoint is finite and no larger than the given endpoint value: the upper bound follows by a secant from the endpoint, and an interior supporting secant gives a finite lower bound near it. Equal small rational parameters on the three incident edges form an equal-weight PCC at a vertex. The vertex value is at most the average of the three limiting values, each of which is at most that vertex value. Thus all three limits equal it. Each outgoing derivative is bounded above by a finite secant. The same equal-parameter inequality, divided by the parameter, precludes any derivative being $-\infty$ and proves their sum nonnegative. This gives (S), with all six derivatives finite.

At an edge point $ra$, choose a supporting slope $s$ for $k_0$, so $p_0\leq s\leq p_1$, and anchor the affine form there. Choose its leaf slopes by
$$
p(a)=s,\quad p(b)=m_b,\quad p(c)=-s-m_b\leq m_c,
\quad p(d)=m_d,\quad p(e)=s-m_d\leq m_e.
$$
These values satisfy both vector relations and specify an ambient form. The intercepts at both vertices lie below $k$ there. Each leaf restriction then lies below its convex ray function, because its slope is at most the outgoing derivative.

For a point $tb$ on the first leaf choose $\beta\in\partial k_b(t)$, set $p(b)=\beta$, $p(a)=p_0$, $p(c)=-p_0-\beta$, and choose $p(d)=m_d,p(e)=p_0-m_d$. Since $\beta\geq m_b$, (S) gives the required upper bounds on the other leaf slopes. Anchoring at $tb$ gives an intercept at zero at most $k(0)$. The edge with slope $p_0$ lies below $k_0$, and its intercept at $a$ is at most $k(a)$. All leaves are bounded as above. Interchange $b,c$ for the other first leaf. For a second leaf use $p(a)=p_1$, anchor at $a$, and interchange the vertex roles; the endpoint support with slope $p_1$ bounds the edge, and (S) bounds the first leaves. Vertices are included by endpoint edge supports. This proves global touching affine supports. $\square$

## Global convex majorants of every raw competitor

**Lemma 2.** For any finite raw PC function $h$ on $\widetilde X$, there is a finite continuous ambient convex $H_h$ on $\mathbb R^3\oplus\mathbb R^m$ which equals $h$ at every rational carrier point and majorizes $h$ everywhere on the carrier. Every continuous upper bound of $h$ on the carrier is also an upper bound of $H_h|_{\widetilde X}$. In particular continuous PC functions extend exactly.

**Proof.** Fix a rational graph point $q$. The values $h(q,z)$ on $z\in\mathbb Q^m$ are rationally convex, using a same-fibre PCC. Let $F_q$ be their finite convex completion. For any $p\in(\mathbb R^m)^*$ define
$$
k_p(q)=\inf_{z\in\mathbb Q^m}\{h(q,z)-p(z)\}\in[-\infty,\infty).
\tag{L1}
$$
It satisfies rational graph PCC inequalities, with $-\infty$ allowed: for finitely many rational graph endpoints choose rational $z_j$ approximating their infima. The rational barycentre $(q,\sum a_jz_j)$ is an actual cylinder PCC, and subtracting $p$ and taking infima proves the inequality. If an endpoint infimum is $-\infty$, let that one go to $-\infty$ while keeping the other choices finite.

If $k_p$ is finite at one rational graph point, it is finite at every rational graph point. At an edge-interior centre, any other point of the edge is an endpoint of a rational same-edge combination with that centre (choose the other endpoint on the other side), so a $-\infty$ value would force the centre value $-\infty$. Thus the vertices of that edge have finite values. At a finite vertex, any rational point on an incident edge can be put in a vertex PCC with endpoints on the other two edges: choose positive rational lengths on them, within the bounded edge if necessary, and weights proportional to the reciprocal lengths. The three weighted directions cancel. A $-\infty$ endpoint is impossible. Propagation across the connected graph proves the claim. This explicitly controls arbitrarily distant endpoints.

For a rational $(q,z)$, take a subgradient $p$ of $F_q$ at $z$. Then $k_p(q)=h(q,z)-p(z)$ is finite, so all $k_p$ are finite on the graph and Lemma 1 gives an ambient affine support $l$ of $k_p$ touching at $q$. The affine function $l(y)+p(z')$ is below $h(y,z')$ at every rational carrier point and touches at $(q,z)$.

Take the supremum of these affine functions over all rational $(q,z)$ and their choices of supports. The resulting $H_h$ is finite everywhere. To see the upper bound, note that the convex hull of rational carrier points is the entire ambient space. Indeed $b,c,d,e$ span three dimensions and have positive relation $(1,1,1,1)$. The tetrahedra with vertices $Tb,Tc,a+Td,a+Te$ exhaust the space as the positive integer $T$ grows: after division by $T$ they converge to the tetrahedron of $b,c,d,e$, with zero strictly interior. Taking their products with rational boxes in the affine factor gives convex hulls of finitely many rational carrier points containing any prescribed ambient point. Every affine function in the supremum is bounded by the corresponding convex average of the finitely many $h$ values there. A single support gives a finite lower bound. Thus $H_h$ is finite convex, hence continuous, and equals $h$ at rational carrier points.

At an arbitrary real carrier point take shrinking simplices with rational vertices in its minimal face of a rational structure. They exist because that face has rational affine hull and the point is in its relative interior; a zero-dimensional face is already rational. Their real barycentric coefficients form same-cell PCCs. The PC inequality and continuity of $H_h$ give $h\leq H_h$ by taking the limit. Finally any continuous bound valid on rational points extends to $H_h$ on all carrier points by density. For continuous $h$ this gives equality. $\square$

This proves the ambient extension needed on the **lifted** carrier; it makes no ambient extension assertion in the original plane.

## Every envelope is PA on finite rational domains

Transport $\gamma,u$ to $\widetilde X$, put $f=\gamma+u$, and choose $C\geq\|u\|_\infty$. Lemma 2 gives a finite continuous ambient convex extension $g$ of $\gamma$.

On a common finite rational structure of $\gamma$ and $f$, every spatial cell is pointed: a nonzero lineality vector and its negative would have opposite affine reference slopes, contradicting strict recession positivity. Write each cell as a finite rational convex hull plus a finite rational recession cone, using the elementary finite-generation result. Its obstacle epigraph is generated by its finitely many lifted point generators $(y,f(y))$, lifted recession generators $(r,f_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}(r))$, and the upward vertical ray. Let $\mathcal A,\mathcal R$ be the unions of these finite sets. They have rational spatial coordinates, though their heights can be arbitrary real numbers. Then
$$
K=\overline{\operatorname{conv}}\{(x,t):x\in\widetilde X,t\geq f(x)\}
=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal A)+\operatorname{cone}(\mathcal R).
\tag{K}
$$
The right side is closed and contains all epigraph pieces. Conversely, adding a multiple of any recession generator to a point of the convex hull is obtained as a limit: combine weight $\varepsilon$ on a far point of that epigraph piece with weight $1-\varepsilon$ on the given convex combination, keeping weight times distance fixed. Iterate for finitely many recession generators and take closure. This proves (K).

The projection of $K$ is the whole ambient space by the preceding tetrahedron construction. Since $K\subset\operatorname{epi}(g-C)$, its fibres are bounded below. They are closed upward rays. Thus $K=\operatorname{epi}(H)$ for a finite convex polyhedral function $H$, the greatest ambient convex minorant of $f$ on the carrier. In particular
$$
\gamma-C\leq H|_{\widetilde X}\leq f\leq\gamma+C.
\tag{B}
$$
Every raw competitor has the ambient majorant of Lemma 2, preserving both continuous bounds $f$ and $\gamma+C_h$. The greatest-minorant property gives $h\leq H|_{\widetilde X}$. Conversely $H|_{\widetilde X}$ is PC by ambient Jensen and is a competitor by (B). Hence the raw envelope is exactly $H|_{\widetilde X}$.

We must prove **rational spatial domains**, not infer them from real polyhedrality. A lower graph face of $K$ is precisely the convex hull of the subset of $\mathcal A$ on its supporting graph plane plus the cone of the subset of $\mathcal R$ on that plane. Indeed all slacks are nonnegative, and any positive-coefficient generator in a point of the face has zero slack. Its spatial projection is consequently a rational polyhedron. Projection is an affine isomorphism on a graph face; intersections of graph faces project to intersections of faces because the graph height is unique. These projections form a finite rational complex covering the ambient space. Equivalently the finite inequality description of $K$, with its full spatial projection and upward fibres, expresses $H$ as a maximum of finitely many affine planes. Its lower faces cover its graph. Intersect the rational projected domains with the lifted strips and pull them back by $\iota$. This gives a finite rational structure for $P$ on the actual cylinder.

Effectivity against every nonnegative local real cycle follows directly from ambient convexity. At a tested face choose a subgradient of $H$ at a relative-interior point, subtract its affine form, and apply (D). The residual inward derivatives are nonnegative, while the residual tangent derivative vanishes; their weighted sum is nonnegative. This works in every positive cycle dimension, for every local open set, and passes back by the local lattice isomorphisms. Thus $P$ is real PAPC, and $P+1/j$ proves its uniform decreasing regularizability. Its measure is the PA recursive measure throughout.

## Two local recursive vanishing facts

The following facts apply to complete rational local function-domain fans and rational balanced cycles with lineality. They concern the whole cycle, not only top-face interiors.

**Quotient vanishing.** If, after subtraction of an ambient affine function, the germ of a PA function on a local $d$-cycle factors through a rational quotient of its ambient tangent space of dimension $q<d$, as a function defined on that entire quotient with a finite complete rational fan of domains, its $d$-fold divisor is zero. Let $T_j$ be the union of quotient fan faces of codimension at least $j$. After one divisor the support is in the inverse image of $T_1$: off the walls the function is ambient affine and (D) vanishes by balancing. Inductively, near a relative-interior point of a face of codimension $j-1$, the previous cycle is supported in that face's inverse image, where the function restricts to an affine function. Its next divisor vanishes there by (D). Thus after $j$ divisors support is in the inverse image of $T_j$. Since $T_{q+1}=\varnothing$ and $q+1\leq d$, the top divisor is zero. Rational refinement compatibility legitimizes each calculation.

**Lineality vanishing.** If the local carrier fan and the function-domain fan have a common nonzero rational lineality space $L$, compute on their common generalized fan retaining $L$. Every face contains $L$, so there are no zero-dimensional faces. All successive divisor cycles remain on this fan and the top zero-cycle vanishes. Subsequent subdivision cannot create an atom by refinement compatibility. An ambient affine term may first be subtracted by (D).

## Noncontact geometry and all lower-face contributions

The PA measure is positive finite atomic and its possible atoms are rational vertices of the common rational structure. Fix such a lifted point $x=(q,z)$ with $H(x)<f(x)$. A finite generator representation in (K), omitting zero coefficients, is
$$
(x,H(x))=\sum_j\alpha_j(y_j,f(y_j))+\sum_\nu t_\nu(r_\nu,s_\nu),
\quad \alpha_j>0,\quad\sum_j\alpha_j=1,\quad t_\nu>0.
\tag{F}
$$
Every active supporting graph plane at $x$ has nonnegative slack on all generators; total slack in (F) is zero. Thus every selected point and ray is on every active plane. The upward vertical ray cannot occur. The rational space
$$
L=\operatorname{span}\{y_j-x,r_\nu\}
\tag{Q}
$$
is nonzero: otherwise all point spatial coordinates equal $x$ and no nonzero selected ray can occur, forcing $H(x)=f(x)$. Differences of active slopes annihilate $L$. Inactive planes stay below in a small neighborhood. Consequently the germ after subtraction of one active affine plane is invariant under $L$.

Its active-domain fan is rational. The global domain complex was proved rational above; the coarsest active cones are convex unions of its finitely many rational local cones and therefore generated by finitely many rational rays. They contain $L$ as lineality. Intersecting with a rational tangent space and quotienting by its intersection with $L$ supplies exactly the complete rational quotient fans needed for the vanishing facts.

Here is the crucial global-to-local geometry. For selected point generators, write the graph coordinates in the five types
$$
t_b b,\quad t_c c,\quad a+t_d d,\quad a+t_e e,
\quad ra\ (0\leq r\leq1).
$$
Strictly positive leaf parameters identify the leaf types; vertices may be assigned to the bounded-edge type. Selected recession rays have graph coordinate a nonnegative multiple of one of $b,c,d,e$, or zero. Let $T_i$ denote the total leaf parameter of type $i$ in (F), including its point and recession coefficients. Let $M_B$ be the total point coefficient of the two second-vertex leaf types, and $R$ the weighted sum of the bounded-edge point parameters. Comparing coefficients in the basis $a,b,d$, for $q=q_a a+q_b b+q_d d$ gives
$$
T_b-T_c=q_b,\quad T_d-T_e=q_d,
\quad M_B+R-T_c+T_e=q_a.
\tag{M}
$$
All parameters and masses are nonnegative. This includes remote points and recession rays from the entire epigraph, not just incident cells.

At $q=0$, write $T_b=T_c=s$, $T_d=T_e=t$. Then $s=M_B+R+t$. If $s>0$, a selected generator exists on each of $b,c$. Their vectors in (Q) have independent graph projections $b,c$ and belong to
$$
W_A=\operatorname{span}(a,b)\oplus\mathbb R^m.
$$
Hence $\dim(L\cap W_A)\geq2$. The restriction of the germ to $W_A$ factors through a quotient of dimension at most $m<d$, so quotient vanishing applies to the full trivalent local cycle. If $s=0$, (M) forces $M_B=R=t=0$: all selected point graph coordinates are zero and all selected ray graph coordinates vanish. Thus $0\ne L\subset\mathbb R^m$, common lineality of both local fans, and lineality vanishing applies.

At $q=a$, the same equations give
$$
t=s+1-M_B-R,
\qquad 1-M_B-R\geq0.
$$
The second inequality is the sum of first-vertex leaf point coefficients and bounded-edge coefficients times $1-r$. If $t>0$, the selected $d,e$ generators give $\dim(L\cap W_B)\geq2$, with $W_B=\operatorname{span}(a,d)\oplus\mathbb R^m$, and quotient dimension at most $m$. If $t=0$, all points have graph coordinate $a$ and all recession graph coordinates vanish, so the nonzero space $L$ lies in the affine factor. The same two vanishing alternatives apply.

At an interior edge point $q=ra$, $0<r<1$, if $s=T_b=T_c>0$, take one selected generator of each first-vertex leaf type. Their graph projections in (Q) are
$$
u_b=t_b b-\delta_b ra,\qquad
u_c=t_c(-a-b)-\delta_c ra,
$$
where $\delta_i=1$ for a point and $0$ for a recession ray. The combination $t_cu_b+t_bu_c$ is a strictly negative multiple of $a$. Thus $L$ has a nonzero vector in the local tangent $W_0=\mathbb Ra\oplus\mathbb R^m$. If $t=T_d=T_e>0$, the analogous combination is
$$
[t_dt_e+(1-r)(t_e\delta_d+t_d\delta_e)]a,
$$
also nonzero. If $s=t=0$, all selected graph coordinates lie on the bounded edge or are zero recession directions, so $L\subset W_0$ and is nonzero. In every case the local $d$-dimensional affine strip germ factors through a quotient of dimension at most $d-1$.

Finally take $q=\tau b$, $\tau>0$, in a first leaf. Equations (M) give
$$
T_b=T_c+\tau,\qquad T_d=T_e=t,
\qquad T_c=M_B+R+t.
$$
If $T_c>0$, then $T_b>\tau$. There must be either a selected recession ray of type $b$, or a selected point of type $b$ with parameter greater than $\tau$; otherwise $T_b\leq\tau$ times the total point mass, at most $\tau$. Such a generator gives a nonzero vector of $L$ in $\mathbb Rb\oplus\mathbb R^m$, the local strip tangent. If $T_c=0$, all selected graph coordinates are on this leaf or at zero, so $L$ is contained in that tangent and is nonzero. Quotient vanishing applies. The other first leaf follows by interchanging $b,c$. The other two leaves follow by the affine lattice symmetry $y\mapsto a+Jy$, where $J(a)=-a,J(b)=d,J(d)=b$; it interchanges vertices and the two leaf pairs. This symmetry preserves all representations and inequalities just used.

These cases cover every point of the carrier. At every noncontact candidate atom the **full** local top divisor is zero. In particular vertex-fibre atoms, interior-face atoms, and all mixed/lower-face contributions have been controlled. Finite atomicity and positivity now prove the support inclusion in (T). Its integrand vanishes at every remaining atom, so its complete integral is zero. Pulling back through the primitive-normal lattice identifications proves the theorem on the actual requested carrier.

## Comparison, limitations, and a useful failed route

The book already classifies one-dimensional finite balanced graphs and proves complete affine contact; n100 already proves the star-times-affine all-real-reference result. The theorem here adds an actual bounded edge, both vertex fibres simultaneously, arbitrary nonseparable references/obstacles, global rational-domain envelopes, and a global contact argument. It uses neither a separable envelope formula nor the inference that local stars automatically glue.

## Result and exact scope

The original P05.2 classification remains open. This manuscript proves a necessary-and-sufficient criterion on an explicit product class, in the real obstacle category and with rational-coefficient references. The new direction is a nonseparable obstruction for **every** positive-growth rational reference whenever two factors are branched. For $1\leq l\leq k$, let $N_l$ be a lattice, $V_l=N_l\otimes\mathbb R$, and let $v_{li}\in N_l$, $1\leq i\leq r_l$, be primitive generators of distinct rays spanning $V_l$. Assume
$$

\ker\bigl(\mathbb R^{r_l}\longrightarrow V_l,\ (a_i)\mapsto\sum_i a_i v_{li}\bigr)
=\mathbb R w_l,\quad w_l\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^{r_l}.
$$
Set $G_l=\bigcup_i\mathbb R_{\geq0}v_{li}$ and give its $i$-th ray weight $\alpha_{li}=c_lw_{li}$, $c_l>0$ real. Let $E=N_E\otimes\mathbb R$ have dimension $m$ and constant weight $e>0$. Take
$$
X=G_1\times\cdots\times G_k\times E,
\quad N=\bigoplus_l N_l\oplus N_E,
\quad d=k+m,
$$
with product top weights $e\prod_l\alpha_{li_l}$. Empty products are permitted. These are embedded, pure-dimensional, finite rational balanced polyhedral spaces. All tangent lattices are saturated in $N$. A factor is **branched** when $r_l\geq3$; a factor with $r_l=2$ is a complete affine line. No saturation of the lattice generated collectively by the rays is required.

The class $\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb Q}$ means continuous functions with rational linear parts and rational affine constants on a finite rational spatial subdivision. This is distinct from literal $\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb Z}$, which requires integral tangent slopes. The class $\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}$ allows real coefficients on a finite rational spatial subdivision. The spatial rationality requirement is retained throughout.

**Theorem (product criterion, partial P05.2).** Let $\gamma\in\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb Q}(X)\cap\mathrm{PC}(X)$ be positive at infinity. Then the following package holds if and only if at most one factor $G_l$ is branched:

For every bounded $u\in\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}(X)$, the envelope $P_\gamma(u)$ belongs to $\mathrm{PC}_{\mathrm{reg},\mathbb R}(X,\gamma)$, and
$$
\int_X\bigl(P_\gamma(u)-(\gamma+u)\bigr)
\mathrm{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(P_\gamma(u))=0.
$$

On the positive side, references may actually have arbitrary real coefficients. On the negative side, if at least two factors are branched, then for **each** reference in the stated rational class there is a bounded, rational-coefficient, genuinely nonseparable obstacle $u$, and an explicitly identified $P\in\mathrm{PAPC}_{\mathbb Q}(X,\gamma)$, with
$$
P_\gamma(u)=P,\qquad
\int_X(P-(\gamma+u))\mathrm{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(P)
=-\epsilon\lambda^d\kappa<0.
\tag{1}
$$
Here $\epsilon\in\{1,2\}$ and $\lambda>0$ are rational, and the positive constant $\kappa$ is computed below by recursive primitive-normal divisors. Thus the particular envelope is in the operator domain without any assumption of whole-pair regularity. If the pair is regular, (1) disproves orthogonality; if it is not regular, the requested package already fails. Whole-pair regularity on products with two branched factors is not asserted.

## A direct effectivity observation

\tag{13}
$$
This proves the mass formula recursively in all dimensions of the advertised product class. For two tripods and $m$ affine directions of weight one it is $2^{m+2}(m+2)!$. No factorial has been omitted.

## 4. Every rational reference admits the obstruction

Fix the reference $\gamma$ in the theorem, a rational $R>0$, and $Q,\rho$ as above. First prove a coercive lower bound, not merely properness. Take a common finite rational structure for $\gamma,\rho$. A cell with nonzero lineality is impossible for $\gamma$: the two opposite recession directions would have opposite affine slopes, both required to be positive. Minkowski--Weyl supplies finitely many rational vertices and recession rays for each cell. On every nonzero such ray, the slope of $\rho$ is positive and that of $\gamma$ is positive. Choose $\eta>0$ below their finite set of slope ratios. On each cell, $\gamma-\eta\rho$ is minimized at some vertex because its ray slopes are nonnegative. Taking a minimum over the finite vertex set yields
$$
\gamma\geq\eta\rho-B\quad\hbox{on }X
\tag{14}
$$
for finite $B$. Bounded cells contribute only their vertices. This checks the pointed-polyhedron hypotheses of the external input.

Choose rational $\lambda>0$ with $\lambda K<\eta$. Then $\lambda Q-\gamma$ is bounded above by (14) and tends to minus infinity as $\rho\to\infty$. Choose rational $M>0$ so large that $\gamma-M<\lambda Q$ on the compact set $\rho\leq2R$. Set
$$
P=\max(\lambda Q,\gamma-M),\quad
b_R=(1-\rho/R)_+,\quad
f=P+\epsilon b_R,\quad u=f-\gamma.
\tag{15}
$$
$P$ is PC by the maximum inequality, and $P-\gamma=\max(\lambda Q-\gamma,-M)$ is bounded both above and below. All functions in (15) have rational coefficients. Equality walls between their affine pieces have rational coefficients, so a common finite **rational** spatial subdivision exists. $P$ is PAPC by the direct effectivity observation and has a uniform decreasing approximation $P+1/j$ in the same growth class. The bounded obstacle $u$ is rational PA and hence admissible in the real category. It is not claimed to have integral slopes.

For each zero-curve ray from Section 2, append zero in all remaining factors and form
$$
x_{ij}=\frac{R}{\rho(h_{ij})}h_{ij}.
$$
These are rational points on $\rho=R$ with $Q=0$. Choose endpoint coefficients proportional to $\theta_{ij}\rho(h_{ij})$ and normalize their sum to one. Because the $\theta_{ij}$ share one real scalar times rational values, the normalized coefficients are positive rational numbers. Their barycentre is zero, by the balancing identity for the zero curve. Every endpoint belongs to a closed product cone incident to the origin face, so this is a PCC on the original product structure. At these endpoints $f=P=\lambda Q=0$. Every raw competitor $h\leq f$ consequently satisfies $h(0)\leq0=P(0)$.

If $x$ is rational with $0<\rho(x)<R$, then $y=Rx/\rho(x)$ is rational, lies in the same closed product cone, and has $f(y)=P(y)=\lambda Q(y)$. The endpoints $0,y$ and centre $x$ form a PCC in that cell; homogeneity gives
$$
h(x)\leq\frac{\rho(x)}R h(y)+\left(1-\frac{\rho(x)}R\right)h(0)
\leq\lambda Q(x)=P(x).
$$
For an arbitrary real $x$ in the ball, work in its minimal rational face of a common affine-domain structure for $Q,\rho$. Take rational simplices containing $x$ in that face and in $\rho<R$; their vertices can be made arbitrarily close to $x$. The same-cell PCC inequality with real barycentric coefficients and the rational-point bounds yields $h(x)\leq\lambda Q(x)$ exactly by affinity. A zero-dimensional face is rational and was already treated. For $\rho\geq R$, $b_R=0$ and the obstacle itself gives $h\leq P$. Since $P$ is a competitor, this proves $P_\gamma(u)=P$ for the raw envelope (2).

$P=\lambda Q$ on $\rho\leq2R$, so the recursive measure on $\rho<R$ is $\lambda^d\kappa\delta_0$, by (D)-locality and (13). The integrand $P-f=-\epsilon b_R$ vanishes outside this ball and equals $-\epsilon$ at zero. This proves the exact defect (1), including all mass elsewhere because its integrand is zero.

The obstacle can be guaranteed nonseparable, rather than only the local envelope. Fix rays of the first two factors and the rectangle with first parameter $R$, second parameter $R$, and all other coordinates zero. The mixed rectangle difference
$$
\Delta g=g(0,0)+g(Rv_i,Rv'_j)-g(Rv_i,0)-g(0,Rv'_j)
$$
vanishes for every sum of functions of separate factors. Here $\Delta b_R=1$. Take $\epsilon=1$ unless $\Delta(P-\gamma)=-1$, in which case take $\epsilon=2$. Then $\Delta u\ne0$, so $u$ is genuinely nonseparable. All four evaluation points are rational and lie in $\rho\leq2R$, and the chosen $\epsilon$ remains rational. This completes the new negative direction for every rational reference in the theorem.

## 5. Reconstruction of the positive direction

It remains to establish the positive package independently on one rank-one star times a complete affine space. Let $G$ have $r\geq2$ primitive rays $v_i$, relation $w>0$, weights $cw_i$, and $\dim V=r-1$. Let $X=G\times E$, $d=m+1$, with any positive constant affine weight absorbed into $c$. The closed cells are $C_i=\mathbb R_{\geq0}v_i\times E$, with common face $S=\{0\}\times E$. The reference $\gamma$ may be real PA and positive at infinity, and $u$ may be any bounded real PA function on a finite rational subdivision. Put $f=\gamma+u$.

### Global rational Jensen and ambient majorants

Every raw PC function $h$ satisfies the usual Jensen inequality for all rational-coefficient combinations of rational points of $X$ whose centre is rational and belongs to $X$. For a centre in $S$, the endpoints lie in cells incident to $S$, so this is already a PCC. For a centre $x=(tv_a,z)$, $t>0$ rational, assign endpoints with zero graph coordinate to cell $a$. Write each endpoint as $(t_jv_{i(j)},z_j)$ and its coefficient as $a_j\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}$. Set $T_i=\sum_{i(j)=i}a_jt_j$. The graph-coordinate equation and the rank-one kernel give
$$
T_i=s w_i+t\delta_{ia},\qquad s\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}.
\tag{16}
$$
Nonnegativity of $s$ follows from an index other than $a$. If $s=0$, all positive-coefficient endpoints lie in $C_a$ and the inequality is a cellwise PCC. If $s>0$, set $b_i=sw_i/T_i$. These equal one off $a$ and are strictly between zero and one at $a$. Split endpoint coefficients as $b_{i(j)}a_j$ and $(1-b_{i(j)})a_j$. The first set has total $B\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1)$ and graph-coordinate moment zero. Its normalized centre $y\in S$ is rational. A PCC at $y$ bounds $Bh(y)$ by the first average. The residual endpoints and $y$ all lie in $C_a$, and their PCC with centre $x$ gives the desired Jensen inequality after substitution. Both totals are nonzero since $s>0$ and $t>0$.

Every rational ambient point has a rational-coefficient barycentric representation by rational points of $X$: express its graph coordinate as $\sum_i a_iv_i$ with rational $a_i$, add a large rational multiple of $w$ to make all $a_i\geq0$, and use equal weights $1/r$ on $(ra_iv_i,z)$. Define $H_{\mathbb Q}(y)$ to be the infimum of the $h$-averages over all such representations of rational ambient $y$. It is finite above. A fixed representation of $-y$ with average $A_{-y}$, combined with any representation of $y$, has centre zero; the preceding Jensen inequality gives the finite lower bound $2h(0)-A_{-y}$. Concatenating representations proves rational convexity of $H_{\mathbb Q}$, and Jensen plus the one-point representation gives $H_{\mathbb Q}=h$ at rational points of $X$.

For completeness, rational convexity provides a locally Lipschitz extension. On a rational box, vertex values give an upper bound. On a smaller symmetric box about a rational centre $a$, reflection gives a lower bound $2H_{\mathbb Q}(a)-M$. Thus there are finite bounds $L,M$ on the rational points of a ball of radius $R_0$. For rational $y,z$ in its concentric radius-$R_0/4$ ball, with $0<\delta=\|z-y\|\leq R_0/4$, choose rational $T\geq1$ between $R_0/(4\delta)$ and $R_0/(2\delta)$. The rational point $p=y+T(z-y)$ stays in the larger ball, and convexity gives
$$
H_{\mathbb Q}(z)-H_{\mathbb Q}(y)\leq(M-L)/T\leq4(M-L)\delta/R_0.
$$
Reverse $y,z$ for the other bound; if $\delta>R_0/4$, the bounds alone suffice. Completion gives a finite continuous ambient convex function $H_h$. At any real point in a cell or in $S$, shrinking rational simplices in that same face, with real barycentric coefficients, show $h\leq H_h|_X$. If $h$ is continuous, equality follows by density. If $h\leq f$ and $h\leq\gamma+A$, continuity of these two upper bounds gives $H_h|_X\leq f,\gamma+A$. Thus every raw competitor has an ambient continuous convex majorant preserving its obstacle and growth bounds. Applying this to $\gamma$ itself supplies a finite convex ambient extension $g$ of $\gamma$.

### Epigraph and rational spatial domains

Take $C\geq\|u\|_\infty$. The convex function $g-C$ lies below $f$ on $X$. On a common finite rational structure for $f,\gamma$, all cells are pointed: otherwise the opposite nonzero lineality directions contradict reference positivity at infinity. Write each spatial cell by Minkowski--Weyl as a finite rational convex hull plus a finite rational cone. Its obstacle epigraph is generated by the lifted points $(a,f(a))$, lifted rays $(b,f_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}(b))$, and the upward vertical ray. Let $\mathcal A,\mathcal R$ be their finite unions over all cells. Then
$$
K=\overline{\operatorname{conv}}\{(x,t):x\in X,\ t\geq f(x)\}
=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal A)+\operatorname{cone}(\mathcal R).
\tag{17}
$$
The expression on the right is closed by the stated finite-generation theorem and contains every original epigraph piece. Conversely, adding any selected recession ray to any convex combination of points can be obtained in the closure of the original convex hull by giving a far-out point on that ray a coefficient tending to zero while keeping coefficient times distance fixed. This proves both inclusions in (17).

The spatial projection is all $V\oplus E$, by the barycentric construction above, and $K\subset\operatorname{epi}(g-C)$. Its vertical fibres therefore have finite lower endpoints and define a finite convex polyhedral function $H$ with $\operatorname{epi}H=K$. It is the largest ambient finite convex function below $f$ on $X$, since the epigraph of any such function is closed and convex and contains $K$. The majorant construction proves, including for raw competitors,
$$
P_\gamma(u)=H|_X,\qquad \gamma-C\leq H|_X\leq f\leq\gamma+C.
\tag{18}
$$

Although generator heights in (17) can be real, their spatial coordinates are rational. Every lower graph face is the convex hull of a subset of the lifted point generators plus the cone of a subset of the lifted rays. Its spatial projection is consequently a rational polyhedron. Projection is injective on a graph face, and common graph faces project to faces because height is affine on either graph face. These projected faces form a finite rational complex of affine domains of $H$. Intersect with the original product structure to obtain a finite rational structure on $X$. This proves the required spatial rationality, rather than inferring it from real polyhedrality alone.

The restriction of ambient $H$ is PC, and the direct effectivity observation gives PAPC on every nonnegative local balanced cycle. The bounds (18) give the right growth. The functions $P+1/j$ decrease uniformly to $P$ and have identical divisors. Thus the envelope is in the actual operator domain and the real regularity requirement holds for every obstacle under discussion.

### Two recursive vanishing statements

If a local balanced $d$-cycle $Z$ carries a PA function $a$ which, after subtraction of an ambient affine function, is the pullback of a conical PA function defined on an entire rational quotient vector space of dimension $q<d$, with a finite complete rational fan of affine domains, then $a^d\cdot Z=0$ in this germ. To prove this, let $T_j$ be the union of faces of codimension at least $j$ in that quotient fan. After one divisor the support lies in the inverse image of $T_1$, because the function is ambient affine away from its walls and balancing cancels its divisor there. Inductively, near the relative interior of a face of codimension $j-1$, the preceding cycle is supported in the inverse image of that face, where the function is ambient affine. Its next divisor vanishes by (D). Thus after $j$ divisors support lies in the inverse image of $T_j$. Since $T_{q+1}$ is empty, the asserted vanishing follows. Formula (D) and its refinement identity apply at each step. All applications below supply precisely such an everywhere-defined quotient function, namely the maximum of finitely many active linear forms.

If both a local rational fan cycle and its function-domain fan have a common nonzero rational lineality space $L$, their top zero-cycle vanishes. Compute on their common generalized fan retaining $L$: every face contains $L$, and there are no zero-dimensional faces. Successive divisor cycles stay on this fan, so after $d$ steps its zero-cycle is zero. Refinement compatibility precludes creating an atom by later subdivision. An ambient affine term can be subtracted before this argument by (D). These statements concern the whole local cycle, not only its top-face interiors.

### Noncontact implies zero mass

The top measure of $P=H|_X$ is finite positive atomic and supported at rational vertices of a common rational structure. Fix such a rational $x$ with $H(x)<f(x)$. Its graph point admits a representation from (17), retaining only positive coefficients:
$$
(x,H(x))=\sum_j a_j(y_j,f(y_j))+\sum_b t_b(r_b,s_b),
\quad a_j>0,\ \sum_j a_j=1,\ t_b>0.
\tag{19}
$$
Every active supporting affine plane of $H$ at $x$ has nonnegative slack on these generators. Its total slack in (19) is zero, so all selected points lie on that plane and all selected rays have zero slack. The upward vertical ray has positive slack and cannot be selected. The rational subspace
$$
L=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{y_j-x,\ r_b\}
\tag{20}
$$
is nonzero: if it were zero, all selected points would have spatial coordinate $x$, and all selected rays would be zero, contradicting $H(x)<f(x)$. All differences of active slopes annihilate $L$. Inactive planes stay below near $x$, so the function germ after subtraction of one active affine plane factors through the quotient by $L$.

This quotient has rational domains. Indeed the coarsest local active-domain cones are convex unions of the rational cones from the domain structure proved above, so are generated by finitely many rational rays. They contain $L$ as lineality since all active slope differences annihilate $L$. Their quotient cones are rational, giving the rational quotient fan required in the vanishing statements.

Write $x=(tv_a,z)$, with $t\geq0$, choosing $a$ arbitrarily if $t=0$. Every selected spatial point or ray in (19) has graph part a nonnegative multiple of some $v_i$. Summing its weighted graph coordinates produces
$$
T_i=sw_i+t\delta_{ia},\qquad s\geq0.
\tag{21}
$$
There are three cases.

If $s>0$, the projection of $L$ onto $V$ is all $V$. To check this, let a linear form $\ell$ annihilate that projection and put $A=\ell(tv_a)$. A selected point with graph coordinate $t_jv_i$, $t_j>0$, gives $\ell(v_i)=A/t_j$; a selected ray on ray $i$ gives $\ell(v_i)=0$; a selected point with zero graph coordinate forces $A=0$. Every index appears in a selected point or ray with positive graph coordinate by (21). If $A\ne0$, all $\ell(v_i)$ have its sign or are zero, and at least one is nonzero since (19) has a point generator. This contradicts $\sum_iw_i\ell(v_i)=0$. Therefore $A=0$, all $\ell(v_i)=0$, and $\ell=0$. Hence $\dim L\geq r-1$, and the ambient quotient has dimension at most $((r-1)+m)-(r-1)=m<d$. The quotient vanishing statement gives zero top mass at $x$, also on $S$.

If $s=0,t>0$, all selected generators lie in $C_a$. Thus $L$ is a nonzero subspace of the $d$-dimensional tangent space $\mathbb Rv_a\oplus E$. Locally the carrier is an open complete affine piece with constant weight $cw_a$. Restrict the quotient germ to this tangent space; its dimension is at most $d-1$, so the same vanishing statement applies.

If $s=t=0$, all selected graph coordinates vanish and $0\ne L\subset E$. At $x\in S$ the local carrier is $G\times E$, with lineality containing $L$, and the function domains retain $L$ as just proved. Their common generalized fan therefore satisfies the lineality vanishing statement. If $m=0$ this case is impossible. These three cases exhaust (21).

Thus every noncontact potential atom has zero top coefficient. Positivity and finite atomicity imply
$$
\operatorname{supp}\mathrm{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(P)\subset\{P=f\},
$$
and the exact contact integral is zero for every bounded real PA obstacle. This reconstructs the positive theorem without using the earlier verdict or a separability assumption.

### Completion of the product criterion

A bivalent factor has opposite primitive generators in a rank-one lattice and equal weights, so it is the entire affine line with constant weight. If there is at most one branched factor, merge every bivalent factor with $E$ and multiply their constant weights. The carrier becomes one rank-one star times a complete affine space, as just proved. If there is no branched factor, it is complete affine space; in positive dimension represent one coordinate line as a bivalent star and apply the same proof. In dimension zero the envelope at the sole point is the obstacle value, and the contact identity is immediate with its positive zero-cycle weight. The negative direction for two or more branched factors is Section 4. This proves the theorem exactly as stated.

## Comparison, limitations and self-audit boundary

The controlling Chapter 72 and its included proofs already settle one-dimensional real orthogonality using local relation cones, complete affine real orthogonality, and separable product envelopes. The one-star-times-affine draft gives the positive nonseparable cylinder range. The contribution developed here is the all-rational-reference negative direction for any number of rank-one star factors once two are branched, its full recursive mass formula, and the resulting criterion on this product class. The previous Bergman obstruction and its positive-growth repair are not counted as progress. No assumption of top-cycle uniqueness, balancing, positive total mass or positive growth has been used as a contact theorem.

The negative all-reference quantifier is **rational**, not arbitrary real. The operation $\max(\lambda Q,\gamma-M)$ can introduce an irrational spatial wall for a real-coefficient $\gamma$. In one dimension rational chordal interpolation can repair this; here such interpolation has not been supplied with all local PCC inequalities, exact trap germ and growth preserved. Hence the converse for all real references is unresolved. The positive direction remains valid for real references because its epigraph proof establishes rational projected domains directly.

No literal all-obstacle classification is claimed. Even (15) with a literal $\gamma$ generally has nonintegral rational slopes. The explicit example (5)--(6) does give a particular literal PA envelope defect, but it does not imply literal regularity. The known noncompact literal regularity obstruction belongs to P05.1 and is background only.

The product theorem does not address graph factors with bounded edges or cycles, arbitrary higher-rank star factors, general singular fans, general intrinsic strict-positivity packages, or all balanced pairs. There is no proper polarized analytic realization, CLD/BFJ comparison or P05.5 assertion. The original exact P05.2 atom remains open: status is **partial**, candidate is **false**. These are unverified mathematical drafts.
