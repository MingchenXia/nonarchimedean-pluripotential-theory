\subsection{Exact result and scope}\label{exact-result-and-scope}

The original classification P05.2 remains open. The following
nonconical special-case theorem has received the source-aware independent
PASS accompanying this paper. It includes every reference and obstacle
stated below. No formal verification or priority certification is claimed.

Put \(A=(0,0)\), \(B=(1,0)\) in the actual lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\). Let
\(G\) consist of \([A,B]\), the rays from \(A\) in directions
\((0,1),(-1,-1)\), and the rays from \(B\) in directions
\((0,-1),(1,1)\). Give all five edges weight one. For \(m\geq0\) let \[
X=G\times\mathbb R^m,\qquad N=\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z^m,
\qquad d=m+1,
\] with product weight one. In particular the requested cylinder is
\(m=1\).

\textbf{Theorem.} For every continuous
\(\gamma\in\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}(X)\cap\mathrm{PC}(X)\) positive in
every nonzero recession direction of every affine reference cell, and
every bounded continuous \(u\in\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}(X)\), the raw
envelope \(P=P_\gamma(u)\) is itself in
\(\mathrm{PAPC}_{\mathbb R}(X,\gamma)\) on a finite rational spatial
complex. Consequently it is real-regularizable, and, for the full
recursive intersection operator, \[
\operatorname{supp}\bigl(P^d\cdot[X]\bigr)\subset\{P=\gamma+u\},
\qquad
\int_X(P-\gamma-u)\,\mathrm{MA}_{\mathrm{poly},\mathbb R}(P)=0.
\tag{T}
\] The operator is \(P^d\cdot[X]\), with primitive normals in saturated
tangent lattices; its factorial is retained. For \(m=1\) it is the full
two-fold divisor. Both \(\gamma\) and \(u\) may be genuinely
nonseparable and have arbitrary real affine coefficients on finite
rational spatial subdivisions.

This theorem does not cover arbitrary finite rank-one graphs, cycles,
general singular fans, or the literal integral-slope category. It
therefore does not solve the original atom. The accepted note n100
concerns stars and is not used as a theorem premise below. The prior
all-real-reference product draft was read in full, together with its
author audit and research notes, as fallible material. Its finite
support/decomposition arguments are not imported. The present proof has
its own independent review; no older receipt is substituted for it.

\subsection{Definitions and exact primary
inputs}\label{definitions-and-exact-primary-inputs}

BMPS means Botero--Mazzon--Pille-Schneider, \emph{Monge--Ampère measures
on balanced polyhedral spaces}, arXiv:2603.08664v1, submitted 9 March
2026, manuscript dated 10 March 2026. The cached PDF and extracted text
are retained as \texttt{sources/BMPS2603.08664v1.pdf} and \texttt{.txt}.

Our \(\mathrm{PA}_{\mathbb R}\) consists of continuous functions affine
with real coefficients on a \textbf{finite rational spatial complex}. It
is distinct from Definition 2.28's integral linear parts and rational
constants. Definitions 3.2--3.3 use PCCs \[
x=\sum_j a_jx_j,\quad a_j\geq0,\quad\sum_j a_j=1,
\quad x_j\in X\cap N_{\mathbb Q},
\] with real coefficients and cells \(\tau\preceq\sigma_j\) on some
rational structure, \(x\in\tau\), \(x_j\in\sigma_j\). A finite raw PC
function satisfies the corresponding Jensen inequality for every such
PCC. We do not assume that a raw competitor is continuous.

Definitions 2.18--2.20 take
\(N_\sigma=N\cap\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(\sigma-\sigma)\), and
normals \(n_{\sigma/\tau}\) primitively generating \(N_\sigma/N_\tau\),
directed into \(\sigma\). Source cycles have rational weights; here
weights are explicitly extended to real scalars. Balancing is
\(\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}z(\sigma)n_{\sigma/\tau}\in N_{\tau,\mathbb R}\).
Generalized fans with lineality are allowed by Definition 2.22.

The essential external algebra result is precisely BMPS Definition 2.31,
Remark 2.33 and Lemmas 2.34--2.35: for a literal PA function \(a\) and
rational balanced cycle \(z\), the divisor is \[
(a\cdot z)(\tau)=\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}z(\sigma)a_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}(n_{\sigma/\tau})
-a_{\tau,\mathrm{lin}}\left(\sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}z(\sigma)n_{\sigma/\tau}\right).
\tag{D}
\] It is lift independent, balanced, compatible with rational
refinement, linear, and commuting under successive divisors. The
inspected source proof establishes lift independence by tangential
cancellation, refinement compatibility by opposite normals or inherited
normals, and balancing/commutativity by quotient local fans and
Allermann--Rau Proposition 3.7. We use the stated BMPS lemma as an
external theorem, not its catalogue description. Its applicability to
real coefficients follows as follows: on a fixed rational complex,
continuity equations for affine pieces and balancing equations for
cycles are rational linear equations. Rational solutions are dense in
their real solution spaces. Rational affine pieces become literal after
clearing denominators; rational cycle vectors can likewise be cleared of
denominators before applying the source identities and then rescaled.
Formula (D) and its iterates are polynomial in these finite coefficient
vectors. All the listed identities therefore pass to real coefficients
and weights. This argument also applies to finite rational local
generalized fans. No nonrational subdivision is introduced.

Definition 3.1 tests PAPC against all nonnegative local balanced cycles
of every positive dimension on ordinary open sets. Effectivity at this
real scope is proved below. Definition 4.4 defines the PA measure as the
finite atomic zero-cycle \(a^d\cdot[X]\), not an Alexandrov measure
divided by \(d!\). Proposition 3.22 and Definition 3.23 motivate the
explicit real regularizable class used here: uniform decreasing PAPC
approximation with bounded reference difference. For an already PAPC
\(P\), the sequence \(P+1/j\) supplies this approximation, with
identical divisors. We need no extension theorem for a non-PA envelope.

Definition 6.2 gives exactly \[
P_\gamma(u)(x)=\sup\{h(x):h\in\mathrm{PC}(X),\ h\leq\gamma+u,
\ h\leq\gamma+C_h\text{ for some }C_h\in\mathbb R\}.
\tag{E}
\] Definition 4.15 is the recession positivity hypothesis in the
theorem. We use no source continuity theorem for raw PC functions, no
uniform density theorem, no analytic realization, no projection formula,
and no total-mass-to-contact implication.

\subsection{Elementary convex facts used in the
proof}\label{elementary-convex-facts-used-in-the-proof}

We record proofs to specify the finite-dimensional inputs, including
rationality.

\textbf{Finite generation and closedness.} A rational polyhedral cone
has finitely many rational generators, including both signs of a
rational basis of its lineality. A nonempty pointed rational polyhedron
has the form \(\operatorname{conv}(A_0)+\operatorname{cone}(R_0)\) with
finite rational sets and \(A_0\ne\varnothing\). A finite convex hull
plus a finitely generated cone of real vectors is a closed polyhedron.

For a pointed cone written \(\ell_i\geq0\), the sum
\(\ell=\sum_i\ell_i\) is positive on its nonzero vectors: otherwise all
\(\ell_i\) vanish and give lineality. The section \(\ell=1\) is bounded,
since an unbounded sequence, normalized on the unit sphere, would limit
to a nonzero cone vector with \(\ell=0\). This bounded polyhedron is the
convex hull of its vertices: a point in a positive-dimensional minimal
face lies between terminal points in smaller faces, and induction
reduces to vertices. Vertices are finitely many solutions of full-rank
active equations and are rational. Quotienting a general cone by its
rational lineality and lifting a rational basis proves the first claim.
Homogenize a pointed polyhedron to \(\{(y,t):Ay\geq bt,t\geq0\}\); its
generators with \(t>0\) supply points and those with \(t=0\) supply
recession rays. Finally eliminate variables in any finite nonstrict
linear system by comparing every lower bound with every upper bound.
One-sided bounds impose no further restriction. This exact elimination
proves that projections are closed polyhedra; rational data stay
rational. Apply it to \(x=\sum a_ip_i+\sum t_jr_j\), \(a_i,t_j\geq0\),
\(\sum a_i=1\) for the closedness assertion, also when generator heights
are real.

\textbf{Rational convex completion and subgradients.} A finite
rationally convex function on \(\mathbb Q^m\) has a unique finite
continuous convex extension to \(\mathbb R^m\). On a rational box, its
vertex values bound it above. Reflection about a rational centre bounds
it below on a smaller box. If \(L\leq F\leq M\) on a rational ball of
radius \(R\), then for rational \(y,z\) in a concentric ball of radius
\(R/4\), extend the segment to \(y+T(z-y)\) with rational
\(R/(4\|z-y\|)\leq T\leq R/(2\|z-y\|)\). Convexity bounds the difference
by \(4(M-L)\|z-y\|/R\); reverse the points for the opposite bound. Large
differences are controlled by \(M-L\). Thus completion is locally
Lipschitz and convex, and is unique by density. The same proof makes
every finite everywhere-defined convex function continuous.

Such a function has a supporting affine plane at every point. Here is a
finite-dimensional proof. Its epigraph is closed and convex. Separate
the point \((x,F(x)-\varepsilon)\) by the normal obtained from its
nearest point in the epigraph. The normal's height coefficient is
positive: it is nonnegative because of the upward ray, and if zero its
horizontal form would bound all of \(\mathbb R^m\), an impossibility for
a nonzero normal. Normalizing gives an affine minorant \(l_\varepsilon\)
with \(F(x)-\varepsilon<l_\varepsilon(x)\leq F(x)\). Evaluations at
\(x\pm Re_i\) bound its slopes uniformly for \(0<\varepsilon\leq1\). A
convergent subsequence as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) gives the desired
supporting plane. Existence of the nearest point follows by restricting
the distance minimization to a large compact ball; the normal separation
inequality follows by differentiating distance squared on segments. The
case \(m=0\) is immediate.

These arguments supply the ordinary convex facts below, rather than
relying on an unstated ambient extension theorem.

\subsection{A rational lift that preserves the actual PCCs and
lattices}\label{a-rational-lift-that-preserves-the-actual-pccs-and-lattices}

Use a new lattice
\(\widetilde N_G=\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zb\oplus\mathbb Zd\) and put \[
c=-a-b,\qquad e=a-d.
\] The lifted graph \(\widetilde G\) has vertices \(0,a\), edge
\([0,a]\), rays \(\mathbb R_{\geq0}b,\mathbb R_{\geq0}c\) from zero, and
\(a+\mathbb R_{\geq0}d,a+\mathbb R_{\geq0}e\). Its leaves satisfy the
unique relation \(b+c+d+e=0\) and span three dimensions. Define
\(\iota:G\to\widetilde G\) by preserving the parameter on each of these
five edges, with the corresponding endpoints. Let
\(\widetilde X=\widetilde G\times\mathbb R^m\).

Every convex polyhedron in either cylinder is contained in one closed
edge strip or a vertex fibre. Indeed its graph projection is a convex
subset of the graph. A segment in this graph cannot pass through a
trivalent vertex and continue onto another edge, since no two outgoing
directions there are opposite. Thus a convex graph subset lies on a
single edge. This also proves the assertion for lower-dimensional cells
and arbitrary spatial subdivisions.

At the first vertex, the lift on all three incident strips is the
restriction of the linear isomorphism sending the original basis
\((1,0),(0,1)\) to \(a,b\). At the second vertex it is the affine map
sending \(B\) to \(a\) and the original basis \((1,0),(0,-1)\) to
\(a,d\). On the common edge the maps agree. They are lattice
isomorphisms onto the saturated planes \(\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zb\)
and \(\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zd\), respectively; the affine factor is
unchanged.

Consequently finite rational structures transfer in both directions. At
a PCC centre in a vertex fibre all endpoint cells belong to these
incident strips, so the single local affine map preserves the
barycentre, rational endpoints, and incidences. At an edge-interior
centre they all belong to that edge strip, with the same property. The
inverse maps do the same. Hence \textbf{all actual PCCs}, even on a
different rational structure, are preserved, and raw PC classes are
equivalent under \(\iota\).

The same local lattice maps identify every saturated cell tangent
lattice and primitive quotient normal. Formula (D) is unchanged in each
germ, including all subdivision and lower faces. All weights remain one.
Iterating (D) identifies the full \(d\)-fold zero-cycles under the lift.
This is a local isomorphism argument, not a pushforward along a
nonproper map.

\subsection{Scalar supports on the lifted two-vertex
tree}\label{scalar-supports-on-the-lifted-two-vertex-tree}

\textbf{Lemma 1.} Suppose \(k\) is a finite function on
\(\widetilde G\cap\widetilde N_{G,\mathbb Q}\) satisfying
rational-coefficient PCC inequalities. It has a continuous extension
along every edge, finite one-sided derivatives at both vertices, and a
global ambient affine support touching it at every graph point.

Write \(k_0\) for the edge function on \([0,1]\), \(p_0=k_0'(0+)\),
\(p_1=k_0'(1-)\), and \(m_b,m_c,m_d,m_e\) for the outgoing ray
derivatives at their vertices. Then \[
p_0\leq p_1,\qquad p_0+m_b+m_c\geq0,
\qquad -p_1+m_d+m_e\geq0.
\tag{S}
\]

\textbf{Proof.} Rational Jensen on each interval gives its finite convex
continuous completion in the interior. Its limit at an endpoint is
finite and no larger than the given endpoint value: the upper bound
follows by a secant from the endpoint, and an interior supporting secant
gives a finite lower bound near it. Equal small rational parameters on
the three incident edges form an equal-weight PCC at a vertex. The
vertex value is at most the average of the three limiting values, each
of which is at most that vertex value. Thus all three limits equal it.
Each outgoing derivative is bounded above by a finite secant. The same
equal-parameter inequality, divided by the parameter, precludes any
derivative being \(-\infty\) and proves their sum nonnegative. This
gives (S), with all six derivatives finite.

At an edge point \(ra\), choose a supporting slope \(s\) for \(k_0\), so
\(p_0\leq s\leq p_1\), and anchor the affine form there. Choose its leaf
slopes by \[
p(a)=s,\quad p(b)=m_b,\quad p(c)=-s-m_b\leq m_c,
\quad p(d)=m_d,\quad p(e)=s-m_d\leq m_e.
\] These values satisfy both vector relations and specify an ambient
form. The intercepts at both vertices lie below \(k\) there. Each leaf
restriction then lies below its convex ray function, because its slope
is at most the outgoing derivative.

For a point \(tb\) on the first leaf choose \(\beta\in\partial k_b(t)\),
set \(p(b)=\beta\), \(p(a)=p_0\), \(p(c)=-p_0-\beta\), and choose
\(p(d)=m_d,p(e)=p_0-m_d\). Since \(\beta\geq m_b\), (S) gives the
required upper bounds on the other leaf slopes. Anchoring at \(tb\)
gives an intercept at zero at most \(k(0)\). The edge with slope \(p_0\)
lies below \(k_0\), and its intercept at \(a\) is at most \(k(a)\). All
leaves are bounded as above. Interchange \(b,c\) for the other first
leaf. For a second leaf point \(a+td\), choose
\(\beta\in\partial k_d(t)\), set \(p(a)=p_1\),
\(p(d)=\beta\), \(p(e)=p_1-\beta\), and give the affine
support its value \(k_d(t)-\beta t\leq k(a)\) at \(a\).
Interchanging the vertex roles completes the construction: the endpoint
support with slope \(p_1\) bounds the edge, and (S) bounds the first leaves. Vertices are included
by endpoint edge supports. This proves global touching affine supports.
\(\square\)

\subsection{Global convex majorants of every raw
competitor}\label{global-convex-majorants-of-every-raw-competitor}

\textbf{Lemma 2.} For any finite raw PC function \(h\) on
\(\widetilde X\), there is a finite continuous ambient convex \(H_h\) on
\(\mathbb R^3\oplus\mathbb R^m\) which equals \(h\) at every rational
carrier point and majorizes \(h\) everywhere on the carrier. Every
continuous upper bound of \(h\) on the carrier is also an upper bound of
\(H_h|_{\widetilde X}\). In particular continuous PC functions extend
exactly.

\textbf{Proof.} Fix a rational graph point \(q\). The values \(h(q,z)\)
on \(z\in\mathbb Q^m\) are rationally convex, using a same-fibre PCC.
Let \(F_q\) be their finite convex completion. For any
\(p\in(\mathbb R^m)^*\) define \[
k_p(q)=\inf_{z\in\mathbb Q^m}\{h(q,z)-p(z)\}\in[-\infty,\infty).
\tag{L1}
\] It satisfies rational graph PCC inequalities, with \(-\infty\)
allowed: for finitely many rational graph endpoints choose rational
\(z_j\) approximating their infima. The rational barycentre
\((q,\sum a_jz_j)\) is an actual cylinder PCC, and subtracting \(p\) and
taking infima proves the inequality. If an endpoint infimum is
\(-\infty\), let that one go to \(-\infty\) while keeping the other
choices finite.

If \(k_p\) is finite at one rational graph point, it is finite at every
rational graph point. At an edge-interior centre, any other point of the
edge is an endpoint of a rational same-edge combination with that centre
(choose the other endpoint on the other side), so a \(-\infty\) value
would force the centre value \(-\infty\). Thus the vertices of that edge
have finite values. At a finite vertex, any rational point on an
incident edge can be put in a vertex PCC with endpoints on the other two
edges: choose positive rational lengths on them, within the bounded edge
if necessary, and weights proportional to the reciprocal lengths. The
three weighted directions cancel. A \(-\infty\) endpoint is impossible.
Propagation across the connected graph proves the claim. This explicitly
controls arbitrarily distant endpoints.

For a rational \((q,z)\), take a subgradient \(p\) of \(F_q\) at \(z\).
Then \(k_p(q)=h(q,z)-p(z)\) is finite, so all \(k_p\) are finite on the
graph and Lemma 1 gives an ambient affine support \(l\) of \(k_p\)
touching at \(q\). The affine function \(l(y)+p(z')\) is below
\(h(y,z')\) at every rational carrier point and touches at \((q,z)\).

Take the supremum of these affine functions over all rational \((q,z)\)
and their choices of supports. The resulting \(H_h\) is finite
everywhere. To see the upper bound, note that the convex hull of
rational carrier points is the entire ambient space. Indeed \(b,c,d,e\)
span three dimensions and have positive relation \((1,1,1,1)\). The
tetrahedra with vertices \(Tb,Tc,a+Td,a+Te\) exhaust the space as the
positive integer \(T\) grows: after division by \(T\) they converge to
the tetrahedron of \(b,c,d,e\), with zero strictly interior. Taking
their products with rational boxes in the affine factor gives convex
hulls of finitely many rational carrier points containing any prescribed
ambient point. Every affine function in the supremum is bounded by the
corresponding convex average of the finitely many \(h\) values there. A
single support gives a finite lower bound. Thus \(H_h\) is finite
convex, hence continuous, and equals \(h\) at rational carrier points.

At an arbitrary real carrier point take shrinking simplices with
rational vertices in its minimal face of a rational structure. They
exist because that face has rational affine hull and the point is in its
relative interior; a zero-dimensional face is already rational. Their
real barycentric coefficients form same-cell PCCs. The PC inequality and
continuity of \(H_h\) give \(h\leq H_h\) by taking the limit. Finally
any continuous bound valid on rational points extends to \(H_h\) on all
carrier points by density. For continuous \(h\) this gives equality.
\(\square\)

This proves the ambient extension needed on the \textbf{lifted} carrier;
it makes no ambient extension assertion in the original plane.

\subsection{Every envelope is PA on finite rational
domains}\label{every-envelope-is-pa-on-finite-rational-domains}

Transport \(\gamma,u\) to \(\widetilde X\), put \(f=\gamma+u\), and
choose \(C\geq\|u\|_\infty\). Lemma 2 gives a finite continuous ambient
convex extension \(g\) of \(\gamma\).

On a common finite rational structure of \(\gamma\) and \(f\), every
spatial cell is pointed: a nonzero lineality vector and its negative
would have opposite affine reference slopes, contradicting strict
recession positivity. Write each cell as a finite rational convex hull
plus a finite rational recession cone, using the elementary
finite-generation result. Its obstacle epigraph is generated by its
finitely many lifted point generators \((y,f(y))\), lifted recession
generators \((r,f_{\sigma,\mathrm{lin}}(r))\), and the upward vertical
ray. Let \(\mathcal A,\mathcal R\) be the unions of these finite sets.
They have rational spatial coordinates, though their heights can be
arbitrary real numbers. Then \[
K=\overline{\operatorname{conv}}\{(x,t):x\in\widetilde X,t\geq f(x)\}
=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal A)+\operatorname{cone}(\mathcal R).
\tag{K}
\] The right side is closed and contains all epigraph pieces.
Conversely, adding a multiple of any recession generator to a point of
the convex hull is obtained as a limit: combine weight \(\varepsilon\)
on a far point of that epigraph piece with weight \(1-\varepsilon\) on
the given convex combination, keeping weight times distance fixed.
Iterate for finitely many recession generators and take closure. This
proves (K).

The projection of \(K\) is the whole ambient space by the preceding
tetrahedron construction. Since \(K\subset\operatorname{epi}(g-C)\), its
fibres are bounded below. They are closed upward rays. Thus
\(K=\operatorname{epi}(H)\) for a finite convex polyhedral function
\(H\), the greatest ambient convex minorant of \(f\) on the carrier. In
particular \[
\gamma-C\leq H|_{\widetilde X}\leq f\leq\gamma+C.
\tag{B}
\] Every raw competitor has the ambient majorant of Lemma 2, preserving
both continuous bounds \(f\) and \(\gamma+C_h\). The greatest-minorant
property gives \(h\leq H|_{\widetilde X}\). Conversely
\(H|_{\widetilde X}\) is PC by ambient Jensen and is a competitor by
(B). Hence the raw envelope is exactly \(H|_{\widetilde X}\).

We must prove \textbf{rational spatial domains}, not infer them from
real polyhedrality. A lower graph face of \(K\) is precisely the convex
hull of the subset of \(\mathcal A\) on its supporting graph plane plus
the cone of the subset of \(\mathcal R\) on that plane. Indeed all
slacks are nonnegative, and any positive-coefficient generator in a
point of the face has zero slack. Its spatial projection is consequently
a rational polyhedron. Projection is an affine isomorphism on a graph
face; intersections of graph faces project to intersections of faces
because the graph height is unique. These projections form a finite
rational complex covering the ambient space. Equivalently the finite
inequality description of \(K\), with its full spatial projection and
upward fibres, expresses \(H\) as a maximum of finitely many affine
planes. Its lower faces cover its graph. Intersect the rational
projected domains with the lifted strips and pull them back by
\(\iota\). This gives a finite rational structure for \(P\) on the
actual cylinder.

Effectivity against every nonnegative local real cycle follows directly
from ambient convexity. At a tested face choose a subgradient of \(H\)
at a relative-interior point, subtract its affine form, and apply (D).
The residual inward derivatives are nonnegative, while the residual
tangent derivative vanishes; their weighted sum is nonnegative. This
works in every positive cycle dimension, for every local open set, and
passes back by the local lattice isomorphisms. Thus \(P\) is real PAPC,
and \(P+1/j\) proves its uniform decreasing regularizability. Its
measure is the PA recursive measure throughout.

\subsection{Two local recursive vanishing
facts}\label{two-local-recursive-vanishing-facts}

The following facts apply to complete rational local function-domain
fans and rational balanced cycles with lineality. They concern the whole
cycle, not only top-face interiors.

\textbf{Quotient vanishing.} If, after subtraction of an ambient affine
function, the germ of a PA function on a local \(d\)-cycle factors
through a rational quotient of its ambient tangent space of dimension
\(q<d\), as a function defined on that entire quotient with a finite
complete rational fan of domains, its \(d\)-fold divisor is zero. Let
\(T_j\) be the union of quotient fan faces of codimension at least
\(j\). After one divisor the support is in the inverse image of \(T_1\):
off the walls the function is ambient affine and (D) vanishes by
balancing. Inductively, near a relative-interior point of a face of
codimension \(j-1\), the previous cycle is supported in that face's
inverse image, where the function restricts to an affine function. Its
next divisor vanishes there by (D). Thus after \(j\) divisors support is
in the inverse image of \(T_j\). Since \(T_{q+1}=\varnothing\) and
\(q+1\leq d\), the top divisor is zero. Rational refinement
compatibility legitimizes each calculation.

\textbf{Lineality vanishing.} If the local carrier fan and the
function-domain fan have a common nonzero rational lineality space
\(L\), compute on their common generalized fan retaining \(L\). Every
face contains \(L\), so there are no zero-dimensional faces. All
successive divisor cycles remain on this fan and the top zero-cycle
vanishes. Subsequent subdivision cannot create an atom by refinement
compatibility. An ambient affine term may first be subtracted by (D).

\subsection{Noncontact geometry and all lower-face
contributions}\label{noncontact-geometry-and-all-lower-face-contributions}

The PA measure is positive finite atomic and its possible atoms are
rational vertices of the common rational structure. Fix such a lifted
point \(x=(q,z)\) with \(H(x)<f(x)\). A finite generator representation
in (K), omitting zero coefficients, is \[
(x,H(x))=\sum_j\alpha_j(y_j,f(y_j))+\sum_\nu t_\nu(r_\nu,s_\nu),
\quad \alpha_j>0,\quad\sum_j\alpha_j=1,\quad t_\nu>0.
\tag{F}
\] Every active supporting graph plane at \(x\) has nonnegative slack on
all generators; total slack in (F) is zero. Thus every selected point
and ray is on every active plane. The upward vertical ray cannot occur.
The rational space \[
L=\operatorname{span}\{y_j-x,r_\nu\}
\tag{Q}
\] is nonzero: otherwise all point spatial coordinates equal \(x\) and
no nonzero selected ray can occur, forcing \(H(x)=f(x)\). Differences of
active slopes annihilate \(L\). Inactive planes stay below in a small
neighborhood. Consequently the germ after subtraction of one active
affine plane is invariant under \(L\).

Its active-domain fan is rational. The global domain complex was proved
rational above; the coarsest active cones are convex unions of its
finitely many rational local cones and therefore generated by finitely
many rational rays. They contain \(L\) as lineality. Intersecting with a
rational tangent space and quotienting by its intersection with \(L\)
supplies exactly the complete rational quotient fans needed for the
vanishing facts.

Here is the crucial global-to-local geometry. For selected point
generators, write the graph coordinates in the five types \[
t_b b,\quad t_c c,\quad a+t_d d,\quad a+t_e e,
\quad ra\ (0\leq r\leq1).
\] Strictly positive leaf parameters identify the leaf types; vertices
may be assigned to the bounded-edge type. Selected recession rays have
graph coordinate a nonnegative multiple of one of \(b,c,d,e\), or zero.
Let \(T_i\) denote the total leaf parameter of type \(i\) in (F),
including its point and recession coefficients. Let \(M_B\) be the total
point coefficient of the two second-vertex leaf types, and \(R\) the
weighted sum of the bounded-edge point parameters. Comparing
coefficients in the basis \(a,b,d\), for \(q=q_a a+q_b b+q_d d\) gives
\[
T_b-T_c=q_b,\quad T_d-T_e=q_d,
\quad M_B+R-T_c+T_e=q_a.
\tag{M}
\] All parameters and masses are nonnegative. This includes remote
points and recession rays from the entire epigraph, not just incident
cells.

At \(q=0\), write \(T_b=T_c=s\), \(T_d=T_e=t\). Then \(s=M_B+R+t\). If
\(s>0\), a selected generator exists on each of \(b,c\). Their vectors
in (Q) have independent graph projections \(b,c\) and belong to \[
W_A=\operatorname{span}(a,b)\oplus\mathbb R^m.
\] Hence \(\dim(L\cap W_A)\geq2\). The restriction of the germ to
\(W_A\) factors through a quotient of dimension at most \(m<d\), so
quotient vanishing applies to the full trivalent local cycle. If
\(s=0\), (M) forces \(M_B=R=t=0\): all selected point graph coordinates
are zero and all selected ray graph coordinates vanish. Thus
\(0\ne L\subset\mathbb R^m\), common lineality of both local fans, and
lineality vanishing applies.

At \(q=a\), the same equations give \[
t=s+1-M_B-R,
\qquad 1-M_B-R\geq0.
\] The second inequality is the sum of first-vertex leaf point
coefficients and bounded-edge coefficients times \(1-r\). If \(t>0\),
the selected \(d,e\) generators give \(\dim(L\cap W_B)\geq2\), with
\(W_B=\operatorname{span}(a,d)\oplus\mathbb R^m\), and quotient
dimension at most \(m\). If \(t=0\), all points have graph coordinate
\(a\) and all recession graph coordinates vanish, so the nonzero space
\(L\) lies in the affine factor. The same two vanishing alternatives
apply.

At an interior edge point \(q=ra\), \(0<r<1\), if \(s=T_b=T_c>0\), take
one selected generator of each first-vertex leaf type. Their graph
projections in (Q) are \[
u_b=t_b b-\delta_b ra,\qquad
u_c=t_c(-a-b)-\delta_c ra,
\] where \(\delta_i=1\) for a point and \(0\) for a recession ray. The
combination \(t_cu_b+t_bu_c\) is a strictly negative multiple of \(a\).
Thus \(L\) has a nonzero vector in the local tangent
\(W_0=\mathbb Ra\oplus\mathbb R^m\). If \(t=T_d=T_e>0\), the analogous
combination is \[
[t_dt_e+(1-r)(t_e\delta_d+t_d\delta_e)]a,
\] also nonzero. If \(s=t=0\), all selected graph coordinates lie on the
bounded edge or are zero recession directions, so \(L\subset W_0\) and
is nonzero. In every case the local \(d\)-dimensional affine strip germ
factors through a quotient of dimension at most \(d-1\).

Finally take \(q=\tau b\), \(\tau>0\), in a first leaf. Equations (M)
give \[
T_b=T_c+\tau,\qquad T_d=T_e=t,
\qquad T_c=M_B+R+t.
\] If \(T_c>0\), then \(T_b>\tau\). There must be either a selected
recession ray of type \(b\), or a selected point of type \(b\) with
parameter greater than \(\tau\); otherwise \(T_b\leq\tau\) times the
total point mass, at most \(\tau\). Such a generator gives a nonzero
vector of \(L\) in \(\mathbb Rb\oplus\mathbb R^m\), the local strip
tangent. If \(T_c=0\), all selected graph coordinates are on this leaf
or at zero, so \(L\) is contained in that tangent and is nonzero.
Quotient vanishing applies. The other first leaf follows by
interchanging \(b,c\). The other two leaves follow by the affine lattice
symmetry \(y\mapsto a+Jy\), where \(J(a)=-a,J(b)=d,J(d)=b\); it
interchanges vertices and the two leaf pairs. This symmetry preserves
all representations and inequalities just used.

These cases cover every point of the carrier. At every noncontact
candidate atom the \textbf{full} local top divisor is zero. In
particular vertex-fibre atoms, interior-face atoms, and all
mixed/lower-face contributions have been controlled. Finite atomicity
and positivity now prove the support inclusion in (T). Its integrand
vanishes at every remaining atom, so its complete integral is zero.
Pulling back through the primitive-normal lattice identifications proves
the theorem on the actual requested carrier.

\subsection{Comparison, limitations, and a useful failed
route}\label{comparison-limitations-and-a-useful-failed-route}

The book already classifies one-dimensional finite balanced graphs and
proves complete affine contact; n100 already proves the
star-times-affine all-real-reference result. The theorem here adds an
actual bounded edge, both vertex fibres simultaneously, arbitrary
nonseparable references/obstacles, global rational-domain envelopes, and
a global contact argument. It uses neither a separable envelope formula
nor the inference that local stars automatically glue.

A planar ambient epigraph construction would fail even for
positive-growth references. Define a scalar function \(k\) on the
original graph by \(k=0\) on \([A,B]\), \(k(t)=\max(-t,t-2)\) on the
upward leaf at \(A\) and downward leaf at \(B\), and \(k(t)=2t\) on the
two diagonal leaves. It is convex on each edge. At either vertex its
outgoing derivatives are \(0,-1,2\), whose sum is one, so its scalar PCC
inequalities hold: local supports exist because the sole local direction
relation is \((1,1,1)\), and along each centre-to-endpoint segment these
supports extend by one-variable convexity. Thus \(k+\sum|z_j|\) is a
rational PA PC reference positive in every recession direction after the
rational absolute-value subdivision. The points \((0,1)\) and \((1,-1)\)
have value \(-1\) and their ambient midpoint \((1/2,0)\) has value zero.
No planar ambient convex function can restrict to \(k\). This midpoint
is not a PCC, since its endpoint rays do not have incident cells
containing the midpoint. The rational lift removes exactly this
extraneous ambient dependency.

No theorem is proved for arbitrary finite rank-one graphs, especially
cycles, weighted nonsaturated graph junctions, or more general
higher-dimensional singular spaces. The explicit two-vertex graph times
arbitrary complete affine space is the entire claimed scope. There is no
literal integral-slope regularity theorem: even a real PA envelope with
rational spatial cells need not have integral slopes. There is no
analytic realization, CLD/BFJ comparison, or P05.5 result. The original
P05.2 classification remains open; \texttt{status.json} is partial and
candidate is false.
