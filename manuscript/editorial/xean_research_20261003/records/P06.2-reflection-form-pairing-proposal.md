# Unreviewed differential-form diagnostic for P06.2

This is a new root research proposal. It is not an analytic counterexample, an accepted supporting theorem, or a proof of finite separable ascent. It was written after admission of the live arbitrary-field recognition and curve two-sign campaigns and is not injected into their frozen inputs. Original P06.2 remains open.

The remaining higher-dimensional finite Galois gate concerns ordinary compact tests of bidegree (n-1,n-1), not just invariant scalar functions. A useful toy distinction is between exact descent of invariant forms and approximation of their differentiated scalar-current pairings.

## A reflection quotient in real superforms

Take coordinates (y_1,...,y_{n-1},d) on R^n, n>=2, and the reflection d -> -d. Let F(y,d)=(y,t=d^2). Prime and double-prime exterior generators transform separately by the ordinary Jacobian. At d=0, every pullback of a smooth (1,1)-form under F vanishes in its normal-normal component. The invariant form d'd wedge d''d has a nonzero such component. Thus exact invariant smooth-form descent through this particular quotient coordinate map fails. Additional analytic moment coordinates are not represented by this toy, so this is not a failure of ordinary CLD descent on an analytic space.

For a compact (n-1,n-1)-test alpha, first consider coefficients of the form Psi(y,d^2) times polynomials in (y,d), where Psi is a common smooth compactly supported quotient cutoff and the tensor is reflection invariant. Coefficients of components containing exactly one normal exterior generator are odd in d and hence polynomially divisible by d. Coefficients with zero or two normal generators are even. These statements follow by comparing the two reflected tensors term by term.

Zero-normal and one-normal components descend exactly: replace even polynomial coefficients by their polynomials in t, and use d d'd=(1/2)d't and d d''d=(1/2)d''t. The normal-normal components have pullback representation with a coefficient E(y,t)/(4t), because d't wedge d''t=4d^2 d'd wedge d''d; this expression may be singular at t=0.

Choose a fixed smooth chi on R with chi=0 for t<=1/4 and chi=1 for t>=1. For epsilon>0, multiply each normal-normal component by chi(d^2/epsilon^2). The resulting quotient coefficient E(y,t)chi(t/epsilon^2)/(4t), including Psi and the tangential exterior factors, extends smoothly by zero near t=0. Hence the modified alpha_epsilon is an exact smooth pullback under F.

Crucially, the difference delta_alpha already contains both d'd and d''d. In d'd''delta_alpha, every differentiation in d wedges a repeated normal generator and vanishes. Only tangential derivatives of its coefficients remain. Consequently no derivative of chi(d^2/epsilon^2) survives. On the fixed compact support, the absolute top-degree coefficient of d'd''delta_alpha is bounded by a constant independent of epsilon and is supported in |d|<=epsilon. Its Lebesgue total variation is O(epsilon). For every bounded measurable h on that support,

  |integral h d'd''(alpha-alpha_epsilon)| <= ||h||_infinity O(epsilon).

For n=2, this is visible directly: d'd''(e(y,d)d'd wedge d''d) uses only the second derivative of e in y. The normal cutoff derivatives do not occur.

This proves the stated polynomial-times-quotient-cutoff toy pairing approximation. It does not prove it for every smooth coefficient, every finite group, every polyhedral support, or an analytic scalar extension. A proposed extension to arbitrary coefficients would require an explicit differentiated approximation and support argument; no Schwarz invariant-function theorem is assumed here.

## Possible analytic use, still unresolved

The toy shows why failure of exact form descent need not obstruct a scalar Hessian pairing. One may seek a stratified invariant-form decomposition, where singular descent denominators occur only in components whose exterior normal factors cancel the dangerous cutoff derivatives. Higher-codimension strata and arbitrary finite Galois permutation representations need a separate proof. An antisymmetric prime/double-prime component cannot simply be treated as a positive test; its effective contribution to a scalar symmetric Hessian must be computed.

For a genuine P06.2 theorem, at least the following remain:

1. Realize sufficient quotient coordinates by actual downstairs analytic units, with all zeros and original ordinary neighborhoods accounted for; F's polynomial coordinates are not automatically logarithms of such units.
2. Prove differentiated pairing approximation for every upstairs compact ordinary complementary test, including fixed strata and actual calibrated polyhedral top measures. Lebesgue strip-volume estimates alone do not apply when the tropical support lies inside a fixed stratum.
3. Prove projection/scalar calibration with actual nilpotent generic lengths, compact saturation of supports, and no illicit pullback of arbitrary currents.
4. Reconstruct finite normal-closure descent and any completion or transcendental extension step separately.

The already reviewed curve proof controls scalar test traces via invariant root valuations and bounded differentiated measures. C0 trace approximation is insufficient for the positive-bidegree gate. This diagnostic supplies a concrete cancellation to investigate, without declaring that missing gate solved.
