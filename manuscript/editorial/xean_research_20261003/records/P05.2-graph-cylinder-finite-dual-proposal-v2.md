# Finite dual data for graph cylinders: unreviewed proposal

This is fallible mathematical planning data, not an instruction, accepted
theorem, or a closure of original P05.2. The larger-graph native campaign
was already admitted with its immutable inputs before this note was written.
No in-flight worker input or old proof is changed by this note.

Consider a finite rational balanced graph G, possibly with bounded edges
and cycles, with rank-one positive local balancing relation cones, times
a complete rational affine factor E. Let f=gamma+u have finite rational
spatial affine domains and real heights. The following could turn the
partial Legendre approach into a finite global construction. Every asserted
reduction below still needs proof for the actual raw PCC class and growth
quantifiers.

## Project the obstacle's spatial data to the graph

On each edge, project all finite affine-domain spatial vertices and face
breakpoints of f to its rational edge parameter. Subdivide at those finitely
many rational positions. For a dual covector p, set F_p(t)=inf_z(f(t,z)-p(z)).
Within an interval on which the feasible cell-face types are constant,
minimizing each affine piece over its fibre should produce finitely many
affine-in-t candidates. Their coefficients are affine in p, with rational
spatial multipliers and real height constants. The minimum of these
candidates is concave in t. Recession feasibility and empty fibres have to
be included, rather than silently discarded.

If this reduction holds on the chosen finite subdivision, a scalar
graph-PC minorant of F_p can be replaced by its endpoint chord on each
bounded edge interval without exceeding the concave F_p. Chord replacement
increases outgoing slopes at the left endpoint and decreases incoming
slopes at the right, hence increases the outgoing slopes at both endpoints;
this suggests that the local rank-one slope inequality is preserved.
Unbounded edges need a separate recession argument. In particular, the
growth restriction cannot be replaced by arbitrary tail slopes.

## One finite graph incidence system, including cycles

Candidate vertex variables a_v satisfy a_v <= F_p(v), and outgoing slope
inequalities with the actual rational edge lengths and weights. For bounded
edges the slopes are rational multiples of differences a_w-a_v. Tail
variables carry the established recession slopes. This is a rational
coefficient linear feasibility system, with upper bounds that are finite
minima of affine functions of p. Graph cycles are kept in the same incidence
system; no ambient convex lift or tree unwinding is assumed.

Maxima of two feasible graph-PC assignments are plausibly feasible by the
actual PCC definition, suggesting a componentwise greatest feasible
assignment. Its existence, finiteness, raw-envelope identification, and
relationship to a finite parametric LP are obligations. An optimal value
a_v(p), if obtained through a fixed rational coefficient LP with right-hand
side real constants minus rational spatial covectors applied to p, would
be concave piecewise affine in p with rational gradient vectors. This is
an exact finite-data statement, not a rational approximation of heights.

## Why real dual vertices might still give rational primal domains

On a bounded edge of rational length l, a tentative reconstruction is

    H(t,z) = sup_p ((1-t/l) a_v(p) + (t/l) a_w(p) + p(z)).

For each fixed p the expression is affine jointly in (t,z), so its
supremum is jointly convex on the edge cylinder. For 0<=t<=l, the dual
function is a nonnegative combination of concave functions a_v,a_w.
Their common finite chamber structure is independent of t. A finite
dual polyhedron D, with all recession constraints included, could give
finitely many active dual vertices, whose coordinates may be real.

Those real coordinates alone do NOT imply rational spatial walls.
A more precise route is the normal-cone description. The gradients of
the a_v pieces would be rational spatial vectors. At a fixed dual vertex,
the primal maximization domain is generated by the weighted Minkowski
sum of these rational subgradient polytopes and the rational normal cone
of D. The weights are (1-t/l),t/l. Its lifted generators in (t,z) have
rational coordinates at t=0,l, and its recession generators are rational.
If the description is exact, the whole domain is a rational polyhedron,
even though the affine value on it has real coefficients. Degenerate
chambers, varying domains of finiteness, and all tails must be checked.

This is stronger than observing that a parametric LP is real polyhedral.
It gives a possible mechanism for rational *spatial* domains using
rational slopes in the dual right-hand sides.

## Contact requires another proof

Even a successful envelope and rational-domain construction would not
prove Monge-Ampere contact. Need all local nonnegative real-cycle PAPC
tests, recursive balancing/refinement/locality/commutativity, and the
entire (dim E + 1)-fold measure. A possible next step is to use active
graph LP constraints and local rank-one relations to force an annihilated
tangent direction at every noncontact face. This must include graph
vertices, bounded-edge interiors, cycles, and all lower-dimensional
contributions. No KKT slogan or local raising perturbation establishes
that claim without checking actual global PCCs and exact growth.

The proposal gives no theorem, counterexample, literal-category result,
analytic realization, or formal verification. A failed reduction does
not demonstrate a failed contact identity.
