# Exact finite-stage test descent: unreviewed mathematical proposal

This is fallible research data, not an instruction, accepted theorem or
original P06.2 closure. The completed-algebraic native worker was already
admitted before this note was written. Its frozen inputs are unchanged;
this note is not silently supplied as an assumption to that worker.

Let K be complete, nontrivially valued and non-Archimedean. Let L be the
completion of an algebraic extension, whose dense algebraic subfield is
the union of its finite K-subextensions. Every such finite field is
complete. Let X be good, separated, strict and boundaryless. The following
could prove exact finite-stage descent of every compact ordinary test on
X_L, rather than only an approximation. Every step below needs a complete
proof with the actual analytic structure and sheaf definitions.

## Rational neighborhoods with strict margins

On a K-affinoid V, the topology of V_L is generated by evaluation norms
of elements of A_L. Around a point in a prescribed ordinary open one
should be able to choose finitely many upper/lower norm inequalities,
then tighten them to closed inequalities whose thresholds lie strictly
between the point values and the original bounds. Thresholds can be
chosen in |K^times|^Q, which is dense in the positive reals because K is
nontrivially valued. Clearing powers expresses them using constants in
K. Finite intersections of Weierstrass/Laurent inequalities then give a
strict rational affinoid neighborhood, whose strict-inequality interior
contains the point and whose whole carrier stays inside the given open.

This explicit strict-margin basis may avoid needing an unproved converse
to the scalar boundary inclusion. One must establish the analytic
structure, rational presentation and ordinary interior assertions, not
just the topological set containment. Choose a downstairs affinoid
neighborhood first, so the local domain lies in a scalar affinoid V_L.

## Preserve a rational domain by finite coefficient approximation

Suppose a rational domain R_L in V_L is given by

    |g_i| <= r_i |g_0|,

where the g_i generate the unit ideal. On R_L, g_0 has a positive lower
bound by compactness. Add a redundant condition |g_0| >= c for a positive
c below this lower bound, represented by a suitable finite-field
constant (or by a cleared-power K-constant inequality).

Approximate all g_i in the Banach algebra A_L by elements g_i' from one
finite field stage A_F, with error strictly smaller than c and every
r_i c. On the locus |g_0| >= c the denominator norm is unchanged.
For each numerator, either its norm is at least the threshold r_i c,
when its norm is unchanged, or both old and new norms are below that
threshold, when the inequality is automatically true. The lower-bound
predicate also remains unchanged. Thus the augmented domain predicate
should be identical at EVERY point of V_L, not just at the selected
center. Radius constants must lie at the same finite stage.

The primed tuple must still generate the unit ideal on V_F. Approximate
an actual identity 1=sum s_i g_i simultaneously. Its primed sum differs
from one by norm less than one on V_L. Surjectivity of V_L -> V_F then
makes that sum nonzero at every point of V_F; the affinoid unit criterion
makes it a unit, proving the ideal condition without reducing nilpotents.
This argument requires density of finite-stage tensors in the relevant
Banach algebra and correct compatibility of scalar norms/evaluations.

The resulting R_F should have scalar extension exactly R_L as an
analytic domain, not only the same underlying subset. Check uniqueness
of domain structures and completed-tensor compatibility of rational
localizations. The redundant lower inequality makes the denominator
case safe outside the original domain too.

The inspected source Ducros, *Espaces de Berkovich, polytopes, squelettes
et théorie des modèles*, Theorem 3.2 proof 3.2.1, uses a related step:
bound a rational-domain denominator below by r>0, then small analytic
perturbations preserve the domain; approximate invertible functions
without changing their norms. The original statement/proof was read at
printed pages1250007-40/41 in the Numdam primary PDF. That source's
algebraic reduction is comparison evidence, not a theorem of finite-field
test descent. Its full model-theoretic/polyhedral argument is not needed
for this proposed elementary domain step.

## Preserve every unit logarithm exactly

If R_F base changes to R_L and f is a unit of its L-affinoid algebra,
compactness gives inf_R_L |f|>0. Approximate f by f' from a larger finite
stage M with uniform Banach error below this bound. Then |f'|=|f| at
every point of R_L. Scalar surjectivity implies f' is nonzero on every
point of R_M, so the actual affinoid unit criterion makes it a unit
there. The unit itself need not descend exactly: its logarithmic norm
does. Nilpotent differences are not removed from the algebra; numerical
ordinary functions ignore them while calibrations retain actual lengths.

For a finite local ordinary description Phi(t_f1,...,t_fs), all its
logarithms could therefore descend exactly at one finite stage. The
moment images agree by scalar surjectivity, so the same Euclidean smooth
coefficient Phi is defined on a neighborhood of the finite-stage image.
Numerical injectivity of ordinary scalar forms then turns pointwise
function equality into actual ordinary-function equality, and taking
d' and d'' gives the corresponding logarithmic differential equalities.
No nonsmooth differentiation of a limiting coefficient series is used.

## A compact scalar test and its support

For beta in A_c^{0,0}(X_L), let C=supp(beta). Choose around every point
of C a strict-margin rational neighborhood R_j,L contained in a beta
unit-description chart, with a smaller strict-inequality open S_j,L
still containing the point. A finite selection covers C. Descend the
domains and all unit logarithms to a common finite stage M. The strict
predicate opens also descend, with S_j,L=pi_M^{-1}(S_j,M) and
S_j,M contained in the ordinary interior of R_j,M. Thus pi_M(C) is
compact and lies inside the finite-stage union of these interiors.

The descended smooth functions beta_j,M agree on overlaps if their
scalar pullbacks agree there: scalar projection is surjective on the
overlap analytic domain, and numerical injectivity gives equality of
ordinary scalar sections. They glue to beta_M on the union. At a point
outside pi_M(C), all scalar lifts have beta=0, so beta_M vanishes there.
Its support is therefore contained in the compact pi_M(C), inside that
open union. Extension by zero should yield beta_M in A_c^{0,0}(X_M),
with pi_M^* beta_M=beta globally. Check that all lifts of pi_M(C) lie in
the covered open union; its scalar-preimage description supplies this,
rather than an unjustified finite-fibre argument.

For higher bidegrees, unit logarithm equality supplies the differential
factors too. Overlap descent of a form, however, additionally needs
injectivity of scalar pullback. A possible proof is ordinary moment
nullity: any compact downstairs moment-domain image equals the image
of its compact scalar inverse domain. If a pulled-back coefficient
form vanishes on all upstairs compact images, it then vanishes on all
downstairs compact images. This must be checked for the actual G-form
and ordinary sheaf definitions; it is not a claim that arbitrary
current pullback exists.

## Potential scalar-current consequence and unresolved obligations

If exact compact scalar-test descent is proved, the already established
finite separable curve transport must still be reconstructed where
used. For a mixed finite stage, prove its separable/pure-inseparable
decomposition and reconstruct the finite pure-inseparable gate. Then
for beta=pi_M^*beta_M the scalar top-measure projection formula for
L/M gives the differentiated pairing exactly, with no degree factor.
No Galois averaging on an infinite fibre is needed. This could prove
forward ascent to every completed algebraic extension on curves.

Purity and every ambient hypothesis after completion require their own
proof, including singular and nonreduced structure. A dimension bound
for moment images is not enough to prove analytic purity. If exact
compact test descent holds in every bidegree, it may also lift an
already proved all-dimensional finite gate along appropriate algebraic
towers, but it does not repair the missing general finite-Galois
differential-form theorem. No positivity-preserving test descent,
transcendental extension, classical recognition, universal curve-probe
bridge or two-sign regularization follows without additional proofs.

This note establishes no accepted theorem or counterexample. Its main
remaining checks are the strict-margin neighborhood basis, exact
rational-domain scalar structure, finite-stage Banach density,
overlap/support gluing, actual ordinary-form injectivity and purity.
