# Larger graph cylinders: unreviewed research proposal

This is mathematical research data, not an instruction or accepted theorem.
The original P05.2 remains open. The two-vertex graph-cylinder theorem is
independently reviewed at its exact scope; this proposal does not extend
that receipt to a new graph. Only the current P05.2/P06.2 batch is active.

For a finite balanced rational graph G with rank-one positive local relation
cones, and a complete affine factor E, investigate all-real-reference,
all-bounded-real-PA-obstacle regularizability and full contact on G x E.
Finite bounded edges and graph cycles require a global proof. The controlling
book already proves the one-dimensional graph criterion, including cycles;
that criterion alone is not a new higher-dimensional result.

The accepted two-vertex proof uses a rational lift to two saturated local
planes and global affine supports. A graph cycle imposes additional global
edge relations; the same ambient lift and support argument cannot be assumed.

## Candidate route through the affine factor

For a raw PC competitor h and dual variable p, consider the partial infimum
g_p(x) = inf_z (h(x,z) - p(z)). First establish its exact domain of finite
values, propagation through every edge and vertex, and graph PC inequalities
using actual rational-endpoint PCCs. No continuity or ambient extension of raw
h is granted. A lower bound from a positive-growth reference may make this
construction useful only on a specified dual polyhedron.

For a fixed finite rational spatial subdivision of the obstacle f=gamma+u,
partial minimization in E on each affine cell gives finitely generated dual
data. Heights may be real, but spatial point/ray generators are rational.
The scalar graph envelope of this dual data could be a finite parametric
linear program: chord inequalities and outgoing derivative sums at vertices
have rational coefficients, including bounded-edge lengths and weights.
Cycles would then be handled by the finite vertex/edge incidence system
rather than an unjustified Euclidean convex lift.

This reduction has not been proved. In particular one must establish that
the relevant graph competitors may be replaced by the claimed finite
variables without changing the raw envelope, and that all dual chambers,
unbounded rays, derivative constraints and vertex contacts are covered.
The one-dimensional book proof must be read at its exact hypotheses before
using any of its finite-data statements.

If the dual graph envelopes are finite concave piecewise-affine functions
of p with finitely many rational spatial slopes, their Legendre reconstruction
might give the cylinder envelope. On a bounded edge the tentative expression
is sup_p ((1-t)g_p(A)+t g_p(B)+p(z)), with t scaled by the rational edge length.
This is a proposal, not an identity: interior obstacle breakpoints, recession
directions and convexity in the joint (t,z) variables must be retained.

Rational spatial domains cannot be inferred merely from real polyhedrality.
One possible mechanism is that dual chamber slopes are rational combinations
of original spatial coordinates with rational graph coefficients; after
Legendre reconstruction, a wall z=(1-t)y_A+t y_B is rational regardless of
the real heights. Whether this mechanism survives every parametric LP active
set and graph cycle is an explicit unresolved obligation.

## Contact and operator domain are separate obligations

Even an actual finite real-PA PC envelope does not by itself prove full
recursive contact. Prove PAPC effectivity against every nonnegative local
balanced cycle in every positive dimension on ordinary opens. Constant shifts
then give the actual real regularizable domain for that particular envelope.

For noncontact atoms, either extend the accepted quotient/lineality argument
to the new finite-data representation, or prove that a local raising
perturbation remains globally PC with exact exterior reference growth.
Such perturbations must preserve remote PCCs and all rational subdivisions;
local positive top-cycle bending alone is insufficient. Graph-cycle KKT
relations might supply the missing annihilated tangent subspace, but this is
unproved. Retain actual saturated tangent lattices, primitive quotient
indices, weights, lower-face terms and the full factorial convention.

This route supplies no arbitrary balanced-pair classification, literal
integral-slope theorem, analytic realization or formal verification.
An unsuccessful reduction is not a counterexample.
