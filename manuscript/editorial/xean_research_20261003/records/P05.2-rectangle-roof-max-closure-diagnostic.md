# Rectangle roofs do not preserve coordinatewise maxima of node values

This is a root mathematical diagnostic, not an independently reviewed paper,
an original-atom solution, or a contact counterexample. It was derived after
admission of the frozen two-graph continuation and was not injected into it.
The example already occurs on one graph times an interval, where the reviewed
global graph-cylinder theorem succeeds by a different construction. It rules
out a particular coarse finite-node reduction, not polyhedral regularity.

Let a rectangle have coordinates `(q,t)` in `[0,1]^2`, with values
`b0,b1` at `(0,0),(0,1)` and `c0,c1` at `(1,0),(1,1)`.
Its convex roof is the lower convex hull of these four lifted points,
equivalently the minimum of their weighted values over convex combinations
with the prescribed position. It is the greatest convex function bounded by
the four corner values. For every `0<t<1`, its inward derivative along the
left side is

\[
 \partial_q^+ H(0,t)=\min\{c_0-b_0,c_1-b_1\}.
\]

To check the formula, subtract the affine left boundary trace
`b0+(b1-b0)t`. A supporting plane containing that side has inward slope at
most both displayed differences. The plane with their minimum is below all
four corners. The lower hull has a face containing the left side and a
right-side corner attaining the minimum; near any relative interior point
of the side that face gives exactly this derivative. Endpoint derivatives
need separate treatment and are not asserted by this formula.

Now use the unit-weight balanced three-ray star with primitive directions
`u1=(1,0), u2=(0,1), u3=(-1,-1)`, and its product with `[0,1]`.
On branch `i`, `q` is distance along `ui`. Put

\[
 s^A=(1/2,-1/2,0),\qquad s^B=(-1/2,1/2,0),
 \qquad A_i(q,t)=1-t+s_i^Aq,\quad B_i(q,t)=t+s_i^Bq.
\]

Both are restrictions of global ambient affine functions, respectively
`1-t+(x-y)/2` and `t-(x-y)/2`. Hence each satisfies every admitted PCC with
equality; neither relies on a separate-convexity assertion. Their maximum
also satisfies every PCC by the elementary maximum inequality.

Take the coordinatewise maximum of the values of `A` and `B` at the
corners of all three branch rectangles. The common left-side corners both
have value `1`. The right-side pairs are

\[
 (3/2,1/2),\quad(1/2,3/2),\quad(1,1).
\]

The corresponding branch convex roofs are

\[
 H_1(q,t)=\max\{1-q/2,1+q/2-t\},\qquad
 H_2(q,t)=\max\{1-q/2,q/2+t\},\qquad H_3(q,t)=1.
\]

These formulas follow by evaluating the two lower supporting planes at
each corner; they agree with the lower triangular hull in each rectangle.
They glue to the constant function `1` on the shared side. At its point
`(v,1/2)`, and for every rational `0<epsilon<1/2`, the three endpoints
`(epsilon ui,1/2)` with coefficients `1/3` form an actual PCC. Their
mean is the centre since `u1+u2+u3=0`, each segment lies in its branch
rectangle, and all endpoints and the centre are rational. But

\[
 H(v,1/2)=1,
 \qquad\frac13\sum_i H_i(\epsilon,1/2)=1-\epsilon/3<1.
\]

Thus the roof of the coordinatewise maximum node data is not PC, although
the roofs of the two original node arrays are the PC affine functions
`A` and `B`. At the shared side their respective weighted sums of inward
slopes are zero; the new roof's sum is `-1`. Consequently the finite
inequalities obtained from
`sum_i min(c_i0-b0,c_i1-b1)>=0` are not closed under coordinatewise
maximum of the node variables. With corner caps equal to the displayed
maximum data, this coarse roof-feasible node set has no greatest element:
an element dominating both arrays would have to equal their coordinatewise
maximum, which fails the PCC. The actual PC maximum of the functions is
perfectly admissible and has trace `max(1-t,t)`, with an additional
midpoint break absent from the coarse roof trace.

For two graphs, a proposed greatest finite-node LP must therefore prove
that it captures additional edge-fibre breaks and all product-node PCCs;
the one-graph incidence argument cannot simply be copied to nodal roofs.
This diagnostic supplies no gamma-growth theorem, global raw-envelope
identity, full recursive measure, negative contact integral, or analytic
realization. It changes no frozen worker, proof, input, source or verdict.
