## Scope and usable boundary

Theorem 1.1 concerns a simple holomorphic bundle over a smooth projective cscK base with discrete automorphisms lifting to the polarization. Its “sufficiently smooth” condition is strict slope semistability with a Jordan--Hölder filtration whose stable equal-slope quotients are locally free. The graded bundle, rather than the original extension, is polystable and admits the attributed Hermite--Einstein connection. The paper claims equivalence between adiabatic slope stability and cscK existence on P(E) for every sufficiently large adiabatic parameter. These hypotheses do not assert general Kähler YTD, stability in big classes, a uniform stability margin along varying classes, or any canonical book frontier's solution.

The appropriate reusable material is the distinction between one fixed class and a family of adiabatic classes; between testing each subbundle eventually and a common eventual threshold for all subbundles; and between eventual positivity and a uniform positive ratio over all test configurations. ## Analytic and construction qualifications

The sequential notes retain several distinct obligations, which do not collectively imply that the main conclusion is false:

- Lemma 3.5's projection calculation loses a squared norm/normalizing factor before claiming an extra power of k; a correct elementary projection inequality alone does not give that gain. Its space of holomorphic fields is not explicitly restricted to Hamiltonian fields, although discreteness of polarized base automorphisms does not exclude every nonpolarized base vector field. Uniform elliptic/spectral bounds and the changing-metric orthogonality in Lemma 3.7 remain inputs needing proof.
- Lemma 4.4's pointwise Weil--Petersson positivity calculation replaces a projective moment evaluation by a trace and discards exact changes before pointwise positivity has been justified. Scalar line-bundle deformations in H1(O) leave projectivizations unchanged; positivity on that full deformation space requires removing these directions or another precise slice. Vanishing of a one-form at a point does not imply vanishing of its derivative there. The extension to the ambient nonintegrable parameter space also requires justification.
- The paper does address the nonintegrable moment-map construction through Dervan--Hallam's universal almost-complex formulation, and addresses nonclosed forms through a Mabuchi transgression. These mechanisms are recorded as supplied approaches, not ignored defects. Positivity and uniform parameter-derivative bounds still depend on the preceding qualifications. The b2 potential has order one, so a blanket O(k^-1) potential assertion needs adjustment.
- The augmented linearization is correctly understood later as a derivative at a fixed reference, despite earlier nonlinear notation. The compact group must be replaced by its complexification for C* subgroups, and scalar automorphisms must be removed before finite stabilizers are claimed. Conditional inverse-function k-power estimates are consistent once the stated inverse/Lipschitz inputs hold.
- “Reflexive subsheaf iff saturated subsheaf” is false without further conditions; saturation implies reflexivity on a smooth base, but O(-D) inside one summand of O² is a reflexive nonsaturated example. Singular subsheaf projectivizations need an actual flat Rees-algebra construction. The smooth subbundle deformation used for the exact diagnostic avoids this issue.
- The simplicity conclusion for a nonzero extension of nonisomorphic stable equal-slope factors has a direct Hom/extension-class proof, recorded in note 21. It does not require the source's incomplete alternative-splitting argument or its impossible-rank short sequence. H2(S tensor Q*)=0 controls the extension direction, not all H2(End E) obstructions.
- The cited Arezzo--Pacard construction requires a spanning and positively balanced moment configuration; a generic spanning condition alone does not supply a positive balance. Sibley's cited preservation of graded objects/polarization stability and Schwarzenberger's cited existence claim have not been freshly verified in those primary texts.
