\subsection*{half open capacity proof}
The set \(A=[0,2)\) is half-open, so we compute its supremum directly.
By \eqref{eq:ch25-segment-measure}, its mass is \(1+f'(2^-)\).
Convexity and the range bound give
\(f'(2^-)\leq(f(4)-f(2))/2\leq1/2\).
For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), the competitor that is minus one on
\([0,2-\varepsilon]\) and then rises affinely to zero at \(4\)
has slope \(1/(2+\varepsilon)\) on its final piece.
It lies in \(\mathcal Q_\Gamma\); its mass on \(A\) is
\(1+1/(2+\varepsilon)\).
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves
\[
 \Capa_\theta\bigl(\{\varphi<\psi\}\bigr)=\tfrac32 .
\]
This uses exact masses of competitors, not weak convergence on the
half-open set.
