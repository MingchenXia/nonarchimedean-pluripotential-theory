## 1. Individual tubes, parameters, and compact tests

The model is regular. Smooth geometrically connected fibres of relative dimension one are geometrically integral, so the special fibre has one generic point and its local ring in the model is a DVR. At a closed residual point p, the residue field extension is finite separable because k is perfect.

The finite unramified extension R_p exists by henselian lifting of finite étale residue algebras. It is a complete local finite étale R-algebra and a DVR, with the same uniformizer; its rank and generic-field degree equal `[kappa(p):k]`. Its map into the completed local ring is uniquely determined by the specified residue map. In particular, this does not require k algebraically closed, nor a choice of ordinary analytic sheets.

Smoothness gives a complete regular local ring of dimension two with maximal ideal generated by pi and a lift w of a residual uniformizer. The induced map `R_p[[w]] -> completed O_(C,p)` is onto by successive approximation, since it identifies residue fields and supplies generators of the maximal ideal. A surjection between these two-dimensional regular local domains has zero kernel: a nonzero prime in the source would have positive height and lower the quotient dimension. This verifies the completed-ring step.

The formal fibre is the open unit disc over K_p. The power-series isomorphism and its inverse converge on every strictly smaller closed disc. The subdomains `|w|^N <= |pi|`, whose radii tend to one, exhaust the disc/tube; the mutually inverse convergent power-series maps identify them with the corresponding formal-fibre subdomains. This is the local formal-fibre argument, not an assertion that the whole tube is one affinoid or that a finite étale cover splits in the ordinary topology.

The product collar is therefore an actual ordinary open K-analytic domain. The preimage of a compact subset of its tropical rectangle is closed in a compact closed polyannulus over K_p and hence compact. Thus every compactly supported target form, including each primitive used to invoke closedness, pulls back with compact analytic support. Extension by zero from the ordinary open collar is legitimate. No properness hypothesis is discarded.

For another formal parameter, its constant coefficient is divisible by pi, its linear coefficient is a unit, and its higher coefficients are bounded by one. Near the outer end, the linear term strictly dominates every other term for all analytic points, not just skeleton points. Hence the two absolute-value coordinates and their actual differential forms coincide on a common outer collar. Henselian uniqueness also handles the coefficient-algebra choice. This proves the claimed parameter and collar independence.

## 2. Finite nonnegative constants without the representation premise

The product form `d'u wedge d''u wedge d's wedge d''s` is a positive top form in the stated convention. Pairing its pullbacks against T defines a positive scalar functional on compactly supported smooth functions on the Euclidean rectangle. A nonnegative smooth function equal to one on any fixed compact set gives the sup-norm bound in the proof, so this functional is a locally finite positive Radon measure.

Deleting the appropriate primed differential yields compactly supported (1,2)-forms whose d' derivatives are the top forms with scalar coefficients partial_u h and partial_s h. Since d'T=0, both distributional derivatives of the scalar measure vanish. Local Euclidean convolution makes the measure a constant density on each ball; an integral-one test identifies the finite limiting constant. Overlapping balls and connectedness of the rectangle give one finite nonnegative number b_T(p). This construction does not slice T or require a common Radon measure dominating unrelated chart coefficients.

The value is determined by any test of integral one and is unchanged on a smaller collar. Together with the parameter comparison above, this supplies genuine individual p-coefficients before introducing a residual rational function. Only d'-closedness is needed for these tests; the additional d''-closedness hypothesis is harmless.

## 3. Every residual rational function lifts; all bad tubes are retained

The local DVR at the generic point of the special fibre surjects onto k(C_0). Every nonzero residual rational function therefore lifts to a unit F of that DVR. It is a meromorphic function on the generic proper curve with vertical valuation zero. This is not a claimed globally invertible lift on the analytic curve.

At p, write F=a/b in the fraction field of the regular local ring. Equality of the vertical valuations allows cancellation of common powers of the prime pi while keeping a and b in that local ring. Their reductions are then both nonzero. If their reduced orders are i and j, their expansions in `R_p[[w]]` have unit coefficients in degrees i and j, coefficients divisible by pi below those degrees, and bounded higher coefficients. On a sufficiently outer annulus, the indicated term is uniquely dominant in each expansion. Hence `|F|=|w|^(i-j)` and `v(F)=ord_p(f)*s` on the whole ordinary collar.

The zeros and poles on the generic curve are finitely many closed points. Their closures are horizontal divisors in the proper regular model and meet the special fibre in finitely many closed points. Let B contain all those specializations. The vertical valuation is zero; normality (or local unique factorization here) then makes F and its inverse regular near every point of the model outside these horizontal divisors. Thus `|F|=1` on the tube of `C_0 minus B`.

Crucially, B can strictly contain the support of div(f). The packet retains residual-order-zero tubes which contain deeper cancelling zeros and poles. On their outer collars, the exact equality is still `v(F)=0`. The example `(T-1)(T-pi)/(T-2pi)` correctly exhibits that issue at the residual point T=0.

## 4. Source cutoff: the critical support and boundary check

Each transition interval `[a_p,b'_p]` lies entirely in a collar where the exact slope formula holds. The smooth source cutoff eta equals one toward the outer end and vanishes on the deeper closed discs containing every zero and pole of F. The function `1-eta_p` is constant near the centre, is smooth on the remaining punctured disc, and has compact support inside the ordinary open tube. Its extension by zero is smooth. Finitely many such extensions construct the global eta.

The support of eta is compact because the curve is proper, and it avoids a neighbourhood of every removed zero and pole. Multiplying by the compact annulus cutoff therefore makes eta*beta a smooth compactly supported (1,2)-form on X. Products of the closed annulus with the proper curve are compact; no assertion that all of X is compact is needed.

The cutoff-error form vanishes before pairing with T. On a transition collar of nonzero residual order m, `|v(F)| >= |m|a_p > epsilon`, so chi(v(F)) is zero. If m=0, the logarithmic function is constant on an ordinary neighbourhood of the transition collar and d''v(F)=0 there. These two cases cover every component of d'eta, including all deeper-cancellation tubes.

There is one interpretation point worth making explicit, but it is not a gap in the proof: `v(F)=0` outside B-tubes need not mean that v(F) is ordinarily locally constant at the good-reduction Gauss point. The support argument instead uses that chi' vanishes on an open interval around zero. Continuity of v(F) on its unit locus then makes the differentiated form vanish on a genuine ordinary neighbourhood of every point with v(F)=0. Consequently no central-face contribution or boundary-supported distribution is being silently discarded.

On the support of eta*d'beta, the remaining portions lie compactly inside the finitely many individual nonzero-slope source collars, and eta is one there. The smooth form itself is thus the finite sum of the zero extensions of those collar restrictions. The decomposition precedes the current pairing. This is why closedness yields individual b_T(p), rather than only an inseparable total coefficient on one target ray.

Differentiation has the displayed sign. The u-derivative term repeats d'u and vanishes; d'd''u and d'd''v(F) vanish on the unit chart. Substituting t=m*s gives two factors m from the two transverse differentials. For either sign of m,

`integral_0^delta m^2 chi'(m*s) ds = m(chi(m*delta)-chi(0)) = -m`.

The negative case is not replaced by its absolute value. Therefore d'-closedness gives exactly `sum_p ord_p(f)*b_T(p)=0`. Changing the lift or the auxiliary cutoffs changes none of the already-defined b_T(p) or residual orders; the same argument applies to every such choice.

## 5. Degree factors, lattice indices, and the conditional interpretation

For the canonical current, the collar over K_p has torus-coordinate calibration one. CLD Remark 14.4.3 explicitly multiplies this calibration by `[K_p:K]` when the same analytic space is viewed over K. Since R_p/R is unramified, this is `[kappa(p):k]`. No further degree factor is inserted in ord_p(f). The coordinates z,w are primitive characters on the actual product torus; no factor of v(pi) appears in the coefficient.

The nonsplit example over R((pi)) checks this normalization: the degree-two point `(T^2+1)` and infinity have divisor relation p-2q and canonical coefficients 2 and 1. The resulting weighted relation is 2-2=0. Declaring two ordinary sheets or assigning coefficient one at both points would be incorrect.

At a Gauss point, the finite constant residue extension remains a field over the purely transcendental residue field of the Gauss norm. This also covers arbitrary real radii: only value-zero monomial ratios contribute to that residue field. There is consequently one source Gauss point above each coordinate pair, not d_p ordinary sheets. CLD's finite-extension skeleton statement is consistent with this argument.

For a nonprimitive coordinate w^e, the top differentials contribute e^2 and the change of variables contributes 1/|e|, so both the pushed coefficient and the canonical coefficient acquire the same factor |e|. Their ratio is unchanged. The principal-divisor integral instead retains signed m, as checked above. These are different operations, and the packet distinguishes them.

Assuming the separate source-representation statement, the collar's moment skeleton is this single injective Gauss plane. Tests supported inside the relative interior of any top cell identify its represented coefficient with the b_T(p) already obtained. An auxiliary target cutoff pulled back through the proper collar map supplies the compact source cutoff required by that representation. The canonical coefficient is d_p; hence the ratio is b_T(p)/d_p. This certifies only the displayed conditional interpretation, not the representation theorem itself or coefficients of other charts/cells.

## 6. Divisor functional and remaining scope

The divisor group is the free abelian group on closed points. Finite coefficients therefore define a finite-valued functional on every divisor, without asserting a finite total measure on all residual points. The established principal relation and nonnegativity meet the proper-curve Riemann-Roch argument: a sufficiently positive H makes every H+nD effective up to linear equivalence when deg(D)=0, for every integer n. Positivity for both signs of n forces lambda(D)=0. Thus b_T(p)=c*deg_k(p), c>=0. No torsion claim about Pic^0 or algebraic closedness is used.

The proof uses the entire proper residual curve and a global annulus coordinate independent of its curve factor. It does not resolve nontrivial normal-bundle transitions in general semistable neighbourhoods, compare all possible current source cells, or prove propagation through arbitrary charts. In particular, equality of these collar ratios does not yet imply T=c*delta_X on the whole product. Positive bidegrees and the other ambient regimes in P21.3 remain outside this result.
