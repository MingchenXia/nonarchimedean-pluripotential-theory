# A dependent-weight obstruction to whole-source outer collars

Written 1 September 2026 in the 31 August dossier batch.
Independent reviewer: /root/p081_weighted_repair_fresh.
Example origin: /root/p082_independent_reaudit; task relayed by /root.

## Verdict and precise claim

The proposed example is sound. It disproves the universal full-inverse-image
outer-collar containment conclusion if the normal-weight independence
hypothesis is simply deleted. The verification below is independent of
L, V, R and every current-representation or residual-functional candidate.
It does not audit the author's full outer-collar proof. Production
verification of this new diagnostic remains pending.

There is a proper strictly semistable model of a smooth projective
K-surface, a whole proper residual curve C, a monomial point xi centered
at its generic point with dependent normal weights, and an ordinary open
neighbourhood W of xi with the following property. For every closed
p in C, every normal interval I containing ell/2 and every nonempty
residual interval J contained in (0,infinity), the full source collar
q_p^{-1}(I times J) is not contained in W. In particular J may be as
shallow as desired.

Normal intervals here are taken in (0,ell), or intersected with this
actual normal range. A normal box around (ell/2,ell/2) gives the same
assertion after restricting to the relation v(x)+v(y)=ell.

## 1. The proper model really exists

Let k be the complex field with the trivial absolute value, let
R=k[[pi]] and K=k((pi)), and fix 0<|pi|<1. Set ell=-log|pi|.
Blow up P^1_R at the special point pi=z=0 in its standard affine chart.
On the z-chart the blowup is

\[
 \operatorname{Spec}R[x,y]/(xy-\pi),\qquad y=z,\quad x=\pi/z.
\]

The other chart has z=pi t and is Spec R[t]. Thus the first chart is
regular at its unique nodal special point: its local maximal ideal is
generated by x,y and its dimension is two. Away from that point its
generic and special smooth loci are regular. The two special components
x=0 and y=0 are reduced and meet transversely. The blowup is integral
and proper over R, and its generic fibre is the original P^1_K.
These are the usual Rees charts, not an existence claim based on
semistable reduction after extending K.

Let this curve model be Y and put

\[
 \mathcal X=Y\times_R\mathbf P^1_R.
\]

Projection to Y is smooth, so the product is regular. It is integral,
proper and flat over R, of relative dimension two, and its generic
fibre is the smooth surface P^1_K times P^1_K. Its special fibre is
reduced strict SNC, with two selected components meeting in

\[
 C=\{\text{node of }Y_k\}\times\mathbf P^1_k
       \simeq\mathbf P^1_k .
\]

The whole tube X_C is the analytic product over K of the open annulus

\[
 A=\{|\pi|<|y|<1\},\qquad x=\pi/y,
\]

and P^1_K analytified. This is an analytic product, not an identification
with a Cartesian product of topological point sets. In particular y and
y^2-pi are actual global analytic functions on X_C.

## 2. The monomial point and its norm

Let z denote the second-factor affine coordinate, now unrelated to the
coordinate used at the original blowup center. Form the Gauss valued
field K(z) with |z|=1, complete it to M, and then put the weighted Gauss
norm of radius r=|pi|^{1/2} on M[y,y^{-1}]. The restriction defines an
original-K analytic point xi of X_C.

Every nonzero element of k(z) has norm one. Together with
|x|=|y|=r<1 this shows that the reduction of xi is the generic point
of C: no nonzero residual function of z vanishes there. Equivalently
this is the monomial valuation on the two regular normal parameters
x,y of the local ring at eta_C, with

\[
 v_\xi(x)=v_\xi(y)=\ell/2.
\]

These normal weights are Q-linearly dependent. By the defining Gauss
formula, for Laurent polynomials in y the norm is the maximum of the
weighted coefficient norms. Therefore

\[
 |y^2-\pi|(\xi)=\max(r^2,|\pi|)=|\pi|.
\]

Equal norms of the two terms do not force cancellation at a Gauss
point. They are distinct Laurent monomials. In the local normal
description they are y^2 and xy, also distinct monomials. There is
no relation y=x at xi.

Consequently

\[
 W=\{q\in X_C:|y^2-\pi|(q)>|\pi|/2\}
\]

contains xi. It is an ordinary open neighbourhood because evaluation
of the norm of an analytic function is continuous. The divisor by
2 here is division of positive real numbers; it is not the
non-Archimedean norm of the scalar 2.

## 3. Excluded points over the original K

Every closed point of C is k-rational. At an arbitrary such p choose
the second-factor parameter w=z-c for a finite p=c, or w=1/z at
infinity. The completed p-tube has actual equation

\[
 xy=\pi,\qquad |x|<1,\ |y|<1,\ |w|<1.
\]

Its punctured version P_p removes w=0. Define

\[
 q_p:P_p\longrightarrow(0,\ell)\times(0,\infty),\qquad
 q_p(q)=(v_q(y),v_q(w)).
\]

Fix ANY real s>0. Let a be an indeterminate with a^2=pi and put
L=k((a)), viewed as the quadratic valued extension of K through
pi maps to a^2. Thus |a|=|pi|^{1/2}. Equip L(W) with its Gauss
valuation of radius |W|=exp(-s), and denote its completion by H_s.
This construction is valid for every positive real radius, not only
one in the value group.

The coordinate assignments

\[
 x\longmapsto a,\qquad y\longmapsto a,\qquad w\longmapsto W
\]

define a multiplicative seminorm on the ORIGINAL K-coordinate algebra
by evaluating in H_s and taking its absolute value. On a sufficiently
large closed polydisc still strictly inside the unit polydisc this is
a bounded seminorm, so it defines an actual K-analytic point q_{p,s}.
The relation xy=pi is satisfied. Formal series of the completed
p-tube converge under the same assignments because all three coordinate
norms are strictly less than one. Thus the point lies in the actual
p-tube, not merely in a proposed tropical model.

Its completed residue field is H_s: the coordinate images generate
the dense subfield K(a,W)=L(W). This explicitly describes the point
over K. One could equivalently view an L-point of the first factor
and a Gauss point of the second over a common complete valued field,
but no current is pulled back to L, and no current base change is
needed anywhere in this argument.

The exact values are

\[
 v(x)(q_{p,s})=v(y)(q_{p,s})=\ell/2,\qquad
 v(w)(q_{p,s})=s,\qquad
 |y^2-\pi|(q_{p,s})=0.
\]

As w is nonzero, q_{p,s} belongs to P_p. Since s>0 it reduces to p,
including the point at infinity when its local coordinate is used.
But q_{p,s} does not belong to W.

## 4. All quantifiers and the skeleton distinction

Let I contain ell/2 and let J be any nonempty interval in
(0,infinity). Pick s in J. Then

\[
 q_{p,s}\in q_p^{-1}(I\times J)\setminus W.
\]

This holds for EVERY closed p, and therefore for some p outside
any prescribed finite exceptional set. No choice of a sufficiently
small positive residual interval or sufficiently small normal interval
around ell/2 can restore whole-source containment.

The obstruction is invisible if one checks only the canonical
coordinate-moment skeleton. Indeed the Gauss point in the (y,w)
coordinates at ANY tropical pair (u,s) satisfies

\[
 |y^2-\pi|=\max(\exp(-2u),|\pi|)\ge|\pi|>|\pi|/2.
\]

All those skeletal points lie in W. The points q_{p,s}, with identical
normal and residual radii at u=ell/2, lie outside W and are not Gauss
points for the normal coordinate: they annihilate y^2-pi. This is
precisely an entire-inverse-image issue, not a failure of a claim
about skeletal points alone.

## 5. What this does and does not establish

The bounded diagnostic confirms the proposed example without correcting
its mathematical claim. It shows that normal independence cannot be
deleted from a universal all-source outer-collar assertion of this form.
It does not claim that independence is necessary at every individual
point, or that no weaker suitable hypothesis could replace it.

There is no current T in the construction, no current counterexample,
no L/V/R input, and no conclusion about P21.3 or all-chart rigidity.
The author's eventual complete outer-collar proof still requires its
own independent exact-byte and production reviews. No such wider review
or acceptance is conferred by this diagnostic.
