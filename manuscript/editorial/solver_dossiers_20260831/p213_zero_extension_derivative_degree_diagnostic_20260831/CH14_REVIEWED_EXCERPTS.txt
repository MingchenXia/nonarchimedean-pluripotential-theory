 380 | \subsection{Extension by zero and removable sets}
 381 | \label{sec:ch14-extension}
 382 | 
 383 | The following lemma is the first place at which the non-Archimedean theory
 384 | departs sharply from the complex one.  Its hypothesis is a statement about
 385 | \emph{tropical} dimension, and it will be seen in
 386 | \Cref{sec:ch14-compactness} to make many sets removable that are not removable
 387 | over $\mathbf C$.
 388 | 
 389 | \begin{lemma}
 390 | \label{lem:ch14-extension-by-zero}
 391 | Let $U\subset X$ be open, $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ the inclusion, and
 392 | $T\in D_{r,s}(U)$.  Suppose that the local tropical dimension satisfies
 393 | \[
 394 |  d_{\trop}(x)<\max\{r,s\}\qquad\text{for every }x\in X\setminus U .
 395 | \]
 396 | Then every smooth compactly supported $(r,s)$-form on $X$ has support
 397 | contained in $U$, the map $\alpha\mapsto\alpha|_U$ is an isomorphism of
 398 | topological vector spaces $A_{X,c}^{r,s}\to A_{U,c}^{r,s}$, and consequently
 399 | restriction
 400 | \[
 401 |  D_{r,s}(X)\longrightarrow D_{r,s}(U)
 402 | \]
 403 | is an isomorphism, with inverse $T\mapsto j_!T$ defined by
 404 | $\langle j_!T,\alpha\rangle=\langle T,\alpha|_U\rangle$.
 405 | \end{lemma}
 406 | 
 407 | \begin{proof}
 408 | Let $\alpha\in A_{X,c}^{r,s}$ and let $x\in X\setminus U$.  By
 409 | \Cref{prop:analytic-tropical-dimension} the local tropical dimension at $x$
 410 | is computed on a tropical chart, and by the dimension vanishing recalled in
 411 | \Cref{chap:cld-forms} (see the remark following
 412 | \Cref{prop:cld-form-pullback}) a superform of bidegree $(r,s)$ restricted to a
 413 | polyhedron of dimension $<\max\{r,s\}$ vanishes, because one of its two
 414 | alternating factors has degree exceeding the dimension.  Hence $\alpha$
 415 | vanishes identically on a neighbourhood of $x$.  As $x$ was arbitrary,
 416 | $\Supp\alpha\subset U$.
 417 | 
 418 | Therefore restriction $A_{X,c}^{r,s}\to A_{U,c}^{r,s}$ is a bijection with
 419 | inverse $j_!$; both directions are continuous because a chart datum on one
 420 | side is a chart datum on the other with the same seminorms.  Dualizing gives
 421 | the assertion about currents; the formula for $j_!T$ is the transpose of the
 422 | inverse map.
 423 | \end{proof}

 425 | \begin{corollary}
 426 | \label{cor:ch14-rigid-invisible}
 427 | Let $X$ be a good $K$-analytic space of pure dimension $n$, and let
 428 | $Z\subset X$ be a closed weakly analytic subset with
 429 | $\dim Z<\max\{r,s\}$.  Then restriction
 430 | $D_{r,s}(X)\to D_{r,s}(X\setminus Z)$ is an isomorphism; equivalently, no
 431 | nonzero current of bidimension $(r,s)$ is supported in $Z$.  In particular,
 432 | on the analytification of a smooth projective curve, no nonzero current of
 433 | bidimension $(1,1)$ is supported on the set of rigid points.
 434 | \end{corollary}
 435 | 
 436 | \begin{proof}
 437 | A weakly analytic subset of dimension $d$ has tropical dimension $\le d$ at
 438 | each of its points by \Cref{prop:analytic-tropical-dimension}, so
 439 | \Cref{lem:ch14-extension-by-zero} applies with $U=X\setminus Z$.  For the last
 440 | sentence one must not treat the full rigid locus as a closed weakly analytic
 441 | subset; it need not be one.  Instead, if a current $T$ of
 442 | bidimension $(1,1)$ were supported on the rigid points, take
 443 | $U=X\setminus\Supp T$.  The complement is closed, every one of its points is
 444 | rigid, and hence it has local tropical dimension $0$ pointwise.  Thus
 445 | \Cref{lem:ch14-extension-by-zero} makes restriction to $U$ injective; since
 446 | $T|_U=0$, one has $T=0$.
 447 | \end{proof}
 448 | 
 449 | \begin{warning}{Removability is not a positivity theorem}
 450 | \Cref{lem:ch14-extension-by-zero} is a statement about \emph{all} currents of
 451 | a given bidimension, and its hypothesis is a dimension inequality that fails
 452 | as soon as $\max\{r,s\}=0$.  It says nothing about bidimension $(0,0)$
 453 | currents, that is, about measures: a Dirac mass at a rigid point is a
 454 | perfectly good positive current of bidimension $(0,0)$.  Nor does it say that
 455 | a current on $X\setminus Z$ of bidimension $(0,0)$ extends.  Extension
 456 | theorems in the borderline case require growth or positivity hypotheses; see
 457 | \Cref{chap:cld-poincare-lelong,chap:cld-bedford-taylor}.

2191 | 
2192 | Currents form a sheaf on locally differentially separated tropical spaces,
2193 | hence on the analytic spaces considered here.  The support $\Supp(T)$ is the
2194 | complement of the largest open set on which $T$ vanishes.
2195 | 
2196 | \begin{proposition}
2197 | \label{prop:form-restriction-dimension-gap}
2198 | Let $i:Z\hookrightarrow X$ be an analytic subspace.  If the local tropical
2199 | dimension of $Z$ is everywhere smaller than $\max\{r,s\}$, then
2200 | $i^*\alpha=0$ for every $\alpha\in A_X^{r,s}$.
2201 | \end{proposition}
2202 | \begin{proof}
2203 | After passing to a tropical chart, the pullback is the restriction of a
2204 | superform to a polyhedron of dimension smaller than $r$ or smaller than
2205 | $s$.  Its alternating $r$- or $s$-covector factor therefore restricts to
2206 | zero.
2207 | \end{proof}
2208 | 
2209 | This restriction statement does not say that $\alpha$ vanishes on a
2210 | neighborhood of $Z$.  Nor does it imply that a current on $X\setminus Z$
2211 | extends, or that an extension is unique: even when an extension exists, one
2212 | may sometimes add a nonzero current supported on $Z$.  Removability requires
2213 | additional hypotheses, such as the precise growth, positivity, or canonical
2214 | extension results used in
2215 | \Cref{chap:cld-poincare-lelong,chap:cld-bedford-taylor}.

2217 | \subsection{Support theorems}
2218 | \label{sec:ch14-support-theorems}
2219 | 
2220 | We collect the statements that let one recognize a current from its support.
2221 | The first is the exact analogue of the classical fact that a distribution
2222 | supported at a point is a finite combination of derivatives of a Dirac mass;
2223 | the non-Archimedean version is stronger, because \emph{no} derivative of a
2224 | Dirac mass survives in high bidimension.
2225 | 
2226 | \begin{theorem}
2227 | \label{thm:ch14-support-dimension}
2228 | Let $X$ be pure $n$-dimensional and let $Z\subset X$ be closed with
2229 | $d_{\trop}(x)<\max\{r,s\}$ for every $x\in Z$.  Then no nonzero current of
2230 | bidimension $(r,s)$ has support contained in $Z$.  Equivalently, restriction
2231 | $D_{r,s}(X)\to D_{r,s}(X\setminus Z)$ is injective, and by
2232 | \Cref{lem:ch14-extension-by-zero} it is even bijective.
2233 | \end{theorem}
2234 | 
2235 | \begin{proof}
2236 | Let $\Supp T\subset Z$ and let $\alpha\in A_{X,c}^{r,s}$.  By
2237 | \Cref{lem:ch14-extension-by-zero} the form $\alpha$ has support in
2238 | $X\setminus Z$, hence disjoint from $\Supp T$, so
2239 | $\langle T,\alpha\rangle=0$ by \Cref{def:ch14-support}.
2240 | \end{proof}

2243 | \label{thm:ch14-support-pushforward}
2244 | Let $g:Y\to X$ be a morphism of topologically separated analytic spaces and

2280 | Let $g:Y\to X$ be topologically proper.  For a current $T$ on $Y$, define
2281 | \[
2282 |                   \langle g_*T,\alpha\rangle
2283 |                     =\langle T,g^*\alpha\rangle.
2284 | \]

2287 | \begin{proposition}
2288 | \label{prop:current-pushforward-formal}
2289 | Proper pushforward is weakly continuous, commutes with $J$, $\dprime$, and
2290 | $\ddprime$, and satisfies
2291 | \[
2292 |               g_*(T\wedge g^*\alpha)=g_*T\wedge\alpha
2293 | \]
2294 | for every smooth form $\alpha$.
2295 | \end{proposition}
2296 | \begin{proof}
2297 | Every formula follows by evaluation on a compactly supported test form and
2298 | functoriality of pullback.  Properness ensures that the pulled-back test form
2299 | meets $\Supp(T)$ in a compact set.
2300 | 
2301 | In detail.  \emph{$g_*T$ is a current.}  By
2302 | \Cref{prop:cld-form-pullback} the map $g^*$ is continuous
2303 | $A_X^{r,s}\to g_*A_Y^{r,s}$, and topological properness makes it carry
2304 | $A_{X,c}^{r,s}$ into $A_{Y,c}^{r,s}$ continuously: the preimage of a compact
2305 | set is compact, and a chart datum on $X$ pulls back to one on $Y$ with the
2306 | same seminorm estimates, because $g^*$ of a chart representative is the
2307 | representative for the composed moment map.  Hence the transpose is defined,
2308 | and it preserves relative order.  \emph{Weak continuity} is immediate, the
2309 | weak topology being that of pointwise convergence and $g_*$ being defined by
2310 | composition.
2311 | 
2312 | \emph{Commutation with $J$.}  $g^*J=Jg^*$ on forms
2313 | (\Cref{prop:cld-form-pullback}), so
2314 | $\langle g_*JT,\alpha\rangle=\langle JT,g^*\alpha\rangle
2315 | =\langle T,J^{-1}g^*\alpha\rangle=\langle T,g^*J^{-1}\alpha\rangle
2316 | =\langle Jg_*T,\alpha\rangle$.  In particular the pushforward of a symmetric
2317 | current is symmetric.
2318 | 
2319 | \emph{Commutation with $\dprime$.}  Let $T$ have bidegree $(u,v)$ on $Y$;
2320 | then $g_*T$ has the same bidegree if $\dim Y=\dim X$, and in general the
2321 | bidimension is preserved, so the sign $(-1)^{u+v+1}$ of
2322 | \eqref{eq:ch14-dprime-sign} is the same on both sides by the parity remark
2323 | after \Cref{lem:ch14-module-leibniz}.  Since $g^*\dprime=\dprime g^*$,
2324 | \[
2325 |  \langle g_*\dprime T,\alpha\rangle
2326 |  =\pm\langle T,g^*\dprime\alpha\rangle
2327 |  =\pm\langle T,\dprime g^*\alpha\rangle
2328 |  =\langle \dprime g_*T,\alpha\rangle
2329 | \]
2330 | with the same sign throughout.  The case of $\ddprime$ follows by conjugating
2331 | with $J$.
2332 | 
2333 | \emph{Projection formula.}  For a test form $\beta$,
2334 | \[
2335 |  \langle g_*(T\wedge g^*\alpha),\beta\rangle
2336 |  =\langle T\wedge g^*\alpha,g^*\beta\rangle
2337 |  =\langle T,g^*\alpha\wedge g^*\beta\rangle
2338 |  =\langle T,g^*(\alpha\wedge\beta)\rangle
2339 |  =\langle g_*T\wedge\alpha,\beta\rangle,
2340 | \]
2341 | using multiplicativity of $g^*$.  This is the current-level form of
2342 | \Cref{cor:ch11-projection-formula}.
2343 | 
2344 | \emph{Relaxing properness.}  If $\Supp T$ is $g$-compact, meaning that
2345 | $\Supp T\cap g^{-1}(C)$ is compact for every compact $C\subset X$, then for
2346 | $\alpha$ compactly supported the intersection
2347 | $\Supp T\cap\Supp g^*\alpha$ is compact, and
2348 | \Cref{lem:ch14-phi-support} gives a meaning to
2349 | $\langle T,g^*\alpha\rangle$.  All the formulas above persist verbatim, with
2350 | the same proofs, since each is an identity between pairings against forms
2351 | whose supports meet $\Supp T$ compactly.
2352 | \end{proof}
