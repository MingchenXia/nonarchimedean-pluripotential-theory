functions in prop:ch20-model-max. The failure is an elementary
counterexample within the exact standing BFJ hypotheses, even on P1.

FAIL also for the upward-directedness assertion in
thm:ch20-model-envelope, with its stated real-model admissible family.
The same example gives a continuous bounded obstacle for which no
admissible common upper bound exists.

The Chapter 21 BFJ psh maximum statement is not disproved. Its written
proof invokes the false real-model claim, but the statement is the
primary BFJ Proposition 7.4 in the exact version listed in the ledger.
Section 5 below gives
a complete repair route, preserving arbitrary real closed theta with ample
generic class. It never asserts that the maximum of real model functions
is a real model function.

This is an independent check of the existing book proof and the
counterexample proposed by another agent, not a novelty claim or a
production-verified replacement theorem. The density/replacement argument

## 2. Why a real model function has only rational edge breakpoints

Fix the unit SNC edge whose node has completed local ring
\(\mathbf C[[z,u]]\), with \(t=zu\). Parameterize its monomial valuations
by
\[
             v_s(z)=s,\quad v_s(u)=1-s,\quad 0\leq s\leq1.
\]
For a regular local function with expansion
\(f=\sum_{p,q\geq0}c_{pq}z^pu^q\), the defining monomial valuation is
\[
        v_s(f)=\min_{c_{pq}\ne0}\{ps+q(1-s)\}.
\]
This is precisely the primary BFJ construction (3.4), not a
no-cancellation assumption at an arbitrary analytic point.

Only finitely many coordinatewise minimal pairs in the support matter:
the monomial ideal generated by the support is finitely generated, or
equivalently the support has a finite set of minimal elements in N^2.
Every other exponent is coordinatewise at least one of these, hence has
no smaller value on the entire edge. Therefore this minimum is a finite
minimum of integral affine functions. A vertical fractional ideal can be
made integral by multiplying by a power of t; its logarithm on the edge
is consequently a finite maximum of integral affine functions, up to an
integer constant. All breakpoints are rational, since equality between
two distinct integral affine functions occurs at a rational parameter.

BFJ Proposition 2.2 represents every integral model function as a
difference of logarithms of vertical ideals on this fixed model.
Clearing denominators handles rational model functions. Thus each
rational model function restricts to an affine function on each interval
of a finite rational subdivision of this edge.

Finally, a real model function is a finite real linear combination of
such rational functions. Take the union of their finite rational
breakpoint sets. Each summand, and therefore the sum, is affine on every
complementary interval. Real coefficients can change slopes and values,
but cannot create a breakpoint outside that finite rational set.

This argument applies to model functions determined on arbitrary higher
models, not only the displayed SNC model. Hence a kink at an irrational
interior point cannot be repaired by passing to another algebraic model.
BFJ Corollary 3.13's first assertion is consistent with this calculation;
its bare words "piecewise affine" alone would not establish the needed
rational-breakpoint restriction. The integral monomial calculation does.

## 3. Explicit nef model realization of the counterexample

Let
\[
 \rho:\mathcal X=\operatorname{Bl}_{(t,z)}\mathbf P^1_R
                       \longrightarrow\mathbf P^1_R
\]
be the blowup of the rational point z=0 on the special fibre. Denote the
strict transform of the special fibre by C and the exceptional curve by E.
The model is regular, flat and projective, with SNC special fibre
\[
                    F=C+E,\qquad b_C=b_E=1.
\]
Both components are P1_C. At their intersection the equation is t=zu,
so they meet transversely in one rational point. The exceptional
conormal bundle is O_E(1), whence E^2=-1. Since F is principal,
\(F\cdot C=F\cdot E=0\); therefore
\[
               C\cdot E=1,\qquad C^2=E^2=-1.
\]

Let H denote the pullback of O(1) from P1_R. It has degrees
\(H\cdot C=1\) and \(H\cdot E=0\). The actual line bundle
\[
          \mathcal L=\rho^*\mathcal O(2)\otimes\mathcal O(-E)
\]
has degrees
\[
                     (\mathcal L\cdot C,\mathcal L\cdot E)=(1,1).
\]
These are every vertical irreducible-curve test, so the model class
\(\theta=c_1(\mathcal L)\) is semipositive. Its generic class is O(2),
which is ample. With multiplicities and residue degrees both one,
the associated reference measure is \(\delta_{x_C}+\delta_{x_E}\).

Put \(g=\varphi_E\). Then \(g\in\mathcal D(X)\), and on the skeleton
\[
                    g(v_s)=s,\qquad g(x_C)=0,\quad g(x_E)=1.
\]
The global bound \(0\leq g\leq1\) follows from the effectiveness of E
and F-E=C. Fix any irrational \(a\in(0,1)\), and set
\[
              f_0=0,\qquad f_1=g-a=\varphi_{E-aF}.
\]
Both belong to \(\mathcal D_{\mathbf R}(X)\). Their curvature classes
are nef:
\[
 \theta+dd^c f_0=[2H-E]\quad\hbox{has degrees }(1,1),
 \qquad
 \theta+dd^c f_1=[2H]\quad\hbox{has degrees }(2,0).
\]
The real constant contributes no curvature because F is principal.
Thus these are genuine real theta-psh model functions on one model,
not merely abstract graph functions with an unspecified realization.

Their pointwise maximum h restricts to
\[
                     h(v_s)=\max\{0,s-a\}.
\]
It has left slope zero and right slope one at the irrational point a.
By Section 2, \(h\notin\mathcal D_{\mathbf R}(X)\). This disproves the
maximum clause of prop:ch20-model-max, while leaving its real convexity
clause intact.

The example is not a counterexample to psh maximum stability. If
\(a_j\in\mathbf Q\) tends to a, then
\(h_j=\max\{0,g-a_j\}\) is a rational theta-psh model function by the
valid BFJ Proposition 5.8, and
\[
                       \|h_j-h\|_\infty\leq|a_j-a|\longrightarrow0.
\]
The retraction inequality and every skeletal approximation condition pass
to this uniform limit, so h is BFJ theta-psh and continuous.

Nor does the counterexample contradict the componentwise vertex maximum
claim prop:ch20-curve-lattice. The componentwise maximum is the vector
\((0,1-a)\); its affine interpolation is \((1-a)s\), not h.
At \(s=a\) the interpolation is \(a(1-a)>0=h(a)\). The corresponding
real model divisor is \((1-a)E\), whose theta-degrees are
\((2-a,a)\), again positive. Vertexwise maximum and pointwise maximum
of the interpolated functions must not be identified.

## 4. Exact failures in the written argument and its consumers

### 4.1 Chapter 20 maximum proposition

At lines 1917--1975 the rational proof is followed by the claim that real
model functions follow by rational approximation in a common
finite-dimensional space and closedness of the nef cone.

The inputs can be approximated on one common model. Their rational
maxima, however, need successively different subdivisions. In the example
these subdivisions acquire vertices at \(a_j\), approaching irrational a.
There is no single algebraic model determining the limit h, so no
finite-dimensional nef cone contains all the claimed limit data.
Closedness of a nef cone cannot establish membership of the limiting
function in \(\mathcal D_{\mathbf R}(X)\).

The rational proof can use closedness legitimately: the rational maximum
is known beforehand to be a rational model function. One can fix its
determining blowup, reflect nefness from each auxiliary domination to
that model, and let the ample perturbations tend to zero there.

### 4.2 The real-model obstacle family is not upward directed

In def:ch20-model-envelope and thm:ch20-model-envelope, the admissible
family is explicitly
\[
 {\cal A}_{\mathbf R}(u)=
 \{f\in\mathcal D_{\mathbf R}(X):
                     \theta+dd^cf\geq0,\ f\leq u\}.
\]
Take the continuous bounded obstacle u=h from Section 3.
Both f0 and f1 belong to this family. If it had an element w dominating
both, then
\[
                       h=\max(f_0,f_1)\leq w\leq h,
\]
forcing w=h, impossible since h is not a real model function.
Thus the upward-directedness statement itself is false, not merely its
proof at lines 2298--2304.

Other purely order/convexity properties of that envelope are not refuted
by this argument. The theorem as a whole nevertheless contains a false
item and cannot be treated as established unchanged.

### 4.3 Chapter 21 maximum proof

The definition at def:ch21-bfj-psh uses unqualified theta-psh model
functions, with the real convention inherited from Chapter 20.
In thm:ch21-stability, item itm:ch21-stab:max, lines 2032--2041 explicitly
claim the maximum of two real model approximants is a model function,
by the failed proposition. This proof step is invalid. The real constants,
convex combinations, and linear curvature calculations are not refuted.

The real extension of the skeletal-properties proof at lines 825--832
also points to the final paragraph of the failed maximum proof.
The density argument below supplies a legitimate replacement for that
approximation interface, but this bounded audit does not certify every
other assertion in Chapter 21.

Related summaries occur in Chapter 20's orientation and toric paragraph,
and Chapter 21's orientation. Any claim that the pointwise maximum merely
needs a model refinement must retain rational inputs. Already on a curve,
a rational interior kink needs refinement; an irrational kink cannot be
made into a model vertex in this category.

### 4.4 Chapter 24 model maximum principle

At prop:model-maximum-principle, lines 2329--2394, the inputs are
unqualified theta-psh model functions. The proof at lines 2340--2343
asserts that their maximum sigma is a model function, then chooses one
determination on which both inputs and sigma are affine on every face.
For the real inputs in Section 3 this determination does not exist.
Moreover, an operator defined only for model potentials cannot at this
checkpoint be applied to sigma without a separate extension interface.

Restricting this model checkpoint to rational inputs restores the specific
maximum/refinement step. A full bounded-psh locality statement requires its
own approximation argument; it is not certified merely by renaming sigma.
The directly following open-set locality corollary
cor:ch24-model-locality-open has a separate proof using common models of
the actual input functions, without taking their maximum. This audit does
not refute that separate real-model locality argument. The dependency
summary at line 3454 must also track the rational maximum convention.

### 4.5 Chapter 26 upper truncation in bounded energy

At prop:bounded-energy-identities, lines 836--843, the proof replaces a
decreasing model approximation by
\(\min\{\varphi_\alpha,\sup\varphi+1\}\) and cites maximum stability
and constant stability. Neither citation implies preservation of psh by
minimum. Even two rational theta-psh model functions can have a non-psh
minimum: in Section 3 take a=1/2. The function
\(\min\{0,g-1/2\}\) is a rational model function, but its restriction
to the original theta-determining edge has slope 1 and then slope 0.
It is not convex, contradicting the necessary BFJ Proposition 5.9(i).
For real inputs even model membership under minimum can fail.

The bounded-net step has an elementary repair without any truncation.
If continuous functions \(\varphi_\alpha\) decrease pointwise to bounded
phi, put \(B=\sup\varphi+1\). The open sets
\(\{\varphi_\alpha<B\}\) cover compact X. A finite subcover and
directedness supply an index alpha0 such that every later term is <B
everywhere. All terms also satisfy \(\varphi_\alpha\geq\varphi\).
Restrict to this cofinal tail, then shift all potentials by one constant.
If rational model approximants are required, take a rational upper bound
larger than B and shift by that rational constant. This repairs only the
uniform-boundedness preparation, not the chapter's other energy inputs.

### 4.6 Chapter 30 pullback, bounded approximation, and finite descent

The proof of lem:ch30-pullback-model-calculus, lines 354--361, asserts
that the pointwise maximum of real model functions remains a model
function. This is false already for the identity map and Section 3.
The displayed equality expressing composition commuting with maximum
is nevertheless an equality of functions for all real-valued inputs.
One must separate that pointwise equality from model membership; the
latter is valid for rational inputs, or when independently known.
Linear pullback of real vertical Cartier divisors is not refuted.

At lines 831--838, the bounded projection proof uses the replacement
\(\max\{\varphi_i^\alpha,\inf\varphi_i\}\). This is identically
\(\varphi_i^\alpha\), since a decreasing approximation already satisfies
\(\varphi_i^\alpha\geq\varphi_i\geq\inf\varphi_i\).
It is unnecessary and does not supply a uniform upper bound. Such a
bound follows after restricting to any fixed cofinal tail, since its
terms are bounded above by one fixed continuous model function. The
compactness argument in Section 4.5 also gives an explicit bound.

At sec:ch30-descent-limits, lines 2833--2838, finite minima are used to
assert monotonicity of averaged model approximants. This is invalid as a
positivity argument, even for rational inputs, by Section 4.5's example.
It is also unnecessary: if \(\psi_\alpha\) is decreasing and G is finite,
then
\[
 \overline\psi_\alpha=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g^*\psi_\alpha
\]
is decreasing on the same directed set. The finite average commutes with
the pointwise decreasing limit when that limit is finite, and also when
all summands decrease to the same invariant value -infinity. Finite
averaging preserves rational model membership and nefness.

The conditional descent argument must explicitly use rational
approximants: def:ch30-model-descent-property, lines 2610--2621, only
asserts surjectivity for D_Q. Its continuous proof likewise currently
calls approximants unqualified and then invokes rational descent.
Section 5.1 supplies rational uniform approximation in the ample setting
for that continuous interface. For singular descent one needs a valid
decreasing rational regularization theorem; arbitrary independent
rational approximations do not automatically preserve monotonicity.
Given that theorem and the stipulated rational model-psh descent, finite
averaging preserves order and the decreasing-limit argument is available
without minima. This audit does not certify the separate ramified,
infinite-extension, or arbitrary-field descent claims.

## 5. Full-scope repair route for the BFJ psh maximum theorem

Retain exactly the original BFJ signature and let theta be an arbitrary
real closed model form with ample generic numerical class. No rationality
or semipositivity of theta itself is assumed.

### 5.1 Rational theta-nef model functions uniformly approximate real ones

Let \(f=\varphi_D\in\mathcal D_{\mathbf R}(X)\) be theta-nef, and fix
\(\epsilon>0\). By BFJ Proposition 5.2, take a sufficiently high common
determination \(\mathcal Y\) of theta and f admitting a real vertical
model potential p with \(\theta+dd^cp\) ample on \(\mathcal Y\).
The proof of Proposition 5.2 constructs exactly such a real divisor.
More explicitly, its Corollary 1.5 gives an ample extension of the chosen
real generic line bundle on every sufficiently high vertical blowup.
Choose the final model high enough also to determine D and theta. Its
ample extension differs from the pulled-back representative of theta by
a real vertical Cartier divisor P; p is its model function. This is a
fresh ample extension on the final model, not an assertion that pullback
of an ample class stays ample under a further blowup. The pullback of
the original nef class theta+[D] does remain nef.
If a rational p is wanted, openness of the ample cone allows a rational
approximation on that same model; the following argument needs only
the real p.

For \(0<\delta<1\), set
\[
 f_\delta=(1-\delta)f+\delta p,\qquad
 \theta+dd^cf_\delta
    =(1-\delta)(\theta+dd^cf)+\delta(\theta+dd^cp).
\]
The class on the right is nef plus ample, hence ample. Choose delta so
that \(\|f_\delta-f\|_\infty<\epsilon/2\).

The real vertical divisor defining \(f_\delta\) lies in the real span
of finitely many integral Cartier divisors on \(\mathcal Y\).
Approximate its coefficients by rationals. The map to
\(N^1(\mathcal Y/R)\) is linear, and the ample cone is open, so sufficiently
close rational coefficients give a divisor \(D'\) such that
\(\theta+dd^c\varphi_{D'}\) is still ample. The model functions of the
finitely many basis divisors are bounded on compact X; coefficient
approximation therefore gives
\(\|\varphi_{D'}-f_\delta\|_\infty<\epsilon/2\).
Then \(f'=\varphi_{D'}\) is a rational theta-nef model function and
\(\|f'-f\|_\infty<\epsilon\).

Crucially, theta has not been changed. No theorem extending positivity
under arbitrary real scalar descent is used. This argument depends on
the ample generic class through the positive representative. It is not
asserted for every merely nef generic class.

### 5.2 Rational and real approximation conventions define the same PSH

Fix any determining SNC skeleton Delta. Rational theta-nef models are a
subset of real theta-nef models. Conversely, Section 5.1 approximates
each real theta-nef model uniformly on all X, hence on Delta, by rational
theta-nef models. The two uniform closures of restrictions are therefore
equal.

Thus Chapter 21 can explicitly use rational model approximants in its
skeletal definition without changing its class under its standing ample
generic hypothesis. The retraction inequality is unchanged. This also
transfers the rational BFJ Proposition 5.9 skeletal properties to real
theta-nef model functions by uniform limits.

### 5.3 Maxima in the full BFJ psh class

Let u,v be BFJ theta-psh, and fix a determining skeleton Delta.
By Section 5.2, choose rational theta-nef models \(u_j,v_j\) whose
restrictions approximate u,v uniformly on Delta. BFJ Proposition 5.8
applies to the rational inputs with the same, possibly real, theta:
\[
                      w_j=\max\{u_j,v_j\}\in\mathcal D(X)
                         \quad\hbox{is theta-nef}.
\]
The inequality
\[
 |\max\{a,b\}-\max\{a',b'\}|
                 \leq\max\{|a-a'|,|b-b'|\}
\]
shows that \(w_j|_\Delta\) converges uniformly to
\(\max\{u,v\}|_\Delta\). Finite maximum preserves upper semicontinuity,
and
\[
 \max\{u,v\}\leq\max\{u\circ p_\mathcal Y,v\circ p_\mathcal Y\}
                  =\max\{u,v\}\circ p_\mathcal Y
\]
gives the retraction inequality for every determining model. The maximum
is not identically minus infinity because either input is not.
These are exactly the defining conditions for BFJ psh. This proves the
full stated maximum conclusion; finite families follow by induction.

This is the mechanism behind primary BFJ Proposition 7.4. No envelope
property, global regularization, compactness theorem, or false real-model
maximum closure is needed for this repair.

### 5.4 Safe handling of the model-envelope consumer

For the same ample generic theta, rational and real model minorants have
the same pointwise supremum below any bounded obstacle u. Indeed, for a
real admissible f choose a rational theta-nef f' within epsilon uniformly,
then subtract a rational constant q with epsilon<q<2epsilon. The rational
model f'-q is <=f<=u and uniformly approaches f as epsilon tends to zero.

The rational admissible family is max-stable, hence upward directed
when nonempty. This gives a directed rational family computing the
same envelope, but does not make the real admissible family directed.
The latter claim must be removed or replaced.

Chapter 20's model-envelope theorem assumes only theta semipositive,
not an ample generic class. The above equality-of-suprema proof must not
be silently extended to that broader signature. A safe narrower repair
is to name the ample generic hypothesis where density is used; a broader
repair needs additional work. Rational maximum closure itself does not
require an ample generic class.
