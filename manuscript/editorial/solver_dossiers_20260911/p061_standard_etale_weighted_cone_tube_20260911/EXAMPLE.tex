\begin{example}[A nonhomogeneous standard-etale perturbation]
\label{ex:p061-standard-etale-weighted-cone-nonhomogeneous}
Assume \(\operatorname{char}K\ne3\), and put
\[
 A=K\langle x,y,z\rangle/(x^3+y^3+z^3),
\]
with its zero-section map \(A\to K\).  Consider
\[
 C=A\langle T,U\rangle/
       \bigl(T^2-T-x,(2T-1)U-1\bigr).
\]
This has the required standard-etale presentation over \(A\), since
\(2T-1\) is the derivative of the monic polynomial \(T^2-T-x\) and is
inverted in the presentation; no boundarylessness assertion is intended.
The central fibre contains the \(K\)-rational point
\[
             \xi:(x,y,z,T,U)=(0,0,0,0,-1).
\]
By \Cref{thm:p061-standard-etale-weighted-cone-tube}, every classically
pluriharmonic germ at \(\xi\) is locally \(\mathbf R\)-PL and
Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff harmonic.

The polynomial \(T^2-T-x\) is not homogeneous for any assignment of
strictly positive weights to \(T\) and \(x\): homogeneity would force
\(2\operatorname{wt}(T)=\operatorname{wt}(T)\), hence
\(\operatorname{wt}(T)=0\).  The example illustrates the genuine extra
presentation allowed by the theorem.  It does not assert that the analytic
germ has no alternative weighted presentation or is intrinsically new.
\end{example}
