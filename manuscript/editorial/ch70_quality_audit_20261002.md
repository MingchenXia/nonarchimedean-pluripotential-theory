## Mathematical architecture and scope repairs

The entire nine-file chapter closure was read. Its 31 formal assertions have

- The opening distinguishes the smooth-germ BFJ theory, the excellent-ring
  boundary and curve calculus, the restricted Gauss-shell comparison, and
  the polarized affine-cone theory. The last two constructions retain their
  different carriers, positivity data, approximation domains and numbers
  of curvature inputs.
- Compactness of the smooth normalized link now treats dimension one
  directly before using the higher-dimensional BFJ interface. The single
  smooth-curve valuation has a labelled worked example. The excellent
  normalized sections retain their exact primary hypotheses, radial
  cocycle, nonprimary failure of compactness, nilpotent reduction and
  conductor quotient. These boundary results do not supply a geometric psh
  theory for arbitrary singular germs.
- The curve ideal-profile completion retains its principal-ideal realization,
  decreasing rounding construction, homogeneous radial transport and
  finite-map ramification cancellation. Its generic-degree trace, signed
  measure lift, conditional expectation, variance identity and conductor
  descent have their stated branch-surjectivity and compatibility scopes.
  The isolated singular-point completion excludes mixed-zero radial faces;
  the ordinary smooth point remains a separate case.
- Finite transfer distinguishes normal nonflat coefficient-ideal traces
  from canonical determinant norms in the finite locally free case. The
  conductor domain is closed and defined by fiber compatibility. The surface
  example prevents a universal continuous degree trace on all ideal gauges.
  The DVR calculation uses finite freeness and residue degrees; it does not
  assert a defectless formula for arbitrary valuation rings. Normalized
  averages compose only under the stated tower condition. The worked
  variable-degree tower gives 2/3 versus 1/2, while raw traces compose.
- The formal-psh cone keeps the unit-or-primary approximation and eventual
  primary condition for a nonzero function. A model retraction need not
  preserve formal psh in higher dimension. The finite-mass construction
  now explicitly proves positivity and the norm bound M times the uniform
  norm before invoking Riesz representation. The link has n-1 measure inputs;
  global n-dimensional Monge--Ampere products have n curvature inputs.
- The multiplier-ideal comparison gives the exact strong-openness locator
  GZ15 Theorem 1.1 and the internal Chapter 51 theorem. The log-norm versus
  squared-norm conversion affects all n differential factors, giving 2^n.
  The generalized-Lelong converse concerns valuative transforms; bounded
  differences of the original germs remain invisible.
- The Gauss-shell section retains its ideal/algebraizable rational-model
  and joint bounded decreasing-closure domains. The normalized link embeds
  as a Gauss core inside the shell; it is not the whole shell. The swept
  difference is shell-supported, whereas the unswept product need not be.
  Reference changes have a point-dependent factor. Raw restriction is local;
  renormalizing each restricted measure to a probability is not.
- The affine-cone action at zero is torification, rather than the identity
  on the full analytification. Reeb translation is an action on the invariant
  locus. The full link X_0 and its compact invariant retract X_0^T are now
  distinguished throughout.
- A new proposition proves density of differences of continuous psh
  functions on X_0^T, using proportional-polarization lattice calculus and
  homogeneous separating tests. The full-link wording of Wu24 Theorem 3.5
  fails: on A^1, the trivial valuation and the rigid point z=1 agree on every
  homogeneous element, while the continuous function |z-1| separates them.
  The chapter gives this counterexample and states the carrier restriction.
- Wu's model probability measures have invariant support. Their
  finite-energy continuation is a precisely imported source theorem, used
  with this carrier restriction. Closed support persists under weak limits.
  The dual energy can be defined on all link probabilities, but only sees
  their torification. Its quasi-pseudometric cannot distinguish measures
  within a torification fiber. Density recovers measures on the invariant
  carrier; it does not solve the image problem. No finite-energy estimates
  are claimed as proved by the elementary density argument.
- The rational projective comparison uses T-invariant Fubini--Study metrics
  and their decreasing completion, as required by Wu22 Lemma 5.12. It gives
  all projective Fubini--Study metrics when T is the Reeb one-dimensional
  torus; rationality does not remove invariance for a larger torus. The
  comparison ledger and frontier statements have the same scope. Neither
  varying nonproportional Reeb polarizations nor the image of the affine-cone
  Monge--Ampere operator is presented as settled.
