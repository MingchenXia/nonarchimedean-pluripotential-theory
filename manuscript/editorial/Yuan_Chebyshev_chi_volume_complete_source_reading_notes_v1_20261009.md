Adelic metrics agree outside finitely many places with integral-model metrics and have continuous ratios at the remaining places. Properness gives integral extensions of analytic points. The paper imposes an EXTRA hypothesis throughout: for every m and v the sup norm of every K_v-rational section belongs to the value group |K_v|. Its claim that every algebraic model metric automatically satisfies this needs a reduced-special-fibre qualification. On a normal model the sup can be attained at a component with nonunit special-fibre multiplicity, giving a fractional valuation. A valuation ceiling supplies bounded losses for estimates, but does not make the printed exact-attainment/value-group assertion literal. Smooth good models away from finitely many primes avoid the issue. Extending scalar values to C_v is suggested in the paper but not proved here as a general replacement.

The source states a conductor/saturation theorem for a finitely generated semigroup Γ generating Z^(d+1). Its Corollary 2.2 extends eventual saturation on compact convex bodies strictly inside the slice to arbitrary Γ by choosing a finitely generated subsemigroup. That choice must also include finitely many elements generating the full lattice; merely containing the compact interior body need not retain lattice index one. Such generators can be included, so this is an explicit precision in the proof route. The application invokes LM Lemma 2.2 for the full section algebra of a big bundle and the smooth rational point. It does not imply saturation for arbitrary subseries or a rational point of higher residue degree without changing the lattice.

For a general cone the slice need not be bounded solely by being closed and convex; the application to a projective complete section series uses the standard boundedness of Okounkov bodies. Lemma 2.8 transfers the preceding polynomial sup/L2 estimate into O(log m) local and O(m^d log m) total errors. Retain smooth archimedean metrics for that rate; for general continuous metrics only the corresponding leading subexponential approximation argument is available without more regularity.

The degree formula on page 13 uses −log(sqrt det Gram) at every archimedean place without distinguishing real from complex. With ordinary hermitian norms, a complex place requires weight 2. The complex unit-ball formula on page 14 prints det(Gram)·V(2N), whereas the actual ellipsoid Jacobian is det(Gram)^(−1)·V(2N). The real expression det^(−1/2) is correct. Thus the displayed complex formula, degree basis independence and ensuing identity cannot be used literally; Ye's explicit n_v=2 resolves the leading complex-place weighting. A concrete rank-one check over Q(i) uses a basis rescaling by 2: the finite-place covolume changes by log 4, so the complex norm term must change by −log 4, not −log 2.

For ordinary dxdy complex Lebesgue measure, the O_K covolume is 2^(−r2) sqrt(|disc K|), whereas the text prints sqrt(d_K). A different Haar normalization can absorb this factor, provided the same normalization is used for complex unit balls. Such rank-linear constants disappear in the leading arithmetic-volume asymptotic. Also the stated a_K N log N error needs N≥2 or N log(N+1): already Q, rank 1, the real unit ball has volume 2 and χ−degree=log 2. These points do not invalidate the leading repaired formula.

The adele-quotient product displayed as a coset identity is a choice of measured fundamental domain, not a canonical topological group product. The volume calculation can use that fundamental domain. Proposition 2.10 begins the exact sum of monic-leaf L2/model degrees; its triangular-basis proof is on the next view. Proposition 2.10 uses one fixed monic K section per valuation as a triangular basis. Each local minimizing monic section has a unitriangular transition matrix, hence determinant one. At an archimedean place the minimizers for L2 are orthogonal: nonorthogonality gives a higher-valuation perturbation with SMALLER norm. The text says greater, which would not contradict minimality; replace it by smaller. The resulting Gram determinant is the product of squared minimizing norms. Complex places still require the weight two identified on pages 13–14; this local algebra does not fix that omission by itself. At the start the printed definition of F'_v as sup needs L2 at archimedean places, as the very next sentence acknowledges.

At a finite place, successive elimination proves that the unit ball is the span of norm-one versions of the minimizing sections, when the paper's value-group hypothesis holds. The first coefficient is bounded by minimality of its monic leaf; ultrametricity preserves the unit-ball condition after subtracting it, allowing induction. Local compactness of K_v and finite dimension justify attainment of the affine-leaf infimum. Haar scaling identifies the unit-ball/lattice volume ratio with the product of norms under the compatible finite absolute-value convention. If values are outside |K_v^*|, a lattice-adapted norm-one assertion is unavailable; a valuation ceiling changes the degree comparison by O(rank) at each fixed bad place, sufficient for the leading asymptotic but not the literal exact identity.

Combining the rank O(m^d), the archimedean norm comparison and the Stirling/unit-ball correction gives the intended χ=−sum_v sum_alpha F_v+o(m^(d+1)); smooth metrics support the sharper O(m^d log m) error after the stated normalization repairs. For fixed m the finitely many valuation leaves and their common finite exceptional-place sets justify changing the order of the two sums. This is a flag-leaf determinant computation rather than a statement that those leaves are HN slopes. The final integration/boundedness proof remains on the next view.

The χ/discrete-sum equation divides by m^(d+1). Therefore its Riemann-sum limit is −vol_chi/(d+1)!, exactly confirming the factorial repair of Theorem 1.2. Replacing a norm by e^(−a) times itself DECREASES convex F by a, contrary to the printed increase. One may instead shift by a sufficiently positive norm factor to make all F nonnegative. Truth of the corrected inequality/equality is preserved by constant metric shifts with the (d+1)! coefficient; the misprinted d! relation would not have this covariance.

Submultiplicativity gives F(km,k alpha)≤k F(m,alpha), and the asymptotic limit is at most F(m,alpha)/m. The normalized sequence need not decrease for every integer k; subadditivity/Fekete, or a nested power subsequence, provides the needed inequality. The comparison to c is used only at interior valuation points, where c is defined. Restricting to a compact interior convex body and using F≥0 for the omitted terms gives the inequality. The nonnegative c integrals over exhausting interior bodies are bounded by the limiting (or liminf, if retaining χ limsup) discrete sums. Each fixed c_v is integrable as well, because the finitely many possible negative local lower bounds give c_v≤c plus a constant. The text's reference to a following lemma has no displayed lemma on these pages; the bound can be supplied directly.

For equality, the step functions occupy disjoint lattice cubes of side 1/m. Their supports are uniformly bounded, but dominated convergence requires the stated UNIFORM bound across ALL valuation leaves, including those near the boundary. The argument then establishes the χ limit and corrected integral equality. Metric equivalence changes F/m by a uniform constant, so this boundedness property itself is metric independent for a fixed flag.

The projective-space coordinate-flag example uses monomials and gives an upper bound by max_i sum_v log||Z_i||, since the same exponent weights occur at all places.
