## Mathematical and reader-facing review

1. The generic-fibre obstruction uses relative nefness on compact analytic
   fibres. A vertical correction only yields the stated consequence when the
   corrected class is relatively nef; it does not assert that every vertical
   correction preserves nefness. The blow-up examples distinguish a nef big
   boundary class, a big non-nef class and an exceptional zero-volume class.
2. Multiplier-ideal inclusions give extended-real superadditivity and the
   Fekete limit. Equality of all multiplier ideals is distinguished from
   ordinary singularity equivalence. The latter is not inferred from ideal
   data outside the positive-mass I-good locus.
3. The raw transform retains its finite endpoint at the trivial valuation,
   antitonicity, scaling and translation conventions, and genuine support
   information when an ideal valuation is infinite. The logarithmic-pole
   example has relative energy zero while its class-normalized DXZ energy is
   negative infinity. The rational weighted-Gauss identity remains in its
   stated projective divisorial domain.
4. Perturbation compatibility uses equality of every integer multiplier
   ideal and a common endpoint. Divisorial inversion determines an inverse-
   system object; it is not a general positivity theorem on the whole
   tropical spectrum.
5. Upper semicontinuity is proved by the monotone-parameter argument and
   explicit bounded continuous finite-ideal expressions. The finite floors,
   the two infima, the left-endpoint grid and the decreasing square minima
   avoid indeterminate infinite arithmetic and arbitrary net-to-sequence
   extraction. No semipositivity of these finite expressions is inferred.
   The separate PL approximation retains the order of its two limits and
   its restriction to zero-support loci for real-valued PL statements.
6. The Kähler finite-energy comparison uses initial level zero, full class
   volume and the MP probability normalization. The ordinary-norm versus
   squared-norm conversion changes both the potential and radial time, so
   no extra factor of two enters the transform. General relative energy
   with smaller initial mass is kept outside this finite-energy comparison.
7. Domination-preserving divisorial approximation includes proper-support
   semivaluations. The proof retains the surjectivity of the modification
   map, positive rational weights chosen from below, valuation ideals,
   simultaneous principalization and the finite-neighbourhood construction.
   Mere topological divisorial density is not substituted for ideal-order
   domination. The all-point comparison uses both upper semicontinuity and
   antitonicity.
8. The MP section is independent of its decreasing PL model net and
   reflects order. The full Kähler comparison uses actual constant tails in
   every perturbation component, full-volume recovery and the explicit
   truncations; a divisorial lower bound alone does not establish finite
   energy. Its scope remains Kähler and does not create an unchanged MP cone
   in a non-nef class.
9. The projective comparison distinguishes rational line-bundle classes,
   real Néron–Severi classes, and ample classes. The common model proof
   algebraizes finite flag-ideal presentations, uses common refinements and
   the appropriate analytic/algebraic nef criteria, and then passes to
   decreasing completions. It does not claim that every analytic test
   configuration algebraizes or identify the two strictly positive loci.
10. Operation formulas retain their source domains: translation holds at
    every semivaluation; the displayed max, addition, rescaling and monotone
    formulas are divisorial formulas with the necessary endpoint bounds.
11. Raw filtration maximality retains the squared-norm, actual sup-norm and
    strict-interior conventions. Resolving a fixed birational linear system
    yields a globally generated **big** line bundle, which need not be ample.
    The smooth-obstacle Berman theorem is applied there with a trivial fixed
    auxiliary bundle. The mean-value loss is subexponential and tends to
    zero after division by the degree. Conductor multiplication is required
    to return complete sections on the normalization to the incomplete
    polynomial series, with its fixed degree shift and norm cost.
    The dyadic envelope limit and strict-interior positive mass finish the
    argument without finite generation. The terminal left-limit corollary
    is kept separate and does not solve arbitrary zero-mass reconstruction.
12. The final ledger keeps the general pseudoeffective image problem,
    non-nef movable/P-equivalence information and zero-volume measure/energy
    construction open. The normalized zero-volume formula is undefined;
    the inverse-system transform still makes sense.
