## The category must be fixed first

BMPS v1 Definitions 2.2 and 2.4 require a finite rational polyhedral
complex with support `X subset N_R`. Definition 2.27 gives its pure
dimension `d` and a strictly positive top-dimensional balanced cycle.
Its compactification `Xbar_Pi` in Definition 2.13 is another object;
compactness of that object does not mean compactness of `X`.

The intended consequence to check is:

> A nonempty, positive-dimensional, globally embedded balanced space in
> this finite category cannot have compact support. More generally, a
> nonzero nonnegative balanced `k`-cycle with `k>=1` on a finite embedded
> complex cannot have compact support.

Here compact support means compactness of the union of cells with nonzero
weights in the original vector space. The argument allows arbitrary
positive real weights. Thus the rational weights in the printed cycle
definition and the real weights mentioned in the introduction cause no
difference to this consequence. No coefficient-density or envelope
regularization assertion is needed.

## Direct proof with the lattice normalization retained

Choose any Euclidean inner product on `N_R`. Let `Pi` be finite, and let
`c` be a nonnegative balanced `k`-weight whose support is compact, with
`k>=1`. Every cell with positive weight is then a bounded polytope. Cells
with zero weight make no contribution to the sums below.

For each face `sigma`, let `T_sigma` be its direction space and
`N_sigma=N intersect T_sigma`. Write `a_sigma>0` for the Euclidean
covolume of this lattice in `T_sigma`; set `a_tau=1` when `tau` is a
point. If `tau` is a facet of a `k`-cell `sigma`, choose a primitive
inward quotient generator `n_(sigma/tau) in N_sigma`, as in BMPS
Definition 2.18. Let

\[
 \eta_{\sigma/\tau}
   =\operatorname{pr}_{T_\tau^\perp}(n_{\sigma/\tau}).
\]

It lies in `T_sigma`, points inward, is orthogonal to `T_tau` and does not
depend on the chosen lift of the primitive quotient generator. Since
`N_tau` is saturated in `N_sigma`, a basis of `N_tau` together with this
lift is a basis of `N_sigma`. Computing the height of that parallelotope
gives

\[
 a_\sigma=a_\tau\|\eta_{\sigma/\tau}\|.
 \tag{1}
\]

The lattice balancing condition is
`sum_(sigma>tau)c(sigma)n_(sigma/tau) in T_tau`. Orthogonal projection
therefore gives

\[
 \sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}c(\sigma)\eta_{\sigma/\tau}=0.
 \tag{2}
\]

Now put `q(x)=||x||^2`. On the affine span of each `k`-cell, its
tangential Laplacian is `2k`. The outward Euclidean unit normal of
`sigma` at `tau` is `-eta_(sigma/tau)/||eta_(sigma/tau)||`, so Green's
formula on a bounded convex polytope gives

\[
 2k\,c(\sigma)a_\sigma\operatorname{Vol}_k(\sigma)
 =-2\sum_{\tau\prec\sigma\atop\dim\tau=k-1}
     c(\sigma)a_\tau
     \int_\tau\langle x,\eta_{\sigma/\tau}\rangle
       \,d\operatorname{Vol}_{k-1}(x).
 \tag{3}
\]

The factor `a_sigma` on the left is essential: equation (1) is precisely
what converts primitive quotient normals into the Euclidean flux
normalization on the right. In dimension one, the face measure is
counting measure, so the same formula applies.

Sum (3) over all positive-weight `k`-cells and regroup the finite boundary
sum by the common faces `tau`. Equation (2) cancels that sum pointwise.
Consequently

\[
 2k\sum_{\sigma\in\Pi(k)}
       c(\sigma)a_\sigma\operatorname{Vol}_k(\sigma)=0.
 \tag{4}
\]

Every summand is nonnegative, and every positive-weight `k`-cell has
positive relative volume and positive covolume. Since `k>=1`, equation
(4) implies that every weight is zero. This proves the stated compact-
support exclusion without assuming quasi-projectivity, smoothness,
lattice saturation beyond the induced tangent lattices, or any
Monge--Ampere regularity theorem.

For a strictly positive top-dimensional cycle on a nonempty pure
`d`-dimensional space, the contradiction applies whenever `d>=1`. It
also applies to a compact connected component: restricting to its cells
preserves balancing, because all cells incident to one face lie in that
same component.

### A local check of the normalization

Take a common edge with direction lattice `Z e_1` in `R^3`. Let the three
adjacent direction lattices be generated by `e_1` and respectively
`e_2`, `e_3`, and `-e_2-e_3`, with lattice cycle weights all equal to one.
The primitive projected normals sum to zero. Their lengths are
`1,1,sqrt(2)`, and these are also the three top-face covolumes, while the
edge covolume is one. The covolume-weighted unit normals sum to zero;
the unweighted unit normals do not. This illustrates why replacing
primitive normals by unit normals while retaining the same weights
would give an incorrect proof.

## A second route using two named BMPS inputs

There is a shorter reconstruction if one imports BMPS Proposition 3.11
and Lemma 3.25. First pass to a finite simplicial quasi-projective
refinement. The positive fundamental cycle then produces a strictly
positive one-cycle `alpha^(d-1).[X]` on that refinement.

If `X` were compact and `d>=1`, this would be a finite graph with bounded
edges and strictly positive edge weights. For an edge from `p` to `q`,
write `q-p=lambda_e u_e`, where `u_e` is its primitive direction and
`lambda_e>0`. Balancing at each vertex says that its weighted outgoing
direction sum is zero. Taking the inner product with the vertex position
and summing over the vertices yields

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0
 &=\sum_v\left\langle v,
        \sum_{e\ni v}c_e u_{e,v}\right\rangle\\
 &=\sum_{e=[p,q]}c_e\langle p-q,u_e\rangle
   =-\sum_e c_e\lambda_e\|u_e\|^2<0.
 \end{aligned}
\]

The last inequality uses the existence of an edge in a positive-dimensional
simplicial complex. This route agrees with the direct volume proof, but
unlike that proof it imports the published quasi-projective refinement
and positive lower-dimensional-cycle assertions. It is not a second
independent model-verifier receipt.

## The earlier Euclidean, quasi-embedded category

The published BBGS22 definitions are broader than global embeddings:
Definitions 2.14--2.15 allow a single global map `iota:X->E` that is
affine and injective on each polyhedron. Definition 3.3 balances
**Euclidean unit normals** with its own real weights; Definition 3.24
requires a strictly positive top-dimensional such weight. These are not
the unconverted primitive-normal weights used above.

The volume proof extends to this category. If `X` is compact, its locally
finite closed-cell covering is finite. Use the metric induced by `iota`
on each cell and the globally defined continuous function
`q=||iota||^2`. Cellwise injectivity gives tangential Laplacian `2d`.
Green's formula now uses BBGS's weights directly, without the lattice
covolume factor. The common-face flux is

\[
 -2\int_\tau\left\langle\iota(x),
       \sum_{\sigma\succ\tau}b(\sigma)v_{\sigma\setminus\tau}
     \right\rangle d\operatorname{Vol}_{d-1}(x)=0.
\]

Summing gives `2d sum_sigma b(sigma)Vol_d(sigma)=0`, contradicting
strict positivity when `d>=1`. Global injectivity of `iota` was not used:
overlapping images of different abstract cells cause no difficulty,
because the integration is over the cells of `X` and each incident face
has the same image and induced metric in every term.

This extension still requires the **single global** quasi-embedding and
its balancing condition. A compact tropical space specified only by
local affine charts, with nontrivial affine holonomy, is a different
category. The function `||iota||^2` need not exist there. Nor does the
argument exclude a compact skeleton, a compactification, a space with
unbalanced boundary, or a weak embedding that collapses positive-
dimensional cells. No claim about regularity on those objects is made.

## Consequence to reconcile with P05.1 after admission

The current inventory and Chapter 72 list a compact higher-dimensional
literal regularity case as residual work. Under the exact embedded BMPS
definitions, the proof above indicates that this residual branch is empty
in positive dimension. Combined with the already admitted theorem
`thm:frontier-polyhedral-noncompact-literal-nonregularity`, it would give
literal integral-slope nonregularity on **every** nonempty positive-
dimensional finite balanced pair in that category with the required
reference. The degree-zero compact branch of the one-dimensional
classification would be vacuous in this embedded category, although its
conditional theorem statement remains valid.

If zero-dimensional spaces are included, their finite point sets have no
nontrivial polyhedral convex combinations. Every real function on such
a set is PC and is uniformly approximated from above by pointwise rational
constants. For a literal reference and literal obstacle, the envelope is
exactly `gamma+u` on each point, and is itself literal PA. The positivity-
at-infinity condition is vacuous. This supplies the elementary zero-
dimensional alternative, subject to allowing `d=0` in the formulation.

These are consequences to reconcile after the repository's independent
admission gate, not current changes to an atom or the catalogue. P05.1
would still contain its higher-dimensional **real-PA** classification,
arbitrary nonseparable product obstacles and source coefficient-convention
repair. P05.2's higher-dimensional orthogonality characterization and
P05.5's realization, lift, mass, calibration and operator-domain gates
are unaffected. No compactness or envelope input is obtained for the
unresolved BJ22/Siu problem.
