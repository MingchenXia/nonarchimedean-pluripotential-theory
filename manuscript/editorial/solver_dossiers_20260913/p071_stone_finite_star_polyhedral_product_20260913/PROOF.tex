\begin{proof}
Write \(\mathcal J_{S\times P}\) for the comparison sheaf in
\Cref{thm:p071-finite-star-polyhedral-collapse}.  Thus its sections are
exactly the extended-real branchwise jointly convex functions satisfying
the product junction inequalities.  The cited theorem proves three facts
that we use below:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\mathcal J_{S\times P}\) is a sheaf, contains the smooth ambient
  generators, and is closed under pointwise decreasing sequential limits;
\item on a compact core
  \[
   D=\Bigl(\{0\}\cup\coprod_i(0,2T_i]v_i\Bigr)\times Q,
  \]
  with \(Q\) a compact convex polytope, the junction-convex envelope of a
  continuous real-valued function is finite, continuous, and is the largest
  comparison function below it;
\item if \(H\) is a continuous real-valued comparison function on \(D\),
  then, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), the construction in the proof of the
  cited theorem gives a smooth ambient generator \(g\) on one prescribed
  smaller product core \(S''\times V'\) such that
  \begin{equation}
                         H\leq g\leq H+4\varepsilon.
   \label{eq:p071-stone-star-one-step-smoothing}
  \end{equation}
  The smaller core depends only on the two nested geometric cores, not on
  \(H\) or \(\varepsilon\).  The construction therefore applies on the
  same smaller core to every member of a finite family.
\end{enumerate}
The third item is the one-step estimate established before the summable
tail correction in that proof; it is stronger than merely invoking the
final sheaf equality.

We first prove that \(\mathcal C_X\) is decreasing-sequentially closed.
Its defining condition is local, and the sheaf property follows from the
sheaf property of \(\mathcal J_{S\times P}\) exactly as for the comparison
sheaf in the finite-star theorem.  Every smooth generator lies in
\(\mathcal C_X\): after a clopen shrinking it is independent of \(z\), so
one takes \(Y\) to be a point.

Let \(u_n\downarrow u\) on an open subset \(W\subset X\), with every
\(u_n\in\mathcal C_X(W)\), and fix a point \((z_0,s_0,y_0)\in W\).
Choose a compact clopen neighbourhood \(C\subset Z\) of \(z_0\) and a
compact finite-star--polytope core \(D_0\subset S\times P\), with the fixed
point in the relative interior appropriate to its stratum, such that
\begin{equation}
                              C\times D_0\subset W.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-limit-core}
\end{equation}
If \(s_0\ne0\), use only a compact interval inside its branch.  If
\(s_0=0\), take a positive compact collar in every one of the finitely many
branches.  Shrink once more to a product neighbourhood
\(C\times V_0\) whose closure in \(C\times D_0\) is compact.

For each \(n\), compactness supplies a finite product cover of the latter
closure by sets \(C_{n,a}\times V_{n,a}\), where \(C_{n,a}\) is compact
clopen in \(C\), and on this product \(u_n\) factors through a continuous
surjection
\[
                        q_{n,a}:C_{n,a}\twoheadrightarrow Y_{n,a}
\]
onto a compact metrizable zero-dimensional space.  Choose a countable
clopen base \(\mathcal D_{n,a}\) of every \(Y_{n,a}\).  Let
\(\mathcal B\) be the Boolean subalgebra of the clopen subsets of \(C\)
generated by the sets \(C_{n,a}\) and
\[
                         q_{n,a}^{-1}(D),
                         \qquad D\in\mathcal D_{n,a}.
\]
There are only countably many generators.  The evaluation map
\begin{equation}
 q:C\longrightarrow
 Y:=q(C)\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathcal B}
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-common-quotient}
\end{equation}
is a continuous surjection onto a compact metrizable zero-dimensional
space.

Every \(u_n\) on the smaller product is constant on the fibres of
\(q\times\operatorname{id}\).  Indeed, if \(q(z)=q(z')\), membership in
each \(C_{n,a}\) agrees, while the inverse images of the chosen clopen base
separate the points of \(Y_{n,a}\).  A member of the finite product cover
containing \((z,s,y)\) therefore also contains \((z',s,y)\), and its local
factorization gives \(u_n(z,s,y)=u_n(z',s,y)\).  Passing to the limit gives
\begin{equation}
                        u(z,s,y)=\bar u(q(z),s,y).
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-limit-factor}
\end{equation}

The function \(\bar u\) is upper semicontinuous.  Indeed,
\(\{u<t\}=\bigcup_n\{u_n<t\}\) is open.  If
\(\bar u(\zeta,s,y)<t\), cover the compact fibre
\(q^{-1}(\zeta)\times\{(s,y)\}\) by finitely many product neighbourhoods
contained in \(\{u<t\}\).  Their finite union in \(C\), their finite
intersection in \(S\times P\), and the open set
\[
                         Y\setminus q(C\setminus N)
\]
give a product neighbourhood of \((\zeta,s,y)\) in
\(\{\bar u<t\}\), because \(q\) is closed.

For a fixed \(z\), every \(u_n(z,\cdot)\) belongs locally to
\(\mathcal J_{S\times P}\).  The sheaf and decreasing-closure properties
recalled in the first item above imply that \(u(z,\cdot)\) does as well.
This property depends only on \(q(z)\), so every fibre
\(\bar u(\zeta,\cdot)\) is a comparison function.  Hence
\(u\in\mathcal C_X\).  We have proved that \(\mathcal C_X\) contains the
generators and is decreasing-sequentially closed.  Minimality gives
\begin{equation}
                          \mathcal P_X\subseteq\mathcal C_X.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-minimality}
\end{equation}

It remains to place every comparison germ in the first stage.  Away from
the node this follows from
\Cref{thm:p071-stone-polyhedral-collapse}, after using a compact interval
in the branch together with \(P\) as one convex polyhedron.  We therefore
work at \((z_0,0,y_0)\).  Let \(u\in\mathcal C_X\) have the factorization
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-factorization} on
\(C\times S'\times V_0\), and write
\[
                  S'=\{0\}\cup\coprod_i(0,r_i')v_i.
\]
Choose numbers
\[
                   0<\rho_i<T_i<2T_i<r_i'
\]
inside the available branch collars, a compact convex polytope
\(Q\subset V_0\), and a relatively open polyhedral neighbourhood
\(V'\ni y_0\) such that \(\overline{V'}^{\,P}\subset Q\).  Put
\begin{equation}
 D=\Bigl(\{0\}\cup\coprod_i(0,2T_i]v_i\Bigr)\times Q,
 \qquad
 D'=\Bigl(\{0\}\cup\coprod_i(0,\rho_i)v_i\Bigr)\times V'.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-nested-cores}
\end{equation}
Both cores are fixed for the rest of the proof.

Give \(Y\times D\) a compatible product metric \(d\).  The upper
semicontinuous function \(\bar u\) is bounded above there.  For \(k\geq1\)
set
\begin{align}
 h_k(\xi)&=\max\{\bar u(\xi),-k\},\\
 \Phi_k(\xi)&=\sup_{\eta\in Y\times D}
                 \bigl(h_k(\eta)-k\,d(\xi,\eta)\bigr).
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-usc-regularization}
\end{align}
Then \(\Phi_k\) is finite, continuous, and \(k\)-Lipschitz, and
\begin{equation}
               \Phi_{k+1}\leq\Phi_k,
        \qquad \Phi_k\geq\bar u,
        \qquad \Phi_k\downarrow\bar u.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-usc-convergence}
\end{equation}
The convergence is the standard compact-metric upper regularization
argument: an upper-semicontinuity neighbourhood controls nearby points,
and the term \(-k\,d\) sends the compact complement below any prescribed
upper level.

For \(\zeta\in Y\), apply the junction-convex envelope from the second
item above only in the \(D\)-variable:
\begin{equation}
                    c_k(\zeta,\cdot)
          =C_D\bigl(\Phi_k(\zeta,\cdot)\bigr).
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-fibre-envelope}
\end{equation}
Every \(c_k(\zeta,\cdot)\) is a finite continuous comparison function.
Because \(\bar u(\zeta,\cdot)\) is a comparison function below
\(\Phi_k(\zeta,\cdot)\), maximality of the envelope gives
\begin{equation}
       \bar u\leq c_k\leq\Phi_k,
       \qquad c_{k+1}\leq c_k,
       \qquad c_k\downarrow\bar u.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-envelope-convergence}
\end{equation}

We record the parameter continuity, since it is what makes the Stone
discretization legitimate.  The envelope is nonexpanding in the uniform
norm:
\begin{equation}
 \lVert C_D\phi-C_D\psi\rVert_D
       \leq\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_D.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-envelope-nonexpanding}
\end{equation}
Indeed, if the norm on the right is \(\delta\), then
\(C_D\phi-\delta\) is a comparison function below \(\psi\), whence
\(C_D\phi-\delta\leq C_D\psi\); interchange \(\phi\) and \(\psi\).
The Lipschitz estimate for \(\Phi_k\) and
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-envelope-nonexpanding} show that \(c_k\) is
jointly continuous on \(Y\times D\).

Choose nested finite clopen partitions \(\mathscr P_k\) of \(Y\) whose
meshes tend to zero.  For \(R\in\mathscr P_k\), define
\begin{equation}
 a_{k,R}(s,y)=\max_{\zeta\in R}c_k(\zeta,s,y),
 \qquad
 a_k(\zeta,s,y)=a_{k,R_k(\zeta)}(s,y).
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-clopen-envelope}
\end{equation}
The maximum is attained and \(a_{k,R}\) is continuous.  It is also a
comparison function: a supremum of branchwise jointly convex functions is
branchwise jointly convex, and applying each junction inequality before
taking the supremum gives
\[
 \sup_{\zeta\in R}c_k(\zeta,0,y_*)
 \leq \sum_a\alpha_a
          \sup_{\zeta\in R}c_k(\zeta,t_av_{i(a)},y_a).
\]
Refinement and \(c_{k+1}\leq c_k\) imply
\begin{equation}
                             a_{k+1}\leq a_k.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-clopen-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
                             a_k\downarrow\bar u
                             \quad\text{on }Y\times D.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-clopen-convergence}
\end{equation}
The lower bound follows from
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-envelope-convergence}.  For the upper bound, at a
fixed \((\zeta,s,y)\) use
\[
 a_k(\zeta,s,y)
 \leq\max_{\zeta'\in R_k(\zeta)}
              \Phi_k(\zeta',s,y).
\]
The shrinking mesh and the compact-metric argument for
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-usc-convergence} make the right-hand side tend to
\(\bar u(\zeta,s,y)\), including when that value is \(-\infty\).

Fix positive numbers \(\varepsilon_k\) with
\(\sum_k\varepsilon_k<\infty\).  For each \(k\), apply the one-step
smoothing estimate
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-one-step-smoothing} to the finite family
\(\{a_{k,R}:R\in\mathscr P_k\}\).  We obtain smooth ambient generators
\(g_{k,R}\) on the one fixed core \(D'\) such that
\begin{equation}
                 a_{k,R}\leq g_{k,R}
                    \leq a_{k,R}+4\varepsilon_k.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-family-smoothing}
\end{equation}
Define on \(C\times D'\)
\begin{equation}
 w_k(z,s,y)=g_{k,R}(s,y)
       \quad\text{when }q(z)\in R\in\mathscr P_k.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-piecewise-smoothing}
\end{equation}
The inverse images \(q^{-1}(R)\) form a finite clopen partition of \(C\),
so \(w_k\in\mathcal G_{X,0}(C\times D')\).  Equations
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-clopen-monotonicity} and
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-family-smoothing} give
\begin{equation}
                         w_{k+1}\leq w_k+4\varepsilon_{k+1}.
 \label{eq:p071-stone-star-almost-monotone}
\end{equation}

Set
\[
                    \tau_k=4\sum_{j>k}\varepsilon_j,
                    \qquad G_k=w_k+\tau_k.
\]
Since \(\tau_k=4\varepsilon_{k+1}+\tau_{k+1}\),
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-almost-monotone} gives
\(G_{k+1}\leq G_k\).  Equations
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-clopen-convergence} and
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-family-smoothing}, together with
\(\tau_k\to0\), give
\[
                              G_k\downarrow u
                              \qquad\text{on }C\times D'.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal C_X\subseteq\mathcal P_{X,1}\).  Combining this with
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-minimality} and the tautological inclusion
\(\mathcal P_{X,1}\subseteq\mathcal P_X\) proves
\eqref{eq:p071-stone-star-collapse} and the fixed-neighbourhood assertion.
The final scope statement is immediate from the hypotheses: the Stone
factor is used only through clopen partitions, the star is finite and
ambient-induced, and the entire \(P\)-factor lies in one convex chamber.
\end{proof}
