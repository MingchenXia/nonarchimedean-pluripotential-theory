## Scope suitable for Chapter 50

This is a finite-dimensional comparison with the norm/filtration approach to stability, over a complete ultrametric field K. It must explicitly override Chapter 50's default algebraically closed trivially valued ground field and projective polarization. No projectivity, algebraic closedness, characteristic-zero, discreteness, local compactness or spherical completeness assumption is added. The finite quiver may have loops and multiple arrows. Vertex spaces have finite dimension. The real degree theta(E) is zero.

The conventions are theta-semistable if every subrepresentation has nonnegative degree, and stable if every proper nonzero one has positive degree. A polystable representation is a finite direct sum of stable degree-zero representations. HKKP harmonicity means semistability of the associated real-graded representation with adjusted weights theta-star; it does not mean polystability of the original representation.

For a weak ramp f, require continuity at minus infinity with a finite value, continuous differentiability on R, strictly positive strictly increasing derivative, and derivative limits zero and positive infinity. Only the finite singular values of an arrow are counted, so there is no infinite kernel/cokernel term. The potential is the sum of ramp values minus the weighted logarithmic determinant norms. Its quotient removes one simultaneous real scaling of all vertex norms. Quotienting separately at every vertex would remove meaningful relative scales and give the wrong problem.

Ben-Bassat Theorem 1.1 gives polystability => existence of a split harmonic norm => semistability. Theorem 3.10 gives attainment in the full space of norms for polystable representations. Theorem 5.4 turns any minimum in degree zero into a split minimum with exactly the same ordered logarithmic singular-value profiles. Corollary 4.2 identifies split minima with harmonic norms. Neither implication is silently reversed. The identity arrow with rank-one spaces and zero theta is semistable but exp(sigma) has no minimum; the D4 example supplies a semistable nonpolystable representation with harmonic norms.

## Foundation and proof chain

The determinant norm is the projective ultrametric quotient norm on the top exterior power. Exact restriction–quotient determinant products and isometric scalar extension hold for all norms, not just split norms. They were checked in BE's published Lemmas 2.8 and 2.9 and Proposition 2.14, together with finite-dimensional norm equivalence, split density and simultaneous diagonalization. Norm profiles are differences of logarithmic exterior operator norms. BB's finite monomial reduction and weighted-minor formula directly substantiate their continuity and partial-sum convexity.

Uniform stable coercivity in BB uses a positive incidence gap, not growth along each individual ray. The projective Grassmannian incidence map has closed K-point image by Moret-Bailly Theorem 1.3 and the projective case of Section 4.3.1. ## Explanatory examples and limitations

The inward D4 representation with center K^2, leaves K, vectors e1, e2, z e1+e2, and theta=(1,-1,-1/2,-1/2) has an all-norm lower bound 2+log(2) for the exponential potential. It follows from the two wedge determinant inequalities and the scalar minima of exp(sigma)-a sigma. At finite nonzero z it is semistable but nonpolystable. A half-line of central split norms attains the bound, so minima need not be bounded modulo common scaling in the semistable case. HKKP Example 6.22 is the prior isolated representation; BB Section 6.1 extends it to an analytic family.

BB Section 6.2 constructs a nonsplit rank-two norm from nested Novikov partial sums with exponents increasing to one. The quotient infimum is exp(-1) and is not attained, proving nonsplitting. The identity arrow nevertheless has minimum 2 at the pair (p,p), since exp(sigma)-sigma >= 1. Thus nonsplit minima coexist with split minima.

BB Section 6.3's multiplicity-two D4 example has minimum 6 and a two-radius reduction. At stages zero, one and two its auxiliary potential is x+2 eta, x+eta, 0; its original potential is 4+2 exp(eta)+x-2 eta, 6+x+eta, 6. For eta=log(11/10), the first original-potential increase is 3 log(11/10)-1/5 > 4/55. Zero arrow profiles at stage one still leave a positive determinant defect. Any exposition must retain these two distinctions.

Twenty-four exact rational triangular-minor fixtures, six determinant-defect fixtures, a split lower-block example and the exact positive logarithmic bound supplement the analytical arguments. Finite fixtures are not proofs of the general theorems or independent-model receipts.
