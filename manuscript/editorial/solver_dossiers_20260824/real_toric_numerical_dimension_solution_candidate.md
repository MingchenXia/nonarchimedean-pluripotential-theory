# P20.1: real toric numerical dimension — source-aware solution candidate

Date: 24 August 2026; production verification updated 25 August 2026.

## Verdict

The live problem is ambiguous as written.

- If \(\nu\) means the numerical dimension of the cohomology class
  \(\alpha=\{T\}\), defined by Boucksom's positive products of classes, then
  the proposed identity is false, even for an integral class on
  \(\mathbf P^1\).  A big class has class numerical dimension \(n\), whereas
  a current in that class can have a zero-dimensional Newton body.
- If \(\nu\) means the intended **current-level Cao numerical dimension**
  from Cao's Definition 3, then the identity has a complete analytic proof.
  In fact, the proof removes both bigness and rationality: it applies to every
  torus-invariant closed positive \((1,1)\)-current in an arbitrary
  pseudoeffective real class on a smooth projective toric variety.
- The same proof also identifies Cao's product with the ordinary
  non-pluripolar product for invariant currents and gives all mixed
  coefficients, not just the numerical dimension.

The exact candidate is

\[
 \int_X \langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
 =\int_X \langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{np}}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
 =n!V\bigl(K[k],Q[n-k]\bigr),
 \qquad 0\leq k\leq n,
\]

where \(K=\Delta(T)\) is the **closed** Newton body, \(Q\) is the moment
polytope of an invariant Kähler form \(\omega\), and the mixed-volume
normalization is \(V(B[n])=\operatorname{vol}_M(B)\).  Consequently,

\[
             \nu_{\mathrm{Cao}}(T)
             =\nu_{\mathrm{np}}(T)
             =\dim K.
\]

## 1. What “irrational” can mean on a projective toric variety

Let \(X=X_\Sigma\) be a smooth projective toric variety.  Its real
\((1,1)\)-cohomology is generated by invariant divisor classes:

\[
                H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf R)=N^1(X)_{\mathbf R}.
\]

Thus there are no genuinely transcendental Hodge directions on \(X\).  In
this problem “transcendental” can only mean

\[
                  \alpha\in N^1(X)_{\mathbf R}
                  \setminus N^1(X)_{\mathbf Q},
\]

that is, an irrational real divisor class.  Such a class is represented by a
torus-invariant real Cartier divisor because \(X\) is smooth, but it is not
the first Chern class of a line bundle after any integer scaling.  Hence it
has no single graded section algebra.  This is exactly why the rational proof
through Cao's Proposition 4.4 cannot be copied.

Multiplier ideals themselves cause no problem.  If \(\varphi\) is a qpsh
potential, then \(\mathcal I(c\varphi)\) is defined locally for every real
\(c>0\).  In the norm-weight convention,

\[
 \mathcal I(c\varphi)_x
 =\left\{f\in\mathcal O_{X,x}: |f|^2
        \mathrm e^{-2c\varphi}\text{ is locally integrable}\right\}.
\]

If \(\Phi=2\varphi\) is a squared-norm weight, the same condition is written
with \(\mathrm e^{-c\Phi}\).  What does not exist for irrational \(\alpha\)
is the expression
\(H^0(X,m\alpha\otimes\mathcal I(m\varphi))\).  The proof below therefore
uses no section algebra and no rational approximation of \(\alpha\).

## 2. Definitions that make the statement well posed

### 2.1 Logarithmic and measure normalization

Let \(M\) and \(N\) be the character and cocharacter lattices.  Normalize
Lebesgue measure on \(M_{\mathbf R}\) so that a fundamental lattice
parallelepiped has volume one.  Throughout, ``torus invariant'' means
invariant under the maximal compact torus; this is the convention under
which invariant psh weights correspond to convex functions on
\(N_{\mathbf R}\).  Use norm weights and

\[
 \langle m,\operatorname{Log}z\rangle=\log|\chi^m(z)|,
 \qquad
 \mathrm d^c=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm i}(\partial-\bar\partial).
\]

Equivalently, one may use squared-norm weights, the doubled logarithm, and
\(\mathrm d\mathrm d^c=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial\).
These paired convention changes give the same formula

\[
                  \int_X\omega^n=n!\operatorname{vol}_M(Q).
\]

### 2.2 The closed Newton body

Choose an invariant smooth representative \(\theta\in\alpha\) and write

\[
                         T=\theta+\mathrm d\mathrm d^c\varphi.
\]

On the dense torus, an invariant primitive of \(\theta\), together with
\(\varphi\), gives a finite convex function
\(F:N_{\mathbf R}\to\mathbf R\) such that

\[
                   T|_{\mathbb T}=\mathrm d\mathrm d^c
                         (F\circ\operatorname{Log}).
\]

The extension of \(T\) to the complete toric variety bounds the slopes of
\(F\).  More explicitly, choose an invariant real divisor
\(D=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}a_\rho D_\rho\) representing \(\alpha\).
The local extension condition on the chart corresponding to each ray gives
\[
 \langle p,u_\rho\rangle\geq-a_\rho
 \quad\text{for every }p\in\partial F(N_{\mathbf R})
 \text{ and every }\rho\in\Sigma(1).
\]
Thus the subgradient image lies in
\[
 P_D=\{p\in M_{\mathbf R}:
          \langle p,u_\rho\rangle\geq-a_\rho
          \text{ for all }\rho\in\Sigma(1)\}.
\]
Completeness of the fan makes \(P_D\) bounded: its recession cone is the
intersection of the half-spaces
\(\langle p,u_\rho\rangle\geq0\), which is \(\{0\}\).  A finite convex
function on all of \(N_{\mathbf R}\) has a subgradient at every point, so
the image is nonempty.  Hence the following set is nonempty and compact:

\[
 \boxed{
 K=\Delta(T):=\overline{\operatorname{dom}F^*}
       =\overline{\bigcup_{x\in N_{\mathbf R}}\partial F(x)}.}
 \tag{D1}
\]

The closure in (D1) is essential.  For a finite closed convex function one
only has

\[
 \operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}F^*)
 \subseteq\bigcup_x\partial F(x)
 \subseteq\operatorname{dom}F^*;
\]

the unclosed sets need not agree.  If the invariant primitive is changed by
an affine function \(x\mapsto\langle a,x\rangle+c\), then \(K\) is
translated by \(a\).  Thus its dimension and every mixed volume occurring
below are intrinsic.

There is a concrete warning in the supplied complex book.  The final
sentence of its Proposition A.2.2 asserts, in excessive generality, that the
subgradient image of a proper closed convex function equals the domain of its
conjugate.  Take

\[
                         F(x)=\log(1+\mathrm e^x).
\]

Then \(F'(\mathbf R)=(0,1)\), while
\(\operatorname{dom}F^*=[0,1]\).  This is the one-dimensional
Fubini--Study model.  All volume and dimension formulas survive after taking
closures, but any live definition using the unclosed gradient image must be
repaired before integration of the present result.

### 2.3 Three different positive products

Cao's Definition 3 applies directly to arbitrary closed positive
\((1,1)\)-currents on a compact Kähler manifold; it does **not** require a
line bundle.  If \((\varphi_j)\) is a quasi-equisingular approximation of
\(\varphi\), Cao defines a pseudoeffective cohomology class

\[
                    \langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}
                    \in H^{k,k}(X,\mathbf R)
\]

by taking limits of wedge products of the absolutely continuous parts of
the approximants.  Cao's Definition 4 merely packages this current-level
construction as \(\operatorname{nd}(L,\varphi)\) when the current is the
curvature of a singular metric on a line bundle.  We set for every real
class

\[
 \nu_{\mathrm{Cao}}(T)
 :=\max\{k:\langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}\neq0\}.
 \tag{D2}
\]

The ordinary non-pluripolar product gives a second current-level invariant

\[
 \nu_{\mathrm{np}}(T)
 :=\max\{k:[\langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{np}}]\neq0\}.
 \tag{D3}
\]

Neither (D2) nor (D3) is the numerical dimension of the class
\(\alpha\), which depends only on \(\alpha\).  The latter interpretation
makes the live problem false; see Section 6.1 below.

## 3. Exact statement for a Rethlas packet

The theorem heading should be unnamed in the book.

```tex
\begin{theorem}
Let \(X=X_\Sigma\) be a smooth projective toric variety of dimension
\(n\geq1\), and let \(T\) be a torus-invariant closed positive
\((1,1)\)-current in a pseudoeffective real class
\(\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf R)\).  Define its Newton body by
\[
 \Delta(T)=\overline{\operatorname{dom}F_T^*}
 \subset M_{\mathbf R},
\]
where \(F_T\) is a convex weight of \(T\) on the dense torus.  This body is
well defined up to translation.

Let \(\omega\) be a torus-invariant Kähler form whose moment polytope is
\(Q\), and normalize Lebesgue measure by the character lattice.  Then, for
every \(0\leq k\leq n\),
\[
 \int_X\langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
 =\int_X\langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{np}}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
 =n!V\bigl(\Delta(T)[k],Q[n-k]\bigr).
\]
Here \(V(B[n])=\operatorname{vol}_M(B)\).  Consequently,
\[
                  \nu_{\mathrm{Cao}}(T)
                  =\nu_{\mathrm{np}}(T)
                  =\dim\Delta(T).
\]
More generally, if \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_{n-k}\) are invariant Kähler
forms with moment polytopes \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_{n-k}\), then
\[
 \int_X\langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}
          \wedge\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-k}
 =n!V\bigl(\Delta(T)[k],Q_1,\ldots,Q_{n-k}\bigr),
\]
and the same formula holds for the non-pluripolar product.
\end{theorem}
```

The assumption that \(\alpha\) is big is absent on purpose.  Positivity of
\(T\) already implies that \(\alpha\) is pseudoeffective, and the perturbation
\(T+s\omega\) used below is a Kähler current for every \(s>0\).

## 4. Complete proof

Put \(K=\Delta(T)\).  The proof is divided into five steps so that every
imported input can be audited separately.

### Step 1: smooth addition is an exact polynomial for Cao's product

Write \(T=\theta+\mathrm d\mathrm d^c\varphi\), with \(\theta\) invariant.
Fix once and for all the decreasing quasi-equisingular approximation
\((\varphi_j)\) furnished by Theorem 1.6.2 of the supplied complex book,
choosing its asserted **gentle analytic singularities**.  Put

\[
                     R_j=\theta+\mathrm d\mathrm d^c\varphi_j.
\]

After comparing the auxiliary Kähler form in the regularization theorem
with the fixed \(\omega\), and rescaling \(\varepsilon_j\) if necessary, the
sequence satisfies \(R_j\geq-\varepsilon_j\omega\), with
\(\varepsilon_j\downarrow0\); this is enough for Cao's Definition 3.  For
every \(s>0\), the same sequence is a quasi-equisingular approximation for

\[
                         S_s:=T+s\omega.
\]

Indeed, adding a smooth form changes none of the multiplier-ideal or
singularity conditions, and \(R_j+s\omega\geq0\) for all sufficiently large
\(j\).  Since a smooth form belongs entirely to the absolutely continuous
part,

\[
 (R_j+s\omega)_{\mathrm{ac}}
       =(R_j)_{\mathrm{ac}}+s\omega.
\]

Binomial expansion followed by Cao's defining limits gives

\[
 \int_X\langle S_s^n\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}
 =\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk s^{n-k}
   \int_X\langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}
                  \wedge\omega^{n-k}.
 \tag{P1}
\]

No line bundle, section algebra, or continuity under variation of the class
has been used here.  Formula (P1) is a direct consequence of Cao's Definition
3 with one fixed approximation.

### Step 2: for \(s>0\), the top Cao mass is the non-pluripolar mass

The current \(S_s\) dominates \(s\omega\), so it is a Kähler current.  Its
potential is torus invariant.  The supplied complex book proves that every
toric qpsh potential on a smooth projective toric variety is
\(\mathcal I\)-good (Example 7.4.1).  Its Theorem 7.1.1, together with the
fact that \(d_{\mathcal S}\) identifies potentials having the same
\(P\)-singularity type, then shows that the fixed quasi-equisingular
approximation converges to \(\varphi\) in \(d_{\mathcal S}\), with background
\(\theta+s\omega\).  Theorem 6.2.1 gives continuity of mixed
non-pluripolar masses under that convergence.

For the tail on which \(R_j+s\omega\geq0\), use the gentle log resolution
provided by Definition 1.6.5 for \(\varphi_j\).  On this resolution
\(\pi_j:Y_j\to X\), the exact decomposition is

\[
          \pi_j^*(R_j+s\omega)=\beta_j+[E_j],
\]

where \(E_j\) is an effective real divisor and \(\beta_j\) is smooth.
Away from \(\operatorname{Supp}E_j\), the form \(\beta_j\) is the pullback of
the positive current \(R_j+s\omega\); continuity therefore makes
\(\beta_j\) semipositive everywhere.  The analytic set carrying \(E_j\)
has Lebesgue measure zero, and the non-pluripolar product discards the
divisorial factor.  Consequently,

\[
 \int_X(R_j+s\omega)_{\mathrm{ac}}^n
   =\int_{Y_j}\beta_j^n
   =\int_X\langle(R_j+s\omega)^n\rangle_{\mathrm{np}}.
 \tag{P2}
\]

Using \(d_{\mathcal S}\)-continuity in (P2) yields

\[
 \int_X\langle S_s^n\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}
       =\int_X\langle S_s^n\rangle_{\mathrm{np}},
 \qquad s>0.
 \tag{P3}
\]

This is the one place where toric \(\mathcal I\)-goodness is essential.  It
is also essential that the fixed approximation was chosen gentle.  Under
the supplied book's definition, a general analytic singularity is allowed a
merely bounded remainder; for such an approximation the displayed
log-resolution identity with a smooth \(\beta_j\) is not available.  Thus
one must neither assert (P2) for every analytic approximation nor assert
(P3) for arbitrary non-toric currents.

### Step 3: the toric mass is a convex volume

Let \(G\) be the smooth strictly convex weight of \(\omega\) on the dense
torus.  Its closed conjugate domain is the full-dimensional moment polytope
\(Q\).  The convex weight of \(S_s\) is

\[
                              F_s=F+sG.
\]

On the dense torus, the local complex/real Monge--Ampère calculation says
that pushing forward the Bedford--Taylor measure by
\(\operatorname{Log}\), after integration along the compact real-torus
fibres, gives \(n!\mathcal A_{F_s}\), where the standard Alexandrov measure
is normalized by
\(\mathcal A_{F_s}(E)=\operatorname{vol}_M(\partial F_s(E))\).
(Equivalently, the supplied book incorporates this \(n!\) into its notation
\(\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbf R}\).)  A finite convex function is locally
bounded, so the
Bedford--Taylor product on the open torus is legitimate.  The complement of
the dense torus is analytic, hence pluripolar, and the non-pluripolar product
does not charge it.  Finally,
\(\mathcal A_{F_s}(N_{\mathbf R})\) is the lattice-normalized volume of the
subgradient image.  Its closure adds at
most the boundary of the compact convex body and hence does not change
Lebesgue measure.  Therefore

\[
 \int_X\langle S_s^n\rangle_{\mathrm{np}}
   =n!\operatorname{vol}_M
       \left(\overline{\operatorname{dom}F_s^*}\right).
 \tag{P4}
\]

For finite convex functions with bounded conjugate domains,

\[
 \overline{\operatorname{dom}(F+sG)^*}
 =\overline{\operatorname{dom}F^*}+s\,
      \overline{\operatorname{dom}G^*}
 =K+sQ.
 \tag{P5}
\]

One proof of (P5) uses the lower-semicontinuous infimal-convolution formula
for the conjugate of a sum and then takes closures.  A proof immune to
boundary-domain issues uses recession functions:

\[
 F^\infty=h_K,\qquad G^\infty=h_Q,
 \qquad (F+sG)^\infty=h_K+s h_Q=h_{K+sQ}.
\]

The closed conjugate domain is the unique compact convex body with this
support function.  Combining (P4) and (P5),

\[
 \int_X\langle S_s^n\rangle_{\mathrm{np}}
                      =n!\operatorname{vol}_M(K+sQ).
 \tag{P6}
\]

### Step 4: compare the two polynomials

Minkowski's polynomial formula, with
\(V(B[n])=\operatorname{vol}_M(B)\), is

\[
 \operatorname{vol}_M(K+sQ)
 =\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk
       V(K[k],Q[n-k])s^{n-k}.
 \tag{P7}
\]

Equations (P1), (P3), (P6), and (P7) hold for every \(s>0\).  Equality of
polynomials gives, coefficient by coefficient,

\[
 \int_X\langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
            =n!V(K[k],Q[n-k]).
 \tag{P8}
\]

The ordinary non-pluripolar product is multilinear under addition of smooth
forms.  Expanding \(\langle(T+s\omega)^n\rangle_{\mathrm{np}}\) and comparing
again with (P6)--(P7) gives

\[
 \int_X\langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{np}}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
            =n!V(K[k],Q[n-k]).
 \tag{P9}
\]

For the multivariate statement, replace \(s\omega\) by
\(s_1\omega_1+\cdots+s_{n-k}\omega_{n-k}\).  Its Newton body is
\(K+s_1Q_1+\cdots+s_{n-k}Q_{n-k}\).  Comparing the coefficient of
\(s_1\cdots s_{n-k}\) gives

\[
 \int_X\langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}
          \wedge\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-k}
 =n!V(K[k],Q_1,\ldots,Q_{n-k}),
 \tag{P10}
\]

and similarly for the non-pluripolar product.

### Step 5: extract the numerical dimension

Let \(d=\dim K\).  Since \(Q\) is full dimensional,

\[
 V(K[k],Q[n-k])
 \begin{cases}
   >0,&0\leq k\leq d,\\
   =0,&d<k\leq n.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{P11}
\]

For \(k>d\), this follows because the \(k\) copies of \(K\) lie in a common
\(d\)-dimensional direction space.  For \(k\leq d\), choose \(k\) linearly
independent segments in \(K\) and complete their directions using segments
in the interior of \(Q\); monotonicity of mixed volume gives strict
positivity.  Equivalently, (P11) is the standard essential-family criterion
for positivity of mixed volumes.

Cao's class and the non-pluripolar class are pseudoeffective.  Such a class
is nonzero exactly when its pairing with \(\omega^{n-k}\) is positive.
Equations (P8), (P9), and (P11) therefore prove

\[
                  \nu_{\mathrm{Cao}}(T)
                  =\nu_{\mathrm{np}}(T)
                  =d.
\]

Finally, the multivariate formula gives equality of the Cao and
non-pluripolar cohomology classes themselves on a smooth projective toric
variety.  Indeed, toric cohomology is generated in degree two by invariant
divisor classes, every real divisor class is a difference of Kähler classes,
and products of such classes span the complementary cohomology.  Formula
(P10) therefore identifies the pairings against a spanning set; Poincaré
duality gives

\[
              \langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{Cao}}
              =[\langle T^k\rangle_{\mathrm{np}}]
              \quad\text{in }H^{k,k}(X,\mathbf R).
\]

This completes the proof.

## 6. Counterexamples and boundary tests

### 6.1 The class-level interpretation is false

Let \(X=\mathbf P^1\), let \(\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\) have mass one, and let

\[
                         T=[0]\in[\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}].
\]

The class is ample, so its class numerical dimension is one.  The toric
convex weight of \([0]\) is affine, hence \(\Delta(T)\) is a point and has
dimension zero.  Cao's current-level product discards the divisorial part,
so \(\nu_{\mathrm{Cao}}(T)=0\).  Thus the desired identity is correct only
for the current-level invariant and false for \(\nu(\{T\})\).

The genuinely irrational version is

\[
               \alpha=\lambda[\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}],\qquad
               T=\lambda[0],\qquad
               \lambda\in\mathbf R_{>0}\setminus\mathbf Q.
\]

Again \(\nu(\alpha)=1\), while
\(\nu_{\mathrm{Cao}}(T)=\dim\Delta(T)=0\).

### 6.2 A genuinely irrational big class with a segment body

On \(X=\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\), let

\[
 \alpha=H_1+\sqrt2\,H_2,
 \qquad
 T=[\{0\}\times\mathbf P^1]
       +\sqrt2\,\pi_2^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}.
\]

The class is big and lies outside \(N^1(X)_{\mathbf Q}\).  Up to translation,

\[
                         \Delta(T)=\{0\}\times[0,\sqrt2],
\]

so \(\dim\Delta(T)=1\).  With
\(\omega=\pi_1^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}+\pi_2^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\),

\[
 \operatorname{vol}\bigl(\Delta(T)+s[0,1]^2\bigr)
          =\sqrt2\,s+s^2.
\]

The coefficient formula gives a nonzero first Cao product and zero second
Cao product, hence \(\nu_{\mathrm{Cao}}(T)=1\).

### 6.3 Extremes and zero-mass cases

- If \(T\) is a smooth invariant Kähler form in an irrational Kähler class,
  then \(K\) is full dimensional and \(\nu_{\mathrm{Cao}}(T)=n\).
- If \(T\) is a sum of invariant divisorial currents, then \(K\) is a point
  and \(\nu_{\mathrm{Cao}}(T)=0\), even when \(\{T\}\) is big.
- On a surface, if \(K\) is a nondegenerate segment, then
  \(\operatorname{vol}(K+sQ)=c_1s+c_2s^2\) with \(c_1>0\); hence the
  numerical dimension is one although the top non-pluripolar mass vanishes.
- For \(T=0\) in the zero class, \(K\) is a point and both numerical
  dimensions are zero.
- For a nef non-big class, for example
  \(T=\pi_1^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\) on
  \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\), the Newton body is a segment and both
  current-level numerical dimensions are one.  This confirms that bigness is
  unnecessary.

### 6.4 Big non-nef real classes

Let \(X=\operatorname{Bl}_0\mathbf P^2\), with \(H\) the pullback of a
line and \(E\) the exceptional divisor.  The irrational class

\[
                           2H+\sqrt2\,E
\]

is big and non-nef since its intersection with \(E\) is negative.  It has
invariant positive representatives, including purely divisorial ones.  The
proof above never uses nefness: it applies to each representative and reads
its current-level numerical dimension from that representative's Newton
body.  In particular, a purely divisorial representative has dimension zero,
whereas adding a non-divisorial invariant positive part enlarges the Newton
body accordingly.

### 6.5 Change of toric compactification

The body is constructed from the convex weight on the dense torus.  Changing
an equivariant trivialization translates it.  Under a smooth toric refinement
on which the current pulls back, the same convex weight is used, so the closed
Newton body and its dimension are unchanged.  The formula therefore passes
the fan-refinement test.  This does not assert that an arbitrary convex
weight extends positively to every toric compactification; extension is a
separate boundary-growth condition.

## 7. Hostile gap audit and discarded routes

1. **Rational approximation is unnecessary and unsafe.**  Hausdorff
   convergence of Newton bodies does not by itself imply convergence of
   quasi-equisingular products.  The proof fixes the current and varies it
   only by a smooth form.
2. **Cao Proposition 4.4 cannot be used in a real class.**  It is a
   line-bundle section-growth statement.  Only Definition 3 is used here.
3. **Cao and non-pluripolar products are not identified in general.**  Their
   equality in Step 2 uses toric \(\mathcal I\)-goodness and the fact that
   \(T+s\omega\) is a Kähler current.
4. **A generic analytic approximation is insufficient.**  The equality
   between its absolutely continuous top wedge and its non-pluripolar mass
   is justified here only after choosing the gentle approximation supplied
   by Theorem 1.6.2.  On its log resolution the residual part is a smooth
   semipositive form.  A bounded remainder in the general definition of
   analytic singularities does not provide this conclusion.
5. **The arbitrary real-class mass formula is a local extension, not a
   verbatim citation.**  Proposition 5.2.5 of the supplied book is stated
   with an ample toric reference.  Formula (P4) instead uses the local
   complex/real identity on \((\mathbf C^*)^n\), non-charging of the
   pluripolar toric boundary, and Alexandrov's total-mass formula.
6. **The Newton body must be closed.**  The formulas
   \(\partial F(N_{\mathbf R})=\operatorname{dom}F^*\) and
   \(\operatorname{dom}(F+G)^*=\operatorname{dom}F^*+
   \operatorname{dom}G^*\) can fail on boundary points.  The closure-safe
   formulation (D1), (P5) is sufficient for dimension and mixed volume.
7. **The class/current distinction is load bearing.**  If the live problem
   retains the phrase “numerical dimension defined by real positive
   products” without specifying the current-level product, the theorem is
   false.
8. **No top-mass positivity is assumed for \(T\).**  It is obtained after
   adding \(s\omega\), and the polynomial coefficients recover all
   lower-dimensional cases.
9. **The \(n!\) factor is fixed.**  It comes from the complex/real
   Monge--Ampère convention, while mixed volume is normalized by
   \(V(B[n])=\operatorname{vol}_M(B)\).
10. **There is no genuinely transcendental toric case on this \(X\).**  The
   only new scope beyond the rational theorem is irrational real divisor
   classes.
