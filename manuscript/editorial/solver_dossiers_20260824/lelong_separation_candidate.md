## 1. Exact source question and conventions

Let (u,v:(\mathbf C^n,0)\to\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\}) be psh germs.  In
the notation of Boucksom--Favre--Jonsson [BFJ], write

\[
 \widehat u,\widehat v:\mathcal V\longrightarrow[-\infty,0]
\]

for their valuative transforms on the normalized valuation space
\(\mathcal V=\{\nu:\nu(\mathfrak m)=1\}\).  The open implication in
[BFJ, Theorem A] is

\[
 \nu_\varphi(u)=\nu_\varphi(v)
 \quad\hbox{for every tame psh weight }\varphi
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \widehat u=\widehat v.
\tag{1.1}
\]

BFJ explicitly leave this implication open in the paragraph following
Theorem B and suggest fundamental solutions for their formal Monge--Ampere
operator.  The primary source is:

- S. Boucksom, C. Favre, M. Jonsson, *Valuations and plurisubharmonic
  singularities*, PRIMS **44** (2008), 449--494,
  [published PDF](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~prims/pdf/44-2/44-2-18.pdf),
  [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0702487v2).

The exact imported inputs used below are:

1. [BFJ, Proposition 1.14]: exceptional Cartier b-divisors are identified
   with piecewise-affine functions, and their integral group is generated by
   (Z(\mathfrak a)) for \(\mathfrak m\)-primary ideals \(\mathfrak a\).
2. [BFJ, Proposition 2.2]: an exceptional Cartier b-divisor determined on a
   model is nef exactly when its incarnation is relatively nef.
3. [BFJ, Theorem 5.1 and Proposition 5.6]: (Z(u)) is nef and
   \(\widehat u(\nu_E)=-\nu_E(u)\).
4. [BFJ, Lemma 5.10]: a psh weight whose exponential is Holder continuous is
   tame.
5. [BFJ, Theorem B]:

   \[
   \nu_\varphi(u)
    =\int_{\mathcal V}-\widehat u\,
       \operatorname{MA}(\widehat\varphi).
   \tag{1.2}
   \]

6. [BFJ, Propositions 4.6 and 4.9]: when the (n-1) Monge--Ampere inputs are
   piecewise affine, the integral in (1.2) is the b-divisor intersection and
   is computed on a model determining those inputs, using the trace of the
   remaining nef Weil b-divisor.

All formulas below use BFJ's variable (u\sim\log|f|\).  In the book's
squared-norm variable \(\Phi=2u\), both sides of a generalized-Lelong equality
are multiplied by \(2^n\); hence the hypothesis and conclusion are unchanged.

## 2. Candidate statement for exact verification

**Candidate statement.**  Let (u,v) be psh germs at a smooth point of a
complex manifold of dimension (n\geq1).  If

\[
                     \nu_\varphi(u)=\nu_\varphi(v)
\]

for every tame psh weight \(\varphi\), then

\[
                         \widehat u=\widehat v
\]

pointwise on the normalized valuation space \(\mathcal V\).  In fact it is
enough to test the weights

\[
 \varphi_{\mathfrak a}=\log\max_j|f_j|,
 \qquad \mathfrak a=(f_1,\ldots,f_N),
\tag{2.1}
\]

as \(\mathfrak a\) ranges over the \(\mathfrak m\)-primary ideals.

The conclusion is literal pointwise equality, not equality merely outside a
set of capacity zero, not equality almost everywhere for one measure, and not
equality modulo an additive constant.  It does not assert that (u-v\) is
bounded: the valuative transform already forgets bounded perturbations.

## 3. Finite-model lemmas

### 3.1 The primary-ideal weights are admissible

Let \(\mathfrak a=(f_1,\ldots,f_N)\) be \(\mathfrak m\)-primary and define
\(\varphi_{\mathfrak a}\) by (2.1).  Its pole is isolated because
\(V(\mathfrak a)=\{0\}\).  Moreover

\[
                 \exp(\varphi_{\mathfrak a})=\max_j|f_j|
\]

is locally Lipschitz, hence Holder continuous.  Thus
\(\varphi_{\mathfrak a}\) is tame by [BFJ, Lemma 5.10], and

\[
              \widehat\varphi_{\mathfrak a}=\log|\mathfrak a|,
 \qquad
              Z(\varphi_{\mathfrak a})=Z(\mathfrak a).
\tag{3.1}
\]

If \(\mathfrak a_1,\ldots,\mathfrak a_s\) are primary and
\(k_i\in\mathbf Z_{>0}\), then their product
\(\prod_i\mathfrak a_i^{k_i}\) is primary and

\[
 Z\!\left(\prod_i\mathfrak a_i^{k_i}\right)
                    =\sum_i k_i Z(\mathfrak a_i).
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is the source of polarization; no closure of the class of all tame
weights under arbitrary real sums is needed.

### 3.2 A multilinear functional with one Weil input

Let (W) be a nef Weil b-divisor and let
\(C_1,\ldots,C_{n-1}\) be exceptional Cartier b-divisors.  On any common
determination \(\pi:Y_\pi\to(\mathbf C^n,0)\) of the (C_i\), put

\[
 I_W(C_1,\ldots,C_{n-1})
 :=W_\pi\cdot(C_1)_\pi\cdots(C_{n-1})_\pi.
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is independent of the determination.  Indeed, if
\(\mu:Y_{\pi'}\to Y_\pi\) dominates \(\pi\), then
\(\mu_*W_{\pi'}=W_\pi\), while the (C_i\) pull back; the projection formula
gives the equality of (3.3) on the two models.  Hence (I_W\) is a symmetric
multilinear functional of its Cartier arguments.  When the (C_i\) are nef,
[BFJ, Propositions 4.6 and 4.9] give

\[
 I_W(C_1,\ldots,C_{n-1})
 =\int_{\mathcal V}g_W\,
      \operatorname{MA}(g_{C_1},\ldots,g_{C_{n-1}}).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Only the one input (W) is allowed to be a non-Cartier Weil b-divisor here.
This restriction is important: the proof does not posit an intersection
product of several arbitrary differences of nef Weil b-divisors.

For the two psh germs set

\[
 \Lambda(C_1,\ldots,C_{n-1})
 :=I_{Z(u)}(C_1,\ldots,C_{n-1})
   -I_{Z(v)}(C_1,\ldots,C_{n-1}).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus \(\Lambda\) is a well-defined symmetric multilinear form on the real
vector space of exceptional Cartier b-divisors.

### 3.3 Polarization from only diagonal generalized Lelong numbers

By (1.2), (3.1), and (3.4), the hypothesis gives

\[
                 \Lambda(Z(\mathfrak a),\ldots,Z(\mathfrak a))=0
\tag{3.6}
\]

for every primary ideal \(\mathfrak a\).  Fix primary ideals
\(\mathfrak a_1,\ldots,\mathfrak a_s\), put
\(W_i=Z(\mathfrak a_i)\), and consider

\[
 P(t_1,\ldots,t_s)
 :=\Lambda\!\left(\sum_i t_iW_i,\ldots,\sum_i t_iW_i\right).
\tag{3.7}
\]

This is a real polynomial, homogeneous of degree (n-1\).  Equations
(3.2) and (3.6) show that

\[
                         P(k_1,\ldots,k_s)=0
\]

for all positive integers \(k_i\).  A polynomial that vanishes on
\(\mathbf Z_{>0}^s\) is zero: this follows by induction on (s\), applying
the one-variable fact to infinitely many values in the last variable.
Therefore every coefficient in (3.7) vanishes, so

\[
                  \Lambda(W_{i_1},\ldots,W_{i_{n-1}})=0
\tag{3.8}
\]

for all choices of indices.  By [BFJ, Proposition 1.14], the integral group
of exceptional Cartier b-divisors is generated by the (Z(\mathfrak a)\)
with \(\mathfrak a\) primary.  Multilinearity now yields

\[
                  \boxed{\Lambda(C_1,\ldots,C_{n-1})=0}
\tag{3.9}
\]

for arbitrary real exceptional Cartier b-divisors (C_i\).

### 3.4 Separation on one model

Let \(\pi:Y\to(\mathbf C^n,0)\) be a simple modification, with exceptional
prime divisors (E_1,\ldots,E_r\).  For (n\geq2\), there exists an
exceptional Cartier divisor (A\) on (Y\), supported on
\(\pi^{-1}(0)\), such that (A\) is \(\pi\)-ample.  Indeed, \(\pi\) is
projective, hence has a relatively ample numerical divisor class.  In
[BFJ, Section 1.2] the natural map from the real vector space of exceptional
divisors on (Y\) to (NS(Y/X)_{\mathbf R}\) is an isomorphism.  The unique
exceptional representative (A\) of that relatively ample class is therefore
\(\pi\)-ample.  Concretely, one may also choose a \(\pi\)-ample Cartier
divisor (H_0\), observe that its pushforward is Cartier because the local
base is smooth and factorial, and take
\(A=H_0-\pi^*\pi_*H_0\).

For exceptional real divisors (D,C\) on (Y\), define

\[
                         Q_A(D,C)=D\cdot C\cdot A^{n-2}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Then (Q_A\) is nondegenerate on
\(\bigoplus_i\mathbf R E_i\); in fact it is negative definite.

Here is a proof retaining the local/global transition.  Compactify the
modification and resolve the boundary, without changing a neighborhood of
\(\pi^{-1}(0)\), to obtain a projective birational morphism
\(\bar\pi:\bar Y\to\bar X\) of smooth projective (n\)-folds.  Choose a
\(\bar\pi\)-ample Cartier divisor \(\bar A\).  Its relative numerical class
near the fibre over (0\) has a unique exceptional representative
\(A\in\operatorname{Div}(\pi)\), by the BFJ identification
\(\operatorname{Div}(\pi)\simeq NS(Y/X)_{\mathbf R}\).  This is the (A\)
used in (3.10).  For a sufficiently ample divisor (B\) on \(\bar X\),

\[
                           H=\bar A+\bar\pi^*B
\]

is ample on \(\bar Y\).  On every cycle supported in the exceptional fibre,
\(\bar A\) and (A\) have the same numerical class.  Moreover, every term
involving a factor \(\bar\pi^*B\) vanishes there, because a line bundle
pulled back from \(\bar X\) restricts trivially to a fibre over a point.
Consequently, for exceptional (D,C\),

\[
                 D\cdot C\cdot H^{n-2}
                  =D\cdot C\cdot A^{n-2}=Q_A(D,C).
\tag{3.11}
\]

The generalized Hodge index theorem says that the form
\(q_H(L_1,L_2)=L_1\cdot L_2\cdot H^{n-2}\) has signature
\((1,\rho(\bar Y)-1)\) on (N^1(\bar Y)_{\mathbf R}\).  The class
\(\bar\pi^*B\) has positive square for this form.  Every exceptional (D\)
is orthogonal to \(\bar\pi^*B\), again because (D\) maps to a point.
Thus (q_H\), and hence (Q_A\), is negative definite on the numerical span
of the (E_i\).

No nonzero exceptional divisor has numerically trivial class.  This is
already part of [BFJ, Section 1.2], where exceptional divisors are identified
with (NS(Y/X)_{\mathbf R}\).  Alternatively, if an exceptional (D\) were
\(\pi\)-numerically trivial, then both (D\) and \(-D\) would be
\(\pi\)-nef.  By [BFJ, Propositions 2.2 and 2.4], applied to the
corresponding Cartier b-divisors, one would have (D\leq0\) and
\(-D\leq0\), hence (D=0\).  Therefore (3.10) is negative definite on the
actual coefficient space \(\bigoplus_i\mathbf R E_i\), proving the claim.

For (n=2\), this is precisely the negative definiteness of the intersection
matrix of the exceptional curves.  For the blowup of a smooth point in any
dimension, (A=-E\) and
\(E^2A^{n-2}=-1\), providing a sign check.

## 4. Proof of the candidate statement

The case (n=1\) is immediate: \(\mathcal V\) consists of the single
valuation \(\operatorname{ord}_0\), and every generalized Lelong number in
dimension one is the ordinary Lelong number of the tested germ.  Hence the
single values of \(\widehat u\) and \(\widehat v\) agree.

Assume (n\geq2\).  Fix an arbitrary simple modification \(\pi:Y\to
(\mathbf C^n,0)\), and write

\[
 D:=Z(u)_\pi-Z(v)_\pi=\sum_{i=1}^r d_iE_i.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let (A\) be as in Section 3.4.  Any exceptional real divisor (C\) on
(Y\) determines a Cartier b-divisor, as does (A\).  Applying (3.9) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
  0
   &=\Lambda(C,A,\ldots,A)\\
   &=\bigl(Z(u)_\pi-Z(v)_\pi\bigr)\cdot C\cdot A^{n-2}\\
   &=Q_A(D,C).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since (Q_A\) is nondegenerate, (4.2) for every (C\) implies (D=0\).
The model \(\pi\) was arbitrary, so (Z(u)\) and (Z(v)\) have equal traces
on every simple modification.  Thus their coefficients agree at every
divisorial valuation.  The associated formal psh functions agree on each
quasimonomial simplex by continuity and density of its rational (divisorial)
points.  Finally their canonical extensions

\[
                  g(\nu)=\inf_\pi g(r_\pi(\nu))
\]

agree on all of \(\mathcal V\).  Hence \(\widehat u=\widehat v\) pointwise.
This proves the candidate statement, subject only to the explicitly imported
BFJ results and the standard generalized Hodge index theorem used in
Section 3.4.

## 5. Required first test cases

### 5.1 Surface germs

On a fixed surface modification, write
\(D=\sum d_iE_i\).  Polarization is unnecessary because (n-1=1\): the
hypothesis directly gives (D\cdot Z(\mathfrak a)=0\) for every primary
ideal.  Those ideal divisors span all exceptional Cartier divisors, so
\(D\cdot E_j=0\) for every (j\).  The matrix \((E_i\cdot E_j)\) is negative
definite, hence (D=0\).  This proves the full conclusion in dimension two
without any fundamental solution on the valuative tree.

### 5.2 Finite polyhedral/model data

Suppose in any dimension that both transforms are being inspected on a fixed
dual complex of a simple modification.  Their vertex differences are the
coefficient vector of (4.1).  Equations (3.6)--(3.9) supply all linear
functionals

\[
                    D\longmapsto D\cdot C\cdot A^{n-2}.
\]

The matrix of these functionals in the exceptional-prime basis is the
negative-definite matrix of (Q_A\).  Thus the finite vertex vector is
recovered exactly.  Convex continuity recovers the values on every face.
There is no passage from almost-everywhere equality to pointwise equality in
this argument.

### 5.3 Monomial germs

Choose a common toric log resolution of finitely many monomial ideals.  The
exceptional divisors are torus invariant, and the primary-ideal weights are
the convex integral piecewise-linear support functions of their Newton
polyhedra.  Products of ideals add these support functions, so the polynomial
polarization in (3.7) is exactly polarization of mixed multiplicities.  The
toric intersection matrix obtained from (Q_A\) is a restriction of the same
negative-definite form.  Therefore equality against all monomial primary
ideal weights already separates two transforms that are carried by the fixed
toric model.  The full proof uses nonmonomial primary ideals only to range
over arbitrary (not necessarily toric) models.

### 5.4 Blowup sign and normalization check

For the blowup of (0\in\mathbf C^n\), with exceptional divisor
\(E\simeq\mathbf P^{n-1}\), take (A=-E\).  Since
\(E|_E=\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^{n-1}}(-1)\),

\[
                         E^2(-E)^{n-2}=-1.
\]

The one coefficient of (Z(u)_\pi-Z(v)_\pi\) is therefore detected with
the expected sign.  The normalization \(\nu_E=\operatorname{ord}_E/
\operatorname{ord}_E(\mathfrak m)\) only inserts the same positive factor
in both generalized-Lelong values and does not affect nondegeneracy.

## 6. Hostile audit and exact failure boundaries

1. **No illicit polarization of singular currents.**  Polarization is
   performed only after BFJ's Theorem B has converted each diagonal analytic
   generalized Lelong number into a finite b-divisor intersection.  The
   polynomial values come from actual primary product ideals.  No mixed
   Bedford--Taylor product of (u\) or (v\) is introduced.
2. **Only one Weil input.**  Formula (3.3) contains one nef Weil b-divisor
   (Z(u)\) or (Z(v)\) and (n-1) Cartier inputs.  Projection formula makes
   this legitimate.  The proof never subtracts arbitrary nef Weil divisors
   inside BFJ's nonlinear extended intersection domain.
3. **Tameness is sourced.**  The analytic ideal weights have Lipschitz
   exponentials and are tame by [BFJ, Lemma 5.10].  It is unnecessary to
   assert that every formal psh function is the transform of a tame analytic
   weight.
4. **Cartier span is exact.**  [BFJ, Proposition 1.14], rather than a density
   argument, says that the integral Cartier group is generated by divisors of
   primary ideals.  Thus (3.9) is exact and no limiting continuity is hidden.
5. **Pointwise endpoint.**  All exceptional coefficients are recovered on
   every model.  Divisorial density and strong continuity recover each
   quasimonomial simplex; BFJ's infimum-over-retractions extension recovers
   every valuation.  The endpoint is not merely quasi-everywhere.
6. **Bounded terms and constants.**  Both generalized Lelong numbers and
   valuative transforms ignore bounded perturbations.  The theorem separates
   valuative transforms, not full psh germs modulo equality as functions.
7. **Moving-pole skill audit.**  The `lelong-clean-moving-singular-limit`
   mechanism does **not** trigger: there is no weakly convergent current, no
   moving singular kernel, and no exchange of a spatial and regularization
   limit.  In particular, the proof does not infer separation from zero
   density or from almost-everywhere identities.  Its only role here was to
   enforce this non-trigger check.
8. **Potentially vulnerable imported step.**  The only substantial input not
   stated inside BFJ is the negative definiteness in Section 3.4.  The proof
   given there reduces it to the generalized Hodge index theorem plus the
   elementary negativity argument excluding numerically trivial exceptional
   divisors.  A Rethlas verifier should attack: (a) existence of an exceptional
   relatively ample (A\) on every BFJ modification; (b) preservation of the
   local intersection numbers under compactification; and (c) vanishing of
   all pullback-(B\) cross terms in (3.11).
9. **Literature status.**  A targeted search found BFJ's original open
   formulation and later relative-type/Zhou-weight results, but no primary
   source claiming this converse.  This is not enough for a priority claim.
   Even if the proof verifies, the book should state it only as a new
   book-level result with a dated verification receipt and invite comparison
   with any unpublished or differently formulated resolution.
