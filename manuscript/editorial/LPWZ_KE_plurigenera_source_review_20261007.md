## Main equations and proof qualifications

**K01 — Family and announced conclusion.** LPWZ Theorems 1.1--1.2,
physical pp.1--2, and 9.1, p.36, concern a proper holomorphic submersion
from a Kähler manifold to the unit disk, with compact fibres. The claimed
conclusion is constancy of every plurigenus and extension of every
pluricanonical section. It is distinct from the previously known
projective case, big-canonical cases, threefold cases and the new
preprint's canonical-metric construction. Reading its complete 40-page
text does not independently verify all its MMP, extension and PDE inputs.

**K02 — Load-bearing reference volume.** Theorem 1.3, p.5, chooses
two Kähler forms `omega` and `beta`, sets
`alpha=theta_omega+beta`, assumes all `alpha_t` big, and writes
`MA_alpha_t(phi_t)=exp(phi_t) beta_t^n`. Its proof, pp.33--34,
defines `J=log(mu^*omega_t^n/eta_t^n)` and obtains equation (7.8)
with `exp(phi_t) omega_t^n`. The canonical curvature and Jacobian
identity use the `omega`-induced metric. General replacement of the
reference volume changes the equation and cannot silently retain the
same total-space semipositivity conclusion. The proof as displayed
therefore does not establish the literal independent-volume statement.
No general corrected theorem or verified counterexample is credited.

The special choice `beta=eta omega`, for a constant positive scalar
`eta`, deserves separate treatment: its volume changes by the scalar
`eta^n`, which can be absorbed by an additive constant in the solution.
That special mismatch alone does not refute the Section 8 route or
plurigenera invariance. Section 8 in fact writes the `omega_t^n`
equation. Other gates in that route remain to be checked.

**K03 — Proposed density test is unverified.** The working diagnostic
considers a product elliptic curve and disk, a flat vertical `beta`
with a small horizontal part, and an `omega` density perturbed by
`lambda |t|^2` times a mean-zero fibre Laplacian. Its tentative
linearized equation is `(1-Delta)b=f`; negative values of a nonconstant
mean-zero `b` could test the literal total-space positivity assertion.
Exact `ddc` conventions, smooth implicit dependence and independent
verification are still missing. The candidate is not a certified
counterexample, is not used downstream, and cannot appear in theorem
typography. No `gpt-6.1-sol`/`xhigh` run or receipt is fabricated.

**K04 — Pseudoeffectivity before removing the twist.** Theorem 8.1,
pp.34--35, is written without an explicit fibrewise canonical
pseudoeffectivity premise. Its proof applies Theorem 1.3 to
`theta+eta omega` for every positive `eta`, and compares with `V_theta`.
Both uses require that premise. In the nonzero pluricanonical-section
application, Section 9 explicitly invokes [CP25] to reduce to it;
Hacon--Xie 7.8 is another source-attributed family input. Neither is
recursively certified here. A product with a projective-line fibre has
negative canonical degree and cannot admit the standalone untwisted
positive metric. This elementary boundary identifies the missing
premise; it does not disprove the claimed extension of a nonzero section.

**K05 — Three distinct normalizations.** Theorem 5.3, p.22,
concerns a nonexponential MA equation. Uniqueness and a lower bound
relative to the sup-zero envelope need an additive normalization,
such as `sup phi=0`. Corrected DGG Theorems 1.1 and 1.9 state that
normalization; the LWZ uniqueness proof at archived p.28 uses it.
An exponential equation instead fixes its constant through its right
side. Section 8 sets `W=integral omega_t^n`,
`v_eta=vol(alpha_eta,t)` and
`u_eta=phi_eta-log(v_eta/W)`, with `dnu_t=omega_t^n/W`.
Then `integral exp(u_eta,t)dnu_t=1`. The subtractand is one scalar
on the total space only after constancy of `v_eta` and `W` is known.
The fibrewise supremum-normalized `u_eta,t-sup u_eta,t` is an auxiliary
object for estimates; independently subtracting fibre suprema does not
automatically preserve total-space psh variation.

**K06 — Comparison at the correct parameter level.** Proposition 7.1,
p.32, states a finite-parameter domination `u_epsilon<=U_epsilon`,
whereas the preceding limiting measure comparison establishes
`u<=U_hat`. The latter, together with the relevant boundary limits,
is the inequality to retain in a limiting submean discussion. The
finite-parameter assertion is not cleared merely by taking limits.
No original comparison-repair theorem is introduced.

**K07 — Order of regularization.** The overview on p.25 mentions
epsilon then tau; the operative argument on p.31 takes tau first,
then epsilon. For a fixed positive model, decreasing densities
`f_tau` make the corresponding exponential solutions increase.
Stability and Dirichlet estimates must retain their precise background,
moment and boundary assumptions before either order is changed. These
inner parameters are also distinct from the outer canonical twist `eta`.

**K08 — Exceptional fibres.** Lemma 7.2, pp.32--33, combines a
Fatou upper bound for the exponential mass, bounded maximum
subsolutions, a punctured-parameter Choquet supremum and a mass
sandwich. The selected parameters must avoid the exceptional fibre
until its measure inequality has been established. Strong upper
semicontinuity identifies the decreasing fibre supremum. Passing the
truncation level to infinity uses nonpluripolar locality on finite
levels; ordinary Bedford--Taylor convergence is not invoked for an
arbitrary unbounded product. This is an explanation of the source
steps with their hypotheses, not an independent general theorem.

**K09 — Green function and constants.** The apparent missing complex
conjugate in the extracted p.26 Green formula was a transcription
false positive. The individually displayed high-resolution crop
retains it (SHA-256
`14722dbb1e0acf8256011f368692b0d765fe3dd93f80cf391996735b107164a7`).
With the appropriate `i/(2pi)` convention, the Green distribution
is boundary harmonic measure minus the pole mass. The introduction
and Section 2.2 use different `ddc` descriptions, and p.21 mixes
log absolute values and log squares in a fibre-cycle formula.
Choose a single convention before importing formulas. Approximation
of the Green kernel also requires the weighted/variation control
used in the integral argument; distributional convergence alone is
not sufficient. No new unbounded-current identity is promoted.

**K10 — Bergman route.** Section 6 uses local kernels with bounded
Hessian weights and a positive background `b+epsilon eta`. Berman--
Berndtsson v3 Theorem 2.1 / Corollary 2.2, pp.7--11, use
`n! K exp(-k phi)/k^n` with their `i/(2pi)` curvature convention;
the `n!` is not a missing-pi error. The convergence is local `L1`
under the specified curvature bounds, with almost-everywhere
convergence on a subsequence, not uniform pointwise convergence.
Berndtsson's published Theorem 1.1 and its first printed proof,
physical pp.2--3, 6--12, give joint psh variation through smooth
product-domain estimates, penalized weights, domain exhaustion,
semicontinuity and Oka translations. The joint weight hypothesis
cannot be replaced by fibrewise positivity alone. Hörmander,
Neumann regularity and nonsmooth-boundary approximation remain
imported foundations.

The Section 6 partition terms cancel after the coordinate-independent
kernel-ratio limit is identified. Divisor error masses tend to zero
for the fixed model because
`integral D_i,t P_t^(n-1)=0` and `P_epsilon=P+epsilon eta`.
This fixed-model estimate is not an estimate uniform in the outer
canonical twist.

**K11 — Extension metric and local/global scope.** A metric on
`(m-1)K` has weight `(m-1)Phi`; the p.9 display with
`h_omega^(m-1) exp(-Phi)` must not be copied instead of the
Section 9 weight `exp(-(m-1)Phi)`, p.36. The L2 extension
signature recalled in Theorem 3.1, pp.8--9, is attributed to
Zhou--Zhu's published Theorem 1.1 / Remark 1.3, whose actual PDF
has not been cleared. Bounded defining-map and weakly pseudoconvex
Kähler assumptions cannot be checked from an access-block page.

Theorem 9.1 states extension over the original disk, but its proof
uses a metric constructed after shrinking the disk. A local-to-global
argument through local extension at every parameter, constant-rank
direct images, base change and the Stein disk would require its own
primary anchors and explanation. That bridge is not credited here.
The audit distinguishes this proof-scope gate from a counterexample
to extension itself.

## Dependency signatures and unresolved inputs

**D01 — Fixed positive twists and MMP.** Hacon--Xie v1 Theorems
1.1(2), 1.4 and 1.5, their definitions on pp.6--17 and applications

Their non-big arguments involve other inputs, including a canonical
bundle formula cited as July 2026 personal communication/to appear.
Reading 22 of 53 pages does not certify the inductive MMP chain.

Lemma 2.10's displayed support condition on an exceptional divisor
does not state the effectivity used in its proof. The LPWZ application
uses an effective exceptional divisor; retain that scope instead
of importing arbitrary signed divisors. Lemma 2.39 has a compact
normal/class-C signature and rational-singularity/cohomology or
rational-connectedness assumptions. It does not by its wording
provide a noncompact-family Bott--Chern descent theorem.
The source's strong-Moishezon witnesses and relative ample-line-bundle
definition of projectivity should not be conflated with a global
embedding into a projective-space product.

**D02 — Central contraction and class descent.** LPWZ Lemma 4.18,
pp.14--17, imports Kollár--Mori Proposition 11.4 for extension of
the central contraction and Bănică--Stănăşilă Chapter III Lemma 1.3

descent argument. Demailly--Păun published Proposition 3.3(i),
physical pp.16--17 / printed pp.1262--1263, supplies positivity
near an analytic subspace once the smooth class and Kähler
restriction are available. Its printed compact-ambient statement

does not bypass the preceding class-descent gate. The acquired
DHY appendix has not been read and cannot clear these inputs.

**D03 — Model and divisorial volume.** LPWZ Theorem 4.19,
pp.17--20, constructs a normal flat model, a Kähler resolution,
a pulled-back semipositive class `P`, and an effective exceptional
part, after shrinking. Good fibres avoid a discrete bad set and
have the modification/SNC properties used later. The critical
image's discreteness requires the proper analytic critical-image
argument, not Sard's theorem alone. The undefined `Sp18` notation
is not turned into an authenticated citation.

Boucksom published Theorem 3.18, physical pp.16--17, concerns a
**big modified-nef** class and the exceptional prime components of
its non-Kähler locus. The arbitrary modified-nef boundary has a
different countable-union statement. Proposition 3.6, p.11,
identifies class minimal multiplicities with those of a minimal
current when the class is big; for a general pseudoeffective class
the displayed statement is only an inequality. Proposition 3.20,
p.17, preserves volume under the complex divisorial Zariski
projection, using its absolutely-continuous-current definition of
volume. Comparison with nonpluripolar/cohomological volume is an
additional known big-Kähler input. None of these complex-current
results repairs the non-Archimedean BJ22 Theorem 6.21 proof or
the unresolved `(SD)_k` division condition.

**D04 — Uniform fibre estimates.** Corrected DGG v3 Theorems
1.1 and 1.9, pp.8--9, 14--15, require positive big volume,

chart condition; this is not global isotriviality of the fibres.
The complete printed locally-trivial proof on pp.28--29 was read.
It uses finite overlapping charts, noncollapsing L1 compactness
and comparable densities. General Conjecture 3.1 remains a
conjecture in this version; reducible fibres are not admitted by
this mean/sup comparison.

Theorem 2.9, p.21, first has a bound containing an exponential
of the fibre mean; Proposition 3.3 supplies the needed mean
control. Its proof on pp.23--25 was not read. Lemma 2.11's
isolated-singularity weighted products require well-defined

silently treated by bounded Bedford--Taylor calculus. The smooth
test-function/Green-kernel application is narrower. This is an

**D05 — DGG corrigendum scope.** All five published corrigendum

**D06 — Stability and boundary regularity.** Guedj--Lu's published
envelope-estimate paper, rather than their differently titled
