# P06.3 hostile audit: the literal harmonic-approximant formulation

Date: 25 August 2026 (Asia/Shanghai)

Status: exact packet audited before production verification.  No live chapter,
problem ledger, or atomic inventory was edited.

## Scope separation

The live third question literally asks for decreasing ordinary CLD-smooth
**harmonic** approximants.  This is not the assertion of either source:

- GJR Proposition 15.11 gives weakly smooth **psh** approximants on every
  rig-smooth boundaryless curve.
- Wanner Corollary 5.4 gives ordinary CLD-smooth **psh** approximants only
  under its three residue-field/curve hypotheses.  Remark 5.5 leaves the
  unrestricted ordinary-smooth psh problem open.

The candidate packet disproves only the literal harmonic-approximant target.
It does not claim a counterexample to ordinary CLD-smooth psh regularization.

## Source checks

1. Ordinary CLD-smooth functions are smooth `(0,0)`-forms locally pulled back
   from smooth Lagerberg functions along moment maps (CLD 3.1.7 and 3.4.7;
   Wanner Definitions 3.9--3.12).  A first production run rejected the
   earlier pinpoint to CLD 3.1.3; that negative history is preserved.
2. GJR Propositions 10.14 and 10.17 imply that an ordinary smooth function
   with zero `d'd''` is GJR harmonic; Proposition 15.7 identifies that class
   with Thuillier harmonic functions on rig-smooth curves.
3. GJR Remark 15.10 identifies ordinary smooth harmonic germs with the real
   span of logarithms of analytic units.  The quotient
   `H_P/F_P = Pic^0(E) tensor R` is Thuillier Lemma 2.3.22 as used in the
   proof of GJR Proposition 17.4; it is not attributed to Remark 15.10 in the
   corrected packet.
4. In GJR Section 17 the three directions at the theta vertex correspond to
   `x,y,z` on the residual elliptic curve.  The lifted coordinate is integral
   PL and harmonic near the vertex, and its residual line-bundle class is
   `[z]-[x]`.  Thus its slope divisor is supported in `{x,y,z}` and has the
   asserted nonzero class (up to the harmless global sign convention for
   slope divisors).
5. The non-torsion hypothesis is enough after tensoring: the infinite cyclic
   subgroup generated by `[z]-[x]` stays injected because `R` is a
   torsion-free, hence flat, `Z`-module.

## Hostile mathematical checks

### Fixed tangent support

The first draft incorrectly spoke of a function defined on a neighbourhood
of the outer closed star, although the approximants live only on the star's
open interior.  The corrected proof uses two stars:

- an outer compact strict affinoid `Y_0`, with `U=Y_0 minus boundary(Y_0)`;
- a fixed inner compact strict affinoid `Y` compactly contained in `U`.

The semistable model of `Y` induced from the theta model has skeleton exactly
the three arms and boundary exactly their three endpoints.  A harmonic
function on `U` restricts to `H(Y)`.  Model-independence of `H(Y)` forces it
to factor through this particular three-arm retraction.  Therefore there are
no hidden slopes in residual tangent directions, and balancing makes the
function affine with constant slope on each arm.  This is stronger and safer
than an unsupported maximum-principle argument on individual open balls.

### Closure of the obstruction

All residual slope divisors lie in the three-point finite-dimensional space
`V_S`.  For ordinary smooth harmonic germs their Picard class vanishes, so
they lie in the kernel `P_S` of the linear map from `V_S` to
`Pic^0(E) tensor R`.  Every linear subspace of finite-dimensional `V_S` is
closed; no topology on the full divisor group or on the Picard group is used.

Uniform convergence on the fixed inner star gives convergence of each arm
slope by the endpoint-value formula.  A decreasing pointwise net of
continuous functions converging to the continuous target is uniform on the
compact inner star by the net form of Dini's argument.  Thus nets and
sequences are both excluded.

### Psh firewall

The slope-convergence step uses zero curvature to make every approximant
affine on the fixed arms.  For positive-curvature approximants, uniform
convergence of convex functions does not imply endpoint-derivative
convergence.  The proof therefore supplies no obstruction to the psh
regularization left open by Wanner Remark 5.5.

## Source-ordering and exact-live release addendum

The direction ordering printed with GJR Proposition 17.4 is not copied into
the live proof because the coordinate formulas in Section 17.2 use a
different indexing convention.  Only the invariant content needed here is
retained: the three slopes are a permutation of `1,-1,0`, hence balanced,
while the same construction separately computes the residual class
`[z]-[x]`.  This avoids tying the proof to a direction label and makes no
reader-facing claim of source error.  The resulting global-model live proof
passed production run `20260825T055907Z_b5c8f0719b60`, item
`pi_d80993bb01a748f0422ae6a5`, final `correct`, with zero errors and gaps.
