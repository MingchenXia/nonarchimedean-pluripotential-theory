## Finite-bad-point gate

For a regular numerator `a`, its order `r` along the smooth center is the
degree of its first nonzero transverse initial form

```text
in_C(a) in I_C^r / I_C^(r+1) = Sym^r(I_C/I_C^2).
```

This is a nonzero rational, and after shrinking regular, section of a vector
bundle on `C = P1`.  Its zero locus and the complement of the shrinking are
finite.  At every other point, the transverse conormal space injects into
the full cotangent space.  The nonzero transverse degree-`r` form therefore
remains nonzero in `m_p^r/m_p^(r+1)`, proving `ord_p(a)=r`.

For a rational function `f=a/b`, apply the argument separately to numerator
and denominator and take the union of their finite bad sets.  This avoids the
cancellation flaw in the rejected first `P2` proof.  The point-blowup
valuation is the `m_p`-adic order, so

```text
ord_{E_p}(f)=ord_p(a)-ord_p(b)=v_C(a)-v_C(b)=v_C(f)
```

off that finite set.  Pairwise distinct centers then give eventual equality
for each fixed rational function and hence weak convergence.

The dimension-one center is essential: a nonzero vector-bundle section on a
higher-dimensional center can vanish on a positive-dimensional locus, which
pairwise distinct points need not eventually avoid.

## Exhaustion gate

For a point, the multiplicity of a degree-`m` polynomial is at most `m`,
with equality for a power of a hyperplane through the point.  For the linear
line, its ideal is generated by linear forms, so a degree-`m` polynomial has
center order at most `m`, with equality for a power of a hyperplane
containing the line.  Definition 1.26 and Lemma 4.46 therefore give
`T_omega(v_j)=T_omega(v_C)=1`, with no degree or normalization factor.

## Strong-gap gate

A hyperplane through `p_j` but not containing `C` has point order one and
center order zero.  The exact section formula in Boucksom--Jonsson Lemma
11.7 gives `d_infty(v_j,v_C) >= 1`.
