## Model consequence and scope qualifications

Coning the published simplex with degree h gives a normal affine
toric family over C[tau]. Its generic fibre is a torus. Its central
fibre is reduced: the principal parameter ideal equals the
intersection of the ray-prime ideals, since all primitive original
rays have height one. The cone has index 39 and is singular.
A smooth toric subdivision with reduced central fibre over the same
parameter would slice to a unimodular triangulation, contradicting
the precisely imported Example 10. This is a direct local toric
consequence of a known result. It excludes no nontoric construction
and contradicts no reduction after base change.

The constant-coordinate family's coherent classification is special;
its IDP equivalence is not generalized to arbitrary one-row simplices.
Projectivity requires a common coherent lifting. Neither IDP nor a
binary Hilbert cover supplies one by itself.

Firla–Ziegler's general smooth-projective-toric-resolution wording
on printed page 207 retains the Hilbert-basis restriction in this
application. Unrestricted resolution may add higher-degree rays.
Its normality wording on page 213 retains the degree-one grading:
every saturated cone ring is normal, irrespective of the degrees
of its Hilbert basis. These are application boundaries, not a claim
that the published obstruction or principal combinatorial tests fail.
The historical introductory conjecture language is not treated as
the 2026 state of the art; the note added in proof already announces
a Hilbert-cover counterexample.
