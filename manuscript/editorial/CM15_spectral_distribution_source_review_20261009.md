## The applicable non-Archimedean construction

Section 3 separately fixes a complete non-Archimedean ground field and
finite-dimensional spaces with genuine ultrametric norms. The degree is
logarithmic determinant-psi minus logarithmic determinant-phi. It is
independent of basis. It is not defined by locally compact Haar balls,
and no local compactness, discrete value group or spherical completeness
is assumed. Exact orthogonality can fail; near-orthogonal bases are used.

Propositions 3.4–3.5 control dual and tensor norms by the explicit
near-orthogonality factors. The quotient determinant norm has the
Hadamard upper bound and the alpha^r lower bound. Letting losses tend
to zero gives exact degree additivity along subquotient flags.
Proposition 3.6 controls the concave degree polygon by operator distance.
Proposition 3.7 constructs a basis nearly orthogonal for both norms,
with fourth-root and square-root losses deliberately allocated, and then
passes from simultaneous diagonal norms to arbitrary norms. This is an
actual source for common diagonal approximation; dense lattice norms
and exact simultaneous splitting are stronger and are not inferred.

Proposition 3.8, on physical page 17, is the exact finite-dimensional
truncation identity. If r=dim V, mu_i are the slopes for psi/phi and
phi(a)=exp(a) phi, then

    deg(V,phi,max(psi,phi(a))) = sum_i max(mu_i,a).

The maximum is a genuine norm. In a common diagonal basis the identity
is immediate; determinant/polygon continuity passes it to nonsplit
norms. The second diagonal display in the proof mistakenly retains psi
in its numerator instead of the truncated norm; the preceding sentence
and conclusion make the required correction explicit.

For the empirical probability of slopes divided by original degree n,
the stop-loss mean is correspondingly

    E max(Z_n,a)
      = sup_{0<=t<=r_n}(tilde P_n(t)-n a t)/(n r_n) + a.

Proposition 5.1 proves that convergence of all such means, with uniformly
bounded random variables, implies weak convergence. It tests smooth
compactly supported functions through the integral of h'' against
E[(Z_n-t)_+], then uses positivity and the common compact support to
obtain a probability measure. This is a bounded-variable argument,
not an assertion that arbitrary unbounded first moments converge weakly.

## Semigroup hypotheses and the negative-tail step

Sections 4.1–4.3 require an integral projective X of positive dimension
and a regular k-rational point. The point is explicitly assumed to exist.
One-dimensional Okounkov leaves use its residue field k and local
parameters. The valuation semigroup must have Gamma_0={0}, lie in a
cone generated by finitely many degree-one elements, and generate the
full group Z^(d+1). These retain bounded positive-volume bodies and the
correct lattice normalization. No arbitrary semiample/big shortcut or
scalar-extension reduction is supplied by this reading.

Each negative logarithmic leaf norm, measured against a canonical
norm-one monomial reference, is superadditive. The relative leaf degree
is a difference of two such functions; a difference of superadditive
functions need not itself be superadditive. The repeated phi in the
last explanatory sentence on page 19 should be psi in its second
occurrence, as the preceding displayed difference shows.

Theorem 4.3, physical page 21, imposes a finite upper bound theta on
Phi/n. It identifies the limit closed-tail body volume only for t!=theta.
At theta the limit tail is assigned by its left limit; convergence is
asserted at continuity points. This is the correct endpoint qualification
missing from the later Reboulet v1 display. On
Gamma={(n,j):0<=j<=n}, the superadditive Phi(n,j)=n-sqrt(n) has normalized
law tending to delta_1 while every finite closed tail at 1 is empty.
There is no contradiction to the correctly qualified Chen–Maclean
Theorem 4.3.

Theorem 4.5 also requires both graded norm families to be
submultiplicative, their operator distance O(n), and an exponential
lower bound on canonical leaf norms. Its proof obtains two concave
transforms, a uniform bound A on their paired differences, and weak
convergence of the marginal laws. On page 23 it clips each variable
below at -T. The clipped marginal expectations converge and are finite;
the difference of limiting transforms is bounded. This avoids subtraction
of possibly negative-infinite limiting means and does not require joint
weak convergence of the paired variables.

The intermediate estimate in (33) incorrectly multiplies
E|X_n-Y_n| by 2 epsilon. Correlation of rare tails does not permit that
factorization. The correct direct estimate is

    |E[(X_n-Y_n)-(max(X_n,-T)-max(Y_n,-T))]|
      <= A P(X_n<=-T or Y_n<=-T) <= 2 epsilon A.

This retains the final estimate actually needed in the source proof.
The rare-event diagnostic with probability 1/100, epsilon=1/10,
X=-T-2 and Y=-T-1 on that event and X=Y=0 elsewhere gives error 1/100,
while the printed middle bound is 1/500. The correct final bound 1/5
holds. No undefined infinite arithmetic is used in this replacement.

Corollary 4.6 applies to sup norms of fixed continuous metrics, using
compactness for O(n) operator distance and a canonical coefficient
estimate. The coefficient step is attributed to Chambert-Loir's 2002
Bourbaki exposition, physical/published pages 205–206. That primary

## Exact normalization and display corrections

Nine elementary diagnostic families are archived with their computations
and formal explanations. They distinguish defects of literal displays
from the intended correctly calibrated statements.

1. Ordinary complex Haar measure is real two-dimensional per complex
   coordinate. On C, phi=|z| and psi=2|z| give ball log-volume ratio
   2 log 2, but determinant degree log 2. Equation (3) and the assertion
   that the arbitrary-norm full-Haar degree equals (4) mix conventions.
   A half-log-Haar calibration agrees with the determinant degree.
   Section 2.2 itself correctly writes Gram eigenvalues exp(2 mu_i),
   so mu_i is half the logarithm of the Gram eigenvalue. Under the
   literal full-Haar definition Corollary 2.6's constant-one scaling
   bound and Proposition 2.8's threshold a fail in complex rank one.
   This issue is separate from the determinant-calibrated non-Archimedean
   construction and does not refute a consistently normalized intended
   spectral convergence theorem.
2. The parallelogram displays in Proposition 2.1 omit squared norms;
   e1/e2 in the usual Euclidean plane give 2 sqrt 2 instead of 4.
   The diagonal sums in Proposition 2.8 need |x_i|^2, not x_i^2, for
   complex coefficients. The determinant operator direction in
   Proposition 2.1 must be phi-to-psi, as its lambda controls.
3. A normalized polygon P_V is on [0,1]; the complete-flag identity is
   tilde P_V(i)=deg(V_i), or P_V(i/r)=deg(V_i)/r. Theorem 5.2(2)
   additionally needs P_n/n, not P_n alone. On P1 with scalar metrics
   psi=e^c phi, all degree-n slopes are nc and P_n(t)=nc t. The missing
   degree division is a literal counterexample to that unscaled display,
   without disproving the degree-rescaled weak-convergence assertion (1).
4. Proposition 3.1's one-norm induction reuses the same (1-epsilon)
   loss for the old basis and the quotient lift. Its bounds can lose
   their product. Over trivially valued Q, use diagonal weights
   (1,9/10,81/100), e1=v1, e2=v1+v2 and er=v2+v3. Both old-basis and
   lift bounds hold with 9/10, but e1-e2+er=v3 has norm 81/100.
   Allocate two smaller losses whose product is at least the target.
   This elementary bookkeeping correction preserves the intended
   existence statement; it does not assert that near-orthogonal bases
   fail to exist.
5. Proposition 3.8's exact max-norm determinant identity is verified,
   with its corrected numerator. For split logarithmic ratios (-2,0,3)
   and threshold 1, the truncated degree is 5 and the mean 5/3.
6. Theorem 4.3 correctly excludes theta. Display (32) needs a sum on
   the empirical side and h composed with G_Phi on the integral side;
   h=1 immediately checks probability normalization. Footnote 8 needs
   E[h(Z)], not E[Z].
7. The correlated-tail middle inequality in (33) is replaced by the
   union-probability estimate above. Its final needed bound survives.
8. Degree-n metric scaling is exp(na). The maximum metric gives
   exactly max(psi_n,exp(na)phi_n) on sections. For Remark 5.3's o(n)
   perturbations, the polygon Lipschitz/Legendre formulas directly
   bound stop-loss mean changes by (d_phi+d_psi)/n in the
   non-Archimedean convention. Its printed proof instead mixes the
   unprimed phi_n into the primed truncation; one must not treat that
   expression as the exact primed stop-loss identity. This direct
   finite-dimensional explanation does not use any general envelope.
9. Rational regular-point, admissible lattice, analytic coefficient,
   complex energy/L2 and polygon-convergence dependencies remain
   scoped. The internal footer is recorded separately from version
   metadata. No proof of arbitrary bounded graded-norm convergence
   or blanket field-extension reduction is claimed from this unit.
