## Known inputs and proof boundaries

All spaces are on a compact complex Kähler manifold with a smooth closed real representative of a big class. The nonpluripolar volume `V` is positive, and endpoint potentials lie in the full-mass finite-p-energy class. Ray sublinearity and the common initial point are retained. There is no arbitrary normal non-Archimedean extension.

Gupta Theorem 1.2, realized by Theorems 4.1 and 4.6, gives convexity of distance along the selected weak geodesic segments for every finite `p >= 1`. Its p=1 proof uses the rooftop energy formula, monotonicity of energy and its affinity on geodesics. For p>1, the proof first treats the big-and-nef case by nearby Kähler representatives and the envelope contraction of Lemma 2.15; it then uses analytic-singularity approximations and the earlier big-class metric construction. The approximation distance in equation (9) is a distance in the relevant prescribed-singularity space. It is not the global full-mass distance applied to a partial-mass potential.

The book uses the precise weak-geodesic inequality. It does not turn the term “Buseman convex” into a claim about every possible d1 metric geodesic, or assert d1 geodesic uniqueness. For two rays with the same base, applying that inequality on [0,t] makes `d_p(u_s,v_s)/s <= d_p(u_t,v_t)/t` for `0<s<t`. Triangle inequalities bound the ratios by the sum of the two speeds. Thus their limit is the supremum, is finite, and vanishes only when the two rays agree. This is the source's Lemma 5.1 and the immediately preceding calculation, written in normalized units.

The source's Theorem 5.2 identifies ray spaces with different finite-energy bases by parallel transport. Its proof constructs limits of long geodesic segments in ordered cases, then uses a rooftop intermediate base in the general case. Bounded distances between parallel rays give equality of their asymptotic distances by two triangle inequalities. Lemma 5.3 proves completeness with a bound uniform in t coming from chordal Cauchyness. For p>1, Theorem 5.4 constructs the interpolating rays by distant-endpoint segments, using the near-geodesic estimate from uniform convexity. Theorem 5.7 uses decreasing minimal-singularity approximations and energy differences to obtain p=1 existence. These are imported known statements, not newly certified general proofs. The book does not import the literal malformed complementary p-range or the printed shorthand limit expression as its proof.

Gupta Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 6.5 characterize finite-p-energy rays from `V_theta` by the weighted integral of the Legendre test curve's mass deficit. Definition 6.2 retains maximality for the correspondence. Its proof combines the contact-set formula with layer cake. Lemma 6.4 is a converse endpoint-finiteness statement with initial potential in the regular obstacle-envelope class; here the initial point is exactly `P_theta(0)`. For this purpose an arbitrary continuous initial potential is not substituted. The Legendre normalization is independently checked against Darvas–Zhang v4 equation (18), also matching Chapter 65's existing plus-sign convention.

## Normalization reconstructed explicitly

Write `d_p^C = V^(-1/p) d_p^G`, as in the preceding Chapter 68 integration. The chordal distance has the identical factor. The supremum/limit identity therefore persists in normalized units.

Let `c` be the terminal parameter of the maximal Legendre test curve and `M(tau)` its mass for strict interior levels `tau<c`. Put `w_t=u_t-ct`. Its Legendre curve is `hat w_tau = hat u_(tau+c)`, with cutoff zero. On the initial measure `mu_0=MA(V_theta)/V`, the velocity of the centered ray is `dot w_0 = dot u_0-c <= 0`. For `a>0`, the contact-set formula gives `mu_0(-dot w_0>a)=1-M(c-a)/V`. Tonelli and layer cake then yield

`d_p^C(V_theta,u_1-c)^p = (1/V) integral |dot u_0-c|^p MA(V_theta) = (p/V) integral_(-infinity)^c (c-tau)^(p-1) (V-M(tau)) d tau`.

Only an interior mass function is integrated, so a separately specified endpoint value at `c` has no effect on this formula. Finiteness is unchanged by subtracting a constant velocity; the normalization is not unchanged. The elementary constant ray `u_t=V_theta-at`, `a>0`, has cutoff `c=-a`, full mass at every strict interior level and zero centered mass moment. Its uncentered normalized speed is `a`. This checks that the integral cannot be equated with the uncentered endpoint distance at nonzero cutoff. At `c=0`, the probability moment uses coefficient p; the source's unweighted p-th moment integral itself has no extra p.

Bounded maximal test curves have full mass below a finite lower cutoff, so their mass-deficit integral is finite for every finite p. This supplies the bridge from the chapter's linearly bounded filtration examples to the broader finite-energy setting. It does not claim uniform spectral support for all finite-energy rays or a quantization theorem for their unbounded tails.

The chordal formula gives `d_p(u_T,v_T) <= T D_p(U,V)`. It has no reverse estimate at one fixed T. A family of separately established finite-time error estimates supplies no bound uniform over all t>0 by itself. The chapter's compact-time Bergman estimate retains its quantifiers. No incompatible claim about fixed-time versus chordal convergence, a chosen A4 topology or convergence of independent degreewise Chow minimizers is inferred.

## Literal initial-version issues excluded from quotation

These locators refer only to the actually acquired arXiv:2406.07669v1, not to an unacquired journal or dissertation edition.

* Page 3's relative finite-p-energy display uses the reference Monge–Ampère measure rather than the standard variable-potential measure. The book does not copy this definition or base a relative-space comparison on it; full-mass Ep is defined separately in the correct preceding display.
* Page 6's second uniform-convexity inequality repeats `p<=2` where the complementary range is needed. No printed p-range correction is promoted as a new result, and the quantitative display is not imported.
* Page 12, Theorem 5.2 proof, omits the endpoint distance factor from `d_p(v_l,v_0)=l/t`. The constant-speed identity is `(l/t)d_p(v_t,v_0)`. The displayed shorthand is not a normalization input.
* Page 12, Lemma 5.3's last displayed bound keeps a free k after the inner limit. The usable proof fixes an epsilon and an index J, uses the chordal Cauchy bound for all j,k>=J and all t, and then takes k to infinity at each t. The book does not import a limit interchange from the malformed last line.
* Page 14, Lemma 5.6, uses the zero function as a full-mass reference in an arbitrary big representative. Zero need not be theta-psh; its proof must retain `V_theta`. After a common linear shift, Remark 3.3 gives `u_t^j<=V_theta`. Lidskii applied to `V_theta>=u_t^j>=u_t` then bounds the p-th chordal distance by `d_p(V_theta,u_1)^p-d_p(V_theta,u_1^j)^p`, which tends to zero. This is the identifiable base-point correction, not an original general repair of the big-class theory. The same page's p=1 interpolation expansion omits alpha on one intermediate energy term; the next line has the two correct affine coefficients.
* Page 15's inverse Legendre formula has a minus sign. With a decreasing test curve tending to `V_theta` as tau tends to minus infinity, that literal supremum is infinite for t>0 wherever `V_theta` is finite. Darvas–Zhang v4 equation (18) uses the required plus sign; that independently identified formula is the book input.
* Page 16's first layer-cake line has an extra p before the p-th velocity moment itself. The factor appears once before the tail integral, as in equation (13), not before both sides. The correct final moment identity is reconstructed above.
