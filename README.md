The replacement author source for P05.2 is explicitly withdrawn and is
preserved as such; the book does not cite its positive theorem.  Chapter 72
instead gives independent real-scalar proofs.  Every finite
one-dimensional balanced space has real-PA regularity, and real-PA
orthogonality holds exactly when the cone of nonnegative balancing relations
is one-dimensional at every vertex.  Bounded edges, cycles, and lattice
saturation introduce no additional condition.  For finite products, the
envelope of a separable bounded obstacle is exactly the sum of the factor
envelopes.  Consequently, products of finite balanced graphs and complete
affine spaces have real-PA envelopes for every separable bounded obstacle,
including products with branched graph factors.  This does not cover an
arbitrary nonseparable obstacle.  On the other hand, the
literal BMPS integral-slope class fails regularity whenever a finite graph
has a vertex where the reference slope vector is strictly positive on every
nonzero local balancing relation.  The construction gives an explicit
fractional-slope envelope and a quantitative uniform separation from every
literal convex PA approximant.  A global integral-roof construction removes
the local strictness hypothesis: literal regularity on every finite
one-dimensional balanced space holds exactly in degree zero, equivalently
exactly when the space is compact.  In positive degree the exact envelope
of a bounded literal obstacle is a rational convex combination of
\(\gamma\) and \(\max\{\gamma,C\}\), with a nonintegral slope on a bounded
edge cell and a quantitative uniform separation from every literal convex
PA approximant.  A vertex-cone obstacle extends this failure to every
noncompact finite rational polyhedral space in every dimension, without any
balancing hypothesis.  Thus literal complete-affine regularity fails in
every positive dimension.  The higher-dimensional real-PA characterization,
the compact higher-dimensional literal case, arbitrary nonseparable product
obstacles, and the source convention repair remain open, so the atom count
is unchanged.
