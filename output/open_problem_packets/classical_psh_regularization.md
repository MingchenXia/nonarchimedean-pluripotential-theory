# Higher-dimensional regularization of classical psh functions

Let \(X\) be a good strict analytic space over a complete nontrivially valued
field and let \(u\) be classically psh in the functorial
Gubler--Rabinoff sense. Determine the strongest natural hypotheses under
which \(u\) is locally a decreasing limit of:

1. semipositive real PL functions;
2. locally Fubini--Study functions;
3. CLD-smooth psh functions; or
4. weakly smooth psh functions.

Known boundaries:

- for real PL functions, model semipositivity is equivalent to classical psh
  by Gubler--Rabinoff Theorem 4.3; on a boundaryless space (and hence on the
  relative interior in general), semipositive real PL functions admit local
  uniform approximation by locally Fubini--Study functions through
  Gubler--Rabinoff Theorem 1.5.  That theorem starts with a semipositive
  real PL function and gives uniform, not monotone, approximation; Theorem
  5.3 is instead the restriction-to-curves criterion for semipositive real
  PL functions;
- every function in the revised CLD-v2 transfinite psh sheaf is classically
  psh, but the converse for general singular functions is not known; this
  must not be confused with the older condition
  \(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[u]\geq0\), whose functorial curve pullback is
  precisely problematic.  Use arXiv:1204.6277v2, posted 25 July 2025 and
  internally dated 29 July 2025, rather than silently reverting to the older
  draft;
- on a smooth boundaryless curve, Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff Proposition 15.11
  gives, on every relatively compact open, a decreasing **net** of weakly
  smooth psh functions;
- Wanner Corollary 5.4 gives decreasing CLD-smooth regularization of
  **continuous** psh functions on open subsets of smooth proper curves only
  in its stated special cases (residue field algebraic over a finite field,
  \(\mathbf P^1\), or a Mumford curve), over an algebraically closed complete
  nontrivially valued field as assumed in the source.

Try regularized maxima, formal/model approximation, harmonic
tropicalizations, curvewise approximation plus gluing, and capacity methods.
Check relative-boundary points, singular spaces, nonmetrizable topology, and
nets versus sequences. A family of curvewise approximants is not a proof
until compatibility and analytic gluing are established. Search for
obstructions from nonsmooth harmonic functions or incompatible tropical
charts.

Return separate verdicts for the four approximation classes and for curves
versus higher dimension. Promote no theorem beyond the exact proved domain.
