# Zero-volume non-Archimedean energy, measures, and convex bodies

Let \(X\) be a compact Kähler manifold and let
\(\theta\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbf R)\) be pseudoeffective with
\(\operatorname{vol}(\theta)=0\). Darvas--Xia--Zhang still define
\(\operatorname{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta)\) as the inverse system, over
Kähler perturbations \(\theta+\omega\), of positive-mass
\(\mathcal I\)-maximal test curves.  This is an
\(\mathcal I\)-type inverse-system object; their finite-energy space is
introduced in the big case.  The usual probability Monge--Ampère and
normalized energy signatures divide by \(\operatorname{vol}(\theta)\) and
therefore do not extend unchanged to volume zero.

Keep the adjacent results distinct.  Darvas--Reboulet--Witt Nyström--Xia--
Zhang Corollary 7.7 associates a natural compact convex body to every
pseudoeffective class and computes its \(n\)-dimensional volume as
\(\operatorname{vol}(\theta)/n!\); at volume zero this does not determine the
body's affine dimension or moments.  Xia's limit partial body in Section 5.6
may have zero mass and loses the continuity available in the positive-mass
setting; its definition is nevertheless independent of the chosen ample
effective divisor and Kähler form.  Conjecture 5.29 predicts that its
dimension equals Cao's numerical dimension.  Xia's Duistermaat--Heckman
construction in Section 7 is for finite-energy non-Archimedean metrics of an
ample line bundle.  None of these statements already supplies the requested
zero-volume energy or renormalized measure.

Find the strongest useful replacement after introducing only the minimum
necessary reference datum. Investigate:

1. an unnormalized relative energy with values in an extended ordered group;
2. rescaling by the first nonzero coefficient of
   \(\operatorname{vol}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)\);
3. path independence of the proposed rescaled energy or measure under
   changing the Kähler perturbation;
4. a nonzero limiting measure supported on a lower-dimensional numerical
   locus;
5. a \(P\)-sensitive transcendental Okounkov or convex body whose dimension
   and moments recover numerical data.

The phrase ``first nonzero coefficient'' in item 2 is a target, not an
available datum: first prove that the chosen perturbation admits an
appropriate asymptotic expansion, identify its first nonzero order, and test
whether that order and coefficient are independent of the perturbation path.

The existing no-go theorem says that an assignment invariant only under
\(\mathcal I\)-equivalence cannot recover actual nonpluripolar mass. Any
positive mass-sensitive construction must therefore use strictly richer
data, for example the full \(P\)-type; do not identify
\(\mathcal I\)-equivalence with \(P\)-equivalence. Test the zero class, nef
classes of positive numerical dimension but zero volume, and non-nef boundary
classes. Distinguish canonical unnormalized zero measures from noncanonical
renormalized probability limits.

The attached zero-volume papers do not themselves state that no-go theorem in
the displayed form.  Use it only if its separate source and exact hypotheses
are supplied to the run; otherwise treat it as a conditional premise, not as
a conclusion of the attached references.

Return verified constructions, path-dependence counterexamples, conditional
reductions, and genuinely open parts separately.
