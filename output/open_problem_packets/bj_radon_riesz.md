# The Boucksom--Jonsson Radon--Riesz problem — resolved record

**Published question:** Boucksom--Jonsson Question 11.16.
**Book status (25 August 2026):** disproved globally; the positive theorem on
one fixed strongly compact SNC dual complex remains valid.

Work over an algebraically closed trivially valued field.  Let
\(X=\mathbf P^n_k\), \(n\ge2\), \(L=\mathcal O(1)\), and
\(\omega=c_1(L)\).  Fix a linear line \(C\simeq\mathbf P^1\), choose
pairwise distinct points \(p_j\in C(k)\), and let \(v_j\) be the exceptional
valuation of the blowup at \(p_j\).  For \(n\ge3\), let \(v_C\) be the
exceptional valuation of the blowup along \(C\); for \(n=2\), put
\(v_C=\operatorname{ord}_C\).  Then

\[
 v_j\longrightarrow v_C\quad\text{weakly},\qquad
 T_\omega(v_j)=T_\omega(v_C)=1,
 \qquad d_{\infty,\omega}(v_j,v_C)\ge1.
\]

For weak convergence, write a fixed rational function as \(f=a/b\) near the
generic point of \(C\).  The lowest nonzero transverse initial forms of
\(a\) and \(b\) are nonzero sections of symmetric powers of the conormal
bundle.  Because \(C\) is a projective curve, each has only finitely many bad
points.  Away from their union, the conormal injection into
\(\mathfrak m_p/\mathfrak m_p^2\) gives
\(\operatorname{ord}_{E_p}(f)=v_C(f)\).  Distinctness of the \(p_j\) therefore
gives eventual equality for every fixed \(f\).

A degree-\(m\) polynomial has multiplicity at a point at most \(m\), with
equality for a power of a hyperplane.  The ideal of the linear line is
generated by linear forms, so its order on a degree-\(m\) polynomial is also
at most \(m\), again with equality for a hyperplane power.  Hence both
exhaustion values are one.  Finally, a hyperplane through \(p_j\) but not
containing \(C\) has values one and zero at \(v_j\) and \(v_C\), respectively;
the Boucksom--Jonsson section formula forces the uniform strong gap.

## Compatibility with the fixed-complex theorem

On a fixed SNC dual complex in the smooth projective characteristic-zero
setting, strong compactness and compact-to-Hausdorff comparison still identify
weak and strong convergence.  The moving point centres above lie on pairwise
different blowups, so no one fixed dual complex contains a tail.  The
arbitrary-characteristic fixed-complex compactness estimate remains a
separate restricted question, not a residual form of the disproved global
implication.

## Historical search prompt

The original packet asked whether, for an arbitrary net \((v_i)\) in
\(X^{\mathrm{lin}}\),

\[
 v_i\to v\text{ strongly}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_i\to v\text{ weakly and }T_\omega(v_i)\to T_\omega(v).
\]

Corollary 11.15 proves the criterion with dual energy in place of \(T\), and
Question 11.16 asks whether \(T\) can replace it.  The historical routes were:

1. exhaustion by dual complexes with model-uniform quantitative control;
2. Izumi-type inequalities and normalized retractions;
3. reduction to divisorial valuations and Dini/compactness;
4. an abstract convex-analytic Radon--Riesz principle;
5. counterexamples from escaping models or thin exceptional divisors.

The fifth route succeeds, but only after arranging that the exhaustion at the
weak limit is still one.  The older curve example converging to the trivial
valuation did not satisfy that premise because its exhaustion dropped to
zero.
