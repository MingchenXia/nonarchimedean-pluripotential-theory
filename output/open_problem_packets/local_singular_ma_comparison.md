# A common local singular Monge--Ampère theory

Compare three local frameworks:

1. continuous functions in the revised CLD-v2 (transfinitely generated) psh
   sheaf and their Bedford--Taylor products on good Berkovich analytic
   domains; this is stronger than merely assuming positivity of the old
   current \(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[u]\);
2. classically psh functions defined by all analytic curve tests;
3. formal psh functions on the normalized valuation link of a smooth
   \(n\)-dimensional complex germ in Boucksom--Favre--Jonsson (2008), with
   the mixed local intersection measure of \(n-1\) bounded formal psh
   functions.

Construct the strongest common Monge--Ampère product that can be proved
without changing these definitions, or prove an obstruction. Required tests:

- construction of an actual common analytic/polyhedral realization for
  ideal/model functions, followed by exact agreement there (no such
  comparison theorem is assumed known);
- independence of models and tropical charts;
- monotone continuity for the largest justified bounded or finite-energy
  class;
- explicit normalization and boundary/reference datum;
- locality and functoriality under étale and finite maps;
- no probability-sheaf normalization under literal restriction.

Investigate whether the normalized BFJ link can be realized as a boundary or
skeletal slice of a nontrivially valued degeneration and whether CLD
intersection multiplicities converge to the BFJ local intersection numbers.
Do not infer products merely from curve-test positivity, and do not replace
the revised CLD-v2 predicate by the older positive-current predicate. For
unbounded functions, supply capacity/integrability estimates or retain an
OPEN verdict.

Separate each genuinely proved model-locus comparison, any new verified
extension, conditional degeneration statements, and counterexamples.  At the
14 August 2026 cutoff, no primary source located by the manuscript audit
identifies the two products on a common local realization, so the existence
of even that comparison must be proved rather than cited vaguely.
