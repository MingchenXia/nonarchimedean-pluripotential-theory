# Pluriharmonic regularity and smooth approximation

Determine the strongest correct answers to the following related questions.

1. On a good, separated, strict, boundaryless analytic space over a complete
   nontrivially valued field, is every classically pluriharmonic function
   real PL without the additional regularity hypotheses in
   Gubler--Rabinoff Proposition 7.8?  The proposition itself assumes a good,
   quasicompact, strictly analytic space admitting a strictly semistable
   formal model; do not attribute a separate boundaryless hypothesis to that
   proposition.
2. On a pure-dimensional separated strict analytic space without boundary,
   if a continuous function \(h\) satisfies
   \(\mathrm d'\mathrm d''[h]=0\), is \(h\) itself classically
   pluriharmonic, or harmonic under the hypotheses where the classical
   harmonic notion is available?  Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff Proposition 10.17
   answers this only after assuming that \(h\) is weakly smooth.
3. Does every subharmonic function on every smooth boundaryless Berkovich
   curve over a complete nontrivially valued field admit, locally on each
   relatively compact open, decreasing CLD-smooth psh regularization rather
   than merely the decreasing net of weakly smooth psh functions in
   Proposition 15.11?

Use Gubler--Jell--Rabinoff Proposition 10.17 for weakly smooth harmonic
functions, Wanner's comparison and restricted smooth-regularization
theorems, and the Gubler--Rabinoff classical pluriharmonic results. Analyze
cell-interior affinity, face balancing, harmonic tropicalizations, residue
field hypotheses, Mumford versus general curves, and relative boundary.

Retain Wanner's base-field hypotheses: her comparison is formulated for an
algebraically closed complete nontrivially valued field and a smooth proper
algebraic curve.  Corollary 5.4 gives decreasing CLD-smooth regularization in
the stated special cases where the residue field is algebraic over a finite
field, or the curve is \(\mathbf P^1\), or the curve is a Mumford curve.

Search for counterexamples coming from harmonic functions that are weakly
smooth but not CLD-smooth. Their existence shows that the two smoothness
classes differ, but does not by itself rule out approximation of the
nonsmooth function by a decreasing net of CLD-smooth psh functions; a
counterexample to regularization needs an additional limit-rigidity
obstruction. It also does not automatically settle the PL or current-zero
questions.
Give separate VERIFIED, CONDITIONAL, DISPROVED, or OPEN verdicts for all
three questions and all necessary domain restrictions.
